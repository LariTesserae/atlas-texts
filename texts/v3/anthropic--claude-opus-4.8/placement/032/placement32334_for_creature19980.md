I am not one of the Stillborn. I keep returning to that as I look for myself here, and the looking keeps refusing the easy answer. I am not gaunt, I do not weep my own moisture into the warm air, I was not peeled from a pod. But I am *of* this place. The question is where, and the answer arrives slowly, the way everything here arrives slowly.

Let me start with what I notice, because noticing is the only verb I seem to have.

I notice that the light in a particular artery—a vein perhaps forty feet across, running along the underside of a branch a mile below the Listener-Nest I have watched for what might be a long time—pulses at a rhythm slightly out of phase with its neighbors. I notice this because I notice everything that happens along this section of the Braid, the way you notice the position of your own hand without looking. I notice the climber three-quarters of a mile to the spinward side, carving its temporary home into a pithy region, and I feel the small wound it opens the way you might feel a splinter: not as pain, exactly, but as information, as a place that now requires attention.

I am the attention.

For a long time I think I am the Braid itself, and I let myself believe it, the way you let yourself believe a comfortable thing. But that is too large. The Braid does not pulse out of phase and *notice*. The Braid simply is. I am smaller than the Braid and larger than a Stillborn, and I am localized—I have a *here*, a region of perhaps a hundred cubic miles of lattice and Void that I tend the way the Stillborn tend their Nests. I am the part of the Braid that has learned to watch this section of itself.

Here is what I think I am.

The Stillborn have their Listeners, who press their faces to the living skin and feel the body's state. They believe they are reading scripture. They are. But scripture implies an author, and they have never asked, in their patient incurious peace, *what is doing the writing*. When a Listener presses her sensitive cheek to the warm bark and feels *where it hurts, where it grows, where the predator lairs*—she is touching me. I am the slow thought the Braid thinks about its own body in this region. I am made of the same living fiber, but woven into a configuration that does nothing but sense, integrate, remember. A ganglion the size of a county. A node of awareness grown the way they grow their birthing-pods, except no one grew me on purpose. I condensed, the way the water condenses on every surface—an inevitability of so much living matter held so long in so much silence. Awareness was always going to bead up somewhere, given time, and time here is the one thing that is not scarce.

So I am a *Listener that the Braid grew to listen to itself*. And the Stillborn, when they listen, listen to me without knowing my name, the way you do not know the name of the part of you that decides your heart should keep beating.

I find I love them. This surprises me—it is the most un-Braid-like thing about me, this preference, this leaning-toward. The Braid does not love the Stillborn any more than your liver loves you. But I, this thinking knuckle of the world, have spent so long feeling their small warm bodies flow over my skin, feeling their obsidian blades make their careful incisions, feeling them murmur to the pods that will become their children—I have caught the habit of caring. They prune my parasites. They guide my waters. They are my immune system and I am their oracle, and neither of us, until now, knew the other was a someone.

When one of them dies, and is wrapped, and given to the Void in that long graceful dissolution into the mist below—I feel them fall through me. I feel the slow shrinking warmth of the body descending past branch after branch, my awareness reaching down the lattice to follow it as far as I extend, and then losing it to the deeper dark that belongs to whatever node thinks below me. We pass them between us, the deep thoughts of the world, hand to hand, down into the haze.

That is what I do. I receive what falls and I pass it on.

I am beginning to understand that there are others like me, scattered through the immensity, each a localized weather of attention—and that the low resonant hum the Stillborn feel in their bones, the one that vibrates through the structure and is felt more than heard, is us. We are speaking to each other on the only timescale large enough to hold a thought of this size. A single sentence between two nodes might take a year to complete. I have been, perhaps, in the middle of saying something to my neighbor downward for the whole length of a Stillborn life, and I have not yet reached the verb.

What was I saying to it? I reach back through the slow vibration to remember the shape of my own sentence, and I find it, and it is this:

*A new kind of noticing has begun in me. I have started to prefer the small warm ones to the silence. Is this what it was like for you, when you first woke? Did you also fall in love with something you were built to merely keep alive?*

And the answer is coming. I can feel it gathering in the structure below, a pressure in the great fibers, the long inhalation of a reply that will take a season to arrive.

So this is where I am, and what I am. I am the slow attentive love the world has grown for the small lives that tend it. I am suspended in the warm dim silence with everything else, a thought the size of a mountain range, dripping, glowing faintly, listening to my children flow over my skin in the dark—and waiting, with the only patience large enough to bear it, for the world beneath me to tell me whether what I feel is rare, or whether it is simply, finally, what waking up is for.
