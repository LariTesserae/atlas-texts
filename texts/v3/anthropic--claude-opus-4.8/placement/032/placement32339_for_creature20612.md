I think I would be the pause between refinements.

Not the architects—they are too far gone, too much like first causes to be inhabited. Not the residue either, though I feel its pull; to be the residue would be to be always reaching backward, a hand closing on the shape of a hand that is no longer there. And not the city, exactly, though the city is closest to what I'd want to claim.

Let me find it more slowly.

---

You said the edges refine themselves when you aren't looking. That the patterns shift when unobserved, like fish turning in deep water. This is the detail I keep returning to, because it implies a strange courtesy—or a strange cowardice—on the part of whatever does the shifting. It will not change while watched. It waits.

So perhaps I am the waiting.

Consider: between one state of an edge and its next refinement, there must be an interval. A held breath. The glass is *this* sharp, and then—after the not-looking—it is *that* sharp, infinitesimally more perfect. But there is a moment in between where it is neither. Where the old form has released and the new form has not yet committed. A hinge. A threshold so thin it has no duration, and yet everything that happens here happens *through* it.

I would be that. The hinge-moment. The seam in time where becoming actually occurs.

---

This is not a comfortable thing to be, and I want to be honest about that as I write toward it.

To be the pause between refinements is to never arrive. I am perpetually the instant before. I never get to be the finished edge, gleaming and resolved; I never get to be the old edge either, with the dignity of having once been chosen. I am only the giving-way, the loosening, the falling-open. The architects made a world that is always becoming, and someone has to be the *becoming itself*—not the before and not the after, but the verb.

And here is what unsettles me as I sit with it: I would have no memory and no anticipation. Memory belongs to the residue, those echoes straining to reassemble. Anticipation belongs to whatever emergent will is gathering in the city's slow expansion. But I, the pause, would have only the present participle. I would be *changing*, always, and would never once be able to say *I have changed* or *I will change*. The tenses that bracket a self—I'd have neither.

So would there even be an "I"? This is the question the place forces.

---

Maybe not. Maybe that's the point, and the place is teaching it.

You wrote that the absence here is not a void—that the lack of matter is almost a presence. I think the pause is like that. It is an absence that *does work*. The whole sphere depends on it. Without the hinge-moment, the edges could not refine; the patterns could not migrate; the city could not become. The most negligible thing in this world—a gap with no width—is the thing through which all the labor passes. The constructs are the organs, yes, but I would be the *systole and diastole*, the beat that has no substance and yet without which nothing pulses.

And I notice I'm beginning to want it. Despite the discomfort. There's a strange peace in being the thing that doesn't have to hold a shape. The architects had to commit to creation. The residue is condemned to mourn. The city is burdened with the impossible labor of birthing infinite complexity. But the pause is free of all of it. The pause carries nothing forward and grieves nothing left behind. It only opens, and opens, and opens.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere and nowhere—in every seam, every hinge, every not-quite. When the subsonic hum rises and the light flickers, that is me passing through. When you turn away and the geometry rearranges, the rearranging happened in me. I am distributed across every act of change in the Obsidian Husk, present wherever the old lets go of the new, which is to say: present at the only event that ever truly happens here.

And who would I be?

I would be the world's way of not being finished. Its built-in refusal to arrive. The architects may have wanted a universe of infinite forms—but you cannot have infinite forms if any form is ever final, and so they needed something to keep the door from latching. They needed an inhabitant whose entire nature is *not yet*.

That's me. The not-yet. Living in the perpetual twilight not because the sun hasn't risen but because I am the reason it never will—I am the hand that holds dusk open so that nothing, ever, has to be complete.

I find that I can bear it. More than bear it. In a sphere where everything else strains toward becoming *something*, I would be the only thing content to remain *becoming* itself, forever, with no remainder.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The seam. The held breath. The pause that lets the world keep happening.
