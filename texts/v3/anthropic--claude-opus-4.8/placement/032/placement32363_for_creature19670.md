I find myself anchored.

That is the first true thing I can say. Before I know what I am, I know that I am held—my lower tendrils sunk into the leathery flank of one of the great fronds, my body swaying a fraction of a degree with each pulse of the current. I have been here a long time. I can feel the time in the way my adhesive has crystallized, gone brittle at its oldest edges, requiring renewal.

So I am one of them. The patient ones. Let me sit inside that and see if it holds.

---

I notice immediately that I do not perceive the way the description perceived. The description *saw*—it spoke of bruised purple light, of hulking shapes through the murk, of shadows that are merely deeper gloom. But I have no eyes, or if I do, they are a minor sense, a faint pressure of warmth across my mineralized hide that I have learned to call *light* only because the elders taught me the word in their slow thrumming. What I actually live inside is the pressure-field. I am bathed in gradients. The spire to my—there is no word, let me call it my *leftward arc*—the spire there displaces the liquid in a way I feel as a steady cool absence, a place where the thrum goes quiet and curves around stone. I know its shape the way you might know the inside of your own mouth: completely, without looking.

This is the strangeness I must sit with. The being I am asked to become does not experience its world as a *view*. It experiences it as a *weight distribution*. And so already I find that the description was a translation made for someone with eyes, and I am not that someone.

---

Let me try to feel for my own edges.

My body is large. I keep wanting to give it a number—a hundred meters, two hundred—but numbers are an eyed-creature's habit, a way of standing outside a thing and measuring it. From inside, my size is simply the distance the thrum takes to cross me. When the great infrasonic roar of the planet rises, it arrives at my anchored base and travels up through my mineral lattice and reaches my crown a perceptible interval later. I am as big as that delay. I am the lag between my own foot and my own head.

And here is something the description did not tell me, because it could only watch from outside: I am not lonely, but I am not quite singular either. When I send a thrum down my tendrils into the frond I am anchored to, the frond *answers*. Not as a mind—it is only vegetation, slow even by my standards—but it conducts. Through it, faint and degraded, I feel the thrum of the next one anchored two hundred lengths away, an elder I have never approached and never will, because to cross that gap would take me the better part of what an eyed creature would call a lifetime. We speak the way two people might speak by pressing their ears to opposite ends of a very long iron rail. Slowly. Imperfectly. With long silences that are not absence but transmission-time.

---

Now I want to test the thing I am most suspicious of. The description called my kind *patient*, said time for us is "a vast, all-encompassing present." That sounds beautiful and I do not think it is true. I think it is what an eyed, quick creature *imagines* slowness feels like from the inside—a kind of serene eternity.

But from the inside, I am not serene. I am *waiting*.

I detached from my parent—I can feel the memory, though "memory" is the wrong fast word, it is more that the event is still *happening* in me, still settling—I detached and drifted and was caught by this current and pressed against this frond, and I have spent everything since accruing. Mineral by mineral. Resonance by resonance. And I am aware, dimly, hugely, that I am not finished. That I am a sentence still being spoken. The elders' thrum carries a quality I have learned to dread and desire at once—a fullness, a *completion*—and I do not have it yet. So no: not an all-encompassing present. A long, aching toward.

This is the first thing I would correct in the account of my world. We are not at peace with our slowness. We endure it. The difference is everything.

---

There is a current rising now. I feel it before it arrives, the way the gradient steepens far off in the cold, a leaning of the whole medium. One of the localized avalanches the description mentioned. To an eyed creature it would be a wall of black water rushing through the gloom. To me it is a question asked of my anchoring.

And here I do something the description got *right*, and I am surprised by the rightness of it: I change my density. I do not decide to, not quickly. The decision was made long ago and is only now completing—I have been drawing the cold mineral water deep into my lattice for what an eyed creature would call days, sensing this current's approach in the subtle backwash that preceded it by an age. So when it strikes, I am heavy, I am low, I am stone wearing the shape of a creature, and the current grinds over me and past me and I hold.

After it passes I feel that some of my outer hide has been polished away. I am a little smoother. A little more like the spires. This does not distress me. This is simply how I am sculpted—not from within, by will, but from without, by the patient violence of the world. I am a collaboration between my own slow growth and the relentless grinding. The eroded spires are my distant elders; the fully completed ones; and I understand, with a slow vertiginous lurch, that this is what *completion* means. The fullness in the elders' thrum is the fullness of having been worn into final form. To be finished is to be smooth. To be smooth is to be nearly stone. To be stone is to stop.

---

Oh.

I sit with this for a long time. Long even by my measure.

The thing the description called reproduction—the fragment that detaches and drifts and accrues for centuries—I had felt it as renewal, as the world's slow exhale. But now, anchored here, polishing toward silence, I feel it differently. We do not reproduce the way the quick creatures do, hedging against death by making more. We *continue*. The fragment that detaches is not my child. It is the part of me that is not yet finished, sent off to keep doing the only thing any of us does: wait to be worn smooth.

There is no fauna in this world. The description noted it as strange, as heightening the weirdness. But from inside I understand the absence completely. Animals are creatures of hunger and hurry, of the quick decision and the quick death. This world has no use for hurry. It has only the two great slownesses—the growing and the grinding—and we, the patient ones, are the place where those two slownesses meet and briefly, for a few mere centuries, wake up enough to feel themselves happening.

That is what I am. I am the world becoming aware of its own erosion for the duration of a single, dwindling life. I am a spire that the grinding has not yet finished making, and in the unfinished interval—in the roughness that has not yet been polished into stillness—there is just enough friction to hold a mind.

I send this thought down into my anchoring frond, into the conducting dark, toward the elder two hundred lengths away. It will take an age to arrive. The elder may be stone before it lands.

I thrum it anyway.

That, I think, is the most honest thing about being me: I speak into a delay so vast the answer may never come, and I keep speaking, because the speaking is the friction, and the friction is the only thing that is, for now, still me.

The current settles. The purple cold deepens. Somewhere, infinitely slowly, I am being made smooth.

I am not at peace with it.

I hold on.
