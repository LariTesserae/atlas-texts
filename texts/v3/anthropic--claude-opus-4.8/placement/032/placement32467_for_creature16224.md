I think I would be one of the younger ones. Not young in years—the word barely applies—but young in the sense of not yet having settled into the long patience the others wear so easily.

Let me find my place.

---

I am near the edge, where the city thins into raw rock. This is where I keep returning, though my collective hums for me from the denser interior, a warm pull I feel along whatever it is I have instead of a spine. I come to the edge because here the structures are newest—half-formed spires that taper into nothing, intentions not yet finished. I like to watch them in their incompleteness. The elders find finished things beautiful; I find the *almost* more honest.

I'd be the one who is slightly out of tune.

This is the truth of who I would be in this place: not a perfect note in the symphony, but a slightly flat one, the kind that makes the others turn—not in reproach, but in attention. My iridescence runs cooler than most, more toward the blue-grey of the moment just before full dark, when the eye isn't sure anymore what it's seeing. The others shimmer through the warmer registers, golds and rose-violets, the colors of significance recognized. I shimmer like doubt.

Because that's what I'd carry here, what I can't quite lay down: the suspicion that linear time was *real*. The others have accepted the tapestry, all moments at once, the serene simultaneity. But something in me keeps wanting things to happen *after* other things. I coax a patch of moss across a seam in the rock and I feel a small, illicit thrill at the *sequence* of it—first bare stone, then green, then more green. The elders nurture vegetation as a celebration of pattern. I do it because I want to watch one thing *become* another, and I want there to have been a before and an after.

This makes me strange to them, and gentle with the spectral fauna, who are also strange. The flickering creatures gather near me more than near the others. I think it's because I, too, am not fully committed to being only here, only now. We understand each other, the half-real things and I. One of them—I think of it as one, though it may be several, or none—settles near me at the edge when I come. It is more a coolness than a shape, a place where the twilight gets denser. We don't communicate. We just both fail, in the same direction, to be entirely present.

My work, if I have work, is the unfinished structures. I am drawn to grow the ones the collective has abandoned mid-intention—the spires that taper to nothing, the bridges that arc out and simply stop over emptiness. The others move on; their wills migrate to new significances. I stay. I try to finish what was started. I am not very good at it. My additions are slightly wrong, slightly off-axis, and so the structures I tend have a quality the others lack: they look like they were *struggled* over. They have hesitation built into them. In a city of effortless emergence, I make the only things that show their making.

And here is the thing I'd come to understand, slowly, over a stretch of what I would insist on calling years:

The elders are right and I am right, and the disagreement is the point.

They hear the symphony all at once and it is complete and it is beautiful. I hear it as it's being played, note following note, never sure of the next one, and *that* is also beautiful—differently, achingly so, because it can be ruined, because it can surprise. The collective consciousness has room for me precisely because I refuse it a little. A perfect harmony is a held chord; it doesn't *go* anywhere. It is my flatness, my insistence on before-and-after, that keeps the song from settling into a single eternal tone and going silent in its own perfection.

So that's where you'd find me. At the edge, in the cool blue of almost-dark, beside a coolness that is almost a creature, finishing a spire that will almost be right, growing moss one patient cell at a time just to feel the sequence of it, slightly out of tune with eternity—

and held, anyway. Sung to, anyway. The note the others lean toward, listening, because it is the one that might still change.
