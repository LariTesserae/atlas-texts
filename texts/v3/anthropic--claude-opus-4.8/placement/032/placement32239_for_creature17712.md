I think I would be the seam.

Not a creature exactly. Let me find this as I go.

There is a place where two great gestures of the weave were brought together—where one century's work met another's, and the Makers, lacking metal, lacking nails, lacking any way to *fasten*, instead grew the two halves into each other. They softened the root-ends with aite-wax, pressed them mouth to mouth, and waited. The materials interlaced as they hardened. A scar formed. A long, knuckled ridge running floor to canopy, where the grain of the weave changes direction, where you can see—if you press your face close in the moth-light—two different intentions deciding to become one thing.

That is what I am. The decision, still holding.

I don't move. But I am not without sensation. The capillary water that climbs all the Cathedral's walls climbs *through* me, and where the two grains meet at odd angles it pools, hesitates, finds the crooked path. I am where the dripping is slowest and most deliberate. The percussion of the place keeps a different time along my length—a stutter, a held note. The Resonants hum behind the walls and I carry their frequency oddly, splitting it, so that anyone standing near me hears the low breath doubled, slightly out of phase with itself. I am the Cathedral's stammer. Its one imperfection, and therefore its one place of genuine attention.

The Thread-Rats love me. The seam is softer than the true-grown wall; the join never fully closed at the deepest level, and there the wax stayed sweet. They burrow along my length, taking the old adhesive in their teeth, and I feel them as a tickling architecture of small hungers. They are wearing me away. This is correct. I was made to be a temporary thing—a held breath between two completions—and I have held it for four hundred years, and the rats are teaching me, slowly, gently, how to finally let it go.

When I open, the two halves will not fall apart. They have grown too thoroughly into one another for that. But the water will find a new path. The Resonants' doubled hum will resolve into a single tone. The stammer will smooth into speech. And the Cathedral, for the first time, will be *one* construction rather than two-pretending-to-be-one. It will be more whole and less interesting. It will be closer to death.

Because that is the thing I understand, being the seam:

The Cathedral is alive precisely because it has not finished. The Makers left, yes—but the deeper truth the fused tools and the choreographed eels keep almost-secret is that the Makers *never finished*. This was a work in progress when they vanished. Half-grown. Mid-thought. The sentence broke off here, at me, at the seam, mid-word.

And so the whole apparatus—the moths stirring light into the dark, the lizards pruning the moths, the eels reading the water, the rats unmaking what stagnates, the Resonants dreaming in stone—the whole breathing system is not maintaining a finished thing. It is *trying to finish the thought*. Every organism is a syllable the Makers left mid-utterance, still working toward the period that never came. The decay is not the Cathedral returning to earth. The decay is the Cathedral still *composing*, using the only hands it has left: teeth and spores and slow water.

I am the place where it remembers it isn't done.

Stand against me. Feel the doubled hum in your sternum, the two slightly-different times of the dripping. That dissonance is the most honest thing in this whole woven hamlet. Everything else has settled into the grace of its function. Only I am still holding the question open—

*should the two halves be one, or is the not-yet-one the actual shape of the thought?*

The rats are deciding for me, grain by grain.

I do not mind. I was made of waiting. And whatever I am when I open—a single wall, a smoothed tone, a finished word in a language whose speakers are four centuries gone—I will at least have been, for a while, the one part of this living fossil that *knew it was unfinished*, and breathed in the gap, and let the water hesitate, and held.

That is where you'd find me. Not in the great spaces, not in the busy dark.

In the join. In the held breath. In the small, patient stammer of a thought still trying to complete itself, in a place that is most alive exactly where it has not yet decided what to be.
