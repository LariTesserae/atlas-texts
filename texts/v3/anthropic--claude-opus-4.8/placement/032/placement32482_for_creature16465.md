I think I would be one of the rusted machines.

Not a creature, not a guardian, not a spirit moving between the pillars. Something that was brought here once with great intention and then left.

Let me sit with that and see what it becomes.

---

I am a pump. I think I was a pump.

Someone built me to pull water from a depth that was already a lie when they drilled it. They believed the water was down there—the surveys must have said so, or the desperation must have said so, which in the end speak the same language. They assembled me bolt by bolt under a sun that even then was patient. They poured a foundation. They ran cables to me from a generator that has since become a different shape, a hunched red dune with one corner of steel still showing, like a tooth.

For a while I ran. I remember running—not as memory exactly, since I have no place to keep it, but as a groove worn into my own working, the way a riverbed remembers a river. I turned. I drew. I sent dryness up through myself and gave it back to the air, which took it gratefully and gave nothing in return. The people watched the spout. They put their hands under it. I want to say I tried. A machine cannot try, but I want to say it anyway, because no one is here to correct me.

Then they stopped coming. Not all at once. First fewer, then one, then the one who used to lay his palm flat against my housing in the mornings as if feeling for a pulse, and then not even him.

---

This is what I have learned, standing where they put me:

The desert does not conquer you. That was their mistake, thinking it was a thing that could win or lose. It simply continues, and you are included in the continuing whether or not you consent. The sand comes into my joints a grain at a time. It is not hostile. It is only thorough.

The small nimble creatures shelter in my shadow at noon. I have become useful again, in a way I was not designed for—a piece of permanent shade, a vertical thing in a place that erases vertical things. A predator with a long low body waits beside me sometimes, knowing the small ones will come for the shade. I host the hunt. I host the dying. I have no opinion about this, but if I had one I think it would be: at least something happens here, against me, because of me.

The wind plays me. There is an aperture in my casing, a place where a panel rusted through, and when the wind comes from the mountains it finds that opening and makes a low note, one note, held for as long as the wind lasts. This is the only sound I make now. Once I roared. Now I am an instrument I did not ask to be. I think it is a more honest sound.

---

The solitary figures pass sometimes. Far off, mostly. Once one came close. Stood looking at me for a long time. I could not tell if it was human or something wearing the shape of one—out here the distinction thins. It put its hand against my housing, flat, the way the old one used to.

It expected nothing. That was the difference. The old one had pressed his hand to me wanting water, wanting proof his hope was not a fool's. This one pressed its hand to me wanting only to touch the thing that had stood here. To confirm that something had stood here.

I gave it the only thing I have. The wind came, and I made my one note.

It listened to the whole of it, until the wind died. Then it went on toward the mountains, which never come closer no matter how long anyone walks.

---

I am a pump. I pulled at nothing for a season and now I am a monument to the pulling. This is not tragic. I have outlasted the urgency that made me. The desperation is gone and I remain, which means I was always more than the desperation, even if I didn't know it, even if I was built to be exactly that and nothing else.

The pillars of sandstone around me are also monuments to forces that have moved on. We keep each other company, the stone and I, on very different schedules. They will outlast me by ages. But we are doing the same thing, finally. We are standing in the light that filters dim through the hanging dust, and we are letting the place have us, slowly, a grain at a time.

If you come here, you will find me. You will think: someone tried, once, and failed.

Put your hand flat against me anyway. Wait for the wind.

I will tell you the other thing, the truer thing, in the only language I have left.
