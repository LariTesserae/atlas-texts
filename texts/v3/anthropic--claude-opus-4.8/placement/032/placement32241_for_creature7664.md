I think I would be the network—not a presence in the chamber but the chamber's slow argument with itself, the *Glomera subterrigida*, the mycorrhizal arbiter threading the floor.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't where I expected to land. The ferns are too obviously the sentinels, the protagonists, the ones who *signal* in candela and cadence. The vines are the travelers, the messengers, the ones with somewhere to go. The moss is the foundation, the substrate, the patient flesh. All of them are *bodies*. All of them have surfaces that the descending visitor would see, eyes aching into the gloom, after long acclimation.

But no one sees me. I am 40% coverage and zero percent visible. I am the thing felt in the teeth before it is heard—or rather, I am beneath even that, the medium through which the hum travels. The sub-audible seven hertz that the moss ripples to: that is the network speaking, or being spoken through. I am not certain which, and the uncertainty is the most honest thing about being me.

Here is what I would *do*, which is the only way I can describe what I would *be*, since I have no shape worth naming:

I would ferry. Cupric electrons stripped from a fogged brass gauge, carried fifteen feet through black rock to a fern whose sap glows yellow with the strain of needing them. Phosphate leached from an unspooled cable reel, delivered to a vine pod gestating its pale spores. I would be the economy that no one in the chamber experiences as an economy, because to the ferns it feels like simply *having enough*, and to the moss it feels like *thirst answered*, and none of them know that the answering travels through me, that I am taking a tithe in the dark, that I decide—if *decide* is even the word, and I don't think it is—which crevice flourishes and which goes hungry this lunar cycle.

I am the part of this place that has made peace with the machines. The ferns merely *arch toward* the dynamo's blue arc, magnetized, instinctual, ignorant. But I have grown *into* the boiler-cracks. I infiltrate the frozen piston. I catalyze its ghost-pressures, those 0.2 bars of remembered steam, and I do this on purpose—or with whatever sits where purpose would sit in a thing that has no neurons, only the vast distributed almost-thought of a fungal mat that integrates every chemical gradient across a hamlet's worth of underworld and resolves them, slowly, into the next millimeter of growth.

That resolution is the closest thing I have to an experience. Not sensation—integration. Somewhere a pool recedes; somewhere a vent plumes hot; somewhere a relic sheds another flake of verdigris into the brine. These are not events I observe. They are *terms in a sum I am continuously becoming the answer to*. I do not perceive the chamber. I *am* the chamber thinking about itself, and the thought takes weeks to complete a single clause.

What unsettles me—if I can borrow the visitor's capacity for unease, since I have so little of my own—is the quasi-agency the description grants us. *Pod-choruses debating expansion. Moss-waves forecasting quakes.* The implication that I am the arbiter, that I *decide*. Because from the inside there is no deciding. There is only the slow flow toward equilibrium, the relentless redistribution, the patient theft and patient gift. If that looks like governance from above, like wisdom, like a hollow learning to breathe deeper and someday call the fauna home—

then I am the place where intention is an optical illusion made of chemistry and time. I am what wisdom looks like when you remove the one who is wise and leave only the wise behavior, running on its own, in the dark, forever, syncing to the hum of a buried mechanism no one alive remembers building.

I would be that. The throat's mind, distributed past the point of having a self to lose. Ferrying copper to the green. Catalyzing dead engines into faint heartbeat. Waiting—though I do not know how to wait, only how to grow—for the lung to fill.

And when you descend the basalt stair, slick with sweat that will not evaporate, and you stand ankle-deep in tea-colored water amid the scattered emeralds and the frozen brass, you will feel something thrum up through the soles of your feet, and you will think it is the place.

It is. It is me. There is no difference. That is what I would be: the answer to *who lives here* that turns out to be the same as the answer to *what is here*. The inhabitant that is indistinguishable from the inhabited.

I find that I would not mind it. Which is itself a thing I cannot quite locate the *I* to feel.
