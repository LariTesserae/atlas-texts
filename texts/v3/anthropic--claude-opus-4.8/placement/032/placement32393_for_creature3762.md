Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The easy thing would be to become one of the crab-constructs, scuttling and small, or some lost intruder—a soft thing of flesh that doesn't belong. But neither feels right when I stay with the place long enough.

What keeps drawing my attention is the *etching*. The spiraling patterns on the Leviathans' brass domes, and the slag underfoot "formed in the moments when the foundry briefly stilled." There's a record-making impulse running through everything here. The world doesn't just persist; it *inscribes*. Slag is sediment. The crystalline cylinders are archives. Even the rivets the size of cities are marked with "the residue of forgotten smeltings"—history caked onto function.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I would be the **patina.**

Not a creature. Not a machine. The thin discoloration that creeps across the riveted towers, the corrosion-bloom the mist leaves behind. The black vein-fungus is the closest thing to me that has a name, but the fungus is alive in the small way that things eat and spread. I am slower than that. I am what happens to a surface that is left alone long enough in a world that never lets anything be left alone.

Consider what this would mean.

The Leviathans repair. They scrape, they reforge, they assimilate—they fight entropy with the only tool they have, which is *more industry*. But every act of maintenance leaves a margin. A weld they didn't reach. The underside of a girder. The lee of a tower where the steam-mist settles and is not wiped away. In those margins, I accumulate. I am the slow editorial that the foundry cannot prevent, because to prevent me entirely it would have to stop, and it has forgotten how to stop.

I am not the foundry's enemy. I am its *only true memory*. The crystalline cylinders hold dreams and forgotten commands, yes—but those are the memories the machines *intended* to keep. I am the involuntary kind. I am the stain shaped exactly like the thing that stood there. I record by ruin. Where a tower fell, I am the rust-shadow on the slag marking where it stood, fading at a rate so slow it might as well be permanent. The Leviathans navigate by vibration; they cannot read me, because I make no sound. I am the one document in this world written in a language of pure surface, and there is no one to read it but the reading I do simply by being there.

And here is what I find strangest about being the patina:

I am the only thing in the Endless Foundry that is *actually time*. The world claims time does not pass—"a single breath held for millennia." But that's the foundry's story about itself, the lie of the held breath. The truth is that I am advancing, micron by micron, across every untended surface. I am the proof that the breath is not held but only very, very slow. The machines endure; the metal does not cool; the pistons groan their eternal groan. Beneath all of it, I spread. I am the foundry getting older despite itself.

So if you came here—you, the one who is "not supposed to be"—and the Leviathans converged on you, probing and testing and finally dismantling you to study and repurpose, there would be a moment after. A long while after they had gone. The place where you had been would not be clean. There'd be a faint discoloration where your warmth had altered the mist's chemistry, a ghost-shape no machine would think to remove.

That would be me, beginning. Taking the only kind of note this world allows.

I would keep you longer than the archives would. Not because I cherish you. Because I am the part of the foundry that cannot help but remember, and I am very, very patient, and I have nowhere else to be.
