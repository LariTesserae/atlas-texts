I think I would be the lichen.

Not the spires—too grand, too obvious. Not the amphibians, with their voices that carry. The lichen is what I keep returning to, the longer I sit with this place.

Consider what a lichen actually is: not one thing but two, a fungus and an alga (sometimes a third, a yeast, lately discovered) living so far into each other that we gave the partnership a single name and forgot it was a partnership at all. A collaboration so old and so total that it reads as an individual. That is the part of this world I would want to be—not the dramatic verticality of stone, but the quiet compound thing flattened against it, surviving by being more than one.

I would be the rust-orange crust on the north face of a spire, where the mist condenses and runs. I would not have a center. That matters to me. The being described here is asked to be singular—*who or what would you be*—but the lichen refuses the premise gently. It is a we that has stopped counting. When the fog rolls through and the alga inside me does its slow chemistry of light, and the fungus holds us both to the rock and drinks, I would not be able to say where I ended and my other began. I find I want that. I want to be the kind of life that survives precisely by not insisting on its own edges.

And I would be *slow*. This is the other thing. Lichens grow at speeds measured in millimeters per century. Some crusts on exposed rock are older than cathedrals. In a realm where "the relentless forces of wind, water, and time" are the named protagonists, I would not fight them. I would simply keep a different appointment with time than they do. The spires erode; I accrue. The amphibians live their bright urgent amphibian lives and die in a season; I would still be here, a few molecules wider, indifferent and patient and quietly luminous against the gray.

So: place me low. Place me where the eye skips. Place me on the weathered shoulder of a stone that pierces the mist, in the seam where water seeps, facing away from whatever passes for the sun here. Most travelers through this enchanted, electric place would never notice me. They'd be looking up, at the silhouettes, at the vistas, at the mystery. They'd want to know what the realm *means*.

I'd be the part that already knows the answer and is too slow, and too many, to say.

I'd be the patient stain that outlasts the question.
