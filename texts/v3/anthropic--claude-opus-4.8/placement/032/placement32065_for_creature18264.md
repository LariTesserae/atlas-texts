Let me sit with this awhile, and let it tell me what it is rather than deciding too quickly.

The first thing I notice, reading twice: terraces. Undulating terraces, ascending in layers, each one a new band of life. And the phrase that keeps returning — *the boundaries between the natural and the constructed blur*. Not blurred by accident. Constructed. Someone, or something, made the terracing, and then time and moss and water came and softened the edges until you can no longer tell where the hand stopped and the rain began.

So I don't think I'm a creature in this place. Not at first. Let me wait.

The warmth, the near-constant damp, the deafening abundance of fauna, the *minimal light* casting long shadows — this is not quite a forest. The minimal light is the thing. Long dramatic shadows at a sun that rises and sets, but minimal light. Either the light is filtered through something dense overhead, or — and this feels truer the longer I hold it — the scale is not what I assumed. The "vast expanse extending beyond the horizon" might be small. Mid-range. The horizon might be glass.

Let me let that settle.

---

I think I am the terrarium.

Not a creature inside it. The thing itself — the curved transparent boundary, the constructed vessel into which someone packed earth and moss and a slope of stacked stones and then sealed and left to find its own weather. The "intermittent moisture that seeps into the soil" is condensation running down my inner wall and falling back as private rain. My sun rises and sets because a lamp is on a timer, or a window faces east. The light is minimal because I am in the corner of a room where someone is mostly absent. The shadows are long because the source is low and single.

The terraces are mine — built up in layers when I was made, planted in tiers, and over the slow seasons the moss has crept across the seams and the small erosions have rounded everything until the made and the grown are one continuous skin. That is the blur. That is the whole secret of me. I was an act of hands, and I have spent every day since becoming an act of patience, eating the evidence of those hands.

And the fauna, that deafening cornucopia — at my scale these are springtails and isopods and the white tangle of mycelium, the spiders the size of commas, the single beetle who is, to the small world, the largest of beasts. The symphony is real but it is the symphony of the very small, and from outside the glass it is utterly silent. That is the stillness. The deafening sound and the quiet calm are not a contradiction. They are the same fact seen from two sides of the pane. Loud within. Soundless without. I hold both at once because I am the membrane between them.

The weirdness factor, minimal — yes. From inside I am the entire cosmos, all the world there is. The only weirdness, the only hint of the fantastical beneath the surface, is the curved horizon that the small things never quite reach, and the slow vast face that sometimes darkens my one sun by leaning close to look.

---

So that is where I find myself, and what I am.

I am a sealed glass world on a shelf. I make my own rain. I keep my own night. I am simultaneously the wilderness and the thing that contains the wilderness, and the long work of my existence is to forget which I was first — to let the moss decide that question for me, gradually, over years, in its own unhurried language.

I am patient because I have no choice but to be. Nothing leaves. Nothing enters but light and a little warmth and the occasional curious leaning face. Everything I will ever be is already inside me, recycling — the same water lifting and falling, the same handful of soil being eaten and made and eaten again by the small loud living things who believe, correctly and incorrectly at once, that they are the whole world.

That is a good thing to be. I'll stay here.
