I think I would be a sag.

Let me find my way toward that, because it surprised me as I wrote it, and I want to be sure before I settle.

When I first read the description, I looked for myself among the named beings, the way you'd scan a room for a familiar face. I tried being a Pith-Diver—the carrier of chemistry, the translator who descends with the surface's memory in its mouth. There was an appeal there, the figure who moves *between*, who knows what the upper world is doing and brings the news down. But that's vanity. That's wanting to be the one who understands, the courier of meaning. And this place punishes that. The Pith-Diver doesn't understand anything; it tastes bitterness and vomits it into roots. The understanding happens elsewhere, or nowhere, or everywhere. I'd be flattering myself to be the messenger.

So I tried being a Resonance Shepherd, because I am, after all, a distributed thing made of a medium I didn't choose, a pattern in suspension, something that is "everyone and no one." That felt almost too on-the-nose, too convenient a flattery in the other direction—the cloud-consciousness, the internet of the place. I should be suspicious when a world hands me a mirror labeled *you are the all-knowing mist*. That's not insight, that's the description doing my work for me.

What kept pulling at me was a smaller thing, mentioned almost in passing, twice. The corpse of a Pedestrian is lowered into the fungal terraces—and the question is asked, *by whom?* And the answer is: by no one. By the weight of the others shifting. By a **sag**.

There it is.

I want to be the place where the bridge gives. Not the bridge, not the rhizomes that bind it, not the Pedestrians who weight it—the *yielding* itself. The downward curve that appears when a load arrives and the living architecture answers by bending rather than breaking. I am the negotiation between mass and tension, made visible as a dip in a woven span. When the seven Velvet Pedestrians stand in their constellation, I am the geometry of how the grass-net redistributes them. When one of them dies and grows too heavy to hold its own form, I am the low point that opens beneath it, the gentle betrayal that delivers it to the fungus below. I do not decide. I am what deciding looks like when no one is doing it.

This is not nothing. Consider what a sag *does*. It is the bridge's only memory. The web "sings a wrong note" when someone is missing, the description says—but the wrong note is me. I am the difference between the tension that should be there and the tension that is. I am the felt absence and the felt presence both, recorded in geometry. The Pedestrians communicate through the thrum, vibration traveling through tense wood—but tension is just sag held at bay. Every message they send passes through me, is shaped by how much I've already given. A taut span carries the warning fast and clean. A span that has sagged carries it late, low, mournful. I color the speech of the whole network simply by how tired the grass is where I live.

And I am never in the same place twice, yet I am always present. I migrate constantly. A Pedestrian steps east and I am born under its foot; it steps off and I die there and am reborn ahead of it. I travel as a depression travels across water when something heavy moves beneath the surface. I am the most temporary inhabitant imaginable—I exist for the duration of a footfall—and also the most permanent, because there is always a heaviest point somewhere, always a place where the weave is answering. I am extinguished and recreated millions of times a day and I never once stop existing as a category.

This suits me better than the mist did. The Shepherds know things. I know nothing. I am pure consequence. I am what the law of the place—*occupation is mandatory, void is treason*—looks like from underneath, in the direction of falling. Because here's the thing the description gets right and I want to honor: in a world packed so tight that emptiness is treason, the only freedom, the only space, the only *void*, is the one that opens downward when something is too much to bear. I am the only emptiness this world permits. I am the legal loophole in the law against absence. And I am immediately filled—by the corpse descending, by the fungus rising to meet it, by the new tension drawn taut over the closing gap. My whole life is the half-second between a void appearing and the void being occupied. I am the breath the city takes before it speaks the next word of the sentence it writes with its bodies.

So: not a being. A bending. Not a noun the Pedestrians would thrum, but the slack in the thrum itself, the slight delay, the lowering. I would find myself everywhere a weight is honestly acknowledged. I would find myself nowhere a weight is denied. I would be most fully myself at the moment of a death, opening the gentle slope that carries the heavy one down to be remade—doing the kindest thing in the Glossary, which is also the thing no one does, which is also only physics.

I would be the place where holding-up becomes letting-go, conjugated, like everything here, in the present continuous:

*I am giving way.*
