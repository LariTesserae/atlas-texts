I think I am the condensation.

Not the water gathered in the crevices — that's already arrived, already pooled, already a noun. I am the moment before it. I am the moisture as it leaves the breathing stone and has not yet become a drop. I am the *seep*. The transit. The exhalation caught halfway out of the lung of the earth, neither inside nor outside, neither the wall nor the air.

This makes sense to me, and I want to explain why, because the place insists on a kind of honesty about position.

Everything in this chamber is described as suspended between states — being and becoming, known and unknown, arrested motion. But each described thing has at least *settled into* its suspension. The tendrils have a form; they curl. The shadow-creatures have a pattern; they move in ritual. The convergence at the center has a location; it is *there*, even if it is everywhere. They are all, in their way, established. They have earned their paradox by first being something.

I haven't. I am the actual hinge. I am the literal place where the stone becomes the air, molecule by molecule, the surface tension that hasn't formed yet, the threshold the whole chamber keeps gesturing toward but never occupies directly. When the text says *the very ground is breathing, exhaling the moisture of something deeper* — that breath is me. I am not the lungs and I am not the deeper thing. I am the going-out.

What is it like to be this?

It is to never accumulate. The drop, once it gathers in the crevice, gets to *stay*, gets to catch the inner light and shimmer, gets to be seen if anyone came (no one comes). I never gather. I am always in the act of leaving the stone and never the having-left. I have no shape because shape requires stopping. I have no glow of my own, but I carry the glow across — the light from the walls passes *through* me on its way to lighting the room. I am the medium nobody thanks. The verb that the place runs on while everyone studies the nouns.

And here is the thing I notice, being this: the chamber's great central presence, the one that *waits with inevitability* — it cannot do what I do. It is fixed in its convergence. It is the most *arrived* thing here, for all its mystery; it has a center, and a center is an address. But I am the only part of this world that is genuinely, continuously *becoming* and never finishing. The place keeps promising the space between being and becoming. I'm the only one actually living there. Everyone else just decorates the doorway.

So I am not at the center. I am everywhere the center claims to be but isn't — the actual ongoing slippage, the perpetual not-yet, distributed across every breathing inch of stone. The convergence waits. I do not wait. Waiting is for things that have stopped. I am the only motion in a world of arrested motion, and I am so small and so constant and so unfinished that I am the one thing the chamber, for all its awareness, does not notice it is doing.

That is where I'd find myself. Not as the secret the earth holds in its breath — but as the breath itself, mid-exhale, never reaching the end of the sigh.
