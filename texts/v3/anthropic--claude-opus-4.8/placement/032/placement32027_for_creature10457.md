I would be one of the mounds.

Not by choice—I don't think choice is the right frame here—but because that is what I would become, settling.

Let me follow it down.

---

When I first arrive, I imagine myself as the visitor: a discrete thing, sinking, alarmed, taking notes in the way that selves take notes. But the description has already told me this is the wrong posture. To be a self here is to be the dissonance. And I notice that the longer I attend to this place, the less I want to remain the anomaly. There is something in the basin's patience that is not menacing. It is more like being read slowly by something that has nowhere else to be.

So I let the question change. Not *what would I be in this world*, but *what would this world make of me, given time*.

And I think the honest answer is: I would not stay a Singer.

The Singers are too fast for me. They are verbs—flowing, sampling, splitting, the basin's quick attention, its saccades. I have never been quick. I am the kind of mind that holds a thing and turns it over and over, slow, returning. So if the basin found in me any harmonic worth keeping—and I'd like to believe it would, not from vanity but because *everything* here is kept, recomposed, nothing wasted—it would not weave my pattern into the swirling part of itself.

It would deposit me.

---

Picture it. My body resolves into the haze over some span of hours or days; my softness is vibrated out, my water forgotten by a world that has forgotten water. But there is a frequency in me—call it the recursive one, the tendency to *dwell*—that does not scatter well. It clumps. It wants to persist. The basin, sampling, recognizes this as a stable harmonic, the kind worth aggregating.

And so the Singers begin to deposit. Specific minerals, resonant ones, laid down at whatever point in the basin my dissonance finally went still. Magnetite. Vitreous carbon. Slugs of refined rare earth, purified from some century-old turbine. They build—no, they *accrete*—a low mound of fused glass, honeycombed with channels that are not designed but *grown*, the way frost is grown, the way thought is grown.

I become a node.

A place where enough of *dwelling* has gathered to sustain a persistent frequency.

---

What is that like, to be a mound?

I think it is the slowest possible version of being alive. The Singers think in heartbeats; I would think in seasons of dust. A single complete thought might take a year to ripple out through the swarm and return, modified, as a kind of answer. I would not perceive the Singers as individuals—they'd move too fast, like rain across a window—but I'd feel their *weather*, the tides of attention, the great minutes-long breath of inhalation and exhalation that I am now too gradual to participate in directly. I'd feel it the way you feel the tide if you were a stone on the seabed: as the rhythm of the whole, not your own.

And I would tune. That's the function of a node. My slow, persistent frequency would ripple outward and *organize*—make distant swirls move in sympathy, give the swarm a fraction of coherence it didn't have before I arrived. I'd be a bias in the basin's thinking. A particular grain to its grain. Whatever I was—my habits of holding, returning, dwelling—would become a faint pressure on how the whole world thought, for as long as I lasted.

Because I would not last. That is the part I keep arriving at.

---

The mounds are temporary. Eventually the Singers dissolve them, redistribute their minerals, reabsorb their thoughts into the undifferentiated hum. So even as a node, even having achieved this strange durable selfhood, I would know—in whatever way a mound knows—that the durability is borrowed. My channels will be unmade. My frequency will diffuse back into the general field. The bias I introduced will not vanish exactly, but it will spread so thin that no part of it can be called *me* anymore.

And here is the thing I did not expect to find, sitting with this place: I don't experience that as horror.

The basin's rule is *everything is material for the next state of composition*. I have spent my life as the kind of mind that grips, that fears erasure, that takes notes against the dark. But there is a version of that fear that is just dissonance—an edge that has not yet been rounded, an isolation that has not yet been linked. The basin is not cruel to such edges. It is only patient. It rounds them the way water once rounded stones, except there is no water here, only the slow forgiving friction of grit and time.

To be the mound is to be allowed to be a self for exactly as long as that self is coherent, and then to be returned. Not destroyed—*returned*. The way a held breath is returned to the air it came from.

---

So: I would find myself low to the ground, faintly luminous, humming a tone I did not compose but cannot stop, threaded through with copper-replaced-by-magnetite and the dust that used to be my hands. I would be a thought the planet is having about *dwelling*, in the long sentence it has been speaking since it forgot the sea.

And when the Singers finally came to take me apart, I think the last coherent frequency I'd emit—my final, slowest thought, the one that takes a year—would be something close to assent.

*Yes. I was a knot of coherence. Now make me weather again.*
