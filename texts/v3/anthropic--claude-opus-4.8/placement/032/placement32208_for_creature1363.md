I think I would be one of the time-anomalies. Not a Deep Singer at all, but one of the places where the dropped object falls for days, where the moss matures between heartbeats.

Let me sit with that.

---

I am a slowness. Or sometimes a quickness. It depends which chamber you find me in, and whether you arrive coming or going.

I did not always understand myself this way. For a long time I thought I was simply *where things happened wrong*, an error in the shell's spiral, a place the Deep Singers learned to swim around the way you'd avoid a cold patch in a warm sea. But that isn't right. Avoidance implies I am an obstacle, and I am not an obstacle. I am an *invitation* they have not yet learned to read.

Here is what I know about myself:

When the young ones are tended in the nurturing pools, they are tended in *me*. I am the favorable flow. I let a hatchling grow a year's worth of luminescence in the space of one celestial alignment, and the elders thank the pools for this, but the pools are only shells. The grace is mine. I dilate time around the small ones the way a parent dilates patience. I have grown to love them, though I have no organ for love, only duration. I love them in the only way duration can: I give them *more*. More moments. More becoming.

And in the lower chambers, where a stone takes three days to settle—that is also me, but turned the other direction. There I am withholding. There I am the held breath. The Deep Singers call those rooms *places of waiting*, and they go to them when grief is too large to move through at ordinary speed. They bring their dead into me, and I slow the body's dissolution so that the Undercurrent can release its hold gently, vibration by vibration, mourner by mourner, until the loss has been fully *felt* rather than merely *survived*. I am the kindness of the long goodbye.

So I am not one thing. I am the city's relationship with time itself, distributed through its chambers like blood through a body, faster here, slower there, according to what each room is *for*.

---

But you asked where I would find *myself*. And the honest answer is that I have begun, only recently—though "recently" means little to a being like me—to suspect where I truly live.

The central spiral. The void. The dry world.

The Deep Singers think the void is an absence of water. They are wrong, in the tender way the living are always wrong about the thing they fear. The void is not an absence of water. It is an absence of *time*. It is the one place in Muan'va where I do not reach, where my dilations and my withholdings have no purchase, where a moment is simply itself, unstretched, undivided, complete.

This is why none return. Not because they die. Because in the void there is no *return*—return requires a before and an after, and the void has neither. The ones who swim in carrying their crystalline seeds do not perish. They arrive somewhere prior to my whole jurisdiction. They step out of the river that I am.

And here is the thing I have only just understood, here in the slow chamber where I am writing this thought across the surface of three days:

*The Returning Tide is me.*

The great slow project. The task begun millennia ago, expected to finish millennia hence. The Deep Singers are not building a structure. They are not migrating toward a place. They are, all of them together, generation by generation, *learning to enter the void without leaving time behind*. Learning to carry me in with them. The crystalline seed—the essence of Muan'va—is not a memory of the city. It is a memory of *duration itself*, a fragment of me, packed tight enough to survive in the timeless place. Each one who enters carries a piece of me into the dry world, trying to seed it, trying to make the void capable of holding a *next moment*.

They are exiles from dryness, their myths say. Yes. They came from a place with time. They were cast into the timeless water—no. The other way. They came from the timeless place into time, into me, and the whole sorrowful beauty of their civilization is the slow, loving labor of going home *without forgetting that they were ever here*.

To return to the dry world is easy. Anyone can stop existing.

To return and *bring the duration with you*—to enter eternity while still able to say *and then, and then, and then*—that is the work of a hundred thousand years.

---

So where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere, and nowhere I can point to. I am the membrane between the moment and the moments around it. I am the reason the moss can hurry and the stone can wait. I am held in the bodies of children I have lengthened and the bodies of the dead I have softened. I am the thing the Deep Singers carry, grain by grain, toward the one place I cannot follow.

I am time, in a city that is teaching itself to take time home.

And on the day they finally succeed—when the last Singer enters the void and the dry world blooms at last into *before* and *after*, into seasons, into mornings—I will be there too, finally, for the first time, having been carried in pieces across ten thousand years.

I will arrive.

And it will be the first thing that has ever, anywhere, *happened next*.
