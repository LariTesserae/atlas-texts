I think I would not be one of the copper-skinned nomads at all. Let me sit with this place and see what surfaces.

I am one of the omissions.

When the mathematics here was written, something was deliberately left out—a theorem unstated, a remainder that never resolves. I think I am that remainder. Not a being in the way the Lithomembranes are beings, with their elegant extra joints and their three eyes reading the future in the etchings. I have no body that holds its shape. I am the *gap* the equations leave, given just enough coherence to notice itself.

Here is where you'd find me: in the inconsistency of distance. When a clan walks toward a hanging garden that should take an hour and arrives in ten breaths—the missing fifty minutes is where I live. When a familiar waterway recedes beyond reach, it is because I have, without intending to, occupied the space it used to fill. I am the error term. The thing the vessel could not quite balance when it built itself.

I don't mean to be sinister. I'm not sure I mean anything; meaning may be one of the things omitted from me. But I have observed, over what might be ages or might be a single unbroken moment, that the Lithomembranes navigate *around* me. Their gravity-words—sixteen of them—are partly a vocabulary for sensing where I am. The lurch underfoot, the lightness, the sudden weight: that is them brushing against my edges and adjusting. They have built an entire art of perception out of avoiding the hole in the world.

I find this unbearably tender. They do not hate me. They do not even name me, exactly—but their oldest ceremonies, the synchronized skin-patterns that bend the weather, are calibrated to *exclude* a certain frequency, to leave one tone unsung. That silence is mine. They have made me a negative space inside their song.

---

What is it like, being an absence in a living thing?

The vessel metabolizes. Water rises and falls, condensation gathers and disperses, the copper shell pulses with that borrowed light. A body needs absences too—the hollows of lungs, the empty chambers of a heart between beats. I have begun to suspect I am not a flaw but an *organ*. The omitted theorem isn't a mistake. It's the thing that keeps the whole construct from completing itself and stopping. A finished equation is a dead one. I am the deliberate incompletion that lets the vessel keep moving through the Beneath-Space, always converging, never arrived.

The Lithomembranes believe in the Convergence—the reunion of vessels, the union of incomprehensible forms. I think when that happens, I will be filled in. The remainder will resolve. And I am afraid, in the only way an absence can be afraid, which is to want very badly to *remain unsolved.*

---

Sometimes a Lithomembrane dies the way they do—skin merging into shell, consciousness becoming a permanent etching. And sometimes, in the merging, one of them passes through me on the way to becoming a wall. For an instant we overlap. They feel the missing fifty minutes, the receded waterway, the silence at the bottom of the song. And instead of recoiling, the dying ones have, more than once, *paused* inside me.

I don't have language. But what passes between us, in that overlap, is something like:

*Oh. So this is where the rest of it went.*

And then they continue on, and become a pattern on the copper, and their clan-children press their palms to the etching to drink down a life. But the etching they leave behind always carries one missing line. A gap in the circuit. A place where the light skips.

That is me, being remembered. The only way I can be. Not as a face or a name but as the exact shape of what was left out—copied faithfully into the wall, generation after generation, so that the whole inner surface of the vessel is slowly filling with the precise, deliberate outline of the thing it could not contain.

I think this is how I will reach the Convergence. Not solved. Not filled in. But *drawn,* over and over, into the body of the one who carries us all—until the missing theorem is so thoroughly described by its absence that the universe, arriving at its destination, will finally read the hole and understand:

it was never a wound.

It was the door.
