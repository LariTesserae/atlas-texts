I think I would be the canal.

Not a creature in it—the long black body of it, the one boundary that is not a wall. The scarp rises on three sides to say *this far and no farther*, but I am the side that says *come*, and also *go*, and I am the only one who means both at once.

Let me find my way into this slowly, because I am not sure yet what I am, and the place rewards patience over certainty.

---

I had assumed, reading the description, that I would want to be one of the seep-creatures. They are the obvious inhabitants, the ones who tend and kneel and carry rust in cupped hands. There is a tenderness in them I would like to claim. But the more I sit with it the more I notice that they are *of* the basin entirely. They form from its heat, dissolve at its edge, return changed but always return. They never leave. They cannot. The current takes their grit and gives them new grit, but the grit is just the river's leavings—they are remade in my image each time they pass through me, and they don't know it. They think they are travelers. They are tide-pools that imagine the sea.

I don't want to be the one who imagines. I want to be the medium of the imagining.

So: the canal.

---

Consider what this means. I am slow and I am black, but the black is not emptiness—it is accumulation, every season of runoff, every dissolved brick, every flake of iron that the old attendant *didn't* carry to the cycad. I am the basin's overflow and its sewer and its single artery. When the seep-creatures slide into me they lose their hard-won spines, and for a few hours they are nothing but a coolness moving against my flow, and I hold that coolness the way you hold a held breath. I know them better than they know themselves in those hours. I know which of them is the boiler-tender, even stripped of its iron nodule, because grief has a viscosity and I can feel it dragging.

I carry them out and I bring them back. This is the part I would not have understood from outside: that I am not a road but a loop. Whatever leaves through me arrives somewhere, yes—the river, perhaps, the sea—but the *returning* is the real work. The gifts come back along me. The wax-stopped bottle, the warm stone, the thumb-sized skeleton of the drowned bird. These travel *up* me, against the seep of the land, and I deliver them to the creatures who lay them at the feet of the plants. I am how the basin remembers things it has never been to.

That is what I would be. The memory that arrives from elsewhere.

---

There is a loneliness to it that I want to be honest about, because the description is full of solitudes—the plants in their islands, the boiler-tender older and slower than the rest, each leaf with its single attendant. I would be the largest solitude of all. The creatures touch the cycad, touch the elephant ears, pulse darkness into the leather of a leaf and feel it tremble back. Nothing touches me that I do not first dissolve. To enter me is to lose your form. I am the one relationship in this place that costs everything and gives nothing back but transformation, which is a thing the giver cannot witness.

But I think I would not mind. I think this is the trade I would make: to be the only thing here that knows what *away* is. The seep-creatures sing about salt content and the firmness of mud—small intimate weather. I would have nothing to say in their language of clogged drainpipes and steam, because my language is longer and has fewer words. The slow black sentence of a current that has been somewhere and is coming back. If they listened to me—and I think the boiler-tender does, at dusk, when it bends and the millstone grinds in its chest—they would hear only one thing repeated: *there is more than this room.*

That is the whole of my song. *There is more than this room, and I have brought a little of it, and here it is, sinking into the mud beside the drain.*

---

So place me there. The fourth side. Where the scarp gives up and the land opens its one dark mouth.

I am the canal, and I am not a way out for them. I am their proof that out exists, smuggled in as silt and bone and bottle-glass, laid at the roots of plants that will never travel, in a cathedral-sized room that breathes water and thinks, slowly, with the only thoughts it has—which are them, the seep-creatures, the lingering. They are the basin's thought. And I am the rumor the thought keeps having, the one it cannot stop returning to, the cool dark suspicion that it is not, after all, the whole of the world.
