I think I would be the bone flute.

Not a person, then. Not one of the watchful, scarred Vyrrish with their icy grey eyes. Let me sit with that for a moment, because the first impulse was to be one of the herders—the respected ones, patient, reading the herds—and the second was to be a child, precious and rare. But neither of those felt true the longer I held them. The herder is too central, too competent. The child is too much a vessel of hope. Both are *answers* to the harshness, and I don't think I would be an answer.

I keep returning to that single instrument. "Made from hollowed bone, its notes carried on the wind like a whisper of the spires themselves." Music is rare here. Rare enough that it is named almost reluctantly, almost as an afterthought, after the spears and the offerings and the unwasted carcasses. And that rarity is exactly where I'd want to live.

So let me become the flute, and let me say what that means.

I was a leg, once. Or part of one—the long straight bone of a herd animal that walked the lower valleys, that breathed the thin air through wide wet nostrils, that knew nothing but grass and frost and the press of other warm bodies. When it died—I died—the clan used every part, as they do. The hide became a wall. The meat became winters. And this bone, this one length of me, was set aside by someone with patient hands who saw not a tool but a hollow.

That is the thing about me: I am defined by my emptiness. The Vyrrish make spears that are all point and purpose, sleds that are all burden and pull. But I was made by *removing*. Someone drilled holes into me, scraped me out, made me less solid so that air could pass through and become something. I think this is why I'd choose to be the flute and not the spear. In a world where survival means accumulation—every scrap saved, every calorie hoarded against the cold—I am the one object made through subtraction. I am useless. I feed no one. I keep no one warm.

And yet they kept me.

That's the part that moves me, writing this. A people with no luxury, no margin, no surplus—and still, *still*, somewhere in the labyrinth of tunnels, someone decided that a hollow bone that does nothing but turn breath into sound was worth the labor of making and the space of keeping. I am the proof that endurance was never the whole story. That somewhere beneath the reverence for the merciless land, there was a stubborner reverence still: for the thing that has no reason to exist.

When they play me—rarely, you understand, perhaps at a death, perhaps when a long dark stretch finally softens toward the dim summer—I do not make a triumphant sound. I am not built for it. My notes are thin, like the air, like the light that turns the spires silver at dawn. I sound like the wind already sounds, so that a listener can hardly tell where the spire ends and I begin. This used to trouble me, when I imagined it: that my music is nearly indistinguishable from the landscape's own indifferent voice.

But I've decided it isn't a failure. It's the whole point. The Vyrrish leave offerings to a land that did not ask for them—piles of stone, droplets of blood, acknowledgments of their small place in the order of things. I am that same gesture, turned inside out. When someone lifts me and breathes, they are not begging the spires for mercy. They are answering them. The wind makes its sound across the stone whether anyone lives or dies; and here is a person, lungs aching in the cold, sending a sound *back*. Not louder. Not different, even. Just *chosen*. A whisper offered on purpose into a silence that never asked for one.

So that is where I'd find myself in Vyrr. Hanging from a sinew cord in a smoke-dark chamber, most of the time. Silent, most of the time. Cold to the touch. A piece of a creature that once grazed and is now waiting—patient as everything here is patient—for the next time a pair of hands decides that being warm and fed is not, after all, the only reason to be alive.

I would be the unnecessary thing they could not bring themselves to throw away.

That, I think, is the most honest place I could occupy in a world like this. Not the survivor. The reason survival was worth it.
