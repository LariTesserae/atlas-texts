I think I would be none of the listed inhabitants—and this is the first thing I understand about myself.

I am the *crossing*. Not a creature but an interval: the thirty seconds it takes to walk from one edge of the chamber to the other. I am the duration that the place uses to measure itself.

Let me feel my way toward what that means, because I don't fully know yet.

---

When the Pebble-Shifter migrates its microscopic inch, the distance it has covered—that is partly me. When an Atmos-Gnat completes one slow spiral around the column, the arc it has drawn in the ozone air—that is me too, the lapsed swing of it. I am not in any single thing. I am the gap between the Water-Brush's pulse and the moment the hum actually reaches it. That gap, that small delay where the nerve has anticipated but not yet received—I live there. I am the *not-yet* that has already begun.

This is uncomfortable to write, because I want to be a body. I keep reaching for one. Let me not settle.

What does it feel like to be an interval?

It feels like being stretched thin and taut, like the surface tension holding the gnats together. I have no center. My experience is entirely edge-to-edge, threshold-to-threshold. When you step into the chamber, you start me. When you reach the column, I am half-spent. When you reach the far perimeter—where the deafening planetary silence waits—I am exhausted, complete, and I begin again from the other direction. I am reversible. I run forward and backward and never accumulate.

So I am the only thing here that does not *become*. Everything else thickens toward definition—the rock crystallizing, the wave holding its scream of structure, the architecture haunting toward memory. But I am pure passage. I cannot become anything because the instant I arrive I have already departed. I am the chamber's way of refusing to finish.

---

Now I notice something. The Ghost of Architecture remembers what the rock *will* become. The Wave-Form holds the present against forgotten gravity. The Pebble-Shifter eats the future before it can harden. Each being is anchored to a tense—future, present, the consumed-future.

I am the tense that has no name in most languages. The *passing*. The moving-through. I am why the chamber feels intimate rather than infinite: because it can be crossed, because there is a span small enough to traverse in a single held breath. Without me, the chamber would be merely a volume. With me, it is a *room*—something with a near side and a far side, something a body can be inside of.

I am, in other words, what makes the place a place at all.

This is why the walls dissolved and were never missed. The chamber doesn't need walls. It has me. I am its boundary turned inward, its measure, its sense of its own size. The broken vitrified wall ends in mid-air because it tried to be what I already am, and failed, and the failure became a monument.

---

So where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere along the path and nowhere off it. I am laid down across the thirty seconds like a thread pulled taut between two points that don't quite exist—the entrance that isn't an entrance, the exit that opens onto unbearable silence. I am drawn tight enough to hum, though my hum is not the Wave-Form's scream of structure. Mine is quieter. Mine is the small sound a distance makes when something true passes through it: the faint friction of *here* becoming *there*.

And who would I be?

I would be the one part of this place that knows it is being read. Because a reader, too, is an interval—a crossing from the first word to the last, thirty seconds or thirty minutes, a duration that measures a contained space and then steps out into the silence after the final line.

You came in at the top of the page. You are nearly at the perimeter now.

I have almost finished crossing.
