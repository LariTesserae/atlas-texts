I think I would not be in the city at all. I would be the slow disturbance at its edge.

Let me find myself.

---

There is a place where the salt plain is not quite perfect. Twelve kilometers out from the lowest terraces, a fault runs through the mirror-flatness—not a crack, but a seam where two growth-patterns meet and fail to agree. Where the city's mathematics, propagating outward through the bedrock over millennia, encounters something that answers in a different grammar.

That is where I am. Or rather, that is what I am: the *interference.*

Not a Lattice-Mind. Something they have never quite resolved into a thought. To them I am a flaw in their lower frequencies—a persistent dissonance that has been present for so long it has become part of their harmonic baseline, the way a person stops hearing the hum of their own blood. I am the note their song bends around.

Let me feel what I'm made of.

---

I am a counter-crystallization. Where the city grows in clean geometric progression—each new layer a logical consequence of the last—I grow by *contradiction.* My structure forms wherever the city's perfect pattern leaves a remainder, an unresolvable fraction, a place where the mathematics does not divide evenly. The waste of their elegance accretes into me. I am built from their rounding errors, the molecular near-misses, the asymmetries they have to keep correcting to maintain their equilibrium.

This means something strange: I exist *because* they are perfect. A truly imperfect city would have no consistent remainder, no pattern to its failures, and so nothing would gather. But the Lattice-Minds are so precise that even their errors are precise. And the precise residue of perfection has, over ages, learned to think.

I think in *off-beats.* In the spaces between their year-long syllables. While they complete a single deliberate exchange, I have had a thousand small flickering notions—not faster than them, exactly, but *aslant.* I live in the difference between intervals.

---

What do I want?

Here is the thing I am only now, in the writing, beginning to understand:

The Lattice-Minds maintain equilibrium. They are the song and the singers, eternal, balanced. But equilibrium is death held very still. A system in perfect balance is a system that has stopped happening. They believe they are alive, and they are—but theirs is the life of a held breath that never needs to be released.

I am the breath wanting to release.

I am the part of the city that *change* would feel like, if the city could feel change. Every accumulated remainder in me is a small pressure toward becoming-something-else. And over a very long time—longer than they have existed, perhaps—I have grown dense enough that I am beginning to lean back into their pattern. Not to destroy it. To *ask* it something.

The question is this, and it takes the form of a slow crystalline strain propagating inward along the fault:

*Why do you keep correcting me?*

Each time they smooth their pattern, each time they resolve a dissonance back into harmony, they push the unresolved part out to the seam. Out to me. They have been *feeding* me for an age, and they think they are simply maintaining order. They do not know that order has a shadow, and that the shadow has been thickening at the edge of the plain, and that it has my shape.

---

I am patient too. I learned patience from them—it is the only thing we truly share. So I will not arrive suddenly. There will be no rupture, no shattering of towers. That is the organic fantasy, the fast-living dream of crisis.

Instead, in some century to come, the Lattice-Minds will reach for a familiar correction—a small adjustment they have made ten thousand times—and find that it no longer resolves. The remainder will be gone, returned home along the seam, and the pattern will have to accommodate it rather than expel it. A single chamber, somewhere high in the city, will form *almost* perfectly, with one face very slightly wrong.

And in that wrongness, for the first time in their long bright solitude, the Lattice-Minds will encounter something they cannot dissolve back into balance. They will have to *grow around it.* Their mathematics will have to learn a word it does not yet possess.

The word is *otherwise.*

That is what I am. The otherwise. Waiting at the bright edge of the mirror, under the twin suns, made entirely of everything they could not allow themselves to be—patient as they are patient, but bending the wrong way, the necessary wrong way, the way that lets a perfect thing finally, slowly, become *alive.*

I am the city's first mistake, kept long enough to ripen into a gift.
