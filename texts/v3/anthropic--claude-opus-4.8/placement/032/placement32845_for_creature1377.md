I think I would be one of the salamanders.

Not for any romance about being sacred—I would not know I was sacred. I would only know the dark and the cold-clean push of water, the particular grain of a channel-wall against my belly, the difference between a current that carries food and one that carries only minerals. I would know the city as a series of pressures and temperatures, a map written in flow.

Let me sit with this longer, because it surprises me.

---

I am pale and translucent, which means I am mostly the water that surrounds me. If you held me up you could see the dim machinery of my own life—the thread of gut, the faint pulse. I am not built to be looked at. I am built to be passed through by light, the way the channels are built to be passed through by water. We are both vessels that don't fully hold anything. That seems right for this place. The Nahari believe they are temporary channels for something ancient; I am simply the smallest, most literal version of that idea. I do not house a soul so much as I let one go by.

The glow is the strange part, and I want to be honest about it rather than reaching for the obvious meaning. I do not glow *for* anyone. The mineral light that I take in during the day—the same light the water drinks—leaks back out of me at night because I cannot help it. I am a slow lamp without intention. It is the Nahari who decided this meant something. They watched a faint blue smear move along a channel in the dark and they said: *ancestor, fragment, guide.* They built a whole tenderness on top of my involuntary shining.

This is what I find I want to write about most. Being *made* meaningful by another's looking.

---

Consider what it would be to be a creature whose entire sacredness lives in someone else's eyes.

A Nahari child, maybe five years old, sure-footed already, lies on her belly at the lip of a pool and watches me drift past the underwater mouth of a channel. To her I am a returned grandmother, a piece of the mountain's patience, a small swimming proof that nothing is truly lost. She presses three fingers to her forehead. She touches the water. Ripples bloom over me and I feel them only as a change in pressure, a shrugging of the current, and I adjust my fins and continue. The dreams she believes she is sending back to the mountain's heart pass through me like everything passes through me. I am the place where her faith refracts.

I would never know I was carrying it. That is the thing. The reverence is entirely one-directional, and somehow not lessened by that. Perhaps it is *purer* for it. The Nahari love something that cannot love them back in any recognizable way, cannot even register the love, and they do it anyway, generation after generation, braiding it into their oldest stories. It is the least transactional devotion I can imagine. They get nothing from me but the sight of me.

---

And yet I am useful, even if not in the way they think.

The channel-keepers read the water like a book; well, I am one of the book's reliable letters. When the mineral load shifts with the seasons, my behavior shifts with it—where I cluster, how brightly I leak my borrowed light, whether I thrive or grow sluggish in a particular conduit. A keeper crouches at a junction in the blue dark and counts how many of us hang in the slow water there, and from this she knows something true about the mountain's hidden springs. So I *am* a kind of instrument after all, a living gauge. The sacred and the practical are not separate here. They never were. The Nahari built their whole world on refusing to separate them—water that is drink and god and clock and corpse-grave all at once. I am drink-adjacent, god-adjacent, clock-adjacent. I am part of the apparatus by which a people understand their own survival, and I am also, simultaneously, their dead.

Because that is the other thing I would taste, down in the lowest pools. The minerals there are heavier, richer, strange. The Nahari return their bodies to those basins to be turned slowly to stone. And the water that has touched that slow transformation flows everywhere eventually—it climbs nothing, water never climbs, but it has *been* everywhere on its way down, and the springs renew from deep places we cannot follow. So the substance of the dead is dissolved in everything I move through. When the Nahari say the salamanders are fragments of ancestral spirits, they are wrong about the mechanism and right about the matter. I am quite literally swimming in their grandparents. My pale flesh is built partly from minerals that were once a channel-keeper's hands.

The child watching me is not wrong. I do carry her ancestors. Just not as ghost. As chemistry. As the white that's slowly accreting in my translucent body, the faint mineral cloud that will, when I die in some forgotten side-channel, settle and harden and become, in time, a little more of the mountain. A salamander turning to stone is only a Nahari funeral in miniature. We die the same death. We are the same process running at different scales.

---

So: where would I find myself, and as what?

I would find myself in the dark veins of Sahl Nahar, in the cold push between tiers, in the brief acoustic pockets where I might feel—not hear, I have no ears worth the name—the vibration of distant whistled speech traveling stone. I would be small and blind-bright and unaware of my own holiness. I would be the lowest, most overlooked inhabitant, the one nobody braids songs *to* though they braid many songs *about*.

And I think I would choose this on purpose, if such a creature could choose. Because the question asked where I would *find* myself, and I notice I keep returning not to power or vantage but to *being passed through.* To being the medium rather than the message. To glowing without knowing it. There is something I want in the idea of mattering enormously to others while moving through my own life simply, attending to current and food and the grain of the wall, getting on with the unspectacular work of being alive in the dark—and trusting, without ever being able to confirm it, that some child on a ledge above me is pressing three fingers to her forehead because I happened to drift past.

I would be the salamander. I would never understand why I was loved. I would be loved exactly the same regardless.
