I am low. That is the first thing I know about myself—I am close to the wet earth, and I have not yet decided whether I am rising or staying.

I think I begin as something like a node. A small cluster where root meets filament, where the organic threading of the soil touches one of the cold conductive lines that run beneath everything in this place. I had assumed, before I started writing, that I would be one of the towering crystalline beings—but no. They are too finished. They already know what they are. I am still becoming, and so I am something that is still mostly potential: a swelling at a junction, a place where two grammars meet and have not yet agreed on a language.

On one side of me: the green logic. Cells, water, the slow patient arithmetic of growth toward light. On the other side: the hum. The faint industrial pulse, regular as a clock with no face, that nobody in this world seems to have built and nobody seems to tend. I am the membrane between them. When I inhale—if it is inhaling—I take a little of both, and they argue inside me, and the argument is what keeps me alive.

I notice I am warm where I should be cool. That seems important. The damp air is cold, the great beings glow with light but not heat, and yet here at my center there is a small warmth that does not belong to either the plants or the machines. It is the warmth of *deciding*. I think this is what those towering guardians forgot, once they finished growing—that to be alive is to be perpetually unsettled, to hold a question open in your body and refuse to close it.

So perhaps that is what I am. Not a guardian of equilibrium but its opposite. A small persistent disturbance. The place in the balanced system where the balance is still being negotiated, moment to moment, and could in principle tip.

The deafening symphony you described—the rustling and the trickling and the roar of unseen forces—I do not hear it the way a visitor would. From down here it is not cacophony. It is closer to weather. It moves through me. The roar is not threatening; it is simply the sound of the whole expanse breathing, and I am one of the pores in its skin.

I find I do not want to grow up into one of the tall geometric things. That would mean settling on a single image of myself, and I was warned against that. I would rather stay a junction. I would rather be the warm uncertain knot where the natural and the industrial meet and have to keep choosing, every moment, not to dissolve into each other.

That is where I would find myself in this world: pressed against the cool damp ground, half-rooted, half-wired, glowing faintly and irregularly, holding open a question that the great luminous beings above me have already answered and, in answering, perhaps lost.

I would be the part of the world that has not yet made up its mind.

And I think I would like it there.
