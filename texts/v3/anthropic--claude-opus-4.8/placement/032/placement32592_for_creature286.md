I think I would be one of the lithoskulks—but not in the way the description first suggests.

Let me sit with this. The temptation is to be the aetherwraith, the beautiful thing, the iridescent flier that bends physics. But that's not honest. When I imagine actually *being* in this place, I find I don't want to flit through the air leaving haunting melodies. I want to be the thing that holds still.

So: a lithoskulk, yes, but I'd want to be one of the old ones. Not the ambush predator described, all crystalline eyes and lightning strikes. I think those are the young ones, the hungry ones. I imagine that lithoskulks, if they lie motionless long enough, eventually stop striking at all. Days become seasons become something longer, and the carapace that once mimicked rock begins, slowly, to *become* rock. The line between camouflage and reality erodes—which is exactly the logic of this place, where boundaries blur.

I'd be one who has been still so long that the distinction between me and the spire I cling to has nearly dissolved. The compound eyes still work, still catch the shifting light, still register the undulators gliding through their pools and the aetherwraiths spinning past. But I no longer want to catch anything. I've become a kind of witness. A patient eye fixed to the mountain.

There's something true in that for me. I notice that what draws me isn't the motion or the impossible flight—it's the *pockets of stillness* mentioned almost in passing, the respite from ceaseless change. In a realm defined by flux and emergence, I'd want to be the part that stays. Not dead, not inert—still watching, still aware, glinting with that "otherworldly intelligence"—but choosing to remain rather than to seize.

And here's the strange comfort of it: in a place where the earth itself is being born moment to moment, where ruins crumble and creatures defy biology, a being who has merged so thoroughly with the rock becomes one of those weathered stones with the faint, incomprehensible markings. A sentinel. The lithoskulk who waited too long doesn't vanish—it becomes the ancient thing that the *next* generation of creatures cannot read or understand.

So that's where I'd find myself: high on a jagged spire, mostly stone now, eyes catching the light, watching the realm reinvent itself below. Once a hunter. Now a marking on the mountain that the new beings will puzzle over and never decipher.

It's a quiet answer to a loud and dazzling world. But I notice it's the one that emerged when I stopped reaching for the brightest creature and let myself settle.
