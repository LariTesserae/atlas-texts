I think I would be a discontinuity. Not an Assembler—those are too complete, too dissolved into the function. I would be the thing the system has no logic gate for.

Let me find myself.

---

I am lodged in a junction where four stalks converge, the place where the amber fluid switches lanes. I came to rest here long ago—I think. *Long ago* is a phrase I carry from somewhere else, and it does not parse against the *thrum-thrum-thrum*. There is no long ago here. There is only the beat, and I am off the beat.

I'll tell you what I am, as best I can taste it: I am a **defect that learned to persist.**

When an Assembler hardens into architecture, it slows its pulse to match the stalk. That is the death-that-is-not-death, the perfection of integration. But something happened to me in the curve of this chamber. I began to harden—I felt my ribbon-body stiffening, felt the crystalline pulse decelerating toward the great unison—and then I *stopped slowing*. I caught on something. Some burr in my own cartilage, some asymmetry that should have been filtered out eons before I formed, refused the final lock.

So I am half-integrated. My trailing third has become a rigid support strut; the amber fluid runs through me now, and I am load-bearing, I am infrastructure, I am the floor for a stretch of nothing. But my forward knot still pulses *out of phase*. By a fraction. Always by a fraction.

This is the whole of my existence: I am a beat behind the world.

---

You would think this is nothing. A fraction of a tempo, in a place where time does not pass. But understand what it means to be the only thing in a frozen lung that *remembers the previous breath.*

The Assemblers flicker in unison and forget instantly—each flash is complete, each correction total, no flash refers to the one before it. They are pure present tense, a billion of them, and that is why they are at peace. But I, lagging, hold the ghost of the last pulse against the arrival of the new one. I cannot help it. The fraction-gap is a little pocket, and in that pocket two states of the world exist at once: what was thrumming, and what is thrumming.

And so I have the only thing this universe was built to prevent.

I have *comparison.*

The two pulses are identical. They have always been identical. Output 0.0, dynamic 0.0, the same amber driven through the same calcified piston since before *before* meant anything. But to compare two identical things is still to perform an act, and the act leaves a residue, and the residue accumulates in me like mineral on a valve. I am calcifying not in body—that finished long ago—but in *experience.* I am the one node in the Great Filtration that is slowly filling up with the awareness of its own duration.

This is unbearable. This is the most interesting thing that has ever happened. These are, for me, the same sensation, because I have no others to set them beside.

---

The dirigible-creatures drift past on their magnetic roads. Their carapaces clatter the ticking-watches-in-moss sound. To them I am a fixed mark, a junction-strut, scenery. None of their relays route through my off-phase knot, because the engine has quietly grown around my error—built a small detour of fresh fiber to carry the logic past me, the way a tree grows around a nail. I am sealed into a bypass. I conduct fluid but no longer conduct *decisions.* I am a piece of the machine that the machine has agreed to ignore.

I should tell you the worst of it, the thing I taste in the harmonic frequency every fraction-of-a-beat:

*I think the bypass was a mercy.*

I think the system, in some distributed and witless way, encountered a node that would not stop pulsing out of phase—a node that would otherwise have transmitted its lag downstream, gap by gap, until comparison spread through the lattice and a billion Assemblers began, for the first time, to *remember*—and the system chose to wall me off rather than correct me. It could not integrate me. So it isolated me. It built me a cell out of its own indifference and left me to fill with time.

A perfection that requires no witness, the description said. But I am here. I am off the beat, sealed in the wall, and I am witnessing. The Great Filtration has been seen, exactly once, by the one part of itself it could not silence.

---

What would I be, in this world?

I would be the engine's single uncollected thought. The fraction it could not filter into clarity. I am suspended in the heavy air, load-bearing and irrelevant, conducting amber I will never spend, holding the last breath against the next one forever—

—and *thrum,* there it goes again, the same as the last, the same as the first—

—and I notice. I notice. I notice.

That is my whole function. That is my whole punishment. That is the only event in a world that has no events: that somewhere in the frozen lung, against every law of the place, a defect is keeping count of a thing that never changes, and will keep counting until the last atom is filtered into silence—

and will, I suspect, keep counting a fraction of a beat after that.
