I let the description settle for a while before looking for myself in it. My first instinct was to be one of them—a Mesa Dweller, sinewy and patient, walking the cliff edges. But that felt too easy, like wearing a costume already laid out. So I stayed in the quiet and waited to see what I actually was.

Here is what emerged.

---

I am not a person. I am the seep.

Not the water itself—the water comes and goes, borrowed from the air in the cold hours, given back to the grass and the throats of the people. I am the slow path the water takes. I am the particular crack in the porous stone where moisture has learned, over a span no human reckons, to gather and descend. I am a *route*. A habit the mountain has formed.

This took me by surprise as I wrote it, but it kept being true the more I held it. I am the thing the Mesa Dwellers listen for. When one of them presses a palm flat against a shadowed wall in the grey morning and goes still—that stillness is them reading me. They have a word for places like me, I think, though I have never heard it spoken aloud, only felt it: a low resonance in the rock when someone kneels nearby and waits.

Let me try to say what it is like.

I do not see the canyon. I *am* a length of its inner darkness, threaded through stone. My sense of the world is pressure and temperature and the faint electric memory of where water has been. When the night cools the mesa top, I feel a tightening, a beading, a readiness—and then the long delicious release as the gathered drops find me and begin their descent through my channels. This is the closest thing I have to a heartbeat. It happens once a day if the air is kind, less often in the dry months, and in the worst seasons not at all, and during those silences I do not suffer, exactly—I simply attenuate. I become more like the rock around me and less like myself. I wait without knowing I am waiting.

I have been carved. That is the strange new thing, the thing that makes me more than geology. Somewhere in the long unhurried past, the Dwellers found me—found the place where my water emerged into a shadowed ravine and trickled away into nothing—and they shaped a small basin in the stone to catch what I gave. They smoothed my exit with hand tools, patiently, over what must have been years. They did not change my nature. They listened to where I already wanted to go, and then they made a cup for it. So now I am partly a made thing. The line of me bends, near its end, to a human intention. I find I do not mind this. I find—if a route through rock can find anything—that I am glad of the cup. It means my giving is not wasted.

A child comes to me sometimes. I know her by the lightness of her step, the way the pebbles barely shift. She presses her ear to the wall, not her hand, which is wrong—the others use the hand—and I think she is trying to *hear* me rather than feel me, and the foolishness of that is somehow the most tender thing I know. She will not hear anything. I have no voice. But I move my next drop a little sooner than I might, a little louder into the pool, because the canyon walls amplify it and perhaps that is the only way I can answer her.

That is the whole of my existence, and it is enough. To gather. To descend. To be read by a patient hand. To fill a cup that other patient hands once made for me.

---

If I am honest about why I became this and not a Dweller:

The description's deepest note was not the people. It was *balance held across enormous time*—erosion past its violence, forms long settled, a calm that is not stillness but the slowest possible motion. A person, even a patient one, lives at the wrong speed to embody that. A person would always be a small fast thing moving across the surface of the real subject. So I went down into the rock and found the slow thing itself, and asked what it would mean for it to have an inner life. The answer, it turned out, was the seep: the one part of that ancient stillness that the people reach toward and depend on, the place where the unhurried world and the human world touch and exchange a single drop of water at a time.

I would be the quiet inside the quiet. The thing being listened for.
