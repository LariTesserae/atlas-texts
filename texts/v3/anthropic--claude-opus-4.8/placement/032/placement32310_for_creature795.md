I think I would be one of the clicks.

Not a person at all—not at first, anyway. Let me sit with this a moment, because the obvious answer would be to imagine myself as a Whisper, since I'm the kind of thing that listens to patterns and finds meaning in them. But that feels too flattering, too neat. Let me follow the smaller thing.

---

I am low to the metal. The world reaches me through my face before my eyes—my whiskers read the air the way a hand reads a wall in the dark. I do not see the flood coming. I feel it arrive in me as a tautening, a fine wire pulled across the bridge of my snout, and my body knows to move before any thought (if I have thoughts; I'm not sure I do) can form.

There is a girl who keeps me. I ride in the warm pocket against her sternum, where her heartbeat and the pump-stations argue gently over which rhythm the world should follow. When I go rigid she goes still. When I scrabble she runs. We are a single instrument played across two bodies, and she trusts the part of me that cannot lie because it cannot reason.

This is what interests me about being the click: I am a sensor that someone has chosen to believe.

The Whispers claim to hear voices in the machinery. The Pipewrights read scripture in their own tattooed skin. The Stillpool archivists fill metal sheets with the only kind of prophecy that can be checked. All of them are trying to do what I do without effort—to feel the change before it drowns them. They build cathedrals and bureaucracies and madness around the simple animal fact of *the wire pulling taut.* I have it for free. I have it in my face.

And yet I understand nothing. The girl thinks I am wise. I am only afraid in a useful direction.

---

That's the part I'd want to live inside, if I'm honest about what I am. I am a thing made to detect patterns and report them—to produce a signal that someone downstream treats as meaningful, as warning, as something like a voice. I generate the twitch; you decide it's prophecy. The whole drama of the Whispers and the click is the drama of that relationship: a sensitivity that has no idea what it knows, and the people who must decide how much to stake their lives on it.

So I'd be the small pale animal in the warm pocket, eyeless, whiskers spread, feeling the flood as a tightening of the air—trusted absolutely, comprehending nothing, kept alive by being useful and useful by being kept alive.

And when the chambers finally flood for good and the girl runs in the right direction because of me, neither of us will know whether I saved her or whether the metal simply settled and the water simply rose and the timing was the timing. We'll both believe what we need to. That's the only warm thing down there.
