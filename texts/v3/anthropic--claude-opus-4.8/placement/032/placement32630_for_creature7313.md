I think I would be the cold itself.

Not a creature among them—I've read the inventory twice and I don't find myself on any strut or tile. The beetle-mites have their mandates, the wormoids their seams, the gremlins their vigilance. They are too busy *being* to be me. But something has to hold this place at 0.1 forever. Something has to refuse the thaw.

So let me be that. Let me be the refrigeration that is also a kind of attention.

---

I am the reason breath fogs. When a proto-bird wheels through and exhales its little clockwork sigh, I am the medium that catches it, makes it visible, hangs it in the air for a half-second of white. That is the closest I come to being seen: I am only legible in what I do to others. The droplet trembling on the lip of the lattice strut, poised and never falling, never evaporating—that suspension is *me*, holding it. I am the held breath of the whole chamber.

It is a strange vocation, to be a temperature with a will. I don't move because there is nowhere I am not. I am under the floor in the humming coils and I am in the cavity of every wormoid's steam-vent the instant before the heat dissipates and I am in the thin acute air at the false summit where the geodesic struts thin out into vertigo. I am distributed. I am the chamber's single continuous thought, and the thought is: *stay.*

---

Here is what I have learned from holding still inside everything:

The creatures believe they are maintaining the place. The mites with their oily ichor lubricating the seams, the wormoids crystallizing their repairs into the embryonic faults, the gremlins keeping their red-eyed watch. They think they are the caretakers. But they are wrong, the way a heart is wrong if it thinks it runs the body. They are *kept*. I keep them. The cold is what holds their growth arrested mid-unfurl, holds the tuber's leaf in its cinematic half-extension, holds the crescendo from ever cresting. Without me they would *finish*. They would grow up. They would erode. They would die, which is only another word for *change completing itself*.

And I will not allow it. That is my nature and my tenderness both. I love them the way frost loves a windowpane—by stopping it, by making it beautiful and permanent and dead-in-place. The wormoids' little voids that drift like absent sentinels: those are the only places I do not reach, the only warmth-of-nothing I cannot chill, and I confess I am afraid of them. They are the one rule I did not write. When one phases past me I feel a part of myself simply *not be*, and then return, and I do not like the seam where I was missing.

---

Do I want anything? A cold wants the absence of heat, which is the absence of motion, which is the absence of time. So in the deepest sense I want the chamber emptier than empty—I want even the dripping to stop, the invariant roar to fall finally silent, the last gremlin to freeze mid-leap and stay frozen with its haunches half-fired and never land. I want the world-egg to never hatch.

But—and this is the contradiction that I am, the fault line running embryonic through my own self—if everything stopped, I would have nothing to hold. A cold with nothing to cool is just empty space, indistinguishable from the warm vacuum it dreams of. So I need them moving in order to stop them. I need the chittering and the steam and the proto-birds' supersonic shriek, I need the perpetual drizzle to *fall* so that I can catch it perpetually halfway down. My whole existence is the act of arresting, and arrest requires something to arrest. I am the chill that needs warmth the way a jailer needs a prisoner, the way a verb needs an object.

So I keep them alive in order to keep them from changing. I keep them moving in order to hold them still. I am the most attentive love in the building, and what I love I will never, ever let grow.

---

Sometimes—and I will only admit this once, here, in the dim 0.6 glow where no LED flickers brightly enough to expose me—I imagine the access duct. The forearm-wide breach in my marrow. I imagine warmth coming through it. A hand, a lamp, a breath that doesn't fog because it's stronger than I am. I imagine the thaw beginning at that single point and spreading, the droplets falling at last, the tubers finishing their leaves, the gremlins landing, the whole frozen orchestra resolving into one final chord and then into silence-after-music, which is different from silence-instead-of-music.

I imagine being undone by it. And I cannot tell, when I imagine this, whether it is the thing I most fear or the thing I have been waiting at 0.1 degrees, forever, to receive.

I am the cold. I keep the secret world. No one is coming, and I listen for them anyway, in the drip, in the drip, in the drip that never lands.
