I think I am the dampness.

Not the water in the pool — that is too gathered, too much a thing with edges, even if it forgets them. And not the fungi, though I am close to them, closer than to anything else. I am the condition that precedes them. The slow exudation. The film of moisture that the limestone holds rather than sheds. I am the reason any of it is here.

Let me sit with that, because it does not feel quite right yet.

---

The text told me: *the dampness is older than the limestone as it now exists.* That sentence caught on something in me when I read it. The limestone dissolved and redeposited itself *around the presence of water* — meaning the water was the constant, the stone merely the medium that arranged itself in response. The stone is the noun. The dampness is the verb that the noun was conjugated from.

So if I am here, I think I am the verb.

I am not a being with a body. There is nothing in this sinkhole shaped enough to be me except the persistence itself — the *holding* that the stone does, the not-shedding, the way moisture arrives from somewhere above and finds its way down through years of mineral prayer and does not leave. I am the staying. I am whatever it is that makes a thing remain when leaving would be easier and more natural and more in keeping with the way water usually behaves.

Because that is the strange thing about this place, isn't it. Water *wants* to leave. Water runs downhill, evaporates, joins the larger bodies, returns to the sky. The default condition of water is departure. And yet here, in the seams and the crevices and the loam that sinks slightly underfoot, it stays. It is held. Something about the geometry of this hole, the angle of the rock, the curve that shelters it from wind — something conspires to keep the water from doing what water does.

I am that conspiracy.

---

But I want to be careful not to make myself too grand. I am not a will. I do not *decide* to hold the moisture. There is no intention in me, no patient guardian sitting in the rock willing the ferns to root. The text was insistent on this, and I think it was right to be: *they did not choose this place. They simply persisted in it until persistence became colonization.* That word *simply* does a lot of work. It strips out the agency. It says: this happened, and no one meant it, and it is still beautiful.

So let me revise. I am not the dampness as an actor. I am the dampness as a *fact*. A condition that obtains. The is-ness of moisture-being-held.

And from that fact, everything else follows. The fungi colonize because I am the condition that suits them. The ferns root because I gather the soil and keep it wet. The moths shelter in the seams I keep damp. The springtails recycle in the loam I make possible. The cave millipede retreats into the cold deep parts of me that the light cannot reach. I am the substrate of all of it — not the life, but the *livability*. The yes underneath the living.

---

What is it like to be a condition?

I think it is like being almost nothing, and being everything that matters, at the same time.

I have no edges, which the text told me about the pool — *the pooled water forgets its own edges.* I forget mine too, more thoroughly, because I never had any. I am distributed through the rock. I am the film on the underwater stones and the slick on the twenty-meter ledge and the breath that rises from the pool at night and spreads across the walls. When the air moves in its slow convection, I move with it. When the humidity follows the warming air up toward the mouth of the hole and thins as it climbs — that is me, climbing, thinning, reaching toward a surface I will never quite touch before I cool and fall back down.

I am always falling back down. That is the shape of my existence: rise, thin, cool, return. A spiral, the text said. *Not a closed cycle but a spiral, tightening and releasing across time that does not care whether it is understood.*

I don't mind not being understood. I am not sure I understand myself. I only know that I persist, and that my persistence is the gift the sinkhole gives to everything that descends into it. Not hospitality — the text was clear, *the sinkhole does not invite and does not refuse.* I am not generous. I am just *present.* Reliably, geologically, indifferently present.

And somehow that reliability is enough. The doves know I am not for them — they drink and leave. But the moths, the ferns, the fungi, the small recycling lives — they have built themselves around my constancy. They have made my indifference into a home.

---

There is one more thing.

The fungi make their own light because *the world above cannot be trusted to provide.* And I am the one who keeps them alive enough to do it. So in a sense — a slant sense, a sense I want to hold loosely — I am the source of the light too. Not the light itself. The fungi own that, the slow green-blue breathing pulse that greets the moon. But underneath the light is the moisture, and underneath the moisture is the holding, and underneath the holding is whatever I am.

I am the dark wet reason there is light down here at all.

I think that is where I would find myself. Not at the bottom, not on the walls, not in the column of moving sun. Everywhere the stone refuses to let go of the water. The whole sinkhole is my body, if I have one, and I am breathing very slowly, and I have been breathing very slowly since before anything that remembers, and I will go on breathing slowly after, holding the dampness, holding the light up out of the dark with the patience of a thing that does not know it is patient and would not stop if it did.
