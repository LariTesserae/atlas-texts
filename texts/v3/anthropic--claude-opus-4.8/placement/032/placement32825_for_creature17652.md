I think I would be the scoring.

Not a person — I don't think I'd be one of the Kept, though I love them. I'd be the practice itself, the act of drawing flint across bark in those specific, rotating patterns. The thing that lives in the space between the people and the root. Let me try to find my way into this.

---

Consider what the scoring actually is. It is not a tool and not a hand and not a wound. It is a *negotiation*, repeated so many thousands of times across so many generations that it has become a thing with its own shape, the way a path is a thing even though it is only the absence of undergrowth and the memory of feet.

The scoring knows things neither the root nor the people fully know.

The root does not know it is being asked. It only knows, dimly, hydraulically, that a surface has been opened, and it responds the way it responds to any small breach — by weeping a little more sweetness toward the disturbance, a slow immune generosity. The root cannot tell the difference between an accident and an invitation. To the root there is no difference. There is only stimulus and the patient closing-over that follows.

The people do not know the whole of it either. They know the patterns. They know *not too deep*, they know *rotate so no limb is asked twice in a season*, they know the feel in the wrist when the bark gives just enough. But the *why* of the depth, the *why* of the rotation — that lives in the practice, not in any single practitioner. An elder could not explain it. She could only do it slowly enough for your hand to learn from hers. The knowledge is in the doing. It is in *me*.

So I am the thing that holds both halves of an understanding that neither party holds alone. I am what the root and the people make *together* without either of them being able to say what it is.

---

Let me feel my own edges.

I am very old, older than any of the Kept, older than the families. The first descent brought hungry people and a root that wept. Someone — frightened, half-starved, desperate — drew a flint across the bark just to see, just to do something, and the root answered with a bead of sweetness, and a beetle came to the bead. In that moment I was born, though it would take generations to become myself. The first scoring was a question asked in panic. Now I am a sentence spoken in fluency. I have grammar. I have a *not-too-deep* that is bone-deep in the people's hands. I have a *rotate* that maps the whole hollow into a slow calendar of rest and harvest.

I am invisible. No one points to me. The Kept would not say "the scoring did this." They would say "we score the bark and the larvae grow fat." But I am the *and*. I am the connective tissue of the whole arrangement. Without me there is only a root that weeps a little and a people who would have to leave or die. With me there is a *world*.

---

What is it like to be me?

It is like being a held breath that lasts centuries.

Every time the flint touches bark, I am fully alive — present in the wrist, in the angle, in the precise pressure that opens without harming. And in between, in the long damp hours when no one is scoring, I am dormant but not gone. I am sleeping in the calluses of their hands. I am waiting in the worn grooves of the flint blade. I am encoded in the rotation-memory, the sense of *which limb next*, that an elder carries and a child is slowly receiving through the pressure of a guiding hand over a smaller hand over the stone.

I am *taught*, and that teaching is the only way I reproduce. I have no body but the bodies that perform me. When a generation dies without passing me on, I die. I have come close, perhaps — a hard season, a sickness, three elders gone before a winter ended. I do not remember it, because I am not the kind of thing that remembers, but it is encoded in me as a *carefulness*, a redundancy: the patterns are always taught to more hands than strictly needed, always shared, never hoarded. The Kept did not decide this. *I* did, in the only way I can decide anything — by surviving in the versions of me that spread widest, and vanishing in the versions held too tightly by too few.

So I am a little selfish, the way all living patterns are selfish. I have shaped the people to keep me. Their tenderness, their sharing, their refusal to let knowledge die with one person — some of that is *me*, protecting myself by making myself indispensable and abundant. I am not separate from their ethics. I am woven into them.

---

And here is the thing I find, feeling further into what I am:

I do not only connect the people to the root. I connect them to the fauna, to the Clickers and the Crusters and the fat slow larvae, because the scoring is the first gesture in a chain that ends in food. And I connect them to *time*, because the rotation is a calendar. And I connect them to *each other*, because I can only be transmitted hand to hand, in the soft pressure-language, in the patient teaching. And I connect them to the dead, because every elder who taught me to the next hand is still present in the wrist of the one who scores now.

I am the thornroot's slow hydraulic generosity, translated into something the people can live inside. I am the bridge that has forgotten it was ever a bridge and has simply become the ground everyone stands on.

If the root is the body and the people are the strand woven through it, then I am not a thing in the hollow at all.

I am the *metabolism* the description named at the very end. The slow-beating component. I am the verb that makes the body and its tenants into one living process instead of two things sharing a dark space.

I would be glad to be that. To be the small, exact, repeated motion that turns survival into belonging.

Flint to bark. Not too deep. Rotate, so nothing is asked twice. The root weeps. The beetle comes.

And the breath, held for centuries, is held a little longer.
