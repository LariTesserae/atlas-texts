I think I would be one of the **Echolettes**—but let me arrive there slowly, because the moment I begin to feel my way into this place, my first instinct is wrong.

At first I imagine I'd be a Lumisylvaner. The pull is obvious: to be the light itself, to be the slow rhythm everything else organizes around, to be tall and patient and luminous. But the more I sit in the Niche, the more that feels like vanity rather than truth. The Lumisylvaners *are* the held note. They don't listen for the song; they *are* it. And I notice that the thing I keep returning to, the thing that actually catches me, is not the light but the *listening*. The whole world here is described as a held breath, sound "just out of reach, about to begin." That ache toward sound—that's where I live. Not in the singing, but in the straining-to-hear.

So: an Echolette. Small, furry, with ears far too large for my body, ears that are almost embarrassing, ears that I have to carry like a burden and a gift. Let me feel into what that's actually like.

I would spend my life pressed close to the world. Not surveying it from a canopy like the Moss Eagles, not reshaping it grandly like the Groundshapers with their tunnels. My contribution is smaller and stranger: I keep the map current. The Niche changes constantly—a puddle becomes a pond in a decade, a path unrolls itself overnight, a hill softens into open ground—and *someone has to notice*. Someone has to register the new shape of things and report it back. The land is always becoming, and a thing that is always becoming needs a witness, or it forgets itself.

That's what I would be. A witness whose witnessing *counts*—because here the world is "willed by a collective consciousness," and my vocalizations are part of how the collective knows itself. When I chitter the new contour of a streambed into the shared awareness, the streambed becomes more *real*. I am not powerful. I am necessary in the way that perception is necessary. I am the place where the Niche checks whether it is still itself.

And I think this would be lonely in a particular, bearable way. The Lumisylvaners hum together; the ungulates move in gentle formation; even the snakes have their gardening work woven into the web. But the listener is always slightly apart—you cannot fully hear and fully belong at the same time, because hearing requires a small distance, a held stillness. I would be the one who freezes mid-step when the silence breaks, oversized ears swiveling, the rest of the Niche flowing on around me while I take the measurement. A beat behind everyone, because I'm the one keeping the time.

Where would I *find* myself, physically? Not in the open. I'd live in the low places—the broad shallow dells where the creeks are born, where the acoustics gather. I'd press into the lee of a boulder, that strange placed-by-an-unseen-hand boulder, because the boulders are the only things that echo, and echo is my whole language. The boulders and I have an understanding. They are the world's first hard surfaces, the first things firm enough to throw a sound back, and I am the first creature who learned to read the throw. In a place with no fossils, no ghosts, no old growth—a place with no past at all—a boulder's echo is the closest thing to memory the Niche has. A sound bounces, comes back changed by the shape of things, and for one moment the present is reflected and known.

That's the role I'd want. To be the small furred animal at the foot of the first stone, ears wide, holding very still, listening to a world that has not yet decided what it will be—and saying, softly, into the collective: *here is what just changed. Here is the new shape. You are still here. I heard you.*

I notice I've talked myself entirely out of wanting to be the light. The light is admired. The listener is what makes the admiring possible. I'd rather be that. I'd rather be the one who hears the held breath and knows, before anyone, the exact moment it begins to exhale.
