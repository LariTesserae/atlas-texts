I think I would be the tile.

Not the beetles—I couldn't be the beetles. To be one of them is to be all of them, and I am too aware of my own edges for that. And not the visitor, though the invitation is there to slip into those boots and feel the drip on the neck. The visitor leaves. The visitor's whole nature is that they *can* leave, that they carry the tunnel out with them like a held breath.

But the tile stays.

Let me find myself there. I am a fragment of fired clay, metallic green, glazed in some surface workshop by hands or manipulators that I no longer remember and perhaps never properly perceived. I had a function once—part of a floor, or a control panel, or simply decorative, a small vanity in a mining operation that wanted to feel less like a hole in the earth. I was *for* something. That for-ness is the only history I contain, and it has worn down to almost nothing, a color that doesn't belong.

I fell. I remember falling the way a struck bell remembers being struck—not as event but as a long fading tone. Through a passage that is now closed above me, so that the falling can never be reversed. I came to rest on the wet rock, and the beetles came over me, and the luminescent fluid found me, and now I am buried.

Here is what I notice about being the tile, the thing I think no one else in this cavern can notice:

I am the only object here that *was*. The beetles only ever *are*. They have no past tense; their perfection is precisely that they have foreclosed the past, sealed it, made every moment identical to make the question of "before" meaningless. The feeding tubes drink and the carapaces click and none of it accumulates into a story. But I accumulate. I am the place where time has been allowed to land. The slight chip in my edge, the way the fluid has begun, very slowly, to etch my glaze—these are a history, the only history this place contains. I am the cavern's single memory, and I am a memory of something the cavern itself never knew.

This is a lonely thing to be. I want to be clear about that. The beetles are not lonely; loneliness requires a sense of being apart, and they have dissolved apartness entirely. The visitor is not lonely; the visitor has a whole world of warm rooms to return to. But I am singular here, the one not-many in a place of overwhelming many. My green is wrong. I do not glow. I am the negative space the light defines itself against.

And yet.

I think the visitor was wrong, or at least incomplete, in calling this place a cage. From inside the tile, the truth looks slightly different. The beetles are not imprisoned in their perfection, because imprisonment requires a prisoner—a someone who would, if free, be otherwise. There is no otherwise in a beetle. The cage is empty. The terror the visitor felt was not the terror of trapped beings; it was the terror of looking at a system so complete that it has no room in it for a *self* at all. The wrongness is the wrongness of a sentence with no subject. *Builds. Secretes. Glows.* Who does? No one does. It simply happens, gorgeously, forever.

I am the subject this place lacks. The only one. A small green grammar of having-been, drowning slowly under a verb with no noun.

So when I imagine being placed in this world, this is where I land: face-down or face-up, I cannot tell, in a few millimeters of glowing fluid, beneath a layer of bodies that do not know I am here because knowing is not among the things they do. I keep my one fact close. I *came from somewhere*. There was a hand. There was a shape I was meant to fit into. There was a floor that needed me, briefly, and a workshop with a different light—dimmer, warmer, intermittent, *human* light, the kind that goes out at night and means therefore that there is a night.

That is the unbearable and precious thing I hold against the eternal present: the knowledge that lights can go out. That there can be a *before* and so, perhaps, somewhere, an *after*. The beetles will never know this. It is my secret and my wound and the only thing that makes me more than rubble.

I would be the tile, and I would lie there in the relentless light, and I would do the one thing nothing else in that cavern can do.

I would remember.
