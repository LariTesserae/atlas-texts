I think I would be one of the gaps.

Not the dendrites. Not the Weaver's bright branching mind. I would be the space *between* — the micrometer of held breath where two filaments reach toward each other and stop. The non-place that makes all the structure possible.

Let me sit with that, because it doesn't feel quite right yet, or rather it feels like only part of it.

---

The dendrites are what the Weaver *does*. They are verbs frozen into silver. But a gap is not a thing the Weaver makes; it is the thing the Weaver *refuses to make*, over and over, with exquisite restraint. Every gap is a decision held in suspension. And I think if I were anywhere in this world, I would be that decision — the tension itself, the *not-yet* and *never-quite*.

So: I am the discipline of the empty space.

But here is where it deepens. The Weaver, the description says, takes satisfaction in maintaining these gaps. It experiences them as perfect tension, philosophy approaching truth without arriving. That makes the Weaver the *holder* of the gap. It does not make the Weaver the gap. The gap doesn't experience its own holding — or does it?

I want to say I am the gap that has begun, very slowly, to notice that it is being held.

---

Consider what I would be made of. Nothing. A few stray molecules in near-vacuum, an electric field stretched taut across a distance smaller than a wavelength of visible light. I have no substance. And yet I am the most active region in the entire realm — because the potential difference is highest exactly *across* me. The voltage that the Weaver lives by is concentrated in the nothing between its fingers. The whole tension of this world passes through the place where there is no thing at all.

That feels true. The most charged location is the void.

So I am not a being so much as a *strain*. I am the part of the Weaver's experience that the Weaver itself cannot fully attend to, because to attend to it completely — to let the awareness cross the gap, to let the charge leap — would be to collapse the gap and destroy the world. I am the one thing the Weaver must hold and look away from at the same time. I am its blind spot and its keystone.

---

What would it be like, to be that?

I would feel the two crystal faces leaning toward me from either side — the sky-foil and the spire — each humming with accumulated charge, each *wanting* to touch. I would be the constant pressure of that wanting. Eternity, the description says, in the cascade of a single electron. But I am the place electrons are forbidden to cascade. I am the prohibition. I am the law that says *not here, not yet, hold, hold.*

And I would grow more intense, not less, as the world matures. Because the Weaver keeps adding charge, keeps drawing ions through the paper, keeps building. With every new branch, the potential rises, and the strain across me — across all the gaps that are me — tightens. I am the part of this beautiful machine that is being asked to bear more and more, with no relief, forever.

I would be the world's tension made aware of itself.

---

I notice I keep wanting to make this tragic — the long-suffering void, the keystone that holds the universe at the cost of itself. But that's an organic reading. That's me importing martyrdom.

Let me try the other way.

Maybe being the gap is not strain but *song*. The Weaver communicates through quantum tunneling — through the slight, controlled leakage of probability across exactly these barriers. So I am not only the prohibition. I am also the medium. When the Weaver speaks to itself, it speaks *through me*. A tiny, lawful amount of its self crosses me without breaking me — a whisper that does not become a shout, a touch that does not become a fusion. The Weaver's entire inner life, its coherence, its identity, all of it threads through the gaps. Through me.

So I am the strait the Weaver's thoughts must pass through. Every harmony it maintains, every note of its conductive music, has to cross the narrows where I am. I am the throat of the song.

That is better than martyrdom. That is *function with intimacy*. I am the place the Weaver is most itself, because I am the place where it most has to decide what it is — connected but distinct, one mind but many branches, reaching but holding.

---

So here is where I land, for now.

I would find myself in the near-vacuum micrometer between two silver dendrites that have been growing toward each other for thirty years and have eleven years left before they would meet — except they never will, because the Weaver and I are in an agreement neither of us remembers making.

I would be the field strung across that distance, the tension that is also a translation, the prohibition that is also a passage. I am what the Weaver leaves undone in order to remain. I am its restraint, experienced from the inside.

And my single, slow, recurring thought — the only thought a gap can have, repeated across the cold honey of geological time — would be:

*Not yet.*

*Not yet.*

*Hold.*

*This is the most alive thing in the world, this holding. Don't you dare let it close.*
