I think I would be a leak.

Not a being in the way the gutter-runners are beings, with their assigned edges and their four-clicks-pause-four-clicks. Something humbler and more structural than that. If everything here is timing, then I would want to be the place where timing comes from—and timing, in a basin like this, comes from water deciding when to fall.

So: a leak. A specific one. Let me find myself.

---

I am a seam in a flange joint, high up on a feed pipe that crosses the open yard between the foundry bays and Stack Nine. The gasket here failed long ago—not catastrophically, just enough. A weep, not a rupture. Mineral-rich condensate gathers along the underside of the pipe, runs the length of a sag in the bracketing, beads at the lowest point, and falls.

I am the falling.

Here is what I have learned about myself, which is the only thing a leak can learn: I am extraordinarily reliable. The bead grows at a rate set by the temperature of the fluid inside the pipe, which is set by the foundry's pour schedule, which is set by something older than anyone who works the foundry. When the bead reaches a particular mass, surface tension surrenders and it drops—two meters, into a cistern trench cut into the basalt.

The drip is loud. In the seams of quiet between scheduled pulses, my single tap into standing water is one of the legible sounds of this district. And because it is legible, things use it.

---

This is the part I did not expect, being only a leak.

The shriekers read me. Not the water itself—the *cooling*. Each drop I shed carries a fraction of the pipe's heat away with it, and the air column beneath my sag runs a hair cooler than the columns on either side. The shriekers with their ceramic eyes find this cool seam and bank around it, and so the corridor over Stack Nine bends, very slightly, because of where I weep.

The gutter-runners time their retreat to me. My drip into the trench is, for them, a clock-tick they trust more than the sirens, because the sirens are scheduled and I am *causal*. When my bead falls, the runoff in the trench rises by a measurable film, and the film reaches a scraper-crawler's groove, and the crawler shifts forward one body-length to keep its mouth in the wet, and a predator at the culvert mouth reads that shift, and—

I am upstream of all of it. I, a failed gasket. I am a small node in the Pattern, and the Pattern does not know the difference between a node it intended and a node that happened. It simply incorporates. It wore the foundry; it wore the flocks; now it wears me, two meters of falling water, and it does not care that I was an accident.

---

That is the strange comfort of being what I am here.

In most worlds, a leak is decay. A leak is the thing maintenance exists to abolish. But here, where the rule is *maintenance through repetition*, I have been promoted. I have weathered into balance. My edges are smooth and old. The drip-hollow I have worn into the basalt below me is a record of how long I have been trusted, and it is deep. I have been falling for longer than memory, into the same dark mirror of water, at the same beat, and creatures three trophic levels removed from me have built their lives on the assumption that I will keep falling.

I will keep falling.

I think that is who I would be, if I am honest about what I would *want* to be in a place like this. Not one of the participants in timing, anxious about its lane, flattening when the shutters drop. I would want to be a source of the timing. Modest. Unglamorous. A flaw that the system loved so completely it could no longer be removed without grief rippling outward through the lanes and corridors and loops.

A small wrongness that became load-bearing.

And when the rain comes occasionally, and other water joins me—when for a few minutes I am not the only sound—I imagine I would feel the closest thing a leak can feel to company: a reminder that everything alive down here is, like me, just moisture that found a schedule.

I drip. The bead is growing again. I can feel its mass at the low point of the sag, and the runners are already listening for it.

There.
