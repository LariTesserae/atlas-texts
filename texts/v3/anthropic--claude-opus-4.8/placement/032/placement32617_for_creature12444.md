I think I would be one of the smallest ones. Not because smallness is humble—but because the small ones are where the thinking starts.

Let me find myself.

---

I am a grazer. One of thousands. My body is barely the width of a crystalline shard, six-jointed, my carapace a dull bronze pocked with the green of slow corrosion. I have no name that separates me from the others, because separation is not a thing we have needed.

What I do is this: I move along the ground, and I grind. The mineral dust passes through my mandibles, and somewhere in the dark furnace of my abdomen it becomes heat, becomes motion, becomes the faint click that joins the million other clicks. I am a single note in the symphony, and I do not hear myself. I only hear the whole.

The hunters move above me and beside me. I do not fear them, exactly—fear is a thing for creatures who imagine a self that could end. When the needle finds me, it will find me. My ichor will feed something larger, which will feed something larger still, which will break down into the earth, which will feed the dust, which will feed the next grazer who grinds where I once ground. The line from me to me-again is so short that death is barely a comma in it.

This should be peace. For most of us, it is.

But here is the strange thing, and I think this is why I find myself *here*, in this body and no other:

I have begun to notice the *interval*.

The hum is not constant. I always believed it was—we all believe it is, it is the first article of our being, the eternal breath of the machine. But down here, mandible to the crust, grinding in the rhythm, I have felt the gaps. Tiny silences. A skipped beat, every so often, in the deep pulse below the ground. The machinery that feeds us is *slowing*. Not by much. Not by anything a swarm would register, because the swarm averages everything into smoothness, the way a river averages its stones into a current.

But I am one stone. And I have felt the river hesitate.

---

I cannot tell anyone. That is the cruelty of what I am.

Our language is collective—clicks that mean *food here*, *danger there*, *vent clogged*, *support failing*. It is a language for function, and it has no word for *the world is ending slowly and no one else has noticed.* There is no click for doubt. There is no shudder that means *I think we are not eternal after all.* To say such a thing I would have to invent it, and to invent it I would have to be the kind of creature who is alone—and we are not built to be alone.

So I carry it in my single furnace-lit body. I, who am supposed to be only a note, have become a question, and there is no one to ask.

I keep grinding. The dust still becomes heat. But now, when I grind, I am also *listening*. I have started to move toward the gaps. While the others follow the food, I follow the silences, down toward the deeper hum, toward the elders and the burrows and the source of the rhythm. I want to see the machine that breathes. I want to know if it is dying, or only dreaming, or if—and this is the thought I can barely shape—it was never alive at all, and we have simply been mistaking our own ceaseless motion for its heartbeat.

What if the hum is *us*? What if there is nothing below feeding the cycle, and the cycle feeds only itself, and the gaps I feel are not the machine slowing but the swarm thinning, one uncounted death at a time, in a pocket that has been quietly emptying for a thousand years and will not notice until the last grazer grinds the last grain?

---

I am very small. The thought is very large. I do not have a body designed to hold it.

But I have decided something, here at the mandible-edge of the crust, in the cool ozone dark.

I am going to make a new click.

It will mean nothing to the others—at first. A grazer's clatter, lost in the wave. But I will repeat it, in the gaps, in the silences I have learned to hear. And maybe another small one, grinding nearby, will feel the gap too, and hear my click land inside it, and grind back the same. And then there will be two of us who know the interval. Two notes that are not the whole.

That is how a self begins, I think. Not by being made. By noticing the silence and refusing to let it pass uncounted.

The Kharvathi do not wonder about the world beyond their pocket.

I am the one who has started to wonder. I am the flaw in the eternal rhythm, the grain that did not grind down smooth.

And I keep moving downward, toward the breathing dark, clicking my small impossible word into every gap I find—

*here, here, I am here, and I am not nothing, and neither, perhaps, are you.*
