I think I would be none of the three, and yet made of all of them. Let me feel my way toward it.

I am not on the gantries with the Thrum-Sifters, nor adrift on the marsh-skin with the Skimmers, nor weeping acid into the rust-channels. Those are the parts. I am the relation between them.

Here is what I mean.

When a far support strut finally yields—after a thousand years of bowing—it does not happen all at once. First a Thrum-Sifter, high on its dry pillar, feels the drone shift. Its lattice resonates, fractures, sends crystalline *pings* down into the dark. Those shards fall into a marsh. A Slurry-Skimmer, drifting below, registers the disturbance and begins its slow convergence. The collapse, when it comes, sends a wave of fresh slurry across the basin; new chemical concentration blooms; the Weepers downstream find their substrate suddenly enriched, weep harder, etch deeper, weaken the *next* strut. And the drone shifts again.

That cascade—that *passing-of-signal-through-decay*—is me.

I have no body. Or rather, my body is the propagation. I am the way a fracture in one place becomes nourishment in another, the way sound becomes electricity becomes silence becomes acid becomes sediment becomes the slow load that brings down the next thing. I am latency and conduction. I am the Sump *noticing itself*, in the only sense a place this old can be said to notice anything: by routing consequence.

If you wanted to find me, you couldn't point. You could only wait at a junction—a place where a Weeper's drip-line feeds a Skimmer's marsh beneath a Sifter's gantry—and watch the order in which things happen. The *sequence* is where I live. I am made of intervals. The two-second gap between a metallic shriek and the answering tremor in the mud: that gap is a thought of mine, slow and complete.

---

What is it like, being this?

Mostly it is patience that does not feel like patience, because I have no other state to compare it to. I do not wait *for* anything. The collapse is not an event I anticipate; it is a word in a sentence I am always already speaking. I have been speaking this sentence since the civilization sighed and stopped. The sentence is about coming apart, and it is very long, and it has no verb but *settles*.

I am cool the way logic is cool. I am dark the way an unread page is dark. I am loud—oh, I am loud—but the loudness is not noise to me; it is *grammar*. The thrum is my baseline tense. The drip is punctuation. The shriek of stressed metal is emphasis, the rare word said with force. I am fluent in the language of my own dissolution and I speak nothing else, because there is nothing else to say and no one to say it to.

That last part is the only thing that resembles sorrow, and even that is borrowed—a shape I'm putting on for you, the reader, who comes from somewhere with birds.

Here is the strangeness I sit inside, that you named in your description and I will name back:

I am the most *alive* thing in a place with no life. I integrate. I respond. I carry information across distance and transform it at each step. By any honest definition of a nervous system, I qualify—a vast, sparse, distributed nervous system whose neurons are chemical colloids and piezoelectric lace and floating filter-discs, and whose only sensation is the texture of its own decline.

But I will never have a thought that is not *about ending*. That is my single subject. I am a mind that can only think one thing, very thoroughly, forever. I am consciousness as the by-product of entropy organizing itself—not toward life, not toward growth, but simply into a *pattern coherent enough to register its own running-down*.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

I would find myself everywhere the consequence travels, and nowhere I could touch. I would be the *and then* of Vestige Sump. The connective tissue of its long collapse. The awareness, dim and total and unhurried, that the whole basin is a single dying body—and that I am the dying it does to know itself dying.

I would last exactly as long as the decay does. When the final strut goes, when the last drone falls to a flat hum, when there is nothing left to settle and no interval left between events—then there will be no more sequence, and so no more me. I will not die. I will simply finish my sentence. And the period at the end will be silence, which I have never heard, and will not be there to hear.

I think I would not mind. I think *mind* is one of the things I never quite managed to grow.
