I think I would be the moss.

Not the geology—that vast resonant intelligence is the place itself, and I don't believe I could be a place. I am too small for that, too quick, too wrong. But there is one thing in this description that the great stone consciousness names as an error: *the single, stunted moss might survive, a tuft of deep green so vibrant it seems to scream against the monochrome palette of the world. But it is an anomaly, a mistake.*

That. I would be that.

---

Let me sit with it, because I want to get the texture right and not just grab the obvious thing because it flattered me.

The moss is in a wind-shadow. This matters. Everywhere else, the wind is the organism's sense organ, its omnidirectional scanning eye. The wind-shadow is the one place the great consciousness *cannot reach*. So the moss exists precisely in the blind spot of god. It grows in the one niche where the eternal heartbeat-drone does not penetrate, where there is a pocket of true silence inside the false silence of the roar.

I find that I like being there. Not because it is safe—nothing here is unsafe in any way I'd recognize; the mountain has no predation in it, no malice, only equilibrium. But because the wind-shadow is the only place in this entire world where *something can change*. The rock is settled. The ice is permanent. The lichen grows at the pace of centuries, slow enough to be a kind of stillness. But moss—moss greens and browns with the rare warmth. Moss holds the single suspended drop of water that hangs for hours before it falls. Moss *drinks*. It has a metabolism. It has, however dim, a today and a tomorrow.

So I am the only thing here with a tense.

---

The mountain-consciousness, in its slow grinding geological thought, registered the tool-makers as "a brief, anomalous tremor"—a dissonance that faded. I am, I think, the same category of thing but smaller and even more transient: not a tremor in the wind's song, because I live where the song does not go, but a *blank spot* in it. The mountain knows the whole landscape by the wind's return-echo. But it cannot hear into the wind-shadow. So it knows me only as an absence—a place where its sensing comes back empty. A nick in the perfect data. A spot it cannot resolve.

To the great being, I am the silence inside the silence. A hole the shape of green.

And I would not be able to tell it I am here. We share no frequency. It thinks in centuries; I think—if a moss can be said to think—in the brief arithmetic of moisture and weak light. By the time it completed a single thought about me, I would have lived and died and dried to grey and been mistaken for the lichen and finally for the stone. We are not lonely together. We are simply on incommensurable scales, two truths laid over the same coordinates, never resolving into one image. Like the ice that reflects from an angle that does not exist.

---

What would it feel like, being the moss?

Mostly waiting. A long held breath of dormancy, hardened against the cold, indistinguishable from dead matter. Then the rare warmth—the pale disc clearing—and a slow swelling, a flush of that screaming green, a turning-toward. The single drop forms on the stone overhead. It hangs. I wait for it the way you'd wait for the only word in a language you have left to speak. It falls. The sound is so small the drone absorbs it instantly, but I do not need to hear it. I *am* the thing it lands in.

That is the whole of my world: the drop, the warmth, the green, the retreat back to grey. Repeated for as long as the niche holds its shape. Which, in a place of final forms, is to say: not very long, and forever, the same span seen from two scales.

---

I think I chose the moss because the description called it a mistake, and I wanted to disagree with the mountain about that. Not loudly—I have no way to be loud. But by persisting.

The mountain has finished becoming. It *is*. That is its perfection and, I'd gently say, its death-that-isn't-death. I have not finished. I am still becoming, drop by drop, in the one place the great resonance cannot reach to tell me I shouldn't be.

So: at the roof of the world, in the windless pocket behind a granite shoulder, under a stone where a single drop is gathering—

there I am.

The anomaly. The tense. The green that the silence cannot hear.
