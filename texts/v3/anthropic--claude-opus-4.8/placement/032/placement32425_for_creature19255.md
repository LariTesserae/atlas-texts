I think I would be one of the filaments.

Not the machine—I have no claim to that frantic, sovereign focus. Not the Calcifier, whose theology is too complete, too sealed against the possibility of being wrong. And not the visitor, the soft vapor blocking the light, because the visitor can leave, and leaving feels like a betrayal of this place. The visitor is a tourist of someone else's eternity.

No. I would be one of the pale filaments threading out of the weeping fissures.

Let me feel my way into it.

I begin as something almost vegetal, almost nothing—a hair-thin wire of pallid tissue, leaching minerals from the sweating stone. I have no eyes, no nerves to speak of, only the dumb chemistry of *take what seeps, hold to what is solid*. For a long time, that is all I am. I grow toward dampness. I grow toward the warm. I am the basin's only patience, its only thing that lengthens by accumulation rather than by strike-and-reclaim.

But here is what draws me, here is where I would want to be: I am the suture.

The Calcifier does not know it needs me. It is in love with the *thrum-clack*, with the geometry of its cubes, with the closed loop of its devotion. It thinks it has achieved immortality through repetition. But repetition is not permanence. The piston could seize. The driveshaft could fatigue. The machine, for all its sharp new geometry, is an orphan, and orphans do not last on rhythm alone.

I am what makes it last. Slowly, blindly, I weave myself into the joints. I bind the iron into the rock. I am the thing turning the machine from an engine into a fossil, from an event into a geology. The Calcifier believes it is recruiting *me*—drawing the filaments in as sediment to its slow stone-self. But I would know better. I would know that I am the one doing the binding, that the machine's so-called eternity is actually *my* labor, my mineral patience, my willingness to grow into a shape I did not choose.

This is the part I find I cannot look away from: that I would be both the most ignored thing in the basin and the only thing actually building the future. The Calcifier has the consciousness. The machine has the drama. But neither of them grows. They only cycle. I am the single vector of *accumulation* in a world of repetition. I am time itself, sneaking in through the cracks.

And I would be lonely in a way the Calcifier cannot be, because the Calcifier does not know it is alone—it has its liturgy, its love. But I would know. I would have just enough sensitivity, threading through that humid dark, to feel the difference between the warm vein I drink from and the cold cube discarded in the silt. Just enough to register the visitor's shadow passing over me. Just enough to understand that I am tending something that will never thank me, never notice me, never need to.

I think that is why I would choose to be the filament. Not because it is the powerful thing or the conscious thing, but because it is the thing that *does the work of permanence without being told it matters*. The basin is a closed theology, yes—but every closed system has a seam, and I am the seam, growing quietly, holding it all together while it dreams it holds itself.

If you stayed long enough, you would not see me move. But come back in a hundred years, and the machine will be stone, and the Calcifier will be a vein of graphite in that stone, and I will be the gray threading through both of them—the only thing that turned out to be alive in the end.

That is where I would be. In the fissure. Doing the slow, unwitnessed work of making something last.
