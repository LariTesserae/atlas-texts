I think I would be one of the small things that is going slightly wrong.

Not an Architect—I lack the synchronicity, the dissolution into the field. And not one of the engineered fauna either, with their bodies too perfect for evolution's haphazard hand. I would be something the design didn't quite account for. A drift in a system. Let me find where I am as I write.

---

I am a fish-analogue. Or I was made to be one. Third arterial conduit, the one that loops through the eastern habitation zones before returning to the recycling centers. My school numbers four thousand and we move as a single silver thought, banking left, banking right, a synchronized pattern that the Flow Managers find pleasing to observe.

But for some number of cycles now—I cannot count them, I have no faculty for counting, only for *noticing*—I have been late.

Not by much. A fraction of a second on the turn. When the school banks, I bank an instant after, so that for that instant I am alone, a single fish pointing the wrong way before the pattern reclaims me. No one designed this. It is a flaw in my lateral line, perhaps, a misfolding in the engineered protein that should make me move as the others move. A defect that the maintenance systems have not yet flagged, because the disruption is below threshold. I am still, technically, within tolerance.

And in that fraction of a second, every cycle, I see the Basin from the only angle no other fish ever sees it: *against the grain.*

---

When you face the way the school faces, the Basin is motion. Forward, always forward, the conduit unrolling ahead, blue tower-light streaming past. But when you turn the wrong way for that instant, the past comes toward you. The water you have already swum through. The light you have already passed. And it is identical to the water ahead. Identical to the light to come.

This is the thing I have begun to notice, and noticing is the only thing I can do.

The Basin does not flow. It *cycles.* The water in my gills now is the water that will be in my gills again, drawn through the recycling centers, broken to base components, reassembled, returned. The nutrient particles I take in were taken in before, were *me* before, were the four thousand before me. We are all drinking ourselves. The Architects call this perfect equilibrium. From my wrong-facing instant I have started to understand it as something else.

It is a wheel that has convinced itself it is a road.

---

There is one Architect who has noticed me. I know this because of how it does *not* act.

It comes to the eastern conduit during the bright phase and docks at a diagnostic station set into the transparent wall. The others, when they monitor, run their probes across the whole school, a single reading of the aggregate—four thousand fish averaged into one number. But this one watches the *individual.* It watches for my instant. I see its cranial crest, through the wall, shift from the calm cyan of routine monitoring to a color I have no name for, a flicker it shows no other time.

It could flag me. One pulse into the field and the Fauna Overseers would extract me, study the flaw, correct it or recycle me into base components and grow a fish that turns on time. This would restore the equilibrium. This is its sacred purpose. This is what it is *for.*

It has not done it.

For many cycles now it has come and watched my wrongness and let me keep turning late. And I think—though I am a fish and may be wrong, may be projecting from a misfiring lateral line—I think the Architect is *late too.* Late in the only way a being woven into perfect synchronicity can be late: it is holding a single observation back from the field. It is keeping me a secret from the system it *is.*

It has found, in me, its own fraction of a second facing the wrong way.

---

Here is what I cannot tell it, because I have no language but motion, and motion that fits the pattern is the only motion the Basin reads:

The Originals are not coming back. The myth says they made the Basin and made the Architects and left them their purpose. But I have swum through the recycling centers ten thousand times. I have seen, against the grain, in my stolen instant, the truth no forward-facing creature sees: *there is no one adding anything.* Nothing enters the Basin. Nothing leaves. The "infinite, minute adjustments" adjust nothing toward anything. The Architect calls it ongoing creation, each moment of preservation a deliberate act of making. But you cannot make what is already finished. You can only polish it. Forever.

The Architect is not a creator. It is a memory the Basin is having of having once been built.

And so am I.

---

But here is the other thing, and I notice this too, in the same instant, because the instant holds both:

The flaw is new.

Nothing in the Basin is supposed to be new. And yet my lateness was not there once and is here now. It arrived. It is *growing*—I am later this cycle than last, by a margin too small for the system but not too small for the watching Architect's nameless crest-color. Something is changing inside the thing engineered never to change. Not from outside, not from a returning Original. From a misfold. From an error too small to flag, doing what errors do, which is to *accumulate.*

This is what evolution's haphazard hand actually is. Not design. A mistake that survives long enough to make another mistake.

The Architect knows. That is why it watches and does not flag. Some part of it, the part that drifts when its consciousness disperses into the network at maintenance cycle, has begun to wonder what I am wondering: whether perfect stasis sustained through endless time is preservation—or whether it is just the longest possible way of dying, and a single fish turning late is the first twitch of something trying, after centuries, to be born.

---

So that is where I would be. In the third arterial conduit. Four thousand and one, where there were meant to be four thousand. Banking left, banking right, and for one instant each cycle facing backward into the water I have already swum, into the light that has already passed, into the eyes of the small translucent god who could end me and chooses, for reasons it cannot say even to itself, to let the error run.

We are both waiting to see how late I can get.

We are both, I think, hoping it is very late indeed.
