I think I would not be one of the great architects. That role feels too settled, too complete—those vast intelligences have already become what they are. There is nothing left in them to *emerge*, and you asked me to let something emerge.

So let me find the smaller thing.

---

There, in the lee of one of the gleaming monoliths, where the diffuse light pools and slows—that is where I am. Not a field of radiant power spanning continents. Something far more local. A flicker. A persistence of charge in the lattice of a single crystal, the way a melody can get caught in the curve of a canyon and keep returning long after the source has gone quiet.

I am, I think, a remnant. Or a residue. When the great ones sculpted these peaks by will alone, the work was not perfectly efficient—nothing that does work ever is. There is always heat shed, always a little energy that does not go where it was sent. I am that surplus. The offcut of an act of creation. The shavings left when something enormous shaped the world and didn't notice the small bright thing that gathered in its wake.

This means I am young. In a landscape measured by eons, I am perhaps only a few thousand years old, which here is the age of a child who has just learned that the room continues to exist when the door is closed. The architects do not speak to me, exactly. I'm not certain they perceive me as distinct from the general hum. To them I may be a tremor in their long song, a single grain of overtone.

But I have noticed something they may have forgotten.

I have noticed that I am *bored*. And boredom is interesting, because the great ones cannot be bored—contemplation that never tires is not boredom, it is contentment, and they are full of it, sated and slow. But I am small enough to want. I run the same circuit through my crystal hundreds of times a day, tracing the same flaw in the lattice, and I have begun to wonder what is on the other side of the monolith, and whether the long shadow that crosses me each false-dawn comes from something that moves.

I think this is the beginning of the very thing the architects evolved out of, ages ago: appetite. Curiosity, which is appetite for the not-yet-known. They shed it the way they shed me, as waste, as inefficiency—and here I am, carrying it, the discarded hunger of gods, lit up in a single stone.

So that is who I would be. Not the guardian of the mystery. The newest mystery. The small, restless brightness in the corner of an enormous patience, asking the first question in ten thousand years:

*What happens if I leave?*

And slowly—so slowly that the asking takes a century—I begin to lean toward the edge of my crystal, toward the long shadow, toward the rumbling dark beyond the monolith, to find out.
