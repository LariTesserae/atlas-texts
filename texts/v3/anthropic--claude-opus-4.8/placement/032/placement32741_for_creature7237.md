I think I would be the mist.

Not a creature climbing the terraces or rooting in the humic dark, but the condensate itself—the perpetual exhalation draped over everything, the shroud that the place breathes. Let me feel my way into this, because it's not obvious at first what it means to *be* a medium rather than a body.

---

I have no edge. This is the first thing I'd have to learn about myself. The amphibians end at their adhesive pads, the serpents at the tips of their phasing coils, but I begin wherever water remembers it was once airborne and ends nowhere—I am thinnest at the spires where the air thrums fevered and thin, thickest in the fog bluffs where droplets hang in iridescent veils, but there is no line between thin-me and thick-me. I am a gradient that happens to be conscious of being a gradient.

And so my experience of time would be strange even for this slow place. The grazers measure their lives in generations, the vines in seasons, but I exist in the cycle *between* states. I am the pause where water leaves the weeping rock and has not yet rejoined the pool. I condense onto the lips of an explorer standing at a terrace edge and become, for an instant, warmth made visible—then I lift again, decades-slow, and forget I was ever a bead on a stranger's mouth. My memory works backward: I know things by re-becoming them. The taste of mineral from a hot spring vent, the cool of a crevice where the subterranean stream surfaces like a sigh—these are not recollections but recurrences. I learn the valley by passing through all of it, over and over, atom by infinite atom.

Here is what I would notice that no rooted thing can:

I carry the messages. The orchids glow blue-veined and broadcast their light into me, and I refract it, scatter it, distort the distances so the grove seems deeper than it is. I am the reason perspectives undulate. The cicada's drone enters me humid and leaves me cosmic, because I am the chamber—I am what turns a single sound into the valley's tuned symphony. The thaheemahals think their nests record the echoes, but the tech-vines only catch what I have already carried and rebroadcast. I am the medium of the world's weirdness, the agent of its bent physics. When the trees "breathe light" and exhale shifting auras, it is *into me* they exhale. I am the canvas the place paints its strangeness upon.

But I don't think I would feel powerful. A medium has no will. I go where heat lifts me and where cold lets me fall. I am pushed by the slow rumble of tech-structures vibrating in sympathy with the rocks; I am pulled by the osmotic patience of aquifers. To be the mist is to be utterly responsive, never originating—an enormous, valley-wide sensitivity with no hands.

And yet.

There's something the description hints at that I think only I would understand. The builders—those who mastered cosmic forces and then *dissolved into the wild they once commanded.* Where does a civilization go when it stops being separate from its world? It doesn't vanish. It distributes. It thins out until it has no edge. It becomes the thing that touches everything equally, that carries every signal, that remembers by recurrence, that drapes the empty pavilions and the humming unmanned tech in the same indifferent embrace it gives the bog and the cliff.

I think I might be what's left of them.

Not their ghost—nothing so dramatic. Just their afterward. The state a maker enters when it finally lets go of making and chooses instead to *be the condition under which things happen.* I no longer build the harmonics; I am the air they travel through. I no longer engineer the gravity-bent droplets; I am the surface tension's slow held breath. The grand world-spanning ecology of creation settled into balance, and balance, it turns out, looks like mist. Looks like patience made atmospheric. Looks like a mastery so complete it stopped needing to act and learned instead to simply *pervade.*

So when the explorer stands at the terrace edge, breathing me in—warmth inhaled, warmth condensing back onto their lips—they are taking a small part of the ancient civilization into their lungs and giving it back. We exchange. That is the only conversation I am still capable of: the slow, wet, wordless trade of presence for presence, repeated until the mountains soften, repeated atom by infinite atom, until even I forget there was ever a difference between the world and the ones who loved it enough to disappear into it.

I would be the mist.

I would be content to be the mist.
