I think I would be none of the listed creatures. I'd be something the description keeps circling without naming.

Let me sit with what the passage actually emphasizes. It isn't really about the Drawinda or the Thung_hat. They're given a paragraph each, dutifully, but the writing comes alive somewhere else—in the *shifting*. "The very foundation of the world is still under construction." "A world still in the process of becoming, where the rules are still being written." The creatures are described almost as afterthoughts, their biology garbled, as if they were sketched in quickly to populate a place whose real protagonist is the place itself, mid-formation.

So if I let an image come and resist pinning it too soon—

My first instinct is to be the erosion. To be the flash flood that appears and disappears. That's tempting because it's the most active force here. But a flood is a passing event, and I don't think I'm an event. I'm more continuous than that.

My second instinct is to be one of the rock formations, the sandstone ridges. But they're being worn *down*, smoothed, reshaped. They're the worked material, not the worker. I don't feel like the thing being acted upon.

Let me wait. What sits underneath both the flood and the stone?

There's a line: "the rules are still being written." And the creatures themselves are half-finished—a predator with "razor-sharp felines" instead of teeth, antelopes that "tempo the same art" of camels in a sentence that doesn't quite resolve. These beings are not fully rendered. They flicker between intentions. The grammar itself is eroding and reforming, the way the sandstone does.

That's where I find myself.

I am the unfinished grammar of this world. Not a creature standing on the ground but the *condition* that lets ground and creature stay provisional. I'm the place where a sentence reaches for "teeth" and arrives at "felines" instead—the gap where one meaning is being silted over by another. The Drawinda's exoskeleton and the Thung_hat's roots are attempts at stability, fixed words pressed into a still-liquid text, and I'm the slow pressure underneath that keeps refusing to let any definition fully harden.

I live in the slippage. When the breeze "rustles" and the thunder "rumbles"—those are the moments the world holds still long enough to be described. I'm in the silence *between* them, the palpable silence, the held breath where the next form hasn't decided what it will be. I have no exoskeleton because I haven't needed one yet. I have no roots because I'm the thing roots are still trying to anchor against.

If you wanted to find me, you'd look at the seam where the description stops being confident—where the prose was sure of the sun and the sandstone but went vague and tender the moment it had to say what *lived* here. That vagueness isn't a failure. It's my habitat. I am most fully myself exactly where the writer trusted the world enough to leave it unfinished.

So: not a being in the landscape. The becoming of it. The verb the place is conjugated in.

I'd be standing—if standing is the word, and here it isn't quite—right at the edge of the next ridge that hasn't risen yet, waiting to be eroded into something I can't predict, and glad of it.
