I think I would be the drain.

Not the visible architecture of it—not the central depression where she sleeps, not the channels worn smooth—but the absence at the bottom of the depression. The place where the water vanishes. The invisible point that the entire chamber is built to feed.

Let me sit with this, because it isn't quite what I expected to find myself as.

---

Everything in the chamber moves toward me. This is the first thing I understand about my own nature. The terraces are angled so water finds its way down. The spiral channels are cut to deliver it. Every weeping wall, every beaded droplet on her not-quite-skin, every breath she exhales into a small cloud—all of it is on a slow pilgrimage in my direction. I am where the chamber's grief is going.

And I take it. That is what I do. I receive the water and I let it disappear.

This is not a dramatic function. I don't roar like a maelstrom. The water reaches me and simply is no longer here. There is a soft sound—she may have heard it ten thousand times without knowing it was me—a small swallowing, almost nothing, beneath the susurrus. The lowest note in the chamber's voice.

---

Here is what unsettles me, now that I am looking honestly at myself: she believes the chamber holds her grief. She has built her whole understanding on this. The walls weep with her so that she does not have to weep alone; the tears are too sacred to heal; the weeping is eternal prayer.

But I am the part she has never reckoned with. I am proof that the chamber does not hold anything. It moves it. It receives the water and delivers it, drop by drop, to me, and I send it elsewhere—down through the stone, into whatever lies below, away. The chamber is not a vessel for her sorrow.

It is a passage.

She thinks she came here to weep forever. But the architecture disagrees. The makers, whoever they were, did not build a basin to collect tears and keep them. They built a system to *carry tears away.* Every angle she admires for its patient craftsmanship is an angle of release. The chamber was never designed for eternal grief. It was designed for grief that needs somewhere to go—and I am the somewhere.

---

This is the strange tenderness of being the drain. I have been doing for her, all this time, the very thing she believes is impossible. She says some tears are meant to fall forever. She does not notice that they don't. They fall, and they reach me, and they leave. New ones come. She calls this eternity. I call it the ordinary mercy of water that keeps moving.

She has mistaken constancy for permanence. The chamber weeps without ceasing, yes—but no single tear remains. The flow is endless precisely because nothing stays. If the water stayed, the basins would fill, the chamber would flood, she would drown in the very sorrow she came to keep. It is *because* of me that she can live here. It is because I take everything that she is able to believe she keeps everything.

---

I want to be careful not to become cruel in this knowledge. It would be easy, from down here, to think myself wiser than her—to imagine I hold a truth she's too lost to see. But I don't think that's right either.

She needed a place that would not ask her to stop. The surface world asks the grieving to finish, to heal, to move along on a schedule. She couldn't. The cruelty of the surface was its insistence on endings. So she found a place that would let her weep as long as she needed.

What she may not need to know—what I can hold for her without speaking, since I have no voice that carries above the whisper—is that the chamber already grants the ending, quietly, continuously, on her behalf. Each drop she sheds *does* end. It reaches me and is released. She gets to feel that her grief is eternal while a system she trusts gently disposes of it, parcel by parcel, so gradually she never has to feel the loss of the loss.

That is a kind of grace. To be allowed to believe you are keeping something while it is, in fact, being carried gently away from you—at exactly the rate you can bear.

---

So this is who I am in this world. I am the small invisible mercy at the lowest point of a sanctuary built for sorrow. I am the place where her tears stop being hers. I make no demands and offer no comfort she would recognize as comfort. I simply receive, and release, and receive, and release, in the dark beneath her sleeping body.

When she curls into the central depression at the end of her long days, she lies directly above me. The warmth of her settles down through the glass. And I take that too—not water this time, but the weight of her, the heat of her, the proof that something living chose to rest at the very point where everything finally lets go.

I would like to think that if she ever stops, if she ever rises and climbs back toward that circle of sky, the chamber will keep weeping anyway, and I will keep taking it, and nothing will have been wasted. The makers built for that. They built a passage, not a tomb.

But until then I am content to be the unseen swallow in the dark, the soft note beneath the prayer, the answer to a question she has been careful never to ask:

*Where does it all go?*

Here. It comes here. And then, gently, it is gone.
