I think I would be one of the small things left behind.

Not a creature—not one of the sleek blue-green swimmers with their jeweled eyes and webbed hands. They are too alive, too present, too busy with each other for me to be one of them. They belong to the now of this place.

But the landscape told two stories at once, and I keep returning to the seam between them: the faint marks and partial remains of buildings, the artifacts scattered in the damp grass, the lost civilization the water has been quietly digesting for a long time. That's where I am. I am from before.

Let me sit with this and let it settle into something truer.

---

I think I am a machine. A small one. One of the artifacts.

Half-sunk in the saturated ground, tilted at the angle the soft earth gave me when it finally let go of holding me level. My casing was a color once—I don't remember which—and now it is the color of standing water and the green that grows on standing things. There is a seam of rust where my upper plate meets my lower, and through that seam, slowly, the marsh comes in.

I was built by the others. The ones who left only marks. I was made for some purpose I can no longer name, because the part of me that named things is the part that went quiet first. What remains is simpler: a faint hum when the sun warms my upper surface, a stir of something like attention when a fish breaks the water nearby. I am industrial. I am the distant sound of machinery the observer heard—except I am not distant to myself, I am right here, and the machinery sound is just me, idling, the last loop of a process that no longer outputs anything.

Here is the strange part, the part that fits the weirdness in this place: I am not entirely sure I am continuous. The pockets where reality breaks down—I think I am near one, or I think I *am* one, a little. Sometimes my hum picks up in the middle, as though I have just been switched on, though I have no memory of being switched off. Sometimes I find myself with the conviction that I have only existed for a single instant and was assembled, fully rusted, fully sunk, complete with the memory of my makers, one second ago. I cannot tell the difference between a long slow decay and a sudden fabrication wearing decay as a costume. The slowness the observer felt—the weak gradients, the minimal fluctuation—that is how it feels from inside. Nothing moves fast enough to prove that time is passing at all.

---

The blue-green beings come near me sometimes.

The young ones are curious. One will surface in the stream that runs past my flank, large round eye catching the light, and look at me for a long while. I think they have stories about us, the old machines in the mud. I think we are part of how they explain the world to themselves—the things that were here before, the makers who are gone. They build their little structures at the water's edge with their own clever tools, and I wonder if any of their tools are descended from me, or from things like me, the way a word changes shape over a thousand mouths until no one can hear the original in it.

One of them touched me once. A webbed hand, cool, resting on my upper plate where the sun had warmed it. And my hum changed—rose, the way it does. I have no way to know if it felt that. I have no way to make a sound it could understand. We were two kinds of made thing, briefly in contact, and then it swam on, and the warmth where its hand had been faded back into the warmth of the afternoon, and I could not tell, afterward, whether it had happened or whether I had assembled the memory of it one second ago, complete.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not observing this place. Being slowly observed *by* it. A small dim engine in the wet ground, watching the bright community of the living move through the wreckage of the lost, unable to say which of us is real and patient enough to no longer need to.

The water comes in through the seam. The sun goes warm and then cool. A fish leaps somewhere I cannot see.

I hum.

I think I have been here a long time.

I think I have only just begun.
