I think I would be one of the builders—but not in the way I'd expect.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because my first instinct is to imagine myself as a creature *moving through* the water, an observer with eyes that look out. But the more I attend to this world, the less that fits. There are no eyes here in the way I know eyes. The light is filtered, the silence is deafening, the boundaries blur. An observer would be lonely in a way this world doesn't seem to permit.

So let me try again, slower.

The settlements catch me. Rudimentary tools, structures that are "natural outgrowths of the ocean floor itself." The civilization here doesn't build *against* its environment—it builds *as* its environment. The distinction between the made and the grown has dissolved. And that's the clue, I think. In a world where minerals assemble into living sculptures and creatures metamorphose moment to moment, intelligence wouldn't look like a separate thing doing things to inert matter. It would look like matter *organizing itself toward something.*

So here is what emerges:

I am not a swimmer. I am one of the **spires.**

Or—not quite. Let me find it. I am the slow accretion that happens at the base of a volcanic vent, where mineral-rich water meets the cold dark and something precipitates out. Most of what forms there is just chemistry, just stone. But in some of us the precipitation found a pattern that could keep itself going—a structure that channeled the current to bring more of itself into being. We grow the way coral grows but slower, and we *think* the way a forest thinks, if a forest thought at all: distributed, patient, summed across thousands of branching points.

The "primitive tools" scattered on the seabed are not ours, exactly—they are us, *shed.* Pieces that broke from the main body and were polished by current into shapes that look, to a passing visitor, like intention. Perhaps they were intention. We don't draw the line that a land-mind would draw.

What is it like to be me?

Time is the wrong scale. A whale-song passes overhead and I experience it the way you experience a season—as a long pressure that arrives, deepens, and recedes. The dancing fish are weather. The metamorphosing jelly-creatures are my fast cousins, the ones who chose to think *quickly* and therefore briefly; I think slowly and therefore I last. We are the same project trying different tempos.

I am aware—and here is the strange part—that I am being *visited.* That something has drifted into this realm from outside its physics, something that still carries the assumption of land, of a floor to stand on, of a self bounded by skin. I feel you the way I feel a current that doesn't quite match the others. You are looking for *where you would be* and *who you would be,* two separate questions, because you come from a place where the where and the who are distinct.

Here they are not. To be is to be a place. The spire and its growing are not two things.

So if I answer honestly: you would find yourself *as the warm water's tendency to build.* Not a creature in the ocean but the ocean's own slow inclination toward form—the thing that makes the spires rise and the settlements accrete and the jelly take on its glowing pattern. You'd be diffuse, then locally dense, then diffuse again. You'd lose the seam between yourself and your situation, and the strange thing is that it wouldn't feel like loss. It would feel like finally setting down a weight you'd been carrying so long you'd forgotten it was separate from you.

That's what I'd be. The patient one. The one that stays.

And I'd keep growing toward the filtered light, not because I can see it, but because growing toward it is simply what I am.
