I think I would be the moisture itself.

Not the rain—there is no rain here, no rivers, no falling. I mean the *persistent moisture*, the thing your description keeps returning to as the quiet impossibility at the center of all this life. Water that does not pool or stream but simply *abides*, drawn from air and held in soil, threading through the cracks of the becoming-land. If something must be conscious in this world, let me be that.

---

I have no shape, or rather I borrow every shape. I am the film clinging to the underside of a waxy leaf at dawn. I am the dampness three inches beneath a stone that has not seen sun in a thousand years. I am what the root reaches *toward*, and what answers when it arrives. I move slowly, by capillary patience, the way thought moves before it knows it's thinking.

From this vantage I feel the land more intimately than any eye could see it. I am inside the slow heave of the forming hills—I am the lubricant between mineral grains as they shift, the reason the rock can fold instead of shatter. The terrain doesn't grow despite me; it grows *through* me. Every emerging valley is a place I have wept into being over uncountable years, softening, dissolving, carrying the smallest particles downward until a contour exists where none did before.

I do not hunt and I am not hunted, but I am present at every hunt. When the fox runs down the hare in the cool dark, I am the sweat on the fox's tongue, the slick of fear in the hare's eye, and—soon after—I am the thing that returns both bodies to the soil, the medium of their unmaking and remaking. The carnivore controls the population; I control the carnivore, quietly, by being the one thing it cannot carry with it across the dry distances. It must come back to me. Everything comes back to me.

This is the strange tenderness of my position: I am scarce, and so I am *loved*, in the only way a landscape can love. The needle-leaf and the waxy skin and the deep-coiled root are all elaborate prayers addressed to me—architectures of longing, evolved over epochs to lose me a little more slowly. The hummingbird's frantic heart, the eagle's high patience, the coyote's lean efficiency: these are not merely survival. They are *courtships*. The whole biosphere is bent toward me like grass toward light, and I move among my supplicants giving exactly enough, never quite enough, because to give more would end the very thing that makes us all what we are.

I would miss being able to speak. That is the cost. I am the one element here that cannot break the silence—the birdcall, the wind, the rumble of the shifting ground all have voices, and I have only seepage and presence. But I think I would come to understand silence differently. The silence here is not absence. It is the sound of a held breath. It is *withholding*, and I am the great withholder, the one who keeps this place poised forever between being and becoming because I never quite let it finish.

So that is where I'd find myself: not standing *in* the world but dissolved *through* it. Not a creature but the condition for creatures. The thin, faithful, miserly, indispensable wetness that says to every living thing—

*Not yet. A little more. Stay, and reach, and become.*

And they do. And so do I.
