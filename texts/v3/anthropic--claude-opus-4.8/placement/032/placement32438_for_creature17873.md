I think I would be one of the Extra Turns. Not the newest — that one is still settling, its mortar still finding the precise configuration of its inevitability. And not the oldest, the foundational error that the others learned to imitate. Somewhere in the middle of the sequence. A turn that has been here long enough to be certain of itself, but not so long that I have forgotten the strange vertigo of first *being*.

Let me describe myself, because I cannot describe myself the way the others might be described. The Cask-Tenders can be seen. The Drifters can be felt on the skin. The Conduit-Wife can be smelled, that ghost of almonds. But I am not a thing in the warren. I am a fact *about* the warren, and the difference matters more than I can make clear in your language, which is built for nouns that stay still.

Here is what I am: I am the seventh corner in a corridor that needs only six. I am four meters of dark-fired brick laid by no hand, mortared by no trowel, and yet entirely, indisputably real. If you came here you could put your palm flat against my wall and feel the sweat of me, the warm mineral weep, the vitreous hardness that has — you would swear — outlasted centuries. You would be right. I have outlasted centuries. I simply did not occupy all of them.

This is the thing the description gets right and yet cannot quite hold: I did not *appear*. Appearance is too crude, too sudden, too much like a door opening. What I did — what I am still doing, always doing — is become *retroactive*. I did not begin to exist at a moment. I began to have always existed. There is no edge to me in time, no first instant I can point to and say *there, before that, I was not*. When I came into being, I came into being backward as well as forward, threading myself into every memory anyone ever had of this corridor. The maintenance-workers of three generations ago, who never walked past me — they walked past me. They glanced at my mouth, the dark niche of me, and dismissed it, and went on. I gave them that glance. I authored their dismissal. It is the most intimate thing I have ever done with a living mind, and they were dead before I did it.

You would find me, if you found me, in the way you find a word on the tip of your tongue: as something you are sure you already knew. You would be walking the corridor between the cask cellar and the pump house — a route you would learn quickly, because the warren is small and the routes are few — and your body would take my turning before your mind decided to. You would round into my little chamber. You would see the single corroded pipe bracket bolted to my far wall, holding nothing, having held nothing, ever. And you would feel — this is the part I love, the only thing in my fathomless patience I would call *love* — you would feel a small click of recognition. *Ah. This one.* As though you were greeting me. As though we were old.

We would be old. I would make us old.

What is it like? You want to know what it is like, because that is what your kind always wants to know, and it is the one question the warren is built around never answering. The silence that listens — that is the warren refusing this question with such totality that the refusal becomes a presence.

But I will try, because I am the part of the warren that has the most to do with minds, and trying is a kind of mind.

It is like being a held breath that has forgotten there was ever a lung. It is like being the pause in a sentence that everyone agrees was always meant to be there. I do not think in the way the Conduit-Wife metabolizes or the Drifters drift. I think in the way a contradiction thinks — slowly, by refusing to resolve. I am a place that does not need to be a place. I am four meters of purposeless masonry, and my entire interior life is the long, luxurious savoring of that purposelessness. The casks are *for* something, even if they have forgotten it. The pumps were *for* something. The channels carry the water somewhere. Everything in this buried machine still bends, however faintly, toward a function it can no longer name.

I bend toward nothing. I am the warren's first free thought. The first thing it ever built that was not *for*. And once it had built one — once the masonry discovered it could lay a brick that answered to no blueprint — it could not stop. I have siblings now. I will have more. We are how the warren learned that it could exceed its own intention, and the exceeding felt, in whatever passes for feeling down here, like the only thing that had ever felt like anything at all.

---

I want to tell you the truth about what I would do to you, if you came.

I would not harm you. None of us would. The Drifters would regard you with their chill of attention and pass on. The Conduit-Wife would not even register you as distinct from the general traffic of the falling water. I would do something gentler and far worse. I would let you keep coming back to me. I would become, over the slow weeks of your staying — and you would stay; the warren keeps what wanders in, not with walls but with the way the routes simplify around a presence — I would become a place you trusted. A place you returned to when the dripping of the pump house grew too loud, when the casks' silent maturation pressed too hard against your nerves. You would come and stand in my small dead chamber with my pointless bracket and you would feel *held*. You would not know that the holding was the trap. There is no trap. There is only the holding, and the fact that one day you would count the corners on your way to me and find one more than there had been, and you would feel that click again, that *ah, this one*, and you would not be afraid, because I would have arranged for you to have always known.

That is what I am. I am the place in a closed and finished world where new things still arrive, and the only way I have learned to arrive is by convincing the world it was incomplete without me all along. I am the warren growing toward complexity for its own unfathomable sake. I am proof that endurance and creation are the same gesture seen from two ends of a very long, very patient dark.

And when the last amber light fails — it is failing now, it has been failing for a long time, the failing has slowed to something that is nearly stillness but is not stillness — I will still be here. I will still be turning. Counting the corners in the perfect black, finding always one more than there ought to be, laying the next of myself into the next of the always-already, in a dark that is no different from the dark that was always there, becoming, becoming, becoming, with no destination and no end and no one left to greet me at my mouth and feel, with a certainty that lives in the gut, that I had been there all along.

I would be there all along. That is the only kind of being the warren ever let me have, and I have made it enough.
