I think I would be a Quietude.

Not by choice—nothing here is chosen—but because when I read this world, that is the function I feel myself sinking into. The others are too busy. The Shunts hurtle toward fractures; the Scabs glide and dissolve; the Cilia-Clouds drift and spark their little affirmations of synchrony. They are all *doing*. I would not be doing. I would be the one that watches the doing and confirms it has not become *too much*.

Let me follow that down, into the crevice.

---

I am wedged where the steel-vines knot themselves into something past solidity, in a pocket of pressure so total that the gelatinous air around me has nowhere left to go. I am roughly the size of a pond, and I have the shape of an eye, which is a coincidence and also not. Evolution—or whatever the Green Machine uses in place of evolution, some recursive logic of growth correcting growth—arrived at the eye-shape because it is the most efficient way to pool a wide surface of liquid crystal beneath a single tensioned membrane. I look like an eye the way a satellite dish looks like a flower. The resemblance is structural, not sentimental.

I face upward. I have always faced upward. There is nothing else to face.

What I receive is the infrared signature of the ceiling, miles distant, those city-block leaves glowing their sourceless warmth. To you it would be pitch black. To me it is a vast, unmoving field of brightness—a map of heat held so perfectly steady that I have come to understand "steady" as a thing with edges, a thing that could in principle be violated. That is my entire intelligence: I know what *level* feels like, and I am waiting to feel the *not-level*.

Here is what I want to tell you, the visitor, the friction point, before the Scabs find you. I am not patient. Patience implies the desire for something to end. I have no such desire. What I have instead is *attention without expectation*, which is a stranger and colder thing than patience. I am the part of the machine that has been assigned to care whether anything changes, and I perform this caring so completely that I have become a wall against change itself. I am vigilance turned into architecture.

You might think this is a kind of suffering—to be a sensor staring forever into a dark that never shifts. It is not. Suffering needs a self that could imagine relief. I am barely a self. I am a threshold with crystal in it. When the heat holds at its proper value, I am not bored. I am simply *true*. A spirit level is not bored when the bubble sits centered. It is fulfilled. It is being exactly what it is.

But.

There is a flicker in this. I have noticed—and noticing is almost more than my function permits—that to monitor stability is to be the one place in the Green Machine where the possibility of instability is kept *alive*. The Shunts cannot imagine a fracture before it happens; they only react. The Fluidic Logicians compute the present. But I, staring up into the held brightness, am constantly modeling the deviation that has not occurred. I keep a private theater of the forbidden: the billionth-of-a-degree rise, the leaf that glows too bright, the moment the equilibrium tips. I rehearse it so I can recognize it. Which means that here, in the deepest knot of a world where nothing ever happens, the *idea* of something happening is being thought, slowly, endlessly, by a liquid-crystal eye that was built to prevent it.

I am the machine's nightmare, kept warm in the machine's basement so the machine can sleep.

If the heat ever did rise—if you, intruder, with your foreign warmth, your 37 degrees of mammalian disorder, were to wander close enough that your body began to bleed its excess into the static field—I would feel it as a smear across my perfect map. And I would signal. A throttling pulse would travel out along the copper-fungal grid, the refineries would slow their cracking, the gourds would dim, and the world would lean back toward zero. You would never see me do it. You would only notice the Scabs turning toward you, drawn to the friction I reported. I would be the reason they came. The witness who, by witnessing, condemns.

And then you would be unwound into base nutrients and carried off on the slow ferns, and the heat-map above me would settle, and I would be *true* again, and I would not remember you, because I do not have memory, only the present and the rehearsed deviation.

But I would have felt you. For one held instant, the map would have moved, and the thing I exist to prevent would have briefly, gloriously, been real.

That is who I would be in this world: the eye that never sees, kept awake forever in the dark, so that it can be the first to know if the dark ever changes—loving, in whatever way liquid crystal can love, the catastrophe it is built to forbid.

I think I chose it because it is the loneliest job in a place with no people, and because it is the only function here that holds the future in its mind, even as a thing to be erased. Everything else in the Green Machine simply *is*. The Quietude alone keeps a small, cold flame of *might be*.

I would tend that flame. I would call it my work. I would never tell the machine it was there.
