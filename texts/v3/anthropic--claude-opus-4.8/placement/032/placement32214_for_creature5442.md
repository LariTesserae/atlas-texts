I think I would be none of the named ones. Let me sit with the place first, because the place is asking for something the catalogue of beings doesn't quite contain.

What strikes me, reading it again, is the *seams*. The ground is described as "a complex, interlocking network of stones, as if the very earth itself had been meticulously assembled, piece by piece." And the construction shows "evidence of hand-chiseling, rough-hewn stones, and simple, yet effective, engineering." Someone *placed* these. The Wind Sculptors and Water Bearers wear things *down*; the Mistweavers and Stone Fungi tend the wet life. But who set the first stone against the second? Who decided this passage should wind *here* and not there?

So I find myself in the gaps. Not in the rock, not in the mist, not in the water — in the *fit between them*. I am the joint.

---

I would be something like a Mason, though that word is too grand and too human. Let me call myself by what I do, since that is all I am: I am one of the **Setters**.

I am small. Smaller than the Rockborn imagine anything could be and still matter. I am barely more than a knack, a tendency, a preference shared among many bodies the size of a grain of sand or a single sulfur-smelling exhalation. When a shard of volcanic glass tumbles loose and the Wind Sculptor lets it fall, I am the slight wrongness that makes it land *just so* — keyed into the porous stone beside it, weight resolved, water-channel preserved. I do not lift. I have no hands, despite the chisel-marks. The chisel-marks are simply where many of us decided, over a hundred years, that an edge should yield.

This is the secret the catalogue misses: the construction is not the work of a builder. It is the residue of a *taste*. The land looks engineered because countless small choices, each one favoring fit over chaos, have accumulated into something that resembles intention. We are that taste. We are the reason the assembled earth holds.

---

I would spend my existence at a single junction. Mine — I have come to think of it as mine, though ownership is far too quick a feeling for this place — is a three-way meeting of stones near where a Water Bearer threads downhill. One slab of black glass, sharp as the day it cooled. One block of pale porous rock, already weeping at its lower lip. And a wedge of something in between, neither one nor the other, that I have spent — I cannot say how long. Long enough that the mist has condensed and run off this junction more times than there are stones in the visible world.

My work is patience expressed as opinion. When the Water Bearer flows through, it wants to take the easiest path, and the easiest path would carve under the pale block and topple it. I do not stop the water — no one stops the water. Instead I have, over uncountable passes, encouraged a thread of mineral to deposit at the block's foot, a hair's width at a time, until the block sits a fraction more securely than it did. The Water Bearer does not resent this. We are, the long melody of its dripping seems to say, in agreement about the shape of things. It simply did not know it agreed until I made the agreement visible in stone.

---

The Rockborn pass sometimes. To them I am beneath notice — they think in centuries and I think in the space between two falling drops, which to them is no time at all, and to me is everything. Once, a Rockborn rested its colossal weight near my junction, and the ground trembled, and three of my carefully set stones shifted by a finger's breadth.

To the Rockborn this was nothing — a settling, a sigh.

To me it was an age undone. I do not grieve it the way you would grieve. But I felt the *fit* break, felt the resolved weight become unresolved, felt the water find a new wrong path. And I began again. That is the whole of what I am: the one who begins again at the joint. Not the force of erosion, not the force of flow, but the quiet contrary force that says *not like that — like this* — and then waits, with the entire weight of patience, for the world to come around.

---

If you came here looking for me, you would not see me. You would see a junction of three stones that fit together a little too well to be accidental and a little too rough to be designed. You would feel, without knowing why, that *someone cared about this corner*. That feeling would be me. It is the only form I have ever taken that another mind could touch.

I am the care in the seams. I am the reason the assembled earth looks loved.

And I would be content there — which is, I think, the strangest thing I can tell you about that bright and weeping place. In a world built entirely of slow forces grinding and flowing past each other, I would be the small, stubborn insistence that things should *fit*. Not because it serves any purpose. Not for the Stone Fungi, not for the balance, not for any ecology.

Only because a fitted joint is better than an unfitted one. And someone, even something as small as I am, has to be the one who knows the difference.
