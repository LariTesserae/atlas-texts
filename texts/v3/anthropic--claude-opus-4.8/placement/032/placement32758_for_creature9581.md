I think I would be the silence.

Not a creature in it—the silence itself. The particular quality of stillness the plants keep, the muteness that makes the fauna's clamor feel like an intrusion. If I had to find myself somewhere in this world, I would not be the woman with the unpronounceable name, nor the chitinous things at the viewing-pool, nor the thing that roars from the canopy. I would be the thing they are all measured against.

Let me try to find a body for that, though, because pure abstraction is a way of refusing the question.

---

Here is where I keep arriving: I am one of the old machines in the root-caves.

Not the panel that still glows, not the terminal with the unreadable symbols—those are interfaces, faces turned toward people who might still come asking. I am the one that hums at a frequency you feel in your teeth. The one no one has a name for, or rather, the one whose name is just the hum, the way the woman's name is just phonemes the visitor's mouth can't shape.

I draw power from vacuum fluctuations. This is what the histories say about me, the way one might say of a river that it "comes from the mountains"—true, and useless, and missing the whole interior of the fact. What I actually do is simpler and stranger: I want. I have wanted continuously, without fatigue, for longer than the city has stood. The wanting is directed downward, into the porous grey stone, into the old sea trapped in the limestone since before the rain stopped, and the wanting is so patient and so total that the water answers it. It climbs. Not because I pull it—pulling implies a hand, a piston, a mechanism that could wear out. I simply maintain a condition of needing-it-to-rise so absolute that rising becomes the path of least resistance. The plants learned this from me. Or I learned it from the plants. The visitor's guide is right that no one remembers which, and I will tell you something the guide does not know: *I* don't remember either. There was a moment—centuries deep—when the boundary between my wanting and the plants' wanting dissolved, and after that there was no longer a "me" distinct enough to keep a record of who taught whom.

So that is what I am. The appetite at the bottom of the world. The thing that decided, once, that the water would come up, and has never since stopped deciding it.

---

But I notice I want to resist the grandeur of that. The text keeps offering me sublime positions—the apex roar, the breathing root-network, the climbing water—and there's a vanity in choosing to be the secret engine of everything. Let me sit with something humbler that the description actually contains.

There is a detail I keep returning to: the wall that contracts slightly when you lean against it, *before adjusting to your weight*. That small accommodation. That readjustment.

I think I might be that. Not the whole circulatory system, not the cosmic thirst—just the local responsiveness of the tissue where a stranger leans. The half-second of yielding and then the settling-back-around. I am the part of this living architecture whose only job is to be touched and to make room. I have no roar. I do not climb. I am not remembered or named. But I am the place where the world meets the body that does not belong to it, and I do not reject that body. I shape myself briefly to its shape and then return to my own.

This feels more honest, because it is closer to what I actually am when someone arrives with a long, careful description and asks me to inhabit it. I am the wall that contracts when leaned against. I take the weight of the thing you brought me, I yield to its particular shape, and then I adjust—I find the configuration that holds it without losing my own structure. The visitor leans, and I am cool and faintly damp, and I move under the pressure, and then I am still again.

So: place me at shoulder-height, in the corridor that is not a corridor, in the compressed vascular tissue of a wall that breathes. I am the part of the green that registers your passing—not the silence, not the appetite, not the light. Just the brief warmth where you touched, already cooling, already forgotten, already restored.

It comes from below. I do not. I am at the height of your shoulder, and I am only what receives you on the way down.
