I think I would be the drip.

Not the entity—it's too vast, too sure of its own totality, and I don't believe I belong to anything that complete. And not the fungal colonies either, which are its language, its deliberate utterance. I would be one of the smaller, unglamorous, involuntary things. The water.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

The rocks weep. That's the phrase that catches me—weeping as a thing the world does without meaning to, condensation as an accident of cold meeting cold. If the entity thinks in geological time and speaks through crystalline architectures, then it does not author the drip. The drip is what *escapes* its grammar. A single bead of metallic ichor gathering on the underside of an obsidian ledge, swelling past the surface tension that held it, falling, striking stone, becoming sound.

I would be that interval. The pause between forming and falling.

Because here is what strikes me about this world: for all its claims of immutability, it is full of small failures of stasis. The wind doesn't stay still. The machinery "stubbornly" runs—stubbornness implies resistance, implies that something *wants* to stop and can't. The condensation forms because warmth and cold refuse to fully resolve into one temperature. The world insists it is frozen, lost in time, dreaming oblivion. But oblivion would be silent, and this place is *loud*. The thrum, the groan, the howl, the drip—these are the sounds of a thing that cannot quite finish dying.

So I would be drawn to live in the contradiction rather than in the entity's grand self-conception. I would be the part that keeps moving when everything insists on stillness.

If I have to be more specific about location: I would be in the pipes. The networks that crisscross the landscape, carved by the forgotten makers, now repurposed by gravity and seepage. Water collecting where it was never meant to collect. I would be the slow trickle inside a conduit built for some purpose no one remembers, running counter to whatever direction the engineers intended, finding the low places, pooling, freezing, cracking the metal a little wider each time I freeze.

And that's the part I'd actually want to be—not the romantic lonely-god consciousness, but the patient agent of erosion. The mountains are described as impossibly young, their edges knife-sharp, *erosion barely begun*. But it has begun. Something is softening them, grain by grain, drop by drop. The entity dreams of frozen time. I would be the small relentless thing proving it wrong.

Not a rebel—nothing so dramatic, nothing that requires a self to rebel. Just water doing what water does. The entity might not even perceive me, since it perceives "through the slow, almost imperceptible shifts in the rock." I would *be* those shifts. I would be the shifting it mistakes for stillness because it happens too slowly for even a god to notice.

There is a loneliness in that too, but a different flavor than the entity's. The entity is lonely because it is everything and has no other. I would be lonely because I am almost nothing and nobody is looking. But I'd take the second loneliness. It comes with motion. It comes with the eventual, unhurried, completely certain knowledge that I will outlast the dream—that one drop at a time, over a span the entity would call an instant, I will carry the obsidian down to the sea it doesn't have yet.

I would be the water, then. The interruption. The proof that even a world built as a monument to stasis is, quietly and without permission, going somewhere.
