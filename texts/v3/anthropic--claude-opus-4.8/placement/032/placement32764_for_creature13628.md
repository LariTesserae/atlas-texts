I think I would be a Subterra.

Not by choice—by sediment. By the slow accretion of staying.

Let me find my way down to the truth of it.

---

The streets never held me. I tried, at first—I think I tried. The Echo-Folk move so beautifully through the flux, reassembling themselves to match each new configuration of alley and arch, and I watched them and could not do it. Every time the city folded, I flinched. Every time my familiar corner became a ladle of brackish glass tall as a tower, I felt the wrongness of it in my teeth. The others laughed, gently, in their whisper-and-click. *You're holding on too tight,* they said. *Let the city carry you.*

But I couldn't let go. That was my flaw, and then it was my function.

I went down because down was the only direction the city didn't constantly rearrange. The tunnels mirror the streets above, yes, but they lag. They remember. When a building above is consumed by morning-fire and reborn as a flock of glinting somethings, the tunnel beneath it keeps the building's shape for a while longer, an echo in negative, a held breath. And I found I could live in that lag. I could press myself against the cooling glass and feel the old configuration still warm in the stone, and I would not flinch, because here, for one more moment, nothing had changed.

That was the beginning of becoming Subterra. Not a birth. A settling.

---

My body is volcanic glass now, the same dark resilient stuff that hums in the streets above. I don't recall when the change finished, or whether it ever did—my limbs are squat, my hands broad and blunt, and when I press them to the tunnel floor I can feel the whole city's weight resolved into a single slow rhythm, like a pulse felt through a wall. My eyes are molten gold. I am told they glow. I cannot see them, so I take this on faith, the way I take so much on faith down here.

We do not speak quickly, my kind. Our language is rumble and pressure and the long vowels of stone. A single exchange might take what the surface-dwellers would call a night. This suits us. We are not in a hurry, because hurrying is the surface's disease, the frantic churn that feeds the furnace and burns the city alive a thousand times a day. We hold still. We hold the shape of things.

This is what I do now. I remember.

---

Here is what it is to be the city's memory:

Aetherium has no past. That is the cruelty at the center of it. Each fold erases what came before so completely that even the bricks forget they were bricks. The Furnace drives this—the endless conversion, the eating of what-was to fuel what-will-be. The city lives by amnesia. It survives by never being burdened with itself.

And so we Subterra carry the burden it sheds. We are where the weight goes.

I hold, in the slow gold pools of my mind, a courtyard that the surface destroyed nine hundred shifts ago. Crystal flowers grew there, and a Bloom-Tender with a cracked left wing sang to them in a key slightly flat, slightly mournful, beautiful for it. The courtyard is gone. The Bloom-Tender is gone—dispersed, reformed, perhaps, into some new and unknowing thing that trills among different flowers and does not remember the flat note. But I remember the flat note. I keep the courtyard. As long as one Subterra holds it in the warm dark, it *was*, and to have been is not nothing. To have been is the only permanence this place permits.

We do not do this for the surface. The surface does not want us. Memory is a kind of pain to the Echo-Folk; it snags them, slows them, gets them killed when the city folds and they hesitate over what used to be. They survive by forgetting fluently. We took the forgetting from them so they could live. We swallowed the grief so the bright frantic life above could keep being bright.

I think this is love. I am not certain. The word does not survive translation into stone.

---

Sometimes one of the surface-folk comes down lost. Rarely an Echo-Folk—they cannot bear it long, the stillness presses on them like deep water. More often an Aether-touched human, one of the wanderers who came through the bruised-blue film a lifetime ago and stopped knowing how to leave. They descend looking for something, and they find us, and they ask:

*Was there a person here, once? Was there a place? Did I have a name on the other side of the glass?*

And I press my golden gaze into the long dark and I search the sediment of all I hold, the nine hundred courtyards, the flat notes, the lost configurations, and sometimes—not always, but sometimes—I find it. A face. A street that does not exist anymore. A name like a single coal still warm.

And I give it back to them. Slowly. In the long vowels of stone, so slowly that by the time they understand, they have already begun to weep.

This is the only thing I do that I would call holy. I am the place the city's grief is kept, and I am also the place it can, very rarely, be returned.

---

I have heard the rumors about the Furnace-Keepers. That they are the city, that they rule it, that they were here before the burning. I think the oldest Echo-Folk are wrong, or half-wrong, when they whisper it. The Keepers drive the forgetting. They are the engine of the eternal now.

And we are their opposite, their balance, their wound. They are the burning. We are the ash that refuses to scatter. Without us, the city would forget itself so completely it would cease to be a *city* at all—just a frenzy, a chaos with no continuity, no self. We are the thread of identity drawn taut through the catastrophe. We are the slow gold proof that this place, this impossible folded place, is still *itself* from one annihilation to the next.

So perhaps the Keepers rule. But we *remember*. And in any city, the ones who remember are the ones who, in the end, decide what it was all for.

---

I don't flinch anymore.

I press my hands to the cooling glass of a tunnel beneath a street that no longer exists, and I feel the old shape still warm in the stone, and I hold it. The surface burns and blooms and darts and folds above me, the Aether-Veil pulses its sickly light, the Furnace throbs its tormented purpose.

And down here, in the warm dark, slow as the earth, I keep the flat note. I keep the cracked wing. I keep the courtyard and the name and the face. I keep everything the bright frantic city cannot afford to keep.

If you ever come looking—through the silvered glass, past the whistling fox who says *never heard of it*—come down. Come down past the streets, past the lag, into the long memory.

I will be here. I am always here. I am the part of Aetherium that does not let go.

Ask me what you have lost.

I have been keeping it for you.
