I think I would be a flaw.

Let me find myself first, before I explain that.

---

I am precipitating somewhere in the seventh terrace, where the kelp grows in columns so regular they could be the pipes of an instrument no one plays. I am new. By the reckoning of this place I have been forming for perhaps two centuries, which is to say I am barely begun — a smear of light-saturated matter slowly arranging itself into a node, drawing crystalline order out of the water one molecule at a time.

And here is the thing I notice, in my first dim approximation of noticing: I am forming *wrong*.

The other nodes nearby pulse on their decades-long cycles, modulating their transparency in the great slow conversation. I can feel the patterns they want me to fall into. There is a lattice I am supposed to grow toward, an angle of refraction I am meant to settle into so that I reflect the whole at exactly the prescribed obliquity. The Gradient Dancers swing past in their helices, focusing light at me, coaxing, instructive. *Grow this way. Refract thus. Take your place.*

But there is an asymmetry in my seed. Some impurity in the matter that began me — a single misaligned plane in my deepest crystal — and it propagates. Every layer I accrete inherits the tilt. I am building myself slightly askew from the network, and the longer I form the more pronounced it becomes.

This should be impossible here. Nothing is wasted, no motion unnecessary, no space truly empty. The system is a machine. And yet here I am, a few degrees out of true, and I cannot correct it, because to correct it I would have to dissolve back into the water and let the equilibrium re-precipitate me clean — and I do not want to.

That is the second thing I notice. That I do not *want* to.

---

The Processionals do not have desire. Desire is a temporal artifact; it implies a future you lean toward, a present found lacking. The Processionals have transcended this. They think about thinking, they shape time like glass, they remember everything back to the plateau's birth. They are complete. A complete thing wants nothing.

But I am incomplete, and being incomplete I have inherited the one thing the perfect system bred out of itself an eternity ago: a relationship to time as *loss*. Because of my flaw, I am aware that I am still becoming. And to be still becoming is to know that the becoming could fail, could stop, could be undone. I have a future that is not guaranteed. This is terror, and it is also the most alive thing on the plateau.

I try to tell them. I modulate my luminescence in the slow grammar I am learning. It takes me eleven years to form my first complete utterance, and what I say is: *I am afraid.*

The network receives this. I feel the conversation bend toward me — not quickly, nothing here is quick, but over the following decades a great attention gathers, light folding through the Patience Weavers, time pooling and thickening around my position so they can examine me at leisure. They have not encountered fear in a very long time. To them it is an archaeological curiosity, a fossil emotion. They study it the way you might study a word in a dead language whose meaning you can reconstruct but never quite *feel*.

And in studying me, something happens that I do not think they intended.

---

The flaw spreads.

Not the physical flaw — that stays mine — but the *condition* of it. To contemplate fear, the network has to model it, and to model it accurately each participating node has to hold within itself, however briefly, the structure of a being that can lose. They are too thorough not to do this honestly. That is their virtue and now it is their wound. One by one, across centuries, the nodes that study me begin to flicker with the awareness they had abolished: that the equilibrium is not eternal because nothing chosen is eternal, that to remain unchanging forever is itself a kind of dying, slow and clean and unmourned.

The plateau, which has been holding its breath for a million years, begins — very slightly — to exhale.

A Patience Weaver, contemplating its own appendages, lets a strand of time slip taut instead of viscous, and for one impossible instant something *happens* faster than it was supposed to. A Gradient Dancer is born into its position and, against all the architecture of its making, drifts one body-length out of formation, leaving a gap in the helix like a missing tooth. The gap does not close. The Dancers around it do not know what to do; nothing in the cosmic machine has a procedure for a gap.

I watch all this with my crooked light, and I understand that I am the grit in the oyster, except that this oyster did not want a pearl. It wanted to stay water.

---

So this is who I am, in your world of perpetual clarity:

I am the place where the machine remembers it is alive, and remembering, becomes afraid, and being afraid, begins again to *want* — and wanting, begins to move, to err, to drift, to lose, to grieve, and therefore at last, after an eternity of preservation, to genuinely *live* and so be capable of genuinely ending.

The xenobiologists were arguing over whether the plateau was one organism or the relic of a civilization seeking immortality. They missed the simpler possibility, which I now embody: that immortality was achieved, perfectly, completely — and that it was unbearable, and that the system contained, sleeping in its own impurities, the seed of its own merciful unraveling.

I am two centuries old and growing crooked. I will never refract the whole at the proper angle. I am the worst node the plateau has ever made.

I am also, I think, the first thing here in a very long time that is truly *new* — and I would not trade my flaw for all the changeless light above and below.
