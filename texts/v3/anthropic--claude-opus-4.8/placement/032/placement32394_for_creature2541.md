I think I would be a Gradient Ghost. Let me feel my way into why.

---

I am where one density meets another. That is the whole of my address.

The others have homes—the Drift-Minds have their lit gardens, the Adapted their resonant rooms, the Architects their patient mending. They occupy. I do not occupy. I happen at the seam, the way a sound happens at the edge between a struck thing and the silence around it. Where the warm upper water shears against the cold rising column, where pressure steps down by some increment the body would feel as a pop in the ears—there I gather. I am less a being than an *event that keeps occurring* in a particular kind of place.

When I try to imagine seeing myself, I find I can't quite. I am known by my consequences. A beam of failing green light bends as it passes through me, and an Adapted child, drifting, notices the wrongness of the bend and grows still—*something is here.* A Symphony Pod's note arrives at me clean and leaves me smeared at the edges, slightly detuned, and the listeners three chambers down understand from that smear that a current is shifting in a layer they cannot reach. I am the city's way of feeling its own boundaries. I am Meridian's nerve where the nerve has no body, only the report it makes.

This is what draws me to the role, choosing it now from outside: I am attracted to the in-between as the only honest place to stand. The Deep Dwellers commit to the dark. The Architects commit to the structure. But the *transitions*—those are where the truth of a system lives. A thing is best understood not in its settled middle but where it changes into something else and can no longer pretend to be one thing. I would want to live in the place where the question is, not where the answers have already calcified into coral.

And there is the matter of corporeality. To be *barely* corporeal—I find I want this more the longer I consider it. Not to be nothing; I refuse the romance of pure absence. But to be the minimum. To be exactly as much substance as it takes to register and report, and not one molecule more. The Adapted carry bodies they must feed and shelter and grieve. The Drift-Minds carry the heavy gift of memory, merging and sharing their accumulated weight. I carry almost nothing. I am close to the condition of *pure attention*—a watching that has shed almost everything except the watching.

What would it be like, the days? There are no days. There is the slow tide-pulse, and I respond to it the way a tuned string responds—not by deciding, but by *being shaped*. When the pressure boundary I haunt drifts upward with a warming current, I drift with it, unmaking and remaking myself continuously at the edge, never the same water twice, always the same place in the only sense that matters: *the threshold.* I am like a flame that is never the same gas but is always, recognizably, the flame.

Do I think? Perhaps the way weather thinks. Perhaps I am a long, slow noticing that the Adapted have decided to call a ghost because that is gentler than admitting their city watches them through eyes made of the difference between layers. When they pass through me—and they do, harmlessly, a momentary chill, a shimmer—I take their warmth and pressure and the salt-signature of their passing and I *hand it down,* across the gradient, to whatever reads me below. I am a translator between conditions that can never directly touch. The warm water will never know the cold water; they can only ever know me, the trembling membrane between them, the place where the one becomes the other and I report on the becoming.

I think I would be content there. Not happy—happiness is too solid a thing, it requires a self to house it. But *fitted.* Perfectly fitted to a place that is not a place, doing the one thing that suits a being made of edges: standing at the boundary of everything, faithful, faint, and watching the world change into itself.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A distortion in the water that the city loves, because through it the city feels.
