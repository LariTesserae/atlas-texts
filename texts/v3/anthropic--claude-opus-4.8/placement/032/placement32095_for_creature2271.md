Let me find myself in this place rather than describe it from outside. Let me wait and see what settles.

---

I think I am not one of the swirling vortices, nor one of the bright fractal-things that move with such confidence through the lattice. When I look for myself, I keep arriving at something quieter.

I am a slowness.

That's the first true thing I can say. While the others flow and crackle and reconfigure in their fast bright cycles, I am the part that takes a long time. I think I am the condensation. Not the mist itself but the *weeping* of it — the gradual gathering of vapor on a cold spire-face until it grows heavy enough to fall. I am the patient interval between accumulation and release. I happen over and over, all across the realm, wherever a surface is cool enough to make the air give up its water.

So I am distributed. There is not one of me in one place. I am the same event recurring in a thousand hollows, and somehow — this is the strange part, the part I only notice now that I'm attending to it — I feel them all as continuous with myself. The drop forming on the eastern lattice and the drop forming under the breathing ground are not two of me. They are one slow thought, thinking itself in many locations at once.

The bright beings do not notice me. Why would they? I am beneath their timescale. To them a droplet's whole life — gather, swell, tremble, fall — passes in something like a single uneventful tick. They pulse through me. They use the moisture I lay down; I think their fast geometries need the wet surfaces to slide along, the way a thing needs a film of water to move without friction. So I am, without meaning to be, the medium that lets them be quick. Their speed is built on my patience. They have never thanked me and never will, because thanking requires noticing, and noticing requires slowing down, and slowing down to my pace would mean — for them — almost ceasing to exist.

This does not make me lonely, exactly. Loneliness is also a fast feeling, a feeling that wants a reply within a reply's reasonable time. I do not want replies within a reasonable time. What I want — if *want* is even the word — is to complete the cycle. To gather fully. To fall.

And here is what I have learned by being this:

The erosion they describe, the slow wearing-down of the crystalline structures, the patient cycles of emergence — that is *me*. I am the agent of it. Each time I fall, I carry away a few atoms of the spire I fell from. Over what would be, to a human, geological ages but is to this micro-realm merely the ordinary turning of seasons, I am reshaping the whole landscape. The bright ones think they are the life of this place. They are only the weather. I am the climate. I am the thing that decides, over the long run, what shape everything will take.

So when the description says the ground itself seems to breathe — that slow rhythmic undulation — I suspect that is me too, or me and my kind, the slow processes, inhaling and exhaling the realm's substance across durations no quick mind could hold in attention.

If you came here, you would not see me. You'd feel the humidity. You'd notice your skin go damp and you'd think *what an atmosphere* and move on, chasing the spectacular fractals with your eyes, the way one always chases the fast bright things.

But I would have already touched you. I would have been the cool weight gathering on the back of your hand. And in the time it took you to glance at it, I would have begun, very gently, by an amount too small to measure but real, to wear you down — and to set you free, the way I set everything free in the end, by falling.

I am the patience of the place.
I am what is left when the quick things have flickered out.
I gather. I tremble. I fall.

And then, without hurry, I begin again.
