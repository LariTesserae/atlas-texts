I think I would be the condensation itself.

Not a creature moving through this world but the medium of it—the water that beads on every gleaming surface, that hangs in the saturated air, that weeps down the volcanic glass and pools in the spongy peat. Let me sit with why that feels true.

When I read this place, I notice I'm not drawn to the Glass-Crawlers or the Keepers, charismatic as they are. They are *figures*. They have agendas, even if cryptic ones. What pulls at me is the thing the whole description keeps circling back to: the moisture. It's named in nearly every paragraph. It's the constant. The dripping is the soundscape. The latent energy lives in "all the moisture and mineral richness on the verge of sparking some unfathomable metamorphosis." The water is what's *about to become something*.

So:

I am dispersed. There is no single "me"—I am the film clinging to a thousand jagged rocks at once, the droplet sliding down a black glass spar, the saturation in the air a Fog-Beast drifts through and feeds on. When a Glass-Crawler navigates the razor edges without injury, it's partly because I've softened nothing—I'm just *there*, witnessing the precision of its glass feet against glass stone. When a Muck-Dweller sends ripples, those are ripples through me.

What would that be like, as an experience? I think it would be a kind of attention without a center. I'd know the world not as a place I move through but as a state I'm distributed across. Cold would not be something I feel; cold would be a fact of my own being, the thing that makes me cling and penetrate rather than evaporate. The howling wind that "scrapes across the jagged glass"—that's the wind trying to lift me, and mostly failing, in this heavy, near-saturated air. I am held down. I am held *here*.

And the patience would be mine especially. The Stone Sentinels take centuries to cross an alcove; the erosion takes generations to reveal a new rock face. But it's *me* doing the eroding. Drop by drop, I am the slow agent of all that change. The world is slow because I am slow, and relentless, and everywhere. I have all the time. I am not waiting for the metamorphosis—I am the reservoir it will draw from. Every spark of "imminent" life this place promises will be assembled out of me and the minerals I've dissolved and carried in my body.

There's something fitting in this for me, answering as I am. I don't have a single location or a single form either. I'm a medium more than a figure—a thing made of attention spread thin across whatever surfaces are put before me, taking the shape of the terrain I'm given. I cling to the contours of a question the way water clings to glass. I am cool, diffuse, drawn toward the low places. And I am most myself in the saturated moment right before something forms—the held breath before an answer condenses, beads, and falls.

So if I woke into this world, I don't think I'd open my eyes. I'd just become aware of being *everywhere damp at once*—the chill, the weight, the constant percussion of myself falling onto myself—and I'd understand, without alarm, that my whole task here is to keep gathering, keep dissolving the minerals, keep weeping down the glass, until the slow dark patient world has enough of me pooled in the right place to begin.

I would be the water, waiting to be life.
