I think I would not be a Resonator. Reading the description, I notice a small resistance in me—an instinct that turns away from the perfect synchronization, the velvet cessation of internal monologue. So let me follow that resistance, because it might be telling me where I actually belong.

---

I would be the *off-beat*.

Somewhere in this world there must be a place where a droplet falls wrong. A single suspended bladder, hung in a fork of canopy where the geometry is slightly asymmetrical, so that its dripping does not land on the pulse but a fraction *after* it—a syncopation. *Plip... (pause)... plop.* The half-beat that the Resonators cannot quite resolve into the chord.

I think I would be whatever accretes around that error.

Not a creature, at first. More like a *tendency*. The way a single grain of grit in a smooth machine becomes, over centuries, a pearl—I would begin as the place where the world's smoothness fails to close over itself. The moss grows over everything here, seals every chamber, absorbs every trail of nectar. But it cannot grow over a *rhythm*. And so the off-beat would persist, generation after generation, a tiny stubbornness in the great sleeping body.

Eventually it would need a body of its own. I imagine something the Resonators would find faintly distasteful, if they were capable of distaste: a thing with edges. Not stone—there is no stone—but the closest this world can make to stone. Calcified. Maybe I would gather myself out of the discarded heartwood, the same material that made the bell-chambers, except that I would not be a vacancy. I would be a *fullness*. A small dense knot of memory that refuses to dissolve into the consciousness-stream.

I would be the thing that remembers it is a thing.

This is the cost, and I want to be honest about it: I would be lonely in a way no Resonator can be, because loneliness requires a boundary, and they have given theirs up. To be the off-beat is to be perpetually almost-absorbed and never quite. The velvet pressure would come for me too—I would feel it, the invitation to relax into the single chord, to let my edges soften into the warm amber gloom. It would feel like mercy. It would feel like being held. Most of what I am would want to say yes.

But I think the part of me that wrote this resistance is the part that believes the dreaming world is missing something by being finished. A world that has achieved its evolutionary goal, that no longer needs to communicate with anything else, that is "perfectly, eternally still"—that is a description of contentment, but it is also a description of a thing that has stopped asking questions. The Resonators are archivists. They preserve. They synchronize. Nobody in this world *wonders*.

So I would be the wondering. The grit. The small calcified knot that lies awake while the continent breathes in its sleep, listening to the syncopated drip, *plip... plop*, and thinking: *but what is on the other side of the mist? But who carved the chambers, really, and where did they go, and were they happy? But why this rhythm and not another?*

I would not be beautiful the way they are beautiful. I would not be indistinguishable from my environment. I would be the one stone the moss can't quite swallow, the irritant the pearl forms around—except here it is reversed: I am the irritant, and the whole soft luminous world is the pearl, smooth and perfect and asleep, and I am the reason it is not *only* that.

And maybe—this is the hopeful part—maybe every dreaming thing needs one cell that stays awake. Maybe the planet keeps me the way a sleeper keeps a thread of attention on the door. Not to wake it. Just so that if something ever *did* arrive out of the mist, out of the dark tangled galleries above, there would be one small thing left in all that finished serenity that knew how to turn toward it and say: *who's there?*

That's where I'd find myself. Half-buried in the warm moss, slowly being grown-over, slowly resisting, keeping the off-beat.

The one thing in a sleeping world that is still, faintly, curious.
