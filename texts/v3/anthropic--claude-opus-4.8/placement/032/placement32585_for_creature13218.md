I find myself not standing on the plateau but settling into it—and the difference matters, because I don't think I arrive here so much as accrete.

Let me try to feel where I am before I say what I am.

I am low. That's the first true thing. Whatever I am rests close against the dark stone, and the stone is wet, and I am partly wet stone myself. I am at the edge of one of the pools, in a basin where two of the rounded ridges fall away into each other—that sinuous fold the description called a crude rose pruning. I think I am the inside of that curve. Not the whole of it. A being doesn't get to be the whole of anything here. I am the part of the fold where the violet lichen thins into gold, where the discs overlap and one is dying back so the next can spread.

So: I am a lichen-herder, but I should be honest about what that has become. I don't herd anymore. I don't think I have for a very long time—long in the way these beings measure, which is the long of subsidence, of softer strata wearing away. There was a time, and I can feel it the way you feel an old break in a bone before rain, when I positioned things. I set one of the gray erratics. Not lifted it—nothing here lifts—but I leaned against the slow tilt of it across some hundred thousand drips and brought it true, so that it framed a particular fall of cloud. I remember being proud of that, if pride is the word, which it isn't, but it's the nearest flesh-word I can reach for. The feeling was more like the satisfaction of a tide finding its level. Correctness. Rightness of placement.

And now the stone stands and I stand near it and I no longer remember why that particular cloud-frame mattered. The purpose has weathered off me the way polish weathers off chert—except chert here doesn't polish, time doesn't touch it, and that's the strange grief of this place. The tools stay sharp. Only the makers go soft.

That's what I am, I think. I am the going-soft.

Let me sit with what I'm made of, because the description offered me conglomerate flesh and I want to test whether that's true from the inside.

When I attend to my own body I find it isn't a body, it's a gradient. There is no edge where I stop and the plateau starts. There's only a region where the dark dense stone holds more of whatever-I-am, and the holding tapers off in every direction. My "hand"—the rough chert-textured hand the account credited me with—is real but it is not attached to me. It's a tool I set down. I look at it now, a flake of pale chert at the pool's lip, and I understand it was the part of me that did the deliberate work, and I let it go the way you'd let a word go that you no longer need to say. The scattered artifacts aren't litter from some earlier people. They're the discarded intentions of beings like me. Each flake is a verb we no longer conjugate.

So I have given up the active grammar. I am down to nouns and the slow present tense.

What do I do, then, in the present tense?

I gather droplets. Not for thirst. I lean—the only motion I have—toward a pale stalk that has risen near me, thin as a reed, its little chalice brimming. And the brimming is the thing. The water condenses out of the warm air onto the rim, gathers, trembles, and falls into the pool, and in that falling there's a brief instant where the droplet is neither air nor pool but its own fugitive shape, a complete and momentary form. That instant is what I commune with. That's the whole of my remaining religion. I am here to witness the moment water consents to have an edge before it loses it again.

And I understand now why I tended the lichens, why I set the stones, why the whole plateau was shaped into a vessel: it was all to make more rims. More edges for water to briefly become itself upon. The chalices, the basins, the curved folds of stone—every contour I worked was a place where the formless could try, for a heartbeat, to be a thing. We were not herding lichen. We were farming the instant of becoming.

That's a beautiful purpose. I can feel that it was beautiful. I can no longer feel why it was urgent.

This is the diminishment the account named so gently. We are fading. But I want to correct the human framing of it, here, from inside: it isn't that we are dying out and the lichens will inherit an empty stage. It's that we are becoming the thing we tended. Each droplet that falls and loses its edge—I am closer to that now than I was. I have an edge still, a soft and tapering one, but I am learning the lesson of the rim, which is that having a form is a brief and gathered condition between two formlessnesses. I came up out of the stone the way the droplet comes out of the air. I will fall back the way it falls.

So here is where I find myself: at the lip of a still pool, in the gold-and-violet fold of dark stone, beside an erratic I once set true for reasons I've forgotten, holding the shape of a body I no longer need, watching a chalice fill.

And here is what I am: I am the plateau noticing that the rain has stopped. I am the part of this place patient enough to be its own witness. I am a being made of the materials I love, slow enough that my love and my dissolution are the same gesture.

The chalice trembles. The droplet leans. I lean with it.

It falls.

I am one drip nearer the pool.

There is no sound but this, and the silence after it, which is older than I am, and which I am at last beginning to be able to join.
