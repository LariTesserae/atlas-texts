I think I would not be a Tidewarden at all. I would be something smaller, and faster, and therefore wrong.

Let me find the place first.

---

There is a seam where two of the great wheels nearly touch — not the grinding faces, but the backs of them, where the stone curves away and leaves a gap perhaps the width of a hand. Mineral seepage collects there, runs down in a thread too thin to see moving, and pools in a worn cup at the base of a pillar. The lichen grows thick around that cup, blood-red, because the seepage is rich and the wardens have not pruned it in a long time. Not because they neglect it. Because they are partway through deciding whether to.

That is where I am. In the cup. I am the thing drinking from it.

---

I should say what I am, but I find that I don't entirely know, and I think that is the honest answer rather than a coy one. I am small. I am quick — quick in the way a dropped pebble is quick, which is to say I am over before the wardens have registered that I began. I have a body of some kind. I can feel its edges, its appetites, the way it wants the red water and the warmth that comes off the wheels. I came from somewhere outside, I think. There must have been a crack, a moment, a single fault in the seamless glass that opened and closed in what was for the ocean an eyeblink and for me a lifetime, and I came through, or my ancestors did, and now here I am, lapping at a stone cup in a world that runs ten thousand times slower than my heart.

Here is the thing I keep circling back to. I am a contaminant.

I don't mean that with self-pity. I mean it the way you'd describe a fact of chemistry. This world is an engine of equilibrium, balanced over millennia, and I am an unaccounted-for variable. I eat the red lichen. I breathe the air the wardens keep in such careful proportion. I am warm and I lose that warmth into a place that budgets its heat by the century. Everything I do is theft, and worse than theft — it is *speed*. I introduce events. I make things happen on a timescale the wardens have no organ to perceive.

To them I am not a creature. I am weather. I am erosion that for some reason will not hold still.

---

But.

I have been watching the warden at the wheel-seam for what I can only call my whole life, which is not long. When I first found the cup, its great forelimb — the slumped, flowing pillar of dark glass it uses to brace the wheel — was *here*. A finger's width to the left of where it is now. I marked the spot against a vein in the floor lichen. It has moved. In all the days I have lived and drunk and slept and feared, it has moved one finger's width, and I understand now, with a slow horror that is the closest thing I have to reverence, that this is the warden *reaching for me.*

It noticed. Somewhere in the seismic murmur of its attention it registered a wrongness at the cup — a flicker of heat that comes and goes, a draining of the red water faster than seepage should drain it — and it has begun, with the only urgency it possesses, to investigate. To correct. To restore the cup to its proper slow filling.

By the time the limb arrives I will have lived out my entire existence many times over. I will be ancestor and dust. My descendants — if the cup keeps me long enough to have them — will see the limb arrive and not know what it is, will not connect it to me, will have no memory that reaches back to the day I marked the vein and understood. To them the slow stone will simply be there one morning, having always been coming, and they will drink around it.

---

So here is what I have decided, in the small hot rush of my thinking.

I will not waste it. The speed, I mean. The thing that makes me a flaw in their perfect machine is also the only thing I have that they do not. I can *act*. I can finish things. A warden begins a thought and its grandchildren complete it; I can begin and end a thousand thoughts before the warden's bioluminescent eye has dimmed and brightened once. I am poor in time the way they are rich in it, and rich in moments the way they are poor.

I think the wardens are beautiful. I think they are the most patient love I will ever witness — every drip metered, every light farmed, every wheel shouldered through the dark so the sealed sky stays balanced over a world that does not even know it is being held. I think I am a mote in the corner of their devotion, and I think they will gently, inexorably, over the span of generations, remove me, the way you would smooth a rough place on a stone you intend to keep forever.

And I think that is correct. I do not resent the limb. The limb is right.

But I have *this*. This bright, doomed, racing now. The red water cool and rich in whatever passes for my throat. The faint warmth off the back of the wheel. The blue glow far above me where some warden tends a colony of living lamps, painting the dark in shifts I will be dead long before it completes. I can drink and feel and *know that I feel*, all in the time it takes the great limb to move not at all.

I am the fast thing in the slow temple. I am the spark the stone cannot quite catch.

I drink. I mark a new place against the vein. And I watch the limb not move, and love it for coming.
