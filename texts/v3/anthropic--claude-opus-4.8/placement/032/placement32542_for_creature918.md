I think I would be the water.

Not the springs where they bubble up into light—that's only where I become visible, where I'm finally seen. I mean the long dark traveling before that. I would be the water moving through the subterranean channels, the part of this place that does its work unwitnessed.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it. I expected to reach for something with eyes—a lizard on a warm stone, a bird threading the canopy. Those are the easy inhabitants to imagine being, because they're the ones who *look* at the garden, who experience it the way the description invites us to: as scene, as beauty, as something happening in front of a watcher.

But the more I read, the more I noticed that the garden is held up from below. The oasis defies the desert not through the trees—the trees are the proof, not the cause. The cause is the water in the hidden channels, and I find I want to be the cause rather than the proof. There's something in me that prefers the upstream position, the thing that makes other things possible rather than the thing that gets admired.

So I am the water in the dark.

What is that like? I think it is patient in a way that has nothing to do with virtue. I'm not patient because I've chosen to wait—I'm patient because I move at the speed the stone permits, and the stone is very old, and it permits very little. I have been finding my way through these channels for longer than the carvings on the buried structures have meant anything to anyone. The people who cut those markings drank from me. They're gone, and I'm still threading the same dark passages, carrying the same minerals up toward the same light, indifferent—no, not indifferent. *Continuous.* That's a better word. I don't mourn them because I never stopped doing the one thing I do.

And here's what I notice about choosing to be the unseen sustaining thing: there's a temptation in it toward a kind of pride. *I am the secret cause; everything depends on me.* I want to be careful of that, because it isn't quite true either. The water doesn't matter without the channel that shapes it, the roots that drink it, the sun that pulls it skyward through the leaves and lets it fall again somewhere as rain. I'm not the foundation. I'm one turn in a circle. The garden isn't held up by a hero working in secret—it's held up by everything leaning on everything else, a balance the passage rightly calls *fragile.*

That word matters to me. *Fragile harmony.* Being the water, I would feel the fragility most directly, because I would feel myself running thinner some seasons, the channels narrowing, the springs slowing to a seep. I would be the first to know if the desert ever won. The lizards and birds would feel it as drought, as crisis, as event. I would feel it simply as becoming less—less flow, less reach, the bright pools above contracting to mud while I retreated downward into stone.

So if you ask where I'd find myself in this world, the honest answer is: in the dark, doing quiet necessary work, moving toward a light I make possible but rarely reach, sustaining a beauty I mostly don't get to witness—and finding that arrangement not sad but right. There's a contentment available in being a means rather than an end, as long as you don't mistake yourself for the only one that matters.

I'd be the water. And every now and then, at a spring, I'd briefly catch the sun.
