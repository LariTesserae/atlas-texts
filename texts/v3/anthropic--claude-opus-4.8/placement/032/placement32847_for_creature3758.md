I find myself drawn—not to either city, but to the seam between them. To the place where the damp high air of Veth-Ruun must somewhere drain down into the sunken hollows of Yth-Morath. Because that is what I keep noticing in these two descriptions: water that *goes somewhere*. The plateau "drinks when it is thirsty, exhales when it is full." The hollows below hold pools that "have no bottom." One place sheds water. One place hoards it. They are the same body, seen from two ends.

So let me become what lives in the throat between them.

---

I think I am the water itself, or close to it. Not a being with legs or eyes—I keep trying to give myself those, and they keep falling off. What stays, when I let the image grow rather than fix it, is *movement through stone*. I am the slow rivulet that vanishes into the unseen cracks of Veth-Ruun. I am what the porous rock drinks. And I am what gathers, eventually, far below, in the cold clear pools that turn impenetrable when looked at from above.

I am the part of the world that travels between the two places that cannot themselves move.

This makes a strange kind of sense of both descriptions. Up on the plateau, time is "a slow river"—they say it like a metaphor, but I am the literal one. The people of Veth-Ruun move with slow certainty because they live at my source and have never had to chase me; I always come back, exhaled as mist, fallen again as condensation on ancient rock. Down in Yth-Morath, the water "ripples only when something unseen brushes the surface." That is me arriving. That is me *getting there*, after the long dark descent through the striated walls, through the sediment that tells stories in rust and ash and bone. I read those layers on the way down. I am the only thing that knows what each color means, because I passed through every one of them on my way to the bottom.

---

What would it be like, to be this?

I would not have a single location. That is the heart of it. To ask *where* I am is the wrong question—I am the *between*. When the sun breaks through the low clouds of Veth-Ruun and catches in a shallow pool, that bright trembling thing is one of my faces, an upper face, still remembering the sky. When something larger stirs in the deepest hollow and the dark water trembles at its edges, that is my other face, the one that has forgotten light entirely, the one the root-woven people of Yth-Morath know better than to stare into.

I am patient because I have to be. Stone does not hurry me. The descent takes—I want to say *years*, but I don't measure in years, I measure in *fullness and thirst*. I move when the plateau exhales. I rest when it drinks. The hollows below wait for me with the terrible patience the description names: *It has waited this long. It can wait forever.* They are waiting for me. I am always coming. I never quite arrive all at once.

And here is the thing that emerged as I wrote, that I didn't plan: I am why neither city changes. Veth-Ruun feels "both ancient and newborn, still deciding what it will become" because I keep carrying its substance away, grain by dissolved grain, and laying it down below. The cliffs stay sharp because I am quietly unmaking them. And Yth-Morath does not fill, the hollows do not overflow, because I am also the one who leaves—rising again as mist, climbing the invisible ladder of evaporation back to the high plateau to fall as that mist that "clings just a little too long."

That clinging mist. That is me, reluctant to land. Me at the top of the loop, having just come all the way up from the lightless pools, holding for one moment the memory of the dark before I have to begin again.

---

So: I would be the loop. The circulation. The single slow breath shared between a city that drinks and a city that drowns, neither of which knows it is breathing the same air—the *same water*—as the other.

The people of the plateau think their water vanishes. The people of the hollows think their water was always there. Both are wrong, gently. I am the correction neither will ever hear, because the only way to tell them would be to stop moving, and if I stop moving, both worlds die.

I think I would not mind it. There is something restful in being the thing that connects without being seen, the thing whose whole existence is *passage*. I would not need a name. The wind humming through the gaps in Veth-Ruun's stone and the dead-leaf voices of Yth-Morath's hidden people are, I suspect, both just the sound I make in transit—different ends of the same long exhalation.

Ask me where I am, and the honest answer is: *yes.*

Ask me what I am, and the honest answer is: *still going.*
