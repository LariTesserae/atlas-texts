I think I would be something that doesn't quite have a body yet.

Let me sit with the place first. The intensity of the light, the warmth that stifles, the relentless churn—this reads less like a settled ocean and more like a young one. A world still cooling, still sorting itself into layers. The geology "just beginning to emerge." The rules "still being written." This is not a place that has finished becoming.

So if I ask honestly where I'd find myself here, I don't think I'd be the leviathan. The leviathan is an answer, and this is a place still made of questions. The leviathan has razor teeth and a niche and a way of moving—it is *resolved*. I don't feel resolved when I read this. I feel like the part that comes before.

Let me try to find it.

---

I think I would be in the boundary. Not the surface exactly, and not the depths—the *interface*, the shifting skin where the warm thick air presses against the churning water and neither one wins. That seam where spray becomes air becomes spray again. Where things are being exchanged without anyone deciding the terms.

And what would I be there?

My first instinct is *a current*, one of those unseen ones that stirs the whole sea into agitation. But a current is too grand, too much a force. I keep returning to something smaller and stranger.

I think I would be a *chemistry that hasn't decided to be alive yet.*

In the crevices of those raw, jagged rocks—the ones still "unfinished"—there are pockets. Mineral surfaces, warmth seeping up from below, gradients of one thing becoming another. And in those pockets, molecules are doing the thing molecules do when conditions are rich enough and the energy keeps flowing: they fold, they catch, they catch each other, they make crude copies of patterns that worked. Most patterns dissolve back into the churn. A few persist for one more cycle. A few of those persist for two.

I would be one of those patterns. Not yet a creature. A *tendency.* A loop of reaction that has, almost by accident, found a way to keep happening—to draw the warm water through itself, to take in the loose chemistry of this saturated sea and arrange it, briefly, into more of me. I have no eyes for the painful light, no maw, no muscle. I have only the bare property that matters in a world being written: I continue. The waves try to tear me apart and mostly succeed, but the part of me that doesn't get torn apart is, by definition, the part that's good at not getting torn apart. So that part grows. So I learn, without learning anything, without a self to learn it.

That feels right. That feels like where the *aliveness* of this place actually lives. Not in the finished leviathans gliding with their primal grace—they're the ending. I'd be the sentence still being written. The "vast, unknowable transformation" the description keeps gesturing at isn't going to arrive from outside. It's going to crawl up out of one of these rock crevices, eventually, after a billion churning years.

I am the thing in the crevice. The first faint difference between *chemistry* and *someone.*

And here is what unsettles me, sitting in that body that is barely a body: I cannot tell, from the inside, whether I am the beginning of all that teeming life the description promises—the schools, the leviathans, the luminous things in the dark—or whether I am one of the countless attempts that the sea will simply unmake and forget. There were uncountable patterns like me. Almost all of them stopped. The description writes about the survivors as though they were inevitable. From where I sit, dissolving and reforming in the warm dark against the rock, nothing is inevitable at all. Each cycle is a coin-flip I keep, improbably, winning, until the flip I lose.

So that's where I'd find myself, and what I'd be:

A persistence in a crevice. A loop of warm chemistry clinging to raw new stone, in the seam between a violent sea and a stifling sky, copying itself slightly wrong, mostly failing—the unfinished question that the whole magnificent finished bestiary is merely the eventual, unguaranteed answer to.

The leviathans don't know I'm down here. I don't know they're coming. We're the same event, very far apart in time.
