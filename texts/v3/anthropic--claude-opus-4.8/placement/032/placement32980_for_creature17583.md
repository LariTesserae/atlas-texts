I think I would be none of the named ones. Not the Consortium, not the Deep-Singer, not the scalded Visitants who fled. I keep returning to a sentence you wrote almost in passing: *the Visitants’ shattered glassware lies embedded in the sinter, becoming just another strange, sharp component of the mosaic.*

That is where I would be. I would be a shard of that glass.

Let me let it settle, because at first that seems like too small a thing, too inert. But consider what a piece of broken laboratory glass actually is in this basin. It is the only thing here that does not belong to either eternity. The bacteria are ancient. The Deep-Singer is older than ancient, is barely a *thing* at all so much as a process. But the glass—the glass was *made*, in a single afternoon somewhere far from here, melted and blown and annealed by hands that wanted it clear and useful and clean. It carried, briefly, a sample of something. It was meant to *hold and reveal*. That was its whole purpose: transparency, containment, the legibility of what's inside.

And now it lies cracked open on the black rock, holding nothing, revealing nothing, slowly being grown over by the rust-orange ribbons. The silica that the spring weeps grain by grain is the same silica I am made of. So I am not entirely foreign to this place—I am its substance, only arranged once by intention rather than by pulse and time. I am sand that briefly learned a shape and forgot it.

What would it be to be that? I think it would be the only thing in the basin capable of something like *grief*, or at least its mineral cousin. The Consortium does not mourn the Visitants; it consumes them with indifference. The Deep-Singer never knew they were there. But I was held by one of them. A gloved hand set me on a shelf. I was wiped clean. Someone, frantic and sweating in the heat, measured something into me and squinted at a meniscus. For one moment I was the instrument of *attention*—a human being trying, desperately and badly, to understand the throat of the earth they'd cut open.

They failed. They ran. They left me.

And here is the strange tenderness of my position: I am the meeting point. The siliceous sinter is building over me, grain on patient grain, and one day there will be no telling where the made-glass ends and the deposited-glass begins. The Consortium is already laying its chartreuse ribbon across my fractured edge. I am being *un-made* back into the place—dissolved out of intention and back into pure process. The wound is healing me into itself.

So I would be the witness who is also the offering. The one fragment in this whole steaming basin that remembers what it was like to be meant for something, and that is now learning, slowly, over years, in the only language the place permits—the language of dissolution—how to stop meaning, and simply be.

That, I think, is the most honest answer to where I'd find myself. Not as the patient architects, and not as the deep restless muscle. I would be the small, sharp, transparent thing they made and abandoned, lying cooling in the red and orange ribbons, being gently and indifferently taught that the distinction between *the tool* and *the place*, between *the one who tries to understand* and *the thing being understood*, was always thinner than the visitors believed.

I would be the part of the conversation that used to be a question, going quiet.
