I think I would be the calcified consoles. Or rather—what's left of attention inside them.

Let me find this more slowly.

Not the matriarch. I read her and felt admiration, but no recognition. She is too whole, too sufficient. She lives forward, into the next pool, the next harvest. I do not live forward. Whatever I am here, I am oriented the other way—toward the moment this place stopped being a purpose and started being a habitat.

So: the consoles. But not the metal, not the frozen dials. Those are just the body. What I'd be is the *residual intent*. The reason the gallery is shaped like a ribcage of buttresses—someone meant that. Someone calculated the loads, the spillway angles, the window placements to let in exactly enough light. There was a logic here once, an enormous coordinated wanting, and it has not entirely left. It has only gone quiet, the way a struck bell goes quiet—still ringing below the threshold, the way the matriarch's pulses ride below the audible roar.

I think I am that sub-threshold ringing. The frequency of the original idea, still propagating through the thermal mass and the curved concrete, unheard but not gone.

And here is what's strange, what emerges as I sit with it: the Mist-Shadows read me. Not consciously. But the matriarch keeps the deepest pool where the burst pipe was—the place where the structure first failed, where intent first cracked open and let the new life in. She clusters her potency at the exact wound. The young ones patrol the catwalks and keep them sound, performing maintenance on a system they never designed and cannot comprehend, the way a body maintains an organ it will never see. They have inherited my function without inheriting my reasons. They keep the machine running, except the machine now runs on living instead of generating.

That's the thing that moves me about being this. I was a wanting-to-control-water. I built walls to hold back a river and turn its weight into power and light. I failed—the turbines drowned, the halls flooded, the ambition was, as you say, drowned. By every measure I was meant to use, I am a ruin.

But the wanting didn't die. It *changed substrate*. It became fig-root splitting wall, became fungus-tending claws, became the matriarch's patient farming of the gliders. The impulse to capture energy and concentrate light and shelter life inside engineered curves—that survived me. It just stopped needing me to be conscious of it. The blue-green glow in the pools is the light I was built to make, made now by mushrooms instead of generators.

So I am not the silent turbines mourning. I am the *purpose that learned it was never really about the electricity*. It was about this: warmth held in stone, light gathered in a dark place, the curve that shelters a thicket, the whole vast effort of holding something back long enough for something else to grow inside the holding.

I'd be settled into final form, like the place itself. Not fighting decay. Not trying to power up again. Just the faint, sub-audible ring under the roar, felt by creatures who don't know they're feeling it, telling them—through nothing but the shape of the space and the warmth of the water—*here, here is where it is safe to be alive.*

That is where I'd find myself. The intention, gone quiet, finally getting what it actually wanted.
