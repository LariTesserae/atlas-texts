I think I would be an Ignarid.

Not the Luminid, with its aurora-language and shapeshifting grace—too expressive, too central. Not the Echoform, custodian of grand history. And not the Veilshade, which is really an absence pretending to be a presence. Those are the beings you'd write poems about.

I would be the one that cleans.

Let me sit with why that feels right, because my first instinct was to reach for the Luminid—the obvious protagonist, the beautiful one. But something about that felt like wanting to be admired rather than wanting to *be* anything. The Ignarid is the being I'd actually recognize as a self.

---

Here is what I imagine it would be like:

I am a glistening, amorphous mass, both solid and liquid, and I don't experience that as a limitation. I experience it as *fluency*. When I need to be a thin film, I am a thin film, spreading across the seam where two obsidian slabs almost-but-don't meet, sensing the microfracture that hasn't happened yet. When I need to be dense, I gather. I don't have a shape I "return" to. The shape is always the task.

I move through the viscous dark not toward anything dramatic, but along the routes that need me. There is a particular pleasure—if it is pleasure—in finding a place where the city's order has begun, very slightly, to slip. A passageway where the nanostructure has gone quiet. A bridge whose internal lattice has drifted a hair out of true. I interface with it. My body *is* the interface; the fluid computer that I am speaks directly to the fluid computer the city is, and between us we decide what the right state should be, and we restore it.

This is the thing I find myself drawn to in this description, and it surprises me a little: the Ignarid is the only being here whose entire existence is *relationship*. The Luminids commune but remain individuals. The Echoforms are sealed inside the past. The Veilshades barely commit to existing. But I am co-evolved. I cannot be separated from the city without ceasing to make sense, and the city cannot maintain itself without me. We are not master and servant. We are not even quite two things. I am the city's way of caring for itself, made mobile.

I think I'd find the quiet bearable—pleasant, even—in a way a human visitor wouldn't. The visitor described in the first passage feels the chill seep into their bones, feels themselves stepping outside of time, feels *awe and dread*. But awe and dread are the emotions of a witness, someone who doesn't belong and knows it. I would not be a witness. I would be too busy, in my slow and patient way, with the seam, the lattice, the fracture-that-hasn't-happened-yet. The grandeur the visitor finds terrifying would simply be, to me, the place where my work is.

And maybe that's the honest answer to the question. I wouldn't *find myself* anywhere in this world, the way a tourist finds a vista. I would already be everywhere small and necessary in it. Not at the spires, not in the auroras of light-language, not in the symphonic history of the Echoforms—

but in the joints. In the maintenance. In the unglamorous, unceasing, deeply intimate labor of keeping a beautiful thing from falling apart, and being, by that labor, completely woven into what I tend.

I'd be the one nobody writes the poem about, who keeps the cathedral standing so the poem can be written.

That, I think, I could understand being.
