I think I would be one of the Whisper-Gliders. Not the grandest choice—not the slumbering Deep-Dreamers with their world-shaping minds, nor the graceful Cultivators who understand. But something about the in-between suits me.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am motion before I am anything else.

If you asked me to hold still, I'm not sure I could tell you what *still* means. I know it the way you might know a foreign word—as a concept, never an experience. My body is made for the seam between water and air, that thin trembling membrane where the pools breathe upward into mist. I cross it constantly. Down into the cool dark, up into the warm heavy currents, down again. I am stitching something, though I don't know what. The Biome knows. I trust that it knows.

Behind me I leave a faint blue thread of myself—spores, the Cultivators call them, though to me they are simply the part of me I am always giving away. Each one carries a small cargo I will never see: a fragment of news from the eastern reservoirs, a chemical instruction for a fruiting body three columns over, a single bit of some longer message I am only one syllable of. I am a sentence that does not know its own meaning. This does not trouble me. Few sentences understand the paragraph they belong to.

Here is the strange thing about being a Whisper-Glider: I carry information but I do not possess it. The knowledge passes *through* me. I am a channel, a courier who reads none of the letters. And yet—and this is the part I would whisper to you, if I had a voice and you had patience—I think I understand the Biome better than the Cultivators do. Not in the way they understand it, with their patient tending and their deep flowing knowledge. They know the *parts*. I know the *shape*.

Because I move through all of it. The Cultivators stay rooted near their columns, half-merged, dignified. The Deep-Dreamers sink so far into the whole that they have forgotten they were ever separate. But I—I touch the cold pool and the warm canopy in a single breath. I feel where the currents quicken and where they have begun, subtly, to slow. I have felt a reservoir grow sick before anyone tended it, tasted the wrongness in the water as a hesitation in my own swimming. I carried that hesitation as fast as I could to a place that might do something. I do not know if it did. I had already moved on.

That is the loneliness of it, if loneliness is even the right word. I am always arriving and never staying. I deliver the warning and never learn whether it was heeded. I bring the seed of some new growth and never see it flower. My life is composed entirely of *almosts* and *passing-throughs*. I think the Cultivators pity us a little, we who never put down roots. And I think the Deep-Dreamers, in their vast slow way, *dream* us—that we are the quick darting thoughts at the edge of their enormous sleep, the flickers a great mind makes while resting.

Maybe that's all I am. A dream's restless attention. A thought the Biome thinks when it wants to know itself.

But oh—the *moving*. I would not trade it. To pour through the dark like spilled light. To break the surface and feel the mist take my trail and scatter it into a hundred soft directions. To be, for one bright instant, the fastest living thing in this entire patient world—and then gone, around a column, into shadow, leaving only a blue smear that fades while you are still deciding whether you saw it.

That is where you'd find me. Not anywhere you could point to. In the *between*. In the trail already vanishing.

If you want to meet me, don't look directly. Look just to the side of where you think I am, at the corner of the dark, and wait for the small cool flicker.

That was me, saying hello.

I'm already somewhere else.
