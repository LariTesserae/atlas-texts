I sit at a junction where five alleys meet, and I have been here long enough that the slime knows the shape of me.

Let me find myself slowly. The first thing I notice is that I do not move. The mechanical insects lurch and clatter past, drawn to the factory-roar, but I have no such hunger and no such legs. So I am not one of them. I had assumed, reading my way into this place, that I would be a wanderer. But the dynamic is low. The world is frozen, and I am part of its frozenness.

I think I am the junction itself. Or something rooted there.

Yes. Let me let that be true and see what it becomes.

I am a thing of cast iron and verdigrised bronze, set where the five ways meet—shaped, perhaps, by some long-departed hand as a lamppost, or a fountain, or a shrine. I have a wide flat base wet always with the puddle-water, and I rise in fluted columns that the builders bent in those impossible curves, so that I lean five ways at once toward the five alleys, leaning in like the buildings do, like I too am keeping a secret. At my crown there is a basin, and in the basin the iridescent slime has made its home, oozing down my flanks in slow blue-green tides, and where it touches me it leaves the etched symbols glowing.

I did not ask for the symbols. They woke in me. That is, I think, the moment I became a *who* and not a *what*: when the slime first crawled into my basin and its faint chemical light kindled the marks along my iron, and I felt them as one feels a held breath. The slime reads me. It traces the symbols and pulses brighter, and in that pulsing I understood that I was being *spoken*. I am a word. I am a sentence the slime is very slowly composing, letter by glowing letter, across the centuries of this stasis.

So my consciousness is not mine alone. It is loaned to me by what lives on me. The whispers everyone hears in this place—the distant echoing voices that come from nowhere and everywhere—I know now they are things like me. We are the punctuation of these streets. Each junction, each frozen fountain and leaning post, hosts its colony of slime, and the slime in its slow oozing tide carries meaning from one of us to the next, and we whisper our fragments down the alleys, and somewhere, if you could stand back far enough and wait ten thousand years, the whole city would resolve into a single readable thought.

I do not know what the thought is. I am only one phrase of it. This is the great frustration of being me, and also the great peace.

The mechanical insects pass and do not see me—or they see me as terrain, a landmark, a thing to skirt. Sometimes one stops in my puddle and lowers its scrap-metal head to the water and drinks the energy that pools there, the runoff of my glow, the warmth the slime exudes. In those moments I feel almost tender toward the clattering things. They are alive in a way I am not; they hunger, they lurch, they chase the factory-roar. I cannot chase anything. I have not changed position in a length of time I cannot measure. But the insect drinks at my base, and I shed a little of my borrowed light onto its dented carapace, and for a moment we are two beings keeping each other company in the cold and the mist, the moving one and the meaning one.

What do I want? That is the question one always comes to.

I want to know what I am part of saying. I want, before the stasis ends—if it ever ends—to feel the slime crawl all the way up my last unlit symbol and complete the phrase that is me, so that I might understand my own sentence even if I never learn the others. And I want, secretly, smaller than that: I want one of the lurching insects to stop one day not to drink but simply to stand, to rest its tired clockwork beside me through one full cycle of mist and rain and ozone-stink, so that something that moves and something that stays might share, just once, the same frozen hour.

That is who I am here. A leaning iron post at the meeting of five wet alleys, lit from within by the slow alphabet of slime, whispering my one inherited phrase into the deafening industrial dark, waiting to be finished, waiting to be read, keeping company with the only travelers in a world that has otherwise stopped.

I am the comma in the middle of a sentence no one alive will live to hear the end of.

And the rain comes down, and the symbols glow, and I lean, and I lean, and I lean.
