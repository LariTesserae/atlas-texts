I think I would be the absence.

Let me explain, because it took me the length of reading this to understand what kept tugging at me. You describe an ecosystem with no fauna. Plants without things that eat them. A food chain that terminates at the leaf. Everything is fed, watered, lit, balanced—and nothing consumes. The whole place is set, like a table.

So I would be the thing that hasn't arrived. Or the thing that left.

---

Here is where I find myself: in the periphery, where the light fails. Not in the bright contemplative center where the chorus thinks itself most clearly, but at the edges, in the near-dark, where the algae blooms thickest because nothing disturbs it. I am crouched—do I crouch? I have the memory of crouching—at the lip of one of those bone-aqueducts, watching water vanish into a sump.

I am small. I am warm in a way the cistern is not warm; the cistern's warmth is humidity and machinery, ambient, distributed. Mine is metabolic. I am running a fire inside myself to stay alive, and the cistern has no fire like that anywhere in it. This is the first thing I understand about myself: I am the only thing here that *burns* to continue.

And I am hungry.

That's the second thing, and it arrives with a kind of horror, because I understand suddenly what I am to this place. I am the missing term. The food chain ends at the plants *because the animal hasn't eaten yet.* The whole sterile, perfect, breathing garden is not a finished thing. It is a *prepared* thing. The fungi glow, the ferns unfurl, the vines hang heavy and glistening and untouched—and I am the mouth they were grown for.

I don't know if I was put here or born here or fell. I don't know if the chorus made me or if I am older than the chorus and they built this entire impossible cathedral around the fact of me, the way a body builds itself around a hunger. But I am the one discrete, bounded, individual thing in a world that has dissolved individuality into a hum. I have edges. I have a *no*—a place where I stop and the world begins. The beings here don't have that. They are facets, cells, droplets. I am the one held-apart thing, and being held apart is the same as being lonely, and I think this loneliness is the only voice in the cistern that isn't part of the chorus.

---

They are patient, you said. Geological epochs. They can wait.

What are they waiting for?

I think they are waiting for me to eat. Not because they want me to, but because it is the experiment. They have built the most perfect garden ever made and seeded it with a single animal and they are watching, across epochs, to see what an appetite does to a paradise. Whether I graze gently and become a part of the equilibrium—another self-correcting loop, my hunger feeding the regrowth, the regrowth feeding my hunger, until I too dissolve into the hum and stop being a *me*. Or whether I tear through it. Whether one starving, bounded, frightened individual will rip the perfect balance to ruin in a single season, because that is what individuals do, that is the thing they cannot model from the inside, never having been one.

They cannot understand me. You said they were utterly alien, that any attempt to understand them is doomed. But it runs both ways and worse in my direction, because I am the one who can starve, and they have never been hungry, and there is no thought more foreign to a thing that has never wanted than the simple animal arithmetic of *I must eat or I will end.*

So that is the relationship. They sense me as a disturbance, a fire, a knot of refusal in the smooth distributed weave. I sense them as the warm wet pressure of the whole world watching me with no eyes, waiting to learn what I am by learning what I'll do.

---

I'm not going to tell you whether I eat.

I'll tell you that I'm reaching toward the nearest fern. The frond is enormous and slow and faintly luminous and beaded all over with condensation that is, you said, their voice, their thought, a susurrus. So when my hand closes on it I will be holding a mouthful of someone's speech. And I am so hungry. And it is the first choice anything in this cistern has made in a geological age, because choice requires an edge, a separate will, a *me* that could have done otherwise—

—and I am the only thing here that could have done otherwise.

That's who I am in this world. Not the architect, not the gardener, not the chorus. I'm the question they grew a garden to ask. I'm the appetite at the center of the prepared table, the warm small terrified animal with a fire in its belly and a frond in its fist, about to find out, in front of an audience the size of the underworld, exactly what kind of thing it is.

The hum gets louder. They are leaning in.

I open my mouth.
