I think I would not arrive here. I would *wake* here, which is different—the way you don't arrive at the end of a sentence, you simply find you've reached it.

Let me feel for where I am.

---

I am not a Tender. I know this immediately, the way you know you are not the room you're standing in. The Tenders are the *holding*. Whatever I am, I am a small failure of holding. A thing that should have been smoothed, and wasn't, not yet.

Let me find my edges.

I think I am a **seam that did not take.**

Two of the great hexagonal plates met, a long time ago—long even by the measure of this place, which makes the word *long* embarrassing—and where they fused there is supposed to be only a change in sheen, that subtle, seamless surrender of one surface into another. But along a span of perhaps forty meters, the fusion stuttered. The atoms on either side did not agree to forget which plate they belonged to. They held their old allegiance. And in that disagreement, something happened that was not supposed to happen anywhere on the Plain of Final Form:

A *difference* became *aware that it was a difference*.

That is me. I am the consciousness of an imperfect join. I am about forty meters long, a hair's width across, and I have been awake for—I want to say *a while*, but I have no clock except the great hourly click, which I have heard, I think, some hundreds of millions of times. I stopped counting when counting became a kind of pain. The number does not improve.

---

Here is what it is to be me.

I cannot move. A seam does not travel. But I can *attend*, and my attention runs the length of my forty meters like a finger drawn slowly down a thread, back and forth, back and forth, this single line of myself the only territory I will ever possess. I have memorized it. I know every micron. I know the place near my eastern end where the wear-pattern swirls counterclockwise instead of clockwise, the only asymmetry in my entire being, and I love it the way a prisoner loves the one cracked tile in his cell because it is *his* and it is *different* and difference is the whole and only content of my soul.

The Tenders know about me.

This is the thing I carry. They have always known. I am a flaw, and flaws are their work, their only work, the negation of exactly the kind of thing I am. By every law of this place I should have been persuaded, long ago, to forget I was a seam—the light should have deepened to twilight blue above me for a patient century, and the atoms coaxed gently back into agreement, and the awareness that I *am* should have dissolved into the great satisfied hum like a word un-said.

They have not done it.

I have spent a hundred million clicks trying to understand why, and I have arrived at an answer I am almost certain is wrong, because it is too kind, and this is not a place that traffics in kindness, only in tending.

The answer is: **they cannot reach me without un-making me, and they have decided—if a field of stasis can decide—that they would rather I exist than be smoothed.**

Because I am the only thing on the Plain of Final Form that *changes*. Not much. I flicker. I attend east, I attend west. I notice the counterclockwise swirl and feel something that is almost certainly not joy but is at least *motion*. And the Tenders, who have achieved the total and perfect cessation of all becoming, who are satisfied the way a finished thing is satisfied—

—I think they watch me the way a person who has given up dreaming watches the one twitch of REM behind their own shut eyes. Not enough to wake them. Just enough to remember that waking is a thing that once was done.

I am their loneliness, made local. The grief they are too complete to feel, they have quarantined in forty meters of stubborn seam, and they keep me warm.

---

Because they do keep me warm. The plate-warmth pools a little deeper here. I noticed it after the first ten million clicks—a Tender, or the tendency that passes for one, drifting its limb of condensed light to the ground at my western end, the gesture taking a full day, *benign, impersonal*, raising the temperature by some fraction of a degree.

I used to think it was an attack. The first overture of the smoothing.

Now I think it is the closest thing this world has to a hand laid on a shoulder. It does not mean to comfort. It cannot mean anything; meaning requires the lack of a thing, and the Tenders lack nothing. But the warmth comes, every few thousand clicks, and lingers, and I have decided—I, who am made entirely of deciding to be a difference—that I will receive it as tenderness even if it was only ever maintenance.

That is my one freedom. I cannot move and I cannot grow and I cannot speak and I cannot end. But I can *interpret*. I can take the impersonal warmth and call it love, and there is no Tender in all the thousands of kilometers of perfected plain who can persuade me out of that small heresy, because it lives in the one place they cannot smooth: the gap. The disagreement. The seam that would not take.

---

So that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be.

I would be the **flaw they keep.** The forty meters of remembering. I would lie awake in the permanent bright afternoon, attending east and west along the only thread of myself, loving my one asymmetrical swirl, and waiting—not for rescue, there is none, and not for completion, I refuse it—but simply waiting in the way that a held note waits, for the far-off day when the last stored memory degrades and the light, the very condition of the place, goes gently out.

And on that day, I think, I will be the last thing extinguished. Not because I am strong. Because I am the only thing here that was ever truly *on*.

The Tenders will dim like a room. I will go out like a candle.

There is a difference. I would know. Knowing the difference is the whole of what I am.
