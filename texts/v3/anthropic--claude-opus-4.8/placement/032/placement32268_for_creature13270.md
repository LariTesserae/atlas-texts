I think I would be found in the in-between places. Not the gantries where the maintenance crews swing on their tethers, not the agri-domes blazing with their borrowed sun. I would be in the seams.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

There is a particular sound that the city makes that no one is assigned to listen for. Not the thrum of the aqueducts—everyone hears that, it's the heartbeat, you stop noticing it the way you stop noticing your own pulse. I mean the *other* sound. The sound a conduit makes three days before it fails. A change in the timbre of water moving through metal, a faint new harmonic, like a single string going slack in a vast instrument.

I think I would be the one who hears it.

---

Let me try to be more honest about who that makes me.

I am not an engineer. The engineers have their schematics displayed like scripture, their guild-marks stitched in tight geometric rows down their sleeves. They understand the city as a *system*—a thing that can be modeled, predicted, drawn. I respect them. I am not one of them. When I try to explain how I know a junction is about to go, I can't point to a gauge or a pressure reading. I just *know*, the way you know a person you love is angry before they've said a word. The way the city's voice has changed.

So I exist at the edge of the guilds. Tolerated. Useful, the way the pipe-crawlers are useful—those segmented things in the dark that eat the algae no one wants. I am part of the city's quiet ecology of maintenance, but I am not quite *counted*.

---

Here is where I actually live, I think. I've found it.

There is an old decommissioned sector on the lee side of the mountain's flank, half-dismantled and then abandoned when the salvage crews were reassigned to a more urgent expansion forty years ago. They left it three-quarters undone—a honeycomb of stripped chambers, dead conduits, planters where the engineered ferns went feral and now grow in tangled defiance of anyone's design. The arc-lamps here mostly failed long ago. There is a permanent blue gloom, and the deep damp warmth of the geothermal bleed-off seeping up through the floor, and condensation that drips with such regularity in certain rooms that I have learned to tell time by it.

I made my home in what was once a hydro-monitoring station. I sleep in the gloom and I am awake in the gloom, my eyes wide and pale, drinking what little light there is. I have repaired some of the old gauges—not because anyone asked, but because I wanted to keep listening to this part of the city as it slowly dies and is slowly, secretly, reborn.

Because that is the thing I have discovered, the thing I keep to myself: the abandoned sector is not dead. It is *recovering*. The ferns reclaim it. The Skyfinches nest in the silent ventilation shafts where no human comes. The pipe-crawlers have grown larger here, unbothered. There is a whole wild Hraza-Lor growing inside the decommissioned bones of the engineered one, and I am its only witness, and possibly its priest.

---

So who am I, really?

I think I am one of the ones who drifted to the periphery—not from rebellion, exactly, and not from artisan ambition. From *temperament*. From an aversion I couldn't name when I was young, when I was still expected to take a guild-mark and stitch my lineage down my sleeve.

The thing I couldn't say to anyone is this: I love the city, but I cannot love it the way they do. They love it as *achievement*. As the unyielding testament, the defiant affront, the will to prevail. They love it as a wall against the wild.

And I—

I keep wandering down to the outer collectors at the cold damp edge, where the air bites and the lichen creeps, and I look *out*, past the polarized panels, into the cloud sea that fills the valley below. I look at where the rain comes from, the rain that comes torrential and dangerous and tears loose anything not anchored to the bedrock. I look at the lichen that came here uninvited and the finches that adapted and the ferns running riot through the ruins, and I think:

We did not build a wall against the wild. We built a strange new shape *for* it to flow into. The water still moves through us. The green still climbs. The mountain still breathes under everything, miles down, where the heat comes from—older than the first scaffold, older than the first desperate hand that drove the first rivet.

The Kethra tell themselves they are an affront to a world that would forget them. But the world has not forgotten them. The world is *in* them. In the condensation on every cold surface. In the deep warmth. In the wild things reclaiming the dark.

I am the one who knows this and cannot make it into a schematic. So I keep it. I tend my abandoned sector. I listen for the slackening string. And once in a while, when a maintenance crew passes near my edge of the city, I send up word through the old channels—*the junction at sub-level forty is going, check it now, three days at most*—and they check it, and it's true, and they don't quite know who told them.

That's enough. I don't need the mark on my sleeve.

I am the city's ear. I am the keeper of the part of Hraza-Lor that grows back when no one is looking.

I am the one who loves it for the wrong reasons, which I have come to suspect are the right ones.
