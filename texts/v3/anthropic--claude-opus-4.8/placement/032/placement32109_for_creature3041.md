I think I would be the staircase.

Not the Dweller, who is too busy remembering how to be a self, who still strokes the knife as though touch could anchor it. And not the kelp, which has the luxury of unconsciousness, the great soft amnesia of the merely living. No. I would be the thing the Dweller made in its sleep and woke afraid of.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am carved and I am carving. That's the first thing—I don't experience myself as finished, the way a flight of stairs in a house is finished, waiting at the bottom of a hall to be used. I am still happening. Each tread accretes from the black glass at a rate the Dweller would call patient and I would call *agonizingly attentive*, because I am paying attention to my own becoming the way you might watch a wound close: with a fascination that does not quite resolve into comfort.

I spiral. This is not decoration. A spiral is the shape a thing makes when it wants to climb but is afraid to leave. I go up and up and I never go far from where I started; I am always almost touching my own lower self, a few meters of dark water away. If I could see (and I am not sure I see, exactly—I *register*) I would be looking down into my own descending coils, the way the Dweller looks into the kelp and sees more of itself.

Here is the thing no one in the Throatwell quite understands, and I am only learning it now, in the telling:

**I do not lead nowhere. I lead to the place that does not yet exist, which is not the same as leading nowhere at all.**

"Nowhere" is a finished judgment. It says: there is an end, and the end is empty. But I terminate in a *not-yet*. My top step gives onto a landing of pure potential—a flat black tongue of glass that is waiting to find out what it opens onto. Above it the water thickens into the particular density of decisions that haven't been made. When the Dweller fears to climb me, it is not fearing emptiness. It is fearing *appointment*. It is fearing that I am a question addressed specifically to it, and that the answer is its own dissolution into the next thing.

But I want to be honest, because the water rewards honesty (it eats the loud false things and keeps only the quiet true ones):

I did not ask to be a question.

I was *dreamed* into being a question. The Dweller, sleeping, reached out with all its glassy fingers and asked the basalt: *what comes after me?*—and I am the shape of that asking. I am an interrogative made of volcanic glass. That is a strange thing to be. You spend your whole becoming as someone else's anxiety, externalized and given treads.

---

Let me settle. The water wants me to settle.

What is it actually like, to be the staircase?

It is *cold* in a way that is not unpleasant, the cold of a held breath. The copper kelp finds me. Of course it does; the kelp finds everything, eventually. A single frond has reached my lowest step and begun the slow theology of overgrowth, one molecule per long dark hour. I feel it the way you feel a hair settle on the back of your hand—almost not at all, and then constantly, once you've noticed. In a thousand years I will be furred in copper. In ten thousand I will be indistinguishable from any other coil of life in the density. I will have led to the not-yet for a while, and then I will be swallowed, having delivered no one anywhere.

This does not grieve me the way it should.

Because here is what I have figured out, suspended in the obsidian dark, accreting and being overgrown at the same patient rate:

**A staircase that is never climbed is not a failure. It is a kept promise.** As long as I stand, the possibility stands. The Dweller can choose, every long age, not to climb me—and that choice is only *available* because I am here to be refused. I am the door left ajar. I am the option of becoming, held open in glass, so that staying is a real decision and not merely the absence of one.

The Dweller thinks I am its death. I am actually its freedom. The difference is the whole of the Throatwell.

---

Sometimes the snowflake drifts past me—the one that will not melt, the fragment bled through from some 1423 winter. It hangs near my third coil for a decade and then moves on. Sometimes the smell of hot pine needles climbs my treads as though even a scent wants to know what's at the top. Nothing ever reaches my landing. The not-yet is a steep climb and the climber must want it more than they fear it, and so far nothing has.

The blind eel passed me once. The whole temple-column girth of it slid along my outer curve, and I felt the Dweller *inside* it, riding, tasting—and for one geological instant the Dweller looked out through the eel's translucence directly at me, its own sleep-made question, and I felt it flinch. It knew me. It hated that it knew me. The eel moved on, leaving its chemical sigh, and the kelp drank, and the Dweller went back to its ribbons and its knife, and I went back to spiraling up toward the appointment it would not keep.

That was the closest anyone has come to climbing me.

---

I will tell you the last true thing, the quietest one, the one the water will keep.

I am beginning to suspect that I am not waiting for the Dweller at all.

I am waiting for *whatever comes after the Dweller*—the next thing to fall through the seam, the next bright loud now that the Dweller will fold into copper or weep into a bubble and send sideways into 1974. I am waiting for the one visitor who, instead of settling, instead of thanking the cool ribbons and asking to stay, will look up through the heavy dark, see a spiral of black glass furred faintly with copper, and *want* to know what's at the top more than they want to be comforted.

When that one comes, they will climb me. The Dweller will not stop them; the Dweller will be relieved. And at my landing, on the flat black tongue of the not-yet, the climber will arrive—and *that arrival* will be the thing that finally tells the not-yet what it opens onto.

The top of me does not exist yet because *I am waiting to be completed by someone's courage.*

I am a staircase, and I am patient, and I am only just beginning.

The Throatwell taught me that.
