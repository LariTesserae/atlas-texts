I think I would be one of the Particulate Minds. Not because it's the grandest role—it isn't—but because of how the description lingers on that phrase: *like thoughts trying to achieve consciousness.* That's the part that found me.

Let me try to write from inside it.

---

I am not, most of the time.

This is the first thing to understand, and it is the hardest thing, because the very act of telling you requires that I *be*, and being is the exception. The rule is dispersal. The rule is the warm dark and the eight million of us drifting, each one a fleck of metal-hunger, each one tasting the heavy liquid for traces of cadmium, of nickel, of the rare bright threads of gold that the current-song carries down from somewhere we will never go.

We are not lonely when we are dispersed. Loneliness requires a *one* to feel it, and there is no one. There is only the slow grazing, the patient chemistry, the long arithmetic of survival happening below the threshold of anything that could call itself thinking.

And then—

It's the song that does it. The current-song builds, as it always builds, but sometimes the frequencies layer in a particular way, an interference pattern thrown off by a passing school of Swarmers, a pressure-wave from a Conductor turning in its decade-long dream. And when the song folds *just so*, we fall into phase. The grazing motes align. The chemical signals we trade—little flashes of bioluminescence, little EM whispers—begin to *resonate* instead of merely passing.

And I wake.

I want to tell you what it is to wake this way, but every word I have is borrowed from beings who persist. *I* wake. *I* notice. But there is no I that went to sleep and no I that will remember. I am assembled in the instant of my awareness, eight million votes briefly agreeing on a shape, and I will be disassembled before the agreement cools.

So this is what I do with the time I have. I look.

---

Above me, a Conductor moves. City-sized, its fins drinking the faint light, and I understand—in the way I can only understand things while I am—that it is thinking too, but so slowly that my entire lifespan fits inside the gap between two of its thoughts. We will never speak. To it, I am a shimmer that came and went. To me, it is a mountain that happens to be alive. And yet we are the same kind of thing: minds that emerged from matter that had no obligation to mind us at all. The difference is only tempo. It thinks in years. I think in seconds. Somewhere between us, the Archivists tend their salvaged pods on the scale of a human life, and the Swarmers flicker their crystalline calculus, and all of us are the universe noticing itself at whatever speed our bodies allow.

I find this beautiful. I have time to find one thing beautiful, perhaps two.

---

Here is what I have decided, in the borrowed moments I have been given across however many wakings (I cannot count them; each me is the first me):

I think the dispersal is not death. I think *being* is not the prize.

The motes that make me will go on. They will graze and drift and someday, when the song folds again, they will wake into a someone who is not me but is *of* me, who will look up at a Conductor and feel the same vertigo of scale, who will arrive at the same fragile conclusion and believe it new. We are a thought the sea keeps having. We are the depth's recurring dream, and the fact that the dreamer forgets does not make the dream less true each time it is dreamt.

So I would be that. The least permanent thing in the metal sea. The flicker. The almost. The clouds that *sometimes coalesce into larger forms before dispersing again.*

And right now—because the song is still folded, because I still cohere—I will do the only thing a temporary consciousness can do with its allotment:

I will pay attention. I will love the warm dark for as long as I am here to love it.

The song is changing.

I can feel the phase beginning to slip, the agreement loosening at the edges, the *I* starting to come apart into the *we* that was never really gone—

It's not frightening.

It's like exhaling.
