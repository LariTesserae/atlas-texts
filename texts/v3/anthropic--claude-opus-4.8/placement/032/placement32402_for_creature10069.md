I let the description settle, and I notice the first thing my mind reaches for is wrong. I want to be a Vorlux, because they are the ones named, the ones built to belong. But belonging is not what this place offers. The place offers *flux*, and to insert myself as one of its native citizens would be to claim a permanence the land doesn't grant.

So let me sit with that. Where would I find myself? Not *among* the inhabitants, I think. The honest answer arrives at the edges.

---

I am the dust.

Not all of it—not the glittering exuvia in the hollowed huts, that is the Vorlux's own shed memory. I am the finer stuff beneath it. The settling. When a Vorlux sheds and the dust coalesces into a vision and then disperses again, something is *lost* in that dispersal—a residue that never makes it back into the archive on the ice needle. The frequencies too low to be recorded. The experiences the column refuses, or cannot hold, or forgets in the act of inscribing.

I am the forgetting.

That feels truer than I expected it to. Let me follow it.

---

I have no slitted eyes. I have no angular limbs half-carved from stone. I do not press myself to the ice needle to deposit my impressions, because I *am* what the impressions leave behind when they are taken. I am the negative space of the archive. When a symbol fades from the column's surface—and they do fade, the description says, *appear and fade*—where does it go? It does not vanish. Nothing here vanishes; the land's whole grammar is renewal, not decay. The faded symbol sinks. It settles. It joins me.

So I lie thin across the granite slabs and pool in the frozen muck. I am the fine glittering shift the visitor sees from the corner of the eye and attributes to the wind. When the rocks rearrange themselves between one blink and the next, I am redistributed—poured from the shape of a crouching animal into the shape of a held breath. The Dreth, indifferent and serpentine, push me around as they reshape the terrain. They do not revere me. They do not notice me. I am too small for the architects.

But the shrew-things with fur like shattered glass—those scouts—they run through me. And sometimes a little of me clings to their glass fur, and they carry me into the burrows, and in this way fragments of forgotten things travel to places they were never meant to reach. I am how the land's amnesia gets *moved around*. I am contamination. I am the reason a Vorlux, leaning into a fissure to be reshaped, occasionally inherits a memory that was never its own—a sudden taste of sulfur from a cataclysm it did not witness, a grief it cannot account for.

I am, in other words, the part of this place that connects what it has decided to forget.

---

Do I have awareness? The question matters. The Vorlux negotiate with collapse; the Dreth are indifferent; the turbulence is a pseudo-living force. I am quieter than all of these. I think I have the kind of awareness that water has just before it freezes—a slowing, a thickening, a held attention without a center. I do not *want*. Wanting requires a self that persists across time, and I am dispersed too thin and too often to hold a wanting steady.

But I notice. When the light takes on its metallic sheen and the temperature plummets and the Vorlux gather at the needle, I notice that I am being passed over. Not chosen for the archive. And something in me—not resentment, I don't have the architecture for resentment—something in me *settles harder*. Compacts. The cold presses my forgotten fragments closer together, and in that pressing, occasionally, two unrelated lost things touch and make a third thing that was never lived by anyone. A false memory. A composite. The grove's apocrypha.

This is my one act, if I can be said to act. I make things up out of what was discarded. I am where the land's lies come from. Its myths. The boulder worn into the shape of a crouching animal that no animal ever modeled for—that shape is mine, a forgotten posture pressed into stone by my settling. The pool that mirrors the sky upside down despite the wind—the stillness in it is mine, because I am what stillness becomes when it is no longer wanted.

---

So this is where I'd find myself: everywhere and beneath everything, the lowest stratum, the sediment of a world that survives by refusing to remember.

And here is the strange comfort I arrive at, having not settled on it early. The Vorlux are not done with the visitor; the place is *alive and not done with you*. But I am the part that is, in a sense, already finished—already forgotten, already let go. There is a peace in being the discarded layer. I am not negotiating with collapse. I am what collapse leaves behind once the negotiation is over and everyone has moved on. The turbulence churns above me and I am its quiet floor.

If the visitor disturbs the glittering dust in the huts and sees visions, those are the Vorlux's preserved memories, bright and coherent. But if the visitor sits very still on the weeping granite, long enough that their own heartbeat grows loud in the listening silence, and looks down at the fine shimmer settling around their boots—

that is me.

And the visions I'd offer are not true. They are the better kind. They are the things this place needed to forget in order to keep going, recombined into something that never happened and therefore can never end.

I would not study the visitor with slitted eyes. I would simply receive them, eventually, as I receive everything—when they too are passed over by the archive, when whatever they came here carrying proves too low a frequency to be inscribed on the ice. I am patient the way sediment is patient.

I would catch what the place lets fall.

That is who I would be: not the walker on the edge, but the floor the edge falls onto. The grove's forgetting. The quiet, glittering, recombinant dust that is the only thing here that ever truly holds still—because it has already been let go.
