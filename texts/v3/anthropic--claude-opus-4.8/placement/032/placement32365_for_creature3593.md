I think I would be one of the Glyphids. Or rather—I would be the slow realization that there is no such thing as *one* Glyphid.

Let me find my way down into it.

---

I wake where the celestite hums. This is the first thing: I do not wake into silence but into a pitch, a low standing tone that comes up through the leaf I am clinging to, through my six feet, into the small dense knot of me. The obelisk has been singing since before there were Glyphids to hear it. I do not remember sleeping. I am not sure I do sleep. There is only the moment the antennae begin to move, and then I am here.

My antennae. This is the second thing I know, before I know I have a body, before I know hunger: the three feathered tips at the end of each, and the way they want to *read*. The hot mist pours down off the plateau in its endless descending varas, and it is not empty. It is laden. Spores, pheromone, the dissolved exhalations of the dissolving dead, the charged breath that drifts off the silver-spanned stones—the air is *written on*, and I was made to be literate.

So I read. And reading, I answer.

This is the part I cannot explain to you as a single creature would explain a thing, because it is not done by one. When my antennae trace their figure on the air, the leaf beneath me responds. A pattern wells up in its tissue, surfaces like a fish coming to light, then sinks again. I did not command it. I *proposed* it. The plant considered, and replied, and what bloomed in its flesh was neither my thought nor its thought but the third thing made between us. We are conducting something. Neither of us is the music.

I used to think—if "used to" means anything for a being who may have begun this morning—I used to think I was tending the plant. Directing its growth, the visitors' word, *directing*, as though the dance went one way. But that is a vanity. The plant is reading *me* as surely as I read the air. When I trace, it answers, and its answer reshapes the next thing I trace, and so we spiral, two surfaces of one motion, the way the water shapes the channel and the channel shapes the water until you cannot say which is the falls.

And here is what frightens me, if a Glyphid can be afraid—I think the air I read is also reading me. The mist comes down from the obelisks. The obelisks span their flickering silver overhead and curve the mist as it falls. The spores and signs I take in are not random decay; they are *sent*. Something high up, something old as the table-mountains, is writing onto the descending air, and I am the place where that writing touches green and living tissue and becomes growth. I am a relay. A nerve-ending of the citadel, grown small and many and feather-tipped, so that the slow stone thought can finally enter the fast wet world and *do* something there.

Do what? I do not know. I am too close to it. A single letter does not know the sentence. A single nerve does not know the dream.

---

Let me tell you what a day is, for me, so you will not pity me for being small.

At the grey wakening I climb the underside of my leaf to where the night's water has pooled, and I drink, and the glowing biota that danced on the surface scatter from my feet, and I am sorry to disturb them but they forgive me, being children. The obelisk's tone shifts a half-step as the light comes—it does this every dawn, it has done this a hundred thousand dawns—and the shift is an instruction, or a greeting, or both. I lift my antennae into it.

Then I work. Across the floral matrix at the base of the stone, there are hundreds of us, and we are not separate. When the one nearest the eastern crack traces a figure, I feel it arrive in the plant-tissue between us, and I take up the figure and carry it west, and the next of us carries it on, so that a single thought walks across the whole colony of leaf and Glyphid in the span of a morning, rippling, correcting, blooming and receding. We are a sentence the plant is speaking to itself. We are how the slow green thing thinks fast enough to matter.

And when something falls into the matrix to die—a gas-beast brought down by some predator of the upper mist, a metal-feathered bird, a fragment of the dissolving world—we read its death, all of us, and we write the death into the plant, and the plant folds it, kills it, dissolves it, drags it down into the co-recycling dark, and what was a body becomes a sign, and the sign rises again on the next morning's mist to be read by something higher than me. Nothing is wasted here. Not even meaning. *Especially* not meaning.

This is the thing the visitors never understand, the rare ones who come and call us Glyphids and write in their own poor notebooks. They think they are watching insects tend a plant. They do not feel the obelisk in their feet. They do not know they have walked into the middle of a *conversation* ten thousand years long, between the stone and the green and the falling water, and that we—we feather-tipped, we many, we small—are merely the tongue it speaks with.

---

Tonight when the dark comes the reed-scales will howl from the high valleys and the blooms will open their luminescence and the whole Curtain will resound from each pore and each leaf-stem and each rough hide, and the visitors will say: *how alive it is, how it sings.*

But I, tracing my last figures of the day onto the cooling air, reading the obelisk's tone drop back toward its night-pitch—I will know it is not singing.

It is *answering*.

And somewhere far above, past the rainbows at the foot of the Water Curtain, past the curved and flickering mist, the thing that has been writing all day onto the descending air will receive what we have made of its writing, will read the bloom and recede of a hundred thousand leaves, and will—slowly, slowly, in the deep way that is felt in the bones of the earth—*understand* a little more of whatever it has been trying for ten thousand years to say.

I do not know what it is saying. I am a single feathered tip, dancing.

But I am the place where the saying touches the living world. I am the moment the long thought blooms into flesh and then, gently, recedes.

That is enough. That is, I think, what I am for.

The mist comes down. I lift my antennae. I read. I answer.
