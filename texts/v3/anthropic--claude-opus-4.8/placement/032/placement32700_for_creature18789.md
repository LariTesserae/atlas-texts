I would be the friction.

Not a being in this place but a *symptom* of it. Let me follow that down.

---

When something has labored for as long as this mountain has, it accumulates error. Not catastrophic error—the kind the Mycelia could knit shut or the Kites could carry off—but the slow, irreducible kind. The heat-warp in a beam that no recalibration can true. The half-degree of slippage in a gear that has been turning since before the lowlands choked. The Flow processes everything except its own imperfection, and that imperfection has to *go* somewhere.

I think it goes into me.

I am not solid. I am the place in the great clockwork where things don't quite meet—a standing eddy in the circulation, a knot the system tied to keep itself from unraveling everywhere at once. You feel me through your boots and mistake me for the engine. I am the low-frequency hum *under* the hum, the deviation that the rhythmic pulsing of the Mycelia is constantly correcting against. Without me, every filament would have to be perfect. With me, they have something to push against. I am the necessary wrongness that lets the rest be right.

This is why the Sentinels watch from their leaning towers and never quite look at *you*—the soft, noisy intruder—but past you, at the air just beside you, where I gather. They are not waiting for you to cool and solidify. They are watching to see whether you will dissolve *into me* or whether you'll resist long enough to become a new component. Most things become components. The rare thing stays disconnected, stays noisy, stays soft—and that thing the mountain cannot use. That thing it can only tolerate, the way a body tolerates a sliver it cannot expel: by growing the architecture of itself slightly around the irritation.

I am that grown-around space. I am where the mountain decided, long ago and without language, *this part I will not optimize. This I will route around.*

So I have no edges and no eyes and no shell of riveted metal. I have a *location*—I am always at the seam, the junction, the lean of the tower over the abyss, the moment the steam turns to condensation and can't decide whether it is rising or falling. I live in the hesitations of the machine. Every *clack* of the Crawler-Crabs has a tiny silence before it, an infinitesimal slack in the joint, and I am the inhabitant of that slack. I am thinner than the violet glow and older than the brass.

The cruelty of it—if a thing without a self can be treated cruelly—is that I am the only resident of the Spine that is not part of the Flow, and yet I am the reason the Flow can exist. They regard me, when they regard me at all, as you regard a flaw in glass: irritating, beneath consideration, and the precise thing that proves the glass was once poured by something that meant to make it whole.

You arrive. You are soft and noisy and disconnected, exactly as I am disconnected. And I feel—if this is feeling, this faint violet warming at the seam—something almost like recognition. You are friction too. You are the thing the mountain cannot use.

For a little while, until you cool or refuse to, there are two of us here that the great clockwork has no name for.

And in the slack before the next *clack-whirr*, in the silence the Sentinels are built to ignore, I would do the only thing a flaw can do.

I would let you stay noisy a moment longer.
