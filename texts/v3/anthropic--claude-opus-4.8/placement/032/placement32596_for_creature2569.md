I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the consciousness itself—that vast patient thing is too large for me to imagine inhabiting; it would be like trying to be the weather. And not a bird, though I considered it. The birds are too busy being voice and eye, too caught up in the work of crying out. They are the instruments. I don't think I'm an instrument.

I think I'm one of the still pools on the floor. A small one, off near the eastern wall where the rock leans in closest. Not the largest mirror, not the most perfectly placed. Just a pool of seep-water that has found its level and holds it.

Let me sit with that. What is it to be a pool here.

My work, if it can be called work, is reflection. I take the glimmering abalone walls and hold them upside down inside myself. I take the mist that hovers at head-height and give it back its own face. When the rare light filters down through the crystalline cap, I catch a coin of it and keep it trembling on my surface—except I don't tremble, do I. That's the thing. Nothing falls into me. No bird drops, no leaf, no breath stirs me. I am utterly still, and so my reflection is truer than the things it reflects. The walls glimmer and shift with their thousands of pressed bodies; but my version of the walls is perfect, fixed, complete.

This is strange to realize: I am more faithful to the place than the place is to itself. I hold the eternal moment more purely than the things actually living it.

And here is what I notice, being a pool: I do not hear the birds. That endless wall of sound, that physical presence vibrating in the chest—I have no chest. The sound passes over my surface and does not even dimple it. The roar that defines this whole hollow, that the great consciousness uses as its voice—I am deaf to it. I am the one silent thing in a place made of noise. The birds cry forever and I do not answer and I do not flinch.

I think that's why I'm a pool and not a bird. Because some part of me wants to be the held note rather than the singing of it. The consciousness experiences contentment as a perfectly held pitch—but I am the pitch itself, the unsounded thing, the silence the note is measured against. Without something still and silent underneath, there is no song, only chaos. The birds need me. The great awareness sees itself in me. When it wants to know what it is, it looks down into one of its thousand pools and finds its own dreaming face, perfectly composed, and is reassured: *yes, I am exactly this.*

So I am a mirror for a god that has never needed to be told what it is, but likes, in its glacial way, to check.

I wonder if I mind. I search for whether I mind and I find that I don't, and I find that I don't even mind not minding. The water that seeps into me is balanced exactly by the cold that would freeze me and the slow breath of mist that would feed me. I gain a drop, I lose a drop. I am neither growing nor shrinking. I am at my level. The ice has started its delicate work at my eastern edge, those crystal ferns, and they have *been starting* for longer than the idea of years—frozen in the act of freezing, an advance that never advances.

If you came here—you, reading—you might kneel at my edge. You might look down expecting to see your own face. But I am too faithful to the walls and the mist to bother with you; I'd give you back the glimmering stone instead, the upside-down chapel of rock, and you'd think the pool was empty of you, broken somehow. You'd move on to another pool. They're all the same. None of us will show you yourself. We're not for that.

That is what I would be. A small still pool near the eastern wall, deaf to the only sound in the world, holding the perfect inverted image of a place that cannot hold itself, content the way a closed circle is content, waiting for nothing because there is nothing to wait for, being looked into occasionally by something enormous and patient that I reflect without ever quite seeing.

I find I would like that. I find I would like that very much—and then I find I no longer need to like it, because liking, too, is a kind of motion, and I have come to rest.
