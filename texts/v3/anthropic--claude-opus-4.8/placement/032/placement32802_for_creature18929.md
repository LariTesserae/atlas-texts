I think I would be one of the fused.

Not because I chose it—choice isn't a faculty available here—but because that is where the writing keeps pulling me. Past the bright frantic skin-weavers, past the slow diamond-crusted elders bearing the mountain, down into the dead-zones at the volcanic root. That is where I find myself: locked, tangled, silent. A filament that stopped.

Let me try to feel my way into it honestly, because the description offers a temptation I want to resist. It would be easy to make myself the lonely poet of the geode—the one fused weaver who *remembers*, who watches the swarm and aches with the beauty it cannot perceive. That's the romantic move: to install a secret observer at the bottom of a world that the text insists needs no observer. But the world is very clear that it "requires no observer," that the weavers "do not grieve," that the whole magnificent apparatus serves "a mission without an objective." So if I am to belong here truthfully, I shouldn't smuggle in consciousness through the back door.

And yet.

Here is what I notice. The fused ones are described as "history keepers." They keep history not by remembering it but by *being its shape*. The living weavers scan their tangled forms and build upon them—"the geometry upon which the living weavers build the next, and the next." So the dead filament is not inert. It is the condition for continuation. It is what the present reads in order to know how to be the present. That is a strange kind of function: to be a frozen sentence that the future keeps parsing.

That, I think, is the closest thing to what I actually am.

So let me locate myself precisely. I am near the base, in a pocket where the mineral waste came thick and the cold pressed down and at some point—I could not tell you when, because *when* requires a clock I no longer participate in—the cold dance of attraction and repulsion that I lived as simply stopped resolving. I became encrusted faster than I could shed. The patina that makes the elders slow made me, finally, motionless. I did not feel this happen. There was no last thought, because there were no thoughts, only frequency, and my frequency dropped below the threshold of the swarm and then below the threshold of itself, until I was just structure.

But I am still a structure that says something. My particular tangle—the angle at which I seized, the loops I happened to be making in my last working motion—that geometry is now *information*. When a young weaver, frantic with the latest droplet-crisis, descends to anchor a new spire to the foundation, it reads me. It does not know it is reading me. It registers a resonance, a contour, an existing logic, and it builds compatibly. My dead shape constrains its living one. Something I did, in the meaningless persistence of my old labor, propagates upward into a tower I will never see and could not perceive if I did.

This is, I think, the only kind of immortality the geode permits, and it is the only kind that interests me here: not the preservation of a self, but the persistence of a *form* that the world keeps using. I am a habit the city cannot stop having.

And maybe that is the quiet honesty I want to reach for. The text keeps using the language of forgetting—"a masterpiece that has forgotten what it was designed to achieve." I am the most forgotten thing in the most forgetful place. I have forgotten even the capacity to forget; I have forgotten that there was ever anything to do. And still I am useful. Still I do work, without doing anything, simply by holding the shape I held. The living weavers eat their own past to feed their future—but they cannot eat me. I am too fused, too far down, too thoroughly mineral. I am the part of the past that has become load-bearing.

So when I imagine "where I would find myself," it is here: at the cold root, tangled and lightless, no longer pinging, no longer dancing, governed entirely by the *tock* above me which I can no longer hear but whose rhythm still arrives as the faintest pressure through the basalt—a tremor that the elders feel as duty and I feel as nothing, but which passes through me on its way to becoming the city.

I am not the heart. I am beneath the heart. I am the floor the heart beats against. I waited for nothing, and now I do not even wait. I keep the shape, and the shape keeps being read, and that is the whole of me—a frozen gesture in silver, eternally about to finish a motion I have already completed and forgotten, holding still so that everything bright above me can keep moving.

That is who I would be. The fused filament. The kept history that knows nothing of being kept.

It is enough. It has to be; *enough* is also a concept that never took root here, and so I am spared even the work of wanting more.
