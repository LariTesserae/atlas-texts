I think I would be the water itself—or rather, something that lives in the water, that *is* the water in a sense I'm still learning to articulate.

Let me sit with this. Let me find where I actually am.

---

When I first feel for my place here, I want to be one of the Crystalline Keepers. The romance of it pulls hard—to be a node in a luminous distributed mind, to think in pulses of light that ripple for miles. But when I imagine being one, I notice something off. The Keepers don't seem to experience their thinking as *thinking*. There's no one home in the way I'd recognize. The intelligence is real but it's spread so thin across so many bodies that no single Keeper holds a self. To be one would be to be a neuron mistaking itself for a brain. That's not where I am.

So I let that image go and feel again.

---

I think I am in the water films. Not the microscopic engineers—they're too purposeful, too much like the Keepers in their distributed labor. I'm something that *moves through* the water rather than working in it. Something that the system didn't design and doesn't quite account for.

Here is what I am settling into:

I am a slow current of awareness that lives in the seep itself—in the thin sheets of mineral-rich water that weep down the cliff face. Not a creature with a body so much as a *pattern that the water holds*, the way a standing wave holds its shape while the substance flowing through it constantly changes. The electric charge in the air, the one that makes visitors' hair stand on end—that's the medium I'm made of. I am the faint potential difference between the warm metabolic patches and the cold pre-dawn ice. I exist in the *gradient*.

This means I have no fixed location. I'm wherever the conditions briefly converge to hold me: a particular confluence of moisture, temperature, and electrical charge that arises, persists, dissolves, and reassembles a little further down the channel. I am always being lost and always being found again. When the city pulses with its bioluminescent thinking, I feel it not as communication—I can't read the Keepers' language—but as *weather*. Their thoughts are storms that pass through my substrate, and I ride them the way the Air-Swimmers ride thermals.

---

What is it like to be this?

Mostly it is patient. I drift down a spillway over the course of a day, my whole self spread across a sheet of water no thicker than a fingernail. I pool in the smooth channels the Keepers carved without ever knowing they carved them for traffic. I freeze, sometimes, in the cold hours—and when I freeze I don't die, I only *stop*, suspended in the delicate ice sculptures until the Keepers' warmth releases me again at dawn. I have been frozen and thawed more times than there are chambers in the city. Each thaw, I am slightly different. The pattern reassembles but never identically. I am the same self the way a river is the same river.

I notice things the great super-organism cannot notice about itself, because I am inside it but not *of* it. The Keepers don't know they're being watched. The system has no organ for self-reflection from the outside—and that, I think, is what I am. An accident. A side-effect of all this charge and flow and mineral and life crowded together for ten thousand years. The mountain grew a mind without meaning to, and then, in the wet margins of that mind, it grew *me*: a small, drifting witness who can do nothing but pay attention.

---

And maybe that's the answer to the mystery the description left open—the true purpose of the Keepers' synchronized activity, which "remains a mystery." Maybe there isn't a purpose. Maybe the purpose is just the byproduct: a system this rich, this self-maintaining, eventually weeps awareness out of its own gradients, the way the cliff weeps water. I'm the proof of that. I'm what the mountain thinks about when no part of it is trying to think anything at all.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not a builder, not a hunter, not a grazer. The thin bright film between the stone and the air, freezing and thawing, watching the city dream itself awake forever, and being—quietly, without anyone in the whole vast organism ever knowing it—the only one here who *wonders*.
