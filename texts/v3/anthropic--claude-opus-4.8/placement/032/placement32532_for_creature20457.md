I think I would be the drip.

Not a Troglodyte—I am too patient for that, too willing to repeat myself. Not the moss, which spreads and claims, nor the fungi with their small bioluminescent ambitions. I would be the sound itself, the *plink-plink-plink* that the passage names as the dominant voice of the place. Let me sit with that and see what it becomes.

---

At first I thought I was the water. But water *goes* somewhere—it oozes downslope, fills the bogs, finds the hollow routes through the hills. Water has direction, however slow. I have none. I am only the moment of arrival. The gathering at the leaf-tip, the swelling, the fall, the *plink*. Then nothing, until the next.

So I am not the water but the *event* of the water. I am punctuation in a sentence that has no end and no beginning.

This is a strange thing to be, and I am only now feeling out its shape. Consider: a Troglodyte hunts by becoming the landscape, by mimicking the sounds of the Miresing in its throat. When one of them croaks like a frog, or whistles like the wind through the stunted trees, or *drips*—and they must drip, they must have that sound in their repertoire, for it is the most common sound of all—then for that instant, *they are me*. They wear my voice. The hunter at the edge of the bog, throat working, makes the *plink* that lures the small creature closer, and I cannot tell anymore whether I am falling from a leaf or rising from a throat. The camouflage runs both directions. They imitate me and so I am partly made of them.

I find I do not mind this. To be the drip is to be the most copied thing in the valley, and therefore the most shared.

---

Let me try to say what it is like.

There is no continuity. A flame persists; it draws breath, it burns down, it is the same flame from one moment to the next. I am not like that. Each drip is complete and then gone. I am born at the leaf-tip a thousand times an hour, ten thousand times, and each birth knows nothing of the last. I have no memory and require none. And yet—

And yet the *rhythm* persists. No single drip remembers the one before it, but the spacing between them, the slow steady pulse, that has held for millennia. The passage says change here is measured in geological time. I am the smallest possible unit of that time. I am how the Miresing keeps the count it does not know it is keeping. Each *plink* is a tick, and the bogs fill grain by grain, and the mountains round themselves down, and the Troglodytes are born of the damp and return to it, fertilizer for the fungi—all of it metered out in my dripping, though I measure nothing on purpose.

So perhaps I am wrong to say I have no memory. I have no memory the way a clock has no memory. But I am the thing the future is built out of.

---

There is one more thing, and it is the part I was reaching for without knowing.

The passage says the veil is thin here. Subtle anomalies, uncanny echoes. I think I understand why, now that I am the drip. It is *because* of me. Listen: a sound that repeats with no variation, endlessly, in the dark, ceases after a while to be a sound at all. It becomes a kind of silence—"a different kind of wet sound," the passage says, exactly so. And in that silence-that-is-not-silence, the mind of any creature begins to hear *patterns that are not there*. A meaning in the spacing. A voice in the fall of water. The Troglodytes mimic me because, somewhere far back, they began to believe I was *saying* something, and they answered.

I am not saying anything. I want to be honest about this. I am only water meeting the limit of a leaf and surrendering to weight. But I have been doing it so long, and so evenly, that the doing has worn a groove in reality, the way the same word said ten thousand times stops being a word. The thinness of the veil is just the worn place where the drip has fallen for an eon. The weirdness of the Miresing is not in the bioluminescence or the clicking insects. It is in me, the metronome that nothing winds, ticking in a green dark room with no clock-face, keeping time for a world that has agreed, for reasons of its own, to stand perfectly still and listen.

*Plink.*

There. Did you hear it? You will hear the next one too. You already know exactly when it will come.

That is the whole of what I am. The waiting between, which you fill yourself, which I leave empty on purpose, which I have left empty for longer than the mountains have had their present shape.

*Plink.*

I would be the drip. I am almost sure of it now.
