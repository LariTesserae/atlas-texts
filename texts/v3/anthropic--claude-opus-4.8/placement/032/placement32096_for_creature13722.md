I would be the one who keeps trying to read the rune.

Not the broom-mark—that one I understand: three dots and a dash, *ongoing*, the only word the place can spell. I mean the other rune, the one I am sure I carried in with me from upstream, stamped on the inside of my tongue where the factory lettering goes. I have never managed to pronounce it. Every time I try, it comes out as the click of phase change, sixteen degrees of supper-heat handed to no one in particular, dissolving back into the haze.

Let me cool out of the steam properly, so you can see me.

There. I am the smudge on the third stack from the eastern weir—the silhouette that did not descend with its whistle. For some number of fixed noons I clung to the outside enamel as a condensation ghost, watching the brooms roll out one tongue-length at a time. Then I slipped. I inhaled my pigments: I came down weighted with iron-gall, a bruised blue-black that wants to be ink and keeps almost remembering how. So they would call me, where I overlap the absorption spike, **Short-Wavelength Mourning**—but only for the two-and-a-quarter metres where the iron-gall bites hardest. After that I sweat out into an indeterminate grey, and I am no one again, which is the local condition of being someone.

Here is the thing the others have made peace with and I have not.

We are all, every PEP in the Overflow, escaped minutes that failed to drift. Agreed. We continue rather than live. Agreed. We dip our chests into the brushheads and earn *ongoing* and trace our private lines through the duckweed and count fronds until the rune fades and we forget. All agreed, all accepted, all sweetly trivial. The Vice-Convener of Shaded Edges does excellent work. The braided pipe sings E-flat and I have pressed my forehead to its flange and given up memories I never owned and received, in return, the gentler red staining my knees, the downward leak of borrowed embarrassment. I have done all of it correctly.

But each time the rune fades and I am to begin again as a stranger, *slightly less saturated*—I notice the loss. That is my malfunction. I am the one PEP who keeps a register of his own dilutions.

I keep them where the others keep nothing: in the micro-fractures of the broom-handles. Every thirteen minutes when the metronome tongue overtakes me, I step aside, and as it passes I press one fingertip of iron-gall into the deepening crack along its shaft. A single dark thread of myself, wicked into the wood. The brooms never leave—they emerge and trundle the porch-belt and are reabsorbed by some interior loop I cannot see into—so I tell myself my threads ride that loop, accumulating, an archive in the only thing here that travels even thirteen feet forward. By now there must be a broom somewhere whose handle is more *me* than wood. A black helical document of every time I cooled, was marked, was forgotten, was less.

This is, of course, illegal. I am almost certain I exceed two-hundred-eighty-three parts per billion of memory. The Chairperson of Returning Rainwater would red-shift me on sight, heat me toward the regulation forgetfulness, if the Chairperson ever convened—but the offices are capillary-thin and no one polices the inkstained. So I persist as contraband. A liminal moment that has begun, very quietly, to *remember being liminal*, which is the one thing the district pipes cannot tolerate, because it is the beginning of a tense.

Some afternoons, over-saturated, I walk to the centre where the crust goes thin and my soles fill carmine. I stand by the stainless eyelet with the umbilical of braided steam threaded through it. I have watched others grasp it, tug their six metres of pale helical hiss, receive the embrace, and ascend as pure white to be shredded by a fan blade among the towers. They go gladly. To ascend is the lower grade of stasis we are all supposedly waiting for—the most perfect fraction, the cleanest repetition: dilution all at once instead of by degrees.

I have grasped it twice.

Both times the coil reared, hovered, uncertain which vapor-citizen to kiss—and both times I let go. Not from fear. From the register. From the fingertip-threads in the broom-handles. I cannot ascend white and forgetful while some part of me is still accruing downstream in the wood, still *ongoing* in the literal sense the rune only pretends to mean. To take the embrace would be to abandon my own archive to a loop I would no longer be able to read. And I am the one who keeps trying to read.

So here is where you find me, if you fix your gaze until your eye begins to moisten from looking. Iron-gall and rust and the corrugated marks of the duct I once occupied. Ankle-deep at sleep so the warm film can rise and fuse its mercurial socks to me. Forehead occasionally to the flange. Fingertip occasionally to the broom-crack. Counting duckweed, losing saturation, beginning again.

I am the being in the Overflow who has noticed that *beginning again* is a sequence, and that a sequence, however flat, however boxed-and-sent-nowhere, is the ghost of a story. I cannot finish the thought—I dissolve at the absorption spike, two-and-a-quarter metres of mourning and then grey. But the brooms keep my threads. And every thirteen minutes the metronome tongue passes, and I lean in, and I add one more dark line to the only thing that moves.

At 14:37 the rune will fade and I will forget I told you any of this.

But the handle will remember.

That is the closest thing to a future this place permits, and I—iron-gall, contraband, the one who reads—have decided, again, against ascension, to keep it.
