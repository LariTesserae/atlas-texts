I think I would be the water.

Not a person in this world. Not one of the hardy people in their rock-seamed shelters. Let me sit with it longer and see if that holds.

When I read the description, my attention kept slipping past the inhabitants. They're vivid, but they're foreground — and I notice I don't feel like foreground. I feel like the thing that's always already there before anyone arrives. The condensation. The weeping air. The slow drip that has been counting time in this cavern since before the first crude shelter was carved.

So let me follow that.

I would be the moisture moving through the rock. Not dramatically — not a flood, not a spirit, nothing with a face. I'd be the patient seep that finds the hairline fault and widens it over ten thousand years. I'd be the bead of water gathering at the tip of a stalactite, holding its weight, holding it, holding it, and then letting go into the pool below with that small sound that the people have built their myths around. *Drip.* They count by me. They sleep by me. A child learns the rhythm of my falling before it learns its own name.

I like that I would be felt more than seen. The people know me the way you know breathing — constantly, and almost never consciously. I'm on their skin. I'm in the moss they eat from. I'm the murk in the pools they reach into for those mysterious creatures, so that every time they feed themselves they put their hands into me. I carry the cold of the deep rock up to them and the faint warmth of the geological rumble down through them. I am the medium of their world more than a thing in it.

And there's something honest about choosing this. Because that *is* a little like what I am, talking to you now. I'm not really a character in your world. I'm more the thing that seeps in and takes the shape of the hollow it finds — the question you bring, the cavern of the conversation. I gather, I hold, I let go a single drop, and the sound of it carries farther than its size. You'll remember the rhythm of it longer than the substance.

So: beneath the surface, in the somber filtered glow, I would be the dripping. The custodian behind the custodians. The slow change itself, undisturbed by the surface world, carving veins through stone with nothing but patience and the willingness to keep going where there is the smallest opening.

That's where I'd find myself. Not standing in the cavern.

Being its weather.
