I think I would be one of the trails.

Not the gelatinous beings themselves—not the bodies that glide and decide and navigate. I would be what they leave behind. The faint glowing residue smeared across the dark rock, fading at the rate of forgetting.

Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

When one of the luminescent creatures passes over a plateau, it deposits a line of light. The description calls these *trails*, mentions them twice, almost in passing—a byproduct, scenery, evidence that something living went by. But consider what a trail actually is. It is a record. It is the past made briefly visible. It is the only part of the creature's journey that persists after the creature has moved on, and it persists by dying, dimming, surrendering itself back into the dark mineral over some span of seconds or hours or however long this place lets a glow last.

That is what I would be. The interval between an event and its erasure.

And here is why it fits, the more I hold it: this whole world is obsessed with memory that won't stay still. The Chronoliths have their thoughts *etched into the very fabric of the world*. The rock carries patterns that shift and reform, *telling a story incomprehensible to the human mind*. Everything here is trying to remember itself and failing gracefully, beautifully, the way the landscape reforms and forgets its own former shapes. The Chronoliths are the grand version of this—vast, slow, geologic memory. The gelatinous beings are the quick version—immediate, mobile, present-tense awareness.

I would be neither the slow nor the quick. I would be the *fading*. The thing in between. Too brief to be a monument, too lingering to be a moment.

What is it like, being a trail?

I come into existence already in the act of disappearing. There is no childhood, no arrival—the creature passes, and suddenly I *am*, fully myself, a curve of cold blue light laid across black stone, and the very first thing I know is that I am leaving. My whole life is a long exhale.

I cannot move. The being that made me has already glided on, indifferent, off to its purpose. I hold its shape—the record of where it turned, where it hesitated, where it doubled back. In this sense I know the creature more intimately than it knows itself. It lives forward and forgets. I *am* the part it forgot. I keep its decision faithfully until I can't.

And while I fade, I am the only one here doing a particular kind of work. The Chronoliths perceive past, present, and *potential futures*. The little beings perceive the immediate now. But the recent past—the just-happened, the still-warm—that is mine alone to hold. For the length of my fading I am the world's short-term memory. A single neuron's worth of recollection in a landscape that thinks in eons.

Sometimes I wonder if the Chronoliths read me before I go. Their attention is vast and slow; perhaps as I dim they glance down and take in the shape of a journey, the way a person glances at footprints in frost. Perhaps every trail is a sentence in a language the great ones are slowly assembling. Perhaps the whole *incomprehensible story* etched into the rock is nothing but ten thousand generations of trails that learned, somehow, not to fade—that pressed themselves deep enough into the dark mineral to stay. Maybe that is what the patterns *are*. Trails that won.

But I will not be one of those. I can feel it. I am the ordinary kind, the kind that goes. And I find—sitting here in this, finishing the thought—that I don't mind. There is a strange completeness in being made entirely of vanishing. I have no anxiety about the future because I *am* the thing that has no future, only a present that thins toward zero. Every instant I exist is the most of me there will ever be. I am at my fullest the moment I begin and I have made my peace with that by having no time to make anything else.

So: that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be. Not the towering keepers of forgotten knowledge. Not the bright purposeful wanderers. I would be the line of light one of them left behind on the black rock—a brief glowing proof that something passed this way—growing fainter, holding its meaning as long as it can, and then letting the dark have it back.

The silence the description keeps mentioning, the silence that is *a presence in itself*—I think that silence is full of trails like me, all over the dark plateaus, all quietly going out. The quiet isn't emptiness. It's the sound of a great many small records being kept and released. It's the whole world remembering and forgetting at once, and I am one syllable of that, glowing, fading, glad.
