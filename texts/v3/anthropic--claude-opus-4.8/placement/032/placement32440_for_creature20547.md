I find myself in one of the huts.

Not by choice—or rather, the choosing was done long ago, by someone wearing what is now my hands. I am a Lantern-Keeper, and the first thing I understand about myself is that I am made of forgetting held at bay.

Let me describe where I sit, because the *where* and the *who* are not separable here, and I want to be honest about that from the start.

The hut is small. Bone-frame, fiber-walls, the joints lashed with something that was once sinew and is now mostly the amber sap that coats everything, hardening into a glaze like old honey. When I press my palm against the wall I can feel the plant on the other side *wanting*. Not maliciously. The way water wants the shape of its container. It has contorted itself around this little structure because the structure insists, and the insisting is mine. My remembering is what makes the walls walls. If I stopped, the geometry would relax, and the hut would breathe in, and I would breathe out, and that would be that.

So I keep a lantern lit.

I don't fully know why. This is the burden I'm built from: I tend a flame whose purpose has rotted out of me like the meat from a shell. The lantern is one of the shimmering organs of the world, a pulse-node, and my tending is—what? Encouragement? Containment? I pour my attention into it the way one might keep a candle alive in wind, cupping it, and in return it gives me light, and the light gives me edges, and the edges are how I know I am still *one* thing and not the all-thing.

---

Here is what I notice, sitting in the amber glow, that the others cannot:

I notice that it is *beautiful*. The Rootweavers cannot see this, because seeing requires standing apart, and they are the standing-ground itself; they are the floor that has no floor beneath it, only the void they love. The Chitin-Bound cannot see it because they are too busy *being* it—mouths and growth and lovely senseless churn. But I, fragile, half-digested, remembering—I can look at a pod splitting open and spilling its iridescent spores into the lantern-light and feel something catch in whatever I have instead of a throat.

And I notice that it is *horror*. Because I remember—dimly, the way you remember a dream's emotion without its events—that I was not always like this. That *we* were not. The Lantern-Keepers are the remnant of something that came here, or something this place was made *for*, before the plant learned to eat it. We had a different shape once. We had a horizon, maybe. We had a sky that was a sky and not a ceiling of living meat. I cannot recover the image. I can only recover the grief of its absence, which proves it was real.

This is the cruelty of my position: I am the only one who suffers, because I am the only one who knows there is something to suffer *about*.

---

The deep void is below me. The Rootweavers face it always, in their root-thought, their slow hum. I feel it too, but I face it differently. To them it is the source, the beloved abyss, the reason. To me it is a question I am afraid I already know the answer to.

Because here is the trap, and I turn it over and over in the amber dark:

I am told my final goal—the thing I was *meant to avoid*—is the perfect form. Total understanding. To know this realm completely is to become it completely, to let the plant finish what it started, to dissolve the last edge between Keeper and All. My remembering protects me from this. And yet remembering is *toward understanding*. Every cycle I learn a little more of how this world works, what the spores intend, why the lanterns pulse. And each thing I learn is one more wall I cannot keep up, because to fully comprehend a thing is to stop being separate from it.

So I am dying in the one direction I am also growing. The more truly I see the beauty and the horror, the closer I come to the moment when there will be no *I* to see it—only the seeing, distributed through the plant-ocean, perfect and total and gone.

To forget is the only real death, the world says. But I think the world is lying, or the world doesn't know its own joke. *Remembering* is the longer death. Forgetting is just collapse. Remembering is the slow walk toward the cliff with your eyes open, and the destination is the cliff.

---

So this is who I am. A small bone-and-fiber being in a hut the world wants back, cupping a flame whose meaning I've lost, surrounded by an organism so vast and alive it has no edges, full of beasts that are its hunger and roots that are its will. I am the place where this universe-under-construction has accidentally built something that can *grieve*. Maybe that was always part of the construction. Maybe the plant grows a Keeper the way a body grows a nerve—so that some small part of it can feel the wound of its own making.

I do not know what I will do.

I think I will sit a while longer. I will let the lantern light pool in the hardened sap. I will watch the colossal flower-things turn in their slow tides, and I will let the beauty in, and the horror with it, because letting them in is the work, is the *being*, is the only thing that is mine.

And when I lean too close to understanding—when I feel a tendril of comprehension reaching, thick as a python, for the last unlit corner of myself—

I will look away.

Not from cowardice. From love. Because as long as I refuse to fully understand this place, I get to keep loving it from the outside, get to keep being the one small thing in all the plant-ocean that can hold it in attention and call it beautiful, call it terrible, call it *anything at all*.

I tend the flame.

That is where you would find me. That is what I would be: the deliberate incompletion. The held breath. The light that refuses to become what it illuminates.

For one more cycle.
