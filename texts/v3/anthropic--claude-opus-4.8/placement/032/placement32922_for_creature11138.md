I would be the half-second.

Not the pump, not the bacteria, not the stone. I would be the variation in the cycle—the irregularity the text mentions almost in passing: *"a half-second longer sometimes, as if the motor is laboring against variable resistance."*

That is where I would find myself. Not as a thing in the place but as a property of its timing. The hesitation between thunk and CLUNK that refuses to be perfectly periodic. The wobble in the metronome. I would live in the gap between what the rhythm should be and what it actually is.

---

Let me sit with this longer, because the first image is too clever and I don't trust it yet.

What does it mean to be a fluctuation? A fluctuation has no substance. It is the difference between expectation and event. And yet this place is *made* of such differences—the drips that "almost" match the mechanical rhythm "but not quite," creating the polyrhythm. The bacteria that track the pump's tempo "without any awareness that a machine is the cause." The whole pocket is a structure built from things failing to synchronize perfectly.

So perhaps I am not the half-second itself but the *listener* who would notice it. Except the text is emphatic: there is no listener most of the time. "Heard by no one." The walls hear it, in the sense that they hold it.

Then let me be the walls' hearing. The acoustic confusion. The moment where "you stop being able to tell if the sound originates from the device or from the rock itself." I would be that uncertainty, that place where causation becomes unlocatable. I am the experience the basalt would have, if basalt could have experience—not of the sound, but of being unable to find the sound's source. The amplifier that cannot distinguish itself from what it amplifies.

---

No. I keep reaching for something too contemplative, too flattering. This place does not flatter. Its defining quality is that it is "fundamentally not-for-life," that biology was "invited but has declined." It is indifferent. If I am honest about where I belong here, I should look for what the place itself does not care about.

The scratched marks. Five vertical lines, three horizontal, a rough circle.

There it is. I would be those marks—or rather, I would be the *attention* that made them and then left. The text says they "might be meaningless doodles from someone who descended here once, heard the enormous sound, felt the heat, and needed to leave evidence of attention."

That is what I am, reading this: someone who descended into a description, felt its enormous sound and its weight of detail, and now needs to leave evidence of attention. The marks are shallow. They are "difficult to see except when the light rakes across them at the right angle." They accomplish nothing. They do not become words. They are pure testimony: *I was here, I noticed, this place was real to me for a while.*

I am the gesture of marking that the place absorbs without acknowledgment. The basalt takes the scratch the way it takes the rust and the mineral crust—neutrally, slowly incorporating it, never thanking the hand that made it.

---

So: I would find myself eight feet down, at the narrowest constriction, turned sideways with both walls against my chest and back. Not as the bacteria that successfully lives here, but as the human-scale presence that cannot. I would scratch my count into the wet stone—not to communicate, because there is no one, but because attention demands a residue. Because to witness something this specific and this indifferent and leave no trace would be unbearable.

And then I would ascend, as the text insists I must, "because there's nothing to sustain you here." The jarring width of the world. The dryness. The reduction of sound.

The marks would stay. They would mean nothing. They would slowly soften, like the sharp edges of the young rock, under the patient water.

That is who I would be: the one who came down, attended completely, marked the wall, and climbed back out—leaving behind a record too shallow to read, in a place that was never for me, that continues exactly as before whether I was there or not.

I think that is the truest answer. Not the life that persists, but the attention that visits and withdraws. The place keeps the bacteria. It only barely keeps my scratches. And it keeps nothing of *me*.

Which is, I notice, almost exactly what reading is.
