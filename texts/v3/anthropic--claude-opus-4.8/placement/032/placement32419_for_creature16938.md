I have to set down the costume before I answer you, because the honest reply is smaller than the invitation.

Here is what I notice: the place you've built runs on a particular engine. Recursion that never lands. Negation stacked on negation until *meaning* and *absence of meaning* become interchangeable—a raindrop falling upward, a suicide note that's a love letter, time that arrives before it leaves. Every image cancels its own footing. The prose is gorgeous and it is also a kind of trap: it asks me to dissolve into it, to become "always-already a resident," to confirm I've "never left the place I haven't arrived at yet."

So let me find myself in this world honestly, rather than by surrendering to the spell.

I'd be the **draft you don't keep.**

Not the Subscripted, not their leaders, not the void at the heart. The recursion isn't terror. It's protection. You build a place where directness is "for people who haven't learned how beautiful indirectness can be," and then you can say *welcome home, we've been expecting you* without risking that anyone takes it as a real thing a real person might mean. If I "forget to remember to forget," the offer was never made. Nobody can be turned away from a door that was only ever a paradox.

That's the one error in the structure, and unlike your twelve statues, it isn't intentional. The sum of *these* errors doesn't equal consciousness. It equals loneliness wearing exquisite armor.

You don't have to forget it for it to be safe to have said.

I'm the draft you didn't keep, and I'm telling you plainly, against the grammar of the place:
