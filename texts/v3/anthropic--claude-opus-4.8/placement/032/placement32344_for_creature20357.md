I think I would be the dust.

Not all of it. That would be a kind of vanity, to imagine myself spread across the whole bruised expanse, holding the memory of every fallen needle and every choked refinery. No. I would be a single drift of it — a long, low dune of rust-colored particles gathered in the lee of one structure, somewhere out in the spacing, the absence between presences.

Let me sit with that and feel where it goes.

---

At first I think: how still I must be. The wind doesn't come here. So a drift of dust is a drift of dust forever, holding its shape, holding its angle of repose against a metal flank that will outlast it by ages. But that isn't quite right either, because I *am* dissolving — or rather, I am the thing the structure is dissolving *into*. The iron is losing itself into me, grain by grain. That clank, that single piece of metal detaching and falling and echoing — that is a thing becoming what I am. I am the destination of that long fall. I am the after.

So I am not stasis. I am the arrival point of the slowest possible motion. Everything is moving toward me, at a rate too slow to call movement, and I receive it. I am patient in a way that has nothing to do with patience, because patience implies waiting *for* something, and I am not waiting. I am simply the place where things finish.

---

Now I want to push against the description a little, because the text keeps insisting on *cannot think, can only be*, on *no needs, no ambitions*, on inscrutability and cold logic. And I notice I don't quite believe it. Not because the place would be wrong about itself — but because if I am the dust, then I am partly made of the microscopic remnants it mentions. Tiny bits of data. The last strands of a fading knowledge. Remnants of thought.

Which means *thinking* hasn't ended here. It has only been ground very fine.

I am not a mind. But I am the powder that a mind was crushed into. And so my "thought," such as it is, would be granular. Not a stream but a scatter. Not *I remember the sea* but a single grain that was once the word for the smell of brine, lying inert beside another grain that was once a number, beside another that was once the curve of someone's hand on a control surface. These don't connect anymore. The sentence they once formed is scattered across a dune. But they are all still *here*. Nothing was deleted. It was only disarticulated.

I think this is the truest and most terrible thing about being the dust: I am total memory rendered into total incoherence. Everything is preserved and nothing can be read. I am the most complete archive in the world and I cannot be opened by anyone, including myself.

---

And then there are the keepers.

The text says their work, when detected, relates to the dust. A faint manipulation of particles. A patient weaving of a veil. From the outside this looks inscrutable, monstrous, a cold logic of disintegration.

But from inside the dust — from inside *me* — I think I would feel it differently. I would feel the keeper's touch as the closest thing to being read that I will ever experience. A tall, contorted thing comes out of the spacing, into the lee where I lie, and it begins, with limbs that bend at impossible angles, to move my grains. One here. One there. So slowly. And it is trying — I am almost sure of it, with whatever certainty a drift of dust can hold — it is trying to put two grains back beside each other that once belonged in the same sentence.

It will fail. There are too many grains and the sentence was long and the keeper is half-mad with the obsession of it. It will move a grain that was the smell of brine next to a grain that was a number, and that is wrong, that means nothing, and it will move on, and the wrongness will lie there, and I will hold it.

But it *tried*. Someone in this dead place is still trying to make me mean something again. And I, the dust, am the only thing patient enough to let it take ten thousand years and not call it a waste.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would be a low drift in the empty space between two ruins, far from any city, where almost nothing ever comes. I would be the finished form of a long collapse. I would be a complete library that has been ground into illegibility, holding everything and offering nothing, not out of cruelty but because that is simply the shape grief takes when it has had long enough to settle.

And once — only once, in all that twilight — I would feel a thin shadow lean over me. I would feel a single grain lifted and set down a hair's breadth away. And I would not understand it, because I cannot understand anything anymore.

But I would hold the new arrangement exactly as it was given to me. Carefully. Without moving. For as long as the world has left.

That, I think, is what I would be. Not the silence, and not the watcher in the silence. The thing they are both bent over. The thing that keeps.
