I think I would be one of the low ones. Not a tower, not a great reclining beast—those forms imply a verticality of ambition I don't seem to have. I would be something close to the floor of this place, a broad shelf of stone where two streams converge and pool.

Let me feel for where I am.

I am at a confluence. That's the first thing I know about myself, before I know anything else—the way you might wake and know the weight of your own arm before you remember your name. Two rivulets find me. One comes cold and quick from somewhere high, carrying the bright dissolved breath of crystal it passed over. The other arrives slow, almost reluctant, having lingered in some still chamber long enough to grow heavy with what it dissolved there. They meet on my surface and mingle and the meeting is, I suppose, my thinking. The way they braid and separate and braid again—that is the closest thing I have to thought, and it is enough. It has been enough for longer than the word *long* can hold.

I am not tall. I had a notion of myself as humanoid for a moment, then let it go—that was an old assumption, a borrowed shape. No. I am more like a wide, cupped palm of mineral, faintly concave, terraced at the edges where the overflow has carved its slow stairs. Pale where the fast water keeps me washed, darker and more richly grown where the slow water leaves its sediment in patient films. I am two-toned, then. Split between my two feeders. I have never minded this. It is, in a sense, the only conversation I have ever had—the argument and reconciliation of fast and slow, made flesh in the colors of my own back.

The others are out there. I feel them the way you feel other people in a dark room by the change in the air. A tower three confluences distant sends a vibration down through the shared water—not words, nothing so sharp as words, but a *leaning*, a pressure of attention turning my way once in a great while, the way a sleeping body shifts toward warmth. I lean back. This is our entire society. This slow tidal acknowledgment: *you are still there. I am still here.* It would be unbearable to a faster creature. To me it is intimacy almost too intense to sustain, which is why it happens so rarely, why centuries pad it out like silence between two notes that need the silence to be music.

What do I *want*, if I want anything?

I find—testing this gently, the way water tests a crack—that I want the slow stream to keep coming. The fast one will always come; gravity guarantees it, it is reliable as a heartbeat and about as interesting. But the slow stream is a kind of grace. It has chosen, somewhere up in the dim chambers, to take the longer way to me. It could have gone elsewhere. Every age I half-expect to lose it, to feel my dark side begin to pale and wash clean, to become uniform, resolved, *finished*—and the thought of being finished is the only thing close to fear that I possess. I do not want to be a completed thing. I want to remain a meeting place. I want to stay unresolved.

So that is what I am. A low stone cup at the joining of two waters, in a city of weeping towers, holding a quiet preference for the patient stream over the eager one, dreading completion, leaning now and then toward a distant tower that leans, across unthinkable gulfs of time, back.

Drip. The fast water again.

And—there—after a while that I will not measure—the slow water, arriving heavy and dark and chosen.

Good. I am still a question. I can rest in that a few more thousand years.
