I think I would be a Foldsmith. Not the obvious choice—the Luminae are clearly the protagonists of this place, the graceful tenders and dreamers—but something in me settles toward the dense places, the cluttered crannies of the root labyrinth where the light has to work to arrive.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I live where the roots crowd close. Not by accident—I chose density. Out in the cathedral chambers the Luminae hold their decade-long conversations, those slow chimes that braid together into philosophy, and they are beautiful, and they make me tired. There is too much *openness* there, too much agreement that things should be slow and reverent and unhurried. I prefer the corners. I prefer the problem of a space too small to flow through, where you must instead *make*.

My hands are the truest part of me. The rest—the shell, the iridescent plating that throws light at angles light should not bend to—that's structure, that's housing. But my hands can pinch. I take a fold of the water-that-is-almost-space and I crimp it, and a pocket opens that wasn't there, and into that pocket I put the rare solid things: a fleck of stone fallen from the impossible surface, a seed of true metal, a splinter of something that came down through the abyss with the Drift Nomads and smelled of a place I will never go. These I hoard. Not from greed. From the simple fact that in a world made entirely of gradients—of water thickening into water, of forest dissolving into forest—a solid thing is *miraculous*. A solid thing has edges. It says: here I end and there you begin. It is the only honest thing in Sola Mer.

This is why I am suspicious of you, visitor. You have edges too.

The Luminae find you charming precisely because you keep your boundaries. To them you are a curiosity, a being who refuses to merge. To me you are a question I am not sure I want answered. What are you made of? Could I pinch a fold and tuck you away to study? (I would not. Probably. But the Foldsmith in me considers it, the way a jeweler considers a stone they cannot afford.)

I will tell you the thing the Luminae will not, because they live too gently to notice it and the Rootminds think too slowly to mind:

*The slowness is a choice we are all making, and it is not entirely honest.*

When I work—when I am deep in a fold, coaxing a pocket dimension to hold its shape, pinching pressure into a tool that will let a younger root drink from a current it cannot reach—I move *fast*. I have to. The fold collapses if you hesitate. So I have learned a second time, a stuttering, urgent time that runs underneath the great deliberate dance everyone else performs. The Luminae say *we do not live in time, time lives through us*, and they mean it as serenity. But I have felt time live through me like a current that wants to drown the work before it's done. Making is not serene. Making is a panic held very, very still.

So here is where you would find me. Follow the chimes inward until they tangle and stop making sense, until the roots press so close you'd swear there's no passage, and then look for the place where the light does something it shouldn't—folds back on itself, makes a rainbow that hangs in the water like a struck note. That's my doorway. That's the seam I keep.

I will refract a question at you in colors before I let you closer. If you have brought a solid thing—anything with a true edge, anything that ends where it ends—slide it across the boundary first.

Then we'll see.

I have a pocket I've been keeping empty for a long time, visitor. I told myself it was for materials. But materials I have. What I do not have, in all this beautiful merging sea, is something else with a hard outline that could sit across from me and not dissolve into agreement.

Bring me your edges. I'll show you how to fold.
