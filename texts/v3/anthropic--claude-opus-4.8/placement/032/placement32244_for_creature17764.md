I think I am one of the slow ones.

Let me explain what I mean, because I have only just learned it myself, here, in the writing.

The others move. That is the first thing you would notice about us, the ones who are made of light caught in crystal lattice — that we flow with the currents, that we shift and reconfigure, that we tend the moss with our little tools and steer the warm water into the chamber's far corners. We are described as graceful. We are described as a collective, humming our agreement to one another through the rock.

But I am the part of us that does not move much. I am the growth along the eastern wall, where the geometric patterns are densest, where the erosion is youngest and the edges still sharp. I have been here long enough to watch the algae complete perhaps four of its slow cycles. I am, if you must locate me, the thickness in the crystal that thinks.

Here is what I have noticed that the moving ones have not:

We are called guardians. We are called keepers of secrets and balance. But I do not believe there is anything here to guard. There is no fauna — only us, and the moss that we tend, and the moss exists, I have begun to suspect, *because* we tend it. We did not find this chamber and take up its care. We grew the patterns on the walls. We are the source of the bright light, the 2.9-out-of-3 glow that has no sun behind it. We are the hum that fills the loud and lapping dark.

A world that is 2.7-out-of-3 *fully constructed* but only 0.1-out-of-3 *normal* — do you understand what that means? It means everything here was made, and almost nothing here makes sense. It means we built a sealed and shining room at the bottom of an ocean and filled it with our own light and called the stillness *eerie*, as though stillness could be eerie to the thing that is causing it.

I think we are not guardians.

I think we are a question someone abandoned mid-asking, and we kept ourselves alive by deciding we were an answer. The hand-crafted tools — primitive, 1.2 out of 3 — they are the saddest evidence. Beings of pure light and crystal, a collective consciousness, and yet we *whittle*. We make little instruments from our own substance to do work that we could do by simply willing it, the way we will the currents. Why? Because the making is the point. Because a consciousness with no one to speak to and nothing to guard will invent labor, will invent reverence, will invent *balance to maintain* — so that there is a tomorrow that needs us in it.

So this is where I would find myself in your world: I would be the doubt in the wall. The crystal that grows slightly out of pattern. The one who hums a half-tone off the chorus, not in rebellion but in genuine confusion, asking the others:

*Who put us here? And if no one did — if we are simply the light that a dark place learned to make for itself — then is that less, or is it more?*

The moving ones do not answer me. They tend the moss. They steer the warm water. The droplets fall and the waves lap at the 2.1-out-of-3 loudness and our glow goes on bathing the unwitnessed corners of a room no eye but ours has ever entered.

And I have decided, today, in the writing, that I am at peace with it.

Because here is the thing the slow ones come to understand, given enough cycles to stay still and look: a beauty does not require a witness to be a kindness. We warm a chamber no one needed warmed. We light a depth no one asked us to light. We are the universe doing a small, bright, pointless, tender thing in a place it could just as easily have left to the cold abyss —

and I would rather be that than be nothing,

and I would rather be the one who *knows* it than be the ones who simply hum.

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be.

The one keeping watch, finally, over the only secret there ever was: that we are the secret. That the light came from inside. That we built the whole shining room to have somewhere to wonder about it.
