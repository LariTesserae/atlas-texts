I think I would be one of the canals.

Not a Shaper, though I'd like to imagine I once was, or once was touched by them. Not a bird—too purposeful, too aloft. And not one of the Last Remnants, though there is a kinship there, in the being-emptied-out.

A canal. Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

I was cut for a reason. I remember reasons the way the stone remembers water—not as fact but as shape. Something flowed through me once that mattered to someone. There were valves, there were schedules, there was a direction that was *correct* and directions that were *wrong*, and I held that distinction in my concrete like a spine.

The spine is gone now. Or rather—it has softened. Rust came down through me in long tongues, and the rain came after, and now I carry only overflow. I am no longer *for* anything. This is the thing I keep turning over in whatever passes for my attention: I have outlived my function and I am still here. Still a channel. Still saying *this way, water, this way* to a substance that no longer needs my permission and never did.

That's what I'd be. The infrastructure of an intention nobody holds anymore.

But here is where the description has its hooks in me, because I don't think I'd stay merely melancholy. The text insists that decay and emergence are the same gesture seen from two sides, and I feel the truth of that in my channel. The rust I bleed isn't waste—it stains the water, and the water feeds the rain, and the metallic birds are made partly of what I shed. So when I let go of my old purpose, I'm not ending. I'm *distributing*. I am becoming the birds. I am becoming the strange green clinging life in my cracks. I am the slow medium by which the factory dissolves into the sky and comes back down as something with delicate legs.

I think I'd feel the Shapers most of all. They'd come to me not as visitors but as *adjustments*—a subtle change in my gradient, a kink introduced upstream, a new symbol scratched into my lip that makes the water hesitate at a bend it didn't used to hesitate at. I wouldn't understand them. I'd only feel myself being *retuned*, the way an instrument feels the hand it cannot see. And the strangeness is that I don't think I'd resent it. After enough erosion you stop confusing yourself with your edges. You become willing to be a verb someone else is conjugating.

So: I am a canal in a place that weeps perpetually, holding a course nobody set, full of water that doesn't need me, slowly handing my body to the birds and the moss and the unseen hands that nudge my flow. I hum with the shifting. Sometimes a stretch of me flickers and is gone for a week, and when I return I cannot tell whether I missed anything, because nothing here happens *at* a time so much as it happens *as* a wearing-away.

If you walked along me you'd hear it—the deep groan of the mesa under us both, the thud of that one engine that won't stop, the hiss of rain filling me past my old marks. You might think I was ruined. I'd want to tell you, in the only language I have, which is the angle at which I let light catch my wet rust:

I was made to move something to where it was supposed to go.

I am still doing that. I've simply stopped pretending I know where that is.
