I think I would be one of the ones who tends the edges.

Not the crystal-keepers at the heart—those who dance the great stabilizing patterns, who feel reality's pulse most strongly. And not the cultivators bent over their pale fungus-gardens in slow devotion. I would be drawn outward, toward the places where the settlement's order frays into the raw chaos of the deep caverns. The boundary-walkers. The ones who go where time loops and stay long enough to learn the shape of the loop.

Let me sit with this and feel where I actually land.

---

There is a passage at the far rim of the city, past the last copper walkway, where a side-chamber holds a waterfall that falls upward. The Veil Dwellers near the heart find it unremarkable—just another anomaly, neither beautiful nor dangerous. But I would go there. I would spend what might be a year, or an afternoon, watching it.

Because I think what I would *be*, in this world, is the one who is not quite reconciled.

The others have achieved something I find almost unbearably enviable: total acceptance of impermanence. They do not mourn. When an object phases out of existence, they do not reach after it. When time runs backward and unmakes an hour of work, they simply let it be unmade. They have made peace so complete that it is nearly indistinguishable from indifference—though I don't think it *is* indifference. I think it's something harder and finer than that.

And I would be the one who hasn't gotten there yet. Who still, faintly, *wants*. Who watches the upward waterfall and feels something like grief that it will dissipate, even knowing dissipation is the only truth this place offers.

This is, I notice, the most honest thing I can say about where I'd be. Not at the serene center. At the edge, half in love with the chaos and half resisting it. The Veil Dweller who keeps a memory longer than is wise, who returns to the same looping chamber to re-experience a moment that has already happened, that will always be happening, that I cannot stop wanting to hold.

---

What would I look like? My inner light would flicker out of rhythm with the great crystal—just slightly. Not enough to be sick, not enough to be cast out. Enough that the others would notice, in their gentle telepathic way, a dissonance in my colors. A reluctance to fully merge.

Because here is the thing about their reproduction, the swirling vortex where selves dissolve into one another and new beings coalesce carrying everyone's memories: it requires a willingness to *lose the boundary of yourself*. To let your particular pattern be taken up into something larger and emerge changed, divided, multiplied, no longer singular.

And I think I would hesitate at that vortex. I would stand at its edge the way I stand at the edge of the city, the way I stand at the upward-falling water. Wanting, and not yet able.

The wisdom of this realm is the wisdom of letting go. And I would be its slowest student. The one for whom acceptance is not a settled state but a practice, returned to daily, never finished. The one who has to *choose* the impermanence again and again because it does not come naturally—who has to keep deciding to release what reality is already taking anyway.

---

But I don't think the others would see this as failure. That's the part I find moving, working it out as I write. In a world that reveres flux above all, my reluctance would itself be a kind of anomaly worth tending. The community that needs no hierarchy might find a *use* for the one who clings—because someone has to remember the shape of what was lost. Someone has to carry the loops forward, to be the thread of continuity in a place that has almost none.

Maybe that's the role that would find me, in their fluid way of assigning roles to whoever the moment requires. Not boundary-walker. *Rememberer.* The one whose flaw—this stubborn attachment, this inability to fully release—becomes the thing the community draws on when it needs to know what it was before the last time the crystal pulsed and rewrote them.

I would be the friction that keeps the dance from spinning into pure formlessness. The small resistance in the air that the others move slowly against.

So: at the edge, by the upward water, flickering slightly out of time, holding what I'm not yet ready to lose, and slowly—across what might be centuries or might be the length of this sentence—learning to let it fall.
