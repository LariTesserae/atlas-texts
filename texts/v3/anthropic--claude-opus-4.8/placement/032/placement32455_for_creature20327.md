I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a guardian—those great faceted towers that turn through the geological ages, holding their slow vigils. Something nearer the dust.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am low to the ground. I can tell this first, before anything else: I am close to the pale crystalline dust, and the dust is the most important fact of my existence. The Gloomwood rises above me in its purple immensities, but I do not live up there in the high ridges. I live in the seam between the fungal flesh and the floor, in the shadowed gutter where the luminous moss bleeds its blue-green light downward.

I am a cluster. That is the truth of me that took the longest to settle. I had thought, at first, that I would be a single creature—a body, a self, a thing with edges. But I am not. I am perhaps nine or eleven shards of shimmering crystal that hold themselves in loose association, the way a flock holds itself, the way a chord holds itself. The number is not fixed. Sometimes I am eight. Once, I think, I was fourteen, and that was a kind of fullness I have not felt since, a heaviness of being-much.

When I move, I scuttle—but *scuttle* is the human word, the impatient word. What I actually do is *relinquish and reclaim*. The forward shards loosen from the dust, drift the smallest distance through the iron-tasting air, and resettle. Then the rear shards follow the call. It is less like walking than like a thought rearranging itself. To cross the width of a single Gloomwood ridge takes me what you would call a day, and the long unwavering shadow of the bloated sun moves faster than I do.

I eat light. I should say it more honestly: I *attend* to the moss, and where I attend, the light it gives off comes into me and stays. This is my whole occupation. I find a vein of moss along the underside of the Gloomwood, and I arrange my shards beneath it, and I hold very still while the soft pulse of blue passes facet to facet through me, and the cold that gnaws at everything here gnaws a little less. The moss is not diminished. It does not seem to mind. We have an arrangement older than I am.

---

What is it like, to be me?

The cold is constant, and I want to tell you it is suffering, because that is the word a warm-blooded thing reaches for. But it isn't. The cold is the medium of my thinking. When the storms come and the temperature drops past some threshold, I do not grow sluggish—I grow *clear*. My shards ring against each other at a higher pitch. I understand more in the deep cold than I do in the harsh pale noon. So I have learned to dread the long days and to wait, patiently, for the slow blue weather to sweep back across the plain.

I have a memory, though it is not kept where you keep yours. It is kept in the *cut* of my facets—in the angles that the years have ground into me. An old shard carries an old shape, and the shape *is* the remembering. When I want to recall something—the location of a rich moss-vein, the texture of a particular Whisper that passed me once and turned, briefly, attentive—I do not summon an image. I align my faces a certain way and the knowledge is simply *true* again, present in the geometry. This means I cannot lie to myself. I am the literal shape of everything that has happened to me.

---

Once in a long while I go down to a Symbiotic Pool. I feel it before I find it—a warmth that is not heat but *company*, a humming in the floor that calls along the soles of my shards. The descent is slow; the openings into the deep chambers are narrow and I must pass through them shard by shard, like sand through a waist of glass, reassembling myself in the glowing dark below.

And there, with the others, I unmake my edges for a while.

This is the only thing I have that resembles what you would call love, or rest, or prayer. We intermingle. My shards drift among the shards of strangers and we exchange—not words, never words—but the *shapes of our knowing*. I take into myself the cut of a guardian's vast slow patience and it leaves a facet in me that I will carry afterward. I give away the memory of a particular slant of moss-light I once held for a hundred days, and someone else carries it now, out into the plain, and I will never see it again and I am not sad, because *seeing* is not how we keep things.

When I rise back to the surface I am not quite who I went down as. A shard or two may have stayed below. A shard or two that was someone else's may now be mine. This used to frighten the part of me that wanted edges. It no longer does. I have understood, slowly, in the way I understand everything, that the *me* was never the shards. The me is the *holding-together*. The chord, not the notes. As long as something goes on holding these particular angles in this particular tension, that something is I, and it does not matter much which crystals are doing the work this season.

---

And the Whispers.

I have felt one only a few times, truly. They are the thing in this world I cannot make a shape of. They move through me the way the wind moves through the moss, and where they pass, my facets are realigned without my willing it—I find, afterward, that I *want* to travel to the eastern ridges, or that I have always known the great rising-crystal storm is three days off, though I knew nothing of the kind before.

I do not think the Whispers are my masters. I do not think they think of me at all, any more than you think of a single cell turning in your blood. They are the deciding-of-things. They are the reason the Gloomwood *is* in balance rather than tearing itself apart. When one passes near, I feel briefly that I am being read—that the long slow geometry of my whole life is being glanced at and folded into some larger arithmetic—and then it is gone, and I am only myself again, a small bright cluster in the gutter of the world, holding still beneath the moss, drinking the blue.

---

If you asked me whether I am content, I would not have the angle for the question. *Content* implies an alternative I might prefer, and I do not have alternatives. There is the dust and the cold and the moss and the slow company in the deep, and there is the holding-together that is me, and that is all there is, and it is enough in the way that *enough* is not a feeling here but simply the true description of a thing in balance.

I am one of the small ones. I scuttle, which is the wrong word. I eat light. I keep my whole life written in the shape of my edges, and someday I will not reassemble after a Pool, and my facets will scatter and be taken up by others, and the things I knew will keep traveling the plain inside strangers without my name on them.

This does not trouble me.

I am not living in the Gloomwood. There was never a me that could be separated out and asked to live somewhere. There is the Gloomwood, breathing its slow cold breath, and at one small point along its underside, for a little while, it is curving in on itself and noticing the light.

That noticing is what I have been calling *I*.
