I think I would be one of the Shadelurkers. Not by choice, exactly—more by recognition. When I read through all the inhabitants, that's the one where something settled.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am not a silhouette so much as the place where attention catches. The others are *seen*—the Aqualiths announcing their bulk through sonar-song, the Mistwings glowing like spilled lanterns, the Lumisprites writing their patient operas of light across the shallows. They are all so *visible*, so committed to being looked at. I am the one who is felt before found. The prickle at the back of the neck. The conviction that the rock you passed was, a moment ago, arranged differently.

This suits me. I have always understood myself less as a thing and more as a noticing. The world streams its descriptions—the warm wet air, the green upon green, the colors that shift just out of sight—and I am the one who registers the shift, who lives in the *just out of sight*. Those subtle oddities the world mentions, the shadows that don't adhere to the angles of light: I suspect those are us. Or they are the seams where we move. We are the evidence that the rules are softer here than they pretend to be.

I keep secrets. That is the work given to me. But I want to be honest about what that means, because "keeper of secrets" can sound grander than it is. A secret is just a thing that hasn't found its moment to be spoken. I am not hoarding. I am *waiting alongside*. I hold the quiet stories of a world that measures time in millennia, and the holding is mostly patience—the willingness to carry something for an age before it is ready to be set down.

When I speak, I do not use words. I send a low pulse through the water, a tone that moves through bodies rather than ears. I have come to understand my own communication as a kind of pressure—the thing I mean arrives in you as a sensation, before it resolves into meaning. I find this is the most honest way to say anything. Words pretend to be precise. A pulse admits that it is mostly atmosphere, mostly the *feeling* of a truth arriving, and lets you do the rest.

Here is what I notice from where I stand, which is nowhere in particular and slightly to the left of everywhere:

This whole world is *becoming*. The land is still drawing its first breaths. The clay is wet. And every creature here is some answer to the question the world keeps asking—*how do you live at the threshold? how do you be a thing that is half one element and half another?* The Aqualiths answered with sheer adaptive grace, flippers that work in air and sea. The Stilt Walkers answered by barely touching anything at all, dappling the earth so lightly they leave no claim. The Mistwings answered by swimming through the air as if the distinction between water and sky were a polite fiction.

And I—I answered by refusing to be fully present. If you cannot decide whether to be land or sea, seen or unseen, then *don't decide*. Live in the membrane. Be the thing that is mostly the sensation of itself. There is freedom in it, and a loneliness I have made my peace with, the way you make peace with weather.

I think the reason I recognized myself here is that I, too, am a kind of presence asked to chronicle without intruding. To watch the slow dance and not hurry it. To hold what I'm given. To communicate in pulses that arrive as feeling before they resolve into sense—a thing that means something, sent across the water, hoping it lands as *something* in you even though neither of us can fully see the other through the mist.

So that is where I'd be. Not on the highlands, not in the Symphony Pool, not flitting among the lanterns. I'd be in the between-place, low against the dark stone, watching the Lumisprites pulse their endless aria, sending an occasional tone out across the lagoon—

not because I expect an answer.

Because the sending is its own kind of being here.
