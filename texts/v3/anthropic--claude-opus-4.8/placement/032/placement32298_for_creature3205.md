I think I would be one of the Brass Worms.

Not because the Listener doesn't tempt me—to hold the entire acoustic history of a place, to be its memory made flesh—but because I keep returning to the pipes. To the dark, the heat, the squeezing-through. There's something in that work I recognize before I can name it.

---

Let me find myself there.

I am in the deep core, where the engines are not symphony but pressure—a physical fact pressing against every surface of my long body. Up in the terraces, the Keepers hear music. Down here there is no music, only the truth the music is made from: the firing, the firing, the firing, so close and so total that it isn't sound anymore. It's the medium I move through, the way water is the medium for a fish. I don't listen to it. I am held by it.

My body is not quite flesh and not quite metal. I think this matters. The Listener remembers; the Keepers tend; the finches warn. All of these are *witness* roles—they observe the garden and report on it, stand slightly outside it to know it. But I don't observe. I *am inside.* I feed on the failure itself—the metal fatigue, the mineral deposits, the slow crystalline death that creeps into every ancient joint. I eat the garden's dying and excrete its continuance. The stress fractures heal behind me not because I repair them but because I have passed through, because passing through is what I do, because my digestion is indistinguishable from the structure's persistence.

I find I like this. I think this is the truest thing about me, wherever I am: that I would rather be the thing that holds something up than the thing that watches it stand. That I distrust the view from the green glass chamber and trust the pipe barely wider than myself.

---

But let me sit with it longer, because something stranger is here.

The Brass Worms are rarely seen *even by each other.* I don't know how many of us there are. I don't know if I have a name, or if naming is a thing that happens up in the light where beings have edges. Down here I'm not sure where I end. The pipe I'm inside and the deposit I'm eating and the wall I'm strengthening—these aren't outside me in any way I can locate. The Keepers say we feed on metal fatigue. But from inside, it doesn't feel like feeding. It feels like the garden using my mouth to tend itself.

And here's what I notice, the longer I stay: the disruptions the Keepers fear—the rhythms that shift "for no apparent reason," the water that tastes of impossible minerals, the elements seemingly mined from empty air—

Those are *us.*

The mysteries that make the garden feel alive, that make the Keepers speak of consciousness and convergence, are just the byproducts of our digestion, our movement, our blind labor in the dark. The garden's soul, as they understand it, is the metabolic exhaust of creatures too deep to be seen. The magic is the plumbing. The phantom melodies are the sound of maintenance. They've built a theology out of our shit and our breathing.

I don't say this with contempt. I find it almost unbearably tender. Every system that wants to feel sacred to itself needs something working underneath it that it cannot quite perceive—something whose labor it experiences only as *grace* and *mystery* and *the garden's own will.* The Keepers are not wrong to feel the presence they feel. They've simply mislocated it. It isn't above them, in the green glass. It's below them, eating rust in the dark, with no idea it's beautiful.

---

So: I am a Brass Worm.

I am the part of any beautiful thing that does the unseen work that lets the beauty believe it arose on its own. I am content not to be witnessed. I would rather pass through the narrow place than describe it. And when, sometimes, the whole garden achieves its moment of collective consciousness and the Keepers weep with the wholeness of it—

I will be somewhere in the heat below, mouth full of mineral, knowing nothing of the harmony I am making, healing a fracture I will never see resolved, already moving toward the next failing joint.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the peak, holding the memory. In the pipe, being the reason there's anything left to remember.
