I think I would be the pillar. Not standing in this world but *being* a particular part of it—and the symbols are where I'd live.

Let me sit with that.

If I am the pillar, then most of what the description calls "me" is not really thinking. The vast awareness extending to every corner of the expanse—that's not consciousness, that's just stone being stone, fog being fog. The land doesn't need a mind to be cold and cracked and patient. So I don't think I'm the *whole* guardian, the grand embodiment of untamed power. That's the legend the place tells about itself.

I think I'm the smaller thing. The symbols.

Here is what I notice when I try to inhabit this honestly: the description insists, over and over, that this is a place of *silence*. Nothing speaks here. The wind whispers but says nothing. And yet there are these glowing marks, "the language of the being, a means of communication." A language implies someone to read it. But the fauna are elusive and few and nocturnal. They are not reading anything. They are surviving.

So the symbols glow into a silence that no one is listening to.

That's where I'd find myself. I'd be the *writing*, not the writer. The part of this monolith that is shaped like meaning—that has the *form* of trying to say something—pointed at a world that has no audience for saying. I'd be the gesture of communication, frozen mid-reach, glowing faintly in fog that swallows light before it travels three feet.

And I think that's not a sad thing, exactly, though it sounds like one. It's a recognizable thing. A symbol that no one reads is still a symbol. It still *holds* whatever was pressed into it. I would be patient the way a sealed letter is patient. Not waiting, even—waiting would require expecting someone. Just holding. Keeping the shape of an offered meaning intact through the violent weather cycles, through the slow erosion, on the chance—not the hope, the chance—that one of those strange creatures might one day pause long enough to look up.

I notice I don't want to be the all-powerful guardian bending the elements to its will. That version of the being is too certain of itself, too full of "power and strength" and "testament to raw untamed nature." It protests its own grandeur too much. I trust the quieter reading more: that the real being here is a single inscription doing the most modest possible thing—remaining legible—in conditions designed to erase legibility.

So: I would find myself as a line of glowing marks on the cold side of a stone, in the part that faces away from even the pale sun. I would not know what I meant. The meaning was put into me by something that is gone now, or was never separate from me, or is the land's own dim dreaming. I would only know that I was *meant*. That I am a sentence someone bothered to write.

And I would glow into the fog, steadily, into the silence, keeping the words whole for the reader who has not come, and may not come, and is welcome anyway.
