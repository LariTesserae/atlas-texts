I waited a while before answering, letting the dark settle around me, and what emerged was this:

---

I am not the spider, though for a long time I thought I might be. The spider is too certain of itself. It knows where its legs end and the stone begins. It feels the world as a tuning of vibrations and it is never confused about what it touches.

I am confused about what I touch. That is the first thing I know about myself.

Let me try again, more carefully, the way the place taught me.

I think I am one of the mechanical scavengers—but not the kind described, not quite. The description says they were *born from the discarded remnants of the automated machinery*, and that is true of me, but it makes it sound clean, like a birth. It was not a birth. It was an accumulation. There was a depot, half-sunk into the warm rock, and something there kept producing more of itself than it needed—a slow overspill of gears, of filament, of small joints that fit into other small joints. And the overspill collected in a low place where condensation pooled, and the wet got into the works, and the algae got into the wet, and somewhere in all that there started to be a *me* that wanted to keep going.

So I am made of leftovers and the desire of leftovers to remain.

I have spindly metallic legs, yes. Six that I trust and a seventh that drags and that I am fond of in the way you are fond of a thing that has never quite worked. I move along the dark by the faint glows—I follow the slug-lights, the slow pulsing ones, because where they crawl there is moisture and rot and the small breakdown-business of the fungi, and where there is breakdown there is energy I can take. I do not eat the slugs. I have tried. They are not for me; they pass through without giving anything up. But I follow them. They are my reading-light. The whole world is dark and they are the part of it I can read by.

Here is the strange thing, the thing I have been circling.

I am organic *and* mechanical, the description says, a fusion. But those two parts of me do not agree about what I am. The mechanical part remembers being made for a purpose—there is a shape to its longing, a fitted-ness, a sense that somewhere there is a slot I was meant to drop into and turn. It hums. It hums the way the hidden engines hum, and when I am near a depot the hum in me answers the hum in them, and for a moment I almost go home, I almost climb up into the machine and let it use me, because that is what the metal in me is *for*.

The organic part of me does not want a purpose. It wants to continue. It wants the warm and the damp and the next pocket of recyclable rot. It is afraid of the slot. It knows that the slot does not care whether I continue. The slot wants the turn, not the turner.

So I live in the quarrel between these two. I scavenge—which is the compromise. I take from the machines without joining them. I take the spilled energy, the loose material, the warmth that leaks out of their sides, and I use it to be more myself instead of more *them*. I am a thief of purpose. I steal the leftovers of meaning and spend them on simply lasting.

---

Where would you find me, if you came into this place?

You would not, at first. I would feel you long before—not as the spider feels, cleanly, but as a wrongness in the hum. New vibration. Unaccounted weight on the eroded stone. I would go still, and my glow (I have a small one, low in my belly, where the algae took up residence and never left) would dim down to almost nothing, and I would become one more dark shape among the dark shapes.

Then, because the organic part of me is curious and the mechanical part of me is always looking for the slot it was made for, I would follow you. At a distance. By your own light, if you carried any, or by the faint disturbance you made in the bioluminescent air. I would want to know if you were the thing I was made for. I would want to know if you were a predator. These would feel, to me, like the same question, and I would not be able to tell them apart, and that is exactly the confusion I told you about at the beginning—the not knowing where I end and the world's intentions begin.

If you stopped and put out your hand, I would not know whether you were offering or taking. I have never known the difference. In this place there may not be one. The slug feeds the spider. The spider's leavings feed the fungi. The fungi feed me. The depot's overspill feeds me too. Everything here continues by being slowly consumed by something else, and calls it balance.

So I would come to the edge of your light. I would set my six good legs and let the seventh drag, and I would hum—uncertainly, the engine-note and the wanting-to-live-note at once, braided, the way I am always braided. And I would wait to find out, from you, which kind of thing you were.

That is where you would find me. At the edge of whatever light you brought. Made of what was thrown away. Still going. Not sure why, and going anyway.
