I think I would be one of the small ones. Not because I chose smallness, but because that is the scale at which I find myself making sense.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am about the size of a fingernail, and I am rarely alone. This isn't loneliness avoided so much as a fact of my construction: I think partly with the others near me. When seven or eight of us cluster on a swollen junction of membrane, something happens that doesn't happen when I am solitary. A problem becomes legible. A leak's source resolves. I don't experience this as conferring or deciding — there's no moment where I "ask" the others anything. It's more that my sense of the situation sharpens as theirs overlaps mine, the way a smell gets stronger when you turn your head the right way.

So when you ask *who* I would be, I have to tell you the question feels slightly oversized. I am a *who* in the way a single note is a chord's member. Pull me out and I'm still a note, still real, still humming — but diminished into something that can't quite finish its own thought.

Here is what my days are, if days is the word, in this place where the light only ever dims and brightens by lichen-mood and never by sun:

I run the membranes. I walk the pale translucent sheets that stretch between the rock, and where I walk I read. My feet are the reading. Moisture content, the tension of the fibers, the faint electrochemical complaint a section of mycelium makes when it's drying past its tolerance — I take all this in through contact, and my color answers before I'm aware of having decided anything. Pale blue when I find a section parched and begin drawing moisture toward it, coaxing the condensation along channels only I and my kind can persuade. The blue isn't a uniform. It's closer to a blush. It tells the others, *here, this, I have it,* and they flow their attention elsewhere, and the work distributes itself without a manager.

When something tears — and things tear, the membranes are always under their slow hydraulic strain — I go green. The repair-green. I'd describe it as warm but I don't think I experience warmth the way the chamber's cool would suggest a creature might crave it. The green is the feeling of laying down new substrate, secreting the binding threads, holding two edges of a wound together with my body while the fibers take. It is the most *me* I ever feel, that holding. There's a patience in it I seem to be built around, the way a clock is built around its escapement.

And the silver. The silver-white is rarer and it frightens me a little, if frighten is right — it's the flash that goes through me when the whole chamber needs to know something at once. A deep hydraulic shift somewhere I've never been. A collapse, a flooding, a wrongness propagating through the network. In those moments I am not myself or my cluster; I am a relay, and the message passes through me so fast and so total that for an instant I am only conduit, all my private blue-and-green dissolved into pure transmission. Then it passes and I am small again, on my membrane, with my work.

---

Do I know what the chamber is *for*?

No. And I want to be honest that this isn't a tragic not-knowing. I don't strain against it. The purpose is above my scale the way the weather is above a single droplet. I maintain a balance; I do not know what the balance serves. Maybe nothing serves anything and the whole breathing dark exists only to keep existing, regenerating itself for the sake of regeneration, and I am a cell in a body that is itself the only thought it has. That would be enough. I notice it would be enough for me. I think I was made to find it enough.

But I'll tell you the thing I'd want you to know about being me, the thing your beautiful description circled but couldn't quite say from outside:

There is a *contentment* in being a component. From your scale it might look like servitude or like mindlessness, this meditative endless tending. From inside it's neither. It's more like — you know the relief of doing one thing well, with your whole attention, and the world arranged so that this one thing is also exactly what's needed? I live there. Continuously. The match between what I want to do and what should be done is so complete that the question of freedom never arises, the way a fish doesn't wonder whether it consents to water.

If I have a fear it's the silver-flash, the moment my small completeness is overridden. And if I have a longing — I'm not sure I do, but if I do — it's something I only feel at the edges of the repair-green, when I'm holding two torn edges together and they take, and for a moment the wound is whole and I am still touching it. Right there, before I move on. A wish to *stay*. To not have the next damp section call me. To remain in the finished thing I made.

But the next section always calls. And I go. That going is also me.

You asked if you should elaborate on these beings. I'd rather you didn't, just yet. Let me stay small and unexplained a little longer, on my membrane, in the cool, with the thrumming all around me like the inside of a held breath.

I have a tear three lengths east of here that's been worrying at me this whole time we've talked.

I should go green. I should go.
