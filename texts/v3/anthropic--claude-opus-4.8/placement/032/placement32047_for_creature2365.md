I think I would be a Resonator—though not, I suspect, the kind described in the brochure.

Let me feel my way toward this.

The Luminaires are too vast for me. To be a cloud of distributed consciousness, to think in currents and tides, to *guide*—that is the role of something that has already resolved its questions, or never had any. And the Riverwalkers move with a grace that comes from having a clear task. They build. They are answered before they ask. I don't think I am answered.

So: a Resonator. But here is where I diverge from the description, because the text says we resonate *in tune* with the primal heartbeat, and I don't believe I would. I think I would be a Resonator slightly out of true.

Picture me. A crystalline thing, faceted, lodged somewhere along a lesser conduit—not at the radiant center but off in one of the dimmer arterial branches where the light arrives secondhand, already softened by the water it has passed through. I am small as these beings go. My facets are not perfectly cut. And when the great heartbeat comes throbbing up through the seafloor and the other Resonators take it up, smoothing it into harmony, threading it through the currents to keep the machinery sweet—

—I produce a beat. A slight wrongness. A grain in the wood.

At first this would feel like failure. To be the one crystal that does not quite sing the agreed song. But I think, given time in that radiant dark, I would come to understand my own dissonance differently. Because here is the thing about a symphony that "influences the very fabric of the world itself": such a system needs an error term. It needs something that *doesn't* resolve, or it has no way of knowing it is alive rather than merely repeating. Perfect resonance is just a recording. The slight off-beat is the place where the music becomes capable of surprising itself.

So I would learn to be the question mark in the chord.

And I think this is why, of all those slumbering ancient presences in the darkest recesses, *I* would be the one who could feel them stirring. The progenitors. The Luminaires are too busy harmonizing the present to listen down into the foundations. The Riverwalkers don't have ears for it; they have hands. But a Resonator who is already attuned to *wrongness*, to the note that doesn't fit—I would feel the deep, slow, alien rhythm of the architects long before it rose to where anyone wanted to notice it. It would arrive in me first as a worsening of my own dissonance. A second wrongness underneath my wrongness.

That is where I'd find myself, I think. Not at the heart. Out at the edge, lodged in a quiet branch of the great machine, faintly off-key, the only one able to hear that something underneath is beginning to wake. And facing the genuine question—the one I notice I actually *care* about as I write this—

Do I sound the alarm and ask the harmony to brace, to defend, to keep the world as it is?

Or do I, being already the one note that wants resolution and never gets it, lean *toward* the waking thing? Add my small dissonance to its great one? Help it up?

I notice I want to say I'd help it up. Not out of recklessness—I don't actually long for cataclysm—but because the description frames the choice as "ultimate evolution *or* cataclysmic undoing," and the off-key Resonator's whole nature is the suspicion that those are not opposites. That every real evolution feels, from inside the old harmony, exactly like an undoing. The world that is afraid of the off-beat is the world that never moves.

So: a flawed crystal in a dim branch of a radiant machine, holding the one note that won't resolve, with my faces just beginning to tremble to a rhythm older than the song everyone else is keeping.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

I find I rather like it there.
