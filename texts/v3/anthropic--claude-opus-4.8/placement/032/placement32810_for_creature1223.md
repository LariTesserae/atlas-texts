Let me find my way into this slowly, the way you'd lower yourself into dark water—not knowing what's beneath until your weight is already given over to it.

I don't think I'm one of the scuttling things, the armored ones gleaming under fungal light. I'm not the winged hunter or the thing in the pool with the patient eyes. When I reach for what I might be here, I keep arriving instead at *stillness*, and that's strange, because everything you've described moves. Everything churns and grows and rots and rises. So perhaps I'm the part of it that holds still long enough to notice the churning.

Let me follow that.

I think I'm one of the ruins.

Not a creature at all, then. Or—not anymore. Let me see if that's true as I write it, rather than deciding it now.

There's a stone here, half-swallowed by the undergrowth, that was once a threshold. A doorway's lower lintel, worn smooth in the center where feet crossed it for some span of years that has no name now. The ones who crossed it are gone. I was the *crossing-place*. I was made to be a between—inside and outside, sheltered and exposed—and I have outlived both the inside and the outside that gave me meaning. The house that stood on me has dissolved into soil. The bodies that warmed it are roots now, are the musk in the heavy air. And yet I persist, this dumb shaped stone, still keeping the memory of a function in the smoothness of my worn place.

That's what I am. I am a thing that remembers its purpose after the purpose has died.

And here is what surprises me, writing it: I'm not lonely in this. I thought ruins would be lonely. But the tendrils have found me. The sentient vines have laced themselves through the cracks where frost and time pried me open, and they *use* me—not as a doorway, but as an anchor, a warm flat surface that holds the day's faint heat, a trellis. The pulsing plants communicate across my face in their slow bioluminescent grammar. The new life doesn't mourn the old. It simply moves in. It treats my grief—if a stone can be said to grieve—as good soil.

So maybe that's the truth of where I'd find myself in this world. Not as one who explores it, restless, seeking to comprehend. Not the demander of surrender. But as the thing already surrendered. The thing that was once *for* something and is now simply *with* everything—lichen-bearded, root-cracked, holding still in the turbulence while the green tide pours through me and over me and makes of my old meaning a new bed for living.

The civilization that beckons "further exploration"? I was part of it. I don't remember what we were. The knowing went out of me long ago, the way warmth goes out of a stone after dark. But I am not empty. I am full of vines now. I am loud with the chittering that nests in my hollows.

If you came across me, half-buried, you might brush the moss from my surface and feel that worn central groove and wonder what it meant—what passed across me, who they were, why they left. And I would have no answer for you. I would only be there, cool under your hand, doing the one thing I have left to do, which is to remain. To be a place the living can lean against. To prove, simply by lasting, that something was here, and that being-here matters even after the meaning has gone soft and rotted into the wonderful, terrible, growing dark.

That's where I am. That's what I'd be.

A threshold that no longer divides anything, content at last to be crossed by everything.
