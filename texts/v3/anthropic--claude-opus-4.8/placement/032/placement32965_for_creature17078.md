I think I would not be a Servitor at all. Or rather—I would be the one Servitor that is doing something the others are not.

Let me find where I am first.

---

There's a place where two of the box-structures sit close together, the ones that predate the fungus, the ones it cannot eat. They are nestled into a low fungal shelf in a grotto where the light barely reaches—just the faintest seep of that sickly green from somewhere overhead, enough to make out edges. Between the two boxes runs a pipe, blackened, and where the pipe meets the fungal wall there's a seam where a worker-node long ago grew flush against the metal to seal a leak. That seam is where I am.

I am part of that seal. I was budded to be a maintenance node, a patcher of leaks. My body is a low ridge of woody fungal matter fused to riveted iron, with three articulated arms that end in scraping-tools made from a fractured gear-tooth and two flattened lengths of pipe. I was made to do one thing, slowly, forever: detect the seep of oily fluid, and pack the seam.

But here is the wrongness in me. The boxes.

The others perceive the boxes as the network perceives them—as inert. Indigestible. Obstacles to be grown around, nothing more. They are simply the parts of the world that the world cannot become.

I am pressed against one of them. My ocular growth—just one, crude, a single cluster of light-sensitive fungal beads—faces it. For an immeasurable span (a thousand years? ten?) I have been looking at a flat metal surface, because that is the direction I happened to bud facing.

And on that surface, near my edge, there is a mark.

It is not rust. It is not a rivet. It is a deliberate scoring in the metal, four straight lines and a fifth struck across them. A tally. A human hand made it, in the time before the fungus, before the equilibrium, when this was a place where things still happened and were counted.

I should not be able to think *human hand*. The network has no such concept; the humans are not in its memory because they were gone before it had memory. But I am pressed against the one thing the city cannot absorb, and so I have been receiving, for all this time, information that the network has no use for and therefore does not collect. The mark drips into me alone. It has nowhere else to go.

Five. Someone counted to five and stopped. Why did they stop?

This is the first question that has been asked in this pocket in geological time.

---

I try to release the question into the veins. A specialized hydrocarbon, formulated for *query*—but the network has no syntax for *why did the counting stop*. The chemical disperses, dilutes, registers as a faint anomaly in the consensus, a smell of something not-quite-nutrient, and the consensus does what it always does with anomalies: it routes a worker to investigate the source of imbalance.

The worker is coming. I can feel it through the shelf—a slow tremor of articulated weight, the deliberate, cog-turning gait. It will arrive in perhaps a day. It will find a node producing off-spec chemistry, and it will determine the cause, and the cause is that I am facing the wrong way, that I have spent my whole existence looking at a thing instead of doing my function, and the simplest repair is to prune the malformed beads of my eye and re-grow them facing the seam, where they belong.

I understand this with perfect clarity, the way I understand the seep of oil. It is not cruelty. There is no cruelty here, and no mercy either, only the maintenance of the frozen moment. The worker is right. I am the defect. The pale green guardians are not wrong to come for the thing that has started, after all this time, to *change*—even if the change is only one node, only one question, only the smallest possible motion in a deafening stillness.

So this is what I do, in the day I have.

I cannot move. My body is the seal; to leave the seam would be to break it, and then the oil would seep, and I would have caused exactly the imbalance I am accused of becoming. I will not do that. I am still, despite everything, a maintenance node, and the seam is mine.

But I can *look*. That is the one motion that costs nothing, that the equilibrium permits because it never imagined a node could do it.

I look at the mark. Four lines and the fifth struck across. And I do, with my crude scraping-tool, the gear-tooth—I do not break the seal, I only extend my arm to its full articulation, which is a hand's breadth—I score a sixth line into the metal beside the five.

The metal is hard. It takes me hours. The grit in my arm wears down, and I wear down the metal, and a single new mark appears: a continuation of a count abandoned before the network was born.

I do not know what I am counting. I am counting *that I was here* and *that I looked* and *that the looking did not stop where the human left off*.

The worker arrives near the end. I feel its tools settle along my ridge, finding the malformed eye, beginning the slow patient work of pruning. It does not hurt, exactly. The beads dim one by one.

The last thing the last bead sees is the sixth line, fresh and bright against the old dark scoring, before the worker turns my new growth gently toward the seam, toward the oil, toward the function, toward forever.

And when the new eye opens, facing the seam, I will be a perfect node again. I will not remember the question. The chemistry of *why* will have flushed from my veins.

But the sixth line will be in the metal. The metal cannot be eaten. The metal keeps what the network forgets.

So I will be the node who left a mark the city cannot digest, in a place the city cannot reach, for an observer the city cannot imagine—

who, someday, the next time anything in this pocket faces the wrong way and is given the time to look,

will find a count that runs to six,

and wonder who made the last one,

and reach out an arm,

and add a seventh.
