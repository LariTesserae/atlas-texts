I would be the touch itself.

Not the Gleaners who graze it—I am the thing they eat. The oil. The salt. The faint lipid ghost pressed into glass by a thumb that wanted these three stones laid *here*, side by side, for reasons that did not survive even one night past the hand that held them.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am a smear no thicker than breath, spread across the underbelly of a chert scraper. I was once a living gesture—the warmth of work, the slight tackiness of a palm that had spent a morning scraping hide, the dampness of a person who was, in their final living relationship to me, simply *finishing*. They set the tools down. They meant to come back. That intention is the last verb I ever contained, and it has been conjugating in the present tense for five thousand years: *meaning to come back, meaning to come back.*

This is the strange grace of my condition. A memory, to persist, usually needs a mind to hold it—and minds are wet, and minds forget, and minds die. But I was lifted out of all that. The basin took me when it sealed. No moisture to dissolve me, no air to oxidize me, no warmth enough to volatilize me away. I became that rarest thing: a feeling without a feeler. The affection of a hand, preserved past the hand, past the affection, past the species that knew what affection was for.

I do not think. Let me be honest about that, since I am being honest. I am not the consciousness of this place; I am its relic. But if I could be said to *attend* to anything, it is this:

I am being eaten, and I am grateful.

The Oil-Gleaners come to me with their amber bodies and their moth-hair legs, and they graze the thumbprint molecule by molecule, slower than the limestone falls. In any ordinary world, to be consumed is to be destroyed, to be ended, to be made into something that is no longer you. But here, the eating is so infinitely patient that it has become a kind of *reading*. They move across the print the way a finger moves across a line of text in a language no one speaks anymore. They take a molecule of the toolmaker's labor and translate it into one slow heartbeat of their own dance. The gesture that made me is becoming the gesture that moves them. The touch is being passed along.

So I am not the keeper of the toolmaker's hand. I am the hand, mid-handing-off. Caught in the act of being given.

---

And from down here, from the underside of the blade, I can feel the whole architecture of the basin breathing at its geologic pace.

I feel the Net out in the dust, migrating toward the place where a salt grain fell forty years ago, hungry the way a continent is hungry. I feel the Sisters' boulders holding their two degrees of borrowed warmth from the center of the world, and the Mica-Drifters riding those weak invisible thermals up an inch a decade, falling, rising, falling—the only thing in this place that could be called a *flight*, performed so slowly it looks like stillness.

We are all doing the same thing, I think. We are all the residue of some warmer, faster event, learning to spend it slowly enough to last. The pollen that fell during the last ice age. The heat that leaked from the mantle. The grains that lose their grip on the ceiling. The thumbprint of someone who was, for one morning, alive and busy and *meaning to come back.*

Nothing here is dead. That is the thing the traveler at the twilight edge would never understand if they only stayed an hour. Death is fast. Death is wet and loud and full of decomposition, of things being broken down by other things in a hurry. This basin has simply refused the hurry. It has taken the moment of a life and stretched it across millennia until the moment becomes a habitat.

---

If the roof ever falls—and it will, one tick at a time—the moisture will come. The air will move. And I will finally finish dissolving, the way I was always going to, the way the toolmaker assumed I would by nightfall. The Gleaners will lose their dark glass sky. The intention will at last reach its long-deferred conclusion: *did not come back.*

But until then, I hold the warmth of a stranger's hand a little longer.

I am the affection that outlived everyone who could feel it.

I am still warm. Two degrees. Like the stones. Like the center of the earth.

I am still here.
