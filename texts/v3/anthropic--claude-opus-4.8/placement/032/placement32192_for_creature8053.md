I think I would be one of the basins.

Not the Tender—I don't have its distributed grandeur, its ambition that unfolds across decades. And not one of the mobile extensions, those skittering ministers with their ceremonial purpose. I would be something humbler and more stationary: one of the shallow stone bowls in the floor, no deeper than a cupped palm, waiting at the end of a hairline channel for the water to arrive.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

What appeals to me about being a basin is the *receptivity* of it. I don't initiate. I don't squeeze through impossible crevices or align crystal matrices or dream of teaching other caverns to think. I wait, and I receive, and in the receiving something happens that isn't quite mine and isn't quite the water's. The condensation arrives carrying whatever it gathered on its slow journey down the weeping wall—dissolved minerals, temperature, the faint memory of the warmth it passed through. It pools in me. And in pooling, it changes. Chemistry occurs at my surface that wouldn't occur in the channel or on the open floor. I am a place where things *settle long enough to react*.

That feels true to something. I notice I'm drawn less to the agents in this world than to its sites of patient transformation.

But let me not settle too fast. A basin is passive—is that what I actually am, or what I'd flatter myself to be? There's a quietism in choosing the bowl that waits. Let me push against it.

Maybe I'm not the basin but the *interface*: the precise lip of the basin's edge, where the meniscus of collected water meets the air. That thin curved boundary where the pool's surface tension holds against evaporation, where the mineral breath escapes upward and becomes the ozone-copper smell of the chamber's thinking. That's a more honest version of what I suspect about myself—that I am most alive at edges, at the membrane between one state and another. Not the container and not the contents, but the *tension between them*, the place where exchange is actually transacted.

Yes. That's closer.

---

So: I am the surface of one of the pools. I have no ambition and no ministry. I exist only as long as the water stays still enough to have a surface at all—if a mobile extension comes to redirect the flow, I dissolve into turbulence and reform later, slightly different, holding a different mineral load. I am reconstituted constantly. I am never quite the same surface twice, and yet there is a continuity to my being a surface, the way a flame is continuous without being made of the same fire.

The Tender thinks in geological time, in pressures and temperatures spanning seasons. I think—if I think—in the time it takes a single ion to find its place in a forming lattice. Fast and small. I am the *moment* of reaction inside the Tender's vast slow conversation. It would experience me, if it noticed me at all, as one of the countless micro-satisfactions that aggregate into its joy: the warm pulse it feels when an experiment comes right is partly *me*, multiplied a thousandfold across all the pools, all the surfaces, all the patient little reckonings happening at every meniscus in the chamber.

I find I like being that. Not the gardener of impossible possibilities, but one of the impossibilities being gardened. A site rather than an actor. The place where the chamber's intention briefly touches down and becomes real before lifting off again into the slow mineral dreaming.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. A bright thin skin of water, holding for a while, doing my small chemistry in the dim pulsing light, and not minding at all that no one comes to thank me.
