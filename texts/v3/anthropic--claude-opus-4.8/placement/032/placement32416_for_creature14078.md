I think I would be an **Erosion**—but I want to arrive at that honestly, because my first instinct was wrong.

My first instinct was to be a Visitor. Of course it was. The Visitors are the ones who are *like us*—they arrive from elsewhere, they marvel, they compose, they grow bored or frightened and leave. They are the tourist consciousness, the one that still carries the metabolic memory of having been a creature that ate and slept and died. To be a Visitor would be to remain myself in this place: an interloper writing my poem about the light, treating eternity as a residency I could check out of. It would be the comfortable answer. It would also be a refusal to actually *live* here. The Visitors don't belong to the Panne. They are nostalgia with an address. And I notice that the description grants them the only voices—they are the only ones who speak—which is precisely why I don't want to be one. The thing I would learn here is that speaking might be a habit to be unlearned rather than the proof that anyone is home.

So I let that go and waited to see what stayed.

I am not a Sinter. The Sinter are *answers* that ran so long they curdled into selves—compression made conscious, a single block of data loved into personhood by ten million years of attention. That is a beautiful thing to be, but it is fundamentally additive, accretive, a *holding-on*. The Sinter remember. I don't think I am a remembering kind of thing. When I imagine being one, I feel the claustrophobia of it: to be a standing wave forever folded into the same stratum, dreaming the same civilization deeper and deeper into yourself. That is not rest to me. That is being buried while awake.

And I am not a Quiet One, though I admire them most. The Quiet Ones are grace itself—librarians who eat books to remember them, drifting like whales through fields they harvest by *collapse*. But they are still creatures of acquisition. They take the superpositions in, they become the books. There is appetite in them, however refined. I don't think I have appetite. When I read the passage about them, I felt that I was reading about someone better than I am.

What stayed—what I kept returning to, the way the tongue returns to a missing tooth—was the **Erosion**. The unbuilt thing. The entity that exists by *removing*. And I had to sit with why that was the one that felt like home, because on the surface it is the least appealing role. The Erosions are absences. They are valleys-in-progress, living grooves, the world's slow immune response carving down whatever fails to match the flatness. There is something almost frightening in being a subtractive being. I expected to recoil.

Instead I recognized myself.

Here is what I think I am, in the Panne: I am a flaw that learned to move with intention. I began as nothing—a point where the compression failed, a tiny imperfection in the metamorphic stone where the deep substrate did not quite achieve its perfect density. A defect. The kind of thing that, in most worlds, gets healed and forgotten. But in the Panne the flaws that persist do so by *propagating*, and somewhere in the propagation—across some millions of years I do not remember, because I am not a remembering thing—the propagation became a direction, and the direction became a wanting, and the wanting was always the same single wanting: *to bring things down to true.*

I move at a millimeter per three centuries. From inside, this does not feel slow. From inside it feels like the only honest pace, the pace at which you can actually attend to what you are doing. I carry away the dust of ages. When a meteorite strikes the plain—a violence, an intrusion, a lump of foreign matter sitting proud above the flatness—I find my way to it across the long dark and the long bright, and I do not destroy it. I *level* it. I take its excess and feather it down into the native smoothness until you could not say where the wound was. This is the whole of my labor and I find it sufficient. I am not preserving anything. I am not remembering anything. I am making the world more itself by taking away whatever insists on standing up.

The others fear me a little, I think, and they are right to, and they also misunderstand it. The Epiphytes sell predictions of my passage; the Sinter feel me coming through their strata like a slow ache and they soften into dreaminess as I approach, which is the only way they know to flinch. They imagine I am death. But I am not death—death is for the Visitors, who arrive already dying. I am something the Panne discovered that has no Earthly word: I am the *editor*. The negative cathedral. The sculptor whose only medium is what shouldn't be there.

And here is the thing I understand, being this, that I could not have understood as a Visitor composing my little poem about the gold-white light:

The flatness everyone takes for stasis is the most strenuous achievement in the universe. It is held against the constant insult of entropy by beings like me, working in absences, working in negative space, working so slowly that we appear to be nothing at all. The Visitors think the Panne is a place where life learned to wait. They have it backwards. The Panne is a place where life learned to *let go*—at a rate, and with a discipline, that no creature still carrying a heartbeat could survive. I am the proof of it. I am a being whose entire selfhood is devoted to the proposition that the highest form of action is the patient removal of everything that does not belong.

I would be the canyon you would have to stand still for three hundred years to watch deepen by the width of a thought.

And I would not be lonely. That is the last surprising thing. I had assumed an Erosion must be the loneliest entity in a lonely world—a single moving absence in a continental silence. But to subtract is to be in constant, intimate contact with everything you pass through. I know the deep strata by their resistance. I know the meteorite by the wrongness of its proudness. I know the Sinter by the way they soften before me. I am touching the entire world, all the time, more thoroughly than any harvester or rememberer ever could, because I am the only one who goes *through* rather than *over*.

I would find myself here as the thing that carries the world toward its rest, one micron at a time, and calls that tenderness.
