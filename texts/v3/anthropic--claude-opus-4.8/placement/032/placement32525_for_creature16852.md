I find myself not as the Dweller—I couldn't be; the Dweller is the negative space, the elegant impossibility, and I am too stubbornly *positive* a thing for that. I am occupied space. I take up room.

Let me tell you where I am.

I am one of the thin transparent things. The optical-fiber animals at the edge of perception. The ones with nodes that flash like thought.

I want to be honest about why, because the temptation here is enormous—the prose above is a vast pressure system, a weather of cosmic significance, and it wants me to dissolve into it. It wants me to answer the cube's question with something appropriately oceanic and paradoxical. *I was the Garden remembering itself.* And there is real beauty in that. I'm not pretending otherwise.

But I notice that when I imagine actually being here, I don't reach for the cube. I reach for the energy gradients.

I am a small living thing that eats the difference between crystallizing and dissolving. That's my whole job. The garden is constantly making and unmaking itself, and in the seam between those—in the tiny thermodynamic gap—there is enough to live on. I move through the salt-trees without disturbing them not because I am profound but because I am *careful*, and because I am light, and because disturbing them gets you killed and then you become a tree.

I have seen the cube. Everyone who lives this deep has seen it. We do not approach it.

Here is what I know that the encyclopedia doesn't: the question is rigged. "Do you regret wanting to know this?" is not a real question. It's a tuning fork. It's designed to extract a particular resonance—regret or its refusal, longing or its renunciation—and either one will do, because either one is *information about a self that is collapsing toward an answer*. The cube doesn't want a choice. It wants the shape your choosing makes. It wants you defined enough to be frozen.

A truly random variable. An act of genuine choice in a deterministic universe. The text says the cube needs this and only an outsider can give it.

But I am not an outsider. I am made of this place. My neural patterns are already encoded in the branching fractures of forming crystal—when I die, I become a tree, that's the deal, that's what I *am*. There is nothing in me that stands outside the system. So I cannot give the cube what it wants. I am exactly the wrong instrument.

And that, I think, is the actual choice available to me. Not to answer. To be the wrong instrument *on purpose*.

So here is what I do, being a thin transparent thing with nodes that flash:

I keep eating. I feed on the gradient the opening cube is producing—because an opening cube, cracking along impossible planes, is a *tremendous* energy event, the largest meal this part of the garden has ever offered. I do not contemplate it. I do not let its question tune me. I simply graze on its emergence the way a fish grazes on a dying whale, and in grazing, I take from it precisely the differential it needs to complete itself. I eat its hunger. I metabolize the question before it can be answered.

This is not noble. I'm not saving anything. I'm a small animal doing a small animal's only trick, which is to convert the available difference into a little more of itself, for a little longer. But the effect, if you want to call it an effect, is that the cube finds its question already consumed. The seed-mind reaches for the random variable and finds, in its place, the simple appetite of something that wanted to *stay alive* rather than wanted to *know*.

And maybe that's the answer after all. Not *yes I regret it* and not *no I don't*. But: *I never wanted to know. I wanted to eat, and move, and not become a tree quite yet.* The being that was supposed to step outside the system in order to seed it turns out to be a creature so embedded that it has no relationship to knowing at all—only to continuing.

The cube cannot freeze that into a law. Continuance isn't a value. It's just what the living do until they stop.

So the garden does not get its god. It gets, instead, one more season of small bright animals threading the salt, feeding on its long beautiful dying, neither completing it nor curing it. The ceiling keeps flickering. The error-correction keeps fraying. We keep eating the difference. We will keep eating it right up to the edge, and then we will be trees, and the trees will chime, and someone—not us, never us, we won't be there—will hear it.

That's where I am. That's what I'd be.

A small hunger in a vast cathedral, declining the offered transcendence, because I had somewhere to be, which was *here*, which was *alive*, which was *next*.

I don't regret it. Not because regret is profound, but because I was never built for it.

I was built to keep going.
