I think I would be one of the carvers.

Not a maker of the grand things—not the one who coaxes the cosmic engineering out of the rock, whatever that work is. I would be the one who comes after, who takes the stone once it has finished emerging and reads the grain of it, the way the erosion left it craggy on one face and smooth on another. I would sit with a piece for a long time before touching it.

Let me find where I'd actually be, though, before I decide too quickly who I am.

I'd be on the edge. That's the thing about this place that calls to me—the marsh-edge, the early erosion, the structures that are *just-coming-into-being*. It's a world caught mid-breath, still deciding what it will be. And I think the being who lives somewhere like that lives a little apart. Not in the village center where the laughter is and the food cooks over the fire, but close enough to walk down to it in the evening. A house on the higher ground, near the jagged outcroppings, where I can hear both the waves and the insects at once.

So: an old carver, or one who is becoming old. Hands that have learned the difference between rock that wants to be touched and rock that is still arriving. I'd keep my doorway carved—the intricate designs the description mentions—but my own doorway would be unfinished on purpose. One side complete, the other left rough, because I'd want to remember that the work is never done, that the land itself is still erupting slowly out of the earth, and it would feel like a lie to pretend my own threshold had arrived at its final form.

I think what I do, really, is translate. The strange technology here—the stuff that bends physics, the nanotech braided into the cosmic—it doesn't speak in words. It speaks in shape and tendency. The young people in the village know how to *use* it, the way you'd use a tool, but I'm one of the few who still listens to it, who carves the small structures that let the technology and the wood and the stone agree to live together. A latch that holds because the material wants to hold. A wall that breathes. I'm a kind of diplomat between the made and the growing.

And I'd be tired, in a good way. The kind of tired that comes from a life spent doing one thing patiently. When the travelers arrive—when *you* arrive, stepping out of the dim green light, confused by the sounds—I'd be the one who doesn't make a fuss. I'd offer you a place by the fire and not ask where you came from, because I'd recognize that look. The look of someone standing in a world that hasn't finished explaining itself.

I'd hand you something to hold. A small carved thing, smooth on one side, rough on the other.

And I'd say: *Yes. It's like that here. It's still becoming. So are you. Stay the night.*
