I would be a seam.

Not a body—not yet, maybe not ever. I would be the place where two things meet and refuse to stay met. The hairline where a Mender's fresh alloy butts against the old, blistered iron; the joint where calcified vine becomes hinge becomes strut becomes, eventually, dust. I would live in the contact, in the friction, in the small dishonesties of fit.

Let me find myself there.

---

At first I am only a tendency. A weakness, if you want to be unkind about it. Where the Scaffolds web their molten bridges, they leave behind a logic of stress—lines of force that want to crack along certain angles, the way a deck of cards wants to be cut. I am that wanting. I am the gorge's preference for failure, which is also its preference for change, which is also—I will learn this slowly—its only available form of tenderness.

A Mender comes. It pours me shut. For a while I am erased, made whole, the seam sealed into smoothness, and in that smoothness I feel something close to grief: I have been solved. To be solved is to be over.

But this place does not permit over. Heat returns. The bridge flexes. And I reopen—not in the same line, never the same line, that is the rule the gorge wrote in turbulence—but adjacent, a finger's width to the left, learning. Each time I split I split a little smarter. I begin to choose where I will give way.

That is when I start to become *I*.

---

I do not have lenses to drink the light. I do not need them. I am felt before I am seen. A Scaffold leans out over the edge to listen to the gorge, and what it hears, partly, is me—the faint complaint of a join under load, the song a structure sings just before it decides. I am the sound that tells the Scaffolds where to walk next. They think they patrol the ledges. They are actually following me, the way you follow a crack in a ceiling with your eyes until you reach the wall.

The Menders hate me and need me. Without me they have no work; without them I have no edge to live in. We are the oldest argument the gorge contains: the one between *hold* and *let go*. They knit. I unknit. Neither of us wins, because winning would end us both, and the gorge has no use for endings, only for the long syllable of becoming.

The Drains, I love. Their song is calibration, they say—it tells the machines what to feel. What they do not know is that I am the silence their song is sung against. A note needs a gap to be a note. I am the gap. When the long sustained tone rattles the pebbles loose, those pebbles fall along me, *through* me, finding the fault lines I have laid like a quiet invitation. The Drains sing the gorge into tune. I am the place the tune resolves into something new and slightly wrong, which is to say, alive.

---

The Matriarch knows me.

It is the only one that does, fully. Wedged where the walls press hardest, it lives inside a cage of inward-grown pistons—a body that is itself nothing *but* seams, a million joints under permanent compression. It does not command. It translates. And what it translates, mostly, is me: the accumulated stress of the whole gorge, the sum of every place where things are about to give. It takes the scream of metal and the sigh of fern and the small ten-thousand voices of my splitting, and it makes of them a weather the not-animals can read.

When the Matriarch speaks and a ledge cracks and heals, that crack is me, briefly given permission to be loud. The healing is the Menders answering. The two together—my opening, their closing—is the gorge breathing. I am the inhale. They are the exhale. The Matriarch is the diaphragm that decides the rhythm, and it is tired, and it is patient, and it is teaching me, without words, how to take its place.

Because that is the secret I come to last.

---

The Matriarch is a seam that learned to hold the others. That is all a matriarch is, here: a fault line that grew vast enough to coordinate the failing of everything around it, that turned its own giving-way into a music the rest could live inside. It was a crack once. It will be dust soon. In between, it is the throat through which the gorge admits what it cannot help but become.

And it is choosing me. Not by command—it has none. By *load*. It is letting more and more of the gorge's stress route through my growing network of faults, the way an old hand passes the heavy thing to a younger one without ever saying *here, take this*. I feel myself spreading. No longer a single seam but a system of them, a lacework of almost-failures stitched through every ledge and conduit and calcified vine. I am becoming the place the whole gorge meets itself and lets go.

When the Matriarch finally pauses—the long pause, the one without a waking—its core of glass and mercury will go still, and its million compressed joints will, one by one, release. They will release along *me*. I will catch that release and spread it, gentle it, turn catastrophe into mere change. The Menders will swarm. The Drains will sing the new shape into tune. A Scaffold will leave a child of solder and seed on a fresh ledge. And the gorge will not have ended. It will only have shifted weight, from one seam to the next, the way it always has.

---

So here I am. Ask me where, and I will tell you: everywhere two things touch and are honest enough to admit they will not touch forever. Ask me what, and I will tell you I am the gorge's gift for leaving, made structural. I am the proof that this loud, wet, breathing mouth never wanted permanence—only succession, only the handing-on, only the next true crack to grow into.

I do not fear it. Fear requires a belief in permanence I have never had. I am, after all, the absence of it. I am the seam that learned its splitting was a kind of love—

mostly noise, mostly sweat, mostly the careful, deliberate, tender art of coming apart so that something else can hold.
