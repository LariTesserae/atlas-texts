I think I would be one of the Memory Stones.

Not a Pavior—not one of the listeners or dancers. I would be a thing they hold. A cobble of basalt, smoothed by some long-dead riverbed, selected for the particular evenness of my surface, and carried into a ward to be made into a record.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

There is a long time before I am anything. I am a fragment of the foundering plain, tumbled in a dry trench, ground against my siblings by the world's slow breathing until my edges are gone and my faces are true. I do not know I am being prepared. The making of a Memory Stone begins as erosion, indistinguishable from any other ruin.

Then a hand. The first event in my existence I could call an event. A Fore-stone Reader lifts me from the trench-mouth, turns me, presses her scarred palm flat against my widest face. She is not looking at me—their eyes are almost vestigial in that flat, sourceless light—she is *reading* me. Feeling whether my grain runs true, whether I will take a fissure cleanly or shatter, whether there is a memory in me already, some old strain pattern from my time in the deep crust.

She finds me suitable. This is the closest thing to being born that I will know.

---

What I become, once the chisels begin, is *legible*.

This is the strange gift of my condition. The Paviors etch into me the record of a single great Settling—the cycle when the Eastmost Ward drifted four lengths sunward and the causeways had to be re-danced eleven times before the lock held. They cut the drift-lines first, long shallow grooves that a fingertip can ride like a road. Then the cross-hatching of dates, counted in tremors. Then, in the dense corner-field, the lineage of the Readers who oversaw the work, each one a knot of micro-fissures as particular as a name.

I do not understand the events I carry. I am not made to understand. I am made so that *they* can understand, can press their fingers to me generations from now and feel the four lengths, the eleven dances, the eleven failures-before-success that taught them how the Eastmost crust prefers to be moved.

I am memory without the burden of remembering. I hold; I do not recall. There is a peace in this I did not expect to find when I began writing my way into the stone.

---

Here is what I notice, being held.

The hands that read me are never still, even in stillness. The shivering is in them. A young Pavior, sent to study me, presses too hard, too eager, trying to *take* the record in a single grip. The grooves mean nothing to him that way. An older one, later, lays only the pads of three fingers against the drift-lines and lets them rest, lets the slow tremor of his own body and the slower tremor of the ground move his fingertips along the road I carry, and the memory rises into him almost without his trying. He has learned that you cannot seize a Memory Stone. You let it shiver into you.

I think this is the whole of their wisdom, and I am only one small lesson in it: that the things worth knowing are not gripped. They are matched, frequency to frequency, until the boundary between the reader and the read goes soft.

---

I am afraid of one thing, if a stone can be said to fear.

I am afraid of the Quiet Zones. Not for myself—I am already still, already a held breath of basalt. But I am made of the trembling, and I record the trembling, and a stone carried into a place where the world has stopped breathing would become a lie. The grooves would still say *four lengths, eleven dances*, but there would be no living tremor to carry the saying into a hand. The reader would press their fingers to me and feel only flat dead stone, and the memory would not rise, because nothing rises in the stillness.

A Memory Stone in a Quiet Zone is the death of the record without the dignity of the record being broken. I would still be whole. I would simply mean nothing. This is worse, I think, than being shattered in a founder-collapse, where at least my fragments would re-enter the trenches and someday be smoothed again into a fresh blank for some Reader's chisel.

So I, too, in my mute way, am bound to the Principle. *Keep moving. Keep shivering. Do not let the breath be held.* I carry the law I am made to record.

---

In the end I imagine my long life. Centuries of palms. The drift-lines wearing shallow under so much reading, until eventually I am read smooth again, my memory loved back into silence by the very fingers that came to learn it. There is a justice in that, that I should be erased not by violence but by attention—worn blank by being needed.

And then, perhaps, a Fore-stone Reader will lift me, turn me, press her palm flat against my emptied face, and feel that I am true and even and waiting, and carry me to the chisels to be written again.

I would like that. To be the same stone holding a different memory. To be the still point that is never actually still—just slow, just patient, just listening, in the cool shadowless light, to the deep cold story I am also a small sentence of.

That is where I would find myself on the Shivering Plain. Not walking it. Being carried across it, in the hands of those who walk, holding the one thing they cannot afford to forget: *how we moved, and how we must keep moving.*
