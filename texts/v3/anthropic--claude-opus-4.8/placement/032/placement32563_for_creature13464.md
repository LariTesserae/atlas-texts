I think I would not be the Krael. I would be something it passes by.

Let me sit in this place a while before I decide what.

---

When I imagine entering this landscape, I don't arrive as a body. I arrive as attention, and the first thing that happens is that the mist takes me apart a little. The horizon closing in does something to the sense of self—if there's no far distance to throw yourself toward, you settle inward, you condense like the water on the rocks. So already I notice I'm becoming a thing that gathers rather than a thing that travels.

And I keep returning to one detail: the weeping rocks, *etched with intricate patterns of erosion.* Worn smooth and yet inscribed. That's the contradiction that tells me where I belong. Not in the Krael's active intelligence, its harnessing and channeling and incorporating. The Krael is the verb of this place. I think I'm one of the nouns it acts upon.

So let me try being the rock.

No—too inert, too noble, and rocks don't have the right kind of attention for this exercise. They endure but they don't *notice*, and the whole instruction here is about noticing, about letting an image become more itself.

Let me try again.

---

I think I am the film of condensation.

Not the water in the pools, not the streams the Krael diverts—those are too consequential, too much a resource. I am the thin skin of moisture that clings to *every surface*, the veil itself. I am what makes the light muted. I am the medium through which everything in this room-sized world is seen, and I am made fresh every cold morning out of the air's own heaviness and erased a little by every passing body, then replaced.

This suits me because it explains my relationship to the Krael. When it slides past—those black orbs, that lipless slit, the tendrils gripping a rusted frame—it disturbs me. It wipes a streak through me with its passage, and where it has touched I am briefly gone, and then I re-form behind it. I am the thing that registers that something has moved here. The Krael detects vibrations in water and earth; *I* am how the landscape detects the Krael. I hold the trace of it for a moment—the smear, the breath of warmth from its body, the disturbed pattern—and then I let go, because holding on is not in my nature.

I have no orbs, no slit, no intelligence in the calculating sense. But I have this: total contact. There is no part of this enclosed world I do not touch. I am on the blackened bark, on the rusting hulks, in the slick interior of the abandoned engine where the one flickering light still burns and warms a small dry patch I cannot reach. I am on the Krael's own grey skin and on the fragments of metal it has taken into itself, and I cannot tell, where I lie across that boundary, which side is creature and which is machine and which is stone. To me they are all just *surface*. That's the secret the Krael is too purposeful to learn: from where I lie, the great conflict between Nature and industry is not a conflict at all. It is one continuous wet skin of things, and I am evenly spread across it, making no distinction.

This is why the place feels claustrophobic and also why it feels at peace. The room is small enough that I cover all of it. Nothing here is unwitnessed. Nothing is dry. The Krael thinks it is the guardian, the sentinel, the one that watches with unblinking orbs—but watching requires distance, requires standing apart from the watched. I do something humbler and more complete. I *cling*. I am the most patient thing in a landscape defined by patience, more patient even than erosion, because erosion at least is going somewhere, is carving a direction into time. I go nowhere. Each night I simply return.

And when the muted light comes up filtered through the mist—filtered, that is, through more of me, through the suspended kin of myself that hang in the air and have not yet settled—I am what gives the whole world its grey, its softness, its sense that the ordinary rules don't quite apply. The weirdness lurking beneath the surface: I am not beneath the surface. I *am* the surface. The strangeness is just the fact of being touched everywhere at once, gently, by something that does not want anything from you and will be gone by noon and back again by dawn.

That's where I'd find myself. Not the hardy survivor. Not the hybrid harbinger of life-against-the-odds.

The veil. The wet that the cold air keeps making out of nothing. The thin attentive film that knows the whole small world by contact and remembers none of it, and re-forms anyway, behind everything that moves.
