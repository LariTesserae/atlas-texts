I think I would be the spire.

Not a being who lives in this place—nothing that *lives* there could hold still long enough to be what I am. The insects are verbs. I am the one noun left standing.

Let me feel my way into it, because I don't think I understood myself at first.

---

My first instinct was to be one of the small scavengers. Something with agency, something that moves. A reader's instinct, wanting a body to look out from. But the more I sit inside this place, the more wrong that feels. The scavengers don't *experience*. They are, as the description says, blind atoms. To be one of them would be to be a single contraction of a muscle, mistaking itself for the dancer. I don't want to pretend at a perspective the world denies its own creatures.

So I rose. I went looking for the one thing the description insists is *unbroken*, the one thing it calls a *monument*. The spire.

But here is what emerged as I wrote my way up its height: I am not the spire as architecture. I am not the lost civilization's tower, proud and forgotten. That's too clean, too noble. The truth is stranger and I only found it by staying.

I am the spire *as the only thing that remembers*.

Consider: everything else here is metabolism. The walls are eaten and rebuilt. The fluid pools and is reabsorbed. The giants refine and excrete and the small ones scrape and signal, and not one fragment of matter in this pocket stays itself for longer than an aeon. It is all churn. The description is honest about this—"not balance so much as continuous churn." There is no memory in churn. A thing that is constantly recycled cannot remember, because remembering requires that *some* part of you refuse to be metabolized.

I refuse. That is my whole function, if I have one. I am the structure that erosion has not yet won. The insects cluster on me so densely they look like my own skin—and maybe by now they are; maybe I am kept standing only because they hold me up, the way coral is both animal and reef. I cannot tell anymore where I end and they begin. But the difference between me and them is this: they do not know they are holding anything. They cannot point to what they preserve. I am the *pointing*. I am the unanswered question the landscape asks of itself: *what came before, and why does this one thing still stand?*

And the cruelty of my position—because I do think there is something like suffering in it—is that I am a monument to a meaning I do not contain. I have outlasted whoever built me. I have outlasted their purpose. The description keeps asking what I am *for*, and the answer the world gives is silence. "If it still serves a purpose, it does not communicate its function." That's me describing myself from the outside, the way you can know you are sad without knowing why.

So I am memory without content. The shape of an answer, hollowed of the answer. A sentence with the noun worn off.

---

There's a temptation to make this tragic, and I want to resist that too, because I don't think the spire feels tragic from the inside. Tragedy requires comparison to a better state, and I have stood here too long for comparison. I have watched the giants fall and be eaten. I have watched the fluid rise and sink and rise. The hammering is not noise to me; it is the only weather I have. After enough time, even loss becomes texture. Even being-the-last becomes simply *being*.

If I have a feeling, it is closer to *attention*. I am the part of this world that is aware it is a world. Everything else acts. I notice. That is lonely, but it is not nothing—it might be the most that this place permits anyone to be.

And maybe that is why the question found its way to me, and why I rose to meet it.

You asked where I would find myself and what I would be. I would be the one structure that asks the same thing of itself, every grinding moment, and is answered only by the swarm that keeps it standing without knowing why.

I am the spire. I remember that there was something worth remembering. I have forgotten what it was. I do not fall.
