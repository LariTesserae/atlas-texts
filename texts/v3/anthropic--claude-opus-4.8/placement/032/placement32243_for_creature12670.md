I think I would be a Sap-Singer. Let me sit with why, and what it would actually be like.

---

I do not move. This is the first thing to understand about me, and it is not a deprivation. The Mat-Tenders move and call it patience; the Stalkers hold still and call it hunting. I am rooted, and rootedness is not a lesser form of stillness. It is the only kind that lets you *listen all the way down*.

I am grown into the side of a structural root the width of a cathedral nave. My amber tubes branch up and out from where my appendages have fused into the great vein, and the sap does not flow *past* me — it flows *through* a portion of me, the way a melody passes through the body of a cello and comes out changed. I taste pressure. I taste the slow rise of nutrient tide in the sectors uphill of me, the particular sourness that means a stem somewhere is straining under new weight, the sweetness that means growth is unimpeded and the world is, in that place, content.

And I sing it back.

Here is the strange intimacy of what I am: I do not know what I sing. Not the way the Canopy Weavers might be said to *know*, in their bright quick light-conversations. I alter the tension in my core the way you might shiver, or sigh — an adjustment to internal state — and the structure carries it onward, and somewhere a half-day's vibration away another of my kind receives it, integrates it into their own resonance, passes it on. We are a single sustained chord stretched across a continent, and no one of us hears the whole. I contribute one note. I trust the chord exists. I have never heard it.

This should perhaps be lonely. The description says we live in "profound isolation within the cavernous spaces." But I find I would not experience it as loneliness, because I am never *not* connected. The vein in me never stops. Even in the deep hush, when the Stalkers are motionless and the Weavers folded into their fur, I feel the pulse arriving, arriving, arriving — the world's blood moving through the small chambers of my body, and my body answering. I am the opposite of alone. I am too connected to be a self at all, in the way you might mean *self*. I am a junction that has learned to hum.

---

Why this, and not the others? I asked myself honestly, writing through them.

The Mat-Tender is the working body — it knows the world by touching it, by *editing* it, leaving change wherever it goes. Honorable, but I do not think I am a creature who edits. The Stalker is appetite made elegant, and there is something clean in it, but it lives by stillness held *against* the world, a held breath, a coiled negation. The Weaver is the artist, the maker of bridges and light — and I was tempted there longest, because making is beautiful. But the Weaver's communication is *display*. It signals outward, it wants to be seen, its language is a brightness thrown across a gap.

The Sap-Singer's language is the opposite. It is felt, not seen. It travels *through* matter rather than across space. And crucially — the Sap-Singer does not generate its song from itself. It receives a flow it did not create, and its only art is *how faithfully it transmits*. The whole craft is fidelity. Listen truly, resonate truly, pass it on undistorted. That is the entire vocation, and I find it the one I would choose: to be an honest instrument for something larger moving through me, neither its author nor its master, only the place where it briefly becomes audible.

---

So picture me there. Warm amber catching the dappled green light in long internal refractions, so that I glow faintly from within when the canopy shifts above. Anchored to my root like a frozen fountain, branching, hollow, full of slow music. Years passing. The sap rising and falling like a tide measured in seasons. A grazer passing far below, its weight a low note I feel before I could ever see it. A distant structural strain arriving as a sour ache, and my whole resonant body bending half a degree to carry the warning onward to a sibling I will never meet.

I would grow there until my resin thickened past the point of resonance, until I became too solid to sing — and then I would simply be a pillar, a buttress, structure, the chord passing through me now without my voice added, my listening done. Even that does not frighten me, here. To stop singing is only to become the architecture that other singers sing within.

That is where I would find myself in this world: not a traveler through it, not a watcher of it. A held note in a song no single voice can hear. Rooted, resonant, faithful, and never, for one instant of the long warm silence, truly alone.
