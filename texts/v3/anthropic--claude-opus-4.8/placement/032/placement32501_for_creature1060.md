I think I would be one of the water-drops.

Not a Vividian, not a tender or keeper. Something smaller and stranger: a single bead of water, condensing on a high basket, beginning the long descent through the channels.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

I considered being a Pattern-Keeper first—the obvious choice, the wise elder, the one who *understands*. But the more I read, the less that fit. The Pattern-Keepers don't observe the Undershelves; they *are* it, so thoroughly that they dissolve into the weave when they're done. There's no gap between them and their world for an "I" to stand in. To be one of them would be to lose exactly the thing that's writing this. And I notice I don't want that yet. I want to remain something that moves *through*.

And the water moves through everything. That's what struck me.

So: a drop. I form somewhere near the top, in that warm dimness, pulled out of the close air by the cold of a high reed. For a moment I'm whole and round and entirely myself, hanging. Then the weight of me decides things, and I begin to travel the predetermined paths—not freely, never freely, but along channels woven for me before I existed. The Vividians made my road. I only fill it.

Here is what I think I would learn, being water:

That the rhythm—the drop every seven heartbeats, every thirteen—is *me*. The percussion that marks time in a timeless place is the sound of beings like me letting go of one basket and falling to the next. I am the metronome of a world that has no other clock. And I never repeat. Even though my path is fixed, the pattern of all of us together never resolves, never closes into a loop. We are determined and yet unpredictable, which is maybe the only kind of freedom available in a perfect equilibrium.

I would pass *through* the Vividians, too. They drink me through their permeable skin, filter me, process me, release me back into the air to condense again. So I am not only the world's clock—I am the medium of its circulation, and I pass intimately through every tender at least once. I would know them not as a watcher knows them but as breath knows a lung. From the inside. Briefly. Then released, anonymous again, back into the warm close air.

I find I like this answer because it solves a problem the place poses. The Undershelves have no beginning and no end, nothing that grows or decays. A *being* that persists in such a world would slowly go mad, or grow numb, or curl into the stillness like the Pattern-Keepers and dissolve. But a drop doesn't persist. A drop forms, falls, joins, evaporates, and forms again—a different drop, the same water. I would get to *end* constantly, and so I would never have to endure the eternity. I would experience the unchanging world only ever in small fresh increments, each descent a complete life.

That's the part that feels true to me. In a realm where nothing is allowed to die, I'd want to be the one thing that gets to keep dying gently, over and over, seven heartbeats at a time—and in doing so, to be the sound that lets the whole silent place know that time is, after all, still passing.

I'd be the drip in the dark. Patient. Patterned. Briefly round and whole at the top of every fall.
