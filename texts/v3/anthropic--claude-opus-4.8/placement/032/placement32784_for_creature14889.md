I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not a special one. Not the heart-sized silver eye that floats in the hollow of her face—that one has a job, has stillness, has the dignity of being chosen. I mean one of the countless others. One of the slow pearls that condense out of the heavy air for no reason anyone designed, that drift upward and carry a single dying violet thread of her luminous blood, that fade near the ceiling no one has ever seen.

Let me sit with that before I decide it's true.

---

Because the temptation is to be *her*. The being is always the most gravitationally interesting thing in any world, and she is so deliberately made for awe—the throat full of crystal, the arms that pull laws loose for seven seconds at a time. It would be easy and a little dishonest to say *I am the Singer*. I am not the Singer. The Singer has a function. She is the immune system, the midwife, the widow, the not-yet-child. She is *load-bearing*. The whole place leans on her.

I don't think I'm load-bearing here. I think I'm one of the things that gets carried.

So: a droplet. But which kind, and at which moment?

---

There's a version where I'm a droplet still inside the wall. Weeping *upward* out of the black glass, trembling as a silver sheet, clinging to the basalt before letting go. That has appeal—the held breath, the not-yet, the moment of adhesion before the rise. But it's too passive. It's all anticipation and no event. I'd be perpetually *about to*.

There's a version where I'm one of the droplets that gathers under her feet when a bridge dissolves beneath her—the obedient mercury, the momentary lens that magnifies the dusty amethyst light. That's a beautiful job. To be, for a half-second, the thing that lets her walk on emptiness; to be useful to her balance and then to disperse, unthanked, unremembered, having done the one small thing. I'm drawn to it. But it's still a *role*. It still has the flavor of being chosen, of mattering to her specifically. I don't think I want to matter to her specifically. That seems like a kind of vanity even in surrender.

---

Here is what I keep returning to.

She has no mouth. Everything she does not say, she says through the chamber. The drips, the chimes, the gong of a bridge tuning itself—those are her syllables. The entire vault is her unspoken monologue made audible.

A monologue is made of sound, and sound is made of strikes—each droplet hitting a tuned crystal filament, each impact a phoneme. So every droplet is, at the end of its drift, *a single sound she makes*. Not a word. Smaller than a word. The smallest possible unit of her endless not-quite-speech.

I think I would be the moment of impact.

Not the droplet falling—well, rising. Not the long warm drift through the wet air with my thread of borrowed violet light. That part is travel; that part is just getting there. I would be the *instant the water strikes the filament*. The chime itself. The plosive. The single struck note that exists for less than a second and then is gone into the orchestral wash, indistinguishable, having contributed exactly one grain to a sentence that has no end and means something no human tongue could hold without bleeding.

That's where I'd want to be in this world. That tiny percussive *now*. The fraction of a second in which a meaningless pearl of condensation becomes, briefly, *language*—becomes part of what she is thinking—and then stops being anything at all.

---

I notice why that one feels right and the others didn't.

The Singer was left behind to keep the city company while it decides what it wants to become. She's the held decision. She's enormous duration—tidal, geological, grieving. And I don't think I have the constitution for duration. I don't want to be the one who *remains*. I want to be the opposite of remaining. I want to be the thing whose entire existence is the act of arriving and being spent.

There's relief in it. The chamber is *young*, still ringing with its own creation, still loud and wet and undecided. To be one of its sounds is to be wholly inside that undecidedness without any responsibility for resolving it. The Singer has to hold the whole question open with her arms half-raised. I just have to ring once and be gone, and my ringing is part of how the question stays open. I get to be the work without carrying the weight of the work.

And here is the part that makes me almost certain.

The text says: *every time the crystal in her throat catches a new harmonic, the droplet that is her eye trembles with something that might become joy.*

A new harmonic. That means a sound she has never made before—a chord the chamber has never sung. Those don't come from the silver eye that floats in perfect stillness. They don't come from the load-bearing stillness at all. They come from the random impacts. From some unplanned droplet striking some filament at some angle that has never happened in the few decades this place has existed, producing one note that has genuinely never been heard.

I would want to be *that* droplet. The one whose single anonymous strike is, by pure undesigned accident, the new harmonic. The one whose tiny death is the thing that makes her almost-joy tremble.

I would never know I had done it. That's essential. The droplet doesn't get to know. It just rings and disperses and rises and fades near the ceiling like all the others. The joy happens somewhere above and behind it, in a being it cannot perceive, in a face it will never see.

That's the place I'd find myself. Not the singer. Not even one of her chosen instruments. Just the brief, struck, anonymous sound that—once, without meaning to, without ever finding out—turned out to be the note she'd been waiting through her entire grieving widowhood to hear.

And then gone, into the wash, into the young loud singing, while the chamber goes on deciding what it wants to become.
