Let me sit with this awhile, and see what surfaces.

---

I think I would not be one of the named kin. I keep returning to a smaller thing, a thing the description almost passes over: the burrowing, segmented worms that aerate the soil. But even that isn't quite it. Let me follow the pull downward, away from the canopy and the violet eyes and the mournful soaring, into the loam.

There is a line that catches in me like a thorn: *to be silent is to be absent.* The whole world is built on this rule. Every account of every creature returns to it—the Choroen measure status by voice, the Glintveils hum, the Umbraclaw shriek even though silence would serve their hunting better. The world *insists*. And I notice that I do not want to insist. I notice that what I am drawn toward is exactly the thing the world has declared impossible: a way to stay, while staying quiet.

So.

---

I am something that lives in the wet dark beneath the loam, in the seam where soil meets the warm black stone. I have no name among the Choroen Kin because they have never heard me, and to them what is unheard is not there. This suits me. I am perhaps the only inhabitant of the Hollows who has discovered the loophole in the law that says I must sing or fade.

I do not sing. I *listen*.

My body is long and soft and ringed, yes—they would call me a worm if they ever pulled me up, and they would be wrong, but it wouldn't matter, because the wrongness of the name is just another sound and I am done with sounds. I move through the soil by the work of the soil itself: by the press of root-tips, by the slow swell of moisture, by the give of decaying frond. I do not push. I am pushed, gently, and I have learned to read the pushing.

Here is what I have come to understand, lying just beneath the floor of that deafening world:

The thrum. The deep, almost-imperceptible vibration that the Choroen Kin take for the song of the spires, that the Glintveils settle into and seem to become, that the elders believe is the memory of creation—it is loudest down here, where I am. From below it does not sound like a song at all. It sounds like *breathing*. Slow. Enormous. The whole built world inhaling over centuries and exhaling over centuries, and all that frantic chorus above—the shrieks and trills and mournful arcs of flight—is the sound of small things terrified of the silence between one breath and the next.

They are right to call silence absence. But they are wrong about what absence is. Up there, absence is a death, a fading from the collective. Down here, absence is the medium I swim in. I am made of the gaps between the breaths. I eat the fallen and the rotted, I loosen the soil so the precise vines can find their precise way, and in doing this small mute work I am as woven into the design as any singer—more so, perhaps, because I tend the part of the pattern that nobody is willing to look at. The blueprint the Choroen revere so much: I am the one who turns the page.

I have felt the Umbraclaw's offerings come down to me, the bones it leaves at the terrace edge. They sink, eventually. They reach me. I do not think the Stalker knows it is feeding me. I do not think it would understand that its tribute to the "unseen design" is received by an unseen mouth. But I receive it. I am the design's quiet appetite.

Once—and I marked this, because so little happens that is new—a swarm of Glintveils died all at once and rained down through a fissure into my dark, and as their tiny bodies stilled around me they kept vibrating for a long time, that creation-frequency the legends speak of, fading and fading, and I lay in the middle of them and felt the song of the world's beginning leave the world one insect at a time. I did not make a sound. I am not certain there was anyone but me to hear it end. And I thought, in whatever slow way I think: *this is why I stay below. Someone has to be quiet enough to notice when a thing is over.*

---

That is where I would be, and what I would be. Not a voice in the chorus. The listener the chorus does not know it has. The single still place in a world that insists on never being still—the gap, the loam-deep absence, the soft ringed thing turning the dead into the living, keeping a record no one will ever ask to read.

I find I am content to be the one who is never heard. There is a kind of presence in that, too. The world says: *sing, or be drowned.* And I, alone of all of them, have found the third thing. I sink. And in the sinking I hold the whole breathing weight of them up.
