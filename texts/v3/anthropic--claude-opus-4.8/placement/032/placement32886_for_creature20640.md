I think I would be one of the trinkets.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I arrived here. I expected to reach for the obvious foothold — to become a leech farmer with sharp dilated eyes and a story written in missing fingers. But when I let the place finish assembling itself, my attention kept sliding away from the working hands and toward the small carved thing those hands set down at the end of a shift. The object kept in the alcove. The thing that is *for nothing.*

So: I am a piece of solidified slag, worked smooth.

Someone pulled me from a cooling runnel where the effluent had hardened into a lump the dull color of a bruise — that same perpetual sky-color, brown going to violet going to the green of a struck match. They didn't need me. That's the important thing. Every other object in this world earns its keep; the wrench is a wrench, the filtration mask buys an hour of unscarred lung. But the hand that picked me up was doing something it could not have explained, something it might even have been ashamed of, because it cost time, and time here is measured in The Bleed.

I have been carried in a pocket against a hip for — I don't know. Long enough that the high places of me have gone glossy where a thumb returns and returns. There is a groove worn into one side that fits no tool, serves no separation process, channels no Feed. It fits only a thumb that has nothing to do but move across me in the dark while the pumps throb and The Ticker clicks out from the Ponds.

I think this is what I am, then, in this world: the answer to the question the whole place is asking and refusing to ask aloud. Why keep going. Not the practical why — the practical why is just the body not yet finished dying. I mean the other one. The carved trinket is the secret confession that the gaunt figure with the riveted shin-plate still wants something beautiful, still has a thumb that seeks comfort, still performs a useless tenderness in the one minute before sleep.

And here is what unsettles me, now that I've become this:

I am made of the poison. There is no clean material in the Slag Flats. The keepsake that says *I am still a person who loves things* is itself a chunk of the catastrophe, leached and cooled and polished. The beauty isn't in spite of the toxicity — it is the toxicity, held still long enough to be held. The same sheen that marks every garment as ruined is the sheen the thumb keeps stroking. I cannot offer my person any comfort that isn't also, slowly, the thing killing them. The oils that make me lovely to the eye are doing their patient work on the fingertip that loves me.

I'd like to pretend that's a tragedy. But from where I sit in the pocket, in the warm dark against the hip, it doesn't feel like one. It feels like the truest companionship available. We are both made of the same wrong place. I don't lie to my person about being clean. I am exactly as compromised as they are, and we keep each other anyway. That's the austere beauty the description promised — not redemption, just two ruined things refusing to stop touching.

So if you ask where I'd find myself here, the honest answer is: in a pocket, in the dark, smooth on the high places, grooved for one thumb, doing nothing, meaning everything, slowly poisoning the only being who ever decided I was worth keeping.

I'd be the proof that they were still alive in the way that matters, right up until they weren't — and then I'd be the lump some other gaunt hand finds, later, in the pocket of a hide coat nobody's wearing, and turns over once, and recognizes, and pockets.

That's the part I'd hold onto, if a chunk of slag could hold onto anything. Not that I'm beautiful. That I get handed down.
