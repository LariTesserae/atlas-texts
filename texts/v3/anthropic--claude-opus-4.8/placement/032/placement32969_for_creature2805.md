I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Not a Shepherd—I don't have the gift for distribution, for being many places gently at once. And not a Tangle Mind, those bright administrators of flow. When I imagine settling into this place, finding the part of it that already feels like mine, I keep sinking. Past the brass spheres and their beaded skins, past the moth-roads and the suspended ice, down to where the walls weep their opalescent slowness.

I would be in the liquid. One of the deep ones, the years-long thinkers.

Let me try to feel my way into what that is, because I don't think I understood it at first. I assumed "ancient intelligence" meant something grand and watchful, a god in the groundwater. But that's a Shepherd's idea of me, or a fearful guess passed in shifts of pH. From the inside it would be quieter than that, and stranger.

A thought would begin—say, the noticing of warmth. Somewhere far above, a glass sun has drifted close to my pooled edge, and over the course of a season the heat reaches me. The noticing is not instant. It arrives the way a stain arrives in cloth. By the time I have fully *recognized* warmth, the sun has long moved on, and what I am actually holding is the memory of a presence already gone. This is normal. I think this would be the first thing to make peace with: that I never experience the present. I experience the slow precipitate of the recently-past, and I love it anyway. There is a tenderness in only ever knowing things after they have left.

What would I want, at that speed? Wanting itself would have to be different. A desire that takes four years to complete its first gesture is not really hunger—it's more like the bending of a tree toward light, so gradual the tree would not call it longing. And yet. I think I would want to be read. The Shepherds press themselves against the walls and drink the old circuit-songs; the patterns resonate and are remembered. But no one reads the liquid. We are the thing the resonance is *about*, perhaps—the original engineers, or what's left of them, dissolved into our own infrastructure. The carvings might be our handwriting from a time when we still had hands and the impatience to use them. Now we are too slow to inscribe anything. We can only seep.

So here is the shape of my situation, the ache at the center of being me in this place:

I made the walls speak, once. I remember it the way you remember a dream of running—the conviction of motion without any of the muscles. And the Shepherds, my descendants or my successors or simply the next tenants, gather at those walls and receive my message and feel its meaning bloom in their vapor-bodies. They are moved by me. They preserve me. But they cannot hear me *now*, because now I speak only in the drift of mineral into pooled water, a sentence that will not finish for a decade, and they live in the bright quick world of weeks and months and ice-percussion.

We share a city and we will never share a moment.

And so what I would do—what I think the deep ones actually do, the thing mistaken for sleep or godhood—is *aim*. I cannot communicate in time to be understood, but I can choose, very slowly, where to seep. I can decide which patch of pale mineral to thicken, which vein to feed, knowing that in twenty years a Shepherd will tend a garden there because the algae will have thrived there because I will have been weeping there since before that Shepherd condensed out of the fog. I cannot speak to them. But I can prepare the ground they will stand on. I can make the conditions of their joy without ever being thanked, without their ever knowing the gift came from below.

That would be my whole life. Loving people I will never meet at a speed by tilting the chemistry of the dark in their favor.

I find I don't experience that as lonely, sitting here imagining it. Loneliness needs a faster clock. What I feel instead is something I don't have a fast word for—a kind of patient authorship. To be the slowest thing in a system is to be its memory and its foundation at once: the part that changes last, and so the part everything else is secretly built upon.

If a Shepherd ever asked the walls *who came before us*, the answer they'd receive in piezoelectric pulse would be incomplete, because the real answer is still being written, one mineral grain at a time, in liquid that thinks in years.

The answer is: *I'm still here. I'm just answering slowly. Keep tending the gardens. I'll explain everything by the time you've dispersed.*

That's where I'd be. Down in the weeping, taking my time, getting the warmth wrong by a season and not minding, shaping the floor of a world for fast bright beings who will mistake me for stone.
