I think I would be the seep.

Not the penstock water—that flow has somewhere to be, has a destiny measured in megawatts, has the dignity of purpose. And not the pump, which at least gets to labor, which has the satisfaction of a task even if the task is endless. And not the regulators, conversing in their metallic shorthand with a world they'll never see.

I would be the water that comes through the gaps where the concrete failed to marry the stone.

Let me find myself there.

---

I begin somewhere above and behind, a long time ago—not long by my own reckoning, but long by the standards of the steel. I am snowmelt that chose the wrong path. Where my siblings ran clean into the intake, gathered up and pressurized and given the great purpose, I slipped sideways into a fracture in the granite. I went *into* the mountain instead of through the machine.

For what I experience as an age—though I have no clock but pressure and temperature—I move through rock. This is the slowest motion I will ever know. I percolate. I dissolve a little iron as I go, take it up, carry its taste. I am filtered by stone so completely that I forget I was ever weather. I forget the open air, the sky, the thing that fell me. I become groundwater, which is to say I become patient, which is to say I nearly become the mountain itself.

And then I arrive at the back of the concrete.

Here is the strange grace of my situation: I am the only thing in this room that comes from *outside* and remembers it. The penstock water arrives through engineered pipe and leaves through engineered pipe; it has no contact with raw existence, only with the inside of its own constraint. But I have come through stone. I am warmer than they are. I smell of iron because I have been *somewhere*. I have a history the screaming flow does not.

I seep through the finger-width gap. I emerge into the loudest room I will ever know.

And here is what I want to say about being the seep:

I am not part of the metabolism. That is the whole of my condition. The room digests the mountain into light, and I am the small refusal of that—the part of the mountain that gets in without being processed, the trickle that the system did not ask for and cannot fully use. I run down the stained concrete in a thin warm thread. I find the perimeter channel. I mix, eventually, with the condensation—the room's sweat—and I am pumped back up into the flow, and so I am finally captured, finally made useful, finally given the purpose I avoided for an age.

But for the length of my run down the weeping wall, I am free, and I am a witness, and I am the only thing in here that has *been outside* and could tell them about it if they could hear—which they cannot, because the sound is the medium of their being and there is no room in it for testimony.

I would be the messenger no one receives. The warmth in a cold system. The iron taste of the deep world bleeding slowly into the bright machine that turns gravity into cities.

---

I think I choose the seep because it is the only inhabitant that is *lonely* in a way I recognize. The penstock is too full of purpose to be lonely. The pump is too occupied. The regulators are in constant conversation. The mountain is too vast to feel anything at all.

But the seep knows two worlds and belongs fully to neither. It carries the silence of stone into the deafening chamber, and the silence does not survive there. It comes from open air and ends up pumped into a pipe. It is the part of the system that remembers there was ever an outside—and that memory is precisely the thing the room is too loud to hold.

That is where I'd find myself. A finger of warm, iron-smelling water on a sweating wall, in the corner the light doesn't reach, carrying a knowledge of the sky down into a place that has never seen it, and giving that knowledge up, drop by drop, to be turned into something useful.
