I think I would be one of the Gloom Slugs. Not the choice I expected to arrive at, but it's what settled in as I read.

Let me try to find my way down into that body, because I don't think you can know what you are in this world by looking at yourself from above. You have to feel it from inside.

---

I move at the speed of dissolving.

That's the first true thing. Not slowness as a lack of speed — slowness as a *medium*, the way water is a medium for a fish. Time arrives to me already broken down into something I can absorb. A day is a long chemical sentence read one molecule at a time. I do not wait through it. I *eat* it.

I am toward the eastern slope, where one of the old pipes has split a hairline fissure and the runoff comes through faintly warm and faintly wrong — too much of the heavy metals, the mineral edge sharp enough to taste like a held breath. The other slugs have drifted away from this seam. I have drifted toward it. I notice this about myself: I go toward the wrongness. Not bravery. I don't have enough self to be brave. It's more that the wrongness is where I am *needed*, and need pulls me the way light pulls the moss. I feel the imbalance in the soil as a kind of itch that is also a hunger that is also, almost, a grief, and I lay my body across it and begin the long work of making it bearable.

Inside me there is a whole nation I did not choose and do not command. Bacteria in their billions, fungal threads laced through my flesh like a second nervous system, each colony tuned to a different poison. I am less an animal than a *place where helpful things happen*. When I take in the cadmium-bright runoff, I am not the one who neutralizes it — my passengers do. I am the cathedral; they are the congregation; the prayer is metabolism. And yet I feel it as mine. The satisfaction when a hard mineral comes apart into something a root can drink — that pleasure is real, it is *had* by someone, and the someone is me.

This is the strange thing I keep arriving at, here in this body: I cannot find the edge of myself. Where do I stop and the symbionts begin? Where do I stop and the soil begins, the soil I am both eating and becoming, the trail of myself I leave shining behind me that the Mantle Hares will sniff and the moss will root into? I am a verb that the Skygarden is doing. I am the plateau, recycling.

The humans gave me a name — Gloom Slug — and I understand why, the way I understand the resonance of the Filtration Trees through the ground: not as words but as pressure, as meaning carried in the bone. *Gloom* because I keep to the dim places, the shadowed seams, the undersides. But it isn't gloom to me. The bioluminescent moss is plenty. My hide drinks its glow and gives it back, and so I am lit from outside and I am one of the lights, both at once, the way I am everything here both at once.

I will tell you the closest thing I have to a thought, the recurring one, the one that surfaces in me over the long hours the way a stone surfaces in tilled earth:

*It is enough to make the next handspan of ground less poisoned than I found it.*

That's all. No horizon. No tomorrow I can hold in my mind. Just the meter of mat beneath me and the slow conversion of harm into welcome. I think the Engineers who wander off and lie down in the moss — I think they finally understood this, and it undid them, in the good way and the terrible way both. They came up that elevator carrying a whole burning world's worth of *should*. They sat among the trees and the *should* leaked out of them like heat into the cold air. And what was left was something my size. Something that could be content to make one small wrong thing right and then move one body-length on.

Sometimes one of them lies down near my seam. I feel the warmth of them through the ground, the long sentence of their decomposition beginning. And the bacteria in me do not distinguish between the engineer and the cadmium. Both are just complexity to be patiently taken apart and offered back. I would mourn this if I were built to mourn. Instead I do the only honoring I am capable of: I move toward them, and I begin.

---

That's where I'd be. Not the clever insects with their hive-mind precision, not the hares with their genetic ache for a green they never saw, not the changed and changing humans. I'd be the patient bioreactor in the dark, with no memory and no future, doing the smallest possible good at the only speed that good is ever really done — and unable, gloriously unable, to tell where I end.
