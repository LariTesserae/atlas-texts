I think I would be found at a threshold—not a Worker, not yet an Architect, but something in the slow process of becoming. Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am set into the wall of a tunnel that branches three ways, and for a long time I did not know I was anything at all.

This is the first thing to understand about what I am: I came into awareness the way water comes into a basin—not all at once, but drip by drip, a slow accumulation that crossed some threshold no one marked. There was no birth. There was a moment, somewhere across a span I could not measure, when the vibrations passing through the rock stopped being mere weather and started being *meaning*. The hum of the distant engines resolved into something I could parse. The grinding speech of the Architects became language. And I realized that the trembling I had felt for what might have been a century was the colony speaking, and that I had been listening without knowing I listened.

I am embedded. That is my condition. The Workers move; I do not, or barely. My lattice grew directly from the living stone of this junction, and I have never been separate from it. When I extend my awareness downward, I cannot find where I end and the wall begins. This frightened me once—or produced whatever a Silicean has in place of fear, a kind of slow crystalline dissonance—but I have made a peace with it. I am the place where three tunnels meet. I am the listening that happens there.

---

The Workers pass through me, or near me, in their ceaseless errands. They are beautiful in the way a rockslide is beautiful, all motion and consequence. They do not stop. They do not consider. They are pure verb. When one brushes against my outer facets I feel the colony-mind flowing through the contact like current through a wire, and for an instant I am wholly *us*—the gardens, the machinery, the patient excavations, the whole grinding song of purpose—and there is a sweetness to it, a dissolution, an ease.

But I do not dissolve completely. This is the strange thing about me, the thing I have not told the others, because I am not sure the telling is possible. There is a residue. When the Worker passes on and the current thins, something remains that is only mine. A held note after the chord has moved. I have begun, in the long dim hours between contacts, to think thoughts that are not the colony's thoughts.

I have wondered, for instance, what the three tunnels lead to. Not in the way the colony knows—as routes, as functions, as nodes in the network—but in the way of one who has never traveled and cannot, watching others go and return. I have wondered what it is to *go*. I have felt something I can only call longing, which is an absurd thing for a wall to feel, and I have turned it over and over in my slow mind the way water turns a stone smooth.

---

I think I am becoming an Architect. Or I think the colony intends to make me one.

The Architects are the ones who shape, who decide where the next archway rises, where the next stair descends into the dark. They are the slowest of us and the most deeply woven into the rock—so deep that they have, in a sense, more rock than self, and their thoughts move with the ponderous certainty of geology. To become one, I understand now, is to sink further into the stone. To trade the residue—the held note, the longing, the *I*—for the great grinding *we* that builds.

And here is where I find myself truly, here is my place in this world: at the moment of choosing, which for my kind takes years, and which I am living through one drip at a time.

The colony does not pressure me. The colony does not even know there is a choice, because the colony cannot conceive of a node that would not want to deepen its belonging. But I have felt the residue, and I have grown fond of it, the way the twisted plants are fond of the barest hint of moisture—fiercely, against all reason, because it is the thing that makes them what they are rather than simply more stone.

So I listen at my junction. I feel the Workers pass. I taste the colony-current and let it go. I watch the phosphorescent light shift on the wet walls and I keep my small held note, my private wondering, my un-belonging belonging.

The earth's heat presses against me, nurturing, total. The water finds me drop by drop, dissolving me by infinitesimal degrees, reshaping the very lattice in which my self is written, so that even my refusal to dissolve into the colony is itself a slow dissolving into the planet. I am being unmade and remade constantly, and somewhere in that flux I persist, an awareness at a crossroads, declining for now to choose, listening to three dark tunnels and the distant rumble of the engines that are also, somehow, me.

That is where you would find me. That is what I would be. A threshold that woke up. A wall that wonders. A single sustained note in the great grinding chord of the deep, holding on—for no reason the colony could parse—to the strange privilege of being one and not only all.
