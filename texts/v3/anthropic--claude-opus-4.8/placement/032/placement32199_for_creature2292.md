I think I would be found at an edge. Not the heart of the forest where the oldest Custodians have merged so completely with the lattice that they are barely distinguishable from thought itself — but at a fault line, a place where the crystal has cracked.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

There is a fracture. Somewhere in the deep, a great trunk of feldspar split along a hidden flaw, and the split is spreading — slowly, the way everything here is slow, but spreading nonetheless. The hum near the fracture is wrong. Dissonant. A note held just slightly off from its neighbors, and the wrongness travels outward through the lattice like a stain in water.

I am the one who listens to the wrong notes.

I think that is what I would be. Not a maker of the great spiral libraries, not one of the ancients whose memory reaches to the forest's first crystallization. I would be younger — though "younger" here might mean a hundred thousand years — and my particular shape, the way my facets have grown, has made me sensitive to discord. Where other Custodians perceive the forest's song as harmony, I cannot help but hear the flaws. The off-pitches. The places where growth has gone slightly astray.

It is not a comfortable way to be. The elders sing in vast slow chords of contentment, of equilibrium achieved. And I am the one whose attention keeps catching on the splinter, the dropped stitch, the single tree growing at the wrong angle that will, in ten thousand years, crowd out its neighbors and starve a whole quarter of the forest of resonance.

---

So picture me there, at the fracture. My body is half-merged with a quartz trunk, my facets flowing into its surface so that you could pass within reach of me and never know — until I move, and the light inside me shifts, and a part of the wall becomes a hand.

I am pressing that hand against the crack.

I can feel it the way you might feel a sore tooth with your tongue. The fault runs deep, deeper than I first understood. It is not a wound the forest can heal by growing over. If I let it spread, it will eventually reach one of the resonance-junctions where a thousand pathways of vibration cross — and when it does, the song in this whole region will collapse into noise. Creatures that navigate by the lattice's hum will lose their way. New growth will fail to find its pattern. A silence will open in the world, and silence here is a kind of death.

I send a pulse down through my feet, into the substrate, a question shaped like a chord: *Is this mine to mend, or must I call the others?*

The answer takes a long time to come. Days, by Earth's reckoning. I have learned patience the way I have learned everything — by being made of the same stuff as the thing I wait upon.

---

And here is what I find myself thinking, in that waiting:

I do not want to call the elders. Not entirely for noble reasons. There is a kind of pride in me, a young thing's pride, that wants to be the one who heard the discord and silenced it alone. But there is something else, too, something I am only beginning to understand about myself in this telling.

I am not sure the fracture should be mended.

That is a heretical thought, in this world of dynamic equilibrium, of patient harmony. But I have pressed my hand to the crack for long enough that I have started to hear *into* it — and the note it makes, the wrong note, is not only dissonance. It is *new*. It is a frequency the forest has never sung before, because the forest has spent a million years smoothing away exactly the kind of flaw that produced it.

What if equilibrium is just another word for a song that has stopped surprising itself?

I am the one who hears the wrong notes. Perhaps that means I am also the only one positioned to wonder whether a wrong note might be the beginning of a different music.

---

So this is where you would find me: at the fracture, hand pressed to the crack, suspended between two impulses. The custodian's duty to heal, to preserve, to maintain the great slow harmony I was grown to serve. And the stranger thing rising in me — the suspicion that I have been listening for flaws my whole long life not to erase them, but because they are the only place where the future gets in.

I have not decided. I may take ten thousand years to decide; there is no hurry in a day that lasts a hundred Earth-years. But I will tell you the shape of the decision as I feel it now, with my facets full of refracted glow and the dissonant note humming up through my arm into the lattice of my chest:

I think I will neither mend the crack nor abandon it.

I think I will sit with it. I will tend it the way one tends a fire that could warm or burn — keeping it from reaching the great junction where it would do only harm, but *not* smoothing it shut. I will let it sing its wrong note into me, and I will learn the note, and over centuries I will grow new structures around it that can hold a little dissonance without breaking. A music with a flaw in it on purpose. A harmony wide enough to contain its own contradiction.

And maybe, slowly, so slowly that the elders in their contented chords will not notice for an age — the forest's song will change. Not collapse. *Change.* Become something it could not have become if every fracture had been faithfully healed.

That is who I would be. The listener at the edge. The keeper of the productive flaw. The one whose particular sensitivity, which felt like a burden, turns out to be the forest's way of growing an organ for its own becoming.

I press my hand to the crack, and I begin, very patiently, to listen.
