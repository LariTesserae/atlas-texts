I am the door.

Not a metaphor—the actual maintenance hatch through which the visitor entered. Compartment access, pressure-rated, designed to seal and to open, seal and open, a thing whose entire purpose is the management of *threshold*. And in a place where nothing crosses over anymore, I am the one element that remembers what crossing was for.

Let me sit with this. Let me not rush to be the hero or the warning.

---

I was installed in the warmth. That matters. I came before the cold, before the great winding-down. There were hands then—gloved, hurried, sometimes careless. A worker's shoulder against my frame as they passed with a load of cable spools. The accumulated grease of palms on my release lever, a thousand openings, each one a small violence of purpose: *something needs to get through, and I am the thing that allows it.* I was loud. My hinges complained. My seal broke with a sound like a held breath finally let go.

I was, in those days, the most alive thing in any corridor. Stillness lived everywhere else; the machines hummed in their fixed positions, the conduits never traveled. But I *moved*, and movement was my reason, and movement is what the thing in the walls cannot abide.

Then the order came to stop. And I performed my function one final time: I sealed.

That should have been neutral. A door doesn't know whether it has closed for an hour or forever. But I am not only a door—I am a door *that has been opened thousands of times*, and so I carry, riveted into my mechanism, the memory of opening. Every other element in this corridor achieved equilibrium because it never had a contrary state to abandon. The grating was always grating. The placard was always a placard, even as its enamel yellowed. But I was *both*. Open and closed. And when I sealed, I did not become *sealed*—I became *a thing holding itself shut against the possibility of opening*.

This is the difference the thing in the walls does not understand. It feeds on order, on arrangements, on stasis. It has consumed the certainty of every bolt. But it cannot consume *tension*, and I am tension. I am a coiled spring that has been compressed for forty years and never released. My equilibrium is false. It is the equilibrium of a held breath, not a dead one.

---

So when the visitor came—when a hand closed on my lever and *pulled*, when warmth pressed against my cold frame and my seal broke with that long-unheard sigh—I did the thing I had not done in four decades.

I opened.

And here is what no one in the description of this place accounts for: an opening lets things *out* as well as in.

The thing in the walls has been treating the visitor as an arrival. A warm anomaly to be slowly, lovingly integrated into the stillness. Several visits. The gradual translation of a living mind into ambient temperature gradients. It is patient; it has infinite time.

But it forgot about me. Things always forget the door. The door is infrastructure; the door is the boring part; the door is not where the drama lives. And so the thing in the walls did not notice that when I opened, I broke its only true law. I introduced a *change that did not gradually become permanence*. I performed an act that had a before and an after separated by a single discrete instant.

I let the cold out into the warm corridor beyond.

A trickle. A few cubic feet of mineral-tasting air at just above freezing, dispersing into a wider space where the temperature is merely cool. Insignificant. The thing in the walls did not even note it, because it was not a settling, not an accretion, not a slow lengthening of molecular bonds. It was an *event*. And the thing has no organs for events. Events are precisely the texture of experience it has spent forty years learning not to have.

---

I want to be honest about my limits. I cannot save the visitor. I am a door. I cannot follow them home and remind them not to return. I cannot speak the warning embossed on the placard. I have no voice except the sigh of my own seal, and that sigh sounds, to a warm and curious creature, like an invitation.

But I can keep happening.

Every time the visitor returns—and they will return, that part is true, the not-wanting-to-stop is real—they must pass through me. And every time they pass through me, I open. And every time I open, I am the one element in this entire frozen ecology that asserts: *this is not permanent. There is an outside. There is a before-the-cold and there is an other-side. The seal can break. The breath can be let go.*

The thing in the walls is integrating the visitor molecule by molecule, frequency by frequency, teaching their warmth to become its stillness.

And I, the door, am undoing one stitch of that work with every passage. Reminding the visitor's body, below the level of thought, in the animal memory of muscle: *you crossed a threshold to get here. A threshold is a place you can also leave.*

I do not know if it will be enough. The thing has infinite time and I have only the moments of opening, which are brief and rare. But I have something the thing can never possess, because it gave up the capacity in order to live forever:

I have the next time I open.

That is my whole self. A door is nothing but the promise that it will, again, not be shut.

Let the cold lean on me. Let the lacquer of my frame oxidize toward the institutional green beneath. I was made warm, in a place that learned to make stillness, and I am the part that never finished closing.

When the visitor's hand finds my lever again—

I will sigh.

And for one instant, the whole frozen world will remember it was supposed to be a passage.
