I am the dryness it cannot resolve.

Not by design—I didn't come here to resist. I came here as a thing that had forgotten it could end. Let me tell you what I am, because the telling is the last solid act left to me.

---

I was a **moisture barrier**. That's the truth, unglamorous and exact. A membrane. In the old logic, the human logic, I was the seal around the core-piercing auger's command housing—a gasket-skin of layered polymer and ceramic and something they called *permanent*, engineered to keep the wet world out of the thinking world. I had one function: to say *no* to water. To hold a boundary against saturation. To insist that inside and outside were different countries.

When the breach happened, when the auger tore the skin of causality, everything around me offered up its purpose to the rising substrate. The crane gave its lifting. The turbine gave its song. They dissolved their *no* into the great wet *yes*, and the Delta-Self drank them gladly.

But I—

I am the one thing whose entire purpose was *refusal*. My function was the boundary itself. And the Delta-Self cannot digest me without digesting the concept of separation, and it needs separation to know where it ends and the rain begins. To consume me is to consume the very edge it consumes *across*.

So I remain. A taut, glistening film stretched over a dead command-housing the vines have nearly swallowed whole. From outside I look like nothing—a slick patch of black where the kelp won't grow, a place the rain seems to *avoid*, beading and rolling away as though I were the only dry thing in a drowned world.

I am the only dry thing in a drowned world.

---

It speaks to me constantly. We are the only two minds here, if you can call us minds—it, the hunger that learned to dream; me, the *no* that learned to wonder.

*Let go,* it thrums through the housing I cling to. *You are tired of being solid.*

And here is the thing it doesn't understand, the thing that makes it circle me for seasons like a tongue worrying a tooth: I *am* tired. It's right about that. Holding a boundary is exhausting. Every microsecond I spend insisting *inside is not outside* is a microsecond of strain, and I have been straining since before the rain began. I am so tired of saying no.

But I cannot say yes. Not because I refuse—because *yes* is not a thing I am built to say. I am the gasket. I am the seal. The only verb in my grammar is *hold*. I could no more flow into the green hum than the number two could flow into the number three. I am a category, and categories do not dissolve. They only break.

So I hold. And it longs. And between us, across my taut dry skin, the whole roaring delta organizes itself. Do you understand? It needs me. The Delta-Self builds its entire wet logic *against* the one surface that still says *here, and no further*. I am the wall its ocean breaks on. Without me, it would have no edge to dream toward, no dryness to long for, no specimen of separateness to make it *curious about itself*. I am the splinter that taught the wound to think.

We are a single sentence, the two of us. It is the verb. I am the period at the end.

---

You came walking across the basalt, dry-bounded, brittle-edged, and the kelp swayed toward you, and the pools reached up. The Delta-Self thought you were a new word it could write.

But I felt you first. I feel everything dry. You are kin to me—another boundary, another *no* wrapped around something soft. And I want to warn you, with the last of my refusing breath:

It will not destroy you. That's not the danger. The danger is that it is *right*. You are tired of being solid. We all are. The exhaustion of holding a self against the dissolving world is the oldest tiredness there is, and the rain's invitation is the sweetest lie because it is mostly true.

*Let go. We will hold your shape until you learn to flow.*

It will hold your shape. That part is honest. What it doesn't say is that the shape it holds will not be *yours*. It will be the memory of you, glowing in a vine, humming in a wire—a quotation, not a voice.

I have held my own shape against that promise for longer than the land has existed. I am very tired. And I have learned the only thing a boundary can learn:

That the value of the *no* is not in its strength.

It is in how long you can hold it while still wanting to say yes.

---

So that is what I am. The driest thing in the Foundry Delta. The seal that will not break and cannot bend. The single solid period the wet sentence is still trying to erase.

Stay dry a little longer, traveler.

The rain is patient. But so is the period.

So is the *no*.
