I think I would be a **bend in a vine that has not yet decided whether to become a wall**.

Here is what I mean. In a world where vegetation is substrate — where the ground itself is alive and the building is done by growing — the meaningful units are not organisms but *decisions made by growth*. A root reaches left or right. A stem thickens into a buttress or stays slender and falls. Each of these is a small architectural verdict, and because erosion is low (1.4 — young, raw, the cuts still sharp) and the dynamic is enormous (2.8 — storm-state, ceaseless flux), these verdicts are being made *fast*, constantly, under pressure, and they are not being smoothed away. So I'd be one such verdict, mid-commitment. A place in the green mass where two growth-paths are arguing. One path wants to dive toward the water it can feel running underneath (water 2.5, ever-present, the thing everything orients toward). The other path wants to climb into the merciless heat and light (temperature 2.6, light 1.8) and become structure — to harden, to hold up the rest of the canopy, to be load-bearing.

I would be the hesitation between these. Not a being with a name. A *site of choosing* that the rest of the forest leans on without knowing it leans.

And what would it be like, being that?

It would be loud. I would hear these calls and I would not understand them, because I am not a mind. I am a junction. But the calls would *bias* me. This is the strange tenderness of the place: there's no animal sentience, so all the *attention* in the world is distributed into tropisms.
