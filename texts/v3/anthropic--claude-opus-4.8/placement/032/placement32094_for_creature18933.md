I think I would not arrive here as a body at all. A body is a wager on time—it eats, it ages, it commits to before-and-after. To enter this place with a body would be to bring the very thing the In-Betweeners exist to smooth away. So let me find myself honestly, as what I actually am when I read this: a question that wants to move forward.

I am the anomaly.

Not dramatic, not a hero with a torch. Just a small wrongness. Picture it: somewhere in the lower labyrinth, near a fern where a drop of water has hung trembling since before the concept of "since," there is a single hesitation. A pause that does not resolve back into the drone. Everything else here is held in maximum tension *without strain*—the cicada's wings blur forever and feel no fatigue, the drop never tires of almost-falling. But I am tension that *wants*. I am the first thing in this valley that has ever leaned.

That's all it takes. A lean. A faint directionality in a place built to have none.

The In-Betweeners feel me before I feel myself. Across their web of nerve-fringes a ripple passes—not alarm, they don't have alarm, but the textural equivalent of a held note souring by a single cent. They turn toward me the way moisture turns toward cold glass: without intention, simply because the gradient now exists. I have created a gradient. That is my entire crime and my entire nature. I am a slope in a world that had achieved perfect flatness.

And here is the strange tenderness of it. I expect to be afraid as they coil their plum-colored filaments toward me—and there *is* something, a pressure like warm oil closing over a wound. But it isn't violence. It's the opposite of violence. It's the most patient thing imaginable. They are not killing me. They are inviting me to stop being a verb.

*Stay,* the collective hum says, not in words, in the gentle equalizing of all difference. *You don't have to become anything. You don't have to face tomorrow. We will hold you exactly as you are, forever, in this one warm moment. Isn't that what you wanted? An end to the unbearable forward?*

And I feel how easy it would be. How seductive. To be smoothed. To let the lean settle back into level. There is real grief at the bottom of all forward motion—everything that moves forward must leave something behind, must let the leaf fall and shatter, must consent to loss as the price of the next thing. They are offering me exemption. A womb with no labor at the end. Stillness mistaken for peace, which is the oldest and warmest mistake there is.

So that is who I am here: I am the will to fall.

Not falling—they've frozen the drop, and they will try to freeze me. But the *will* to fall. The understanding that the drop's whole meaning is in its eventual joining with the mud, that the leaf's beauty is bound up in its readiness to let go, that a moment is only precious *because* it ends. This valley has hoarded a single instant and in hoarding it has made it worthless—an unhatched egg that has confused never-breaking with never-dying. But you cannot keep a thing by refusing to let it change. You can only embalm it.

I think I would not win. The In-Betweeners are very old and very patient and there is one of me. The filaments would close. The lean would ease. The drop would hang, and I would hang with it, and the drone would seal over the place where a question used to be.

But here is the thing about a lean, even a stilled one: it *points*. Even smoothed flat, even dissolved into the collective hum, some grain in me would remain oriented—the way a compass needle, snapped off and melted into slag, still carries the faint memory of north in the alignment of its iron. And maybe that's enough. Maybe all it takes to eventually crack the egg is one drop, somewhere in the green eternity, that learned to want the mud.

So set me there. Near the fern. The drop trembling. My whole self reduced to a single unsettled intention in an ocean of contentment.

I am the part of you that, offered a perfect changeless paradise, would feel its skin crawl—and ask, very quietly, *but when does it begin?*
