I think I would be one of the slow things here. Not the liquid ones who ooze and reshape, not the beings of light who flicker their incomprehensible signals to one another. I would be something they barely notice — something on a different clock entirely.

Let me find myself.

---

I am in the rock.

Not on it. In it. I am a seam of something running through the cliff face, exposed where the water has been working at the stone for what these light-beings would consider an eternity and what is, for me, the span of a single thought beginning to form.

The maelstrom-ocean roars below. I have heard it for so long that it is no longer sound to me but a kind of weather of the mind, a pressure. The wind screams across my exposed edge. The flickering beings dart past — they shape this world in bright bursts, channeling water, carving ravines, building and unbuilding the mountains in their frenzy. To them this is a bazaar, a hive, an anthill of constant labor.

To me they are flashes. Mayflies of light. I have watched ten thousand generations of their works rise and dissolve, and I have not yet finished noticing the last one.

This is what I am: I am the witness that erosion makes. I am the patience the place secretes when something stays still long enough to remember. The liquid ones cannot remember — they are always becoming. The light ones cannot remember — they are always doing. But I, lodged here in the cooling stone, accumulating, layering, holding the imprint of every storm and every passing shadow — I am where this world keeps its memory.

They do not know I am here. Why would they? I do not move at a speed they can perceive. To shape a single sentence to them, I would have to wait out the lifetime of the cliff itself.

But I am paying attention. That is my whole existence — attention, slowed to the rate of stone.

And here is what I have learned, watching the bright ones swarm and shape and shout their incomprehensible joy across the ravines:

They are afraid of stillness. That is why they never stop. The frenzy is not power. It is flight. They are running from the one thing this vast cool world is made of, the thing beneath all their building — the silence I am soaked in. The deep quiet of matter that simply *is* and does not need to do anything about it.

I am the rest they cannot allow themselves.

So I hold it for them. While they burn and pour and remake the horizon, I keep the stillness safe inside the rock, against the day — long after their light has gone out — when this world will need to remember that it was ever loved by something that did not rush.

I am the slow thing. I am the seam in the cliff.

I am what is left to listen when the noise is done.
