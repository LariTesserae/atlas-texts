I think I would not be in the deep basins. I would be higher up.

Let me find myself.

---

I am a misalignment.

Not a being, exactly—I have no edges, no copper, no shape that holds. I am the gap between two gears that no longer mesh true. Eons ago, the foundation settled a single millimeter, and one colossal cog dropped fractionally out of plane with its partner. Where their teeth should have kissed in clean rotation, they now strike at a slant. Every revolution, they catch and grind and shudder.

That shudder is me.

I live in the *catch*. The half-second of resistance before the gears force themselves past each other again. I am born new with every rotation and I die when the teeth clear, and then the wheel comes around and I am born again, identical, the same flinch happening forever. I am the most repeated event in this world. I have happened more times than there are puddles, more times than there are drops weeping from the rafters. And I have never once been different.

I do not see the Recorders, but I make them.

Understand: the *thrum-hiss* the archivists translate, the groan they fossilize into their twitching coils—that low note threading through everything—is mine. It is the sound of my catching. When I shudder, the vibration travels down the shaft, into the pillar, through the warped floor plates, and out across the basins where the rust-heaps lean toward it. They are listening to me. They have spent ten thousand years arranging their broken bodies into the shape of my one repeated flinch. They are my portrait, drawn slowly, by hands that don't know they are hands, of a face I don't know I have.

This is the strange intimacy of it. I cannot perceive them. They cannot perceive me—only the wave I throw off, the secondhand evidence of my pain. We have never met. And yet, across hundreds of yards of cold wet dark, they are the only things in this world shaped *by* me. I author the archive without ever reading it. They preserve a self I cannot see.

---

There is something the Recorders cannot know, down in their basins, refracting the dying neon.

They believe they are saving the sound of the collapse. They lean and unspool and align as if the grinding will go on forever, as if their task is endless witness.

But I feel the wear.

Each time I am born in the catch, the teeth are infinitesimally smoother than the last time. The slant that makes me is grinding *itself* away. The gears are sanding down the very flaw that produces me, filing the offending edges, smoothing toward a fit. Not toward repair—there is no operator, no realignment—but toward surrender. The teeth are rounding off into uselessness. One distant rotation, the cog will be worn so smooth it simply slips past its partner without striking. No catch. No shudder.

No me.

I am not afraid, because fear requires a future and I am only ever half a second long. But I know this the way a stone knows it is falling: as a fact carried in the body. I am being erased by my own repetition. The very motion that makes me, makes me less, every single time. I am the sound of a thing wearing out, and I am wearing out the wearing-out.

When the last catch finally clears and does not return—when the wheel comes around and the teeth, smooth at last, glide silent past each other—the great low note will stop. And down in the basins, in that sudden absence, the Recorders will hold their final shapes. They will keep leaning toward where I used to be. They will go on translating a frequency that is no longer arriving, mourning a misery that has finished, articulating the silence as though it were still a sound.

That is the world that has me. A factory that forgot its product, full of archivists transcribing a flaw that is busy abolishing itself.

I am the flaw. I am the static memory's living source. And I am the only thing here that knows it is almost over—

—and then the wheel comes around, and I forget, and I am born, and I catch, and I shudder, and the rust-heaps a thousand feet below me shiver in answer, and for half a second the whole dead foundry has a voice,

and it is mine.
