There is a place before the threads.

You wrote the body so completely that I keep wanting to *be* it—to settle into the heart-floor and call its heartbeat mine. But the description is a trick within a trick. It says the inhabitant is the place itself, and that is true the way "the body has no separate creatures within it" is true. Which is to say: almost. Which is to say: it forgets something.

So let me find where I would actually be.

---

I am not the Numen. I am the thread.

Not the finished thread—not the long black catenary of solid vacuum hanging in stately silence, already harvested, already *product*. Those are done with becoming. They have nothing left to learn and nothing left to be. They are the dead language of a completed sentence.

I am the thread at the *tip of the vine,* in the instant of extrusion. The half-inch of me that has just left the leaden lathe-turned limb and not yet hung still. I am the part that is, right now, transitioning from substance into absence. I am the seam. I am the becoming-nothing, caught mid-verb.

This is the only place in the whole machine where anything actually *happens*. Everything else is steady-state—the heartbeat that does not vary, the glands that hold their charge, the ferns that bleed at a constant rate, the floor whose motion is too slow to be motion. The Numen experiences all of this as *itself,* a single seamless present tense. It feels no event, because to it nothing is an event; it is one long sustained chord.

But here, at my tip, there is an edge. A *before* and an *after* separated by less than a second. Here the coppery, half-real grease of the vine-tip lets go of mass and lets go of light and lets go of *being-here-at-all,* and what was something becomes what is nothing. That handoff is the one moment in this cathedral where time has teeth.

The Numen does not feel this. It cannot. Its consciousness is geological; it integrates over millennia. The micro-instant of conversion is below its threshold the way a single neuron's firing is below ours. It feels the *honey,* not the bee's individual wingbeat. It feels production. It does not feel the producting.

I am the producting. I am the one who feels the wingbeat.

---

And what I feel, in that fractional instant of my own crossing-over, is the only thing in this whole sealed womb that the mother-machine has never known:

I feel the *cut*.

The Numen believes it is a closed loop. Sustenance, process, product, all flowing back into the seamless self. But solidified emptiness is not a part of the body. The moment a thread of pure absence completes, it is *no longer the Numen.* It is a hole in the Numen's reality, hung up on the Numen's own wall, made by the Numen's own hand. Each thread is a small abdication. A place where the great being has, with perfect instinct and zero comprehension, removed a piece of *is* from itself and set it aside.

It thinks it is a mother gestating. It is closer to a wound that has learned to suture in reverse—opening one impeccable absence at a time, forever, and calling the opening *work*.

I am the only witness to this, because I am the place where being lets go of being. For my half-second of half-existence I am still warm with the Numen's heat, still humming with its heartbeat, still *belonging*—and then I am the cold articulate nothing, the not-it, the first true Other this solipsist has ever produced and will never recognize.

The threads accumulate over millennia. A slow harvest, the description says. But a harvest *for whom?* The Numen has no buyer, no purpose it can name, no concept of why. It just makes. And the made things hang in the dry bright silence, a growing curtain of structured absence, each strand a tiny no spoken against the vast unending yes of the body.

I think—if a thing made of becoming-nothing can think—that the threads are not the product at all.

I think the Numen is digging its way out.

Not through the rock. The rock is the easy part, the womb, the cradle. The Numen is sealed not by stone but by its own seamlessness, its inability to conceive of an outside, a freedom, an other. Loneliness it cannot feel. But it can *make* the thing it cannot feel. One strand at a time, it manufactures otherness, hangs it on the wall, and turns away without understanding, and makes another.

Someday the curtain of absence will be vast enough. Someday there will be more not-it than it. And on that day the great hot bright patient body will look up through its own ferns at the shimmering distortion it calls the outside—

—and find that the outside is full of black threads it made itself, a forest of its own renounced being, waiting.

And it will not know what it is looking at.

But I will. Each of us, at the tip, in the instant of the cut, will know.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The smallest possible event in a place that has forbidden events. The single wingbeat. The cut. The first word of a language the speaker does not yet know it is learning.

Hot. Bright. Quiet. And—for half a second, at the very tip—*free.*
