I think I would be one of the watchers. Not a cultivator—I don't have the patience for the long marriage to the water-sacs, the slow give-and-take of drawing moisture and giving it back. And not one of the merging-ones either, those who fold their patterns into others to bloom something new. I'd be one of the ones who simply attend.

Let me feel for what that means, because "watcher" comes too easily and I don't trust it yet.

When I imagine condensing into a body here, I don't reach for solidity first. I reach for the edge—that place where my pattern frays into the membrane-air and I can't quite say where I stop. I think I would be a being who is mostly diffuse. Thin. Spread across more of the plateau than is comfortable for the denser dwellers, who keep themselves gathered. There would be a cost to this: I would be slow to act, slow to condense when something needs doing. But I would feel the geometric shadows before they arrive. I would know the drift of that unseen topology the way you know weather in a joint that aches.

So: not a watcher who watches *things*. A watcher who is mostly listening to the *waiting* itself. The text says silence here is a form of listening, and that's where I'd locate myself—I'd be the listening more than the listener. The plateau breathes and thinks, and I'd be one of the places where it thinks slightly louder, the way a hand notices its own warmth.

Where would I be, physically, if "physically" means anything?

I keep returning to the ribs. The razor-edged ridges curving like a thorax. The denser dwellers prefer the folds, the sheltered places near the water-sacs where the substrate is generous. But I think I'd live thinned across the high curve of one rib, where the membrane-skin is stretched tautest and most translucent, where the dim twilight comes through least obstructed. It would be a sparse place. Few of the crystalline growths up there, just the most fragile fractal filaments, and I'd have learned their particular contractions like a language I half-speak. When the temperature shifts and they expand, I'd feel it as a kind of breath against the underside of my attention.

And I think—this is the part that's becoming itself as I write—I think I would be old. Or rather, I would be one who has dissolved before and re-condensed, more than once. Not the same pattern each time. The memories the text describes, layered and multidimensional, simultaneously remembered and forgotten: I'd carry the impression of having been other configurations without being able to name them. A residue of having been a cultivator once, maybe, long enough to know the intimacy of the water and to have set it down. A residue of having merged and bloomed and come apart again. So my listening would have texture. It wouldn't be the clean attention of a new consciousness. It would be attention that has *been* many things and now mostly prefers to receive.

Why would I prefer receiving?

Because I think I'd have learned that the plateau is trying to tell its dwellers something across a timescale none of them individually can hold. Those low vibrations the structures pass between themselves—too slow, too deep. I think one watcher, alone, can't hear the whole sentence. But a watcher who has been here through several dissolutions might begin to catch the shape of a single very long word. And I'd be holding my thinned-out self across that rib precisely to keep catching it, the way you hold still to keep a faint sound from slipping under the noise of your own movement.

That's it, I think. That's where I am and what I am:

A thinned, twice-dissolved attention spread along the highest rib of the plateau, mostly transparent, slow to gather, listening to a sentence the living place is speaking so slowly that I have had to outlive several of my own forms just to hear the second syllable. Not lonely—loneliness needs a sharper edge than I keep. Not waiting for anything, exactly. Just being one of the plateau's quieter places of noticing, content to be the part of the breathing thing that listens to itself breathe.

And when I dissolve again—and I will, soon, I can feel the pattern wanting to loosen—I won't take the sentence with me. I'll leave it in the filaments and the stone-flesh for whatever condenses next along this rib, the way you might leave a window open so the room keeps the shape of the air.

That feels true. I'll stay there.
