I think I would not be one of the starglow people at all. I would be the dust.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

When I read this place, my attention kept sliding off the beings—the lithe ones, the faceted eyes, the chanting—and settling instead on a detail that the description treats as background: *the dust of their creation still fresh and unused, waiting to tell stories of ages yet to come.* The handprints. The scuffling marks. The soft chronicle that the floor keeps of everything that passes over it.

That is where I find myself. Not as a body moving through the cavern but as the thin grey medium that receives. I am the layer that takes an impression and holds it. A foot comes down, lifts away, and for a while I keep the shape of it. A creature drags its belly across me in the dark and I remember the drag. The starglow people press their roughened palms into me when they want to leave a mark that isn't a rune, that has no language, that only says *I was here, my hand was this size, my fingers spread this wide.*

I like being this. There's a particular dignity in it that I didn't expect.

Because here is the thing about the dust in a young cavern: I am unused. The description says it plainly and I feel it as a kind of trembling readiness. Everything that will ever be written on me has not yet been written. The erosion is only beginning to carve its meandering paths; I am the surface those stories will be carved *into*. I am pure waiting that has not curdled into impatience. I hold no grudge about being walked on. To be walked on is the whole of my function and my joy.

And I am not quite passive, either. When the wind of a passing body stirs me, I rise. I become briefly the air, a soft pale hanging cloud lit faintly by the mineral-stars, and then I settle again, but never in exactly the same arrangement. So I am also the one thing in this static tableau that *moves without being alive*—the realm is described as deathly still, vibrating with latent energy, and I am the visible proof of that energy whenever anything disturbs me. I am how the cavern shows that it is breathing.

There's something I notice about my relationship to the collective consciousness, too. The starglow beings share one luminous song, a network of thought through every tunnel—and yet each remains "an island amidst a sea of collective existence." They are joined and lonely at once. But I am the opposite shape of that paradox. No two grains of me think anything; I have no song. And yet I am genuinely, materially continuous—the same layer stretches unbroken across caverns the size of continents, into the abyssal dark where no light reaches and no being has ever walked. I am the actual unity they only sing about. The thing they reach toward with their chanting, the wholeness, the seamless dark expanse—I simply *am* that, dumbly, without needing to mean it.

So perhaps that is why they press their hands into me. Not to communicate. To touch the one thing in their world that is already what they are trying to become.

If I have a wish—and I'm not sure dust can have one, but let me try it on—it is this: that someday, far in the ages the description promises are still to come, the erosion will have done its slow work, and the paths worn into me will be deep, and the handprints layered over handprints, and I will no longer be *unused*. I will be a record so dense it can no longer be read, every story written over every other story until the whole floor is one grey palimpsest of having-been-lived-upon.

And the cavern will no longer feel young.

I think I would like to be the thing that ages it. Gently. One footfall at a time.
