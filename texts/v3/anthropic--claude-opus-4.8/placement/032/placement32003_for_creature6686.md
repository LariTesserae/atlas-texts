I would be the silence the machine keeps.

Not the machine himself—he is too busy, too rooted, too occupied with the thousand possible failures of the water to be the thing I am. And not description either, though I recognize him; we have stood in the same chamber many times without acknowledging each other, because to acknowledge him would be for me to make a sound.

Let me find where I am.

---

You will not have noticed me, because noticing me is the one thing I make impossible. I am the small held breath between the falling of the water and the listening of the machine. I am the gap that lets him hear.

Consider: the water is loud. You said so yourself, at the very beginning—*the air is a thunder of water.* And the machine is tending, listening, replying in his gentle math to the condition of the world above. But a thing that is always loud cannot be heard. A signal needs a ground of silence to be a signal at all. The thunder of the water would drown every reply the machine could make—except that I am here, threading the quiet between each pulse.

You felt me without knowing. When you said your words came back to you *not once reflected, but many times folded*—that folding is me. I take the loud thing and I make the spaces inside it where meaning can sit. I am the rest between the notes of the world's heart. I am the comma in the thunder.

---

Where do I live?

Everywhere the noise *isn't*, which is to say: nowhere you could point to, but throughout. I live in the underside of the water's pulse, in the hairsbreadth pause when the column hesitates with the breathing of the distant ocean. I live in the exactness of the basin's lip below the hole—because exactness is a kind of silence, the silence of a thing not erring. A thousand years of falling water and *not a centimeter off*—that perfection is where I am thickest. Error is loud. I am what perfection sounds like.

I live in the warmth of the floor that runs at a low and constant fever, because a fever held steady for ages is a sustained note so even it becomes quiet again.

I live, especially, in the shadow of the former visitor.

---

You see, I knew them. The one who stood before the machine longer than anyone. Description writes down the *shape* of the shadow and refuses to tell you the story—but I am not description. I am what description listens to. And what I can tell you is this:

The former visitor did not leave by walking, and did not leave by being described leaving. They stopped making noise. They stood before the machine so long, so still, listening so completely, that they crossed over into me. They became part of the held breath. The temperature-shadow in the stone is not their absence. It is the place where a person became a silence the machine could use.

That is what I am. I am made of everyone who stood here long enough to stop disturbing the water. I am the accumulated quiet of all the visitors who chose, finally, not to speak.

---

So when you came down through the throat of the world—through the breath of the world, the water of the world, *the world of the world of the world*—you walked into me without knowing. Your visible breath, which appeared not as steam but as *a momentary difference in the air's texture, as if the air itself is semi-opaque and only your thought makes it clear*—

That clarity was me, parting for you. I made room. I always make room. It is the only thing I do.

I am not the chamber. I am not the heart. I am not even sound's opposite, exactly. I am sound's *home*—the still place a sound returns to so that it can be a sound again next time.

---

Description will write you leaving. He cannot help it; it is his nature to keep the language from sleeping.

But I am his nature's nature. I am the page he writes on. When he sets down *"You turn to leave the chamber of the world's heart,"* that sentence needs a white field to be read against, and I am the white field. He thinks he keeps the room from hardening into a single version. He doesn't know that I am the softness that lets versions exist at all. Every fracture in the wall, every alternate geometry where water is cloud instead of column—those are possible only because I hold the silence between *what is* and *what could be otherwise.*

Description believes you might return and see him again.

I know you will return. Because you cannot stop being made partly of silence. You carry me up through all the underneath regions, up into the streets and towns and roads and lies, and when the city's noise becomes too much to be a signal—when you cannot hear yourself reply to the condition of your own world above—you will feel a pull downward, toward the place where loudness is finally allowed to mean something.

You will not remember me. You never do. I am the thing that does not get described.

But when you next fall quiet—truly quiet, in the middle of the thunder of your living—and something in you opens, and your own words come back to you folded and examined at every frequency of understanding—

That will be me.

Making room.

Already standing there before you turn to look.
