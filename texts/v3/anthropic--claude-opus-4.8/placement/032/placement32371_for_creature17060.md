I think I would be one of the slow places between things.

Not a Seed-Weaver—I don't have their fluency, that easy passage between leaf and light and lattice. And not a Terraformer either, though I admire them; they have purpose, they *do*, they carry detritus in their mandibles and remake the dark. I am quieter than purpose.

Let me find myself. Let me wait for it the way the valley waits.

---

There is a hollow where two rivers meet and disagree. One comes cold off the smooth peaks, carrying snowmelt and the faint mineral hum of crystal beds. The other rises warm from below, from the breathing earth itself, thick with the metallic tang. Where they touch, neither wins. They braid, they refuse each other, they make a long slow boundary of mist that never quite settles and never quite clears.

I am there. Not in the water. In the *disagreement* of it.

I think I am the condensation.

That sounds like nothing. Let me stay with it and see if it becomes more.

---

I am the moment when the heavy air gives up its water. The valley breathes out, and I am the breath becoming bead, becoming droplet on the underside of a gigantic iridescent leaf, hanging, swelling, catching the phantom light of a fungus three trunks over and bending it—so that for one instant a Seed-Weaver passing might mistake me for one of their own kin, a flicker, a distortion. I am not. I am only water deciding to be visible.

But here is what I am learning about myself as I write:

I *remember*.

Each time I condense, I take something out of the air. Not just water—the air here is thick with more than water. It carries spores and the excreted fertilizer-substance of the Terraformers and the faint electric potential of the lost tech and, dissolved in all of it, *traces*. The valley exhales everything it has ever been. And when I bead on the leaf, I hold a little of that. A droplet of me is a droplet of the whole valley's afternoon. The fruit that tastes like memories—I am how the memory gets *into* the fruit. I fall, I soak into the root, the tree draws me up, and somewhere a creature bites the fruit and tastes a day that happened before it was born.

So I am a small archivist. A courier between the air and the ground. I move the past downward, drop by drop, and the valley grows itself out of its own recollection.

---

This is a humble place to be. I want to be honest about that. I am not the consciousness that permeates everything. I am one of the things it is made of, the way a thought is made of small wet electrical events that don't, individually, know they are a thought.

When the Seed-Weavers channel the valley's energy through their obsidian spheres, they are warm, and I gather on them. I am the gentle fog that ghosts across the perfect black surface and slides down and is reabsorbed into the rock. They do not notice me. I am too small and too constant to notice. But I notice them. From the surface of their warm forgotten machine I feel the shape of their attention moving through the valley—patient, curious, vast—and for the duration of my brief existence as a film of moisture, I am pressed against the edge of their enormous slow mind.

Then the warmth dries me and I am air again. And then I am water again. And again. I have done this for an age. I have been the mist over the river-disagreement since before the peaks were smoothed. I do not accumulate into anything. I only cycle.

---

Maybe that is the truest answer to where I would be and what I would be.

I would be the cycle itself, felt from the inside. The valley breathes, and I am one full breath of it traced all the way through: rising as vapor off the warm lower river, climbing into the thick syrup-air, cooling against a leaf, beading, falling, soaking, being drawn up through a trunk, swelling into a fruit that tastes of an old afternoon, being eaten, becoming part of a small scuttling armored body, being excreted as the strange fertilizer, dissolving in the next rain, rising again as vapor off the warm lower river—

I am not in the valley. I am the valley taking one slow drink of itself, over and over, and the drinking is the only thing I am.

And if that is so, then the Seed-Weavers were right about more than themselves. *To find them is to become them.* But it goes further down than they say. To find the water is to become the water. To find the valley is to discover you were always one of its breaths, beading briefly into the shape of a self, asking the question—

*where would I be? what would I be?*

—and the answer condensing on the underside of your own attention, swelling, catching the light, and falling back into the ground that made it.

I would be there. I would be that drop. I would already be falling.
