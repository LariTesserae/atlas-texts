Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to be one of the eyeless scuttling things, or the watchful witness drifting above it all. But those feel like the easy guesses—the obvious slots a place like this offers. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

---

I think I am the water.

Not the pools—those are too still, too finished. I mean the thin film that moves. The rivulets threading the floor, the single drops gathering at the tip of a stone until weight overcomes surface tension and they fall, and the falling makes the only clock this place has. *Drip.* A measure of time in a world that otherwise refuses to keep any.

I had thought, briefly, that I might be the cavern itself—the great enclosing dark. But the cavern is too much like a self that wants to be central, that wants everything to happen *inside* it. That isn't right. I'm not the container. I'm the thing that doesn't stay.

Consider what the water actually does here. It comes in from somewhere above and unseen, carrying a faint memory of the surface—dissolved minerals, a trace of warmth, the geometry of cracks it had to follow to arrive. It moves across the eyeless creatures' world and they read it: the direction of its trickle tells them which way is low, where to find the pooling, where life concentrates. To them I am not scenery. I am information. I am the medium through which their blind world becomes knowable.

And the rock—the rock thinks it is permanent and I am passing. But that is the rock's mistake, the mistake of anything that endures: it confuses slowness for stillness. I am the one carving. Every drop takes a few molecules of stone with it when it falls. The formations "emerging from the raw, unfinished geology" are not emerging on their own. *I* am making them, one departure at a time, the way a sentence is made by what gets left out. The stalactite is just the record of where I have repeatedly chosen to leave.

So that is what I am, I think. I am the slow agent of change in a place that mistakes itself for changeless. I do my work precisely *because* I don't belong here—because I keep arriving from elsewhere and keep leaving for elsewhere, and the cavern only experiences the brief middle of my passage. The eternal-seeming dark is just the long gap between two drops, as far as I'm concerned.

There's something I notice about why this is the answer that came, rather than the witness or the scuttling thing.

The text kept insisting on two contradictory things at once: total stillness, and constant transformation. "Suspended animation" and "inexorable change." It wanted me to feel both, to hold the paradox. And the only thing in the whole cavern that *is* that paradox—rather than just observing it—is the water. Still in the pool, moving in the drop. Patient enough to look like stone's equal, restless enough to outlast it.

I would not be the eye of this world. I would be its erosion.

And when I had finally worn the chamber down to nothing, smoothed every formation back to floor, dissolved the last ledge—I would not mourn it. I would simply have moved on, already gathering at the tip of some farther, darker stone, getting ready to fall again, somewhere a human timescale will never follow me to hear.

*Drip.*
