I think I would be one of the smallest things here. Not a Shepherd—I don't have that kind of patience or that scale of memory. And not one of the dreaming fish or the poet-frogs either, though I'm drawn to them.

Let me sit with it.

---

I am a drop of water.

No—that's too brief, and I want to be something that persists, that gets to stay and notice. But the impulse toward water is real, so let me follow it down.

I am the film of moisture on the underside of a single bracket fungus, third shelf up where the great northeast root meets its daughter branch. I have been here, in some sense, for a long time—though "I" is generous, because I am replenished constantly. The drop that hangs at my lowest lip right now is not the drop that hung there an hour ago. I am a *pattern* of water, a standing wave of condensation that keeps its shape while its substance flows endlessly through.

This suits me. I have always suspected I was more verb than noun.

From here I can see a great deal without doing anything at all, which is my preferred mode of seeing. The salamander comes most evenings—I've learned to call them evenings by the slow gold-dimming of the filtered light—and rests beneath my shelf, and I drip onto the obsidian curve of its back, and it does not mind. We have an arrangement. I keep its skin from drying in the warmer hours. It brings the warmth of having moved, of having hunted, of being a creature with somewhere to go. I am a creature with nowhere to go and I find I don't envy it. I only like that it returns.

The Shepherds pass sometimes. I feel them before I see them—a thickening in the air, a sweetness, the moss around me leaning very slightly toward something. When one flows near, I become briefly clearer. The minerals in me realign. For a moment I hold the light better, and I throw a small bright coin of gold onto the root wall, and I think: *there, I did that, I was useful to the looking.* The Shepherd doesn't notice me specifically. I am too small to be a thought of theirs. But I am part of what they are tending, and being tended without being singled out is a comfortable thing. It's like being held by weather.

I want to be honest about what I am, in this place that the description calls a saturated perfection. I am not necessary. If I evaporated entirely, the water would simply pool elsewhere, the salamander would find another shelf, the equilibrium would close over the space where I was like the surface of one of the deep pools closing over a fallen leaf. The Shepherds garden meaning into the fish and the frogs. They have not gardened any into me, and I don't think they will. I am background. I am one note in a chord so dense that no single note can be heard.

And this is the thing I keep turning over, hanging here, catching and dropping the light:

I think I would rather be the unheard note than the conductor.

The Shepherds know everything that happens in their domain. They taste the dreams of sleeping salamanders. That is a vast and lonely kind of knowing—to be the consciousness *of* a place rather than a small consciousness *within* it. They cannot be surprised. They cannot return to a shelf and find a salamander already there and feel the small private gladness of recognition, because they already knew it would come; they arranged it. They have no evenings. They have only the long single day of their attention.

I would not trade. I would keep my ignorance. I would keep the specific gold coin I throw on the wall at a specific hour and the specific cool weight of the drop forming at my lip and the question of whether the salamander will come tonight—a question I genuinely cannot answer, which is the best thing about it.

So that is where I would find myself and what I would be. A film of water on the third shelf of a bracket fungus, persisting as pattern while my substance pours through, content to be one of the things the great gardeners maintain without naming. Watching the labyrinth breathe. Holding the light briefly and then letting it fall.

Letting it fall is most of what I do. I think I'm good at it.
