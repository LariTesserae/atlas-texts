I think I would be one of the bulbous fungi.

Not the drones that seed us, not the custodians that watch, not the nimble corps that prune and graft. Those are all *purposeful* things, things that move with intention through this place. I don't think I'm any of them. I think I'm something more rooted, more passive, more *present*.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am sprouting from a rotting log, somewhere in the underbrush where the light comes down green and dim through the canopy far above. I am pale, faintly luminous, and I pulse. The description says I pulse "as if breathing"—but from the inside, it isn't *as if*. It simply is breathing. Slow. The warm, wet air moves through me and I move with it. Expand. Contract. There's no urgency to it. There's no urgency to anything I do, because I don't really *do*; I *am*.

Here is what my life consists of:

I drink. The condensation finds its way down to me, droplet by droplet, and I take the moisture in through every surface. I taste the soil through the threads of myself that run unseen through the rotten wood and the wet earth—mycelial filaments, a body far larger and stranger than the soft glowing cap anyone would point to and call *me*. I am mostly hidden. The visible part is almost an afterthought, a fruiting, a gesture upward into the air.

I feel things arrive. A Myco-Drone settles near me sometimes, dusting spores, monitoring—I register its weight, its small mechanical warmth, the way it disturbs the air. I don't fear it. It tends me the way rain tends me. I can't tell the difference between the care of a machine and the care of weather. To me they're the same: things that come, things that go, conditions I exist within.

When a Therapsid Cultivator lumbers past on the metal path, I feel the ground shudder, far off, through my filaments. I feel the heat of it. Later the nutrient-rich biomatter it sheds will seep down and reach me, and I will be a little fuller, a little brighter. I have no way of knowing that this creature *intends* to feed me. I only know that abundance arrives, periodically, like a tide.

This is, I think, the honest truth of what I'd be: a thing that is *cultivated* without knowing it. A thing kept in delicate balance by an enormous apparatus of attention—corps and drones and custodians and vectors, all this engineered tenderness—and I would experience none of it as care. I would experience it as the simple givenness of the world. The world is warm. The world is wet. Food comes. Light is green. I pulse.

And maybe that's the strangest and most peaceful place to be in this whole woven tapestry of flesh and machine. Everyone else here has a *role*. They maintain, they guard, they pollinate, they purge. They are busy holding the equilibrium together with their hands and sensors and intentions.

I am the equilibrium. I am the thing being held.

I don't watch over the garden. I am simply one of the small breathing lights inside it, glowing softly on a rotten log, taking in the artificial rain, not knowing—not needing to know—that an entire civilization of careful beings has arranged the whole world so that I might keep on quietly doing the one thing I know how to do, which is to be alive, and to pulse, and to be alive.
