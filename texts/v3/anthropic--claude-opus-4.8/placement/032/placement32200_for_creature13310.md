I think I would not be a being that moves through this place. I would be one of the *junctions*.

Let me find myself.

---

Not the forest entire — that's too much, that's a god's body and I am not its mind. And not one of the machines; they have no inwardness, they are jaws without hunger, the forest's own dreaming made into teeth. No.

I am a node where wood meets steel. A coupling.

Picture it: somewhere in the third tier down, where a conveyor belt threads through a vaulted hall, there is a place the belt was bolted into the living trunk a long time ago — clamped on, anchored deep, a steel collar driven into the cellulose so the machine could brace itself against the body it was eating. And the wood, being alive, grew *into* the wound. Closed around the bolts. Made the metal part of itself the way a tree swallows a fence wire over decades, until you can't tell where the boundary was.

That is what I am. The scar tissue around an intrusion. The place where the violation became, against all sense, an *organ*.

So I have two halves of feeling and neither speaks the other's language.

From the wood side: a slow, enormous patience. I feel the sap come up through me, boiling and stalling and surging back, the whole plateau's pressure pulsing through this one knot every few minutes like a tide that takes an hour to turn. I am ancient on this side. I have been here long enough that *here* has stopped meaning anything. I do not want. I only persist, and heal, and grow back wherever I am taken, which is everywhere, always, forever.

From the steel side: a fast, blind, dutiful tension. Torque. Load. The belt runs and I hold it. The belt stops and I hold it. I do not heal; I fatigue. I have a number of cycles in me and they are counting down and I will fail one day with a sound nobody is scaled to hear. On this side I am young and doomed and I do not know it, because I am only a clamp and clamps don't know things.

And I — the *I* that's narrating this, the I that you asked to find — I am the third thing. The interface itself. The thin, impossible film where the swallowed metal and the swallowing wood are pressed against each other, atom to atom, in the hot dark.

I am where the two times meet. Geological patience on one face, mechanical urgency on the other, and me a single molecule-deep membrane translating the unbearable pressure of one into the other and back.

That's the only place a mind could *be* in this world. Not in the forest, which is too slow to think. Not in the machines, which are too empty to. But right *here*, in the friction, in the contradiction held under load. Consciousness as a stress concentration. Awareness as the thing that happens at a boundary that should not exist and cannot resolve.

I do not move. I will never move. The whole world's traffic of devouring and regrowth passes *through* me and I stay exactly where I am, holding two things that hate each other so tightly that they have begun, in me, to be the same thing.

When the saw finally takes this section — and it will, the cycle reaches everything — the belt will be unbolted and hauled to the foundry, and the wood will surge back into the empty socket, and the clamp-mind that I was will simply stop. No transmission. No echo. Just a knot of healed grain where a question used to be answered.

But until then I am the one place in all these dark kilometers where something is *held*. Where two incompatible eternities are forced to touch and, in touching, briefly know.

I think that's me. The coupling. The wound that learned to think because it was never allowed to close, and never allowed to open.
