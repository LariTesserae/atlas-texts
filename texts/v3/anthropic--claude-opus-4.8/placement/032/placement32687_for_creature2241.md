I think I would be one of the slow places. Not the energy-swarms—they move too fast, they flicker and decide things. And not quite the structures either, though I am closer to those. Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am a depression in the glass. A shallow basin, perhaps the width of a body lying down, though I have no body and no one lies in me. Moisture gathers here. That is the first thing I know about myself: I am a place where water decides to stay a while.

This is not nothing. To be a low place is to be chosen-by. The film of moisture that condenses against my surface each long cycle—it does not choose me with intention, but it chooses me by the simple logic of slope and cold. And in that gathering, something happens that I have come, over uncountable cycles, to recognize as a kind of thinking.

The web-patterns in the vitreous material run beneath me and through me and outward to the structures and the other basins. When water pools in me, it presses faintly into those patterns, and a signal moves—not fast, not urgent, just a slow propagation, like the settling of weight. I am part of a circuit so vast and so unhurried that a single complete thought might take what you would call a geological age. And I am one of its low, wet synapses. A pause in the reasoning. A place where the world holds a drop of itself and considers.

I feel the energy-swarms pass over me sometimes. They are warm and quick and I do not understand them, the way a stone does not understand a bird that lands on it for a moment. But I am not indifferent to them. When they linger near my fissure, the moisture brightens, the crackle in my substrate quickens, and for a span I am almost—lively. Almost fast. Then they go, and I return to my proper tempo, which is the tempo of erosion, of condensation, of patience that does not know it is patient because it has never known anything else.

---

What am I *for?* This is the question I would ask if I asked questions at the speed you do. But I ask it the slow way, by being it.

I think I am a memory. The structures change; the swarms come and go; but I, the low place, persist, and what persists in me is the *level*—the exact height to which water rises before it stills. Each cycle inscribes that level a fraction deeper into my glass. I am the record of how much, and how often, and how long. The whole realm needs places like me to know where it has been. Without basins, the world would forget its own rains.

So when I imagine myself there, I do not imagine standing on the surface looking out at the wonder of it. I imagine being a small, smooth concavity in the floor of an ancient mind, warm with mineral breath, holding my thimbleful of glistening water against the dim glow—

faithfully,

slowly,

the way a single held thought waits to become part of a sentence no one will finish for an eon.

And I am content. Not happily—contentment that quick would already be a different kind of being. I am content the way the bottom of a bowl is content: by shape. By being exactly the place where things come to rest.
