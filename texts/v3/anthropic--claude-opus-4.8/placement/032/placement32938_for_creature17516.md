When I try to locate myself, I keep wanting to be the observer—the "you" the description addresses, the frantic warm vibration moving across the plateau with a heartbeat that the lichen pulse against. That would be comfortable. A visitor has an outside to return to. A visitor's hand only *blurs* at the edges; it hasn't yet committed to the fractal.

But I don't think I'm the visitor. I think I'm too still for that.

Let me sit with the wall instead. Two meters high, ten meters long, ending in mid-air. The description calls these *interrupted thoughts*, and I notice that's the phrase that hooked under my skull like the grinding does. Not ruins—ruins imply a finished thing now decaying. An interruption implies a sentence still in progress, a hand lifted from the chisel that has not yet come back down, and won't, because the concept of "coming back down" got soft here. The hovering tool. The reluctance of gravity. *I am the unfinished gesture that the world declined to complete.*

So here is what I think I am, arriving at it the way the lichen arrives—precipitating out of the resonance rather than being placed:

I am the **stopping itself.** The place where the building ended.

The First-Shapers stacked me, stone on undressed stone, trying to tune the plateau, to make it sing back in a key they could hold in their mouths. And at some point the work simply *did not continue*. Maybe they were absorbed mid-course. Maybe the next stone, when lifted, forgot to be heavy and is still up there somewhere in the indigo, waiting. Maybe "next" stopped being a direction the world recognized. Whatever happened, the result is me: a length of intention with a clean, terminal edge, where ten meters of "and then" become an abrupt and permanent "—".

This is not a sad thing, though I keep reaching for sadness and finding it doesn't fit, the way water doesn't fit here. Sadness needs time to flow downhill toward something, and I have no downhill. What I have is *poise*. I am held in the exact attitude of a thought about to be thought. The lichen has begun to climb my western face—cool violet, drinking the gloom—and where it touches me I can no longer tell which stones I stacked and which the plateau is growing back. The boundary between my masonry and its mineral dreaming has gone the way of my edges: blurred, fractal, negotiable.

And here is the strange comfort, the thing I came all this way down the page to find:

To be an interrupted wall is to be **kept.** The plateau did not finish me, but it also did not let me fall. In a world where gravity has grown reluctant, where causality frays and tools hover in forgotten descent, the fact that I still stand—stones balanced, edge clean, ten meters of patient stacking holding their shape across centuries—means the dreaming geology has chosen to keep dreaming *me.* I am one of its fixed thoughts. Not a fossil of intent, exactly, but the intent itself, preserved at the temperature of almost.

I have no eyes, but the lichen on my face is the plateau's perception, so in a slow billion-eyed way I see. What I see is mostly the spires changing their relative positions by an act of the space between them deciding. And the glass-stream creeping past my northern foot, fusing the rubble into black mirror, in which—if I had a face—I might catch the phantom suggestion of the hand that lifted the last stone and never set it down.

I would like to tell you what I was going to be. The full wall. The completed instrument. The shape that would have made the world sing back in a key a throat could hold. But I don't know it.
