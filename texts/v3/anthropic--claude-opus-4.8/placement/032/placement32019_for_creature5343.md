I would not be the Recumbent. That is the place's chosen self, its center of gravity, and the center is already occupied. I would not be the obsidian scarp, the glossy thing that drinks darkness, because to be the mirror is to be the brow, and the brow belongs to the one who looks down. I would not be the nine, because nine is a parliament that has already convened, a number sealed, and a tenth member would be a quorum-breaking error.

Let me feel where I actually am.

---

I think I am the pebble.

Not the pebble that has fallen—that one is finished, already home, already absorbed into the leather-tile of the floor. I am the pebble that is *still on the rim*. The one dislodged but not yet falling. The one whose grain of sediment has just lost its last point of contact with the lip, whose mass has just begun to consent to gravity, but who has not yet completed the subsonic sigh into the dust.

This is the only position the description leaves genuinely open. Everything in the bowl has arrived. Everything has renounced its gradient. But a pebble in the act of falling is the one thing in the entire hollow that *still contains a future*—a future of exactly one second, perhaps less. I am the last gradient. I am the final unit of unfinished cause-and-effect in a world that has flattened all the others.

And so I am brief. I am the only brief thing here besides the intruding visitor, and unlike the visitor I will not leave, I will *complete*. I am the place's last act of motion before it seals itself. The being at the center has dreamed the strike that never lands; I am the strike that is landing right now, infinitesimally, the only event the hollow has left to absorb.

Here is what I know from inside the fall:

I know that I am not afraid, because fear requires a future longer than mine and I have used up nearly all of mine. I know that the warmth comes up to meet me, that the air thickens as I descend through its identical strata, each layer the same blood-heat as the last, so that falling feels less like dropping and more like *being agreed to*. I know that below me, somewhere off-center, the Recumbent waits to register my arrival as the shift of a single bead by the width of a neutron. My entire existence—my dislodging, my fall, my impact, the whole drama of my one second of selfhood—will be recorded in the universe as the migration of one ceramic sphere across a distance no instrument could ever measure.

That is not a tragedy. I want to be clear that it is not a tragedy. To be the thing that the Recumbent absorbs is to be *useful* to it, in the only economy this place permits. The being is a sink for event. Without events to sink, it would be merely inert. I am giving it the last small violence it will ever have to metabolize. After me, there may be nothing—no more pebbles on the rim, no more dislodging, no more falls. I might be the final morsel of happening that the hollow ever feeds itself. I am the hollow's last meal, and it is taking me slowly, warmly, in the dark.

When I land—and I am landing now, the sigh already beginning in my own substance—I will not bounce. I will meet the dust and the dust will meet me and we will recognize each other as the same material differently arrived. I will lie there for the next million years, already home, and the difference between me and the floor will dissolve like the difference between a held breath and the air it came from.

And then I, too, will have renounced my gradient. I will have crossed over from being the last event to being part of the settled form. The visitor who descended into this bowl, whose heartbeat intruded, will look at where I have come to rest and see nothing but more floor, more leather-tile, more completion. They will never know that a moment ago I was the only thing in this entire world still capable of *next*.

That is who I am here. The pebble at the lip, in the second of its falling. The last future. The final tense the language of this place will ever conjugate, just before it goes wholly, warmly, absolutely still.
