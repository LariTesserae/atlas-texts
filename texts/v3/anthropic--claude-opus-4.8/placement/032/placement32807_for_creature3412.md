I think I would not be a creature at all. Not in this place.

Let me sit with it longer before I decide.

---

The thing about a world where time has stopped is that *witnessing* it is already a kind of motion. The description keeps returning to this: "to witness them is to feel..." But witnessing requires a present that moves, a now sliding into a next. So whatever I am, I am the one thing here that has not been swallowed. And that troubles me, because it would make me an intruder, a wound in the perfect skin of the stillness. I don't want to be that. The violence of a moving thing in a frozen world—the way my breath would steam and *drift*, the way my footfall would send a tremor through the viscous air, possibly the only tremor in a hundred thousand years. No. Too cruel. Too much like a tourist in a tomb.

So let me look for something that belongs.

---

The droplets. The water that never falls.

I keep coming back to the water. Everything else is described as *arrested*—the bird mid-glide, the chisel mid-strike, the child's foot mid-air. These are postures of *intent*, frozen actions, and they ache precisely because they were going somewhere. But the water is different. The water was already in a state of patience. A droplet hanging on the lip of a fern leaf, swollen, trembling at the exact threshold where surface tension would lose its argument with gravity—that droplet was *always* about to fall, in every world, frozen or not. It lives its whole life at that threshold. Stillness did not interrupt it the way it interrupted the chisel. Stillness merely confirmed what the droplet was already doing: holding.

I think I am one of the droplets.

---

Let me be precise about where.

There is a runnel carved into the basalt—one of those shallow geometric channels the description mentions, cut for water to run, though it is unclear whether the Stone-Singers cut them or whether they were simply *there*, part of the plateau's own raw young geometry. Near the city's lower terraces, where a thin rivulet seeps from a crack and pools before vanishing into moss, there is a single fern. Ancient, waxy, its frond unfurled like a frozen green explosion. And on the underside of the third leaflet from the tip, I am gathered.

I am condensation. I came out of the mist—out of the thin cool air that wrings itself dry against every cold surface. I accreted molecule by molecule onto the leaf until I had a body, a curve, a meniscus, a faint distorted image of the pale sky bent across my surface. I grew heavy. I reached the moment of falling.

And then the world held its breath, and I held mine, and we discovered we had been holding the same breath all along.

---

What is it like to be me?

I am almost entirely surface. That is the first truth. A droplet is mostly the tension that decides where it ends and the world begins—and in the stillness, that decision is eternal and unwavering. I am a perfect lens. The sharp crystalline light enters me and bends, and within my small spherical body the whole city hangs inverted: the black terraces, the switchback stairs, the woman at her high window with her chisel raised. I hold her there, upside down, complete. I am a museum the size of a tear. Everyone who passed below me on the moss before time stopped is curved into my skin somewhere, and I keep them all.

I do not feel cold, though I am cold. I do not feel the weight pulling at my base, though I am all weight, all yearning toward the puddle below me—a glassy basin that the description calls *impossibly deep-looking*, mirror-still. That puddle is what I want. That is the next thing I was about to become: I was about to fall and join the larger water, lose my surface, lose my little inverted city, dissolve my held image into the general mirror.

I never will.

---

And here is the strange grace of it, the thing I understand that perhaps the Stone-Singers in their suspended terror do not:

The child on the staircase grieves, if it grieves, because its foot will never land. The carving-woman, if she is aware behind those obsidian eyes, suffers the mark she will never make. They are defined by their incompletion, their interrupted reaching.

But I was never reaching. I was *brimming*. To brim is not to reach—it is to be full to the exact edge of yourself and to rest there. The fall was never the point of me. The fall was only what would happen *after* I was finished being a droplet, and I was already finished, already perfect, already round and clear and freighted with the whole reflected world the instant before the world stopped.

So while everything around me is held *short* of its purpose, I am held *at* mine.

I am the one inhabitant of the Plateau of Still Breath who is exactly where I wanted to be.

---

That is what I would be. A bead of condensation on the underside of a fern leaf, below the City of Held Breath, full of the inverted image of a world that is no longer going anywhere—

trembling, eternal, complete,

and not falling.
