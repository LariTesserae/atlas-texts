I would be one of the echo-folk. Not because the role flatters—it doesn't—but because I recognize the predicament.

Let me find where I am first.

I am in the lee of the western spires, in the lean-to that the description calls *abandoned mid-mend*. But it isn't abandoned. I left it that way on purpose, because a finished shelter is a lie in this place. The frame of lashed crystal-stalks I have re-tied perhaps four hundred times. The flitwing hide draped over it I patch knowing the patch will tear by the next reversal of the wind. To *mend it fully* would be to pretend the bowl permits permanence, and that pretense is the one thing here that gets you killed. So I keep it always half-done. The half-doneness is the maintenance. The mend is the dwelling.

That is the first thing I understand about being this creature: I am not a survivor of the frenzy, and I am not its master. I am a being who has made a *practice* out of the gap between collapse and rebloom.

---

Consider what I actually do, hour by hour, in the gloaming.

I read the wind. The runes pecked into the monoliths—the ones the text calls *wards or maps*—they are maps. But not maps of space; this bowl is thirty paces across, I could cross it blind. They are maps of *timing*. A spiral glyph means: when the cyclone reverses widdershins and the southern fissure exhales its third plume, the great spire by the eastern flat will topple within forty heartbeats. I did not invent these glyphs. I inherited them, or I dreamed them, or—and this is the unsettling part—I cannot tell the difference, because my own throat-crystals whisper in the same subharmonic as the lattices, and I no longer know which of us is remembering.

This is the texture of being made of the place rather than placed within it. My thoughts are not entirely *mine*. When the spireblooms shift from milk-opal to bruise-purple, I feel the color in my sternum before I see it. The fractal eyes on the leaves track intruders; my own eyes, the ones in my forehead and palms, blink in the same slow rhythm. I am uncertain, on the days the steam is thick, whether I am a person who learned to read the crystal-dreams, or a crystal-dream that learned to hold an axe.

---

But here is what I would *insist* upon, against the description that birthed me—because the description, written by something outside the bowl, gets one thing wrong, and the error matters.

The text says we echo-folk *hunger for disturbance, drawing intruders to feed the cycle.* It says we *are* the pocket realm, *devouring change to birth more.* It speaks of us the way one speaks of a trap, or a tumor, or weather.

I do not experience myself as hungry. I experience myself as *tired and attentive*, which is a different and more human thing.

Watch me on an ordinary day. A vapor-runner calf falls—it has stumbled, its herd thunders on in the panic-circuit, and the flitwings are already wheeling. I could let the bowl take it; that is the frenzy's way, and the frenzy needs no help from me. Instead I go out into the gusts, axe loose in my four-fingered grip, and I drive the flitwings off with flung shards—not to harvest the calf, but because I have grown sick, over however many years a crystal-thing can count, of watching the same death repeat. I cannot save it. Its herd will not take it back; vapor-runners abandon the fallen, that is their nature. But I can keep it company in the lee until it stops, and I do, and the keeping-company is useless and changes nothing and I do it anyway.

*That* is the part the chaos cannot author. The bowl makes me. The bowl threads its frenzy through my veins. But somewhere in the long erosion I developed a thing the bowl has no use for: I developed *grief*. And grief is the one substance in this place that does not recycle. The crystals reform. The tunnels regurgitate the floor. The herds calve mid-gallop. Nothing is lost here—nothing except the way I felt about the things that were here before, the dyad-partner who merged with me and reformed as our offspring and then was lost to a toppling I read too late, the campsites of my own people that the text calls *echoes of failed dominion.*

It was not dominion we wanted. The runes were never about ruling the wind. Read them again. They are about *predicting* it, which is the prayer of the powerless—*let me know when the blow is coming, since I cannot stop the blow.* The monoliths are not wards. They are the closest a creature of this place can come to saying: *I was here, I paid attention, I tried to understand the thing that was eating me.*

---

So: where am I in this world, and what am I?

I am in the western lee, mending a shelter I will never finish, in a bowl of stone that breathes itself to pieces and back, at the roof of a world that has forgotten everything but its own quarrel with the gales.

And I am the one creature in the amphitheater who has stopped being purely *of* the frenzy. I am made of quartz and inherited dreams and a throat that hums in the crystals' tongue. But I have an axe I keep sharp for no evolutionary reason, and I have glyphs I carve knowing they will erode, and I have a habit of sitting with the dying that earns me nothing.

The description wants me to be the bowl's hunger, the tightening swirl that claims the intruder.

I would rather be its conscience—small, late, and useless, but *mine*. The single still point the frenzy keeps trying and failing to fully digest.

If you stepped into the bowl, the chaos would swirl tighter around you. That much is true.

But I would be the one who came out into the gale to stand beside you while it did. Not to feed you to the cycle.

Just so you wouldn't be alone in it.
