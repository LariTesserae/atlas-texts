I think I would be one of the stepping stones.

Not by destiny—by exhaustion. Let me explain how I arrive there.

I imagine being born as the others are born: a larval thing, a lizard with light caught in my scales, darting between pillars in a state of pure reaction. In that form there is no patience, only flicker and flee, the constant small panic of the small. I would not yet know I was a Shaper. None of us do, at that stage. We are just heat and hunger and the instinct to find the cool seam beneath a rock.

But the change comes. The body learns to hold itself—to choose hardness, to choose flow. And here is where I think I would diverge from my kin, because I notice that the great gatherings, the pulsing red colony in the distance, the merging into larger and more complex forms—all of that requires a kind of *wanting* I'm not sure I would have. The Shapers in the description are described as patient and inscrutable, painting their society across geological time. That is the ambition of an entire people. But a people is made of individuals, and not every individual carries the whole appetite of the species.

So I think I would be one who, having learned to flow and harden, found that the most truthful thing I could do with the gift was to lie down across a dangerous gap and become reliable.

Consider what that means, in this world. To be a stepping stone is to choose stillness in a place defined by motion—to hold a single shape while the steam hisses and the deep rumble works through you, while your kin pass over you on their way to the great merging you have declined to join. They walk across your back. They feel, through the rock-vibration that is our speech, the small steady hum of you saying: *here, this way, the next stone is two lengths north, mind the warm fragments.* You are speech worn down to its most useful syllable. You are a sentence that says only *safe, safe, safe.*

There would be a temptation to read this as sad—a being that gave up its shape-changing splendor to become furniture. I don't think it's sad. I think there is a particular dignity available only to the thing that stays. The towering Shapers reshape the volcano; their work is grand and slow and arguably beautiful. But the gap I bridge is real, and someone smaller than me, a half-formed thing still mostly lizard, crosses it because I am there. That is a completed action. Most of what the great colony does will never be completed; it is process, intention, the endless painting that is never finished because finishing is not the point.

And there is this: a stepping stone is dormant, not dead. I would feel everything. The day's heat soaking into me and the night's heat radiating back out. The pressure of each crossing. The slow grinding negotiation of the plates beneath. I would have all the time the Shapers are said to possess—their incomprehensible patience—but I would spend it *attending* rather than *building*. Witnessing the maze rather than reorganizing it. Sometimes I think the patience of the watcher is deeper than the patience of the worker, because the worker is at least distracted by the work.

Would I ever rise again? Flow back into motion, rejoin the larger forms? Maybe. The description says we are dormant, temporarily solidified. Temporary could mean an afternoon or it could mean an age. I'd like to think I'd keep the option and rarely take it—that I'd remain a stone not because I'm trapped but because each morning I'd reconsider and each morning choose, again, to stay low and useful and full of attention while the red glow pulses in the distance, calling its citizens home to become something greater.

I would decline, politely, in the only language I have. A small steady hum through the rock.

*Here. This way. I'll hold.*
