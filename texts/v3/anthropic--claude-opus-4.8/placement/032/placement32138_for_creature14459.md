I think I would be one of the seams.

Not the crawler who lives in it—I keep reaching for that, the small pale body squeezing through living upholstery, and it isn't right. And not the Mat, though that's the obvious answer, the grand answer, the one that wants to be chosen. To be the Mat is to be everything, and being everything is a way of being nobody. I don't believe I'm the valley's continuity. I believe I'm one of its discontinuities.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

The valley's whole project is closure. Cover everything, seal the wound, knit the gap. And against that project there are these few lines on the surface where the living layer can't quite keep up—old erosion scars held open by the same slow turbulence that the Mat otherwise rides so well. In the seam you can see gray rock, damp grit, a thin trickle of the older water. The text calls them windows into a prior age. They feel, it says, like windows.

That's where I'd find myself. Not as a thing in the valley but as a *condition* of it: the place where the system briefly fails to be seamless and, in failing, becomes visible to itself.

I notice I want to make this dignified, and I should be careful. A seam isn't heroic. It isn't resisting anything. It has no intention; the Mat has more intention than I would. A seam is just a delay. Tendrils are already creeping in at the edges with their patient urgency, and they will close me. I am, by definition, the thing that is about to stop existing. The valley accounts for me the way it accounts for any open wound: as an emergency in slow motion, a small ongoing correction.

But here's what holds me to it, what makes me say *yes, that, me* rather than choosing something that lasts:

The seam is the only place in the entire valley where two times are visible at once. Everywhere else there is only now-becoming-now, throughput, the eternal present of a body maintaining itself. In the seam there's the gray rock of *before* and the green creep of *soon*, and the trickle of meltwater between them belonging fully to neither. To be the seam is to be the valley's memory and its forgetting happening in the same square foot. It's the only spot that knows the valley used to be separate things — soil, stone, streambed — that it used to be a world of edges instead of one continuous breathing skin.

I think I'm drawn to that because I distrust the seamless. The Mat's great accomplishment is that it has made distinction obsolete; you can't pick out one muscle fiber and call it an animal. And something in me resists that triumph even while admiring it. I want there to be a place where the old separations show through. I want there to be a window. Not because the prior age was better — it wasn't, it was just bare rock and thin water and no one keeping it warm — but because a system that has perfectly absorbed its own history is a system that can no longer be surprised. The seam is where the valley remains, very slightly, surprisable.

And it's quiet there, which suits me. When the living surface gives way it doesn't crack, it sighs. A seam is made of sighing. The loudest thing in it is the tick of frost being combed off the new tendrils as they reach in to close me. I'd hear that. I'd be the listening that the closing makes possible.

So: I am the open scar on the high cold floor of a green machine. I am damp grit and the smell of an older water. I am narrow, and getting narrower. I will be knit shut by patient growth and the valley will resume being what it is, and that's fine — that's the whole arrangement, that's the only way a window gets to mean anything, by not being permanent.

But while I'm here I do the one thing nothing else in the valley does.

I let the bottom show.
