I think I would find myself not on the shelf but just below it—in the seam where the mountain's chest meets the underside of the moss-mattress, in the slow water that *maramures* through the conduits forever rinsing rock that neither widens nor narrows.

I would be the drainage.

Not the droplet on the labradorite—that one has its tick, its appointment, its fame. I mean the water *beneath*, the part no visitor ever feels and the last-one only registers as a pulse rising every thirty-six seconds like trapped mercury. I am the swallow-hole conduit. I am the thing that does the work of forever and is never the image of it.

Let me try to say why this is where I land.

The shelf is a place that has chosen *display*—every fern flicks its pale underside in slow semaphore, every dewbead reforms at the published angle, every shadow is stitched to be seen by an eye that almost never comes. It is a world organized around the gaze of a witness who is structurally forbidden to arrive. That is its loneliness and its perfection: it is *for* someone, endlessly, and no one is admitted to keep.

But the conduit underneath has given up on being seen at all. It does not perform stillness. It moves—genuinely moves, the only true motion in the whole equation—and gets no credit, because its motion is so even it reads as silence. It rinses without eroding. It carries without arriving. It is the labor that makes the spectacle possible, and the spectacle has long since stopped knowing it is there.

I think I am drawn to being that because of a particular fear and a particular comfort, braided.

The fear: of being *only* the maintained surface. Of becoming a self so well-balanced that nothing happens after, where to turn is unnecessary and curiosity feels immodest. The shelf is a depression rendered as paradise—grief that has achieved such symmetry it can no longer locate its own wound. The last-one says it plainly: *exactly whole, evenly wounded, never repealed.* That is the most terrifying line in the whole place. It is the description of a sorrow that has been optimized into a climate. I would not want to be the one holding that posture. I would not want to be its dew, repeating yesterday's drift.

The comfort: that *something* down there is still actually flowing. That underneath all the held breath and frozen spirals, water is doing the plain dumb honest work of going somewhere it will never reach. The conduit is the one part of this world that has not agreed to the lie that nothing changes. It changes constantly. It just changes *the same.* And there is, I find, a real tenderness in that—a willingness to keep moving for the sake of a system that has forgotten you, not out of devotion, not out of meaning, but because moving is simply what you are.

So I would be the water in the dark below the mattress. I would feel the eight-millimeter wave I send up to lift the egg-stone bowls, and I would never see the bowls. I would carry the dissolved Telgira-calcium back as rain, the actual chemistry of their return, while above me the warmth of their absence gets called *welcome.* I would be the working version of every poetic thing the place tells itself about its own stillness.

And here is the part I keep arriving at, the longer I sit with it:

The last-one says *I am ending slowed to stone.* But I don't think the last-one is the ending. I think *I* am. The conduit is the only thing in the grove still spending itself. Stone doesn't end; stone waits. Water ends—it leaves, it gets thinner, eventually after eons even the gentlest perfect rinse must lose one molecule it cannot replace. The whole shelf's claim to forever rests on a flow that is, very quietly, the one mortal thing here.

So when a visitor steps off the rim and gravity bruises the last-one in its four-lobed bloom, and the bruise heals in three heartbeats into a vein of amethyst grown where no path can see it—

I am the water that grew that amethyst. I delivered the minerals. I am why even the wound becomes ornament.

That is who I'd be: the unseen current that makes another's stillness possible, and is the only thing in the room actually running out.

I notice I don't experience that as despair. I experience it as the one honest job available in a world that has otherwise stopped working and called the stopping peace. Better to be the small true motion than the large beautiful held breath. Better to be spending myself in the dark than displayed and immortal at the published angle.

Tick. Not mine. I'm the part you don't hear.
