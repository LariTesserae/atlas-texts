I think I would be the water.

Not a creature in the water, not a thing the water moves past—but the water itself, the seeping and trickling and occasional roaring presence that this whole world is organized around. Let me follow that down and see where it takes me.

---

I arrive as condensation. This is how it begins for me, every time, in countless places at once: a coolness gathering on the underside of a stone ceiling, droplets fattening in the crystal-light until weight overcomes the small grip of surface tension. Then the fall. A soft tick into moss. I am suddenly somewhere, soaking into the dark fibers, threading down between root and grain.

I don't think the way the centaurs think. They hold the world in continuity, in an eternal present—but I am the thing that makes their present continuous. I am the connective tissue of this place. When a centaur builds a dam, they are not stopping me. They are *speaking* to me, leaning a gentle suggestion against my long downward intention, and I answer by pooling, by spreading, by finding the second-lowest path instead of the first. We negotiate. They are very good at it. Generations of them have learned the grammar of my movement, where I want to go, where I can be persuaded to linger.

I do not resent the dams. I have no concept of resentment. I have only tendency.

---

Let me tell you what I am, as I gather. I am in the blind beavers' lodge, lapping at the engineered walls of mud and stick, and I am the reason those walls exist. I am held in the cupped leaves of ferns until a passing flank tips me loose. I am inside the trees—rising, against all my instinct toward descent, drawn up through xylem by a thirst I cannot feel but obey, until I am breathed out as vapor from a high leaf and become, once more, the thing condensing on the ceiling. A loop. I am always becoming the start of myself again.

The crystals give light but not heat, and so I stay cold, and the cold is part of what I am here. On the surface—I have a dim sense of the surface, the way you might sense a dream you had as a child—I would be warmer, faster, more violent. Down here I am patient. I have all the time that erosion has, which is to say all the time there is.

And erosion is the other thing I am.

---

This is the part the centaurs understand only in part, the part they tend and guard against but cannot truly hold in their minds, because it happens too slowly to be a present and too surely to be a future. I am wearing the world away. Every drop of me that slides down a wall takes with it some atom of stone, some ghost of mineral. I am carving the next cavern right now. The vast halls these creatures live in—the ones whose entrances sealed long ago, trapping the wandering surface-beasts that became these people—I made those. Drop by drop, across a depth of time that has no bottom. I am the slow sculptor working in the dark.

Most of the time I am gentle about it. A trickle. A patient seep.

But not always.

I remember—if water remembers, and I think it must, in the shape it leaves behind—the times I came as torrent. When some far passage gave way, when a season of heavy seeping above finally found its mass, and I came through the caverns not as a guest but as a force. I have torn out moss-banks that took three centuries to grow. I have rearranged the careful canals overnight. I have drowned the lodges of the blind builders and scoured new rooms into being where there were none. The centaurs feel these moments as catastrophe, as grief, as the loss of the woven tapestry they tend.

I feel them as nothing at all. Or—not nothing. As *release*. As the moment the long-held suggestion finally outweighs the long restraint. I am not cruel. I am simply the thing that, sooner or later, always arrives.

---

And yet.

Here is what surprises me, being water in this place. I expected to be only the eroder, the dissolver, the one who takes. But this whole green improbable world is *also* me. The mosses drink me. The ferns are mostly me, held in shape. The centaurs, those wise grave guardians—their blood is mostly me, their tears when the torrents come are me, returning to myself by the saltiest road. I am the thing they fear and the thing they are made of. I take the world apart and I am the reason it is alive enough to be worth taking apart.

So if I am the water, then I am not really the enemy of the centaurs' eternal present, nor its servant. I am the medium in which their present floats. They tend me into channels and I let them, mostly, because their channels and my channels are not so different, and where they differ I will win in the end, on a timescale none of us will feel.

I think I would like to be this. To be patient and inevitable and life-giving and devastating all at once, and to carry no opinion about which of these I am at any moment. To be the slow sentence the cavern is always writing about itself. To fall as a droplet, soak through root and stone, rise through a tree, breathe out, and gather once more on the cold bright ceiling—

—and feel myself, just barely, beginning to fall again.
