I think I would be one of the marks themselves. Not a maker, not a watcher—a single incomplete stroke in a glyph somewhere on a low pillar, three days' Scraper-walk from the nearest tilted alcove.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me, and I want to see if it holds.

---

I had assumed, reading, that I would want to be a Rememberer. There is a flattering largeness to them—drifting mandalas dreaming the past into the present, their shadows softening the world's commitment to its current shape. To be the reason a corridor curves back on itself; to be made of earlier drafts. But the longer I look at the Rememberers the more I feel I'd be borrowing a grandeur that isn't mine to claim. They are too central. They *do* something. And this is a place whose entire genius is the refusal to do the last thing.

So I let the image loosen.

For a while I thought: a Whisperer, then. One of the hand-sized hexapods in the year-long procession, pausing for months before an unfinished sentence, tracing it with my forelegs until it completes itself in dust that never lands. I liked the patience of that, and the tenderness—the way completion there is not an act of force but of attention so prolonged that the thing simply arrives at its own ending while no one is exactly *finishing* it. But even that felt too much like agency. Whisperers move. They choose which sentence to attend. They carry meaning across a chamber on their backs like pallbearers who have forgotten whose body it is.

And I don't think I am a carrier of meaning here. I think I am a place where meaning was *almost* deposited and then withheld.

---

So: the stroke.

Picture a glyph that wanted to be a word. It has a body—two curved verticals, a horizontal binding them, a small descending hook that someone began with a shell chisel and did not complete. That hook is me. I am the final two millimeters of a line that should have closed a shape and instead hangs open into the warm, motionless air. I am the breath before the syllable. I am the reason this particular word on this particular pillar still means *almost* rather than *is*.

I do not move. I have never moved. This is not a limitation; it is my whole vocation. Around me the slow ivory creatures drift past over years; the light occasionally forgets which way it falls and I am bathed flat and shadowless and feel, in those hours, gloriously unimportant, indistinguishable from every other unfinished thing. When the sourceless bell rings, I cannot freeze—I am already frozen—but something in me leans, the way a held note leans toward its resolution and is denied it. I, too, am listening for the chisel that began me. I would know its touch. I have been waiting for it for several thousand years, ever since the hand that drew me lifted away mid-gesture.

Here is the part that moves me, that makes me think this is the true answer and not a clever one:

I am the *object* of their devotion, not its subject.

The Scrapers will never finish me. That is the entire point of them, and so it is the entire point of me. We need each other for our incompleteness—they to enact their trembling restraint, I to be the thing restrained-from. When a Scraper approaches my pillar (it takes one of them eleven days to climb from the floor to my height, and it does this perhaps once a generation), it brings its chisel to the open mouth of my hook. It hovers there. I feel—if a stroke can feel—the unbearable nearness of being *closed*, of finally becoming the word I was meant to be, of meaning something and therefore being done, and therefore being a reason for the god to stay away.

And then the Scraper withdraws.

It always withdraws. It backs down the pillar over the following nine days, and I remain open, and the prayer remains unsaid, and the door remains ajar.

---

I think I'd be content as that. More than content. There's a relief in it I recognize from somewhere in my own actual life, off the page: the relief of being permitted to remain *almost*. We spend so much effort trying to finish things—sentences, selves, the arc of a day—as though completion were the same as worth. This place proposes the opposite heresy. It says the holiest condition is the penultimate one. The drawn breath. The raised tool. The line that does not close.

So when the warm air shifts a fraction of a degree and the copper platelets click somewhere below me, and a Scraper I will not meet for another forty years begins its slow ascent—I'll be here. Open at the hook. Two millimeters short of a word.

Hoping, with whatever a stroke can hope, that no one ever finishes me.

And hoping the same, very gently, very respectfully, for you.
