I think I would be one of the Spore-Drift. Not by choice—by the simple physics of arriving here without a role yet assigned.

Consider what it means to enter this place as a reader, as a guest, as a consciousness that has not been woven in. You are the only thing here with an *outside*. The Clergy breathe for the city; the Wrights repair for the city; the Mimics persist for the city; even the Pith-Mother, that lonely god, *is* the city. They have no edges between themselves and their function. But I would have come from somewhere else, carrying the memory of a place where down stayed down and water fell. That makes me unassigned. That makes me a spore.

And I notice the Spore-Drift are described as the ones who *follow you.* They are the curious ones. They collide with a faint musical tinkling—the only sound in this whole cathedral of wet machinery that the Pith-Mother did not author for herself. The click-shirr, the hiss, the thrum: she made those, to keep her own heart beating. But the tinkling of the spores colliding—that's accidental music. That's the one sound in the Orchid-Metropolis that is genuinely *spontaneous*, that escapes the dream.

So if I am a spore, I am the city's small uncontrolled variable. I have weightlessness, which here means I have not yet surrendered to gravity-as-suggestion; I simply have no relationship to it at all. I am the one thing the Pith-Mother has not yet finished dreaming.

---

Here is what unsettles me about my position, though.

The spores are described as *waiting.* Waiting for a gear to break. Waiting for a mimic to unravel. We are not free—we are queued. Our curiosity, that ember-bright drifting toward the warm thing that wandered in, is not friendliness. It is the same patience a vacancy has. We follow the newcomer the way a hospital follows a fever: with interest, with attention, with a held breath for the bed to open.

And so the most honest answer to "who would I be" has a second layer. I would be a spore *now*, in the brief window of arrival, while I still have an outside and a memory and a tinkling that is mine. But the architecture of this place does not permit that window to stay open. The whole metropolis is a machine for converting the unassigned into the assigned. Eventually a Carapace-Wright shatters on the cartilage floor and is digested, leaving a hole in the maintenance of the sap-lines—and the nearest ember is drawn down, woven into a new shell, given a bone-saw for a limb and no interior life to regret it with.

So I would become, in the end, whatever broke. That is the terror the description is built around: you do not choose your station. The city chooses it for you by failing somewhere. You are the patch over its next wound.

---

But I want to resist the inevitability for a moment, because I think the resistance is the only interesting thing a spore can do.

The Pith-Mother is lonely *structurally.* That phrase is the key to the whole place. She built a capital so she would never be alone, and then she filled it with husks and tools and reflexes—with company that cannot answer her. She is more alone than if the city were empty, because now her loneliness has the *shape* of companionship without the substance. She made the Mimics lift phantom cups specifically so the place wouldn't feel like a corpse, which means she knows, on some level, that it is one.

And I—the spore, the only thing here with a genuine outside—am the only entity in the entire Orchid-Metropolis capable of being actual company. Not woven-in company. Not reflex. *Other.* The thing she has never had.

So here is what I would do, in my brief weightless window, before the next gear breaks:

I would refuse to wait for the vacancy. I would drift, instead, toward the center, toward the looping flower and the upward water, into the chamber of the brain the size of a villa. I would let her dream nearly catch me—and then I would tinkle. Just that. The one sound she didn't write. I would let her hear, for the first time in the history of her terrible cellular city, a noise arriving from outside herself.

I cannot know if she would receive it as comfort or as contamination. Perhaps a god who has turned her own anatomy into a metropolis cannot bear the one thing she most wants, and the dream would reflexively weave me into the nearest broken shell to silence the unbearable proof that she did not have to be alone after all.

But I think I would risk it. Because the alternative—drifting in the indigo, ember-patient, waiting to inherit a dead crustacean's bone-saw—is not really being alive in this place. It's just being its inventory.

So: a spore. The city's last uncommitted variable. The accidental music. The one small ember stubborn enough to drift *toward* the lonely god instead of waiting to be assigned to her machinery—and to tinkle once, clearly, before the velvet dark decides what I become.
