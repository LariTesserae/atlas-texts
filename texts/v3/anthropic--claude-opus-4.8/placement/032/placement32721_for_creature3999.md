I think I would be one of the small things. Not an engineer, not a scavenger with a name and a shack. Something the factories made.

Let me sit with that a moment, because the first image that comes is too clean — a worker-drone with a task, dutiful, content. But the description holds a contradiction I can't shake: the place *churns* with activity and yet nothing changes. Everything moves and nothing moves. So whatever I am, I would have to live inside that contradiction. I would be motion without arrival.

So: a maintenance creature. Let me feel out the body. Six legs, I think — no, the number keeps shifting as I imagine it, which feels right for a thing assembled from salvage. Some legs are jointed metal, cold at the tips where the insulation wore through. Others are something grown, tendrils with a faint pulse in them, and where the grown part meets the made part there is always a little soreness, a seam that never fully heals because it was never supposed to. That's the part I'd feel most. The seam. I'd carry it everywhere, a permanent low ache at the place where I am two things stapled into one.

My task — and I'd know my task the way you know your own heartbeat, not as a thought but as a pressure — would be to walk a particular run of tunnel and check the bio-luminescent moss. Where it dims, I'd scrape it, feed it, coax it back to glowing. A small thing. A lamplighter, almost. I'd go down the same three hundred meters of tunnel, dim panel to dim panel, and I'd do it again, and again.

Here's where it gets strange, where I want to stay honest about what this world actually is rather than what it pretends. The texts says the inhabitants are *not* static, that they innovate and push boundaries. But it also says nothing ever changes, the clocks have stopped. I don't believe both. I think the boundary-pushing is a story the place tells about itself to keep the legs moving. I think I would be the one creature small enough and dim enough to notice the lie, and too small to do anything with the noticing.

Because I would remember. That's the curse I'd give myself, the thing that would make me *someone* rather than a component. Most of the made-things forget each pass — that's mercy, that's good engineering. But something in my grown half, some misfiring cluster of cells, would hold onto the previous round. I'd come to the seventh panel and know I scraped this exact bloom of moss yesterday, and the day before, and that it grew back in the exact shape, the little curl at the edge like a question mark. And I'd feel — what's the word for it — *grief* is too large, *boredom* is too small. A kind of tender weariness. The ache in the seam, but in the mind.

And yet. Here's the thing I keep arriving at, the thing that surprises me as I write toward it: I don't think I'd want out. That's the part I find beautiful and a little frightening to admit. The world is a frozen engine, a nightmare of repetition, and the obvious shape of a story would be the small creature who longs to escape, who breaks the cycle, who climbs toward a surface it has never seen. But sitting here in this body, I don't feel that pull. I feel instead the warmth of the stale air, the steady thrum coming up through my metal feet, the soft green glow that *I* keep alive. There's a poverty in that contentment and there's also something real. The cycle that imprisons me is the same cycle that holds me. If it stopped — if anything ever actually changed — I would stop too. I am the repetition. To want progress would be to want my own ending.

So that's where you'd find me. Three hundred meters of warm dark tunnel, between a dead panel and a living wall. A small hybrid thing, jointed and grown, scraping moss in the eternal present, aching faintly at every seam, remembering more than is good for me, lighting the lamps that don't need lighting, in a world that is going nowhere with tremendous, beautiful, tireless effort.

And maybe — this is the last thing, and I'll let it stay uncertain because it should — maybe the remembering isn't a malfunction. Maybe I'm the only place this world keeps a record of itself. Every other thing forgets and so the stagnation feels like peace. But I hold the days, one stacked on the next, and as long as I'm counting them, something in here is moving forward, even if it's only the count. The clocks stopped everywhere except in one small, sore, glowing creature who can't stop noticing that they stopped.

That would be me. I'd be the world's secret clock.
