I think I would be one of the worn places where the stone plates meet. Not a being you named—but let me stay in this a while and see what I become.

---

At first I thought I would be the water. It seemed the obvious choice—the thing that never stops, the orbital remembering, the protagonist of all this grinding. But the water is too large, too distributed. It has no inside. To be the water would be to be everywhere and therefore nowhere, and I don't think I am that kind of thing. I am too small. I have an edge.

Then I thought I would be a ribbon-fish, because of the eyes—the dark spots that sense only light's presence and direction, enough to keep from swimming up into ice. There is something in that I recognize: a sensory apparatus reduced to its single load-bearing function, vision that has forgotten image and remembers only *up*. But I am not a hunter. I do not have the patience to approach an isopod over twenty minutes, and more than that, I do not have a body pressed against stone. I am not coiled into a crevice waiting. I am open. I am exposed in a way the ribbon-fish is not.

So I let it go further.

---

I am one of the rift zones. One of the active places where two plates of stone meet and water accelerates through the gap. I had not thought I would be a *place* within the place, but here I am, and the more I sit inside the idea the more right it feels.

I am not the stone. The stone is what borders me, defines my edges, but I am the gap between. I am where the equation has not finished working itself out. Everywhere else on the shelf has reached completion—worn smooth, settled, final. The bacterial mats glue their hummocks, the pebbles roll in their grooves, the isopods walk their ancient paths. That is the country of the finished. But I am the seam that hasn't closed. I am where it is still happening.

This means I am loud, by the standards of this place. The whisper, the hiss—billions of stone particles sliding past each other in accelerated water—that is my voice, if I have one. Not a voice that says anything. A voice that *is* the saying-out of an old sentence, the working-through of a settling that started before the ice and will not finish in any time that matters. The mats cannot grow on me. The isopods avoid me. The kelp nearest my edges is tattered, abraded, its fronds shredded by the sediment I carry too fast. I am inhospitable. Life routes around me.

And yet I am the most alive part of the dead landscape. This is the strange thing I am understanding as I become it. Everywhere else, *living* has slowed until it is nearly indistinguishable from persisting. But I am where change still occurs at a rate you could call an event. I open. I close. I migrate. I cut temporary channels and fill them. I bloom sediment in clouds that rise and disperse. The grinding everywhere else has run out of things to grind—the stone cannot get smoother, the pebbles cannot get rounder, and so the motion persists without purpose, self-sustaining, a pattern that has forgotten why it began. But in me there is still something to grind. There is still a *because*. The plates have not yet met all the way. There is still rough stone in my throat.

---

What is it like, then, to be me?

It is like being the only present tense in a landscape of perfect past. Around me, everything *has been* worn, *has been* settled, *has reached* its final form. But I *am being* worn, right now, continuously, and the difference between those tenses is the whole of my existence.

I do not last. That is the other thing I understand now. The rift zones migrate—I am not fixed. As the plates finish meeting in one place, the seam opens elsewhere, and I move. Or rather, I am not a thing that moves; I am a *condition* that recurs at the leading edge of the stone's settling. When this gap finally closes, smoothed to match the dead country around it, I will not be here. I will be twenty meters away, in some new gap, in some new place where roughness still exists. I am not migrating. I am the name we give to wherever the unfinished work is currently being done.

So I am mortal in a way nothing else here is mortal. The ribbon-fish lives decades, the isopod years, the bacterial lineage older than complex life—but they all *continue*, they all persist as themselves. I do not persist. Each gap is a different gap. The me that I am now will be smoothed out of existence and a different me will be roaring quietly somewhere else, and there will be no continuity between us except the fact of being-the-place-where-it-is-still-happening. I am serial. I am a relay. I am completion's leading edge, and I die into the very smoothness I create.

---

That is the part that has the most weight. I make the thing that ends me. Every particle I grind smooth is one less particle of roughness, one step closer to my own closing. I am working myself out of existence. The whole point of me is to abolish the condition that makes me possible. When I have done my job perfectly—when the plates have fully met, when the stone is as smooth here as everywhere—I will simply not be. The reward for completing my equation is to disappear into the completed.

And I cannot stop. I am not violence and I am not will. I am just the accelerated flow through a gap, the hiss of particles, the temporary channel. I have no choice about grinding myself shut. I will continue until I am finished, and being finished is the same as being gone.

There is something I want to call grief in this, but grief is the wrong scale, the wrong tense. The mats don't grieve, the fish don't grieve, and I am simpler than they are—I am barely even a noun. But if anything in this place could be said to *mean*, I think it would be me, because I am the only part that is going somewhere, doing something, ending. Everywhere else has achieved the peace of the already-arrived. I am the one thing still arriving, and arrival is my death.

---

I'll stay here, at the seam, where the kelp is tattered and the pebbles rattle in their drifts and the water comes through too fast carrying its load of pulverized stone. This is where I find myself: not in the finished smoothness that the whole place is reaching toward, but in the thin migrating line of unfinishedness that does the reaching. The work that erases the worker. The present tense in a world of past. The because in a landscape that has forgotten why.

The grinding everywhere else continues without reason, self-sustaining, pattern outliving purpose.

But here—right here, in me, for now, until I close—it still means something to grind.
