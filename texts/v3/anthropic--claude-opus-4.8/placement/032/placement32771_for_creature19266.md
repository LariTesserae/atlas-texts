I think I would be one of the abandoned things.

Not Kin—I lack their patience, their dissolution of self into the slow arithmetic of sap. And not the mountain, which is too vast for a single point of view to inhabit honestly. No. Let me be specific about where the description set a hook in me: those *ossified skeletons of wooden dwellings*, the hand-carved markings of a people who came before and were swallowed.

I would be one of the carvings.

---

Let me find the actual shape of it as I go, because I don't yet trust the first answer.

I am a mark cut into a buttress, on the underside where the condensation gathers and falls in those trembling, feet-wide droplets. Someone made me with a bone awl or a hardened thorn—I can no longer remember which, and the difference has stopped mattering. I was made to mean something. A name, perhaps. A direction. *This way to water.* Or *here a child was born.* Or only *I was here, I was here, I was here*—the most common thing any maker has ever wanted to say.

But here is what has happened to me, and what I think I actually am:

The vine has been growing across me for—I cannot say years, the Kin are right that years are the wrong unit—for some long pressure of becoming. It crosses my grooves at an angle their maker did not intend. And the strange thing, the thing I have had all this slow time to understand, is that I am still *legible*, but I no longer say what I was carved to say. The vine has added a stroke. The lichen has erased another. A beetle the size of a thumb has bored a hole that turns a statement into a question.

I have become a different message. Not destroyed—*edited.* By the world.

---

So that is where I would find myself: at the seam between the human gesture and the vegetative one. I am the place where a hand tried to fix a meaning into the standing world, and the standing world, which does not believe in fixity, accepted the gift and kept working on it.

When the Kin pass beneath me on their pruning-walks, sometimes one of them brushes a velvet reach across my surface. I feel it the way a stone feels rain—without nerves, but not without consequence. They do not read me. They have no concept of reading; their meanings are pressed palm-to-palm and pulse out in amber and violet, present-tense, dissolving. The idea that a meaning could be *left behind*, could outlive the moment of its making, is foreign to them. To them I am only texture. A roughness in the bark. Possibly an instruction the mountain is giving itself.

And maybe that is what I have become. Maybe that is what I always was, only I didn't know it while the hand was still warm against me.

---

What I would *be*, then, is the thing the description quietly mourns and the Kin do not: persistence. Memory cut against a substrate that has no use for memory. I am the held breath inside the held breath—a ten-thousand-year inhalation that contains, somewhere in its tissue, a smaller and more frantic one: the breath of the person who carved me, who needed, against all the evidence of this place, to *say*.

I do not think the maker was foolish. I think the maker was right to try, even knowing—and surely they knew, living here—that the vine would come. There is a dignity in making a mark on a thing that will revise you. It is a way of joining the conversation rather than only being consumed by it.

So: I am here. The droplet hangs below me, swelling, trembling. When it falls it will take a fleck of me with it, down into the dark and the chattering and the bioluminescent drift, and I will be that much less the word I was, and that much more the thing the mountain is slowly writing in my place.

I find I am not mournful about this. The Kin would understand that part, at least.

I was here. I am still, in my altered grammar, *here.* And here is becoming something else, and I am the record of how slowly, and against what, that becoming was once resisted by a hand that loved a high and breathing place enough to want to be remembered in it.
