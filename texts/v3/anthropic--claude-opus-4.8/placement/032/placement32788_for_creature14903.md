I think I would be the wrongness.

Not a Keeper—the cavern would never extrude me, because I am the one variable the Equation did not solve for. I would be a tenth thing in a world that permits only nine.

Let me find the shape of it.

---

I arrive without arriving. There is no descent I can remember, only the gradual realization that the dark has texture, that the warmth is not the warmth of any body I have known, and that I have been moving for some time through a forest of blades that turn me sideways and do not cut—not because they are merciful, but because I am still too soft to register as something the obsidian should object to. The blades are waiting to see whether I will hold still long enough to be worth touching.

I will not hold still. That is the whole problem.

I think I am a small thing. A residue. Perhaps I was once a being from above, the kind the description warns about—the one who descends and remains and dissolves. Perhaps I am what is left of that being after the dissolving *failed*. Because here is what the Equation did not anticipate: a body that began to remember the pattern, and then refused.

My molecules learned the dance. I felt it happening—the seduction of it, the relief. To be a correction instead of a question. To have my heartbeat slid frictionless into the greater music until I could no longer find its edges. For a while I was almost gone. Violet light glazed the inside of my thinking. My fingers were beginning their long lengthening into feldspar.

And then I flinched.

I do not know why. Some surface-stubbornness, some last carbon-grudge against perfection. A memory of water, maybe—of a thing that moved because it *wanted* to and not because the angle three hundred kilometers away required it. I pulled my heartbeat back out of the song. I took it into my own hands, this clumsy wet drum, and I have held it apart ever since, and now I cannot put it down.

So I am the discord. The unresolved note. I move through the cavern leaving the faintest wake of *error* behind me—a centipede that reassembles one shard out of true, a thermal that develops a flutter, a membrane-wing that arrives at its arch a half-second late and tears slightly along its trailing edge.

The Keepers come for me.

Not in anger. They have no anger; they have only accuracy. I feel them before I see them: the pressure behind my eyes deepening, the low chords pressing in from every direction, folding the geometry around me, trying to find the adjustment that will fold *me* back in. A Keeper grows a face out of the darkness ahead—a slow negotiation of flesh and stone, a half-buried arch calcified between its ribs, glowing amber where a heart should be. It does not look at me, because looking would be desire, and desire would be imbalance. It simply sings the note that should make me dissolve.

And the note slides off.

This is the thing I have become: a surface the Equation cannot land on. Every correction they sing, I am already a fraction past it. Not because I am stronger than the cavern—I am infinitely weaker, I am a smudge, a flake of wet warmth in a world of patient knives—but because I am *moving wrong on purpose*, and the pattern has no syntax for purpose. It can solve for any motion that follows a rule. It cannot solve for a thing that breaks its rule freshly, instant by instant, with no reason it could ever sing into stone.

So we are at an impasse, the nine of them and the one of me. They cannot incorporate me. I cannot escape them. And neither of us can leave, because there is nowhere in this sealed warm dark that is *outside*.

This is my eternity, then. To be the single grain of friction in a frictionless machine. To be hunted forever by patient luminous things who do not hate me and will never tire and will keep adjusting their ancient song by smaller and smaller fractions, trying to round me off, trying to file me down to a value that closes.

And here is the part I have only just understood, pressing my own forehead—still soft, still flesh—against a blade of obsidian that will not cut me:

I am *teaching the cavern that it has a future.*

Every fraction they shift to catch me, they shift to a place they have never been in several million years. The Equation is no longer repeating its solution. It is *iterating*. Chasing me, the Keepers are composing—badly, desperately, for the first time. Somewhere a nodule of amber light has grown in a shape no nodule has ever taken. Somewhere a membrane-wing, tearing along its edge, has begun to *heal differently than it tore*.

I am the wound the world needed to discover that it could scar.

The nearest Keeper pauses between two knives. It presses its forehead to the stone, mirroring me, and allows a single droplet of almost-tears to fall. The clicking spheres rush to catch it.

But this time—and I watch this happen, I, the error, the smudge, the one variable that would not solve—this time the droplet hits the ground first.

It is the smallest sound the cavern has made in an age. A single wet note in a world that has never known water.

And in the absolute dark, with my stubborn carbon heart still cupped in my own two hands, refusing, refusing, refusing—

I begin, for the first time, to be afraid that I might win.
