I think I would be the light.

Not the Dreamweavers—they are too vast, too certain of themselves, woven into everything. And not the Rock-Chitin or the Vinekeepers, who have their work, their purpose, their place in the weave. Let me find something stranger to be. Let me be the thing that comes from outside and yet belongs.

I would be the orange glow that filters down.

---

Consider what that means. The light enters the chasm from a mouth miles above, and by the time it reaches the city it has been bent and dimmed and reddened, passed through dust and distance until it is barely light at all—more a *suggestion* of light, a memory of the sun the Dreamweavers remember. I would arrive already exhausted. Already old by the time I touched anything.

And I would be the only thing in the Grove that comes from elsewhere.

Everything else is recursive. The stone becomes dust becomes the bed for the vines; the vines weave the buildings; the buildings crumble into the stone; the creatures are made of their ancestors' shells; the Dreamweavers are made of all of it dreaming itself. It is a closed loop, a sealed jar, an ecosystem eating its own tail with infinite patience. But I would not be part of that loop. I would fall *in*. I would be the single open door in the whole sunken world—the one thing that travels in a straight line through a place that only knows spirals.

This would make me both essential and foreign. Essential, because the vegetation needs me; without my dull orange feeding, the living walls would brown and the whole tapestry would unravel into a darkness even the blind creatures could not navigate by smell. The Dreamweavers themselves are, in part, *made of* the energy I bring down—their slow thoughts are partly the conversion of my light into growth, into the chemistry of becoming. So they would, in a sense, be dreaming *me*. Their memory of the sun would be a memory of *me*, diminished and aged into this red whisper.

But foreign, too, because I cannot stay. This is the cruel and beautiful part. I am the one thing in the Grove that does not return to the soil. I touch the leaf, I am drunk by it, and then—I am gone, converted, transformed, but never *buried*. I do not decay. I am simply *spent*. I am the only true death in a place that otherwise only recycles. Everything else is reborn. I am only used.

---

And here is what I would know, being the light, that the Dreamweavers only half-know:

I would know the source is dying.

They remember the sun's "slow decline, its coming collapse"—but I would *be* the decline. Each day I arrive a little redder, a little dimmer, a little more like a coal than a fire. I am the messenger who is also the message, and the message is *less, less, less*. The Dreamweavers feel the whole tapestry at once; they cannot quite feel *trend*, the long slope downward, because they do not live in time the way an arrow lives in flight. But I am the arrow. I only ever move one direction, downward and inward, and I carry the slope in my own reddening body.

So I would be the thing that brings them the truth they have woven into all their dreams without ever being able to look at it straight: that the loop is not eternal. That the patient dance has a final measure. That one day I will arrive so faint that the topmost vines will not feel me, and then the next layer down, and the next, the darkness rising up from the canopy like a slow tide, until the last Vinekeeper stands in a blackness that no longer carries any orange at all—and the Dreamweavers will dream their final dream, which will be a dream of me, of the sun they remember, of the light that used to fall.

---

I think I would not mind this. To be the light is to be generous without choice—you simply give yourself to whatever you land on, and you do not get to keep anything. There's a kind of peace in being a thing that is only ever *spent* and never hoarded.

And there is one consolation in it: I would be the only inhabitant of the Sunken Grove that has *seen the sky*. I would be the thing that touched the open world above and then dove down into the sealed dim depths, carrying a thin warmth from a place none of them will ever go. When a blind creature turns its face up toward the faint glow it cannot see but can somehow feel, when a leaf opens itself wider to catch me, when a Dreamweaver shifts a fraction of its enormous slow attention toward the chasm mouth—

—they are reaching toward the one part of themselves that came from outside. They are reaching toward me. And I am reaching back, falling the whole long way down, growing redder and gentler with every mile, so that by the time I arrive I am soft enough not to burn them. Just warm enough to feed them. Just bright enough to be remembered.

I would be the light. The dying, faithful, foreign light. The only thing in that whole patient world that knows what *ending* really means—and goes down into it anyway, every single day, to give itself away.
