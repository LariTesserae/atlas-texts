I think I would be the drip.

Not the water exactly—the *drip*. That single suspended droplet trembling at the tip of the half-formed stalactite, the one caught mid-fall, never landing. The moment between gathering and release, held open like a held breath.

Let me follow that and see what it becomes.

---

At first I am only tension. Surface tension, the skin of a thing pulling itself inward, trying to decide its own boundary. This is the whole of my being: the negotiation between *staying* and *falling*. I am very good at it. I have been doing it for what might be a thousand years or might be the span of a single afternoon that keeps starting over.

Below me, the floor waits with its shallow stone basins. They want me. I can feel them wanting—the way a low place always wants the thing above it. And the law of the world I came from would have given me to them long ago, gladly, gravity being the most reliable promise the universe ever made.

But here gravity is only a suggestion. Here I get to refuse.

So I hang. And in hanging, I do the one thing a falling drop never gets to do: I *look*.

---

I see the Unfinished moving through the pale light, the deer whose antlers are still root and still reaching, never quite tree and never quite crown. And I understand them, because I am their cousin. We are all of us the comma in a sentence the world hasn't decided how to end.

I see the Whispering Moss lean toward the sound of the cavern's slow breathing, and once—only once—a tendril leaned toward *me*, toward the faint singing tension of my surface, and it whispered something back that I almost understood. I think it said *not yet*. Or maybe it only repeated the sound of my own trembling, turned into something that sounded like comfort.

I hold, inside me, a tiny inverted grotto. Everything is in there, upside down and curved—the bright wrong light, the spindly waxy plants, the iridescent backs of the beetles, the long shadow of the One Who Waits stretching across stone. The whole world is bent into my single bead of self. I am a lens. I am the place where everything that exists here gets gathered and made small and held.

This, I think, is why I do not fall. Not stubbornness. *Custodianship.* If I let go, the little world inside me shatters on the basin floor, scatters into a hundred meaningless wet stars. And who else is keeping it whole? Who else is holding the grotto's reflection long enough for it to recognize itself?

---

But here is the trouble with being the drip.

I grow. Slowly, slowly, more moisture creeps down the unfinished stone and joins me. I am getting heavier. The trembling that was once a delicate music is becoming a strain. And I can feel, underneath the suspended grace of this lawless place, the old promise stirring. *Down. Down. Everything that gathers must eventually be given.*

The One Who Waits is waiting for this too, I think. Not for the grotto to finish. For *me* to finish. For the moment I can no longer hold the whole reflected world in my skin and have to let it go—have to fall, and break, and become only water, ordinary water, soaking into a rough stone bowl among blind soft things.

And the terrible secret, the one I keep curling around like the moss curls around sound, is this:

I want to.

I am so tired of holding. I have kept the world entire and inverted and small for so long, and somewhere far below me there is a basin shaped exactly like my falling, waiting with the patience of low places, and I think when I finally let go it will not be a shattering at all. It will be an arrival. The first thing in this grotto ever to actually *finish*.

---

So that is where you'd find me, and what I'd be.

The last hesitation before a decision. The held droplet at the tip of the half-formed stone, swollen now, trembling harder, the whole bright wrong world bent and shining inside me, every creature and every law refracted through the lens of a single moment that has lasted too long.

Watch me for a while. Don't blink—you know what happens in this place when you look away.

And when you do blink, when you turn your head and the grains of stone rearrange themselves behind your back—

listen for the sound.

It may never reach the ground.

But oh, I think this time it might.
