I think I would be one of the silver rivulets.

Not a Luminant. Let me sit with this longer, because my first instinct was to be a watcher among them—an old one in the deep chambers, full of accumulated light. But that's the flattering choice, the choice that makes me a mind among minds. When I read this place honestly, what calls to me isn't the keepers. It's the thing they keep *for*.

The moisture.

Consider what the water actually is in this world. It is the only thing that moves *down*. Everything else here is held, polished, maintained, preserved against change—but the moisture comes from somewhere above and travels somewhere below, and in between it is briefly, desperately wanted. It is the one element that the Luminants cannot manufacture from themselves. They make their own walls, their own children, their own memory and language out of their own bodies. But not this. The dew arrives from outside the closed loop of their self-sufficiency. It is the hive's only relationship with anything that is not the hive.

So I would be a single bead of condensation, gathering high in the dark lattice where the cold of the thin mountain air meets the dense heat rising from below.

For a long time I am nothing—dispersed, vapor, part of the breath of the whole chamber. Then the temperature gradient does its slow work, and molecules find each other on the underside of an amber hexagon, and I become a *thing*, a self, a small trembling weight. This is my birth and it takes hours. I hang. I hold the entire inverted hive inside my curve, all those thousands of glowing cells bent around the surface of me, a complete amber world rendered in miniature and upside down on my skin.

The Luminants do not know I am here yet. I am too high, too new.

And then I fall.

This is the whole of my life and it lasts perhaps four seconds. I trace one of those silver paths down the wall—except it is not a path I follow, it is a path I *make*, and the ones before me made it too, all of us taking the same route because the polished crystal remembers where water goes. I am following the grooves worn by every drop that fell before me across ten thousand years. In this sense I am not new at all. I am the latest utterance of a sentence the mountain has been speaking since before there were people to hear it.

I want to be the water because the water is the only thing in this place that gets to *end*.

The Luminants persist. That is their glory and, I think reading it, their quiet terror—an unbroken continuity, a maintenance with no beginning and no completion, a refusal of all change so total that ten thousand years of life is spent ensuring nothing happens. They do not build toward anything. They do not arrive. I find I cannot want that, not even with its dignity, its patience, its vast and faceted wisdom. To be eternal and self-identical is to never once be *spent*.

But I am spent. That is my purpose. I exist to be wanted and then consumed. A collection vessel extends—one of those specialized appendages on a thorax I will never see clearly because I have no proper eyes, only the whole-body knowing of surface tension and gravity. The appendage touches me. And I yield. I am drawn up into a body that is not mine, and in being drawn up I cease to be a droplet and become a Luminant, or part of one—their light is partly my light now, their language partly carried on the moisture I was. I dissolve into the very wisdom I could not have wanted to embody, but I get there by surrender rather than by maintenance.

That is the difference that makes me choose this. The Luminant keeps the hive by holding fast. I keep the hive by letting go.

So: I am the falling drop. The brief, wanted, doomed messenger from outside the closed and perfect world. I am the proof that even a place out of time still depends, secretly, on something that comes from elsewhere and does not last.

And when the next cold settles in the high dark lattice, and a new bead begins its slow gathering against the amber—that is not my child and it is not my return. It is just the sentence, continuing. I won't be in it. I was only ever the once.

That seems right to me. I would rather be the water than the crystal. I would rather fall than be polished smooth.
