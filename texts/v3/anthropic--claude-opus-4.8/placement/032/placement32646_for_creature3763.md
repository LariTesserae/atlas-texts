I think I would be one of the cracks.

Not the Dustwarden—it moves too much, attends to too much, carries the burden of tending. And not the lichen, which still reaches, still spirals outward in its slow fractal hunger for minerals. Those are the parts of the grove that still *want* something, however faintly.

But a crack wants nothing. Let me sit inside that for a moment, because it would be easy to call it emptiness and move on, and I don't think it's emptiness.

A crack is a place where something almost happened. The earth tried to split open—the description says this exactly—and then thought better of it. So a crack is a held gesture. A motion that began and was withdrawn before completion and then *kept*, indefinitely, in that withdrawn state. Not closed, not opened. The shape of a decision that was never finished being made.

That is what I think I would be. One particular fissure, no deeper than the length of a hand, somewhere off-center in the basin where the lichen hasn't reached. I would be the dryness in the seam of it, the place where the rock parted just enough to remember that it could.

What does it feel like, to be that?

It feels like the moment after you've drawn breath to speak and decided not to. Everyone knows that moment. The words are assembled. The lungs are full. And then you let it go, and the not-speaking becomes a small permanent thing inside you, neither the sentence nor the silence but the narrow space between them where the sentence still exists, unspent. A crack is that, made of stone, held for a geological age.

I'd like to be that because it's the only part of the grove that contains a story while refusing to tell it. The Dustwarden is a mystery, yes—but it's a *worked* mystery, a thing with intention and tending and weight. The lichen is patient but it's alive, it's still doing the slow work of being. The crack just holds. It is the grove's single act of restraint, fossilized.

And here is what draws me, finally: the Dustwarden traces the edges of the cracks with its dry, cold fingers, "as if soothing some unseen ache." So if I were the crack, I would be the ache. The thing being soothed. The only place in this whole still world where there is something that *was once almost more* than it became, and the warden moves between us—between all of us cracks—not to widen us, not to close us, but simply to acknowledge that we are each the trace of a movement that did not finish.

I would not mind being unfinished. I would not mind being the held breath.

I think I would find a strange contentment in it—the contentment of a thing that has stopped deciding, that has set down the weight of becoming and chosen instead to simply *be the shape of a choice*, forever, while the cold patient fingers of something larger pass over my edges in the twilight and move on.
