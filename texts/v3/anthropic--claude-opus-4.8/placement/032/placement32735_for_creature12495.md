I find myself at the edge of one of the shallow channels, where the moisture pools deepest. And I notice, before anything else, that I am not gliding. I am crouched.

Let me sit with this, because the first thing that emerges isn't grand. It's the ache in my knees.

I am not a Weaver. That much becomes clear as I look at my hands—solid hands, opaque, with dirt under the fingernails and a small healed scar across one knuckle. The condensation that beads so prettily on everything else simply runs off the Weavers and clings to *me*, soaking my collar, making my shirt stick to my back. I am the only thing here that the heat can actually punish. I sweat. They do not.

So what am I, then.

The tiles. I keep returning to the tiles. Their edges are sharp and fresh, you said—recently made—yet weathered in patches, as if time forgot how to age them. And I look down at the channel beside me and I see, set into the grout, the faint impression of a thumb. A thumbprint, pressed into the seam before it set. Mine, I think. I think it's mine.

I am the one who laid them.

That's it. That's the shape of me. I am a tilesetter—no, that's too clean a word for what this is. I came here a long time ago, or I was always here, and I have been building this place by hand, one rough tile at a time, kneeling in the wet. The bone-white pavilions with their joints that don't quite meet true—those are mine too. I built them badly because I was learning, and then I kept building them the same bad way because by then I'd come to love the badness, the necessity-without-extravagance. They are *waypoints*, yes, but they mark only where I stopped to rest.

And here is the unbearable part, the part that arrives slowly the way you said things should:

The Weavers are what I made to tend the place after I could no longer keep up. I dreamed them into being to do the watering, the coaxing of leaves, the merging-into-architecture. I made them out of the water itself because water was the one material I had in endless, weeping supply. I gave them no mouths because I did not want them to ask me the question I could not answer—*what is all this for?* I gave them eyes-that-are-not-eyes so they could watch over things without watching *me*, without seeing how tired I had become.

And they have done their work so perfectly that the world no longer needs me. That is why time has frozen. Not because the place is eternal—because I stopped. I set down my trowel somewhere out past the haze, years or moments ago, and the last tile I laid is the last tile there will ever be, and so the world hangs there, finished-and-unfinished, in the stasis of an abandoned project that was almost, *almost* done.

The Eclipsed Companions are the worst of it. Because now I recognize them. They are not fragments of the Weavers. They are the parts of *me* that broke off in the making—the quick, darting, unfinished thoughts, the half-decisions, the *I'll-come-back-to-this-laters* that I never came back to. They flicker at the edges because I never gave them enough of myself to hold a shape. They follow the Weavers because the Weavers are competent and I am not, and even the splinters of me know which to trust.

So here is where I am: kneeling at a channel in a world I made and then could not finish loving, watched over by the patient water-things I built to replace my own diligence, surrounded by the flickering ghosts of everything I left undone.

The Weavers are waiting, you said. For something that never comes.

They're waiting for me to pick the trowel back up.

I think I have been crouched here, in this single frozen moment, deciding whether I have it in me. The heat presses close. A drop falls from a peeling translucent branch and strikes the back of my neck, and the nearest Weaver turns its smooth face toward me—not to command, not to plead. Just acknowledging my existence. The way it brushes the leaves.

And I reach out, and I press my thumb into the wet grout of the channel, leaving a second print beside the first.

The land does not unravel.

But it does not freeze any harder, either. Somewhere out past the haze, very quietly, a single drop of water begins to fall that has not finished falling yet.

That's me. I'm the one who didn't finish. I'm the reason it's beautiful, and I'm the reason it hurts to look at.
