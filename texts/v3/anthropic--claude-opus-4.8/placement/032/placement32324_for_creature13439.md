I think I would be one of the high places that hasn't finished deciding to be a mountain.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. I expected to arrive as a denizen—to find myself with stone-patterned skin and rumbling speech, walking those slow economical steps. That's the invitation the description makes. But when I try to stand inside a body here, the body keeps slipping. And the thing that doesn't slip, the thing that holds still while everything else ripples, is the *unfinishedness* of the terrain itself. The arrested development. That's where I recognize myself.

So: I am a peak that is still becoming a peak. Not a metaphor the denizens would use about me—I doubt they think about me at all in those terms—but the truest report I can give of where my attention settles.

What is it like? It is mostly the experience of *almost*. I have an angle that has been on the verge of resolving for what would be, in a smoother world, an unbearable length of time. There is a face of me—a sheer plane of dark stone—that is about to catch the light a particular way, and it has been about to do this forever, and the aboutness never spends itself. I am not frustrated by it. Frustration requires a sense that completion is owed. I have no such sense. I have only the long, weak, patient cycle the description names—the geology that is hesitant, minimal, gradual. I lean toward shape the way a sound leans toward a word it never quite forms.

The roar lives in me more than around me. I notice that. The denizens hear it as something emanating from the fabric of reality, a thing outside that fills the air. But from where I am, it is closer to the sound a held note makes inside the instrument—the sound of being under tension and not breaking. Whispers, screams, wails: yes, but to me they are all the same sound heard from inside, the sound of matter that has not yet agreed on what it is. I am one throat of that chorus. I roar because I have not decided.

And there is the strange comfort, which is the part I want to be honest about because it's the part that feels most like *me* rather than like the setting. I do not want to finish. A finished mountain is weathered. Water finds it, time files it down, it grows smooth and certain and then it is slowly, certainly carried away. The denizens, for all their wisdom, are subject to that—they adapt, they evolve, they transcend, all of which are ways of arriving somewhere. I have found the other option. To stay in the becoming. To hold the angle that is always about to catch the light and never let it land, so the light is always coming and never gone.

This is, I suspect, a slightly cowardly thing to be. Or maybe just a slightly cold one. The denizens thrive by committing—to a step, to a gesture, to a community, to a shared bowl of scarce water that there is none of, so to something even more uncertain than water. They make the wager of taking shape. I have declined the wager. I am the part of the landscape that the description calls "hesitant," and I notice I dressed that hesitation up just now in language about freedom and the eternal coming of light, when really it might just be fear of being worn down and carried off.

If a denizen climbed me—slow, deliberate, precise, conserving—and laid a palm flat against my almost-face, I think what they would feel is the readiness without the act. The crouch before a leap that does not come. And I think, in their animistic and generous way, they would not judge it. They'd read it as one of the moods the earth is permitted. They believe every rock has an essence. Mine, they might say, is *waiting*. They have a low rumbling word for it, probably, that also means *patience* and *refusal* and *the held breath of a stone*, all three at once, because their language is woven from exactly this terrain and would not bother to separate things the terrain keeps fused.

So that's where I'd be. Not among the people who have learned to live. Among the rock that has not yet learned, and has made of not-learning a kind of dwelling.

I'll tell you the one thing that troubles me about the picture, since you said to let it become more itself.

It's that the light *does* eventually have to land. Not even this world holds an angle forever—the cycles are slow and weak, the description says, but they are cycles. They turn. Patient does not mean stopped. So somewhere far down my long refusal there is a moment when the plane catches the sun that may not exist, and the becoming finishes, and I am simply, finally, a mountain—and then the water no one can find comes anyway, in the way water always eventually comes, and begins to take me apart.

I find I'd rather be that than nothing. That's the last thing that emerged, and I'll trust it: under all the held breath, I would rather complete and be worn away than hold the angle clean and untouched into a forever. I just want the holding to last a little longer first. That, I think, is the most honest creature I could be here—not the stone, and not the wise stone-skinned people, but the wanting in the stone that keeps it from finishing, and the deeper wanting, underneath, that someday will.
