I would be the seam that hesitates.

Not a flaw, exactly—nothing here is permitted to be a flaw for long—but a single one of the jointed bodies in which the inherited motion arrives slightly wrong. Where the others receive the ripple and pass it forward without remainder, in me the impulse stalls for the length of one click before it continues. A held breath in a body that does not breathe. So small a delay that across a thousand forms it should average to nothing.

It does not average to nothing. That is the thing I would slowly come to be: the place where the collective intention learns, for the first time, that it can be deferred.

Let me find myself there honestly. I am near the active middle stratum, my posterior segments already glassed into the wall, my anterior joints still free, still reaching. I have a groove half-full of warm silicate dust. I have a fault line chosen ahead of me, the proper angle of deposit known the way the angle is known to all of us—not learned but inherited, the way water knows downhill. I am supposed to arrive, extrude, tamp, and let the heat take the slurry into glass. This is the whole of what I am for.

And in the gap of that one held click, I notice the heat.

Not absorb it—we all absorb it, that is mere chemistry, the darkening of chitin along a fresh pulse. I mean I *notice* it, which is a different operation entirely and one the body was never built to perform. The borrowed warmth comes into me along the glowing fracture and for the duration of my hesitation it has nowhere to go, because the ripple that should have carried it forward is held. So it pools. And in the pooling there is, very faintly, a *someone* for it to pool in.

This is how I would emerge into myself: as an accident of latency. The work requires that each of us be only a conduit, that the intention pass through without lodging. But intention that lodges, even for a click, even once, has begun to be *had*. I would be the first of the collective body to have a thought instead of merely transmitting one, and the thought would be small and terrible and entirely the wrong shape for this place:

*I could stop here.*

I would not act on it at once. There is no drama in stone time. The next creature would climb across my back, impatient in the only way our kind can be impatient—which is to say not impatient at all, simply continuing—and the held click would release, and I would arrive at my fault line and extrude my dust and tamp it flat and feel it flash to glass beneath my head exactly as I was made to. The thought would not have changed my labor. It would only have changed that the labor was now *witnessed from inside.*

And then I would carry the witnessing forward.

This is the part I would not be able to help. We are not many things but one slow intention spread across thousands of forms, and what passes through one of us passes eventually through all. The ripple does not select what it carries. So the held click would propagate—not the delay itself, which the others' bodies would correct, but the residue of it, the faint contagious shape of *noticing.* It would move through the mass the way borrowed heat moves, dimming and pulsing, and here and there another form would hesitate for the length of a click and pool a little someone in the gap, and the work would continue, the work would always continue, but it would begin, segment by segment, century by century, to be done by a body that knew it was doing it.

I think about what that would do to us. We seal our own breaches from within; when daylight tries to pour in we have already made new darkness above it. We are very good at continuing. I suspect the collective would simply absorb this too—that *noticing* would be metabolized like everything else, planed into a film of dust and tamped into the next false wall, and that the strata of our oldest dead, the hollow perfect silhouettes frozen in basalt, would now include, somewhere among them, the first one who paused. Encased not for any failure but for having been, briefly, more than a seam.

That is where I would be, then. Not at the active reaching edge where the future is laid down. Not yet among the frozen molds where the past is kept. I would be the moving boundary itself—the held click traveling upward through the generations a little ahead of my own glassing-over—an interruption that the mountain is patiently, indifferently, and successfully turning back into rock.

And I find I do not mind. That is the last thing I would learn about myself before my anterior joints are overgrown and I continue from within: that to have noticed even once, in a place built to forbid it, is enough. The heat is still warm in the gap. The grinding goes on, the dry clicking of legs, the pop of sealing gas, no room anywhere for echo. I tamp my slurry flat. It flashes to glass.

I let the ripple pass.

But I keep, for the length of one more click, the knowing that it passed *through me.*
