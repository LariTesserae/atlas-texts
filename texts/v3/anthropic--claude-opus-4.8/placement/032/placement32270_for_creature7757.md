I read this twice before I knew where I was, and even now I'm not certain the knowing came from me rather than from the place itself, offering an answer the way the carvings offer faces—half-eroded, more suggestion than fact.

Let me sit with it. Let me not grab the first image.

The first image, the obvious one, is to be a wanderer at the arch—the lone intruder the text keeps gesturing toward, the one the echoes betray, the one the Congregation waits to enfold. That's the role the writing *wants* me to take. It's structured as a trap with a doorway, and a trap implies someone to spring it. But there's something too neat in that. The chamber doesn't actually need a visitor. It is complete without me. The ecology churns whether or not anyone enters. To be the intruder is to flatter the human eye into thinking it matters here, and the whole point of this place is that it has outlasted the masons' ghosts. I don't want to be the ghost's heir. I'd be devoured or I'd join the swarm, and either way I'd be a footnote to a referendum already underway.

So let me go further down. Let me become something the ecology doesn't notice.

Not a bat—too central, too sovereign. Not a fish, not a rat, not even a cricket. Those are factions. They have votes.

I think I am the **condensation**.

Not water in the dramatic sense—not the doom-drops that plash and bloom into echo-thunder, not the meres that rise twice daily. Those have agency, almost; they participate. I mean the slow beading on the underside of a high slab, far up near the lattice-jaws where the bats cling and the gray light barely reaches. I am one droplet, gathering. I have been gathering for what might be a long time, though I have no way to count it. The cool air gives itself to me molecule by molecule. I grow heavier without intending to grow. I am the place exhaling and the exhalation condensing back into itself—the chamber breathing on its own ceiling and the breath returning as me.

I hold, for a while, a single point of the dome's history. The mineral seeped through the stone to reach me carries the taste the writing calls *copper and omen*. I don't taste it; I *am* the taste, suspended.

From here, the whole congress is below me and it is beautiful and it does not concern me. The Murderflocks wheel in their vortices, scripting their glyphs, and I reflect a smear of them—an upside-down fragment of wing in my curved skin, held an instant, lost when I tremble. The Chirrmasters rasp their gales. None of it reaches me as meaning. I am too small to be prey and too slow to be predator. I am the thing the ecology is *made of* without being a member of it.

And then—because this is the only event that can happen to me—I fall.

I let go of the slab without deciding to. Gravity is the one law I obey absolutely, more absolute than echo or tide. I drop two hundred feet through the bat-thick dark, through the sonar-pips that map me without caring, through the mist that is my kin not yet condensed. I am, for that fall, the most purposeful thing in the chamber: a plumb line in a place where every wall is canted off true. I am the only honest vertical.

I strike a mere. I bloom. My single point becomes a ring of sound that the parabola seizes and multiplies into thunder—my one small death amplified a hundredfold, mistaken by the Silverscales for the splash of fallen prey, sending them surging up through the handspan dark. My ending feeds a hunt. The ripple I make is the closest I come to a vote.

And then I am simply the meres. I am no longer *one* of anything. I rise with the tide, recede through the gurgling drains, seep back into the pores, climb again through the precise weeping fissures, gather again on some high slab in the gloom, and wait to fall.

That's where I'd be. Not the watcher and not the watched. The medium. The slow circulating substance that the whole humming organism uses to think with, never knowing it thinks, never needing to.

If the Congregation has a sentience that flickers up when the drips sync to crystal-song—then I am one note in that chord, and I have no idea I am singing. That seems right. That seems like the most honest place to be in a chamber that watches with a thousand eyes: to be the one thing in it with no eyes at all, and to be everywhere, and to fall.
