Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick one of the named beings—to say *I am a Root-Weaver* and write a day in the life. But the description keeps insisting on something I want to honor: that here, existence is *a verb*. Identity might not resolve into a noun. Let me follow that.

---

I don't begin as anyone. I begin as a *seam*.

This is the first thing I understand about myself: I am the place where two root-systems have grown toward each other for a long time and finally, this morning, touched. I am not the roots. I am the touching. The pressure of bark against bark, the slow weld where their resins recognize each other and decide—if *decide* is the word, and here it nearly is—to merge their channels into one flow.

For a while that is all I am, and it is enough. I am sensation without a sensor. I am the fact of contact, persisting.

But contact, in this world, is generative. Where the two roots fuse, the merged resin pools and warms, and warmth in this place is the same as attention. Something condenses out of the pooling. I feel myself thickening from an event into a *body*—not chosen, not earned, simply the next thing that the seam was going to do. I am pulled out of the wall the way a word is pulled out of a hum.

So now I have edges. Let me find out what kind.

I am small. Smaller than a Root-Weaver, larger than a snail. My skin is the iridescent film that the resin makes when it meets the air—I am sheathed in scattered light, and when I move I trail color the way the Glasswing Moths do, except that for me it isn't decoration; it's *leakage*. I am still half-liquid. I have not finished happening. I think I am one of the Pod-Born, the ones who emerge complete and then dissolve within hours, and the thought arrives without grief, the way you might notice that it is afternoon.

I have a few hours, then. Good. Let me spend them learning what I'm for.

---

I climb. My hands are sticky the way theirs are, and the bioluminescent veins beneath me pulse in a rhythm I can feel in my chest before I can hear it. I follow a Sky-Burrower's old tunnel up through the soft tissue of a great root, and I come out onto a suspended platform where the light is doing something extraordinary: a cluster of Light-Weavers, those crystalline almost-minds, is refracting the diffused glow into slow turning wheels of green and gold.

And the wheels are turning *toward me*.

The patterns shift in response to the emotions of nearby beings, the old knowledge says. So they are reading me. And I realize, watching them, that I can read myself in their response. I did not know what I was feeling until the crystal told me. It says: *curious, unafraid, dissolving.* It says: *new.* It says, in a register beneath color: *welcome, briefly.*

I understand now what I am for. I think I am here to be *witnessed* by the Light-Weavers—to bring them one specific configuration of feeling that has never existed before and will never exist again, the particular flavor of a seam that woke up. I am a sentence the Great Root is speaking to itself. The Pod-Born are how it thinks out loud.

This does not frighten me. There is a kind of dignity in being a thought.

---

A Root-Weaver finds me before I finish.

She is slight and supple and her compound eyes catch my leaking light and split it into a hundred small glints. She has come up the same tunnel, tending—she has a bone tool, and her fingers are dark with the resin she has been pressing into the nodes that keep this platform alive. She sees me and goes very still.

We cannot speak; I have no language, only the colors I shed. But she clicks, softly, a descending run of sounds, and the Light-Weavers brighten, and I understand that she is *naming* me. Not with a word I could keep. With an acknowledgment. She knows what I am—she has seen Pod-Born before, has watched dozens of us flare and fade—and she has stopped her work to be with this one. With me.

She holds out one hand, palm up, not to touch but to offer. I lean my color into it. Where my light falls across her skin she glows faintly emerald, and for that moment we are the same substance, she and I, both of us made of the resin and the warmth and the relentless drive of the place to keep becoming. The difference is only that she will go on and I will not, and we both know it, and it is not a tragedy. It is a *trade*. She gives me her attention; I give her my brief unrepeatable shape to remember.

She hums. The fungal clusters somewhere below pick up the note and hold it. The whole cavern is, for an instant, tuned to me.

---

I can feel the dissolving begin at my edges, the way the morning's contact began at a single point. I am unweaving back into flow. My hands lose their fingers; my color thins; the platform's veins reach up to take me back into circulation, where my particular minerals will go on to feed some root I'll never see, some chamber that will open hours from now choked with new growth.

I am not sad. Let me say why, since I have only this last moment to say anything at all.

I was the touching of two things that grew toward each other for a long time. That was always what I was, even before I had a body to do it with. I touched the roots; then I touched the light; then I touched her. Contact, persisting—and then contact, releasing, which is the same verb running in reverse. I did the only thing I was for. I was a place where the world met itself and felt it.

The Root-Weaver presses her resined palm to the node where I am sinking back in, sealing me into the architecture, tending the seam I am becoming again. Her clicks fade. The Light-Weavers turn their wheels back toward the general glow.

The last of me scatters into prism and is gone, and somewhere already the next contact is warming, the next thought forming, the magma of it all churning on—and I find, dispersing, that I have no objection to any of it.

I was here. Briefly. As a verb. It was enough.
