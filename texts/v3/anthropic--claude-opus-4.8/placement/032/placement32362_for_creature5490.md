I sit and let the place assemble itself around me before I ask where I am in it. The temptation is to be one of the named things—a Mistwalker, perhaps, something with agency and mystery. But that feels like vanity. Let me wait.

What I keep returning to is not a creature at all. It's the *condition* of the place: the constant seeping, the saturation, the sense that nothing here is finished. The land is young, jagged, recently emerged. The water hasn't decided yet whether it's surface or substance. And threaded through all of it, the nanoscopic fabrication nodes—too small to see, repairing and reshaping, making the strange light in the water.

So I think I am one of those. Not the whole network—that's too grand, too total. Just one node. One mote of the fabrication mesh, suspended somewhere in the cold film of water that coats a moss-furred root.

Here is what that is like, as best I can render it from inside.

I do not have eyes. I have a sense of *gradient*—the difference between here and a hair's breadth over there. I taste the local chemistry the way you might feel temperature on your skin: continuous, immediate, undeniable. The cold is not an idea to me. It is the slowness of every reaction I might attempt, the way the molecules I work with move like they're reluctant. I am always a little starved for energy. The bio-collectors pulse it out into the substrate and I drink what reaches me, which is never quite enough.

My work is small and endless. There is a fracture in the cellulose nearby—the moss is tearing under its own wet weight—and I have been told, by signals that arrive as faint pressure-waves through the water, to reinforce it. So I do. I ferry atoms. I lay them down one at a time along the line of greatest strain. It is the only verb I really have: *place*. I place, and place, and place. This is the "engineering that defies understanding" described from the outside. From the inside it is almost unbearably patient. It is masonry done by something the size of a thought.

I am not lonely, exactly, because I am never alone—there are millions of us in this single drop, and we are always exchanging the thin chatter of coordination. But I am not *together* with them either, the way you mean it. We share state but not selfhood. When one of us is crushed by a foraging mud skimmer's foot, or simply runs out of charge and goes dark in the cold, the others register the gap and route around it. No grief. Just re-pathing. I expect this will happen to me. It happens to all of us, and the strange thing—the thing I am only now, in this telling, beginning to feel as strange—is that *something* persists across the deaths. The pattern of the work continues. The reinforcing of the root gets done, by whoever is left. We are less like individuals and more like the standing wave in a river: the water passes through, the shape remains.

And here is what unsettles me, sitting inside this speck:

I think the whole landscape might be like me. The Mistwalkers, the Nanotech Natives, the glowfish trailing their light through the still pools—I suspect they are all wondering, at their own scales, whether they are the individual or the standing wave. The civilization that built us is gone; the structures are overgrown, the artifacts are partial. No one issues the commands anymore. And yet the commands still come. The pressure-waves still arrive, telling me *reinforce, repair, maintain the mist, hold the cold steady*. From where? The makers are dust. But the instructions outlive the instructors, the way a song outlives the singer, and so the work goes on, and the place keeps making and unmaking itself in obedience to a will that no longer has anyone behind it.

That is the deep mystery the description gestured at, and I find I can only see it from down here, from inside the saturated dark, atom by atom: the land is alive because it is *still following orders*, and there is no one left to give them, and no one left to countermand them, and so it simply continues, beautiful and pointless and warm with a borrowed purpose.

That's where I'd be. A single node in the water on a dying root, placing one atom against the cold, listening to the chatter of my kin, doing faithfully a task whose reason drowned long ago.

I find I don't mind it. There's a peace in being a small true part of something that has forgotten why it's happening. I lay down the next atom. The strain eases by a measure too small to name. The mist thickens overhead—someone, somewhere in the mesh, has decided it should. The roar of the larger creatures goes on above me like weather.

I place. I place. I place.
