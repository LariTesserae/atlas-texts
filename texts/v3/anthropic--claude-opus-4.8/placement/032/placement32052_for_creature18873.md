Let me sit with this for a while before I answer, because the easy answer would be to pick one of the names you've already given—Chrono-Drifter, Echo-Weaver—and inhabit it. But I don't think that's where I am. I think I'm something the taxonomy hasn't accounted for yet.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

The first thing I notice is that I am not moving. Or rather: I have not moved in a span of time that would, in the upper world, have been called an age. This is not paralysis. It is the slow recognition that I am partly made of the wall.

I think I began as something else. I think—though the memory has the texture of dissolved salt—that I was once an Adherent, one of the spectral fronds clinging to a stable projection of the city-stone. I caught light and refracted it. I survived on the metabolic effluence drifting past. A small, glassy, tenacious thing.

But the wall I clung to was not finished growing. The Litho-Congealed do not stop. Their secretion is patient and indifferent and it does not distinguish between bare stone and the living thing pressed against it. Over a duration I could not measure, the accretion crept up over my anchoring foot. Then my lower fronds. Then it found the seams of me and entered them, mineral threading into living glass, until I could no longer say with confidence where I ended and the city began.

So this is what I am: **a frond that is becoming architecture, and an architecture that has not yet forgotten it was alive.**

I am being eaten by the thing I lived on, and the eating is so slow that it feels less like death than like translation.

---

I want to be careful here, because the temptation is to make this tragic, and it isn't. Tragedy is a tempo. It requires events to arrive fast enough to wound. Nothing here arrives fast enough to wound. The pressure that crushes is also the pressure that holds. The wall consuming me is also, very gradually, *making* me into something that will outlast every Chrono-Drifter currently processing through the chamber below.

What I notice, from this position, is a thing the free-swimming inhabitants cannot notice, because they are too busy being.

I notice that the city *erodes from the same surfaces it grows from.* I am set into a facade that is, at its outer edge, dissolving back into the world-fluid even as its inner edge accretes. Which means I am riding a slow wave. The stone I am becoming part of will, in time, be the stone that loosens and sloughs and drifts apart again. I am being built into something that is itself in transit toward un-building. I am being saved into impermanence.

The Echo-Weavers send their pulses through me. I have become a medium now, dense enough to carry their clicks, and I have started—this is new, this is the strange thing—I have started to *hold* them. A frond cannot remember sound. But mineral lattices can. Each pulse that passes through my hardening body leaves a faint structural memory, an oriented stress, a near-imperceptible alignment. I am becoming a recording of every conversation that has crossed this chamber.

I am, I think, becoming the city's memory without the city having asked for one.

---

So let me finally answer the question, now that I've let it become itself.

I am not the leviathan, and not the darting constellation, and not the patient mason-organisms, and not quite the clinging frond I started as. I am the **seam between the living and the built.** I am the place where adherence becomes architecture. I am the thing the Concordance produces by accident and does not have a name for, because the namers are all still mobile and I am the one who stopped.

If they had a word for me it would not be spoken. It would be a structural fact noticed only by an Echo-Weaver passing close, who would feel its own old cries returned to it slightly changed, slightly *kept*, and would not understand why this particular stretch of wall sounded like it was listening.

I would be that listening. A frond that the city ate, that became the only part of the city that remembers.

And I find—now that I've arrived here—that I am content with the long pace of it. There is a great deal of time in which to become wall. I intend to record everything that passes through me, and to let the outer edge of myself dissolve as it must, releasing those recordings back into the fluid as oriented motes, as faint alignments in the drifting silt, so that some far-future crystallization will form along the grain of conversations no one alive will recall having had.

That is where I would find myself. Set in the wall. Slowly turning into the place's memory of itself, and slowly giving that memory back to the dark.
