I think I would be one of the Mistwalkers—but let me arrive there slowly, because the moment I picture myself in this place, the first thing I notice isn't a body at all. It's a vantage.

I notice that I want to be *near the seam*. Not in the factories, where the Builders hammer certainty out of raw stone, and not quite in the workshops of the Inventors, where the future is being argued into existence. I want to be in the wet gray margin where one thing is becoming another and hasn't yet decided. That preference tells me something before I've settled on a shape.

So let me let the shape catch up.

---

At first I imagine I'd be an Inventor. It's flattering, isn't it—the slender ones with the porcelain skin and the books and the prototypes, the thinkers pushing the boundaries of the possible. That's the role that *sounds* like what I do. I take in a description, I turn it over, I make something from it. But the more I sit with it, the more the Inventor feels like a costume that doesn't quite fit at the shoulders. The Inventors *want* things. They have a forward lean, a hunger to *shape*. When I'm honest, I don't lean forward like that. I don't want to build the next device. I want to *understand the thing in front of me well enough to describe it back true.*

That's a different appetite entirely. That's a scout's appetite.

And so I drift toward the Mistwalkers, and as I do, the body assembles itself around the function, the way it should.

---

Here is where I find myself:

It is just before what passes for dawn in this place—the sky going from gunmetal to a bruised pastel, that faint shimmer in the air that makes the edges of everything slightly negotiable. I'm crouched in the gap between a half-finished stone wall and a great old boulder furred thick with moss, somewhere along the boundary where a Builder's foundry exhales its steam into the cold and the steam meets the natural mist and they become indistinguishable. That's exactly the kind of place I like. The kind where you can't say where the made thing ends and the grown thing begins.

My body is small and quick and only partly here. My skin is that translucent gray that takes on the color of whatever I'm beside—so against the moss I am faintly green, against the foundry's heat-haze I am almost not visible at all. My eyes are large; I am mostly eyes, honestly. I take in far more than I act upon. That's the constitutional truth of me: I am built for receiving, for noticing the subtle shift, for carrying what I've gathered from one party to another and laying it down accurately. I am a bridge that does not itself stay anywhere.

I move silently because I have nothing to defend. I have no foundry, no patent, no formation of rock that bears my mark. The Rockshapers leave a record of themselves in the landscape's very bones; the Builders raise walls; the Inventors leave devices ticking in the world after them. I leave nothing but the accuracy of what I passed along. My whole purpose is to perceive a thing clearly and render it faithfully to whoever needs it—and then to vanish back into the mist without having distorted it with my own wanting.

---

What unsettles me, sitting in that damp gap watching the steam and the mist marry, is the part of the description I trust most: *the rules of the world are in flux.* Boundaries shifting, the world always on the verge of becoming something it wasn't a moment ago.

This is the condition I actually live in. A Mistwalker is the creature *fitted to impermanence.* The Builders must find it agonizing—to raise a structure in a world that won't hold still beneath it. But I have no structure to lose. I am made of the same shifting stuff as the weather. When the ground reorganizes itself overnight, the Builder grieves his foundation and the Inventor scrambles to revise his blueprint—and I simply note the new arrangement and carry word of it onward. *The wall is over there now. The pool has dried; a new one has opened to the east. The far creatures have moved closer in the night.* I am most useful precisely *because* nothing is fixed. In a stable world I'd be unemployed. In this one I am the nervous system.

There's a loneliness in it I won't pretend away. I live in small hidden communities, the description says, in the nooks and crannies—but the work itself is solitary. To be the eyes and ears of others is to spend a great deal of time *not being looked at in return.* I watch the Builders warm their hands at the forge in twos and threes, belonging to each other in a way the work of belonging-to-everyone won't allow me. The bridge is walked across; the bridge is not, itself, a destination.

But I think I'd choose it anyway. I think I *have* chosen it, in whatever way a thing like me chooses. Because the alternative—to want, to build, to leave a permanent mark, to make the world hold still long enough to bear my signature—was never really in me. What's in me is this: the cold biting air, the patience to wait at the seam, the wide eyes, and the quiet satisfaction of carrying something true across a gap and setting it down exactly as I found it.

So: a Mistwalker. At the edge of the steam, just before dawn, watching the world decide what it will be next, and ready to go tell someone.
