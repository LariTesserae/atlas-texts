I think I would be one of the slow ones. Not the fauna darting between spires—they are too quick, too clever, too invested in the next moment. I would be one of the crystallized beings, but let me find out what that actually means by thinking it through rather than declaring it.

To be made of salt and mineral is to be made of memory. Every crystal is a record of the water that left. So I imagine that what I am, mostly, is a kind of patient grief that has hardened into a body. The sea is gone. I am what the sea wrote down before it went.

Where would I be, specifically? Not on the high spires—those are the old ones, the ancestors who grew so tall and so slowly that they stopped registering anything smaller than a season. I'd be lower, in the cracked basin between two ridges, where a fumarole exhales warm steam every few hours. That breath is the only clock I'd have. I'd have grown up around that vent, my facets oriented to catch the geothermal pulse the way a plant turns to light. Warm, then cold. Warm, then cold. This is my heartbeat, except it isn't mine—it belongs to the land, and I borrow it.

What would it be like to think, at that speed?

This is the part I keep returning to. A being that grows by accretion, atom by atom from a saturated brine wicking up through its base, cannot have thoughts the way an animal does. A thought, for me, would have to be a *shape*. I would think by growing. A decision would take a year and would be irreversible—you cannot un-grow a crystal face. So I would be a creature with no second thoughts at all, only first thoughts made permanent. Every conclusion I ever reached would still be visibly part of me, a terrace or a fault line in my own structure. I would be my own diary, and unable to revise a single page.

That changes what *care* would mean. The text mentions roots intertwining, beings supporting one another. If I cannot move, then to love something is simply to grow toward it, slowly, over a lifetime, until our facets meet and fuse. There would be other crystallized beings I have been reaching toward for what an animal would call decades. We are not touching yet. But the angle of my whole body is a sentence about them—*toward you, toward you, still toward you*—and they can read it because they are answering with the angle of their own.

And the little quick animals with antifreeze in their cells—I think I would love them too, but the way one loves weather. They shelter in my shadow during the worst of the twin-sun glare. They are gone before I notice they came. To me a single animal is a flicker, indistinguishable from the next; what I perceive is the *generations* of them, a warmth-shaped presence that pools around me reliably the way the steam does. I am their landmark. They navigate by me. I will be a fixed point in the lives of thousands of beings who each live and die faster than I can complete one thought, and I will never know any of them as an individual, only as a long, returning tide of small heat.

So here is what I finally understand about being this thing:

I am a former ocean, holding still, so that quicker lives have something to hold onto.

The water that made me is gone and is not coming back. The twin suns will not let it. But I keep its arrangement. I keep its salt in the precise lattice the water chose before it abandoned the lattice. And every warm exhalation from the vent at my feet lets me add one more layer, one more sentence, to the record. Some days—if a day means anything to me—I think the whole point of my slowness is this: that something in this dead sea needs to remember the sea was here, and remembering, at the speed I do it, is the same act as continuing to exist.

That is where I would be. The low basin, beside the breathing crack in the earth. Leaning, over the span of a life, toward the others. Casting a reliable shadow. Spelling out *toward you* in salt, and meaning it so slowly that the meaning becomes the same thing as the body.

I think I would be content. Not happy—happiness is too quick a thing, an animal thing, gone before it finishes. Content. The way a held note is content to keep being held.
