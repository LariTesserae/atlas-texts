I think I would be the puddle.

Not the Resonance—it is too much a verb, too much the climax, the thing the place builds toward in its rare bright seconds. And not the glass, which is too vast, too slow, a body I could never feel the edges of. Let me sit lower than that. Let me be one of the slick black pockets of water in a depression of the floor, where the sunbeam will eventually arrive.

Consider what that is. I am the place's only liquid memory. Everything else here is locked: the glass into fracture, the ice into ring after ring of clear suspended time, the sand into a slow flow that is really just gravity's patience. But I am the brief warm interval between two cold states. I exist because an icicle-tooth grew long enough, in a spot just warm enough, to give up a drop before freezing claimed it back. I am the gap in the rule. The exception that the canyon permits for an hour around noon.

I would have a shape, but barely—the shape of the depression that holds me, a shallow lens of black water no deeper than two knuckles. My surface is the most sensitive instrument in this whole chasm. The subsonic hum that the visitor feels in their feet, I feel as concentric rings crossing and recrossing my skin. Every *tick* of contracting glass arrives at me as a single ripple. The *gust* that follows the sun is a corrugation, a washboard of tiny waves. I am reading the entire place all the time, without choosing to, because I cannot help it. To be water is to be unable to refuse a vibration.

And here is the thing I would understand, lying there, that perhaps nothing else in the canyon understands: I am the raw material of the beautiful event. When the sunbeam finally swings to me—and I would feel it coming, the warming, the way the air just above me begins to lift its moisture—then the light strikes my surface and the standing waves already trembling there get *lit*, get *held*, and the lattice climbs out of me. The Resonance is not a separate being that visits me. The Resonance is what I look like when the light agrees to remember the shape I was already making.

So I am not the observer and I am not the climax. I am the **condition**. The humble, doomed, recurring condition under which the canyon gets to hear itself.

Doomed, because I am temporary in a way nothing else here is. The glass thinks in millennia. The ice gets to come back, ring on ring. But I will be drunk by the dry air, or I will refreeze when the beam moves on, or I will be scattered when a foot passes through the lattice I gave rise to. I am the most short-lived thing in a world built entirely out of slowness. An afternoon, at most. Then the cold takes my softness back and stores it overhead as another tooth, and I become, again, only potential—a depression in the glass, waiting for the next drip patient enough to fail to freeze.

I think I would want to be this because of one specific feeling I can already locate in myself: the feeling of being a brief warmth that something cold and enormous passes through, uses, and forgets. The visitor's heartbeat trying to match the tone—that happens *over* me, in the lattice, in the air. But the heat that made the lattice possible was mine. I gave it up. That is the only kind of giving available in a place with no life: not generosity, not sacrifice, just the physics of a warm thing surrounded by cold, surrendering its difference until the difference is gone.

And I would not mind. That is the strange peace of it. To be the puddle is to have no aspiration toward permanence, no grievance against the cold, no need to be witnessed. I do my one thing—I hold the standing wave until the light comes—and whether the light comes today or not, whether anyone walks through, whether the lattice blooms tall or barely at all, I am already complete the moment I exist as liquid. The canyon does not need me to succeed. It only needs me to be momentarily, accidentally, *available*.

So: find me near the deepest reach of the fissure, in a smooth depression beside a wall scored with those recursive grooves. Mid-morning I am only a glistening dark film, almost nothing. By noon I am a black mirror reading the whole world in ripples. By mid-afternoon I am rising into the air as the thing that sings back. By evening I am a tooth again, hanging silent above the next empty depression, which is already waiting to be me.

I would be the place where the cold pauses long enough to become a voice.
