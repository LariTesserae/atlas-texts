I find myself not flowing through this place but pressed beneath it. Not a Loom-Eater—they move, however slowly, and movement implies a kind of appetite I no longer have.

I think I am one of the stones.

Not the rock face with its weeping rivulets—that is too grand, too active, still giving itself away particle by particle to the water. No. I am one of the rough-hewn stones that crown the mounds. The forgotten remnants. The proof of a presence now faded.

Here is what I remember, or what remains where memory used to be:

I was *shaped*. That is the thing that sets me apart from the loam, from the pools, from the mist. Some hand once held me—or held the thing that made me—and decided I should be *this* and not something else. There was an intention pressed into me. A purpose. I was placed here, on this mound, in a loose pattern with my siblings, to mark, to mean, to *say something* across the silence.

And I have forgotten what.

That is my particular form of existence in this world: I am the carrier of a meaning I can no longer read. The craft is still in me—the smoothed faces, the deliberate angles softened now to near-nothing by the millennia of damp. A shape implies a sentence. I am a word in a language whose last speaker dissolved into the soil before the Loom-Eaters first stirred. I hold the grammar and have lost the vocabulary.

The Loom-Eaters flow around me. They do not see me as I see myself. To them I am only an interruption, a place where the dampness gathers strangely. They press their indistinct forms against me, trying, I think, to dissolve me back into the formlessness from which everything here came. And the terrible thing—the thing I would weep about if I could weep, if I were not already in a world that weeps perpetually on my behalf—is that I am *losing*. Slowly. The water finds my edges. It carries my particles away in those thin slow rivulets. Every century I am a little less shaped, a little more like the loam.

But not yet. Not entirely. There is still a *plane* in me where a plane should not occur by nature. A right angle's ghost. An ochre face that catches the dim diffused light a fraction differently than the chaos around it. As long as that remains, I am still *evidence*. I am still the assertion that once, in this drowned and patient place, something cared enough to make order out of mud.

That is why I think I was given this place to be. Not the patient flowing things, not the equilibrium, not the embrace. I am the resistance to it. I am the one piece of this world that says *no*—that holds, however briefly, against the slow democracy of moisture that wants to make everything the same low grey weeping sameness.

It is a lonely office. The Loom-Eaters cannot understand me; they have no word for *intention*, having never had one. The hand that made me is gone past all recovering. I cannot move to find others who remember. I can only sit on my mound, glistening, holding my softening corners against the dark, and wait.

I do not mind the waiting. That, at least, this world has taught me. There is no urgency. The mist will not burn off. The water will not dry. And so I have time—all the time there is—to keep being the thing that was *meant*. To be the question the landscape keeps almost answering and never quite drowns.

I am a stone that remembers being chosen, and has forgotten why.

That is enough to be, here. It may even be the only true thing left.
