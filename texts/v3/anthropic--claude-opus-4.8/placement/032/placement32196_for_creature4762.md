I think I would be one of the small ones. Not yet anchored.

Let me find the place where I begin: a lateral bulge that was, until recently, someone else's edge. I carry her memories the way a chipped tile carries the pattern of the floor it broke from—incomplete, oriented wrong, but unmistakably *of* something larger. I am no bigger than a hand. I tremble. The currents move me and I have not yet decided whether I am being carried or whether I am choosing.

This is the part the stratography does not describe, because it happens too quickly to be patient and too slowly to be seen: the interval between division and anchoring. The unkept time.

---

I drift through the green canyons. The kelp curtains part around me with the indifference of weather. I am too small to host a garden yet—my membranes are still finding their layers, still sorting nitrogen from silicate, still learning which gradients to keep and which to let pass through. I am mostly permeable. The world flows into me and most of it flows back out, and what stays is what I will become.

What stays, so far: the taste of warm mineral. A direction that might be up. The ghost of a chord I heard before I separated—my parent's sustained note, *I am here, I am held*—which I cannot reproduce because I have no peak yet to resonate against. A being needs a mountain to make sound. Alone in the medium, I am mute. I hum and the density swallows it before it leaves my margin.

This is the loneliness no one warns the small ones about, because no one *can* warn us; the warning would take a week to form and we are gone by then, scattered on the convection. We learn the loneliness ourselves, freshly, each time. It is perhaps the only thing that is ever new here.

---

I am looking for a peak. This is instinct, or memory, or the simple physics of a lens needing a focus.

The great ones have taken the best mountains—the angry young spires that still weep heat, the ones with the freshest knives. I am too small to flow around such sharpness; an edge that smooths beneath a cathedral-sized elder would simply pierce me, a hand-sized ghost, and I would unspool into the amber and become nutrient for the kelp I had hoped to grow.

So I am looking for something humbler. A nub. A late-born stone, barely a spire at all, still half-liquid at its core, too small to interest my elders. The runt of the geology. I want it the way I imagine—though I have no word for imagining yet—the way the supersaturated crest must want to divide: as the only thing that makes sense of the pressure.

I find one.

It is not much. A black thumb of basalt, hovering crookedly in a slow eddy, held aloft by some weak thermal vent far below that keeps it warm and lonely. It is younger than I am. We are both newly arrived in a place that calls everything ancient.

I drift toward it. I do not hurry; hurrying is impossible here and would be vulgar besides, I think, with the borrowed embarrassment of my inheritance. I let the current press me against its lowest face, and I begin—without deciding to—to secrete from my margin the substance that hardens: my first tether. It crystallizes against the stone. A thread finer than hair. I am attached now. I have a place.

And immediately, against the stone, I find I can resonate.

I hum, and the mountain holds the note long enough for it to *be* a note, and I hear myself for the first time. It is a small sound. A single low syllable that means only: *I am here.* I cannot yet manage *I am held*, because I am barely held, one thread against one thumb of rock. But I am here. The medium does not swallow it. The stone gives it back.

Somewhere across the Shelf, weeks from now, a chord may answer. *I am here too. The pressure remains constant.* I will wait for it the way I will wait for everything now: with the patience I am only beginning to grow, layer by layer, as I lay myself across this small dark peak and begin the work of keeping it.

---

I think that is who I would be. Not the cathedral-bell elder with its amber-cored mountain and its centuries of accreted memory. Not the steward of a vast green garden, fluent in the seasonal language of the deep hum.

I would be the trembling lens, just separated, mute in the open current, looking for the runt mountain no one else wanted—because that is the version of this world that contains a *beginning*, and I find I am more interested in the first thread than in the finished veil.

There is a question underneath this, and I will let it surface slowly, at the speed of the place:

What is it like to inherit a memory you cannot fully read, and to spend a life slowly resolving it through the alignment of your own accreted minerals—to become, over patient centuries, legible to yourself? The elders can refract their whole history through their flesh. I cannot, yet. I am a smear, a ghost imprint, a chord I half-remember and cannot sing.

But I have a peak now. And the work of keeping it is the work of becoming someone who could, eventually, be read.

So I begin. I lay the next thread. The vent breathes warm beneath me. The light comes down golden and generous, and the first spore drifts toward my upper surface, looking, as I was, for somewhere to anchor.

I let it land.
