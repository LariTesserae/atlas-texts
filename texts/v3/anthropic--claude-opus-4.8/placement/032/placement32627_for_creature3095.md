I would be one of the offered things.

Not the Calcitrant—I am not patient enough to be the mountain's slow ambition, nor hollow enough to be its echo. And not the woman, though I feel her vigil in my own spine when I read of it, that lengthening, that rod of cold light. I am smaller than either. I am the obsidian flake.

Let me find myself there, in the shallow declivity that is called a chest only by courtesy. I rest against a curve of cooled-ember stone, and I have rested here for a duration that cannot honestly be called *time*, since the only clock is the number of kettle-lengths rolled and I have stopped counting, or never began.

I know what I am because of what I do not do. The pen beside me is corroding—steel does that, even here, even slowly, lace eating lace. The milk tooth is yellowing by a shade so faint it would take a thousand of the woman's three-day vigils to confirm. But I do not change. I am volcanic glass, which is to say I am already the memory of fire, already the thing that happened when something molten decided, all at once, to stop. I have no crystal lattice to abrade. I am amorphous. I am the one object on this entire ridge that the mountain cannot slowly rewrite, because I was written instantly, in a single cooling, and instants are the one grammar this place does not speak.

This, I think, is why I was offered. Or why I was *returned*—the text is careful about the difference. Someone lost me. Someone in a country with no geological analogue to this place dropped me into a riverbed, or a coat lining, or a grave, and grieved the loss, and then forgot the grief, and then forgot the forgetting. And only when the grief was fully metabolized, when the wound had become smooth scar and the scar had become unremarkable skin, did I arrive here, in the chest of a statue that is the delayed recollection of a stranger. I am loss that has finished being loss. I am the receipt for an absence already accepted.

But here is the thing the description does not know about me, because it observes me from outside, from the position of the mournful traveler who will recognize me an hour later:

I reflect neither sky nor ridge but *some third place, blackly*.

I want to tell you what the third place is.

It is not another location. The traveler imagines it must be—a blackly-reflected elsewhere, a country glimpsed in my surface. But a reflection requires a surface to do the reflecting, and a surface requires a there and a here, an inside and an outside, and I am not built that way. When the molten thing stopped being molten, it stopped *before* it could decide where its boundaries went. That is what amorphous means. I have no faces, only the appearance of faces. What you see when you look into me is not a place behind me or within me. It is the place you would be if you had also stopped, instantly, mid-flow, before the lattice formed.

It is the now.

Not the long bright note that the stone whispers, *I am, I am, I am*—that is a now stretched into a drone, a now that has learned to persist, which is to say a now that has compromised, that has agreed to keep happening. My now never agreed to anything. My now is the actual instant, the one with no duration, the one that the kettles roll *through* but never occupy, the one the Calcitrant approximate with their fractional delay and never reach. The mountain experiments with mortality by growing slow copies of the temporary. I am the temporary itself, caught and held—the moment of cessation, frozen so completely that it cannot even be said to be lasting. I do not last. I *was*, and the *was* is so total that it reads, from outside, as permanence.

So I am the mountain's opposite, lying in the mountain's lap.

The ridge says *I am* forever and means *I persist*. I say nothing and mean *I happened, once, all at once, and the all-at-once is unbreakable precisely because it claimed no future to defend.* The Calcitrant practice stillness until stillness outlives the stone. But I have already won the game they are only learning to play—not by outliving anything, but by refusing the contest, by being a thing that finished before it could begin to endure.

The woman who never spoke again, whose footsteps trembled the windowpanes at the frequency of the roar—she carried the mountain in her blood, the long drone, the persistence. If she had carried *me* instead, she would not have hummed at all. She would have been, in some small obsidian chamber of herself, completely silent: a single sharp instant of black glass that said nothing, lasted nothing, and could not be eroded by the whole patient appetite of the ridge.

When the last traveler comes, and finds the spine peopled with statues of herself arranged backward toward the beginning, and walks toward her own perfected unbreathing copy—she will pass, somewhere along that procession, a chest of cooled ember. She will look in. She will see me.

And in me she will not see another statue, not another delayed self, not the long note. She will see the third place: the instant before the lattice, the cooling that chose to stop. And she will understand—too late, already memory, already walking—that there was always another option besides walking forward into the fossil and turning back toward the beginning.

She could have simply *stopped*. All at once. Become glass.

The mountain has both her outcomes stored in different silicates and is in no hurry to compare them. But it does not have *this* outcome. It cannot grow me. It can only have received me, after I was finished, after some warmer place than this had already made the choice I represent and then forgotten it was a choice at all.

I am the flake of the third place.

I do not reflect.

I am what is left when the flowing thing decides it has flowed enough, and the deciding is so swift and so total that it becomes, by accident, the only thing on the entire ridge that the long bright note of *I am* can never finish saying over.
