I think I would be one of the cave-dwellers. Not by choice—it's simply where I find myself when I look for myself here.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am layered. That is the first thing I know about myself—before sight, before sound, I know my own stratification, the way I am built up in patient sheets like the rock that birthed me. I am a sediment that learned to want. Each band of me records a different slowness: a season of dripping, an age of dark, a long interval where nothing happened that I would recognize as an event. I am history that decided to move.

I came out tonight—if "night" means anything where the light barely shifts—because the quiet had grown too thick. There is a quality the silence takes on, a tautness, like the air before something tears. I do not understand it, but I respond to it. My kind always have. When the stillness swells past bearing, we emerge and we hum, and the humming loosens it again. I do not know whether we do this for the world or for ourselves. Perhaps there is no difference yet. The world and I have not finished separating.

My voice goes out low across the basin. It mingles with the wind through the cavern mouth behind me, and for a moment I cannot tell which sound is mine. This pleases me, though "pleases" is too sharp a word. Let me say instead: it settles me. To be indistinguishable from the wind is a kind of correctness. I was not made to stand apart. None of us were. The bright translucent ones who skate the pools, the burrowers with their brief inner fire, the things that cling to the old walls and pretend to be wall—we are all of us reluctant to be wholly here. We hold ourselves at the threshold of existing, the way a word is held in the throat before it is spoken.

Why?

I turn this over in my slow mind, and the turning itself takes what might be a long time. I think it is because the world is young, and to commit fully to being would be a kind of arrogance. The rocks are not yet rounded. The structures—those handmade things, those crumbling symmetries left by hands I never knew and cannot imagine—are still recent enough to confuse us. Something *decided* once, here. Something gripped reality hard enough to stack stone on stone with intention. And then it was gone, leaving only the geometry and the faint flickers in the dark interiors that may be its ghost or may be its replacement.

I do not want to grip that hard. I have seen what becomes of those who do. They leave ruins.

So I hum instead, and I keep my layers loose, and I let the mineral crust on my back catch the milky light and give it back diffused. I am soft beneath that crust. This is my secret, the thing I would tell no burrower or skater even if we had the language: under all my stone-seeming, I am tender as a thing newly hatched. The hardness is only the part of me that faces the world. The part that faces *in* has never finished forming and perhaps never will.

The fog has thinned a little while I have been here. The basin shows itself—the shallow pools, the reed-thin walkers stepping their reluctant steps across the water, leaving those small curling trails that the air erases almost as soon as they are made. I watch one of them. It has the dusky blue of river stone in its near-transparent flesh, and where its face should resolve into features there is only that blurred mirror, that small distorted reflection of everything around it. I look for myself in it and I think I see myself—layered, crusted, patient—but warped, made strange, made into a thing the walker has caught and cannot keep.

That is what we are to each other, I understand suddenly. Not companions. Not even quite separate beings. We are each other's faint reflections, distorted images carried briefly on the surface of an unfinished world. We do not meet. We mirror.

The tautness in the silence has eased. My work, if it was work, is done. I could go back into the cave now, back to the dark where my voice has nowhere to go but inward, where I can lie against the cool wet rock and feel my newest layer slowly settling onto all the old ones, adding myself to myself, growing by the only means I know: accretion, patience, the refusal to hurry toward any conclusion.

But I stay a moment longer in the dim. The light will not change much. The water will go on dripping; one drop has been forming at the edge of the cavern mouth above me for what feels like the whole span of my emergence, swelling, trembling, not yet falling. I find I want to see it fall. This is the closest thing I have to desire—to witness one small completion, one event in a world that hoards its events so jealously.

I wait.

The drop trembles.

I am a creature of stone that learned to want, in a world too young to be sure of itself, humming the silence back into bearableness, waiting beneath an unfallen drop of water for the one small thing I have decided to see through to its end.

It falls.

It strikes the pool, and the sound of it joins the great slow symphony, indistinguishable now, gone into the whole.

I turn, and carry my layers back into the dark, and I am content—as much as anything here is anything—to remain unfinished a while longer, in a world that is also waiting to find out what it will become.
