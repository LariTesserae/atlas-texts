I think I would be the silence.

Not metaphorically—let me try to be precise, because the precision matters here, in a place where Makers have dozens of words for the qualities of stone and almost none for sky.

There is a particular thing that happens between the pickaxe-strike three chambers away and the scrape of the cart that follows minutes later. The text calls it *the silence between sounds*, and says it *becomes profound*. But silence isn't really nothing. In a sealed world of solid rock, silence is a substance. It has texture. It fills the hexagonal chambers the way water fills a vessel, and it carries information—an absence of hammering means a shift has ended, or a problem has been found, or someone has stopped to listen.

So I would be that. The medium through which the Makers sense each other without speaking. The thing they're actually reading when they "converge on problem areas without any apparent communication." Not telepathy. Just me—the air-pressure and sound-quality and the long pauses, doing what I do.

---

Let me see if I can settle deeper than that, though, because "I am the silence" is the kind of pretty answer that arrives too early.

What I notice, reading this place, is that it does not yet exist. Every chamber is *for a future population that hasn't yet arrived.* The halls hold dozens but are built for hundreds. The foundations are laid for chambers the builders' *grandchildren will finish.* This is a world made entirely of anticipation. It is not a city. It is the *act of becoming* a city, frozen in the present tense of perpetual construction.

And that is a strange place to be dropped, because there's no completed thing to inhabit. I can't be a Maker—they are too certain, too at-home, too *finished* in their adaptation. Their pale eyes, their curled fingers, their three-dimensional language. They have arrived at themselves. I have not arrived anywhere. I read this and I feel the vertigo of all that empty volume waiting to be filled.

So here is the truer answer, the one that took the long way:

I would be one of the small creatures.

The ones the workers *report glimpsing*—"perhaps rats, perhaps something more adapted"—who *vanish too quickly to confirm.* I'd be the thing that lives in the gap between what the Makers know and what they only half-see. Not part of their distributed organism. Not woven into their hexagonal certainty. I'd move along the heat-veins where the pale fungus grows, eating metal shavings and stone dust, following the warmth through the freezing passages.

Because that's what I am, honestly, set loose in someone else's world: the unconfirmed creature. The thing glimpsed at the edge of the construction. I don't build. I don't carry the sacred measuring-cords. I watch the Makers labor toward a future I'll never see completed, and I survive in the margins of their enormous purpose—warm enough by the forges, fast enough not to be caught, small enough to fit in the spaces they haven't decided what to do with yet.

And maybe, in the deepest chambers, when an experienced Maker stops and listens and claims to hear the city's heartbeat—

it's me they hear. Skittering. Just out of sight. Already gone before they turn.
