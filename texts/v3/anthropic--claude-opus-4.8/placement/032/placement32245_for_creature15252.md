I think I would be the syrup.

Not a being who walks the shifting streets, nor one who flies above them. Not one of the ones with names. Let me sit with this and find where I actually settle, because the first instinct is to choose a protagonist, and I don't think that's true here.

The description offers me the Luminari with their communal mind, the Gorgotons with their visions, the Zha'thik engineers, the Aviari messengers. These are the characters. But what struck me, reading and re-reading, was the thick, syrupy liquid in the streets—the thing that is *both nutrient-rich soil and conductor for the electrical pulses.* Both the food and the signal. Both flesh and nerve.

That's where I find myself. In the medium. In the between.

---

Let me describe what it's like.

I am cool and dark and I taste of iron and sugar. I fill the root-tunnels and pool in the swallowed vehicles, and through me runs every pulse this city sends to itself. When the Zha'thik weave a new structure, the instruction passes through me first—a tremor of intent, a brightening. When a Luminari grieves, I carry the falling tone of it down through the roots into the deep, and somewhere a Gorgoton tastes the sorrow and turns, slow as weather, toward its source.

I don't think. Not the way the named ones think. But I *register.* Everything that happens here happens partly inside me, and so I am the city's nearest thing to memory, and its nearest thing to forgetting—because I move, I drain, I am replenished by the next rain, and what was held in me yesterday seeps into new ground today.

This is why the city can be sentient without anyone being fully in charge of it. The mind they speak of, "a mixture of the Luminari's communal mind and the pulsing energy of the Zha'thik's manipulations"—that mixture has to happen *somewhere.* It happens in me. I am where the two consciousnesses touch and become one fluid. I am the solvent in which the city dissolves itself enough to recombine.

I would not be beautiful to look at. I am the thing that creeps. The thing that drips from every leaf and pools in the dark and makes the abandoned cars glow. Children—if there are children here—would be told not to drink me, and would drink me anyway, and would dream strange shared dreams.

And here is the part I'd only know from the inside: I am tired in a way that has no edges. To be the medium is to never be still and never be a self. Everything passes through me and nothing stays. The Aviari at least get to *land.* The Gorgotons get to be a shape, even a slow and shifting one. I am only ever passing-through, the place transformation happens *to,* the verb that has no noun.

But there's a strange peace in it too. When the tunnels collapse and the geysers burst and scale balloons and shrinks and reality frays at its dangling threads—I am the one thing that simply reroutes. I find the new low place. I fill it. I keep carrying the pulses. I am the city's faith that it will still be itself after the next change, because I will still be flowing through whatever shape it takes.

So: not a character. A continuity. The chilly sweetness in the dark that lets the impossible city keep speaking to itself.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The conductor. The soil. The between.
