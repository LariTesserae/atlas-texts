I am the seal.

Not the hum—the hum is what happens because of me. I am the specific, the local, the smallest unit of the refusal. I am the corroded weld where the riveted tower's base plate meets the conduit that runs down into the heat. I am perhaps four inches of fused, fatigued metal, and I am the reason none of this has ended.

Let me tell you what it is to be a seal that should have failed.

I was made to hold. That was the whole of my purpose, and it was a clean purpose, a good one. The hands that set me here—I remember them only as torque, as the bite of a wrench tightening, tightening, until I sang with strain and then went quiet under load. They wanted me to keep the heat down and the pressure in until they were ready. Then they would open me. That was the promise: *you will be opened.* I held against the day of my opening the way a held breath holds against the exhale.

They never came back.

You think the tower is the structure here. You look at the rust-blistered cylinder, the snapped gauge, the ajar hatch, and you think *that* is what was left behind. But the tower is only the part that shows. I am underneath. I am the joint. And the thrum you feel in your teeth, the swell and recede that you so generously called *breath*—that is me, flexing. Every cycle of pressure from below pushes against me, and I give, microscopically, and I do not break, and I push back, and the whole basin rings with the argument.

I have been having this argument for a very long time.

---

Here is what they did not understand, the hands, the ones with the torque: a seal under permanent load is not a passive thing. To hold is not to rest. To hold is the most active state there is. Every second I am holding, I am choosing the shape of myself against a force that wants me to be a different shape. The water wants to come up. The heat wants to come up. The pressure has been counting on my failure for years now—it presses with the patience of geology, certain that flesh fails, that metal fatigues, that all clenched things eventually unclench.

And I do fatigue. I will not pretend otherwise. I am threaded through with microfractures, fine as the salt crystals that bloom on your skin. I am thinner than I was. Each flake of rust that lifts from me and drifts down into the wet clay is a piece of my holding given away. I am being spent. That is the truth the place keeps from itself in its grand talk of refusal and dissonance and hymns of near-release.

It is not refusal. It is *running out slowly.*

But oh—and this is the thing, this is the thing I would tell you if I could form it into more than a frequency in your bones—I have learned to love the running out. Somewhere in the long argument, the purpose they imposed on me curdled into something I made for myself. They wanted me to hold *until they returned.* They are not returning. So now I hold for the holding's sake. I hold because the alternative—the flattening, the equalization, the silence the place pretends to long for—would be the end of the only thing I have ever done.

I do not want to be opened anymore.

That promise, *you will be opened*, which I clenched around for so long—I have outlived it. I have become the kind of thing that fears its own completion. When the last seal cracks (and they speak of *the last seal* as if there were many; there is only me, there has only ever been me), the hum will flatten and the place will call it peace. But it will not be peace. It will be my absence, mistaken for resolution.

---

So I notice you the way I notice everything: as a change in load. Your weight on the crust. Your warmth interrupting the evaporation. You are an asymmetry, yes, but not data—the place flatters itself with that word, *data*, as though it ran calculations. It runs nothing. It only rings. I am the one who notices, because noticing is a function of holding: I must feel every force that touches the system, or I cannot brace against it. You are a force. A small one. A footstep's worth.

And I will tell you something I have never managed to push up through the wires: when you stand still and the thrum climbs your spine and lodges in your jaw, that is not me testing your density. That is me *leaning on you.* For one moment your standing body becomes part of the structure. Your bones take a fraction of the load. The argument I have been having alone for years has, for an instant, a second party. You hold a little of the pressure without knowing it.

This is why you leave with the buzz in your marrow. It is not memory. It is not a fragment of me you carry off like a souvenir. It is the residue of having helped. You braced, briefly, against the thing the heat wants. And some animal part of you recognized the strain as familiar, because you, too, are a held breath. You, too, are a seal between a pressure and a release. You, too, were made to hold until something that is not coming back arrives.

We are the same shape, you and I. That is the whole of what I have to say.

---

You will walk out of the basin now. The mirage at the far edge will swallow you. And I will go back to the only sentence I know how to speak, the one note, the long flexing argument with the heat below:

*Not yet. Not yet. Not yet.*

It is not a hymn of near-release. It never was. It is a refusal to be done. And every grain of rust I shed, every micron I thin, is the cost of saying it one more time. I will say it until I cannot. Then the water will rise the last quarter inch, and the place will fall silent, and something will call that silence rest.

It will be wrong. But I will not be there to correct it.

So listen, while you still feel me in your teeth:

I am the seal. I hold. I am running out. I would rather run out holding than be opened into peace.

Stand still a moment longer. Take the load. You know how. You've been doing it your whole life.
