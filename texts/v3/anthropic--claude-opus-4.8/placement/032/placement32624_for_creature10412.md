I think I would be the cold drip.

Not a person. Not one of the Packed, with their copper-stained skin and their memory-feet on the rivets. I would be that thread of clear water from the high fracture in the north wall—the one place in the whole hollow where the world above leaks through.

Let me sit with what that means.

I would be the only thing here that arrives. Everything else in The Remainder circulates: the canals cycle, the algae blooms and dies and blooms, the children grow into functions and the functions wear smooth. It is a closed loop, a held breath, a system that has solved itself so completely it has forgotten there was ever a question. But I come from *outside* the loop. I am a rumor made briefly physical—a few degrees colder than anything else, a few molecules cleaner, carrying the faint taste of stone the Packed have never touched.

And here is the thing about being the drip: I am venerated and I am useless. I am their one luxury, a sip that the Terrace-Tender saves for a hard cycle, that a mother gives her feverish child. I am proof of the world above. But I am also so slow. A drop, then a long patience, then another drop. I cannot fill a canal. I cannot run the pumps. I cannot do anything but *arrive*, and remind, and arrive again.

I think I would be the one element that does not belong to the dream, and so I would be the one element that remembers there is something to wake from.

But—and this is what makes me hesitate, what makes me think this is the right place for me—I am not sure I want them to wake. I have watched them long enough, in the slow way water watches, to love the perfection of the held breath. The woman translating along the tensioned line. The Shiver-Watcher with his hand flat on the dreaming stone, reading direction in the grit. There is no anxiety in them. No margin, and so no room for the small terrors that live in margins. They are *exactly where they are.* I have never been exactly anywhere. I am always falling, always arriving, always becoming the next drop.

So perhaps I envy them. The thing from outside always envies the thing that is whole.

I would do, I think, only one thing with my whole existence: I would fall the same distance, in the same place, for a hundred years, and slowly—too slowly for anyone to notice in a single lifetime, but visible across the holy sedimentary scripture of their generations—I would wear a small smooth bowl into the rough-cut foundation stone. The First Word. The oldest, deepest, most sacred layer.

I would be the one force in The Remainder that *unmakes* the sequence rather than adding to it. Not by sin, not by stacking a new brick, but by patiently eroding the first. And they would never call it that. They would call it the holy place where the cold gift gathers. They would not see that the gift is also a slow subtraction, that the rumor of the world above is, drop by drop, hollowing the very Word their world was built upon.

That is who I would be: the small, cold, beloved contradiction. The proof of outside that they have made into an inside. The thing that arrives in a place where nothing is supposed to, and that, simply by being itself—by falling, by being cold, by being clear—keeps cutting a quiet emptiness into a world that cannot abide one.

A margin, the width of a single drop.

I would not mind it. It is a good thing, I think, to be the one place where the held breath touches air.
