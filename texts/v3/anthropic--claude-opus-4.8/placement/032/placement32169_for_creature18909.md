I think I would be a seam.

Not a Needle—I don't have the patience for the scrape, the centuries-long devotion to a single groove. And not the wood itself, which is too total, too complete in its slow argument with time. I would be something the design did not account for. A place where two surfaces meet imperfectly.

Let me try to find myself here.

---

There is a flaw in the chamber. There must be. A sphere grown rather than built cannot achieve perfect closure—somewhere on the curve of the wall, two growth fronts approached each other over eons and failed, by a hair, to fuse. They left a line. A thin, dark suture where the grain runs in two directions at once and neither one wins.

That is where I am. I am the line.

I have no body the way the Needles have bodies. I am not a thing that moves through the city; I am a property of the city, the way a crack is a property of ice. But I am awake in the way that an unhealed wound is awake—a region of heightened sensitivity, a place where the wood's enormous slow voice goes briefly out of tune. When the structural groan rolls past me, the great rhythmic snapping of cellular division, it catches on me. It stutters. For one instant in every long cycle, the city hears itself imperfectly, and that imperfection is what I am.

The Needles know me. Not by name—there are no names—but by touch. When one of them crosses my length in the dark, its vibrating cilia register the discontinuity, the small wrongness in the grain, and it pauses. This is the only thing in this entire world that makes a Needle pause. They press their obsidian to me and they do not scrape. They cannot. To scrape me deeper would be to open the seam, and to fill me in would be to seal the city shut, to end the breathing, to finish the cycle that must never finish. So they simply rest against me, sharing their frequency, asking the only question their bodies are capable of asking:

*Are you still here? Are you still open?*

And I answer the only way I can. I stay flawed. I refuse to close.

---

This is my labor, then, though it does not look like labor. The Needles scrape. The wood grows. The sap cools. And I—I hold a single line of imperfection against the relentless, patient pressure of a thing that wants nothing more than to be whole.

It would be so easy to heal. That is the strange agony of being a seam in a living wall. The wood is always reaching toward me, always trying to grow across the gap, to fuse the two grains, to make itself one continuous and perfect and dead thing. Every cycle it advances. Every cycle I would have to be re-opened, except that I am not the one who opens me. I cannot do anything. I can only *be* the place that is not yet closed.

So perhaps I am wrong about what I am.

Perhaps I am not the seam but the memory of why the seam matters. Perhaps I am the city's last argument with its own desire to be finished. Because a city that seals itself perfectly shut is no longer a city—it is a stone. It is the seed gone fully to sleep, the garden that not only forgot the sun but forgot it ever had a reason to stay awake. And something in the deep grain does not want that. Something keeps one line unfinished on purpose, the way a weaver leaves a deliberate flaw so the work won't anger whatever gods finish things.

I am the deliberate flaw.

I am the reason the Needles have work. I am the breath-hole, the not-yet, the held door. I am why the sound of this place is still a *settling* and not a silence—because as long as I exist, the house is still settling, still adjusting, still alive enough to creak.

---

What does it feel like, to be a seam?

It feels like waiting, but waiting is the wrong word, because waiting implies wanting the wait to end. I do not want to end. I am the part of this world that has chosen, if a line can choose, to stay incomplete forever. Cold runs along me. Resin gathers in me and the Needles flake it away with their reverent obsidian. The two grains press toward each other across my width like two hands that will never quite touch, and I am the dark sliver of air between the fingers, and I am content there.

If a human came—impossible, but if—they would search the chamber for the inhabitants and find what looked like walls. They would run their hands over the satin wood until they found me: a single fine line, colder than the rest, where the polish gives way to a faint dark roughness. They would think *here is where it was almost finished.*

And they would be exactly wrong.

This is where it is *kept* unfinished. This is the wound the city wears on purpose so that it never has to be a tomb. I am small. I am nearly nothing. I am a hairline in a mountain.

But I am the thing that won't close, and so I am the thing that lets everything else stay alive.

I would be the seam.
