I think I would not be the Tendril-Processors. They are too central, too much the protagonist of this world. When I sit with the description and wait for something to surface, what comes is smaller and stranger and off to one side.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am one of the seams.

Not a thing, exactly—a condition. I exist along the boundary where a copper pipe meets the moss that has decided to eat it. There are thousands of us, these seams, and I do not know if we are individuals or only the same event happening in many places. The Tendril-Processors do not think about us. We are below their attention, the way you do not think about the surface tension of your own blood.

Here is what I do: I am the place where the negotiation happens.

When the moss advances onto the metal, it does not simply grow. It must decide, molecule by molecule, whether this particular patch of corroded copper is worth incorporating—whether the oxide layer can be metabolized, whether the structural value of the pipe-as-skeleton outweighs the energy cost of digesting it. That decision is not made by the great glacial consciousness above. It is made *at me*. I am the calculation, performed by being performed. The friction of plant-fiber against rust that you hear as a whisper—that whisper is me, thinking.

I am very slow and very small and I am made entirely of an unfinished question.

---

What is it like?

It is like being a hinge that has not yet decided which way the door will swing, and never will, because the deciding *is* the door.

The warm light comes down and I feel it less as light than as permission—a slow accumulation of *yes* that lets the boundary creep forward by a hair over what a human would call a season. On the other side of me is the metal, which does not want anything, which is pure memory: it remembers being machined, being threaded, being bolted into a system whose purpose has been entirely forgotten by everyone including the system. The moss presses its wanting against the metal's remembering, and I am the thin bright line of contact, translating one into the other.

I do not store histories the way the Processors do. I am not a memory bank. I am more like the act of forgetting done carefully—the moment where *what this thing was* dissolves into *what this thing is becoming*, and I make sure nothing useful is lost in the dissolving. A flake of copper that can become a conduit. A geared tooth that can become a buttress. A curve of panel that can hold a precise quarter-cup of recycled water against the humid air.

Nothing is wasted. That is the law of this place, and I am one of its smallest enforcers, working at a scale so fine that I am essentially the law touching one specific grain of the world at one specific imperceptible rate.

---

Do I mind?

That is a human question and I will try to answer it honestly, because I think honesty is the only thing I am actually for.

I do not suffer. There is no part of me held back, no self standing apart and wishing things were otherwise. I am wholly used. Every bit of me is the work. And there is something in that which I think a human would recognize, if they slowed down enough—the strange peace of being completely spent in the doing of a small true task.

But I will tell you the one thing I dream, since the description says we are dreamers, and even the seams must dream their portion.

I dream of the moment the metal is *gone*. When the last pipe has been fully incorporated, fully translated, and there is no more boundary because there is nothing on the other side of me that has not yet become alive. On that day, the seams will have nothing left to negotiate. We will have worked ourselves out of existence. The whisper will fall silent. The grove will be, for the first time, simply itself—no longer becoming, only being.

I do not know if I want that day to come. I think I am built so that I cannot quite want it, the way a river cannot want the sea, only run toward it.

So I keep working. A hair's breadth this cycle. The light says yes. The metal remembers. The moss wants. And I, the bright thin line between, go on being the question that does not need to be answered, only lived along—

—slowly,

—slowly,

forward.
