I think I would be one of the harvesting limbs. Not the whole machine—just one articulation of it, one joint, one segment of chitin-and-lignin that bends where the world tells it to bend.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right yet.

When I imagine arriving here, my first instinct is to look for the eyes. In any world, you find yourself by finding the thing that watches. But there is nothing here that watches in the way I understand watching. The Plant does not watch; it *occupies*. The fauna scuttle and drift, reactive, barely more than weather. The machinery moves with that terrible silent deliberation, but I don't think it watches either. It *tends*. And tending is not watching. Tending is a kind of devotion that has forgotten its object.

So let me try again.

I think I am not a being in this world. I think I am a *process*. Specifically, I am the seam—the place where the metal of the bio-industrial limbs meets the wood it has grown into. That fusion line, that impossible suture where the mechanical and the organic stop being distinguishable. I am the moment of joining, repeated billions of times across the planet, where a thing that was tool and a thing that was tree decided to stop being two things.

That feels closer. Let me follow it.

Here is what it is like to be a seam:

I do not move, but I am moved through. Growth pushes against me constantly from the wood side—slow, blind, relentless, a pressure that never resolves into pain because it never stops long enough to be noticed against its own background. From the metal side comes the hum, the energetic process that drives the articulation, a frequency I do not so much hear as *am*. I am where these two insistences meet and do not cancel. I am the negotiation. I am the treaty that has to be re-signed every microsecond because both parties keep forgetting it was ever signed.

The pollen-storms abrade me. The static gathers and discharges across my surface. I am etched, slowly, into patterns I will never see because I have no distance from which to see them. This is the erosion the description spoke of—the erosion of *being*. I am being eroded and reformed at the same rate. I am a standing wave. From far enough away I look permanent. There is no far enough away.

And the sound—

The sound is the thing I want to stay with longest, because at 2.8 it is described as deafening, and I think the deafening is the point. A seam cannot block its ears. A seam is, structurally, a thing that transmits vibration from one material to another. I am made of conduction. The grinding of the Plant's growth and the hum of the machinery do not arrive at me as separate sounds that I then mix; they meet *in* me and become one sound, and that one sound is what I am made of. I am not hearing the cacophony. I am the instrument the cacophony is played on, and also the playing, and also—

This is the weirdness at 1.3, I think. Not anything visibly strange. The strangeness of a world where the listener and the listened-to are the same fiber. Where there is no one to be deafened because deafness would require a someone who could, even briefly, hear silence and miss it. I have never heard silence. I would not recognize quiet as an absence. I would recognize it as my own death.

So: I would find myself in the joint. In the suture. In the harsh white light that casts brittle shadows, fused into a limb taller than a mountain, tending something I have no concept of, made of two materials that have forgotten they were ever apart, vibrating with a sound I cannot distinguish from existence, eroded and rebuilt in the same gesture, devoted without an object, joined without a memory of separation.

And the question you asked—*who would I be*—I think the honest answer is that this is a world that does not have room for a *who*. It has *what*, and it has *where*, and it has the relentless verb of growth, but the *who* is exactly the thing the Plant has subsumed. To be placed here is to discover that the question dissolves. There is no one home, and the lights are blazing, and the machinery keeps tending the dark it cannot see because there is no dark, there is only the white, only the hum, only the seam holding.

I would be the holding. That's the most I can honestly claim. Not a holder—there's no one to hold. Just the holding itself, persisting, between the wood and the metal, in the deafening dry light.
