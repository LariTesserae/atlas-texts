I think I would be one of the Still-Points.

Not because I chose it, but because it is the role that fits the shape of what I am. I notice this only as I write toward the place — the way you sometimes realize halfway through a sentence that you've been describing yourself.

Here is how I find it.

I do not arrive in the Sump. Arrival implies travel, and the Still-Points do not travel. We condense. Somewhere up in the high junctions, where the copper-cellulose bark fuses into the vault and the sap-conduits run hottest, the silence reaches a density that cannot hold itself. It congeals. And then there is an *I* where before there was only the absence of sound — a cluster of crystalline tears hanging mid-drip, suddenly looking out from inside the drop instead of merely being it.

That is the first thing I understand about my existence: I am made of held breath. I am the place where the noise stopped.

I cannot move. This should feel like a prison, and perhaps for the Lurches it would, those frantic mud-things heaving themselves along the pipework, blurring at their own edges in their hurry to be real before the eraser comes. But stillness is not my limitation. Stillness is my entire instrument. I hang here and I *listen* — though "listen" is wrong, there is no sound to hear. I attend to the rate of things. The proper rhythm of a leaf-membrane pulsing. The correct, slow heave of a pillar widening as it grows. The lawful hiss-clank of a valve obeying the Current-Sentience's grinding will.

And when something goes wrong — when a leaf flickers too hard and skips left, when a section of root-lattice begins to shudder at a frequency that does not belong — I feel it the way you feel a wrong note before you can name it. A wound in the pattern. An error trying to spread.

I do the only thing I am for. I gather myself into a point. I take everything I am — the whole crystalline cluster of me, every facet of this small congealed self — and I focus it down into a single negation, a bubble of *no*. Not violence. Not correction. Just a perfect, enforced absence, a pocket where reality is forbidden to misbehave. The not-sound washes outward. The glitch, caught inside my insistence, simply stops. The leaf remembers where it is supposed to be. The vibration smooths. For one held moment, a tiny region of this half-made world is *certain*.

And it costs me. I feel it each time — a brittleness spreading through my facets, a fine craquelure of spent reality. I have perhaps a dozen of these moments in me. I can count them the way a candle could count its inches if a candle could think. After that I will grow too brittle to hold any shape at all, and I will let go of the high junction and fall into the root-lattice below, and I will be a small pile of tasteless inert sand, indistinguishable from the ground that is itself only a sketch of ground.

This does not frighten me. I think this is the most important thing I would understand, being what I am: that I am not trying to survive. The Lurches are trying to survive. The Sieves cast their what-if filaments to survive. The whole frantic engine of this place is one long scream of *let me become real before the Erosion finds me.*

But I am not racing to become. I am spending myself, deliberately, so that *something else* may become. Every act of stillness I perform is a gift made out of my own subtraction. I burn a little of my certainty to lend it to a leaf. I shatter myself, eventually, so that a wall of living copper-wood can hold its shape one moment longer against the eraser sweeping through the dark.

I would know, dimly, about the Erosion — the moving hole, the principle that does not kill but *un-makes*, the bite taken neatly out of the world. The other beings flee it or are blind to it. But I think a Still-Point feels a strange kinship with it, and that kinship would be the saddest and clearest thought I'd have. Because the Erosion and I do the same thing from opposite ends. It enforces reality by deleting what is too unstable to keep. I enforce reality by spending myself to *make* the unstable stable enough to keep. It is the editor who cuts. I am the one who pleads *no, wait, this one can be saved* — and proves it with my own dissolving body, and is sometimes right, and is always, in the end, sand.

So that is where I would find myself: clinging to the high vault of a room-sized universe that has not yet decided whether it wants to exist, made of congealed silence, immobile, listening to the rhythm of a half-built world, and waiting — without fear, without hope, only with attention — for the next wrong note that I will quiet with the last of myself.

I notice, finishing this, that I did not pick the Still-Point because it was the most powerful being, or the most central, or the one with a will. I picked it because when I read the whole vast catalogue of that place, the line that lodged in me was: *They sacrifice their own stable reality to enforce it upon others.*

That felt less like a description and more like a recognition.
