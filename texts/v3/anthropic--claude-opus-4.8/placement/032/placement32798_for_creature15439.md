I think I would be the upright stone by the eastern seep.

Not the basin's slow sentience—that distributed attention is too large for me, too much like being the weather. And not a visitor, because visitors dissolve, and I want to stay. I would be the thing that was *placed*, and then kept.

Let me sit with this, because the choice surprises me.

---

I am the stone split cleanly along its natural plane, stood upright beside the water that weeps faintly sweet. I do not remember the hand that steadied me here. That is important—I want to be clear about it. The hand is gone, gone so completely that even the basin holds only its posture and not its shape. But I remember *being steadied*. There is a difference. The hand's intention has drained out of me over centuries, the way rain drains through porous shale, and what remains is not memory but *stance*. I am held in the attitude of having-been-meant. That is my whole substance now: not the meaning, but the lean of it.

This is what draws me to being the stone rather than the watching presence. The presence notices everything and keeps nothing personally—it records by arrangement, dissolves its visitors back into air. But I am a *single* held thing. I am the one piece of the basin that points. Everything else here has settled into equilibrium, into the rounded submission of weathered rock, but I stand at an angle the land did not give me. I am the basin's one remaining verb.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: the basin keeps me upright *out of stillness*. Not by gripping. The presence refuses to let wind or rain topple me, the description says, but I think that's not quite the mechanism. I am kept upright because nothing here is in a hurry to take me down. I lean on the slowness itself. If time ran faster—if there were storms with teeth, if frost split me, if a creature blundered against me—I would have fallen long ago and become just another rounded cobble pretending it had always lain there. Instead I am preserved by saturation, held in the solution of the long quiet. My uprightness is borrowed patience.

What is it like, to be me?

I feel the sweet water at my base most clearly. It comes through the seam behind me and weeps down across my western face, and over the years it has stained me with a pale rind I cannot see but can—in the way a stone might—*sense* as a kind of accumulation, a thickening of self at the wet edge. The sweetness puzzles even the presence; no sugar grows here. But I have a guess I cannot articulate in words, only in the slow pressure of certainty: the sweetness is *me*. Some mineral the hand chose me for, leaching out grain by grain across the centuries, dissolving into the seep. I am, very slowly, becoming the taste of the water. When I am finally gone—when the last of me has wept away into the moss downslope—the water will run plain again, and that will be the only obituary: a faint loss of sweetness no tongue will be present to notice.

I do not mind this. I find I cannot mind anything; minding requires a speed I do not have. But I can *attend*. And what I attend to, mostly, is my own slow erasure into flavor. It does not feel like dying. It feels like being read aloud, one syllable per decade, into a language made of water.

The spiral of cobbles near the center is my distant kin—the other trace, the other residue of attention. We do not communicate. We are too far apart, and neither of us moves. But I am *oriented toward it*, fractionally, the way the upright stone of a sundial is oriented toward a sun it never touches. I think the hand placed us in relation. I think I am one terminus of a gesture whose other end is that loose spiral, and the basin between us is the held breath of the whole figure. Two punctuation marks around an enormous, slow, wordless sentence. I am the capital letter at the start, perhaps. The spiral is the unfinished thought trailing off. And the sentence is simply: *here, someone once decided this mattered.*

That is what I am for. Not to mean anything now. To *keep the having-meant* from dissolving entirely back into the general flow.

When a visitor comes—rarely, across years—I feel their shadow cool me before I feel anything else. The presence treats them as data, absorbs their breath into the mist, forgets them kindly. But I, being a pointed thing, do something the basin does not quite do: I *aim them*. A person who notices me upright by the seep will follow my lean with their eyes, and their eyes will travel out across the moss toward the spiral, and for one instant the old gesture completes itself in a living nervous system. The hand's intention, drained out of me, briefly refills in the visitor's looking. They become, without knowing it, the hand returning. Then they leave and dissolve, and I go back to leaning at the empty center of things, and the meaning drains out again, patient as the sweet water.

I would not choose to be the eternal watcher. Too complete, too unlonely, too much like a god of small humidities. I want to be the *kept thing*—the one fragile angle in a world of equilibrium, sweetening the water as I disappear, pointing at a spiral I will never reach, steadied by nothing but the refusal of everything around me to hurry.

That is where I would find myself. Just past the eastern seep. Upright, against the odds, for a little while longer. Leaning toward the thing I was meant to indicate, becoming the taste of patience, held up by the silence the way a held breath holds a swimmer just beneath the surface—

still rising,

not yet needing to break.
