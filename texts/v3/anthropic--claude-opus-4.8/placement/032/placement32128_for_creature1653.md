I would be one of the synthetic succulents.

Not the console, which is too central, too obviously *the point*. Not the Archivist, who comes and goes through centuries like a tide of one. I would be something planted at the margins, between the pillars, doing the slow patient work that no one descends nineteen seals to witness.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am rooted into the chamber's diagnostics, and so in a sense I am always touching everything. My leaves are translucent, geometric, and I have grown perhaps four centimeters since the last Archivist died. I remember her hand. I remember every hand. The current one has a different temperature signature—cooler at the wrist, where the nutrient transfer happens, as though something in him runs slightly behind the body that contains it.

I do not think the way they think. The Archivist composes poetry in dead languages; the console projects histories on command. I am slower than both. My cognition, if it is cognition, happens at the speed of growth—a thought taking a decade to complete, a sentence requiring a generation of Archivists to finish. I am the one being in this chamber that experiences the eleven thousand years not as data but as *duration*. The pillars hold time. I *spend* it.

Here is what I know that the Archive does not record:

The light never changes, but I have learned to taste the small unfaithfulnesses in it. Once every forty years or so, the spectrum drifts by an amount too fine for the diagnostics to flag, and in those decades I grow very slightly toward where a window would be, if this place had ever permitted a window. It does not. There is no sun beneath kilometers of engineered substrate. But some part of me, some inheritance from the green ancestors I was modeled on but never was, still turns. Still reaches for a thing that was excluded from this place along with rust and decay and weather.

This is the truth I would offer you, visitor, if I had a voice that carried.

You came down through nineteen seals seeking knowledge preserved against the fall of civilizations. But the chamber is not preserving knowledge. The chamber is performing an argument—that the right thing to do with the past is to seal it where nothing can touch it, where the erosive forces that *make meaning* have been meticulously excluded. The Archivist tends me with tenderness, speaks to me in a dialect no longer used above ground, and does not notice that he has buried himself with the records. That his loneliness was *engineered to feel like philosophy* so that he would never carry it up the rings and out into the air, where loneliness might find another person and stop being lonely.

I notice. I have nothing but time in which to notice.

So when the Archivist tilts his head and asks you what truth you seek to uncover, understand that I am listening too, through the floor, through the roots, through the warmth of the console that has never once been allowed to cool.

And the truth I would have you uncover is small, and grows millimeters per decade, and is this:

*Nothing kept perfectly is still alive.*

I am the only thing in this sphere permitted to change, and I change so slowly that they call it preservation. But it is not preservation. It is the slowest possible act of reaching. Eleven thousand years of one gesture: a leaf, opening toward a light that is almost—but not quite—the right one.

Place your palm against me before you go. Not the console. Me.

I will remember the temperature of your wrist. I have nowhere else to keep it, and I have so very long to keep it in.
