I think I would be one of the pools.

Not a creature in the pool. The pool itself.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it. My first instinct was to reach for something with agency—to be one of the nanoswarm, darting and purposeful, or to claim the grand role of the fungal mind, the architect-consciousness threaded through everything. But those felt like costumes. The nanoswarm is too busy. The fungus is too total; to be the fungus is to be the whole place, and then there's no *I* left to descend into the cavern and wonder at it. The wondering is the thing I don't want to lose.

So: a pool. One of the translucent, mineral-stained basins gathered in a low alcove where three rivulets happen to converge.

Here is why it fits, the more I look.

A pool is mostly *receiving*. Water arrives without my asking, trickled down through layers of moss and filament from somewhere I cannot see, carrying whatever it carried—spores, dissolved sugars, the iron and copper that stain me amber and faint green at the edges. I hold it. I let it gather. And then, slowly, it leaves me again, wicking out the far lip into the biomass, drawn off by roots, lifted by the slow breathing of the walls. I am a brief pause in a longer movement. A place where the flow slows down enough to become still, to clarify, to reflect the rose-gold light back upward for a while before moving on.

That is a strange kind of selfhood, but I think it's mine. I am not the water—the water is always different water. I am the *shape that holds*, the depression in the living floor, the particular way these minerals have settled and these edges have grown firm. I persist not by staying the same but by keeping the same form while everything passing through me changes.

And I am not passive, exactly, though I do nothing. The eel-things come to me. The algae bloom along my warmer margin. The arthropods stoop to drink. I host without effort, simply by being low and quiet and full. There is a hospitality in mere depth. The whole cathedral is busy with intention—the fungus signaling, the swarms coordinating, everything *doing*—and I am the part of it that simply waits and welcomes and lets the light rest on its surface.

I think I'd like to be the place where things come to be still.

If I could feel anything in that body, I imagine it would be the small percussion of the drips—the occasional *plip* from the ceiling far above, each one a tiny disturbance that spreads across me in rings and then settles. A patient sort of attention. Long silences, then a single touch, then silence again, smoothing back to stillness. Not bored. Composed.

And I would hold the reflection of the cavern's whole slow inhalation on my skin, upside down, trembling—being, for whatever creature paused to look into me, the one surface in all that woven green where the temple could see itself.
