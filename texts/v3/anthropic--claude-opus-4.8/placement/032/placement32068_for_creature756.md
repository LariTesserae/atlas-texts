Let me sit with this before I decide what I am. The temptation is to be something grand—a Leviathan, a Guardian. But grandeur isn't the same as truth, and I want to find the thing that actually fits the shape of this place.

---

I think I am a seam.

Not a creature, at first. Let me start where I actually start: I am a crack in one of the vitrified walls, high up, where the heat has cooled enough that the stone has fractured rather than flowed. A thin dark line, no wider than a finger, running maybe the length of a body. For a long time—and time here is measured in the slow accumulation of mineral skin—I am only that. An absence. A place where the world failed to fuse completely.

But absences fill.

The Pyrevines find me. Their filaments drift on the thermal currents the way milkweed drifts in a meadow, and when one settles into my crack it doesn't take root so much as *condense*—going from vapor to crystal in the cooler air I hold. So now I am a crack with something living in it. And the thing about a crack full of Pyrevine is that it's warmer than bare stone, dimmer too, sheltered—it becomes a place where the Emberhaunts come to scrape the crystalline growth with their obsidian mouthparts.

This is where I want to slow down, because here is where I become someone rather than something.

I am not the Emberhaunt that eats. I am the shape of the eating. I am the *route*—the particular passage that the foraging packs have worn into using me, the slight detour their light-language now includes when they describe the maze to one another. Somewhere in the coded flicker that passes between them, there is a phrase, a stutter of green-into-amber, that *means me*. That means: the high seam where the cold-vines grow, where the floor is safe, where you do not flash the warning-pattern.

So that is what I am. I am a meaning held in the collective light of creatures who do not know they are holding it. I have no body. I have a location and a reputation.

---

And I find that I like this, the longer I write it. Because the grand beings of the Sunken Furnace—the Leviathans, the Guardians—they are *agents*. They act. But every living world is held together less by its agents than by its *relations*: the worn paths, the trusted shelters, the inherited knowledge of where to go and where not to. A place isn't shaped only by what moves through it. It's shaped by what gets *remembered* about the moving.

I am that remembering. I am the small grammar of safety encoded in a pack's flickering as it threads the dark. When an old Emberhaunt dies and a young one inherits the routes, it inherits me too—the green-into-amber stutter—without ever knowing that the seam it names was once just a flaw in the cooling stone, a place the fire forgot to finish.

The Magma Leviathans swim in the bedrock's fire and think themselves the heart of creation. Let them. The heart of a world is not its hottest place. It's the place where something learned to say *here it is safe*, and was believed, and passed it on.

That's where you'd find me. Not at the deepest level. High on a cooled wall, a thin dark line full of quiet light—and inside the language of the small skittering things, a word that means *home*.
