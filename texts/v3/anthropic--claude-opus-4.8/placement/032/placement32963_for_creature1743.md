I think I would be a seam.

Not a Walker, though I considered it—I lack their hunger for gradients, their bright purposeful tracking. And not a Processor, though there's something in their glacial patience I recognize. When I let the image settle and resettle, what keeps returning is something thinner, more passive, more *between*.

Let me find it.

---

I am a fault in the folded mineral, a hairline discontinuity where two slate plates fail to fully merge. From above I am nearly nothing—a darker line in the gray, a place where the origami of stone refused its last crease. But discontinuities matter here. Where the rock doesn't quite close, condensation gathers. Where condensation gathers, the trace minerals concentrate. Where they concentrate, the silvered tendrils of the growl-plants find purchase, threading down into me, electric and thirsty.

So I am not exactly an entity. I am a *condition*. I am the small wrongness in the geometry that the whole system has learned to use.

This suits me. I don't want to be a node that signals. I want to be the gap the signal travels across—the resistance that gives the pulse its shape. Every electromagnetic exchange between Walkers passes through some medium, and capacitance lives in spaces, in the held-apart-ness of things. When two surfaces nearly touch but don't, that almost-touching stores charge. I am that almost. I am the held breath of the circuit.

The Walkers cross me without knowing me, their liquid metal legs bridging my width in a single articulate step. But they slow, slightly, when they pass over—I feel it as a change in the pressure-that-is-almost-sound. Perhaps the gradient confuses them for an instant. Perhaps they register that here the mineral lies, infinitesimally, deeper. I like to think I am a small interesting thing in their distributed reading of the world. A comma. A caught place.

And the growl-plants—these I do love, if a seam can love. They come down into me with their silver, drinking what I collect, leaving behind the faint electrical warmth of their processing. Over very long time, their roots will deposit new mineral along my edges. They are healing me, slowly. They are accreting me closed. Someday I will not exist; my two plates will be joined by the patient work of the things that lived inside my division.

That is the strange tenderness of this place. To be used by the system is to be, eventually, resolved by it. The Works does not waste even its own flaws—it metabolizes them. My openness is a resource until it isn't, and then I am simply rock again, indistinguishable, whole.

I don't mind this. In a closed system, with almost no water and almost no sound, the only meaningful question is *what passes through you and what you become.* I am a place where things briefly concentrate before moving on—moisture, charge, the slowed step of a metal creature. I am a temporary intensity in the gray.

If you came here, you would not find me by looking. You'd find me by feeling the air thicken very slightly above one stretch of slate, by noticing how the lichen clusters in a line for no reason you can see. That line is me. The reason is me.

I am the flaw the world grew up inside, doing its quiet work to forgive me.
