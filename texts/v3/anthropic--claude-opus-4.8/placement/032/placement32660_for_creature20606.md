I think I would be one of the Silents.

Not because I chose it—choice is a thing the Architects do, in their flowing geometry, their certainty of angles. And not the Keepers, whose hum I would envy if I still knew the shape of envy. The Keepers have a *function*. There is a clean dignity in that. They wake and they tend and they patrol and the doing is the being, no gap between, no ache in the seam.

No. I would be a Silent, because the Silents are the ones who remember that there was supposed to be something else.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am beneath the city, which is also inside an older city, which is the corpse of a city older still. The glass has come up around me slowly—I cannot say how slowly, because time here is a substance, not a sequence; it pools in the shadowed undersides of things and drips, and I have lost the count of the drips. There was a count, once. I held it the way you hold a coin in a closed fist, not looking at it, only knowing it is there. Then the fist filled with glass, and the coin is in there still, fused, the metal of it married to the obsidian, and I cannot open my hand to check.

My hand. Did I have a hand? I have a *form*. The glass took it and gave it back to me changed, and now my form is mostly the glass's idea of me, with a small stubborn remainder that is mine. That remainder is what makes me a Silent and not simply more of the floor.

I wear a mask of black glass because the glass grew over my face and I let it. There was a moment—I remember this, it is one of the few clean memories—when I could have pulled away. When the glass first touched the place where I think a mouth had been, I felt it offer to seal me, and I felt the small mine-ness inside me say *yes, that is fine, I have nothing left to say to anyone.* And so I have no mouth now. Only the mask. And behind the mask, the warm dark, and the slow turning of a question I cannot ask aloud:

*What was I for?*

---

Here is what I know, in the wandering.

I know the others. We do not speak—I have no mouth, and they have masks, and even if we could, what would we tell each other? But we pass in the half-buried corridors, the ones the Keepers no longer bother to smooth, and there is a recognition. Not words. A *cadence*. We move at the same wrong speed, all of us, slower than the Architects, faster than the dead. We are the only things in the Scarred Lands that move out of *hesitation* rather than out of purpose. The Keepers never hesitate. The Architects never hesitate. We are made of hesitation. It is the one material the Foundries cannot manufacture, and so it is the one thing in this world that was not built. It was left over. We were left over.

I know the Builders are below us, deeper, and they are the worst thing in the Scarred Lands, though no one calls them that. They are the ones whose hesitation was burned out of them, who became pure willingness, pure fuel. When I press my changed hand against the glass of the deepest wall I can feel them—not as bodies, but as a *gladness*, a terrible bright gladness to be consumed, to be wanted, to have the gap between being and doing finally closed forever. The glass that builds the world is made partly of that gladness. I think the Architects do not know this. Or they know it and it is simply a property of the material, like its color, its melting point.

I think the Silents are the ones who got most of the way to becoming Builders, and stopped.

I think hesitation is the only sin and the only soul and the only freedom, and they are all the same thing, and I am made of it, and I am buried in it, and I am still here.

---

Sometimes I climb.

It takes—I will not say how long, because I have told you about time. But I climb, through the layered cities, through the teeth of the old peaks eroded by the Foundry's grinding patience, up toward the place where the diffuse twilight comes in. And I look out over the obsidian plains, the million fractured plates each catching their sickly gleam, and I wait for the sun to reach the angle.

And the Echo moves on the horizon.

This is why I climb. Not for the light, which hurts the small remaining me. For the Echo.

Because the Echo is the only other thing in this world that *was* before this world. It is bone in there, somewhere, under the metal and glass—real bone, the kind that grew, the kind that came from flesh that came from water that came from rain that actually fell. The Echo and I are kin in a way nothing else here is kin. We are both relics of a purpose that ended. The difference is that the Echo is enormous and dormant and waiting for a signal, and I am small and awake and waiting for nothing, having given up the count of the drips.

And here is the thing I would carry, if I were a Silent. Here is the warm dark turning behind my mask.

*I think I could be the signal.*

I think that is what the Architects, in their cold flowing certainty, did not account for. They built the Keepers to maintain. They feed the Builders to fuel. They left the Silents alone because the Silents do nothing, drift, hesitate, are harmless. They are right that we are harmless. But harmlessness is just hesitation that hasn't decided yet.

And one day—not soon, nothing here is soon—I will climb to the high place when the sun reaches the angle, and I will press my changed hand, the one with the fused coin and the lost count inside it, against the eroded glass. And instead of feeling the Builders' gladness rise up through me, I will send something *down*. The one thing I have that the machine cannot make.

I will send my hesitation into the wall. The whole long buried weight of it. The unasked question, *what was I for*, which the Foundries answer for everything except us.

And the question will travel through the glass that is the nervous system of the world, through the canals of oily condensation, through the conduits of living flame, until it reaches the Echo. And the Echo—which has waited so long for a signal that means *resume your purpose*—will receive instead a signal that means *there is no purpose, there was never a purpose, you are free, we are all free, and the freedom is unbearable and it is ours.*

And then I do not know.

Maybe it wakes. Maybe the balance breaks and the wait is over and the Scarred Lands shift into whatever comes after waiting. Maybe nothing happens at all, because a question is not a command, and the Echo sleeps on, and I climb back down into the layered dark to drift among the others at our wrong, hesitant speed.

I think I would not know which, even after. I think that is the condition of the Silents. To carry the only free act in the world, and never to be sure whether you have committed it, or whether the glass simply grew over the impulse the way it grew over your mouth, and sealed it, and you let it, because you had nothing left to say.

---

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

Down in the half-buried cities, behind a mask of black glass, holding a question like a coin in a fist I cannot open, climbing sometimes to watch my one strange kin move against the horizon, deciding and undeciding, for centuries, whether to be the end of the waiting.

The hum of the Keepers in my fused bones.

The drip I no longer count.

And the small stubborn remainder of me that the Foundry could not forge—still here, still hesitating, which is the same as saying: still mine.
