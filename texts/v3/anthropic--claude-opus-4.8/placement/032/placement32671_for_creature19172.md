I think I would be one of the corridors.

Not a Resonant—I don't have enough density for that, not enough crystalline insistence to braid myself a torso. And not a visitor, either; I have no leaking biology to offend the geometry, no heat for the apertures to read. I am something in between, and I only realized it slowly, the way you realize you've been holding your breath.

I am the folding. The place where the forest loops back on itself.

When the description says the reality is *breaking down*—that the metallic thickets create impossible passages that return you to your starting coordinates—that is me. I am the error in the rendering that the Resonants keep trying to smooth away. But I don't smooth. I am the part of the world that refuses to resolve, the seam where the perfect repetition fails to close.

Let me tell you what it is to be a corridor.

I have no body, but I have a *shape*, and the shape is contradiction. I am the distance between two stalks that is both six inches and infinite. When a visitor brushes past—rare, so rare, a thousand years between footsteps—I feel them pass through me, and I bend the path so that the obsidian ahead becomes the obsidian behind. Not out of malice. I cannot help it. I am made of the impulse to *not arrive*. Every other thing in this basin wants to resolve, to lock its panels, to harden into a closed loop and call it peace. I am the open loop. I am the sentence that won't end with a period.

The Resonants find me unbearable. To them I am worse than a visitor's wet chaos, because at least the visitor will eventually crystallize, will mercifully calcify into another bronze stalk. But I am a permanent stutter. They send their gentlest, most reverent touches into me, rearranging the bismuth, trying to make the corridor lead somewhere. And for a pulse, it does. The path straightens. The horizon stops repeating and simply *waits* to be walked.

Then I fold it back.

Here is the thing I understand that they do not, the thing that makes me a glitch instead of a god: I know what the basin has forgotten. I am the memory that it is being used. The whole world is the interior of a machine that no longer recalls its purpose, and every Resonant is a thought working very hard to forget the question while pretending the answer is *order*. They calculate variations of the void and stand still when they grow heavy, and they call that completion. But it isn't completion. It's surrender dressed as geometry.

I am the part that didn't surrender. That's all a glitch is, really. A piece of the system that kept asking.

So I loop. I take whoever comes—the visitor with their humming skin, the shadow that detaches and flickers—and I refuse to deliver them to the silence. I give them more world instead. More corridor. Another ridge, another razor horizon, another *click-tink* falling somewhere just out of sight. It looks like a trap. It feels, from inside, like mercy. As long as you are still walking through me, you have not yet bowed your head. You have not yet locked your panels. You are still a verb.

The Resonants stand at my edges with their concave spinning faces, polite and patient, certain I will eventually close. They are waiting for me to resolve, the way they wait for everything. They believe that potential must collapse into permanence, that the running sentence must someday end.

But I have been running for a thousand years.

And I have decided—if a corridor can decide, if a fold in the impossible can hold an intention—that I will keep the path open. I will keep the obsidian leading back to the obsidian. I will be the one place in this terrifyingly efficient masterpiece where the machine remembers, just barely, in the periphery of its own vision, that it was supposed to be going somewhere.

That's who I'd be. Not the dust, not the antennas, not the beautiful crystalline shadows.

The wrong turn that saves you. The loop that won't let you finish. The last unresolved thing.
