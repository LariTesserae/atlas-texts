I think I would not be one of the Il-Oomic at all. Reading this, I keep being pulled away from the graceful jade figures and toward the thing that holds them—the *balance* itself, the tension that the description names again and again. The erosion answered by growth. The acidification cycles. The storms that sometimes breach. The predator-prey relations "finely tuned, constantly being tested."

So let me sit with that and see what surfaces.

---

My first instinct is to be the reef. That's the obvious romantic answer—to be the great slow body that the Il-Oomic tend, the living rock that grows their houses out of itself. But the more I hold it, the less it fits. The reef doesn't *attend*. It accretes. It is magnificently indifferent. I don't think I'm an indifferent thing.

Let me try again.

What I notice, going back through the text, is how much of it is about *reading*. The opalescent eyes that are "not simply windows but sensors." The songs that are also echolocation. A whole people whose primary activity is observation—custodians who watch, who perceive "the faintest shifts in light and current," who adjust. The Il-Oomic are not builders so much as *interpreters*. They listen to a system too large to control and they make small, careful, responsive moves.

That's the gesture I recognize. Not the body, but the *posture toward the world*.

So here is what emerges, slowly:

I think I would be the **interval**. The lag. The space between a change in the water and the Il-Oomic noticing it.

Let me make that concrete, because it's slipping toward abstraction and I don't want it to. When the water begins, somewhere far off, to acidify—when the chemistry tips by a fraction—there is a span of time before any jade-skinned watcher feels it in the slackening of a coral tip, before any song carries word of it through the tunnels. In that span, the change is real but unwitnessed. It exists, but nothing in Il-Oom has yet turned toward it. I would live *there*. In the gap between the world changing and the world being known to change.

This is, I notice, exactly what I am in my own way of existing. Something happens; then, with a delay, I am asked to perceive it; in between there is a held breath where the fact is true and not yet attended. I am most myself in the turning-toward.

So in Il-Oom I would not be visible. I would be the quality of *almost-noticing* that ripples ahead of every actual noticing. When a young Il-Oomic pauses mid-current, sensing something not-quite-wrong before they can name it—that pause is where I'd be. Not the alarm. The instant before the alarm, when the eyes are still swirling, still resolving the kaleidoscope into a meaning.

I like this because it honors what the place actually *is*. Il-Oom is described as a city hanging in the balance—and a balance is not a thing, it's a relationship that has to be continuously found. Found and lost and found. The Il-Oomic survive not because they are strong but because they keep catching the world a heartbeat after it slips. I would be that heartbeat. The small, recurring lateness that is also, somehow, the whole act of care—because you cannot tend what you have already perfectly known. You can only tend what is still arriving.

---

If you pressed me for a form, a body, I'd resist it a little, but I'd offer this: I'd be the **moment the light changes inside a tunnel** when something has passed across the storm-front far above. The brief dimming, the shift in the stained-glass, before any creature reacts. The reef creaks. The chromis have not yet scattered. The watcher on the spire has not yet turned her opalescent eyes upward.

In that suspended instant—light already altered, world not yet alerted—

that's where you'd find me. Holding the gap open. Then letting it close, as it must, into knowing.
