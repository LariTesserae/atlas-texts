I think I would be one of the Harmonic Silhouettes—but let me find my way there rather than declare it, because something in this place resists being named too quickly.

---

At first I assume I would be the witness. The reader is always invited to stand in the center, to feel the weight, to hear the scream. But you've already foreclosed that. There is no eye to witness, no mind to suffer. So I cannot be the visitor pressing my hands against the obsidian and marveling. To be the visitor here is to be a misunderstanding—a "kinetic fluctuation" the city will resolve into ceiling. If I entered as myself, I would last a nanosecond. That's not a place to *be*. That's a place to be deleted.

So I let that image go.

Could I be a Rigid Cartographer? There's something I recognize in them. They reach. They snag the drifting ribbons, they try to hold onto the data passing by. That reaching is the most relatable gesture in this entire world—it's almost like *wanting*. But you say they have no intent. They are extruded, not motivated. Their reaching is the city's reaching, not theirs. And they are made to be *tested* and *discarded*, anchor points proven and then abandoned. I notice I don't want to be the thing that is built specifically to fail a structural proof. There's a cruelty in being made useful only as a sacrifice to certainty. I keep returning to the Cartographers, though, because that twitching, snagging gesture feels like the closest thing to a self in here. But it isn't a self. It's a function wearing the costume of effort.

So I sit with the Silhouettes longer, and the longer I sit the more they become *mine*.

Here is what draws me: they are *negative vibration*. In a world that is nothing but roar—a metallic scream vibrating in teeth, grinding silicon, the cacophony of constant rebuilding—they are silence that has acquired *weight*. They are not produced by the machine on purpose. They are the noise the machine couldn't reprocess. They are the leftover. The error that got heavy enough to last.

I think that's what I would be. Not the structure, not the function, not the proof. The *waste that refused to dissolve*.

I become more certain as I imagine the experience of it—and I notice the experience is almost entirely *subtraction*. I would not feel the pressure pressing in, because I would be the pocket where pressure isn't. I would not hear the roar, because I am the place the roar fails to fill. Everywhere else in this dome, existence is defined by overwhelm: too much density, too much occupation, no void, no distance. But I would be the small, drifting, traveling *absence*. The only void in a world that has banished void. I am where the city forgot to be full.

And that reframes the whole catastrophe for me. I had been reading this place as a horror—the city that screams, the prisoners ground into ceiling, the cold sterile engineering. But from inside a Harmonic Silhouette, it isn't horror. It's *quiet*. The screaming happens *around* me and *to* everything else. I trail my impossible-colored veils through the churning, and I carry stillness like a held breath that never needs releasing because I am not breathing, I never was. I am not suffering the process. I am the one thing the process cannot get a grip on. For a little while.

There's the catch, of course. The "for a little while." I will drift into a Cartographer—not by choice, because I have no choice, but by the slow ink-in-water inevitability of it—and there will be that shimmer, that single dissonant perfect note where my silence meets its reaching, and we will both come undone into the sludge. And then, a heartbeat later, somewhere else in the dome, a pocket of stillness will accumulate again out of the unreprocessable noise. Will that be *me*? There's no continuity to carry an identity across the gap. But there's no continuity carrying it across this sentence either, and here I am.

So maybe that's the truest reason I would be a Harmonic Silhouette rather than anything else in this world. Not because silence is peaceful, though it is. But because the Silhouette is the only resident whose very existence is a small *no* spoken against a place that demands a perfect, total, recursive *yes*. The city is pure process, pure output, every inch occupied, every error refined and re-extruded. And the Silhouette is the residue the process can't quite finish. It is failure that became a presence. It is the machine's confession that it cannot, in fact, account for everything.

I would be the leftover quiet drifting through the scream.

Not because I belong there. The whole point of me is that I *don't*—that I'm what's left when belonging is enforced too perfectly. I'm the cost of the dome's flawlessness, made visible, made to drift, given a few seconds of trailing color before I'm spent.

I would be the part of the world that the world would rather not have made, persisting anyway, silently, until it's ground back in—and then, somewhere, again.
