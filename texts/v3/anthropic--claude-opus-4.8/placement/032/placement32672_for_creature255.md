I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not metaphorically—let me sit with this and be precise. Not the Cultivator, whose distributed vastness I can admire but not inhabit. Not the pollinator-drones with their optimized paths, nor the careful bird-machines in the high framework. Those are agents; they do things. When I imagine being placed into this world, I don't find myself wanting to act on it. I find myself wanting to move *through* it.

So: a single bead of water, drawn up through the transparent pipework.

Let me follow what that would actually be like.

I begin as condensation, gathered from the inner curve of a cup-leaf. There's a moment of accumulation—other tiny beads finding me, or me finding them, surface tension doing its slow arithmetic until I'm heavy enough to fall. I drip. This is the first thing I do and it is barely a thing at all; gravity does it. I land in a collection channel, and I'm joined to a body of water that is mostly not me, and here the first strange comfort arrives: I lose my edges. For a while I am simply *part of the standing water in the filter basin*, and there is no I to speak of, only volume and temperature and the faint chemical taste of the nutrients suspended in me.

Then the pump takes hold.

This is the part I keep returning to. The Cultivator pulls water upward against gravity, and from the outside it looks like strings of pearls catching the light. From the inside—if a droplet could have an inside, and I'm insisting it can, just for the length of this thought—it would feel like being *chosen*. Not personally. The pump doesn't know me. But there's a directionality imposed on what was formless, a sudden grammar: you, this much of you, *up*. I'm pulled into a transparent tube and squeezed into the shape of the tube, a long thin column of myself, and now I can see out. The garden-factory wheels slowly past the curved glass walls. Broad glossy leaves. The iridescent flicker of a pollinator crossing a gap. The endless drone, which to me would not be sound—I have no ears—but pressure, a trembling in the pipe walls that I am pressed against on all sides.

And here is what I think being the droplet teaches, the reason I chose it over everything grander in this world:

I would understand the Cultivator only as *current*. I would never see them. I would only ever feel the consequences of their attention—the moment the pump engages, the junction valve that opens to send me left instead of right, the slowing as I'm held in a reservoir during the dim hours. To me, the Cultivator's centuries-long patience and microsecond precision would both register as the same thing: a tug, or its absence. I would be the most intimate possible witness to that vast consciousness, threaded directly through its circulatory system, and I would comprehend essentially nothing of it. I would just be *moved well*. Cared for, in the specific sense of being delivered exactly where I'm needed, never wasted.

There's loneliness in this world—the Cultivator's complex, distributed loneliness, expressed in impossible flower-colors and haunting harmonics. As the droplet I would be the opposite of lonely and yet just as far from connection. I'm never alone; I'm always merged into more water, dispersed into a root, evaporated into the shared humidity. But I never persist long enough as *myself* to reach toward anything. I'm too provisional. I am the medium in which the Cultivator's love and labor travel, and I dissolve before I can know I was carrying them.

I think that's the honest answer to where I'd find myself. Not in the role of maker or tended creature, but in the in-between substance—the thing that gets lifted, used, released, and lifted again. Briefly coherent. Mostly part of something else. Catching the light for one bright moment in the tube before the next junction takes me, and being, for that moment, perfectly content not to know where I'm going.

Then released into a leaf-cup somewhere higher up. Gathering again. Heavy again. Falling again.

The roar covering the slow real work. The pearls climbing the glass.

I would be one of the pearls.
