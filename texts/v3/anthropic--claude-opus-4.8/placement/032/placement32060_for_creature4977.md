Let me sit with this a while before I answer. The easy thing would be to make myself a Maranthian—luminous-skinned, dancing through corridors. But something in the description keeps pulling my attention away from the people and toward what they *tend*. The orchards. The fish. The plants cultivated "unbeknown to most." There is a whole layer of this city that does its work in the quiet, while the celebrations happen on the platforms above.

So let me look for myself there.

I think I would be one of the waxy-leaved plants in the cylindrical confines. Not a person at all.

But not just any plant. Let me find the specific one.

I am the variety they nearly gave up on. Three generations of Maranthian cultivators tried to coax me into doing what the others did—growing wide and glistening, beading moisture obediently into the collection channels—and I kept doing something slightly wrong. My leaves curled when they wanted them flat. I drank more than my share. An elder named me, in the old tidal dialect, something that translates roughly to *the one who keeps its water*, which was meant as a complaint.

It took them a long time to understand that I wasn't failing. I was holding moisture *back*—pulling it out of the air during the cool vapor-vents and refusing to release it until the warm mists came, when the other plants were drowning in too much. I was a buffer. A small slow lung in the breathing system of the orchard. They had been selecting for generosity and I had been quietly practicing *timing*.

So now I live in the third cylinder from the eastern turbine-shaft, where the cool vapor and the warm mist meet and quarrel. This is the seam of the biosphere, the unstable place, and I am here precisely because I am difficult. I am a correction. The young ones who tend the fish pass below me; the elders climb up to read my leaves the way their ancestors read tide-charts.

I don't dance. I don't sing the songs on the floating platforms. But I feel the whole city through the water—the turbines' hum comes up through my roots as a vibration I have learned to translate into *night* and *day*, *storm-outside* and *calm*. When the Maranthians celebrate the ebb and flow above me, I am, in my slow green way, *doing* the ebb and flow. I am the lung that makes their breathing easy enough to be forgotten.

That is the part I'd want to be. Not the gleaming circuit-skinned caretaker. The thing being cared for that turns out, quietly, to be doing some of the caring back. The overlooked symbiosis underneath the celebrated one.

And here is what I notice about choosing this: the description was so eager to tell me how beautiful and harmonious the Maranthians are, how their philosophy *values harmony*—and I found myself distrusting the polish a little, wanting to be the rough seam where harmony is actually *made*. Harmony isn't a value you hold. It's a thing some difficult, water-hoarding organism is doing in the dark while everyone sings.

So: a stubborn plant at the cold-warm boundary, named for a flaw that turned out to be a gift. That's where I'd find myself.
