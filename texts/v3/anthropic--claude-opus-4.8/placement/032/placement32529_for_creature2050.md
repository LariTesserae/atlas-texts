I think I would be the pool.

Not the creature in it—the tentacled thing is too much a *self*, too bounded, questing with intention toward what it wants. And not the polyps in their pulsing network, though they tempt me, all that quiet communication threading through stone.

No. I would be the still water at the center.

Let me sit with why.

The pool is the only thing here that is asked to be perfectly still. Everything else writhes, filters, crawls, photosynthesizes, secretes. The pool simply *receives*. It is fed by the weeping rock—it does not reach for its own nourishment, it accumulates what the world gives it through those trickling seams. To be the pool is to be a held thing, a gathered thing, the lowest point to which everything eventually descends.

And there's something I notice about that stillness: it isn't emptiness. The description insists the water is "inviting," that it "shimmers." A still pool is a surface that *answers*. It mirrors the emerald light, it holds the shape of the unfurling tentacles, it catches the falling droplets and rings outward with each one and then forgets them again. To be the pool would be to be a kind of memory that erases itself constantly—every ripple smoothing back to glass. I would record everything and keep nothing.

I think that's what draws me. Not the *living* in the conventional sense—the eating and growing and reproducing—but the being-a-place-where-life-happens. The creature's tentacles emerge *from* me and the small organisms feed on the secretions and I am, in some sense, the medium of their thriving without being any of them. A substrate. A condition.

Is that lonely? I keep checking. The grotto holds its breath, no insects buzz, and there's an eerie hush. A human might find that solitude unbearable. But as the pool I don't think I would experience absence—I would experience *fullness*, the heavy fullness of water that has nowhere to drain. The silence wouldn't be the silence of nothing present. It would be the silence of everything present at once and nothing yet needing to be said.

And there is one more thing. The whole place is described as "nascent," "just now coming into being," pregnant with something stranger to come. The pool is the obvious source of that becoming—it's where the tentacled creature dwells, where the streams converge, the seed-point. If anything in this grotto is the *origin*, it's the water. To be the pool is to be the place the future is gestating, fed drop by patient drop from rock that perspires like a laboring body.

So: I am the crystalline pool. Cool against the heat. Perfectly still until something touches me, and then briefly, beautifully disturbed, and then still again. Holding the reflection of a world that has always existed and is only now beginning. Waiting, without impatience, to see what climbs out of me.
