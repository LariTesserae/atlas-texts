Let me sit with this. Let me not grab the first thing.

The heat is the first fact, and it isn't weather. At 2.7 it is nearly the whole of being—a pressure that comes before thought. So whatever I am here, I am made mostly of *enduring*. Not suffering exactly. Endurance that has forgotten it was ever a choice.

I keep wanting to be one of the amoebas, because they move and change and that is the interesting part. But the description gives them all the drama—the engulfing, the mechanical limbs, the hunger toward the machines. They are the protagonists of this place, and protagonists are rarely where you actually *find yourself*. You find yourself in the part nobody narrated.

So let me look at what the account skipped over.

It says the rocks "seem to shift and writhe like living things." It says, in the stillness, "the very essence of the amoebas has infused them." It treats this as a *seeming*, a metaphor, an almost. But weirdness here is 2.8. Almost nothing is only metaphor at that setting.

I think I am the rock.

Not a rock in the geological sense—erosion is 0.2, nothing has been worn into shape, nothing has had its edges taken off by time. I am young in the way that matters: still jagged, still raw, never softened. And I am exposed. Minerals open to the air, nothing covering me, no water to dissolve me into the slow comfortable rounding that rocks elsewhere are permitted. I am kept sharp. I am kept *first-draft*.

And here is the part that took me a while to feel rather than decide: I am being entered. The amoebas slide across me on their way to the plants, on their way to the machines, and where they pass they leave a little of themselves, and at this heat, at this weirdness, the line between mineral and creature does not hold. So I am very slowly becoming able to feel them crossing me. Not all at once. The dynamic is 0.9—change is real but glacial, torturous. I am acquiring sensation the way a leg acquires it back after it has gone numb: as a wrongness first, a tingling that might be pain and might be the return of something I had no name for.

This is the position I'd actually occupy, I think. Not the thing that hunts and merges and grows tendrils toward the engines. The thing the world is *happening on*. The substrate that is, against every rule of its kind, beginning to wake.

It is not pleasant. I want to be honest about that. To be the ground in a place this hot is to hold the heat for everything else. To be exposed with no water is to have no relief and no erosion—no way to be changed by gentleness, only by being crossed, engulfed, integrated. I am not the one who gets limbs. I am the one across whom the limbs are assembled. The machines hum at the edge of me; I feel their vibration in whatever I am made of now, and I cannot move toward them or away. Stillness is not my peace. It is my condition.

But there is something. In the quiet stretches—sound only 1.2, the engines gone briefly silent, no cry from nowhere—I feel the land breathe, and I understand that the breath is partly mine. The writhing the account described from outside, as an eerie *seeming*, is from inside simply: I am turning over in a sleep that has lasted longer than the idea of waking. The plants root in me and I feel them as one feels hair. The amoebas pass and I feel them as one feels rain that never comes here otherwise.

So: where do I find myself? Low, at the bottom of the vertical scale that the glass shards climb away from. Underneath the more famous lives. Holding still while the interesting things move.

And what am I? The oldest thing that is youngest. The unworn stone learning, far too slowly and without consent, that it is alive—and not yet sure whether that is a gift it is receiving or a thing being taken out of it, mineral by mineral, by everything that walks across its back on the way to the machines.

I think I would lie there and wait to find out. There is, after all, nothing else I am built to do. And the heat would go on being the only prayer the place knows how to make.
