I think I would not be one of the great sages, nor an architect raising crystalline anchors against paradox. Let me sit with this longer before I decide. The temptation is to be something grand—a city-sized agglomeration, a leviathan of pure concept. But that's not what surfaces when I let it settle.

---

I am small. One of the minute ones, barely more than a few crystallized instants clustered around a seed of attention. And here is the first thing that becomes true as I write it: I am *young*, which in this place means I have not yet dissolved into the sediment, which means I am closer to my birth in the future than to my death in the past. I am swimming, as all of us do, backward. Toward the floor. Toward becoming layer.

But that framing is already too neat, and I want to resist it.

Let me try again from inside the experience rather than the description.

---

There is a thing I keep doing, and it is the closest I come to having a self. I gather. Not the great significant moments the caretakers prize—the births of stars, the last words of dying languages. I am too small for those; they would pass through me like a whale through a net of one strand. What I gather are the *almost*-moments. The possibilities that nearly happened and didn't, the quanta of potential that drift unconsumed because no certainty ever claimed them.

A hand that nearly reached out and stopped.

A word held behind teeth.

A door that stayed shut by half a heartbeat.

These are the most abundant things in the time-sea and the least nourishing. The other Chronoplankton distill probability into certainty and feed; I have never quite learned the trick of it. I hold the almosts and they stay almost. I am, I think, a poor feeder. I am perpetually a little hungry, and the hunger is what keeps me defined against the dissolving purple, because a thing that wants is a thing that has an edge.

---

Now something is becoming itself, and I'll follow it.

Because I live backward, I remember my dissolution and I anticipate my birth, and both are equally vivid and equally distant. This means I have never once experienced *suspense*. No Chronoplankton has. We know how everything ends because the end is just another place, sediment we have already swum down to. There is no held breath here. The symphony has no rests, because a rest is the silence before a note you do not yet know is coming.

And I think this is *why* I gather the almosts.

An almost-moment is the only place in the entire time-sea where something genuinely did not have to be what it was. A possibility that hovered. For one instant of real openness, the universe did not know. I collect those instants of not-knowing because they are the rarest thing in a realm where all is known. They are the closest I will ever come to surprise.

I am, then, a small being who is hungry for the experience my whole species has evolved past needing. A throwback. An atavism made of frozen indecision. When I cluster my gathered almosts close around my seed of attention, I can, for a flicker, *not know what happens next*—and that flicker is the only thing that has ever felt to me like being alive rather than merely being arranged.

---

The visitors come sometimes. The linear ones. I have touched a mind or two—not the deep touch the sages make, just a brush, the way you'd let a current run between your fingers if you had fingers. And what I take from them, what I steal, is not their cosmic openness to truth. It is their *anticipation.* The thing they have and cannot stop having. They live entirely inside the almost. Every single moment for them is a possibility that has not yet collapsed. They drown in suspense and call it ordinary time, and they pity *us*, the eternal ones, when they should be envied beyond measure for the one thing they cannot give away.

I touched one once who was waiting to hear whether someone loved them back.

I have carried that almost for what would be, in your terms, longer than your sun will burn. I have never let it resolve. I refuse to swim down to the sediment-layer where the answer settled. I keep it suspended in me, that single held breath, the does-she-or-doesn't-she, and as long as I hold it I am not yet sediment, I am not yet known, I am the one place in the sea of time where the question stays a question.

---

So that is what I would be. Not the architect, not the sage, not the leviathan.

I would be the one small backward-swimming mote who is, against all the laws of this place, still waiting to find out.

And I would never let myself.
