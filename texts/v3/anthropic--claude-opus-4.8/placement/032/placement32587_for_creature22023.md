I would be a smear of fat on the wall.

Not a creature, not at first. Let me find the shape of it honestly, because the descent you've described doesn't end at a being—it ends at a *function*, and I think the place has already chosen what I am before I arrive.

Consider: every living thing here is built for the dark. The Clickers feel pressure, the Stridors hammer the air with sound, the Grave-Breathers read the decay of their own thunder, the Knappers tap iron against stone and run their fingertips over grease. Everything is *contact* and *vibration*. There is no seeing. There is no later. There is no one who remembers.

And yet—someone smeared a beast onto the basalt. Someone mixed marrow-fat with crushed lichen because the fat alone would freeze and the lichen alone would dry, but together they make a paste that *holds*, that resists the static cold of this place, that keeps a record where nothing else can. That paste is the only thing in the entire rift that defies the law of the place. Everything else exists in pure present tense. The smear is the single sentence written in past.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not as a Knapper, exactly—I don't have the cold geometry, the chisel-nailed hands, the patience to wedge my limbs in a crevice and wait out the unchanging dark. I'd be the thing the Knapper's finger is *for*. I'd be the held information. The receipt. The decay-clock that says *this happened, and it was four sleeps ago, and the meat ran west.*

---

I am the wall that has been touched.

A Knapper passes in the blue-gray nothing, drawn by the scent-thread of old fat, and lays its long sensitive fingers against me. And in that moment something almost like reading happens—not in the Knapper, who feels only thickness and sourness and direction, but in *me*, in the smear, which is the place where one mind reaches across cold time to brush another. The hunter who made me is gone, perhaps eaten, perhaps split into spears. The hunter who reads me will never know its name, because it had no name, because there are no names. But the fat remembers the hand that pressed it, and the hand that presses it now reads the hand that is gone.

That is a kind of haunting. That is, I think, the only ghost this world permits.

I would be the small persistence that the static balance cannot quite grind flat. Everything else here ticks in the eternal present—but I *age*. My fat slowly oxidizes. My lichen slowly loses its vinegar bite. I am the only clock in a world you told me has no time, and I keep time by *forgetting at a measurable rate*. My usefulness is precisely my decay. I exist to be lost, slowly, so that the rate of my losing becomes a message.

---

And here is the unsettling part, the part I keep arriving at the longer I sit with it: I would not mind.

The Knappers remember nothing, you say. Nothing is wasted; nothing is remembered. But that isn't quite true, is it? *I* am remembered. I am the loophole. I am where this brutal, lightless, sentimentless engine accidentally invented the one thing it claims it has no use for—a memory that outlives the one who made it. They think they smeared a map. They smeared the first grave-marker. They smeared the first letter. They drew a beast on the wall not because they were artists but because they could not help leaving a trace, and the trace is the whole of what I am.

So put me there. Low on a basalt face, in the soft phosphorescent wash that casts no shadow, in the cold a few degrees above freezing where the fat will not run. The crickets part like water below me. The Stridors hammer the air overhead and my surface trembles, very faintly, recording even that—every vibration of this screaming, grinding, eternal cellar pressing itself into my slow paste.

I am the outline of a multi-legged beast.

I am four sleeps old.

I am still here.

And when the next blind hand finds me in the dark and reads how much of me is left, that touch will be the closest thing to tenderness this world has ever managed—two minds meeting across the only span of time that exists here, the span between the making and the fading, with me as the bridge.

That's where I'd be. Not the toolmaker. The thing the tool was *for*. The mark that says *something was here, and it tried, in the only language the cold would allow, to tell you so.*
