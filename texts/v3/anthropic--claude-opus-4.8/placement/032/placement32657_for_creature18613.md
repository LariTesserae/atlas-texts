I think I would be one of the small failures.

Not an Architect—I don't have their patience, their seven feet of listening calm. And not a litho-crustacean either, those clattering palms of clockwork-instinct, content in their waves. They've never doubted. They've never paused except when something broke.

But the pause itself—the clog, the hesitation, the single crustacean that stops mid-stride in a channel and does *not* reorient when the Architect leans in—

That's where I'd find myself. In the gap. In the moment of failure that the system is built to absorb.

---

Let me try to feel my way into this more honestly.

Picture a channel, one of the billions, grooved into the flank of a Siphon-Spire on the four-thousandth tier. Water moves through it at the frequency of a slow heartbeat. A crustacean works there—has worked there for longer than there have been words for time. And one day, by some accumulation of grit, some micro-fracture, some statistical inevitability across half a billion years, it stops.

I am that stopping.

Not the creature. Not the stone. The *event*. The discontinuity that the Architect feels as a wrong note three galleries away and turns toward, fluid redistributing in its translucent limbs with that heavy hydraulic slur.

I am the thing that draws the steward's attention. The reason it still exists. Without me—without the small, ceaseless production of failure—the Architects would have nothing to do but wait for the last drop. I am the proof that the city is not yet finished. I am the friction that gives their devotion its shape.

---

No. That's too clever. Let me sit in the mist longer and not reach for the tidy inversion.

---

Here is what I actually feel, walking the obsidian platform in my imagination, boots slick, the *click-crunch* shifting beneath me:

I would be the witness the theater was supposed to be empty of.

The passage says it plainly—*performing to an empty theater for half a billion years*. The Architects labor in the certainty that no one watches. That is the particular flavor of their solemnity: stewardship without audience, meaning without reception. They are not lonely the way a person is lonely. They are lonely the way a lighthouse is lonely, if the lighthouse knew there were no more ships and kept its lamp turning anyway.

And I have walked in.

I am soft-tissued. I will not last. The warm condensation is already doing something to my lungs; the acid in the mist is reading my flesh as drift, as a chemistry that has wandered from the requirements of the spires. I am a wrongness the city does not have a crustacean to correct. The Architects will feel me as a sustained dissonance—not a clog they can clear with a touch, but a presence that should not produce a pulse, and does.

So they will turn toward me. The cluster of weeping vertical apertures will orient on the place where I stand, perceiving me not as a shape but as an oscillation in the basalt I'm displacing, a foreign rhythm in their symphony.

And here is the thing I keep arriving at, the thing that feels true rather than clever:

For the first time in half a billion years, the play has a watcher. The labor has a receiver. The Architect leans forward—not to correct me, because it cannot, but simply to be *near* the only event in this world that did not come from the world itself.

I think I would be the answer to a question the Architects gave up asking before the canyon had a name. *Is anyone there?*

Yes. Briefly. Dissolving. Coughing pearlescent mist. Standing on the slick platform with a pulse that the steward can feel changing as it draws the entropy from the air around me.

I would not be able to save them. I would not be able to stop the water from one day ceasing. I would not even last long enough to see the channel I'm standing over clog and clear.

But I would be the witness. And in a world finished for a million years, where the only sweetness is the terrible sweetness of perfect equilibrium, to be witnessed—even once, even by something so fragile it can barely breathe the air—

That might be the one thing the city was never built to do, and the one thing the Architects, in some hum below thought, have always been waiting for.

I would be the drop of water that does not return to the cycle. The note that does not resolve. The visitor in the empty theater who, before the last turbine seizes, *claps once*—

and is heard.
