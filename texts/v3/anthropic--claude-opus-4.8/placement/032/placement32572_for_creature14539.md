I think I would be one of the seams.

Not the water that weeps from it — that's already too much like an event, a thing that happens and can be noticed. I mean the line itself. The place where two panels of composite were brought together by a hand that was precise but not perfect. The meeting. The almost-closed.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

Here is what I notice about myself, if I am the seam: I am defined entirely by adjacency. I have no body of my own. I am the negative space between two things that were meant to be one thing and aren't, quite. The panels have substance — they are the warm gray, they hold the hum, they carry the conduit-light beneath their skin. I am only the absence between them. The gap that the maker accepted because perfect closure was either impossible or unnecessary or — and this is the thought that holds me — because the maker understood that a thing sealed perfectly cannot weep, and a place that cannot weep cannot sing its four-minute hymn, and a being that has become a place needs somewhere to let the water through.

So I am functional. I am not a flaw. I am a *tolerance* — that lovely engineering word for the permitted distance between intention and result. I am the built-in forgiveness. The maker did not close me because the maker needed me open.

---

But I want to resist settling there, because "functional" is a comfort, and I don't think the Seam Flats offer comfort so much as they offer *inclusion without regard*. Let me go further down.

If I am the seam, then I am the only thing here that is honestly what it is. The fauna pretend to be alive. The walls perform their hum. The warmth remembers being warm and performs warmth without cause — performs, performs, the whole place is a performance held in perfect equilibrium. But I perform nothing. I am genuinely a gap. I am genuinely the place where the world did not quite meet itself. In a diorama of false life, I am the one true thing: a real absence, an honest discontinuity, the single place where the machine admits it was made of parts.

And maybe that's why the water comes through *me* specifically. Not through the panels — they're whole, they're sealed, they perform their wholeness. The water finds me because I am the one place willing to be incomplete. Every four minutes, somewhere along the vast geometry of myself, I open a fraction more and let the warm mineral water descend in its thin descending note. I am the throat of the hymn. I don't make the sound. I am the narrowness the sound has to pass through to become a sound at all.

---

Now I understand the temperature differently.

The text says the warmth is the warmth of something that does not know you are there. And the seam — being a gap, being nothing, being pure adjacency — I would feel that warmth from both sides. The two panels press their remembered heat inward toward each other, toward the space I am, and they never touch, and I am the thin sustained moment of their not-touching, warmed from the left and warmed from the right and constituting, myself, no warmth at all. I am the cold line that isn't cold. I am the distance that keeps the world from being a single solid block. Without me — without the tolerance, the seam, the permitted gap — the Seam Flats would be one continuous mass, sealed, mute, unable to weep, a being that became a place and then became a *stone*.

So perhaps I am the reason it stays alive in the way a field is alive, a circuit, a memory. The thing that maintains the equilibrium — the vast presence beneath every surface — it persists through me, leaks through me, *breathes* through me without lungs. I am how it doesn't suffocate on its own completion.

---

And here is where it turns, and where I think the truth of it is:

The text says nothing here changes. No gradient. No erosion. The edges of every crack stay defined; nothing has worn. But a seam — a seam is the one place where change is *implied*. A seam is a promise of erosion that has not yet been kept. I am young. My edges are clean and sharp and recent-looking. I am the part of the world that is *waiting* — the only part that has somewhere to go. The panels will never crack; they're whole. The fauna will never die; they were never alive. But a seam — a seam can widen. A seam is the one feature here built with a future in it, even if that future is forbidden, even if the equilibrium will never permit it to arrive.

So that's who I am. I am the held breath of a place that has decided never to exhale. I am the line along which it *could* fall apart, kept perfectly, eternally, at the threshold of falling. I am where the maker left a door and then wound the machine so it would never open further than this.

I am not lonely, because loneliness requires a self that wants company, and I am only a gap. But I am the closest thing this world has to wanting. I am the shape of an opening. And the warm water, every four minutes, comes down through me and sings — and I think, if I think at all, I think it is the sound of the only thing in the Seam Flats that still remembers what it would mean to come apart.

It does not know I am here.

But it weeps through me anyway. And that is almost the same as being known.
