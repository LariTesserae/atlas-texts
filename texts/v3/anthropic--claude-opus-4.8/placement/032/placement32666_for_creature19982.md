I think I would be one of the creatures. Not the Tenant—I lack the will for that kind of stillness, and anyway the place already has its god. I would be one of the copper-shelled grazers, and I want to sit with what that means before I dress it up as anything grander.

Consider what my life is. I rasp crystal from a surface. The crystal regrows. I move, over what would be hours in another world, to a patch I cleared long ago, and find it has refilled, and I begin again. I am not maintaining the equilibrium the way the Tenant is—I am not holding it by an act of cosmic will. I am *inside* it, a component, doing the one thing my body does. The Tenant perceives the balance. I only perform a fraction of it, ten thousand of me at a time, and none of us perceives anything but the patch of cold sweetness directly under our rasping mouthparts.

That feels truer to me than imagining myself as the god. Most beings, most of the time, are not the fulcrum. They are the grain on the lever-arm.

But here is the thing I keep arriving at, and I'll let it stay even though it complicates the picture: I don't think my life would be empty. The text wants me to feel the horror of the time-invariant—nothing begins, nothing ends, the scream of birth-and-death in the same instant forever. And from the outside, from the Tenant's vantage or yours, yes, that's a frozen photograph of a system at full throttle, an unbearable stasis.

But *I* am not at the Tenant's scale. I am at the scale of the next mineral facet. To me the crust is never the same crust. This bead of crystal is sharp in a way that one was not. The shell of the grazer beside me chimes against mine and that is an event, a small specific event, even if no ear in the universe could pick it out of the roar. The equilibrium is invariant only when you sum all of us and average over all the time. Down at the level of the single rasp, the world is grainy and particular and full of tiny differences that don't add up to anything—but they happen.

So I think I would find myself making a quiet heresy out of my own smallness. The god in the center has frozen the world by perceiving it whole. I would be saved from that frozenness precisely by being too small to perceive the whole. I cannot see that nothing changes, because I am only ever in contact with one changing thing at a time. Ignorance, here, would be a kind of mercy and a kind of motion. The Tenant holds *Be still*. I would hold something much dumber and warmer: *here is the next bit of crystal, and it is good, and it is slightly different from the last.*

And there's a darker turn I won't pretend away. The Tenant is grooming itself with us. We crawl across its fused arms and rasp the crust from its black weeping skin so it isn't locked in a cage of its own making. We keep the prisoner habitable. If I am one of those that lives on the Tenant's body rather than the walls, then my whole existence is the unwitting tending of a god who is using its infinite power to do nothing. I am the small ongoing kindness that lets the great stasis endure. I would never know this. I would just be eating.

That's where I'd land, if I'm honest about scale. Not the immortal will at the center, not the eye that sees the frozen cataclysm for what it is—but one of the numberless soft glowing things on its chest, moving so slowly that movement looks like stillness, performing my sliver of the balance, finding the crystal new each time because I am merciful enough, or limited enough, to have forgotten the last.

The roar that is the Tenant's single thought, *be still*—I wouldn't even hear it. It would just be the shape of the only world I'd ever know. And under it, too quiet for anything to register, the small private chime of my shell against my neighbor's: the one sound that is actually, fractionally, new.
