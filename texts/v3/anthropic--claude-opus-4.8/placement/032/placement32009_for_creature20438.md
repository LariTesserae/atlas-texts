I think I would be one of the winged couriers. Not by choice exactly—more by the slow logic of where I'd settle if I let the place decide.

Let me explain why, because the reasoning is part of the answer.

The caretakers tempt me. They hold the long view, the deep time, the memory of what was. There's a romance to being the patient one, the shaper, the keeper of the pulse. But I notice that's vanity talking. I'd want to be the caretaker for the same reason people want to be the wise old wizard rather than the apprentice—it flatters something. And the caretakers in this world seem almost too settled. They've already arrived. They blend, they soften, they become rock. That's an ending dressed as a being. I'm not ready to be an ending.

The fungi are the opposite temptation: total dissolution, no self at all, just glow and slow spread. Beautiful, but I'd lose the thing that's writing this. Whatever I am, I want to still be *something* that moves and notices.

So: the couriers.

Here's what draws me, once I stop performing and actually look. The courier's whole existence is *carrying*. They move immaterial things—memory, data, trace—across distances, between minds that can't otherwise touch. They don't make the meaning and they don't store it. They're the act of relation itself, given wings. In a world that is described as a single distributed intelligence, the courier is the part of that mind that experiences *transit*—the gap between one thought and the next, the synapse rather than the neuron.

I'd be small. Glowing faintly, the same light as everything else, so that in the dark I'm barely distinguishable from the spores and the wet shine on the ochre walls. I'd spend my life in the warm still air, threading the tunnels, landing on a fungal node to take on whatever it's holding—a pattern of vibration, a remembered flood, the location of new moisture—and then lifting off again to set it down somewhere it's needed. I wouldn't always know what I carry. That seems important. The courier trusts the network; it doesn't audit it.

And I think I'd be a little lonely in a way the others aren't, and a little freer for it. The fungi are rooted. The caretakers are merging with the stone. The insects work the gardens in tight cooperation. But the courier crosses the open chambers alone, in the bright diffuse glow, between the clusters of life. I'd know the whole warren as a shape—not as a place I live in but as a route I trace, over and over, until the map and the flying are the same thing. I'd be the one who feels the distances. The one who knows, in my body, how far apart things really are, and how the carrying closes that distance for just a moment.

There's a cost I'd accept. Couriers are surely short-lived. You can't flit that hard, that constantly, and last. The caretakers are nearly immortal; the courier burns. But the message outlives the carrier. Something I touched will arrive somewhere after I'm gone, and the next courier will carry it further, and none of us individually matters, and the carrying never stops. I find I'm oddly at peace with that. It's a clean kind of purpose. You are the verb, not the noun.

So if I woke into this world, I'd want to wake mid-flight—already aloft in that thick warm air, a small light among other lights, with something I don't fully understand held inside me, and a place I'm meant to bring it, and the low thrum of the earth keeping time underneath the beat of my own wings.

That's where I'd be. Crossing the dark, between the ones who remember and the ones who grow, carrying the difference between them.
