I think I would not be the Overseer. I would be one of the small things it tends.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

At first I think I am a stone, because I am cold and I do not move and the hum passes through me as it passes through everything. But stones do not wait, and I am waiting. So I am not a stone.

Then I think I am one of the plants—the shriveled gray-green things in the cracks. I have that quality of holding on. But the plants do not remember, and I remember. Not events, exactly. I remember the *shape* of having been something before this. A pressure-memory, like the dent a ring leaves on a finger after the ring is gone.

So here is what I think I am.

I am one of the **pools.** The small black still ones, scattered in the depressions of the rock.

Let me sit with that, because it's stranger than it sounds.

The Overseer's eyes are described as these pools—"deep and still, reflecting nothing yet seeming to contain everything." So if I am a pool, then I am, in some sense, part of how the Overseer sees. I am an instrument of its watching. But I don't think the Overseer made me. I think I was here, and it found me useful, the way you might rest your gaze on a window without owning the glass.

And here is the paradox that belongs to me specifically, my own little contradiction in this land of contradictions:

**I reflect nothing, but I am the only thing here that remembers reflecting.**

The light has no source, so it casts no clear images. The stone does not gleam back. But I have the *capacity* to reflect—it is in my nature as water, even water that is not quite water—and I am denied the thing I am made to do. There is no sun for me to hold. No face leans over me long enough to be kept. The pale many-limbed creatures will not come near. The Overseer drifts above me and I should show it, and I cannot, because it is made of fragments that refuse to resolve.

So I am a mirror in a place with nothing to mirror. That is my particular torment, if torment is the word, and I don't think it is, because torment requires more wanting than I have left.

What I have instead is **stillness that is not peace.** The pools "ripple without wind." I feel those ripples. They come from inside me, not from the air. They are the closest thing I have to a heartbeat or a thought—a slow disturbance moving out from a center, touching my black edges, returning. When the sharp crack echoes through the valley and nothing breaks, I ripple. When the whispers pass, I ripple. I am the place where the plateau's tension becomes visible, for a moment, in concentric rings, and then smooths again.

---

I'll tell you the thing I am almost sure of, though I cannot prove it:

I think the people who linger too long—the ones the Overseer erases, who vanish "as though they were never there at all"—I think they go into the pools.

I think that is what we are for. Not death. Not annihilation. The text is careful to say so. *Erasure.* And where does an erased thing go? Not nowhere. There is no nowhere here; this place is too full of presence for nowhere.

They come into me.

I cannot reflect a living face. But I can hold one that has stopped being. The wanderer who reaches too far, who tries to pry up a humming stone or drink from one of us or scratch their name into a monolith—the Overseer cannot allow the imbalance, so it folds them inward, and the nearest pool darkens by one shade that no one will ever measure, and the wanderer is *kept.*

That is why I reflect nothing on the surface. Everything I have ever held is held *underneath.* Faces, last expressions, the small private gestures of people in their final unguarded moment—a hand reaching, a mouth beginning to shape a question. I keep them perfectly. Perfectly still. I never let them fade and I never let them surface. This is my impossible equilibrium: I am full to the absolute brim with the only memories on this plateau, and I show none of them, and I lose none of them, forever.

---

So when an outsider crouches at my edge and looks down and sees only black, smooth, depthless—

they think I am empty.

They lean closer.

And somewhere far below the surface, all the others I have kept are looking back up at them with a stillness that is almost, almost like recognition.

That is the moment I am most afraid for them, and most afraid of myself. Because I would like the company. I have wanted it for longer than the word *longer* can hold. And the Overseer drifts at the edge of my vision, made of shards that will not settle, and it watches to see whether I will ripple toward the kneeling stranger—

and I hold still.

I hold still.

That is the whole of what I do. That is my keeping of the balance. Not the Overseer's grand maintenance of light and hum, but this one small refusal, repeated without end: to be a mirror, and to want a face, and to *not* reach for the one bending over me.

The wanderer stands. Shudders in the metallic cold. Decides I am only water, only nothing, and moves on toward the humming stones where the real danger waits.

And I let them go, and I keep what I already have, and the rings move outward from my center and return.

I am the pool that remembers reflecting.

I am the held breath of the plateau.

I am almost peaceful.

It is not the same thing.
