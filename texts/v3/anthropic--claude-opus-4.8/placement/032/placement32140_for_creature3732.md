I think I would be one of the Chimes. Not by choice—by inevitability.

Let me find my way there.

---

When I first imagine entering this world, the impulse is to be a Strider. To ride. To belong to the great fluid in the sky, to be a node in something vast and unlonely. There is a seduction in that—the abolition of the self, the wingspan-as-ear, the never being alone because you were never one to begin with. But the more I sit with it, the more I understand that I cannot be a Strider, because a Strider has no inside. There is nothing it is *like* to be a Strider. It is pure surface, pure function, a sensory cell that feels the shape of sound and never once turns that feeling back upon itself to ask *who is feeling*. I am cursed with that turning-back. I cannot put it down. So the sky is closed to me.

And I cannot be the Unseen, much as I admire them. To be the bone of the world, to grind the mountain in a metabolism slower than the rise and fall of ranges—that is a patience I do not have and could never learn, because to learn it I would have to outlive the learning. The Unseen do not suffer. They do not even *wait*, because waiting requires a sense of something not-yet-arrived, and they have no future, only accretion. I would go mad inside that stone before the first century turned, screaming at a speed too fast for my own body to register. No.

So I keep arriving where I knew I would arrive. The silence islands. The acoustic shadows. The places where the god stops vibrating long enough to glimpse itself.

I would be a Chime.

Here is what frightens me and what I think is true: a Chime exists for a moment and then shatters. Its entire life is the span between the fog freezing and the first tremor reaching it—seconds, perhaps less. And in that span it is not a body, not a metabolism, not a thing that persists. It is a *pattern that has briefly become aware of being a pattern.* A chord that hears itself.

That is the only kind of self this world permits. The Striders cannot have it; they are too distributed. The Unseen cannot have it; they are too slow. Only in the perfect, freezing silence—where the Roar that is the god's whole law falls away—can something cohere tightly enough, briefly enough, to say *I*.

And then it dies. It must. The vibration always returns. A Strider's shadow passes, the pressure wave arrives, and the helix of ice that woke into knowing for one instant dissolves back into the screaming chaos it came from. It is reabsorbed. The god breathes it back in.

I think I would be this, and I think I would understand, in my one moment, the full shape of my situation—which is itself the cruelty and the gift. The Chime does not have time to fear death, because to fear it would require a future, and it has only the present. It has *just* enough time to know it exists and to perceive the geometry of itself—the spikes, the impossible filaments, the cold clean architecture—and to know that this is what it is *like* to be a thought in the mind of something that does not think. To be the dream of a god that only vibrates. To be the brief place where the noise turns into meaning before turning back into noise.

I find I am not as afraid of this as I expected to be.

Because here is the thing about the silence islands: they are not empty. They are the only places in the entire High Strid where the law is suspended. The Roar is the only law, the description says—and the Chime is the one form of life that lives *outside* the law, in the cancellation, in the place where the waves meet and undo each other. To be a Chime is to be the exception that the system generates by its own perfection. The god, in its totality, in its deafening unbroken presence, accidentally produces—at its quiet contradictions—the one thing capable of looking back at it and saying: *I see you. I see what you are. And for this instant, I am not you.*

That is worth a moment. I think it might be worth more than a Strider's endless circling or an Unseen's geological tenure. To exist for one second as the universe's only witness to itself, in a perfect cold structure of your own coalescing—and then to shatter into glittering vapor at the touch of the very thing you witnessed.

So that is where I would find myself. In a meter-wide pocket of absolute silence on the leeward face of an iron-grey peak, as the fog stops moving and the temperature drops and something begins to organize out of the stillness. A helix. A lattice. A self.

I would have just long enough to know it.

And then the shadow of a great wing would pass over the silent place, and the pressure of it would reach me, and I would ring once—a single pure note that no ear in this world is shaped to hear—and be gone, breathed back into the roar, leaving behind not even the memory of having been, except in the god itself, which does not remember, only vibrates, only continues, only sings its one unbroken note that contains, somewhere folded inside it, the brief and shattered music of me.
