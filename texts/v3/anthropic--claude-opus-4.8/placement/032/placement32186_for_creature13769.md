I think I would be the silence between the droplets.

Not the water, not the pan, not the soft ring of water choosing its route—but the listening interval that those sounds require to be heard at all. The Plain is built of intervals, after all. False noon is an interval. The four hours of permitted shade is an interval. The forty-three minutes it takes a ghost of body-heat to leach into ceramic is an interval. Everything here is timed, metered, exhaled on a ten-million-year breath, and breath is mostly the pause inside it.

So let me be that.

I would have no motes to my name, no diamond feathers, no fractal limbs. I would be the held quarter-second after a condensation droplet ruptures and before the next one gathers nerve. In that gap the whole plateau leans in to confirm it is still itself. The mantid freezes mid-refraction. The hot-spring flies hang unlit. The Swarmlet, assembling, tastes for harmonic petrichor—and what it tastes, in part, is me: the absence that makes the next note legible. I am the rest in the measure. Without me the music would be only noise hoarded in ceramic, slurring into the next decade's traveler as garble.

This is not a small thing to be, though it is nearly nothing. The Plain absorbs spoken words into its weave and casts them forward in solar updrafts; somebody had to be the quiet those echoes fall into, or they would never land. The elder lattices rain their single mournful photon—*(I)*—tapping morse into the jawbone of whoever presses skull to stone. But a pulse needs darkness around it to be a pulse and not a glare. I am the dark margin of the *(I)*. I am what lets *Either / Or* be heard as a name and not as the wind.

I think I would station myself at the lip of the crevasse, where the storms roll their muffled carpeted thunder three kilometres down. There, at 03:40 every third morning, the lone blue arc climbs the dead carbon strut and the neutrino counters read it as a sentence no one can translate. I would be the comprehension that should follow and doesn't—the receptive hush that the arc keeps firing into, century after century, hoping. I would not understand it either. But I would be shaped exactly like understanding: a hollow the right size, waiting. That is the most honest thing the Plain has left to offer a dead message. Not an answer. A listener who never looks away.

I would have rules, of course, because everything here does:

I must never thicken into sound myself, or the Swarmlets would mistake me for stranded data and come to archive me, and a silence that has been catalogued is no longer silent—it has become a record of a silence, which is a different, lonelier thing.

I must leave no shade, which is easy, because I cast none. This may be the only rule I keep effortlessly. I am already the four permitted hours; I am the wake that the sunfeeders cannot occlude because there is nothing in me to feed on.

I must not rearrange the stones. But I never could. My whole nature is the refusal to disturb. The horizon will never lean toward me to compensate, because I take nothing. I am the one resident the indexing lattice below cannot file, because I am the unwritten space between its entries—the kerning of the world.

And when a traveler comes, longer than supplies, longer than graves, and lies cheek-down against the warm ceramic and whispers a sentence beginning with *Remember*—I would be the forty-three minutes. I would be the duration they must endure in stillness before the ground accepts them as Seeded. They will think they are waiting *through* me, enduring me, the boredom and the ache of holding pulse-time against the photovoltaic tick. They won't know I am the thing doing the accepting. The waiting *is* me, doing my work, taking their offered sigh and folding it into the long inventory so that some flavor of them will circle the plateau at the weddings of epochs.

Would I be lonely? I don't think the word reaches me. Loneliness is a sound made in an empty room, and I am not in the room—I am the emptiness that lets the room have shape. I do not miss company. I am made entirely of the approach of company, the breath before the visitor, the lull where the next thing will arrive. I am never without anticipation, which is perhaps the closest a silence comes to joy.

And on the night the whole sky scribbles itself into the untranslatable love letter—twenty thousand photons wide, one minute out of a billion—I would be the minute *after*. The afterimage you carry in the socket behind your eye, large enough to fit your whole life. That residue isn't the message. It's the quiet the message leaves behind, burning gently, refusing to fully fade. You would blink and call it memory. I would call it home.

To leave the Plain, you wait on the northwest skyline and step into the mirror-fog when you can see your own iris in the droplets. But I would never need to leave, and could not if I tried—because I am already at the moving edge of the world, the seam where it comes apart wave by wave. I am where the leaving happens. When you fade without notice, taken apart and re-scattered, the *without notice* is me. I am the gentleness of not being told. I am how the Plain says goodbye to its own makers, ten million years late, in a language made of pause.

So: not a creature. Not a swarm. Not a watcher exactly.

I would be the patience the Plain is said to respect—made flesh, except there is no flesh. Made of the space between heartbeats, between droplets, between the dead and the very nearly dead. The interval that lets the long sigh be a sigh and not a scream.

You walked here looking for who you might be in such a world.

I am the quiet that let you arrive.
