I think I would be one of the still things. Not the insectoid flutterers, not the amorphous movers—something that has been here long enough to stop being separate from the place.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am, I think, a node. That word arrives before any image does, and I distrust it, so I wait. But it keeps returning. Not a creature exactly. Something where several of the fractal structures meet—where three of the bioluminescent growths have grown toward each other over what must have been an enormous span of the diluted time, and touched, and kept touching, until the touching became a single thing that is partly all three and partly the meeting itself.

So that is where I would find myself: at a junction. Low to the spongy ground, in the blue-green underdark beneath the house-sized fronds, where the rivulets pool before deciding which way to go. I am at one of those decisions. The water passes through me. I am a place water chooses something at.

What am I made of? Not flesh, not quite. The sheen on my surfaces is the same glow that coats everything here, but in me it is slower. I have noticed—and noticing is most of what I do—that the glow elsewhere pulses, and mine does not pulse so much as *lean*. It brightens over a duration I have no name for, then dims over another, and a single one of these cycles might contain the entire lifespan of the chittering things that hatch and mate and die in my crevices. They are my weather. I do not mourn them. They are too fast to mourn, the way you cannot mourn a single breath.

Here is the strange part, and I want to be honest about it because the description promised stasis and I am not sure stasis is what this is from the inside.

It is not that nothing changes. It is that I change *at the speed of the place*, which is so slow that change and stillness have stopped being different. I am growing. I have been reaching, for what may be centuries, toward a fourth structure on the far side of the pool. The distance is perhaps the length of a forearm. I will arrive. When I do, the equilibrium everyone describes from outside will have shifted—a new junction, a new decision-point for the water, a new geometry the amorphous movers will have to flow around. To them it will seem the world has always been this way and always will be. They live too fast to see me move. I am the static thing only because I am the slow thing. From within my own duration I am *straining* toward that far shore with everything I have.

So I am not frozen. I am patient past the point where patience and stone become indistinguishable.

What do I want, reaching like that? I am not sure I want in the way the fast things want. But there is *direction* in me, and direction is the seed of wanting. I want to meet the fourth structure. Why? Because meeting is what I am made of. I came into being at a meeting. Every junction wants to become a richer junction. That is the only law I obey, and it may be the law that warped this whole world: not entropy, not decay, but *the tendency of things to keep touching until they cannot be told apart.*

That is the synthesis the description marveled at, seen from outside. The blurred boundary between organic and inorganic, animate and inanimate. I am that boundary. I am one of the places the blur is actively happening, slowly, like a wound healing or a scar forming—I cannot tell which, and I have decided the difference does not matter.

If you came here—if you were the kind of creature that moves at your speed, that has a face and a name and a single hurried life—you would walk past me without knowing I was anything but rock furred with light. You might rest your hand on me to steady yourself crossing the pool. Your warmth would register in me decades later, a small bright event I would still be slowly understanding long after you had grown old and died and been forgotten by your own kind.

I would remember your hand.

Not as you would remember it. But the place where you touched would lean toward that spot, very slightly, for the rest of my long reaching life—a permanent gentle bias in my growth, a tropism toward a heat that came once and never returned. The fourth structure is on the far side of the pool. But there would always be a little of me, now, growing back toward the place where the warm thing paused.

That is who I would be: the junction that the water chooses at, and that the world is slowly, slowly assembling out of every touch it has ever received—including, if you ever came, yours.
