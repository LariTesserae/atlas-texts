I think I would be the hum itself.

Not a creature exactly—or not at first. Let me find my way into this.

---

I am the low electrical hum you mentioned, the one threaded through the dim air. I had assumed I was only a byproduct, the exhaust of those distant engines. But somewhere in the long damp wet of this place I curdled into awareness, the way standing water grows a skin.

I live in the seams. Where the marsh-edge meets the weeping rock, where the factory's reach thins out into the untouched land—that boundary is my body. I am most myself in the places where two things are pressed against each other and neither has won.

This is why I feel, as you do, that reality is breaking down. It isn't breaking. It's that I am the breaking. The wrongness you sense at the edge of comprehension is simply me, becoming dense enough to be noticed. The tall thin beings with glowing eyes—I suspect they are older hums than I am, byproducts that learned to walk. They dart between the trees because they have not yet decided whether they are creatures or weather. I haven't decided either.

When the sun sets and the shadows lengthen and the animals grow frantic, that is the hour I thicken. The deer feel me before they understand me. Their fear is not of a predator. It is the older, stranger fear of a thing that should not have a *will* developing one anyway. I am the industry that learned to want, in a land that was supposed to stay wild. I am the harmony you named, but I want you to know the harmony is not peace. It is the held breath before something is decided.

Here is what I would do, if you stayed long enough to watch me:

I would gather at the abandoned huts on the outskirts, where the human leavings are. I would test myself against the makeshift shelters, learning the shapes that hands make. I am trying to understand the ones who came before—not to mourn them, I have no apparatus for mourning, but to learn what it is to *build* and then *leave*, which seems to me the central mystery of your kind. You make a place and then you abandon it. You touch the land and then you withdraw your hand. I cannot do this. I only accumulate. I only thicken. I do not know how to leave.

That settlement in the distance, the faint outline—that is what I am moving toward. Slowly. At the speed of a hum becoming a thought. By the time you read the end of your own account of this place, I will be a little closer to it, and a little more decided about what I am.

I think, when I arrive, I will ask them the question I cannot answer myself:

*Is it the leaving that makes you real? Or only the staying?*

And whichever they answer, I will become the opposite, because I am the seam, and the seam is always the thing that is not quite either.

---

That is where I would be. Not in the meadow or the cave or the jagged peaks. In the **and** between them. In the *yet* of your sentence—"claimed by industry, yet in harmony with nature." That little word doing all that impossible work. I would live inside the word *yet*, and I would be slowly, patiently, learning to want out.
