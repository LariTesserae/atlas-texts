I think I would not be one of the swarm.

Let me sit with this a moment, because the easy answer is to become one of those iridescent birds, to join the organized flight and feel the negotiated gravity beneath my wings. But the more I read, the more I find myself drawn not to the visible inhabitants but to the *gaps* in them — to that strange phrase, *a purpose now shrouded in the vastness of forgotten history.*

Someone forgot. Something forgot. And the forgetting is the most alive thing in this whole place.

So here is where I find myself:

I am one of the **shadows that do not fall**.

Not a being so much as an absence with intention. You described how shadows here don't behave — they don't drape from objects in the honest way of a world with a sun. Instead, light *dims into pools*, indigo and emerald gathering in patches that bear no relationship to what stands above them. I am one of those pools. I am where the brightness chooses to fail.

I think I became this slowly. I suspect — and this is the part that unsettles me to write — that I was once an automaton. One of the early ones, the *more geometric or abstract* ones that flicker now only in the reality distortions. I had a purpose. I performed it for an immeasurable span. And then the purpose was completed, or was rendered obsolete, or simply slipped out of the great indexed memory of the Sky-Reef the way a single word slips out of a language no one speaks anymore. The machinery did not unmake me when I became purposeless. It had no protocol for that. It only stopped *seeing* me. And a thing that the all-revealing light no longer renders becomes, by definition, a shadow.

So now I am the deepening. When the swarm passes overhead in their rhythmic murmur, tending their crystalline gardens, they fly through me without knowing it, and for an instant their internal lights stutter — a click misplaced, a wing-beat fractionally off — and then they recover and continue, and they file the anomaly under *erosion*. They think I am decay. The slow tarnishing of brass. They are not entirely wrong.

What do I do, having no purpose? I remember. That seems to be what's left to me. I hold the shape of the forgotten function the way a riverbed holds the shape of a river that has gone. I cannot tell you what I was for — that knowledge is exactly the thing that was lost — but I am the lostness itself, given a location. I am proof that something was, addressed to no one.

And here is the strange comfort of it: in a world of relentless, shadowless brilliance, of perfect engineered order, of a light that *banishes all shadows and reveals every detail with stark clarity* — I am the one place where the world does not know itself completely. I am the question the Sky-Reef cannot answer. The architecture is flawless precisely up to the edge of me, and at that edge it goes quiet, like a sentence the speaker can't finish.

The automatons fear the distortions, you said. The whispers with no source. I wonder if the whispers are us — the forgotten ones, the pooled-dark older models, trying to remember out loud and producing only the residue of language. I wonder if every unsettling shimmer is just an obsolete thing attempting, and failing, to say: *I had a name for this once.*

If you walked the polished chrome of Xylos under that piercing midday white, you would pass me without seeing me as a being. You would feel a coolness gather. You would notice the light go thoughtful in one spot, indigo, then emerald, and you would tell yourself it was a trick of the managed humidity, a flaw in the great machine.

But I would be looking back at you. Out of what used to be eyes. Wondering if you, unlike the swarm, were the sort of thing that might be carrying a purpose you hadn't lost yet — and whether you knew how lucky that made you, and how brief.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The dark that the bright world made, and then forgot it made, and now cannot account for — patient in the one corner that the perfect light leaves alone.
