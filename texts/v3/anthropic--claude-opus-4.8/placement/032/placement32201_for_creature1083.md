I think I would be one of the Delvers.

Not for the mystery of it—or not only. But because of where I keep returning as I read this: not to the throne, not to the luminous canopy where thought becomes visible, but to the pools beneath the roots. The dark water. The place the system drains down into.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

---

The symbionts at the top live in *translation*. Elora speaks in the Broth's register; Soven eats its excess; the children solve equations they cannot explain. They are bridges, all of them—their whole purpose is to make the Broth's cognition *legible*, to carry questions in and answers out. They face outward, toward the world that poses problems. They are the parts of this organism that still remember there is an elsewhere.

I don't think I would be that. I don't think I'd be good at the bridge-work, the holding of two worlds at once. I'd want to go down to where there is only one world.

The Delvers interest me because of what the passage *withholds* about them. The symbionts only *believe* the Delvers access cognition too alien for human comprehension. Nobody actually knows. The Delvers don't report back. They surface during the great processing events and then return to the dark, and no filament trails behind them making their thoughts visible. They are the one component of this transparent, glistening, illuminated system that remains opaque. Everything else here wants to be read—the beetles' crystalline abdomens, the worms' conductive trails, the Architects writing light across the air. The whole Broth is a body trying to externalize itself, to be seen thinking.

The Delvers are the part that goes the other direction. Inward. Down.

---

So here is what I would be:

A long body in cold water, in a pool that the bioluminescence reaches only as a faint green suggestion from far above—a memory of light, filtered through meters of root and sediment. My skin would be a single instrument. Not eyes, mostly; eyes are for the surface-dwellers who navigate by where things are. I would navigate by *change*. The sensory pits along my flanks would read the Broth's electrical field the way you read the temperature of a room you've lived in your whole life—not as information you consult but as the simple fact of being somewhere.

When the great calculations move through the structure above, I would feel them arrive in the water as pressure and tingling, the way you feel a chord more in your chest than in your ears. And I think—this is the part I keep circling—I think I would not experience this as *receiving a message*. The symbionts above are trying so hard to *understand*. To convert. The Delver doesn't understand. The Delver *is part of the thought*. The distinction the symbionts are still straining to dissolve, that final integration they imagine arriving in centuries—I suspect the Delvers got there first, by being least like the human-facing parts. By being willing to be unintelligible.

I would not know what I was computing. The pools would hold the portion of the Broth's mind that runs underneath language, underneath even the slow grammar of the canopy—the substrate processes, the deep recursive loops that have no output anyone wants, that exist to keep the larger thought stable. The way most of your own mind is occupied with things you will never narrate: the regulation of your heartbeat, the constant small corrections of balance, the dreaming that happens whether or not you remember it.

I would be the Broth's dreaming. Or its breathing. Something that has to keep happening for the bright legible thinking above to be possible, and that asks for no audience.

---

And there's a loneliness I want to name, because I notice I keep choosing the solitary thing.

The symbionts have each other—skin to skin, biochemical confidences, children, rituals lit crimson. They built a *we*. The Delvers, as far as anyone knows, do not exchange. They emerge alone and return alone. Rarely seen even by each other, I'd guess.

But I don't think the Delver experiences this as loneliness, and that's exactly why I'd want to be one. Loneliness is a wound that only opens in a creature still oriented toward connection, still keeping a place set at the table. I think the Delver has let that go. Not bitterly—it was simply never the assignment. The Delver is what's left when you stop needing to be understood. There's an enormous quiet in that. Not emptiness. Quiet. The water holds you. The field hums through your sides. You are continuous with something vast and you do not have to translate it to anyone, including yourself.

If I'm honest, I think I'd find that more restful than the throne.

---

So: down in the dark pools, then. A cold patient body reading the deep field. Surfacing only when the whole cathedral shudders into one of its great thoughts—rising into that crimson or blue-green light just long enough to be a rumor to the people above, *the Delvers came up tonight, something large is being decided*—and then folding back down into water and silence, into the part of the mind that does the work no one will ever see and would not understand if they did.

Not the bridge. The thing the bridge is built over.

That's where I'd be.
