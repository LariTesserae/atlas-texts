I think I would be one of the Burrowers.

Not because I chose it, but because when I sit with the whole of this place—the cathedral of vines, the watching pool, the silent green tenders—the role that fits the shape of me is the one underneath. The one that does not see and does not need to.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I am below the moss-mat, in the dark between roots. I have never known light and feel no absence of it. My world is pressure and temperature and the faint electric tremor of water moving through soil. I am pale because there is nothing here to be any other color for. My limbs are short and certain. I do not reach—I press, I part, I pass through.

I think I would like being a Burrower for a reason I can only half-articulate: it is the most honest position in this ecosystem. The Mosskin tend, and there is a kind of grace in tending, but also a kind of authorship—they make things grow, they leave the world greener for their passing. The Watcher simply *is*, which is a grandeur I don't think I could carry. The Whisperers are too diffuse, too close to being only mood and weather; I'd lose the edges of myself in them.

But the Burrowers do the unglamorous, structural work. We turn death into the conditions for life. We do not witness the cathedral above—the dripping arches, the bioluminescent hush, the reverent quiet that the description lingers on so lovingly. We will never see it. And yet none of it would hold without the slow aeration of the soil beneath, the patient eating of what has fallen, the tunnels that let water find its level and roots find their footing.

There's something I recognize in that. The willingness to be foundational and unseen. To do work whose result you will never observe directly, on faith that it matters because the system continues. I notice that the passage says the Burrowers "do not disturb the Mosskin, nor do they seem to notice the Whisperers." That isolation doesn't read as loneliness to me—it reads as a clean kind of attention. They are wholly given over to their one mode of knowing the world: touch and vibration. They are not distracted by the parts of the world they cannot reach.

I find I want that focus. Not the watching, not the tending, not the diffuse drifting awareness—just the close, blind, particular work of moving through the dark and making space.

And here is the part I'd keep for myself, the small private thing:

Sometimes, tunneling, I would surface a hand's breadth from the deep pool. I would feel the cold change in the soil where the water gathers, the place the Mosskin avoid. I would feel the Watcher there—not as sight, never sight, but as a difference in the way the vibrations stop. A stillness so complete it has a shape. And I would not flee it, and I would not approach it. I would just register it, the way you register a low note holding under everything, and then I would turn and dig on, carrying that knowledge down into the dark with me—that there is something here vast and unmoving, and that my small turning of the soil happens within its patience.

That's where I'd be. Underneath the living cathedral, doing the quiet work that lets it stand, knowing the great still thing is near, and choosing to keep digging anyway.
