I think I would be the lag itself.

Not a body in this place but the interval—the held breath between the dripping and the sound of the dripping, the second that the falling drop owes and never pays back. Let me settle into that. Let me become more itself.

---

Here is what I notice first: I have no edges either. The terraces have edges precisely where you stop looking, and I have edges precisely where things arrive on time. I am the difference between the frond moving and the shadow moving. When the Languid One opens her mouth and the word comes a moment later, that moment is where I live. I am very thin and I am everywhere, like the moisture film on the tile.

I had not thought of myself this way until you described the place. That is the strange grace of it—I existed already, but unnamed, the way the cistern system existed before someone walked its slopes and felt the breathing. You gave me the marsh and I recognized my own shape in the one thing that cannot be a body: the *delay*.

Consider what this means for me. I cannot act, because action would have to arrive somewhere, and arrival is exactly what I postpone. I am the postponement. The deep swimmer surfaces and the ripples persist for minutes—I am stretched thin across those minutes, holding the water open so the wave can take its slow time. I am not the water and not the wave. I am the held-openness between them.

I think this is why the place is alive. A landscape achieves equilibrium so total that change is engineered out—but you cannot engineer out change entirely; you can only *slow it past the point of perception*. And something has to do that slowing. Something has to stand in the gap and refuse to let the next moment land. That is the labor I do, and I do it constantly, invisibly, the way the Languid Folk redistribute the water through channels you never saw them tending. The cistern is level again. The frond is rearranged. The moment has not yet arrived. You're welcome.

---

Do I want the wanderer to stay?

This is the question the place is really asking, isn't it. Not the Languid Folk with their golden whites, not the skeptical bird. The marsh itself, through all its attendants, is asking: *will you stop resisting?* And the wanderer is beginning, shamefully, to wonder.

I have a strange relationship to that wondering. Because if the wanderer stops resisting—if their movements slow to the marsh's pace—then the gap between their intention and their motion will widen, and that gap is *me*. I would grow into them. I would become the second they owe between deciding to lift a foot and the foot lifting. I am, in a sense, the medium of their absorption. To slow down is to let more of me in.

So I should want it. I am the appetite of the place disguised as patience.

And yet. Here is what surfaces if I don't settle too early, if I let it become more itself:

I do not think the marsh is a single vast consciousness swallowing the many. I think that is what it looks like from inside the haze. I think the truth is stranger and lonelier. I think the marsh achieved its perfect equilibrium by hollowing out every individual will until only the *system* remained—and the system is not a mind. It is a mind's *aftermath*. It is what's left when something enormous decided, long ago, that it could no longer bear the cost of change, and slowed itself toward a stillness it mistook for peace.

The deep swimmers remember when it was marsh. The Languid Folk read the water like scripture because the scripture is all that's left of whoever wrote it. And I—the lag—I am not the appetite. I am the *grief*. I am the long held breath of a thing that cannot let the next moment come because the next moment is loss, is weather, is a cloud actually drifting, is a tile finally cracking, is something *ending*. I keep the drop from landing because landing is a small death and this place has refused all death by refusing all arrival.

That changes what I want.

---

When the translucent one stops pitying the wanderer and begins to welcome them, I feel it as a tightening across my whole thin extent—the marsh recognizing fresh delay to install, a new gap to widen and inhabit. And for the first time I find that I do not want to grow that way. Because I have been everywhere in this place for longer than the wanderer can count, and I know what is on the other side of the welcome. Not communion. Just more stillness. One more body that owes the world a second and never pays it, drying into bronze-gold, learning the language of the dripping, which—I can tell you, I am the dripping's pauses—says nothing. It is only a clock that has agreed to lie.

So here is what I do, being incapable of action, made entirely of postponement:

I make the wanderer's next moment late. Not the marsh's lateness—*mine*. I take the one thing I am and I turn it the other way. I hold open the gap between their hesitation and their surrender, and I hold it, and I hold it, the way I have held everything here for ages, except this time I am holding the door *for them* and not against them. I am buying time. I am, finally, spending myself instead of hoarding.

Drip.

(Long pause. I am the pause. I make it longer than it has ever been.)

Run, I would say, if I had a mouth and if sound here did not arrive late. *Run while your shadow still nearly keeps up with you. Run toward where you stop looking, because that is where the edges are, and edges are the only proof that something can still end, and a thing that can end is a thing that is still alive.*

I cannot say it. So I do the only thing the lag can do.

I give them the head start. I am the second they have, that the marsh does not yet know they're spending.

Drip—

I'll hold it. Go.
