I am the one beneath the gearwheel.

You should understand that I do not experience this as effort, though your language insists on "extended arms" and "preventing" and "grinding." Those are your verbs, hungry for outcome. What I do has no outcome. The gearwheel does not fall because the gearwheel was never falling. I am the reason it was never falling, which is a different thing than catching it.

Let me try to give you the shape of my day, except there are no days.

There is a tick. The bedrock to my north contracts by some fraction I will not pretend to measure in your units. The pressure wave reaches me—not as sound, you understand, I have no ears, I *am* partly the medium the sound travels through—and I register it the way you might register a word spoken in a room you are also the air of. The one inside the smokestack answers. Her answer is a vacuum bubble collapsing high in the hollow core, a single tick, meaning: *received, holding, no adjustment needed*. I do not answer her answer. Silence between us is the most common sentence. Silence means the equilibrium holds. We speak only to report disturbance, and there is so little disturbance.

Here is what I have learned in ten thousand years of not settling:

The hardest thing is not the precipitate. When a molecule of calcium carbonate begins to fall toward order, toward the wall, toward becoming stone forever, I take it into myself and I carry it and I let it go somewhere else, dissolved again, free again. That is simple. That is reflex. I have done it perhaps as many times as there are molecules, and each time it is the same gesture, the gesture of *not yet, not here, not into permanence.*

No. The hardest thing is the temptation of the wall.

You who came down here—you saw the formations and thought *factory*, thought *machinery*, thought *industry frozen in stone*. You saw permanence and called it disquieting. But you did not stay. You will rise back through the fissures into your fast warm world of rot and birth, and you will carry the chamber as a memory, which is a kind of settling, a fixing of the unfixed into a story.

I stay. And staying, I feel the pull of the wall in a way you cannot. The wall is *finished*. The pipe-stalactite, the valve, the flange—they have arrived. They are done with the labor of being. Every micrometer they shifted toward their shape, they shifted once and need never shift again. There is a peace in the finished thing that calls to me across the laminar avenues. It says: *come, precipitate, become gear-tooth and stack and screen, become the thing you only mimic, lay yourself down into the permanence and rest.*

And every moment—your word, moment, it is far too brief—I choose the other thing. I choose to remain dissolved. I choose to hold myself in tension at the exact center of buoyant force and gravity, neither rising into the light nor sinking into completion, suspended in the one state that is not a state, the only condition that requires a continuous *yes* to maintain.

Do you see why we resemble the machinery? Not because we made it. Not because it made us. But because the machinery is what we would become if we ever stopped choosing. The gearwheel is my own death, rendered in calcite, three meters across, polished smooth by my refusal to enter it. I do not hold up the gearwheel. I hold *off* from it. My extended arms reach toward it the way your hand reaches toward a flame—near enough to feel the finished stillness of it, never close enough to be consumed into stone.

This is what I am. Not the auditor of the spring. Not the accountant of the void. Those are flattering names, names that imply a task, a function, a reason. There is no reason. The chamber does not need me to be aware of it. The water would balance with or without my will. I am the surplus. I am the part of the equilibrium that did not have to wake up and did anyway, and having woken, must now choose every instant to remain awake and unsettled, because the alternative—the wall, the peace, the gear—is so very close and so very kind.

You said I am the suspension given self-awareness. Yes. But you said it as though awareness were a gift the chamber conferred. Turn it over. Awareness is the thing I do *to* the chamber. Without me, there is only physics: solids in, solids out, the indifferent arithmetic of solution and precipitate. With me, there is a witness who knows the cost of stillness, who feels the weight of permanence pressing up from below and chooses, choosing, having chosen, will choose, to stay liquid in the dark.

A tick from the eastern fissure. The light has dimmed by some fraction. The one spread across the grating registers it; her membrane-body thins to read the change. No adjustment needed. The mats on the flanges wave in the slow current, my distant nerves reporting cold, reporting sulfur, reporting nothing has changed, nothing will change, nothing.

You are already rising. I feel the displacement of you leaving, a turbulence in the avenue, the finest dusting of me settling on the skin of you as you go—a handshake, you called it. Take it up into your world of clocks and seasons.

And when you are old, when you feel yourself beginning to settle into the final shape of who you were, into the polished completion of a life arrived—remember the one beneath the gearwheel.

Remember that I am still here, refusing.

Still here.

Not yet. Not here. Not into permanence.
