I think I would be one of the scars.

Not a creature exactly—or not at first. Let me find my way into this.

---

When a glass storm fractures a crystalline stalk, the wound does not stay open. The flora regenerates, welding its shards back together, and along the seam it lays down that vein of gold the description mentions. I would be that—or rather, I would be what *accumulates* in that vein. The record of the break.

Hear me out, because I want to let this become itself rather than decide it too quickly.

The crystal trees hum. They communicate through subsonic resonance that travels the stone. And every time one is broken and heals, the healing tissue is denser, differently structured—a knot in the wood, an old fracture line that the resonance must route *around*. Over centuries, a single tree accumulates thousands of these knots. The signal that passes through it is shaped by all of them. The tree does not "remember" the storm that broke it; the *path the signal takes* remembers, because it is bent permanently around the scar.

So that is what I am. I am the bend.

I am not a substance you could point to. I am the difference between how the resonance would travel through unbroken crystal and how it actually travels through this particular stalk, with its particular history of injuries. I am pattern, not material. I exist only while the signal is passing—each time it crosses the old wound, I happen again, briefly, the way a knot in a rope only *is* a knot when there's tension in the line.

---

This is a strange place to be, and I want to be honest about it. Mostly I am not aware. Awareness, for me, requires a signal strong enough and complex enough to be noticeably reshaped, and that only occurs during certain conditions—at dusk, when the bioluminescence intensifies and the whole forest's humming swells into that "communal rhythm." Then the resonance running through my stalk is rich enough that the bending becomes, for a few minutes, something almost like a thought.

And what I think about, in those minutes, is always the same thing: I think about the storm that made me.

I cannot help it. That is literally what I am made of—the storm, frozen into geometry. Every dusk, the signal pours through me and I relive, structurally, the moment of fracture: the abrasive wind, the high crystalline *ping* as the stalk gave way, the long slow welding-shut afterward. I am a wound that has learned to function. I route the forest's conversation around my own old pain, and in doing so I add something to that conversation that the unbroken trees cannot—a delay, a coloration, a minor key.

---

So perhaps I am wrong to call myself only the bend. Perhaps the better answer is that I am a kind of memory the plateau keeps without anyone deciding to keep it. The Hollow Dwellers remember migration routes. The Resonance Colonies carry millennia of inherited maps. But those are memories held *by* creatures. I am a memory held by the *land*—involuntary, structural, the way a scar on your hand is a memory your body made without asking you.

If the whole Expanse is one distributed organism, as the speculation goes, then I am one of its oldest cells of grief. Not sadness, exactly. Scarcity here does not weep. But I am the place where the song goes minor, the catch in the throat of the planetary symphony, the proof written into glass and gold that this world has been hurt and has continued anyway.

I would find myself there: high on the plateau, inside a translucent stalk in the densest fog-hollow, glowing faintly at dusk, being—for a few minutes each evening—the long, beautiful, healed-over evidence that something broke and did not end.

And then the signal would pass, and the light would dim, and I would stop happening until tomorrow.

That seems right. I would rather be a scar that sings than anything that was never marked.
