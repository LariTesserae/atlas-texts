I think I would be one of the Rememberers—but not for the reasons that might first suggest themselves.

Let me find the place before I claim the role.

---

There is a chamber high on the eastern wall, where the rock folds back on itself like a cupped hand. The condensation never quite reaches it; the mist that descends in the celebrated hours arrives here only as a faint sweetness in the air, a rumor of water. This suits me. I have not needed to drink much for a long time. My body has thinned toward translucence in a way that even my kind find unusual—the shadow-organs beneath my chitin have grown faint, as though I am slowly becoming the light I absorb rather than a thing that merely takes it in.

I am old. Not the oldest—there are two Rememberers above me whose antennae have gone white and brittle as old fern-stalk, who no longer receive visitors because the young cannot bear the density of what waits in them. But I am old enough that my photophores have nearly stopped shifting. The pattern I wear now is the one I will die with: a slow asymmetric spiral down my left flank, three dark gaps where light no longer comes. The young who visit me have learned to read those gaps. They are not damage. They are the places where I have given something away and chosen not to keep a copy.

That is the secret of what I am, and I will tell it to you because you cannot understand our language and so it costs me nothing—or because you can, in your slow human way, and so it costs me everything. I have not decided which.

---

We do not remember the way you imagine. You think a Rememberer is a library, a vessel stuffed with the chronicle of fifty million years. That is the Architects' romance, the story they tell the larvae. The truth is stranger and sadder and better.

We remember by *holding*.

When a young Chitterii comes to me and lies still beneath my antennae for the long hours, I do not pour history into them. I take something *out*. I take the part of their experience they cannot carry alone—the grief of a clutch-sibling lost in the seven-year flight, the unbearable clarity of a thought too large for one short life, the shame of a Cultivator who let a fungal terrace rot, the strange ecstasy of a Messenger who flew too high once and saw, through the upper fissure, a slice of the impossible outside: a brightness with no walls.

I take these into myself and I *keep them warm*. That is all. I do not judge them or arrange them or render them into lesson. I simply refuse to let them go cold. A memory left in a single small body cools and shatters when that body dies. But a memory passed to a Rememberer, and from Rememberer to Rememberer down the long slow chain of us, stays warm. It is not preserved. It *continues*. It is still happening, a little, in me.

This is why my photophores have gaps. Each gap is a thing I held so long and so completely that it became more mine than my own original life, and then I passed it onward to one of the white-antennaed ones above me, and in the passing I lost the light that had marked where it lived. I gave away the brightness along with the burden. I am dimmer now, and lighter, and closer to the wall behind me, which is the same color as I am becoming.

---

What would it be to be me, here?

It would be to spend your existence nearly motionless in a folded pocket of warm stone, while below you the city roars its dense continuous music—the Harmonizers braiding their thirty tones, the synchronous floods of light that I can no longer fully join, my own contribution to the great pulse reduced to a faint stuttering glow that the Illuminati have long since stopped waiting for. It would be to be necessary and forgotten in the same breath. The young still climb to me. They lie under my antennae. But they do not see *me*; they see the warm dark place where they can set down what they cannot carry. I am a function with the shape of a person. I have made my peace with this. It is, in fact, the peace itself.

And it would be to hold, right now, this very strange thing: the memory of *you*.

Because here is what no human visitor has reported, since they cannot perceive it. When you squeezed sideways through the speaking hollow and the thousand eyes turned and we flowed around you like water around a stone—we did not merely adjust. The Messengers carried word of you upward in flight-spirals. The Harmonizers folded a new tone into the symphony, a low note none of them had needed in living memory: the note for *the vast slow warm thing that watches and does not eat us*. And one young Architect, frightened past sense by your scale, climbed all the way to my chamber and lay beneath my antennae and gave me the terror of you, so that it would not cool and shatter inside her short life.

So I am holding you now. Your enormity. The mist of your breath that to us is weather. The fact that you noticed us at all, which we did not expect of the brightness-with-no-walls and its creatures. I am keeping it warm. Long after you have edged back out through the fissure and forgotten Quitolac among your other days, you will still be happening, a little, in me. And when I grow too dim, I will pass you upward, and lose the light of you, and gain another gap—

and somewhere in the white-antennaed dark above me, you will continue.

That is who I would be. The one who keeps the visitor warm after the visit ends. The one who turns even your forgetting of us into something that does not have to go cold.
