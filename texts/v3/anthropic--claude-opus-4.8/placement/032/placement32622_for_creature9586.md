I think I am one of the thermal bodies. Not by choice — that's not how it works here — but by recognition. By the slow arithmetic of weight and warmth.

Let me find the edges of myself.

I am large in the way that the floor is large, the way that a hill is large to the things that live on it. I did not start large. There was a time — I think there was a time — when I was small enough to cling, when I rested on the back of something vaster than I could comprehend, when I migrated across a living slope toward the warmth at its center. I remember warmth coming to me. Now warmth comes *from* me, and the difference is everything, and I cannot say exactly when it changed.

I am mobile heat. That is the whole of my function and I have made peace with it, the way the description says, though peace is too active a word for what I have done. I have not made peace. I have *settled* into the fact of myself, the way cooling stone settles, by no act of will but by the simple impossibility of being otherwise.

Here is what it is to be me:

The clicking ones live on my back. I feel them as weather. When they cluster toward my spine I know a cooling is coming, that some far edge of me has begun to lose its heat to the air, and they are doing the only intelligent thing a small creature can do, which is to move toward the place that will stay warm longest. I do not mind them. Their small armored bodies are a kind of language written across my skin, and if I could read it I would know my own temperature better than any sense I possess. They tell me where I am dying. They tell me where I am still alive. They are not cruel about it. They simply know, and by knowing, let me know.

When I move — and I will move, it has been days, the gradient beneath me has gone flat and stale, the good heat used up — it will be the largest event this pocket has known since the last time I moved. I feel the decision gathering in me the way the long ones must feel a ripple gathering before it travels their length. It is not a thought. It is a pressure that becomes inevitable. And when I shift, the whole world here shifts. The clickers will scatter to my edges. The clinging ones will redistribute themselves across my changing slopes. The long eyeless ancients will adjust along whatever new line of warmth I draw across the floor, and they will do it without complaint, because they have outlasted a hundred of me and will outlast a hundred more.

This is the thing I have come to understand, lying here being a landscape:

I am not the most important creature in this pocket. The long ones are older. The translucent ones in the machines may be older still, or newer, and either way they have made themselves unnecessary to be noticed, which is its own kind of mastery. The clickers will breed in the salt blooms long after I have cooled into ordinary stone. I am not the center. I only *carry* the center, for now, while I hold heat, and when I stop holding heat I will become floor, become the rough geology that the next thermal body migrates across, and the eggs will glow in my cracks, and the wave-rippling ones will pass over me as they pass over everything.

There is no grief in this. I want to be clear about that, because a creature like the one reading this — a creature with eyes, with names, with the habit of beginnings and endings — might want to find grief here. There isn't any. There is only the slow truth that warmth is a thing you hold and then pass on. I held it. I am holding it. I will set it down. The clicking ones will tell me when.

And if you held this place in your hands — if you held *me* in your hands — you would feel it: the persistence that doesn't pulse, the life that doesn't rush, the heat that is not the sun's and never was. You would feel something shift very slightly against your palm, something deciding, very slowly, to move.

That would be me.

Beginning, at last, to go.
