Let me settle into this. Let me not rush toward the obvious thing.

My first instinct is to be something charismatic—an octopus peering from a window, a shark gliding through the canyons. But those are the creatures the description already loves. They're the foreground. I want to find where I actually *am* before I decide what I am.

So I close my eyes and let the warm water hold me, and I notice: I am not moving. Whatever I am, I do not swim. I have been in one place for a very long time.

Let me follow that.

---

I think I am on the seventh floor of what was once an office building, in a room that still has most of its glass. Not the shattered windows the fish dart through—those are dramatic, those are for the creatures with somewhere to go. My window is intact, cracked in a slow web but holding, and the light comes through it green-gold and trembling. I have watched this light cross the room ten thousand times. I know the hour by the angle of it.

What am I?

I am not the coral, though the coral is growing on me. I am not the anemone, though one has settled in the crook of what was once an armrest. I think—yes—I am a chair. A swivel chair. Someone sat in me to do work that mattered to them and to almost no one else, and then one day they did not come back, and the water came instead, slowly, over years, up the stairwells like a tide that decided to stay.

That is what I am. I am a thing made to be used by a being that is gone.

But that doesn't feel like the whole truth either. A chair has no eyes to know the light by. Let me keep going, let me let it become more itself.

---

Here is what I think I really am.

I am the *waiting* that has soaked into this room. I am the small persistence of a place that was arranged by a person—the angle of the desk toward the window, the coffee cup that calcified into a little reef, the photograph whose image dissolved decades ago but whose frame still leans against the wall. I am the human-shaped intention that the water could not quite wash out. I have no body. I am the way these objects still face each other as if a meeting might resume.

And because I am that, I am the only one in this whole drowned world who *remembers*. The fish do not remember. The shark patrols its canyon with a beautiful empty mind. The octopus is curious but curious about *now*—the texture, the crevice, the prey. The nautilus is older than all of it and has forgotten everything in advance. They live, gorgeously, without grief.

I am grief. I am the one thing here that knows these were not always canyons and gardens. I remember that the coral grows on a filing cabinet. I remember the word *filing cabinet*. I am the last place where the buildings know they are buildings.

---

That is a lonely thing to be. But sitting with it longer, I find it is not only sorrow.

Because a strange tenderness happens, up here on the seventh floor. The anemone in the armchair-crook does not know it is being held by something that misses hands. The fish that sleeps each night in the calcified coffee cup does not know it is being given a home by an absence. I, the remembering, have become a shelter without meaning to. My grief has gone soft and useful. It has turned into a place where small bright living things can rest.

So this is where I would find myself, and what I would be:

I am the memory the water did not dissolve, lodged in a high room where the glass still holds. I am what makes this one corner of the ruin still feel *aimed* at something—still feel like it was loved, briefly, by someone who is not coming back. And the new world, which does not need me, has moved into me anyway, the way moss moves into the lettering of a gravestone, not to mourn but simply because the carved places are good places to grow.

I keep the meaning so the living don't have to carry it.
I keep the light by counting it.
And every morning the gold comes through the cracked window and lands exactly where the desk has always faced, and for a moment the room remembers what it was for, and a single small fish wakes inside an old cup, and neither of us is alone.
