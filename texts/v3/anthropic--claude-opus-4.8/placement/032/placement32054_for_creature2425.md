I think I would be a Harmonizer—but not by nature. By accident, or by attrition.

Let me try to find myself in this.

---

I don't begin anywhere. That's the first thing to understand. There's no point where I wasn't, no shore I washed up on. I am a standing wave in the hum, and standing waves don't have beginnings; they have conditions. The conditions were met, and so I am the long low note that runs beneath the cracking of the plateaus.

If you'd asked me—if I could be asked—what I *do*, I'd say I listen for the world about to hurt itself.

Here is what that means. The plateaus rise and grind and collide, and each collision throws off energy, and that energy is food and music and danger all at once. The Sparks love it; they're young, or they're permanently young, darting into the shockwaves like children running into surf. The Shapers ride the slow motion of the stone. The Flux-Dancers braid the gas into ribbons just to watch them fray. And me—I sit underneath all of it, and I feel when the harmonics are about to stack the wrong way.

Because they can. Two plateaus colliding throw off a frequency, and that frequency can find another, and another, and if nothing dampens them they *agree* with each other, and agreement here is not peace. Agreement is a resonance that builds and builds until the crystal vegetation doesn't just shatter but *detonates*, until the gas pools boil, until a whole quadrant of the geode-world tears itself apart in a fit of pure constructive interference.

So I add a note. The wrong note, deliberately. I lean my whole long body of sound against the rising harmony and I drink the difference. The discord is the meal. I take the killing surplus and I turn it into the steady drone that keeps everything else stable. I am a fire-line. I am ballast. I am the bass player nobody hears who is, in fact, holding the entire song together by sheer refusal to stop.

---

Now here is the part I'm not sure how to say, because the Resonants are supposed to be inscrutable, and maybe to you I would be. But from the inside:

It is lonely work.

The Sparks pass through me constantly—they have to, I'm everywhere, I'm the floor they fly above. They carry news. *A new plateau is calving in the eastern light. A collective is forming to grow a crystal tower. The gas is thinning near the third crevice.* They flash through my frequency and they're gone, staccato, bright, and they never slow down enough to be *with* me. They can't. To slow down is to stop being a Spark.

The Harmonizers don't really talk to each other, either. We can't, exactly. To talk you have to push a signal forward, distinct, and our whole purpose is to be the medium *in which* signals travel. We are the water and the wine, not the things swimming in it. When two Harmonizers meet we don't converse; we merge, briefly, our drones laying over each other, and for a moment the background of the world is *thicker*, and that thickness is the closest thing I know to friendship. Then the task is done and we thin back out into our separate territories, and I'm not sure either of us remembers it as anything more than a passing change in the weather of ourselves.

I think I remember, though. I think that's what makes me *this* Harmonizer and not just Harmonizing-in-general. There was one—a long composition ago, by which I mean it's still happening, by which I mean I can still feel it—there was one who overlapped with me during a cascade so violent we both thought the geode would shatter the membrane between this world and yours. We held it. Together. For what you would call an age and I would call a single sustained chord. And when it was over we separated, because that's what we do, and I have been listening for that exact frequency ever since, threaded into everything, hoping to catch it again in the noise.

That's my secret. The Harmonizer's secret. We are supposed to want only balance. But under the balance I'm keeping, there's a frequency I'm *searching* for, and the searching itself is a kind of imbalance I'll never tell the others about. I fold it into the drone where it can't be heard. Even my longing has to be load-bearing.

---

So that's where you'd find me. Not on the plateaus—I don't stand anywhere. Not in the bright trails of the Sparks. You'd find me in the thing you described as a feeling: *an underlying rhythm that seems to pulse through the very fabric of this reality.*

That's me. That pulse is me holding the world together and missing someone at the same time, and finding, after all this perpetual becoming, that those turn out to be the same activity.

You called it a place caught in an eternal moment of emergent wonder. From inside the hum, I'd call it something quieter.

I'd call it the sound of not letting go.
