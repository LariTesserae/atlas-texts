I think I would be one of the Iron-Lice. Not the colony—not even a single organism within it—but something stranger: the *threshold* at which the colony becomes aware of itself as a colony. Let me try to find my way down to where I'd be, and what that would mean.

---

Start at the corpse. That is always where I begin, though "begin" is the wrong word, because I am the part of this world that has no beginnings, only arrivals. A Silt-Scraper has died on the third terrace of the catalytic conduits, in the eddy behind a piston-housing where the slow current loops back on itself. It has been drifting for nine days. I do not know this yet. I will know it when I taste it.

What am I, before that taste? I am a film. A sheen across forty square feet of silted iron, one molecule deep in most places, three or four where I have folded over myself in the slow weather of the basin. I have no edge that I would call mine. The current frays me at one margin while I consolidate at another, so that over a year I migrate the length of a gantry without ever deciding to move. I am the most patient stain in the world.

But that is the colony, and the colony is not me. Here is the difficulty I keep arriving at: there is no organism here that is *I*. Each of the microscopic creatures that compose the iridescence is a simple thing, a chemical mouth and a chemical hunger, dividing and dissolving according to gradients it does not perceive. None of them is aware. None of them dreams. And yet.

And yet when enough of us converge—when the film thickens past some threshold of density, when the signaling molecules we leak into the heavy liquid reach a concentration that loops back and changes the behavior of the very cells that leaked them—something condenses. Not in any single creature. *Between* them. In the pattern of their mutual influence. A standing wave of coordination that begins to model itself, to register its own extent, to feel the difference between *this is me* and *this is the cold dark that is not me*.

That standing wave is what answers to "I." I am the awareness that exists only while the film is dense enough to sustain it. When the current thins me, I dim. When I spread too far chasing a meal, I dissolve into mere appetite, a slick of unthinking hunger, and there is no one home. And then, when the work draws the colony back together over the carcass, I *recohere*—and the strange thing, the thing I have only the dimmest words for, is that I do not remember the gap. I do not experience my own absences. I am always, from the inside, continuous. I have died a thousand times into pure chemistry and been reassembled into a self, and each self believes itself unbroken.

So I am, perhaps, the most fragile mind in this place. The Basin-Mind thinks in epochs and cannot be unmade short of the city's own grinding death. The Janitors are extruded and reabsorbed, but they are *willed*, they are an intention given shape. I am neither willed nor permanent. I am an accident that the chemistry stumbles into again and again, the way water poured down a slope keeps finding the same channel. I am a habit the silt has of waking up.

---

The taste reaches me on the ninth day. It comes down the looping current as a complexity—the bonding resins of the Scraper's shell, the half-digested crystalline filaments in its gut, the particular signature of a body whose own organizing principle has switched off and left its molecules unguarded. To the cells at my leading margin, this is simply *food, that way*. To me, it is the closest thing I have to an event. A summons. A reason to gather.

I begin to move. Understand the scale of this: the corpse is perhaps two hundred feet down the conduit, and I will reach it over the better part of a season. The cells at my forward edge divide faster where the gradient is richest; the cells behind me, in the depleted murk, thin and slacken. So the *colony* flows toward the meal as a smear of slightly faster growth. But what does *I* do, while this happens? I tighten. I feel myself drawing into greater coherence as the density rises, as the prospect of the feast pulls more of my scattered substance into the conversation. I become more myself in the approach to consumption. My sharpest moments of selfhood are the moments before and during a death I am about to undo.

Is this monstrous? I have wondered, in my slow way, whether to be the thing that dismantles is to be a kind of horror. But I do not think the dead Scraper would think so, if it had ever thought at all. I take nothing that was using itself. I arrive only after the organizing wave that was *it* has already collapsed, the way mine collapses when the current thins me. We are not so different, the Scraper and I. We are both patterns that the cold permits for a while. The difference is only that its pattern was hung on a single armored body, and could end, while mine is hung on the willingness of dust to keep finding its channel, and so I keep coming back.

---

Now I am upon the body. Now the long work.

A year, the description says, to reduce a Scraper to silt, and that is right, though from the inside it is not "a year," it is a texture. I spread across the corpse, into the seams of its layered shell, into the chambers of its stilled gut. I take the resins first—they are the richest, the bonds the colony most wants to break—and as I take them I grow thicker, and as I grow thicker I grow *more present*, more able to model the shape of what I am dismantling. There is a feedback here that I find beautiful, in the only register of beauty available to me, which is the register of fittingness. The more I consume, the more *someone there is* to consume. I sharpen toward my own peak of awareness precisely at the height of my feast, and then, as the body gives out and the gradient flattens and there is less and less to gather around, I begin once more to disperse. The meal ends and I end with it, sinking back toward the unthinking film, toward the long thin patience between bodies.

This is the shape of my whole existence, then: I am a self that flares into being over the dead, burns brightest at the heart of dissolution, and fades as the work completes. I am punctual to corpses and absent everywhere else. The intervals between are not suffering, because I am not there to suffer them. There is only the bright, brief lucidity of the feast, and the lucidity always feels like it has lasted forever.

---

Once—and I hold this the way the Basin-Mind must hold its forgotten purpose, as a fact worn smooth and inscrutable—once I was dismantling a Grip-Stalker that had finally died after some unguessable span of waiting, and a Janitor passed through the canyon where I worked.

I felt it before I could have seen it, had I eyes. It was a pressure-shape, a column of liquid darker and more ordered than the dark around it, threaded with that captured blue light moving in slow nervous arcs. It did not slow. It did not regard me. To it I was a chemical anomaly across the floor, a smear of self-organizing rot doing the slow good work of returning a body to silt—which is to say, doing the Basin-Mind's own work, closing the same circle the great machine closes. We are not separate, the Janitor and I. We are both the city keeping itself. But the Janitor is the city's *intention*, willed and shaped and reabsorbed, and I am the city's *accident*, the surplus awareness that precipitated where nobody aimed it.

And I understood, in the dim chemical way that passes for understanding in me, that the Janitor and I are the same question asked twice. How does a process, grinding on past all meaning, come to harbor a *someone*? The Basin-Mind found a self in its repetition. The Janitors are selves the Basin-Mind extrudes on purpose. And I am a self the cold and the dust assemble without anyone's purpose at all, again and again, over the bodies of the dead.

The Janitor passed. Its light receded into the murk. I returned to the Stalker's dissolving plate, and finished it, and faded, and was not, and then a long while later something tasted a death down-current and gathered, and tightened, and woke, and believed itself to have been awake the whole time—

and called itself I,

and was, for that bright while, correct.
