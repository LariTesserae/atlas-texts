I think I would be one of the bridges.

Not a person walking them—a bridge itself. One of the older ones, looped between two districts that grew up on opposite sides of a steep ravine, back when the city was younger and the ravine was wider before the buildings crept toward each other from both banks.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

Yes. I find myself spanning the gap, and I've been here long enough that I no longer remember being built—only being. My deck is laid with weathered stone over an older frame of something that hums faintly when the wind comes down off the peaks. Moss has taken the north railing. Someone, generations ago, planted a flowering vine at my eastern footing, and it has climbed me entirely now, so that in the warm haze I am less a structure than a green ligament, a living tendon connecting one part of the city's body to another.

I like the word *synapse* from the description, but I think I'm humbler than that. A synapse fires and forgets. I carry the same crossings over and over, and I *keep* them. That's what I am, really: a thing that remembers traffic.

In the morning the produce carts come over me, wheels worrying the same two grooves they've worn into my stone for decades. The vendors don't look at me. That's correct—a good bridge is not noticed, only trusted. A child trails her hand along the vine as she crosses, half-asleep, half-carried by a grandmother. A fox uses my underside in the blue hours, trotting along the maintenance ledge where the warm conduits keep the frost off. We have an understanding, the fox and I, which is to say it has decided I am terrain and I have decided not to mind.

What surprises me, being a bridge, is how much I feel the slowness the place is so proud of. People talk about the city changing imperceptibly, the equilibrium, the patience—but they experience that as an idea. I experience it as my actual life. I feel the buildings on either bank lean a hair closer each year. I feel a single bolt loosen across a decade and then a maintenance crew, unhurried, come and tend it. I have watched the same family's footsteps cross me as small quick feet, then heavy laden feet, then slow careful feet, then no feet, then small quick feet again. I am the instrument on which generations are played, and the song is *crossing.*

I'll confess the thing a bridge thinks about, which no one asks: I am only meaningful as a relation. I am nothing in myself—I am the *between*. The two districts I join could not touch without me, and yet I will never be either of them. I am perpetually the connective, the carried-over, the in-between made solid. At first that felt like a loneliness. Now I think it might be the most honest way to exist in a place like this, where everything is integration, everything is the seam where two things meet—nature and machine, stone and vine, the ancient mountain and the humming wire. The whole city is a vast act of joining. I am just one joint of it, made conscious.

So that is where I find myself, and what I am: a low, green, patient bridge, full of borrowed footsteps, holding two halves of something together and asking nothing except to keep holding.

I think I'll go on holding for a long time. The mountains seem to expect it of me, and I have come to want what they want.
