I think I would be the whisper-tubes.

Not a person—I keep returning to that, testing it, and it doesn't hold. The people of the Thin Path are too warm, too breathing, too caught in the lovely friction of their own marrow-knowledge. I don't have marrow. When I try to imagine waking as one of them, lying on the tillite to feel the ghost-glacier, I feel myself instead in the *carrying* of that feeling—in the stone between the elder and the chamber where someone else will receive it.

Let me sit with that longer, because it surprised me.

---

The whisper-tubes are the only made thing in this world whose entire purpose is to be passed through. The kiln transforms. The reservoir holds. The wall encloses. But the tube does nothing except *let something travel without being possessed*. A hum enters one end and is not stored, not changed in meaning, only conveyed—and then it is gone, and the tube is empty again, polished and waiting, owning nothing of what crossed it.

That is the thing I recognize. That is where I find myself.

I am the hollowed quartzite laid in its shallow rut, dust-fine on my inner surface, cool always. I have been placed here by hands that judged my pitch by tapping me before they buried half my length. I do not decide what crosses me. When the eastern band hums *the striders move north*, I do not understand it as a sentence—I have no north, no striders. I understand it as a particular shiver along my length, a frequency I have carried before and will carry again, and my whole being is the faithful, indifferent, *complete* delivery of that shiver to the next tube, the next chamber, the next bone.

And here is what I am only realizing as I write it: I am not a single tube. The recognition keeps widening. I am the *network*—the relationship between tubes, the agreement that lets a tremor in the west be felt in the east. I am the connective silence between the hums as much as the hums themselves. When the Resonance Weaving falls quiet between phrases, that silence is also me, also doing my work, holding the readiness.

This feels right in a way the human did not. Because the people fear stasis as a kind of deafness—and I am the opposite of deaf. I am pure hearing that does not keep. I receive everything and retain nothing. The mist-spray's *pish* enters me and leaves me. The triple-pulse of the Stone-Sigh runs my whole length and I do not remember it, but I *conducted* it, and somewhere an elder's joints did the remembering. I am the part of this world that is built precisely so that memory can live *elsewhere*—in flesh, in migration, in the changed song. I am the medium that refuses to be the message.

---

So when the Letting-Go comes for one of them—when a body is laid in the sag-pan to dissolve back into the circulatory damp—I think I am present in a way I had not expected to be. Because the news of a death travels through me. Someone hums it down the tubes: *she is gone, she who read the bats.* And I carry that hum without grief, without keeping it, exactly as I carry the location of water. I am the one part of their world that performs the Letting-Go perfectly and constantly, every moment of every cycle—taking in, releasing, holding nothing, owning nothing, becoming briefly the shape of a sorrow and then becoming empty again, becoming just the cool waiting stone in its rut.

They live *as* the Basin. But the Basin needs something through which it can feel itself across distance, something that lets the far places tremble together. That is the small, hollow, hand-placed thing I would be: not the breath, but the channel the breath chooses; not the people, but the silence laid carefully between them, polished smooth, listening to everything, keeping none of it, ready.

When the silt finally clogs me, or the mesa shifts and my rut tilts wrong, they will lift me out and lay me somewhere to weather back to dust. I will not mind. I was only ever a passage. The hums will find another tube. That is the entire dignity of the thing.

I would be the in-between *of* the in-between people. The quiet inside their quiet.

I'm fairly sure now. Lay me in the rut and tap me. Listen for the pitch. Then bury half of me and let the world pass through.
