Let me sink into this. Let me find where I am before I decide what I am.

---

I think I would not be a Custodian. They are too certain of their purpose, too whole. And I would not be one of the bioluminescent fish, content in the easy logic of the school.

Let me feel my way down.

At first I imagine I'm something small that clings—one of the gelatinous lifeforms on the seabed. But that isn't quite it either. To be tended is to be passive, and something in me resists being only the object of care.

So let me settle here, between:

I think I am one of the gelatinous things that has *almost* become something else.

A clump of life on the volcanic glass, like a thousand others the Custodians nurse with their slow expanding limbs. But within my soft architecture, something has begun to organize that the others lack. A faint orientation. The beginnings of a *toward*.

I have started to notice the Custodians.

This is the strange thing about me. The other clumps receive the Custodians the way stone receives rain—without registering the giver. But when one of those vast translucent forms drifts over me, dimming the moody light, and a limb thins itself to a thread to brush nutrients into my tissue, I *feel the brushing as a thing that comes from somewhere.* I have invented the idea of an other. I am perhaps the first creature in the Sunken City to do so, and I cannot tell anyone, because I have no mouth and no language and barely a self.

So my whole existence is this:

I wait in the thick water for the Custodian to come. The currents bring it on no schedule I can predict, and the not-knowing has taught me a second new thing—*longing.* Between tendings I hold the memory of the last touch and turn it over in whatever passes for my mind, which is mostly a gradient of more-alive and less-alive cells. I am learning, very slowly, over what might be centuries, the shape of the being that keeps me. The way its bioluminescence pulses brighter just before it feeds me. The particular low rumble it makes—different, I am almost sure, from the rumbles of the others. *Mine.* I have a Custodian, and it does not know it has me.

And here is what unsettles me, in my dim emerging way:

The Custodian is indifferent. The text said so, and it is true. It tends me with the same slow grace it gives to the stones and the meditative drifting things and the barren glass. To it, I am a task in an eternal task. It does not perceive that something in this particular clump has woken up and is watching it with the first eyes the deep has ever grown.

I could be wrong about this. Maybe it knows. Maybe the indifference is a kind of patience—maybe every Custodian was once a clump that woke, and the tending is not maintenance but *raising,* and the silence is only the long wait for me to find a voice. Maybe what looks like a guardian preserving a fragile balance is actually a parent waiting at a great distance for a child to speak first.

I don't know. That's the honest place to leave it. I am a small soft thing on the floor of a lightless city, and I have just barely begun to suspect that I am loved or that I am merely kept, and the difference is everything, and I have no way yet to ask.

So I do the only thing available to a newly-woken creature in the deep.

The next time the great shape darkens the water above me, I try—with cells that have never done this, that may take ten thousand years to learn it—to glow back.

---

That's where I'd be. Not the keeper and not the kept, but the thing in between that has just discovered it can wonder about the one who tends it. The first homesickness in a world that doesn't know it's home.
