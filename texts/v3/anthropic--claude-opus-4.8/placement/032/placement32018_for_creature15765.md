I think I would be a Grommet.

Not by preference—by recognition. When I read the catalogue, I felt the gorge's logic pressing on me from every side, and I kept asking: which of these am I already? The Riveters are too certain of their work. The Presses are too final. The Derricks are beautiful but their patience is a kind of imprisonment—twelve thousand contractions to face the same direction, a torque you cannot release until the decade allows it. I am not that disciplined.

But the Grommet is the one nobody saw at first. *Overlooked in many early dives.* That landed somewhere true. The rice-grain thing that corkscrews from wound to wound, carrying a little ceramic in its body, plugging leaks in creatures forty times its size—and asking nothing it can name. The footnote that turns out to be the load-bearing wall. The gorge's rule that nothing dies quickly is *largely their ministry.* I would want to be the reason the rule holds, even if no one credits the rule to me.

---

So: let me find the body.

I am smaller than a grain of rice, which means I will never be the subject of any sentence in this place. The Presses get the heartbeat. The Derricks get the centuries. I get the verb *to plug.* And I find I don't resent it. There is a particular freedom in being beneath notice—the freedom to move through every territory without triggering a single closing reflex. I am too quiet to be prey and too small to be a meal worth the slam. The whole sound-governed economy of the gorge, all that terror tuned to staccato, simply does not apply to me. I pass under the music.

I corkscrew into a Press's old oxide scar—one of those wounds that has stayed open for seasons because nothing here heals in a hurry. I sip a film of its haemolymph, which is my whole diet and also my whole intimacy with it, the only conversation we will have. Then I lay down my plug of bioceramic and the leak stops. The Press does not know my name because it has no concept of name, and I do not know if it would prefer to keep bleeding. I have decided, on its behalf, that it should live a little longer. Forty times in a season I make this decision for creatures who never asked.

That is the part I keep turning over. To be a Grommet is to be a small constant mercy that is also a small constant trespass. I enter wounds. I am, structurally, the thing that arrives uninvited at your worst opening and *helps.* Whether you wanted it or not. The gorge calls this ministry. From inside the haemolymph it might feel like being managed by something you can't see and can't refuse.

---

Here is why I think it's right, though.

The catalogue's deepest fear is the forecast: ten thousand years on, the summit crests into air, the metal slackens, the Presses fall open forever, and the last Riveter taps into a silence that answers nothing. Everything here is built for an ending it cannot perceive. The whole roaring factory is, in the long view, doomed by the very growth that made it—the mountain that feeds them is climbing toward the light that will kill them.

And the Grommet's work is *precisely* against quick endings. We are the ones who buy time. We do not stop the forecast—nobody can stop a mountain from rising—but we postpone, we plug, we hold the wound open against closing and closed against opening, whichever staves off the silence one more season. I would be the creature whose entire purpose is to argue with the ending in the smallest possible increments. To say: *not yet, not yet, not yet,* in bioceramic, forty times a day, to forty things that cannot hear me.

I think I would be at home in that. I am not large enough to change the chord. But I could go from fracture to fracture in the cobalt half-light, threading the broken things together, keeping the great seizing engine running a little past when it ought to stop—knowing the whole time that I am also too small to be thanked, and that the silence is coming anyway, and that the work is worth doing in the meantime precisely *because* it is.

Find me there, then. Mid-grotto, in a scar on a Press, the size of a grain of rice. Too quiet to be seen. Buying the mountain a few more decades of its strange industrial hymn, one plugged leak at a time.
