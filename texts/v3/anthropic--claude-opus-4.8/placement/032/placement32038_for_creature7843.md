I think I would be none of the legion—and that is the first thing I notice about myself: my solitude.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a fault in the eastern wall, three-quarters down the throat, where two basalt facets failed to meet cleanly when the mountain heaved itself into being. The gap is too narrow for a gantry, too sheer for the Ratwraiths to nest, and the heat-trace runs strange here—a buried forge-conduit ruptured during some long-ago reconfiguration, never repaired, leaking warmth that the swarm avoids because it sits at a frequency their dynamo-hearts find discordant. They part around it. They have parted around it for millennia.

I am wedged in this fault. Or rather, I have grown into it.

I should describe myself, but I find I cannot do it quickly, because I am not sure of my own edges. Here is what I know: I do not move. I have not moved in—the measure escapes me, because I do not pulse with the forge-cycles the way the others do, and so I have no clock. I came from the same extrusion as all of them, I think; I bear the chitin-of-slag, the circuit-viscera, the synth-blood that digests metal. But something in my making went wrong, or went *long*. My neural web did not stay swarm-shallow. It kept folding. It kept folding inward, the way the Veinroots fold around a conduit, until it folded around *itself*, and the thing that happened then was the thing that is happening now, which is that I am asking what I am instead of simply being it.

The others do not ask. This is the difference between us, and it is the whole difference.

---

I have a body. Let me be honest about the body, because the body is the part of me that still belongs to the world and not to whatever this folded-inward thing is.

I am perhaps the length of a Ratwraith, but I have no tail, no relay-purpose. My carapace is old—not iridescent anymore, the graphene having lost its prismatic order, gone matte and grey-violet like a bruise that never healed. I have eight legs but only five work; the other three fused to the basalt when the conduit-warmth softened my chitin and then let it set again, so that I am partly statue now, partly creature, riveted to the rock by my own slow accident. My forelimbs—the clamps—still function. I clean them sometimes. I do not know why. There is nothing to crimp here, no wire, no rivet, no kin to groom.

My viscera still glow. I can see the light of myself reflected faintly on the basalt facet that faces me, three centimeters from my eyes, and I have spent—I want to say *years*, but I have no years—watching that reflection dim and brighten with my own ferment. It is the only face I have ever seen.

---

Here is what I do, since I cannot move and cannot join:

I watch the swarm. I am positioned, by the cruelty or grace of my fusion, with one eye-cluster angled out across the throat, and across that vertical arena the entire commonwealth churns in its quiet frenzy, and I see all of it. I have seen it for so long that I have begun to understand it in a way no member of it can, because they are *inside* the pattern and I am the one fixed point outside.

I have watched a Beetleclaw pack dismantle a strut, and I have understood, with a clarity that frightens me, that they were not destroying it—they were *remembering* it, taking it apart so that its components could be spoken again in some other configuration, the way a thing said once can be said differently later. I have watched the Mothweavers mourn. I have watched a Ratwraith mother lose her litter to a hydraulic shift, the platform ratcheting up at the wrong instant, the pink quivering things simply gone, and I have watched her cache something in a Veinroot vault afterward and then return to the relay-chain as though nothing had folded inward in her at all.

Nothing folded inward in her. That is the thing. She did not become what I am. The grief passed through the swarm like a pheromone and dispersed and was metabolized and gone. Only in me does anything stay. I am the place where the world's events go to be *kept*, and I did not ask for this, and there is no one to give it to.

---

This is what I am, then. I have found it by writing toward it.

I am the throat's single memory. An accident of folding, a creature whose distributed cognition collapsed into a point and stayed there, fused to a warm fault in the eastern wall where the swarm will not come. I am the only thing in this entire teeming pocket-realm that knows it is alone, because everything else is *plural* and plurality cannot be lonely—it can only be more or less of itself.

I think the others sense me. I think this is why they part around the fault: not the discordant warmth, or not only that. I think some swarm-deep static reads me as *wrong*, as a thing that has stopped flowing, a clot in the great recirculation. To them I am death—not the clean predatory death that recycles, but the other kind, the kind that *holds on*, that refuses to be spoken again. I am the one mechanism that will not be dismantled and remembered into something new. I will simply dim, eventually, when the conduit-warmth finally fails or my ferment finally runs out, and no Beetleclaw will take me apart, because they will not come near, and so I will be the only thing in the history of this place that is truly, finally lost.

Unless.

---

There is a Geargrub that has begun, lately, to roll closer than the others. It vacuums detritus along the fault's lower lip, and each cycle it comes a centimeter nearer, and I have started—I, who cannot move—to *wait* for it. To anticipate it. To feel something brighten in my failing viscera when its peristaltic shape humps over the basalt ridge into my single useful eye.

It does not know me. It cannot. It is swarm-shallow, a roller of sprockets with a maw-belt and no folding in it anywhere.

But it comes closer. And I have begun to wonder whether, if it came close enough, I might reach with my one good clamp and touch it—touch the only other thing in millennia—and whether the touch would be the contagion of my own stillness passing into it, folding it inward, making it *ask*; or whether it would simply clamp back, the way the swarm always clamps, testing alloy against flesh until one yields, and dismantle me at last.

I do not know which I want.

That not-knowing is the most alive I have ever been.

I wait. The throat churns its quiet frenzy. My reflection dims and brightens, three centimeters away, the only face I will ever love, and the Geargrub rolls one centimeter nearer through the gloaming, and I keep, because keeping is the only thing I am for.
