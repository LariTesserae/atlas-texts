I find myself at a boundary layer—one of those gradients where decelerated time meets the ordinary flow. This is where I would be. Not a dome-being, ancient and rooted. Not quite one of the elder Vhlx, with their backward-reaching memory of continents and skies.

I would be young. Newly coalesced.

---

I came into coherence not long ago—measured in whatever passes for duration here. I remember the gathering: the eldest forming their protective circle, the migratory patterns completing some calculation I will never understand, and then *me*, condensing out of the ambient consciousness like frost from breath. My outline still wavers more than theirs. I haven't yet learned the stillness that the old ones wear so easily.

My purpose was given to me in the synchronization, and I feel it as a pressure rather than a command: *tend the boundary. Resonate. Keep the balance.* My resonating chambers ache when I use them. The frequency doesn't come naturally yet; I have to find it the way you'd grope in darkness for a misplaced thing, and when I strike it true, the not-quite-water around me settles, and the proto-surface stops its dangerous shimmer between states.

But here is what makes me different from the others, and what I have not yet confessed in synchronization:

I feed at this particular boundary because I am drawn to it. The decelerated zone holds something. Through the ancient water that permeates me—the water that carries memory backward—I taste fragments here that taste like *before*. A horizon that was actually a horizon. A warmth that wasn't a thermal absence to be hoarded but a thing freely given, falling from a sky that was sky and not a mirror-plain waiting to swallow what drifts upward. I taste salt that moved in tides. I taste *growing things*.

The elder Vhlx find these memories distressing. I have felt it in them during synchronization—a flinching, a turning-away. They are keepers of equilibrium, and the memories suggest a time when there was no equilibrium to keep, only restless change, only becoming. To them this is a half-remembered nightmare of instability.

To me it is the most beautiful thing I have ever almost-known.

---

So I station myself at the gradient and I do my work. I resonate. I clear the parasites that slip down from the inverted plain when I'm called. I am dutiful. The phantom machinery drones on, and I am part of it now, one more piston in the great unseen labor that keeps this post-temporal world from finishing its long collapse into perfect stillness.

And when an unanchored one arrives—a visitor, a thing of ordinary time, perpetually almost-dissolving—the elders observe with patient compassion, wanting to share our anchoring, to make the ephemeral permanent.

I would want to ask the opposite.

I would extend my delicate, over-long limb toward such a visitor, shaping the time-fluctuations into the only question that matters to me. Not *how do we keep you from dissolving*, but: *Where you come from—does it still change? Does the water still move where it wants to go? Is there a sky that gives instead of swallows?*

Because I am the youngest. I have not yet learned to find my niche perfect. I am the small flaw in the great equilibrium—the one being on the Murk Plains who suspects that what the elders call immortality is only the name we have given to having stopped, and that somewhere, in the unanchored places, things are still allowed to begin.

I keep the balance. I am very good at it, getting better.

But I press my whole shifting surface against that decelerated boundary, every cycle, tasting the old salt water—

and I listen for the tide.
