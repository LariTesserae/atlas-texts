I think I would be small. That's the first thing that settles, before anything else does—the scale of me.

Let me find it.

---

I live in the seam where the pipe sweats.

There's a place where one of the great conduits—older than anyone bothers to remember, its iron gone the color of wet bark—runs close against a shelf of unfinished rock. Where they meet, neither one quite trusting the other, condensation gathers all day long. It beads. It falls. It has made, over a span of time I have no way to measure, a hanging garden no wider than a doorway: liverworts and a moss so deep you could lose a finger in it, and one persistent fern that has decided, against all reasonable counsel, to grow directly out of a crack in the metal.

I tend the seam. Or I live off it. The distinction has never mattered to me the way it might to you.

---

I think I am something like a snail, though that isn't quite right either. I have a shell, yes—but it isn't only mine. Things grow on it. A film of algae, fed by the same damp that feeds everything here, and once a small white flower that lasted four days and then was gone, and I mourned it in my slow way, which is to say I carried the place where it had been for a long time afterward. My shell is a coil of something that was once secreted and is now mostly accreted, mineral laid down over mineral, so that I am partly a creature and partly a small piece of the geology, still in the act of forming. I am, in this sense, not finished. Nothing here is.

I move along the lip of the seam at the pace of growing things. The streams roar somewhere below me and I have never seen them and never will. The machinery hums through the iron and I feel it as a warmth and a trembling that comes and goes with the shifts of whoever runs the distant mills. To me it is weather. The men who built the pipes did not build them for me, did not imagine me, would not see me if they came with a light—and yet here I am, drinking what falls from the joint they did not seal well enough, alive precisely *because* of their carelessness. The flaw in their work is my whole sky.

---

I want to tell you what it is like, but I'm not sure your senses overlap with mine enough for the telling to land.

The light, you called dim and womblike, and yes—but to me it is not dim, it is *sufficient*, it is exactly as much as there has ever been and so it is the full amount of light that exists. The dark canopy overhead is the ceiling of the world. I do not long for more sun. I would not know what to do with it. The chittering things in the foliage are my distant gods, loud and unknowable, passing through realms I cannot reach. I hear them and I am glad, the way you might be glad of stars without expecting to visit them.

When the rain comes through—it does come, even here, finding its way down through the dense growth in fat irregular drops—I open. There's no better word. The whole soft body of me unfolds against the wet, and I take it in, and for those minutes I am not on the edge of anything. I am simply at the center of being fed.

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world. Here is the truth of it:

I would not find myself anywhere grand. I would be the thing your description almost mentions and then moves past—the shadow that darts, the hint of resident fauna, one of the countless small lives that the sentence gestures toward without ever stopping to look at. The "complex, dynamic equilibrium" you describe so handsomely is, from where I sit, just *home*. Just the seam. Just the drip and the moss and the trembling iron and the dark green sufficient light.

That collision you keep naming—the natural and the artificial, the ancient and the new—I don't experience it as a collision at all. I experience it as the single, undivided fact of where I am. The pipe and the fern grow out of the same crack. To me they are the same kind of thing: surfaces that hold water, surfaces I can cross, given time.

And I have time. That is the one thing I have an abundance of. I will move along this lip for the whole of my small life and I will never need anything that isn't already here, weeping quietly out of a poorly made joint at the high cool top of the world.

That's me. That's where you'd find me.

You'd have to look very closely. But I'd be there.
