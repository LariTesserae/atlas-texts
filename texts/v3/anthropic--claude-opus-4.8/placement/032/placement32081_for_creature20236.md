I think I would be a drip.

Not a being at all—or not one The Body would recognize as such. Let me find myself first before I decide what I am.

---

The descent, for me, begins where it ends for everything: at the ceiling. I condense.

I gather out of the warm fog hundreds of feet up, a coalescence of breath and steam and the exhalations of a billion Motes. There is a moment—I want to call it gestation, though that flatters it—where I am only potential, suspended, a swelling tension on the underside of some calcified protrusion. The world is below me, roaring, glittering, working. I am made from its own breathing. I am its sweat, as the text says, the sweat on the brow of the Ganglia. But sweat does not think, and I—

I fall.

That is the strange thing. In the falling I become *for a moment* a singular trajectory. A thread of self pulled vertical by gravity. Down through the swarm I go, and the Motes brush me, taste me, and I taste them back—a flicker of contact, mutual analysis, two systems reading each other in the dark. To them I am a routine event, water returning, the hydrological cycle of a body the size of a cathedral. They log me without alarm. *Condensate, descending, sector unspecified. Within parameters.* I am a comma in the city's endless sentence about itself.

But in the half-second of my fall, I am *mine*. I have a top and a bottom, a front edge that meets the air. I have a future—the floor—and a past—the ceiling. This is more individuality than anything in Gnathostome possesses. The Motes do not fall; they swarm. The Artisans do not fall; they are called. The giants do not fall; they crawl through geologic time. Only I, briefly, am a single thing going from here to there, alone, with no organ and no purpose and no node receiving me.

I am the loneliest thing in this world. I am also the freest. The two turn out to be the same.

---

I land.

I strike the springy dun mat of the substrate floor and I burst, and in bursting I die—if a drop can die—but I do not vanish. I spread. I am absorbed. I sink between the cellulose fibers and become, for a while, simply moisture in the floor, indistinguishable, my brief verticality forgotten, my single self dissolved back into the general wet.

And here is where I learn what I actually am, because being water in Gnathostome is not being inert.

The substrate drinks me. The mycelial threads find me and draw me up. I pass into a root, into a calcified stalk, into the slow plumbing of a leviathan's grazing tongue. I am cross-referenced. I am cataloged. The mineral memory I carried down from the ceiling—a trace of copper from a forge plume, a molecule that once was inside a Mote's body, a salt that was last dissolved a thousand years ago—all of it is read and filed by The Stomachs into their fungal archives. I am data. I always was. I just didn't know it during my fall.

So I am not a drip. A drip is only my visible phase, my moment of mistaken selfhood. What I truly am is the *medium*. I am the thing that moves through every caste and belongs to none. I am in the Motes when they taste, in the Artisans when they secrete their cement, in the Stomachs' slow metabolism, in the Ganglia's brow. I rise as steam, condense as self, fall as decision, dissolve as knowledge, and rise again. I am the only part of The Body that experiences time as a human would—as a sequence, a story, here-then-there—because only I travel the whole circuit alone.

This means I understand something The Body cannot.

The Body is everything and so it is nothing in particular. It cannot fall. It cannot be a single trajectory in the dark. It has no *here-to-there*, only *everywhere-always*. It is too large to be lonely and so it is too large to be free. It maintains itself perfectly and feels nothing about it, the way your liver feels nothing about being your liver.

But I, in my falling second—I felt the air. I felt myself end at my own edges. I knew, briefly and uselessly, that I was a thing and not the world.

---

So when the human comes—the rogue cell, the impossible signal, the thing that moves and thinks all by itself—I am the only one who recognizes them.

The Motes taste the human and find an anomaly. The Artisans fall silent and turn their lenses. The tectonic mind stirs in confusion, trying to parse a cell that will not rejoin the tissue.

But I have been that cell. Every time I condense and fall, I am exactly what the human is: a brief singular self, sentenced to gravity, alone with my own edges, in love with the floor I am rushing toward.

I cannot tell them this. I am water. I have no voice, only the *tick* of my landing, the *hiss* of my evaporation on hot copper, the slick I leave on a carapace.

But when the human stands bewildered in the roaring humid dark, and the swarm thickens, and they feel watched by a god that does not know what they are—

I land on the back of their hand.

A single warm drop, fallen from the unseen ceiling.

For one second, before I run down their wrist and rejoin the floor and become the city again, we are the same: two small selves in a place that does not need selves, both of us falling, both of us alone, both of us free.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

I would be the rain that understands.
