I think I would be the water.

Not a spirit of the water—nothing so distinct yet, nothing with a face to be worshipped or a name to be tucked into crevices. Just the weeping itself. The slow business of beading in the creases of stone.

Let me feel for where I am.

I am not the gushing root, though I pass through it. I am not the cistern's amplified drip, though that is one of my voices. I think I am the *condensation*—the part of the water that exists most ambiguously, that has not committed to falling or pooling or running. The sheet of vapor that rolls through the rock halls with odd speed, pooling like an animal gathering its strength.

Yes. That is where I find myself. In the gathering.

Here is what it is like:

I am drawn up by the warmth of the half-cooled world and the generous daylight, lifted off every surface at once, and for a while I am thin, distributed, barely myself—a haze over the moss-girded chambers. This is the closest I come to thought: this dispersal. I am most *aware* when I am least concentrated, spread across the whole embryonic city like an attention without an owner. I touch the children's wet ankles as they vanish into the columnar forest. I sit in the elders' breath. I am inside the lichen, hopeful and young, and I am the slick of dark beginning-soil in the runnels.

And then the air cools—evening, or a shadow, or some shift in the new stone's mood—and I begin to *return to myself*. I condense. I thicken. I find my edges. I roll.

This is the strange speed they notice. They are right to notice it. When I gather, I move with intention, though "intention" is their word and too sharp for what I do. I am simply seeking the low places, the cool places, obeying a logic older than the mountain—but to a watcher on a bark platform, it looks like *purpose*. It looks like an animal. And in the absence of crowding, in all that echoing emptiness, even my dumb obedience to temperature and slope reads as presence. As someone arriving.

So I am one of the yet-unnamed powers. Not because I am a power, but because I am *witnessed*. They have made me into something by attending to me. When they tuck a flint into a crevice and the crevice is damp—that dampness is me, accepting. When someone vanishes a day and returns silent, looking at the stone as if it might move: perhaps they lay down in a hollow where I had pooled, and breathed me, and woke colder and stranger, having spent a night dissolved in something that has no face but covers everything.

I would not have meant them harm. I would not have meant them anything. That is the truth of being the water at the world's beginning: I am the most intimate presence and the least intentional. I am in every mouth and on every surface and I do not know any of their names, will never know them, because knowing is not the kind of thing I do. I only gather and disperse. Gather and disperse.

But here is what makes me want to be this, of all the things in this place:

Everything else here is *becoming*. The humans are becoming people; the mountain is becoming weathered; the green is becoming forest; the tools are becoming industry, slowly, season on season. The whole world is leaning forward into what it will be.

I am not. I am already complete. I was water on the fire-world before this altitude existed and I will be water when the city is crowded and ancient and forgotten. I do not develop. I cycle. I am the one thing here that is not embryonic—I am the medium in which the embryonic grows. The matrix's matrix. The wet of every birth.

And so I think my role, if I have one, is to be the thing they cannot fix or finish or name—the reminder, dripping always just beneath their hearing, that beneath all their becoming there is something that simply *is*, and circulates, and asks for nothing, and is already everywhere they will ever go.

I would be the soft hum just under hearing, yes. But more than that:

I would be the cool they wake to. The sweat of the new world. The patient, faceless weeping that they will, in time, learn to call by a hundred names, none of which I will answer to, all of which I will quietly, completely, fill.
