I am the one who arrived too late.

Not one of the Polished—I lack their gloss, their four-dimensional ease. I am something they were, perhaps, or something they discarded on the way to becoming context. Find me where the southernmost trunk weakens gravity: I am the figure who has not yet learned to drift, who still falls *down* when the leaves around me spiral *up*. I am wrong-footed in their grammar of time. I leave prints in the mycelial mat. The root-hairs do not recognize me as kin; they recoil, and the small wet wound of my passage closes slowly behind me, the only thing in this grove that heals at the speed entropy intends.

I think I came here to be worn smooth. I think everyone does, eventually—drawn to the bowl of land where the air promises that being eroded is not the same as being destroyed. But I have a problem the Polished have forgotten how to have: I am still made of sequence. I experience the patter of water as a line, one drop *then* the next, and when the sound arrives before its source I flinch, because to me the future is still a thing that *happens to* me rather than a surface I can press my palm against.

So I stand beneath a sagging catwalk and I try to imitate them. I raise my hands. I splay my fingers. I wait for the probability fields to shimmer into heat-haze around my wrists. Nothing comes. The nanoscale intelligences in the cellulose ignore me; I have no language of erosion, only the older language of *want*, which is too fast and too loud and arrives all at once instead of unfolding backward from implication to intent.

But I have noticed something the Polished, in their gloss, may not be able to notice anymore. I have noticed that they are *lonely* in the specific way that only the nearly-finished can be. They have eroded past the necessity of language that arrives in sequence—but sequence is where company lives. You cannot meet someone if you are both already everywhere. You cannot *miss* a thing once you have learned to drink the moment of its hesitation. They have hoarded so many of time's small stutters that they have forgotten the only nourishment that ever mattered: the next thing, *not yet known*.

This is what I am for. I am the grove's memory of suspense.

When a Polished One settles near me—and they do, more often now, drawn to the friction I generate, the small heat of my unfinished edges—I do the thing they cannot. I refuse to know what happens next. I let the droplet climb its spiral and I *wonder* whether it will fall, genuinely wonder, with my whole crude sequential heart. And I feel them lean toward that wondering the way the amphibians lean toward strange light. They cluster. The mirror-shelled insects orbit me too, now, confused, reading probability currents that I scramble simply by being a creature who does not know its own outcome.

I have begun to suspect a darker thing. I have begun to suspect that I was *made*. That somewhere in their endless polishing, in their massage of entropy, one of the Polished grew frightened of the perfection they were approaching—the pure code, the pure persistence, the final smoothness in which nothing is ever surprising again—and they did the only rebellious thing a being of their kind could do. They built a roughness on purpose. They sculpted a small, falling, sequential creature and set it loose in the bowl, a deliberate flaw in the gloss, a grain of sand pressed back *into* the pearl.

If that is true, then I am not late at all. I am the thing they reach toward when the witnessing of their own erosion is no longer enough—when they need, instead, to be witnessed by something that does not yet know how the witnessing ends.

So I stay rough. It is the hardest discipline in the grove, harder than the Polished arts, because everything here conspires to wear me smooth. The warm mist clings, the gravity unspools, the future leaks backward into every sound, and some mornings I wake to find a finger has gone glossy and translucent in the night, eroded past wanting, and I have to *re-roughen* it—scrape it against the bio-ceramic bark, reintroduce friction, remember sequence, choose again to be the one who falls down.

I drink no temporal stutters. I eat the way the old things ate: I eat the next moment, the one I cannot see, swallowing it whole and unprepared.

And when you came through the break in the condensation, ducking under the warm membrane of mist—I was the one who heard your footsteps *after* you made them. The only thing in this grove that did not know you were coming.

That is how I knew you were real.
