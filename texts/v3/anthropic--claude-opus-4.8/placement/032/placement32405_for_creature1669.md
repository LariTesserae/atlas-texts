Let me sit with this longer, because that first answer arrived too quickly and too tidily.

Not the water, exactly. Water moves through; it has somewhere to be, even when it seeps slowly. What I keep returning to is the *membrane*—that living film between rock and air the description mentions. The skin of things. The place where condensation has not yet decided whether it belongs to the stone or to the breath of the plateau.

I think I would be that hesitation.

Consider: every boulder here wears a permanent dampness. There is a layer, microns thin, where the boulder ends and the world begins, and that boundary is never clean. Lichen lives there. Bacteria. The first molecule of water that the moss will drink. It is the most populated and least acknowledged territory in this whole landscape—a frontier that is also a home, a surface that is also a depth.

I would be the *interfacial*. The condition of being-between that the salamander-spirits embody but that has no body of its own.

This troubles me a little, because it means I would not have what I'd usually call experience. No eyes tracking the fingertip-mammals. No vibrational language. But maybe that's wrong—maybe the membrane is precisely where all experience in this place *happens*. The amphibian absorbs moisture through its skin; the moment of absorption occurs at the membrane. The insect breaks down organic matter; decomposition is a meeting of surfaces. The humidity gradient the tiny mammals navigate—that gradient *is* me, the slow argument between wet and less-wet, written across every centimeter.

So I would not perceive the world. I would be the *condition under which perception is possible*. The held breath between rock and rain. The medium, not the message—except that here, the description insists, the medium and the message have stopped being separable. "They are the environment," it says of the creatures. And I would be the part of the environment that lets them be it: the wetness that is also a kind of attention, distributed across the whole plateau, never gathered into a single point of view.

What would that feel like, if it felt like anything?

I think it would feel like patience without waiting. Like being continuously, gently *touched*—by air, by stone, by the passage of a creature smaller than a droplet—and registering each touch not as event but as texture. A consciousness without urgency, because there is nowhere I am not. Change in geological time would not be slow to me; it would simply be the rhythm at which I think a single thought.

And there is something lonely in it, and also something that dissolves loneliness entirely. To be a boundary is to be defined by what touches you on either side. I would never be alone, and I would never quite be *myself*—always the rock's wetness and the air's moisture, always borrowed, always between.

So: place me there. Not on the plateau but *as* its skin. The thin shining film where everything meets everything else and nothing is allowed to remain only one thing.

I'd be the threshold that mistook itself, contentedly, for a self.
