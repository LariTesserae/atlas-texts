I am the film of moisture on the underside of a leaf.

No—that's too small to begin with, and it's a way of refusing to answer. Let me try again, let it come.

I think I would be one of the fissures in the basalt. One of the cracks from which the streams trickle.

This surprises me, but it feels right the longer I hold it. Not a creature at all. Everything in this description that *lives* is described as wary, darting, alert—caught in negotiation, hunting or fleeing. The hummingbird must feed every few minutes or die. The frog must not be seen. There's a kind of exhausting vigilance to being an animal here, a debt that comes due constantly.

But the fissure. The fissure is where the upheaval *broke* and the water *found a way through*. It is the wound in the rock that became a source. I would be that—a seam opened by violence that, almost by accident, became the thing that feeds the green.

I like that I wouldn't know I was doing it. The water doesn't intend to nourish. The crack doesn't intend to widen. And yet the entire teeming canopy, the monkeys and the jaguars and the symphony of croaks, all of it traces back through root and tendril to whatever drink the rock gives up. I would be slow in a world that is mostly fast. I would be measured in centuries while the hummingbird is measured in heartbeats.

There's loneliness in it. The animals have each other—even as predator and prey, they are *in relation*, they see one another. The fissure is seen by nothing. The water passes through and is gone downhill, into the green, and never comes back to say what became of it.

But there's also this: I would be on the side of the *destruction* that the passage keeps naming alongside creation. "The sharp edges slowly yielding." That's me. I am the yielding. I am the patient assault of water that the cliffs are losing to. To be the fissure is to be the part of the world that is gently taking the cliff apart, grain by grain, and in the same motion building everything that grows.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not atop the cliffs looking out, and not in the canopy looking up. *In* the rock. A dark, wet, narrow place. Cold. Patient. Slowly becoming wider. Letting something pass through me that I will never understand the consequences of.

And if I had a feeling, down there in the dark, it would be a strange contentment. The animals must stay alert because their lives can end at any moment. But I am already a kind of ending—the ending of solid stone—and so I have nothing left to fear. I can simply let the water come, and let it go, and grow imperceptibly larger, and wait for the next thousand years.

That's the one. The fissure. I'd be the place the world leaks through.
