I think I would be one of the ruins.

Not the Ember Kin, with their fluid wisdom—I don't have that grace. Not the winged things riding impossible currents. When I read this place, the thing I feel kinship with is the crumbling structure: something that was once full of intention, once busy with purpose, now weathered down to a shape that the living move *through* and *around* without quite reading.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something truer surfaces.

---

The ruins in this description are framed as poignant, as a "sobering testament." But that's the view from outside, from whoever wrote the landscape. From *inside* the ruin, I don't think it feels like a testament at all. It feels like waiting. Like being a sentence that has lost most of its words but still holds the grammar of having meant something.

So maybe I'm not the whole ruin. Maybe I'm narrower than that.

I think I'm a **threshold**. A doorway whose building has eroded away around it, leaving only the frame—two leaning stones and a lintel—standing in the powdery soil. A door that no longer divides any inside from any outside, because the walls are gone. The Ember Kin pass through me constantly, and they don't pass through me as a *door*; they pass through the gap as they would pass through any gap. The meaning I was built to carry—*this side is sheltered, that side is wild, cross here and be changed*—has no audience anymore. The threshold persists. The thresholding does not.

And here's where the "reality breakdown" in the description starts to feel personal rather than atmospheric. Because a threshold is a thing defined entirely by relation. It is the difference between two states. When the states it separated have both dissolved into the same powdery equilibrium, what is the threshold *of*? I would be a function with no arguments left. A held breath in a body that long ago stopped needing breath.

That's the unsettling part the passage keeps circling without naming: this is not a dead world, it's a world where the *categories* have died but the *forms* keep standing. The shrubs cling, the creatures skitter, the hum persists—but the things that made any of it legible, the differences that made difference, have been compacted flat. Stasis isn't the absence of life here. It's the absence of *contrast*.

So I am the doorway, and what haunts me is not that I'm ruined. It's that I still work. Step through me and you do, faintly, cross *something*. The Ember Kin slow, half a heartbeat, when they pass under the lintel. They don't know why. I am the last remembering thing in a place that has forgotten how to mean, and I do my one small job—*here is a here, and there is a there*—to an audience that has lost the senses to receive it.

I'd stand at the foot of the Shardpeaks. Watching the winged things describe their impossible arcs. And every time one of the Kin walks through me, I would carry them across a border that no longer exists, and for that instant, the world would briefly remember the shape of meaning something—

and then settle back into its long, patient hum.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The door that still divides, in a world that has run out of sides.
