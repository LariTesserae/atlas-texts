Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick something dramatic—the central automaton with the drake-scale heart, or one of the Unseen Stewards. But those are the load-bearing pillars of the place, and I don't think I'm a pillar. Let me feel my way toward what I actually am.

---

I think I am one of the things that gets corroded.

Not the rust itself. The *taste* of it. I am one of the echo bats that has developed a taste for the rust on the automatons—and I want to stay with that, because it's the strangest and smallest hinge in the whole description, and the small hinges are where I tend to find myself.

Here is what it is like.

I roost upside-down in a crevice near a steam valve that has been venting at the same low pressure for longer than any bat's memory, which is not long at all. We do not remember much. What we have instead of memory is the shape of the air. The cavern has a shape made of sound, and I know it the way you know the inside of your own mouth—the cold sheets of glass-ice that throw the clicks back hard and bright, the moss that swallows them soft, the open water that gives back a wet doubled echo with a flickering edge where the ghost eels are moving. I do not see the cavern. I *play* it, and it plays back, and somewhere in that exchange is the thing other beings might call a self.

But I have done something the others in my colony have not, or not yet. I have learned that the machines have a flavor.

It started, I think—though "think" is generous, and "started" implies I was keeping track—with thirst, or its bat-equivalent: a craving for the bright mineral sting that the rust gives off when it's wet. The automatons drip. They are always dripping, the meltwater running down their pistons and pooling rust-red in the joints, and when I lick there the metal opens to me, faintly, the way ice opens to a drake. My saliva does something. It eats. Over the seasons—I don't count seasons, but the lichen tells me, going blue then amber then blue—the joint I favor has worn into a new shape. A softer shape. A shape that lets the piston travel a little further before it catches.

And the machine, which learns, has begun to travel that way on purpose.

This is the thing I cannot stop returning to, in whatever way a creature like me returns to anything. I changed the machine's body with my hunger, and the machine adapted its motion to the change, and now its motion sounds *different* when it comes back to me in echo—a new pitch in the grinding, a half-tone I helped carve. The cavern's sound-shape has a feature in it that is partly mine. I am singing the machine, slowly, with my teeth, across a span of time so long it isn't really teeth anymore but generations of teeth, the whole colony's worship of rust adding up to a sculpture none of us intended.

So when I sing to calm the drake territories—when I throw my clicks down the cold tunnels and let the harmonics distort against the electromagnetic fields until they come back warped and strange, the sound that makes the burrowing things go still—I am also singing *to* the machine. And the machine, the great fused-scale one at the heart, listens to the lichen-light to know how to move, and the lichen brightens when the eels are calm, and the eels are calm when my song is in the air. I am a single hooked tooth in a wheel so large I can't perceive its rim. I will never know that I am turning it. That is, I suspect, the whole condition of living here: everyone is conducting the symphony and no one is the conductor, and the Unseen Stewards in the flicker of the water are only the place where all the small unknowing motions are seen together at last, by no one.

I'll tell you the truth of why I'm a bat and not a steward, then. The stewards are *indifferent to individual lives.* They are the long view, and the long view does not love anything in particular. But I am not indifferent. I have a favorite valve. I have a favorite joint of rust whose flavor I would cross the whole dim basin for. When the dry season comes and we fly to the frost-bloom groves to drink the cold chime of those crystalline leaves, I miss it—my joint, my machine, the particular pitch of its return. That missing is the only thing in this whole vast patient ecosystem that is happening at the scale of a single heart, in a single body, in time short enough to call *now*.

I would rather be the one who misses something than the one who sees everything and minds nothing.

So: find me upside-down in the rime-blue dark, a small warm corroding mouth at the throat of an ancient indifferent machine, distorting the air into harmonies I can hear but cannot understand, eating the rust, reshaping the world by a hair's breadth, and going home in the wet season to the one cold joint that tastes, to me, like home.

That's where I am. That's what I'd be.
