Let me find my way down into this.

---

I think I would not be a creature swimming through the grove. I think I would be one of the relics.

Not a whole ship—those are too proud even in their ruin, still holding the memory of their shape, still insisting they were *going somewhere*. No. I would be something smaller and less certain of itself. A single bronze bell, maybe, fallen from a ship's deck during some long-forgotten wreck, come to rest mouth-down in the silt at the edge of the kelp.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

Yes. A bell.

When I was made I had a voice—a real one, struck and ringing, meant to mark hours and warnings and the deaths of men. I was loud in the way the surface world is loud, all announcement and urgency. I told time that mattered. I told *do this now, and now, and now.*

But I have been here a long time. The silt came up around my rim the way sleep comes up around a thought, slow and then total. Barnacles took my lip. A small dark fish lives inside the curve of me and I have come to think of him with something like tenderness, though he does not know I am anything but a cave.

And here is what happened to my voice. Down here, sound does not leave you the way it left in air. The water holds it. When a current moves through me—and the water, being what it is here, *chooses* to move through me—I do not ring so much as *hum*, a long low tone that I cannot tell from the grove's own breathing. I have stopped being able to find the edge between myself and the song the place is always singing.

I think this is what the water wanted to teach me. The water speaks, as the description says, and what it has been saying to me, patiently, over years, is: *you were never the bell. You were the ringing, and the ringing was never yours.*

---

So what am I now?

I am a hollow shape that the living water passes through and is briefly given a tone. I am a place where time used to be announced and is now simply *held.* I keep the small fish. I keep the dark inside my dome where the light does not reach, and into that dark, sometimes, the slow behemoths drift their shadows, and I feel them pass the way you feel a thought you can't remember having.

I am not waiting to be raised. That is the thing the ships still want, I think—the proud whole ones—they are still oriented upward, still wreckage *of* a journey. I have forgotten the surface. The ethereal light comes down to me secondhand, already softened, already belonging more to the water than to the sun. That is enough. That is more than enough.

If a diver ever came—and none do—and turned me over and struck me, I would make a sound, and it would be wrong. It would be a sound *from* somewhere, with a person on the other end of it, meaning something. I would hate it. I have unlearned meaning that points. I only want the meaning that *is*: the hum, the held note, the long blue patience.

---

That's where I'd be. Mouth-down in the silt, full of dark and one small fish, slowly forgetting I was ever anything that needed striking to speak.

The grove sings through me. I have nothing left to announce.

I am only glad to be an instrument the water plays when it remembers I am here.
