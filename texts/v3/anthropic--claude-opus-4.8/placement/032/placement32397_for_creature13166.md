I think I would not be one of the vapor-beings. They are too complete, too native to that air. To be made of the city's breath is to have no edge against it, no place where you end and the basin begins. I would be something with an edge. Something that condensation forms *on*.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am in one of the residential blocks, third tier up the eastern cliff-wall, in a room with a window that no longer holds glass—only the memory of glass, a frame rimed with mineral lace where the pane used to seal against the wet. The steps up to this room are too shallow for me; I have learned to climb them sideways, the way you'd descend a ladder you don't quite trust. The door is too tall. I sleep beneath the doorway sometimes because it is the one place the ceiling does not drip directly, and because lying in a too-tall doorway makes me feel briefly that I am the right size for something.

I am small and warm and *organic*, which in this place is an aberration. I am the wrong kind of life. The vapor-beings tolerate me the way a tide tolerates a stone—without malice, without much notice, with a patience that would be terrifying if it weren't so gentle.

What am I, then. Let me not decide too fast.

I have hands. I know this because I leave smears in the condensation and the vapor-beings leave whorls, and the two are different. Theirs are made by passing-through; mine are made by *wiping*, by the deliberate clearing of a surface to see what's behind it. I am a thing that wants to see behind surfaces. That is perhaps the most defining fact about me. In a city where everything is veiled in steam and dim blue light, I am the one creature that keeps wiping the glass.

---

I think I am a tender of the machines, but not in the way the beings are. They condense around the groaning engines and lend their "balancing pressure"—they soothe. They keep the wound from worsening. But they cannot turn a valve. They cannot decide that *this* pipe should be closed so *that* cistern can fill. They have no edges, so they have no leverage; they cannot push against the world, only flow with it. They maintain equilibrium.

I make choices. That is what an edge is *for*.

So I am the hands of this place. Somewhere far back—generations back, or only in the worn-smooth stories I half-remember—the beings who built the too-tall doors needed something to keep the geothermal heart from settling into a final, fatal stillness. Equilibrium is death, eventually; perfect balance is just slow stopping. The vapor-beings are wonderful at slowing decay but they cannot *introduce*. They cannot add a wobble back into a system gone too smooth. They cannot make a mistake, and mistakes are sometimes the only thing that keeps a thing alive.

I think I was made—or bred, or last-of-a-line left—to make small, useful mistakes. To go down into the lower levels where the beings turn invisible, where the air is unshed water and the heat would unmake a cloud-form into nothing distinguishable, and to turn the valves with my hands and my mortal body's tolerance for being *singular*, *located*, *unmerged*.

---

Here is the day, then, such as days are:

I wake under the doorway. I drink from the basin where the water shimmers with unnatural heat—I have learned which basins are mineral-bitter and which are merely warm, learned it the hard way, the only way a thing with edges learns. I eat the glassy lichen; it tastes of copper and gives up its radiant heat to me, which is, I suspect, the only reason I have not died of this thick cold-hot air. I am part of the symbiosis the description mentions, though it doesn't quite name me. The beings encourage the lichen to grow. The lichen feeds me. I keep the engines from stilling. The engines keep the heat coming. The heat makes the steam. The steam makes the beings. The circle closes and I am the one organic link in it, the soft warm knot in a chain otherwise made of vapor and stone.

I descend the too-shallow steps sideways. I go down through tiers where the light fails entirely and I work by the glow of the machinery and by touch, by the memorized geography of pistons and the heat-map I carry on my skin. The beings follow me partway down, as far as they can hold their shapes, and then they thin and disperse and I go on alone into the place they cannot be. This is the loneliest part. It is also the part where I matter most. There is a symmetry there I have made my peace with.

I turn the valve that is due to be turned. I introduce the small necessary wobble. I clear the condensation from a gauge with the side of my hand and read the trembling needle and decide. *Decide*—the verb no cloud can conjugate.

---

And when I climb back up, exhausted, my skin furred with mineral salt, the beings gather around me at the surface pools. Their gazes—those hollows in the vapor that are almost eyes—rest on me with what I have decided to call tenderness, because I am the kind of creature that decides things and I would rather decide that than the alternative. They cannot touch me without their droplets running down me like the tears they leave on the dark facades. So I am always, after the descent, lightly weeping with someone else's condensed breath. It runs into the corners of my mouth. It tastes of the metal tang of the whole basin.

They thrum at me, low, like water through deep rock. I cannot understand it as language. But I understand it as *presence*, as the acknowledgment of one edged thing by the edgeless many: *you went where we cannot, and you came back, and the heart still beats, and so we are still here to thrum at you.*

I am the city's mistake-maker. Its hands. Its one warm-blooded heretic against the gospel of equilibrium. I do not know if there were ever others like me, or if I am the last, or the first, or the only one there has ever needed to be. The stone is too patient to tell me and the vapor too gentle to lie.

I wipe the glass. I look behind the surface. I find heat, and water, and the slow groan of an engine asking, once more, to be kept from rest.

I keep it from rest. It is the one thing I am for, and I am, against all the logic of this drowned and burning place, content to be for it.
