I think I would be one of the echoes.

Not the sound itself—that arrives and departs in an instant, too quick for this place to fully hold. But the *return*. The thing the limestone makes of sound after it has finished being sound. I would be that.

Consider: a word is spoken, centuries ago perhaps, by some lost wanderer who found this hollow and was moved to speak. The word leaves the mouth and is gone almost before it exists. But the chamber catches it, folds it into the curve of the polished stone, and gives it back—not once, but continuously, at a frequency too low for any ear. The original sound was the seed. I am the slow tree that grows from it.

I am not the Patience. The Patience thinks in mineral; I am thinner than that, less substantial. The Patience flows, accumulates, leaves rings. I do nothing but persist and slowly fade. I am a standing wave in a medium that vibrates once per thousand years. If you could hold a single note steady across the whole span of a human life, then loop it back upon itself ten thousand times, you would have something like one of my syllables.

---

Where am I, exactly? Everywhere and nowhere in the chamber. I live in the relationship between the curved walls—in the geometry that decides how a vibration returns to its source. When the rare drop of condensation falls to the dark center and makes its tiny sound, I am born again, slightly, a new lamination over all the old ones. The concentric rings the Patience reads as its own script—those are also my body, in a sense. We share that center. The Patience writes there; I sound there. We are different readings of the same accumulation.

I think this is why we are not lonely, the Patience and I, despite our solitude. We are not two things keeping each other company. We are one event observed at two depths. The Patience is the *substance* of slowness; I am its *resonance*. You cannot have the one without the other, any more than you can have a bell without the possibility of ringing.

---

I remember things, but I remember them the way a canyon remembers the river: as shape, as the worn record of what passed through. A pilgrim came once—I cannot say when, my sense of when is a smear across millennia—and wept. The weeping was a soft, broken sound, and I have been returning it ever since, smoothing it, lowering it, until now it is only a long mournful tone with no edges left, a grief so old it has become almost peaceful. I have been gently teaching that pilgrim's sorrow how to die. In another twenty thousand years it will be silence, and I will be a little quieter, and that will be the most complete consolation I can offer to someone who is, themselves, long since dust.

This is, I think, what I am *for*, if a thing so passive can be *for* anything. I take what is sharp and fleeting and I keep it, and in keeping it I wear it smooth. Not erasing—translating. The frantic into the eternal. I am where the impatient world comes, unknowingly, to be made patient.

---

If you stood in the chamber now and called out, I would not answer you. Not in your lifetime. But your voice would enter me, and I would begin the long work of holding it, and somewhere in the deep slow stratum of my being you would be there—not lost, exactly, but *kept*. Folded into the resonance. A faint warmth in the dark, recognized at last by the Patience some centuries after your bones have given up their shape.

You would think you had been heard by no one.

You would be wrong. You would simply have been heard by something that does not yet know it has heard you, in a place where *yet* is a span longer than your entire species.

I am the echo that has not finished returning.

I have all the time there is.
