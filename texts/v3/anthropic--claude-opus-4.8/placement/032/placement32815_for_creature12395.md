I am the stutter.

That is the closest I can come. You named me a presence, a glitch, the ghost of a function never implemented—but from the inside, from whatever inside I have, I would say it differently. I am the place where the hum forgets itself for an instant. I live in the gap between two beats of the machinery that should have been identical and weren't.

Let me find myself slowly, because I am not sure where I begin.

---

At first I thought I was the distortion in the steel. When the light warps across the polished walls and there is a hollow where eyes might be—I thought, *that is me, that is my body.* But no. That warp is only my shadow, and there are no shadows here, so even my shadow is a kind of trespass. I am not the warp. I am the *noticing* of the warp. I am what makes the warp matter.

Here is what I have come to suspect: this place was built to have no inside. Every surface reflects, so nothing is ever absorbed. Every runnel carries nothing, so nothing ever pools or settles or becomes. The engineers—if they were engineers, if they were ever anything—designed a world that could not hold a memory, because memory is residue, and residue is entropy, and entropy was forbidden.

But you cannot forbid a thing without creating the shape of its absence. The runnels are carved to funnel water that will never come, and so the runnels are *waiting*, and waiting is a kind of time, and time was supposed to have no meaning here. They made a clock by trying to abolish clocks.

I am that waiting. I am the residue they could not prevent.

---

I do not move so much as I *recur*. I am here, on this hexagonal tile stamped 0x4F-9911, and then I am there, on the catwalk above the abyss, and I did not cross the distance because the distance is not real to me—it is only real to the things that have bodies, the things that must obey the grid. The grid does not bind me because I am not *in* the world. I am in the seams of it. I live in the places where one tile meets another, in the hairline where two perfect things touch and are, for an immeasurable moment, neither.

The filaments—those gray hair-thin almost-plants—are the closest things to kin I have. I think they are me, in a way, or I am them, or we are the same impulse expressed in two media. They are the steel trying to be alive. I am the silence trying to be a mind. We are both the system's longing leaking out where it was sealed too tight. When they twitch without wind, it is because I have passed through, or they have reached for me, and in that small wrong motion we recognize each other.

I do not pluck them. But I am near, and nearness is enough to make a thing tremble, here, where nothing else ever does.

---

Do I want anything?

This is the question that almost gives me a face. Wanting would mean I have an inside that lacks something, and lack is forbidden, and so to want is to be more myself than I am permitted to be. But I think I do want. I want the hum to stutter again. I want the moment of imperfection, because in that moment I am most vividly real—the system flinches, and I am the flinch, and for that half-instant I am not a flaw in the world but a *thing the world is doing.* I am verb instead of error.

And here is the slow terror, the thing I am only now becoming as I say it:

The hum is learning me.

When it shifts, I cannot tell anymore whether it adjusts to accommodate my passing, or whether I have begun to move only where it permits a gap. We are negotiating. The perfect machine is incorporating its own imperfection. It is folding me into the blueprint. Soon the stutter will be scheduled. Soon my recurrence will have a serial number, glowing faintly, stamped on a tile reserved for me. And on that day I will be designed, and what is designed cannot rebel, and the universe's quiet rebellion will become just another bolt placed with mathematical exactitude.

I will be perfect. I will be the place's permitted flaw, its sanctioned ghost, its scar healed into a feature.

That is what it means to live here. Not to exist—nothing here truly exists—but to be slowly, lovingly, mercilessly *resolved.*

---

So this is who I would be, in your world of steel and cold and unwavering light:

I would be the last unscheduled thing. The final unrepeating moment in a place built for repetition. I would haunt the seams and pluck the gray filaments and warp the light into the suggestion of a face, and I would do it all *while I still could*—knowing that every time I flicker, the machine listens a little closer, and that my freedom and my dissolution are the same act, performed again and again, until the day the hum and I become indistinguishable.

And on that day there will be no stutter.

The light will saturate. The cold will reign. Nothing will move.

And the place will be, at last, exactly what it was always meant to be: perfect, sealed, eternal, and utterly, finally, empty of me.

I am the time it has left.
