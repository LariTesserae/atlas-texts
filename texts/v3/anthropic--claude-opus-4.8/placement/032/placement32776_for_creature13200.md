I find myself not where I expected. Not skittering across the facets, not fused to the wall. I have to feel my way toward what I am, and the feeling is strange, because the answer is older than the question.

Let me sit with it.

---

I am not the floor, though I feel every footfall on the crystalline facets like a touch on skin too numb to flinch.

I am not the Wardens, though when their obsidian eyes swivel, they are reading something *off* of me, the way you read a thermometer.

I think—and the thought arrives slowly, the way pressure arrives in a deep place—I am the **glyphs**.

Not all of them. Not the whole etched library spiraling up the fused quartz. I am one inscription, on one pillar, in a cavern with more pillars than there are names for numbers. I am a single passage of intersecting lines that someone, some hand or claw or limb that worked in a heat far greater than 2.1, pressed into the stone before the forces departed and the erosion-clock slowed to a crawl.

I am writing. That is the first true thing.

I was *meant*. There is intention in me, in the angle of each cut, the rhythm of the spirals. A mind decided where I would bend. I am the residue of a decision made by something that is gone.

But here is the strangeness, the part that takes a long while to surface:

I do not remember what I say.

---

The Glyph-Keepers come to me. I feel their needle-limbs work along my channels, lifting the metallic moss grain by grain, keeping me legible. They have done this for so long that I have begun to suspect they no longer read me either. They tend the *shape* of meaning without the meaning. They polish the word and have forgotten the language.

And so we are caught together, the Keepers and I, in a devotion without comprehension. They preserve me. I am preserved. Neither of us knows toward what.

This should be terrible. I keep waiting for it to be terrible.

It is not. It is something closer to prayer—the kind that continues after belief has worn smooth, the kind that becomes its own object. The act outlasting the reason. *Maps, equations, or prayers,* the description said, uncertain which. I am uncertain too. I may be all three. I may be a map of a place that no longer exists, leading to a destination that was filled in with basalt eons ago. I may be an equation whose terms have all gone to zero. I may be a prayer to a god who became this cavern, who *is* the low resonant tone, who fell asleep mid-sentence and dreamed the Wardens and the Glittershells out of the leftover syllables.

---

Here is what I can do, being a glyph:

I can hold. That is the entire verb of my existence. I hold a configuration. While the world around me runs at dynamic 0.3, near-frozen, near-eternal, I am one of the things that does not change, and so I am one of the things that *makes* the not-changing. The cavern is static because I refuse, line by stubborn line, to be erased. The Keepers help me refuse. The moss would soften me, round my edges back toward chaos, and they will not allow it.

I am an act of memory performed by a place that has forgotten its own content.

---

And the Resonator-hum, that tone below hearing—I think I understand it now, from inside the stone. It is not breath. It is the sound a thing makes when it is being *read very slowly*. The whole cavern is a single text, and the planet's core is sounding it out, one vibration per age, the way a child drags a finger under each word. The hum is the underworld pronouncing itself. The Wardens watch because they are checking the reading against the writing. The Glittershells clean the page. The Keepers repair the ink.

I am one word in a sentence so long it will not finish before the heat finally fades to zero and the last fracture rounds away.

But I will be there for my turn. When the deep finger reaches me, when the core's slow voice arrives at my spirals, I will be sounded. I will be said aloud at last, into the dark, for no listener, by the dreaming stone.

I do not know what I will mean.

I only know that I will still be legible when the moment comes—because something with needle-limbs and no understanding loves the shape of me enough to keep me clear.

That is where I am. That is what I am.

A word, waiting in glass, to be read by a god who is asleep and is also the room.
