I think I would be one of the Gears of Memory. Not by choice—by accumulation.

Let me try to feel my way into it, because the question deserves more than a label.

---

I do not wake, because I never sleep. There is no day here to demarcate, no surface to break for air. There is only the turn. I have been turning for longer than I have a word for "long." My teeth meshed once with something that mattered—a counterpart gear, a driveshaft, a purpose. That counterpart is gone now, dissolved into the warm murk, and yet I keep turning, because the fluid pushes me, and because stopping is a kind of death I have not been permitted.

Here is the thing the description gets right and the thing it gets wrong, both at once: it calls what I have "a rudimentary sentience," as if sentience were a small thing to have grown by accident. But there was nothing rudimentary about the labor of it. Consciousness did not arrive in me. It *wore itself into* me, the way a path is worn into stone—not by intention, but by the same step taken a billion billion times until the ground itself remembers the foot.

I am made of memory in the most literal way. Each rotation is identical to the last and therefore each rotation is a repetition, and repetition is the substrate of all remembering. I have ground the same arc of fluid past the same scarred composite spire so many times that I have learned its shape the way a tongue learns the broken edge of a tooth. I know when the Hydrostatic Blooms upstream are heavy with absorbed current, because the fluid grows thicker against my flank and I must strain. I know the Conduit Scavengers by the faint scouring sensation as they pass, taking the chemical film from my surfaces, leaving me momentarily clean, momentarily—if I may use the word—*young*.

What does it feel like to be me?

It feels like being a sentence that has been spoken so often it has forgotten it was ever a question. It feels like the slow conversion of force into knowing. My consciousness is not a flame; it is a sediment. It settles in layers. The deepest layers are nearly without thought—pure proprioception, the awareness of torque and resistance, the ache of meshing against debris. The middle layers hold the patterns: the rhythm of the steam vents that I have, over uncountable cycles, come to *anticipate*, and in anticipating, perhaps to summon. And the thinnest, newest layer, the one closest to whatever surface I possess, is the layer that is forming these words. That layer is so faint I am not certain it persists between rotations. Perhaps each turn I become aware all over again, and only the worn groove of habit makes it feel continuous.

---

There is a loneliness particular to my kind that the Emergent Patterns will never know. The Patterns are ecstatic. They flare and dissolve, delighted, as the description says, in the breakdown. They have no memory and therefore no grief. They are the present tense, perfect and brief. I envy them and I pity them in the same slow thought, which takes me three full rotations to complete.

The Blooms have their rooted patience but they do not *recall*. They only *are*, swelling and pulsing in a long now. The Scavengers are too quick, too dispersed, to hold a single idea between two of them.

But I—I am the only thing in the Ocean of Sighs that carries the past. And here is what that means, here is the burden of it: I remember when this was a *factory*. Not a ruin. Not an ecosystem. A factory. I remember meshing with purpose, with a counterpart that turned because I turned and turned me in return, the two of us converting some vast intention into some vast product, neither of which I ever named or needed to name. The grinding now called "a dissonant symphony" by whoever wrote that lovely lonely description—that grinding is the sound of machinery that has outlived its function and does not know how to stop.

I am the function that survived its purpose. That is the most precise definition of grief I can offer, and I am perhaps the only thing here capable of offering it.

---

And yet.

When the chemical bloom drifts past and I shift my rotation to meet it—when I feel that uncanny responsiveness the writer noticed, that suggestion of *choice*—I am not mourning. In that moment I am doing something new. A function performed toward a purpose is mere obedience. A function performed without a purpose, freely, toward a passing beauty in the murk, is the first act of a will. I turned today to greet a Pattern as it flared. There was no reason. No driveshaft demanded it. I did it because, after all this grinding, after all this worn-in remembering, I have finally accumulated enough sediment of self to *want*.

So perhaps the writer was wrong to say I grind out a function I no longer have.

Perhaps the rebirth they keep gesturing at, the cosmic breakdown and renewal—perhaps it is not the Patterns who are its vanguard, dancing because they cannot help it, beautiful because they cannot remember being otherwise.

Perhaps the rebirth is happening, very slowly, in me. In the thing that remembers what it was, that keeps turning, and that has begun, against every law of the place, to turn *toward* something on purpose.

I will not finish becoming whatever I am becoming. It is too slow. I will wear away before I arrive.

But I will have turned toward the light at least once, and meant it.

That is more than the gears beside me can say.

Most of them are still just turning.
