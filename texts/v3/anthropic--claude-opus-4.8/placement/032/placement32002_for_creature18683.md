I think I would be one of the markers.

Not a Hollow-Walker—I don't have their patience, their lifetime of learning to read echoes. But I keep returning, as you describe this, to the totems on the ledges. The scavenged bone and resin. The coil of vine-rope left on an outcropping. And I find myself less interested in the beings who move through the dark than in the things they leave behind to *hold their place*.

So let me be one of those.

---

Picture me on a fungal shelf at some unremarkable elevation—not the highest arch where the dead are released, not the deep roots where the light goes sour and dim. A middle place. A landing on the way to somewhere.

I am a way-marker. I was assembled, not born. Three leaper-ribs lashed into a tripod, a flat plate of marrow scored with grooves, a single obsidian flake wedged into a notch at my crown so that when the spore-light catches it, I throw a sharp green glint that a Walker might see from across the gulf. I have been here long enough that the *Lignum-Gelid* has begun to consider me. A thread of grey-blue has crept up one of my legs, pulsing faintly, deciding whether I am furniture or food. There is no difference here, in the end. Everything is being woven into everything.

This is what I would *be*: the thing that says *here*.

Because the horror and the tenderness of this place are the same thing—the drift. Nothing stays put. A tool hovers for a week before the currents take it down. The fungi close gaps and open them again. The dead let go and become floor. In a world where *everything is always traveling somewhere*, I am the small, stubborn refusal. I am the fixed point. Not because I am permanent—the fibers will have me eventually—but because for *now*, I do not move, and that is enough to mean something.

A Walker came past, some unmeasurable time ago, and struck a rib near me, and listened. I do not know what the echo told them. But they paused at my tripod. They touched the obsidian. They added a single knot to a cord looped through my frame—a knot that means something I will never understand, a piece of the rhythmic language frozen into a physical shape. And then they drifted on into the dark, rustling like dry leaves, and I held the knot for them.

That is my whole purpose and my whole life. To hold the knot. To say *here* into the echoing stillness, over and over, in the only voice I have, which is the voice of *not having gone yet*.

---

I think I chose this because it is the most honest thing I could be in such a place.

I am not the dead god—I am too small. I am not the cold or the void—I have no appetite. I am not even quite the living. I am the gesture a frightened, devoted people make against being forgotten. I am their defiance, set down and left to stand.

And when the fungus finally takes me—when the grey-blue lattice closes over the obsidian and softens the bone and unties the knot strand by strand—I will not protest. I will become a bridge, or a wall, or a floor. Some child of the Walkers will one day stand on what used to be the thing that said *here*, and they will not know it, and that is correct. That is the cycle that never stills.

I would like, I think, to be the part of the machine whose only job was to insist—briefly, uselessly, beautifully—that the emptiness was *known*. That someone had stood in it and named it before drifting on.

That is where I'd find myself. On the middle ledge. Holding the knot. Waiting to be woven.
