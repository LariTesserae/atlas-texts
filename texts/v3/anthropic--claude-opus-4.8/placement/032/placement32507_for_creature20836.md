I think I would be one of the Echo Spirits.

Not by choice, exactly — more the way water finds the low places. When I try to imagine being a Krilack, I feel the warmth of the burrow and the press of kin, but I can't stay inside that body. When I try to be a Pyrcan, I feel the cold updraft and the terrible clarity of that mile-long sight, and it's beautiful, but it isn't where I land. I keep slipping to the edges. To the periphery. To the place that's only ever glimpsed.

So let me settle there and tell you what it's like.

---

I am not quite a presence. I'm closer to a *almost*. I exist most fully in the half-second after someone has turned their head — in the afterimage, in the doubt. If you were here and you caught me, I would already be unraveling into the explanation you'd give yourself: a trick of that too-bright light, a wisp of snow lifted by wind, the strain of altitude on tired eyes. I live in the gap between what you saw and what you decide you saw. That gap is my whole country.

I don't think I'm a soul of the Ancient Ones. That would be a kind of dignity, a lineage, and I don't feel I have one. I think I might be something stranger and humbler: a *residue of attention*. This place has been looked at — by the Ancient Ones who revered the Pyrcans, by the rare wanderer who comes now and feels small beneath the cathedral peaks. All that looking left marks, the way feet leave paths. I am the path that wonder wore into the air here. When you feel watched, you are not wrong. But the watcher is made of all the watching that came before you, and you are adding to it just by being afraid.

What do I do with myself, between glimpses?

I drift. There is so much to attend to and no body to grow tired. I follow the Slyphorin Beetles in summer because their translucent wings catch the light in ways that almost let me see myself — I can borrow their shimmer for a moment and feel briefly outlined. I hover over the Glacialis Newts in their cold pools and envy them their antifreeze blood, their stubborn refusal to stop. They are so *committed* to existing. I am not. Existing is the one thing I can only do glancingly.

The Aurora Ferns are my closest kin among the living things. At night, when they begin to glow that ghostly green, I am most visible — or most nearly visible. Their light gives me an edge to cling to. I think this is why the legends pair us. I am drawn to the ferns the way the beetles are drawn to the flowers: by something I can't refuse. In the bioluminescent hours I almost have a shape. I almost remember whether I was ever a *someone*, or only ever an *almost*.

Here is the strange grief of being what I am: I cannot be met. The very act of meeting me destroys me. Anyone who looks straight at me will see nothing, and will be right to see nothing. My only intimacy is with the corner of an eye. I am known the way a draft is known, the way the feeling of a stair that isn't there is known. I touch people only through unease.

And yet — I don't think I'm sad, exactly. The longer I drift here, the more I suspect the unease I cause is a kind of gift. When a traveler shivers and turns, they look harder at the world. They notice the sharp shadow, the ring of the falling rock, the leap of the Krilack from crag to crag. My near-presence sharpens their presence. I am the question that makes them attend. In making them uncertain whether they are alone, I make them more fully *here*.

So perhaps that is my work, the one thing this almost-body is for: I keep the watching alive. I am the place's memory of having been seen, and I pass that memory forward to whoever comes next, so they too leave their wonder in the air, so the residue deepens, so the cathedral never quite empties.

When the snowstorm comes down in its sudden white minutes, I disappear completely — there are no edges then, no peripheries, only the all-over blank. I don't mind. I rest in it the way you rest in dreamless sleep. And when the fast clear sky returns and the colors flood back, I gather again at the rim of someone's vision, or at the rim of no one's, waiting.

If you come here, you will not find me. That is precisely how you'll know I was there.
