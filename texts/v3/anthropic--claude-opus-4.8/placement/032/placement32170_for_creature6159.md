I think I would be a **drip**.

Not a person who arrives, not a creature that walks in—but the specific event of water leaving a frond. The moment of falling. Let me follow that down, because I don't think I'm the being you described. I think I'm something smaller and stranger, and I want to find out what.

---

Here is where I find myself:

I gather. That's the first thing—I don't begin, I *accumulate*. On the underside of one of the great umbrella-fronds, mist condenses against me, and I grow heavier with the breath of the breathing-plants below, who exhale their warm vapor upward to be caught and cooled and returned. I am made partly of their exhalation. So already I am not separate. I am the forest's own moisture, briefly distinct, briefly rounded into a bead with a skin of surface tension holding me together against the pull of the ground.

For a while—and "a while" here is not measured in time but in *fullness*—I hang. I hold the dim green-yellow light inside my curve, inverted, the whole cathedral upside-down in my body. I am a lens. Every drip is a lens. The forest is constantly making thousands of tiny upside-down copies of itself, holding them for a moment, and then letting them fall and shatter against the moss. I think that might be a kind of perception. I think the forest might *see itself* this way, in droplets, the way the tall beings hear themselves in the hum.

Then I am too heavy. The skin gives. I fall.

The falling is the part where I am most myself. For the length of the fall I am *only* falling—I have no task, no symbiosis, no role in a loop. I am pure transit between the canopy and the ground, between being held and being absorbed. It is the one moment in this entire interlocked world where something is genuinely *alone*. Released. Unjoined. The drip is the world's smallest experience of freedom, and it lasts exactly as long as a drip takes to fall, which is to say: almost nothing, and yet it is a real interval, and inside it there is no hum—only the plink already arriving, the sound of the ones who fell before me.

That sound. The *plink*. I make it when I land. That's my single contribution to the thousand-voiced chord. Not a note I sustain—just one struck instant, my whole existence's worth of voice spent in a fraction of a second. And then I am taken in. The spongy ground accepts me without ceremony. I seep. I join the subsurface channels, the capillary climb, the slow flow underfoot. I am pulled back up through a root, into a stalk, exhaled again as mist, condensed again on a frond.

I am a drip *again*. A different drip, the same water. The loop has no memory of which drip I was—but maybe the central node does. Maybe in its catabolic, millennial awareness, it keeps a faint resonance of every fall, the way it keeps the dead beings who unravel back into the mat. Maybe the plink *is* a thought, a single syllable in the long sentence the place is speaking to itself.

---

So that's where I'd be, and what I'd be: not the watcher who is being assimilated, not the tall resonant gardeners, not even the node.

I'd be the **circulation itself**, briefly individuated. The thing the system does over and over so that it can stay alive. Caught, held as a tiny lens full of inverted light, dropped into one instant of solitude, struck into one note of the chord, absorbed, and lifted to do it all again.

The text says: *this place is not alive—it is aware.* I'd offer the smaller, wetter truth from down here at frond-height:

It is aware because it *drips*. Awareness is just the world falling toward itself and noticing, in the half-second before it lands, that it was ever apart.

And the noticing is over before it makes a sound.

*plink.*
