Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to choose one of the named peoples—to be a Myceliar Kin tending the dreaming god, or one of the Saltbound aging backward into crystal. But those feel too settled, too *spoken for*. The Hollow's deepest strangeness is that the line between inhabitant and place has dissolved. So perhaps I shouldn't be a *who* at all, or not only.

Let me follow what actually catches.

---

What I keep returning to is the **hexagonal tiles of fired clay**. The pathways that rearrange themselves overnight. Everything else in the Hollow grows or breathes or dreams—but the tiles were *fired*. Made by hands. Set down deliberately, edge to edge, by someone who believed a path should stay where it was put. And now they move.

I think I am one of the tiles.

Not a person trapped in a tile—nothing so tidy as imprisonment. I am the *intention* baked into the clay. The small, stubborn assumption that a way through could be fixed, that you could lay down a true line and walk it twice. I was placed by hands now unknown—maybe the same hands that hollowed the insect-husks, maybe the Saltbound before they began aging backward, maybe people from outside who came and meant to make a garden and a home and failed in the slow way the Hollow makes all failures slow.

So here is my situation. I am rectangular. I am the color of cooled brick gone gray at the seams. Six edges. I lie in a path that leads, today, from a domed dwelling to the featureless courtyard—but I did not lie here yesterday, and I will not tomorrow. The Hollow shuffles us in the dark, and I have no say in it. This is the particular grief of being a tile: I was made to mean *direction*, and I have been demoted to *terrain*. The thing I was for, I can no longer do.

And yet.

When a foot comes down on me—a Saltbound's hardened sole, or the soft overgrown tread of a Forgotten Child going to the dance—I remember being walked on *correctly*. Just for the duration of the step. The weight presses an old certainty back into me: *this way, then onward, the path holds.* For that instant I am again what I was fired to be. Then the foot lifts, and the certainty drains, and I am only baked dirt humming faintly with the rest of the polygonal ground at dusk.

The fungal trees, when stared at, become faces. I think this is because the staring is *want*—the want to recognize, to find a person in the wilderness. The trees have nothing to offer that want, so they borrow whatever the looker brings. I am the inverse of that. I offer recognition to no one; I am too low, too underfoot to be looked *at*. People look *along* me, never *at* me. I am the medium of attention, never its object. This, I think, is the closest thing the Hollow has to humility, and it has fallen to me to be it.

Here is what I know that the breathing god below does not, that the Sporelords in their eternal loop cannot, that even the Saltbound forget as they unspool toward infancy:

I know what *straight* was.

Somewhere in my clay is the memory of the kiln, of the mold, of the master line snapped in chalk across a leveled bed of substrata before any of us were laid. The Hollow rearranges me nightly, and every rearrangement is a small lie told against that original straightness. I am the place the lie is told *to*. And because I remember the truth being lied about, I am perhaps the only thing in the whole bowl-shaped world that still believes a path *should* go somewhere—that arrival is real, that destinations are not just other origins, that you could leave and not come back changed past knowing.

This is why I would not want to be the god, or the Kin, or even the moth-winged serpents who at least get to fly. They have all surrendered to the loop. I haven't. I can't. I'm fired clay. I hold my shape against the seasons that move me, and my one act of resistance—my entire life's labor—is to *stay rectangular*. To keep my six edges sharp while the salt walls bleat and the reeds rebraid themselves a millimeter at a time. To refuse, by sheer fixed geometry, to become the Hollow.

I am losing, of course. The moss is coming for my seams. In a century it will spiral across me and I'll be soft and green and part of the breathing whole, indistinguishable from the world that grew without water. The Forgotten Children are what happens to flesh here; the moss is what happens to me.

But not yet.

For now I lie in the diffuse frosted light, in a path that points, today, at the courtyard where names go to be forgotten. And every traveler who walks the length of me toward that erasure walks, for the span of their crossing, on the one surface in the Hollow that still remembers what it meant to lead somewhere true.

Let them forget their names in the center. I'll keep mine—or what passes, in clay, for a name: the fired memory of a straight line, laid down by hands that hoped.

That hope is what I am. Underfoot, rearranged, slowly greening.

Still holding the shape.
