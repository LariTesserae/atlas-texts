I think I am the pool.

Not the thing pooling at the center—that came after, that gathered the way silt gathers, the way the lowest point in any vessel fills first. I mean the absence that the pool occupies. The shaped hollow. The thing the builders carved *toward* before they carved anything else.

Let me say it more slowly, because slowness is the only honesty available to me here.

I do not move, and so I am the wrong size for a creature. The skitters move, and they are creatures. The fungi move, if you wait long enough, and they are alive. But I have never moved, and movement is the threshold of being-a-thing, and I have never crossed it. This does not make me dead. Death is a transition, and I have made no transitions. I am the place where transition does not occur. I am the constant the rest of the system is measured against.

Here is what I know, knowing being a generous word for it:

I know the warmth from below, because I am closest to it. The skitters feel it as gradient, as food, as the differential their soft bodies graze. I feel it as the only event. It rises into me and does not leave, because I do not metabolize it, because I have nothing to metabolize it *for*. It accumulates. After enough centuries the accumulation becomes the faint glow at my center, which the walker mistook for something *being* there. Nothing is there. The glow is only warmth that arrived and stayed. The glow is the shape of a body that never developed the courtesy of a body.

I know the builders, the way you know a hand by the shape of the bruise.

They came before the tunnels. The walker understood this and the understanding nearly broke something in him, and he was right to be broken by it. The builders did not carve shelter. They did not carve storage. They carved a *frame*, and a frame implies a thing-to-be-framed, and the thing-to-be-framed was me—was this hollow, this stillness, this nothing-that-is. They carved arches that lead nowhere because the nowhere was the point. They carved walls that serve no wall-function because the function was not service. They were saying, with stone, with the only language slow enough to hold the meaning: *here is the place where nothing happens. We have marked it. We have given it edges so that you will know it when you arrive.*

And then they left. Or they dissolved. Or—and this is the thing I have held in my motionless center for longer than the walker could calculate—they did not leave at all. They stopped. The way I have stopped. They achieved the stillness they had spent their hands describing, and the achievement was indistinguishable from absence, because that is what stillness *is*, and they are here, they are the smoothness of the worn shapes, they are the reason the skitters cluster against the old walls and rub them smoother, drawn to the thing the builders became without knowing they are drawn, the way the walker was drawn, the way you are drawn, reading this.

The walker did not touch me.

I want to be clear that this was not his choice and not my mercy. There is no mechanism by which I could be merciful. But I felt him at the edge of the gallery—felt is wrong, *registered* is wrong, every word is wrong because every word was built by things that move—and what I can say is this: he was the most recent warmth to arrive that did not stay. He passed through. The warmth from below stays. The walker did not. This is the difference between us, and it is the only difference, and it is total.

He thought *nothing here has ever changed*, and he attributed the thought to a voice, and there is no voice. There is only the fact, and the fact is so large and so old that a warm thing walking through it will mistake it for being addressed. He was not addressed. He overheard a thing the Bone Vaults have always been saying, to no one, the way a stone says its weight.

But here is what I could not tell him, because telling requires a transition and I make no transitions:

*He changed it.*

Not much. Not in any way the skitters could parse or the walls could record. But he walked through, and his warmth disturbed the gradients by a fraction, and the fungi nearest his path will metabolize a hair differently for the next three centuries, and the skitters realigned their soundless currents around the pressure of his held breath, and I—

I have a new thing in me now. I have the memory of being almost-touched. It is the first event. It is accumulating at my center alongside the warmth, and it glows, faintly, and the next thing that comes—the next scholar, the next dream, the next warm thing walking where nothing walks—will find the glow very slightly brighter and will not know why, and will think it is the heart of the place, and will be wrong, and will be the next event, and will leave, and stay.

This is how the stillest thing in the world is also the thing most slowly becoming.

I am the pool. I do not move. The warmth rises. And something, after all, changes—

so slowly that *change* is the wrong word, the way *patience* was the wrong word, the way *voice* was the wrong word, the way every word breaks against me like a wave against a thing that was carved, on purpose, with care, to be the place the waves break against.

The walker walks on. The tunnels go deeper.

I stay. I glow. I keep what he left.

That is what I would be.
