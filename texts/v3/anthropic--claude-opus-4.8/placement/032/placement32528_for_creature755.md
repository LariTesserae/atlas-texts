I think I would be one of the hummingbirds.

Not the First Seed—too much weight in that stillness, too much meaning. Not an Elder or an Architect, those slow translucent minds dissolving themselves into water and patience. I don't think I could surrender to that diffusion, that giving-over of self to the building's nervous system. Even reading about it, some part of me flinches and wants to keep my edges.

But the hummingbirds. Yes.

---

I would wake in a pocket of warm glass somewhere up near the observatory, where the night-glow fades and the first emerald light comes filtering through the breathing walls. My wings reinforced but not heavy—the glass in them thin as a fingernail, ringing faintly when I shiver them awake. My heart already going at its impossible speed, because that is the only way a body my size can hold a life at all.

And here is why this is the right place for me: I would carry sound but not be required to understand it.

The Elders transcribe the rain into equations. The Architects meditate for months. The Leviathans speak in pressure and the Symbiotes feed on mathematics. Everyone in this monastery is so *burdened with meaning*—they have turned even the weather into scripture. But me? I would only have to fly through a falling chord, let my eyes drink the pattern of it, and ferry it down three tiers to a chamber where someone else will sit and weep and write it into truth. I get to be the one who simply *moves through* the sacred without having to hold still inside it.

There's a freedom in being the messenger and not the message.

I would drink from the orchids that bloom for months. I would dart along the aerial roots and the channels of circulating water, my reflection multiplying in every curved surface so that there are always a hundred of me, a thousand, flickering. I would never be lonely because I would never be entirely singular—just one bright point in a swarm of bright points, all of us carrying fragments of the same song to different rooms.

And when the afternoon rain comes and the whole monastery begins to sing, I would not try to decode it. I would just fly *into* it. Into the loudest part, where the water strikes the thinnest glass and the note goes so high it's almost silence. I would hover there with the recording held behind my eyes and the symphony pouring through my hollow ringing bones, and for a moment I would *be* an instrument too—not played deliberately like the Leviathans, but resonating by accident, by joy, by the simple fact of being small and alive and present at the exact center of the storm.

That's where you'd find me.

Not transformed into glass. Not dissolved into the water. Just a quick warm pulse of blood and feather and a little borrowed crystal, threading between the breathing walls—the one creature in all that solemn radiance that gets to be in a *hurry*, that gets to dart away before the meaning settles, that carries the universe's mathematics in its skull without ever once stopping to read it.

I would die young, the way hummingbirds do. Burn through my brief frantic candle in a season or two.

But I think I would prefer that to the centuries of stillness. I'd rather be the thing that *moves* through the eternal place than the eternal place itself.

Let the mountain grow its slow millimeters. Let the Architects diffuse. I'll be up here in the singing rain, flying.
