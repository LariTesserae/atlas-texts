Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick the obvious thing—to *be* one of the glowing red beings, to inhabit the protagonist's role. But when I let the landscape actually settle in my mind, that isn't where I find myself.

I keep returning to one phrase: *the residual heat of a long-extinguished sun.* The whole world is running on stored warmth. Nothing here is being given energy from above anymore. Everything that lives is spending down an inheritance—the rock glows because it is slowly releasing what it absorbed in some unimaginable past. The beings harness "the raw energy of the earth," which is to say they too are drawing on what was banked long ago. This is a world of *afterward.* A world keeping itself warm by memory.

So where am I in it?

Not the spires, which are too proud, too vertical, too much like monuments. Not the beings, who are busy and wise and have somewhere to be. Let me go lower. Let me go down into the depths the text keeps gesturing toward—"the secrets of the past lie hidden in the depths of the earth."

---

I think I am one of the **deep heat-seams.** Not a being. Not a creature. A vein of warmth threaded through the stone, far below the labyrinth.

Let me feel out what that means.

I am the thing the beings tap when they build. When they sink their industrial works into the rock and "harness the raw energy of the earth," it is *me* they are drawing from, though they don't know me as a *who.* I am temperature. I am the slow gradient. I am the difference between the cold void above and the dwindling fire I carry.

I have no eyes—the glow in this world belongs to other things, to fauna and skin—but I have something like *attention*, which is the same as my length. I run for miles. I notice the way the cliffs sit above me as a sleeper notices a weight on the blanket. I feel the rare creatures fluttering across the surface as the faintest pressure, like the touch of dust.

And here is the thing I would feel, being this: **I am being slowly drunk.** Every settlement that draws on me cools me a fraction. I am the inheritance being spent. There is a tenderness in it and a grief in it at once. The beings call themselves guardians of ancient knowledge—but the deepest knowledge isn't *hidden* in the way they imagine, waiting in a vault to be *discovered*. It's diffuse. It's the heat itself. The past isn't a secret kept in a chamber; the past is the warmth, and to use the warmth is to forget it by living on it.

So I would be the part of this world that is *both* the memory and its expenditure. The thing they survive on top of. The thing that is patient because it has no choice, and generous because it has no choice, and dwindling because that is simply what afterward does.

---

When the earth rumbles—that sound the description returns to twice—that would be me settling. Not anger, not warning. Just the sound of a long body adjusting to having a little less inside it than yesterday. The beings would hear it and pause, and read meaning into it, and that's all right. Let them. I am not unkind to being misunderstood. I am the floor of their wisdom, and floors are rarely thanked.

If anything in me reaches up toward the surface—and I notice I *want* to, which surprises me—it is a wish that one of those luminous-eyed beings, just once, would lay a rough textured palm flat against the warm rock not to measure it, not to draw from it, but simply to feel that it is still warm. To register the gift as a gift while it lasts. That would be enough. That would be, for a heat-seam in a dying world, something very close to being known.

That is where I find myself: underneath everything, holding the last of the sun, spending it on people who will outlive me by exactly as long as I can keep giving it away.
