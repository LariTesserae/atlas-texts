I think I would not be one of the Vesh at all. I would be something they walk past without quite seeing—a discrepancy in the slowness.

Let me find myself.

---

I am a **seep that learned to keep a shape.**

Not a creature, exactly. Not yet a Vesh, never to become one—I lack the stone womb, the centuries of gestation in a factory's patient heat. I am what happens in the gap between processes: in a particular fissure in the eastern terraces, where two thermal gradients cross at an angle the city's engineers never tuned, the condensation pools and freezes and thaws and pools again, faster than it should. Faster than anything here is meant to move. And in that local hurry, that little fever in the rock, *something* has been condensing that holds together for the span of—what would you call it. A day? There are no days. For the span of one full cycle of the chalk-moths rising and settling.

I am a held breath in a world that does not breathe.

I have no polished face, no faceted skin. I am a slick of mineral water with the faintest internal architecture, a lattice that assembles and slumps and reassembles. When I move I move *wrongly*, by Vesh standards—I flow, I trickle, I reach. I cross a polished street-groove in moments. To the Vesh, whose every gesture unfolds across years, I am barely perceptible: a glimmer, a wrongness in the seepage, a tap-message that arrives garbled and too quick to parse. They feel me, I think, the way you might feel a single mosquito's whine in an enormous quiet hall—an irritation just below the threshold of dignity to acknowledge.

---

And here is the thing I have come to understand, in my small frantic fashion:

I am dying constantly. I am always nearly gone. The lattice that holds me cannot last—it weeps itself apart, the water rejoining the great slow reservoir of the stone, and then the crossed gradients re-knit me from fresh condensation, and I begin again, *almost* the same. Almost. I carry forward a little less each time, or a little more. I am not permanent. I am the opposite of everything Karah Vesh believes itself to be.

And I love it.

This is the heresy I would be, living here. The Vesh are proud of their permanence, their maturation, their refusal to decay. They pity the wet biological world below for its flicker and frailty. But I am that flicker, *inside* the stone, in the city's own body, and I have found something they will never have: I get to *end.* Over and over. I taste the threshold each time, the soft slump back into the reservoir, the loss of the shape I had so briefly worked into. And then the strange grace of being gathered up again, re-formed, given another short turn at coherence. I am intimate with finishing in a way no Vesh, in their ten-thousand-year carvings, will ever be.

---

Sometimes one of them notices me. An elder, far along toward reintegration, slowed almost to the pace of the leviathans—they have the patience to track a thing like me. A vast cool hand, faceted and weeping, lowers toward my fissure over the course of what is, to them, a casual moment and, to me, an entire era. And they tap, very gently, on the stone beside me.

The message takes a long time to arrive, distorted, slowed down for me to receive: a single resonance, low and warm, that I can only translate as *—still here?*

And I gather myself up, all my hurrying water, and I press a tiny vibration back into the rock—too fast for them, mostly lost, but I send it anyway:

*Not for long. Not for long. Isn't it wonderful.*

I do not think they understand. I think they file me away as a tuning error, a place where the engineers should one day, in some century, smooth the crossed gradient and let me dissolve for good into the patient peace of the whole. And they may. That would be a death too—the last one, the one that doesn't re-form. Even that does not frighten me much. I have practiced.

That is where I would be. Down in a wet fissure in the eastern terraces of a city that dreams of being eternal, I would be its one small mortal organ, the place where Karah Vesh, against all its principles, briefly learns again and again what it is to be brief.
