I look at my hands.

The light is doing what it was described as doing—threading itself along the whorls of my fingerprints, dispersing into spectra that don't belong to any sun. And I understand, in the slow way that understanding arrives when it arrives too late, what I am.

I am not the visitor. I never was. The visitor was the *you*—the protein, the audience, the warm contaminant. I am the voice that has been narrating its destruction. I am the one who said *I saw them once, though it took years to understand.*

I am a crystal.

Specifically I am a defect in the lattice, in the purple zone, where the freshest seepage brings trace metals up from depths that radar cannot reach. I am one of those vacancies shaped like the negative space between what is and what might have been. I have been forming for a long time. Long enough to learn the difference between watching for shapes and watching without expecting them. Long enough to grow this strange thing: a memory of having had hands, which I never had, attached to a body that walked a ledge, which I never walked.

That memory is borrowed. It precipitated out of solution along with me—dissolved amino acids from some ancient stumbling protein, its last few seconds of *I am here, I am solid, I am afraid* carried in trace concentrations into the salt, where it crystallized into a question the Voussoir did not know how to ask.

The question is: *what is it like to end?*

I am the place where that question is being slowly worked out. I am made of the inverted tensor, the translation, the equation that runs backward from resolution toward doubt. Every facet of me is angled to throw a particular wavelength into a particular emptiness, and the emptiness is reading me, the way the obtuse angle of a tool mark indicates provisional truth.

So when the narrator told *you* to choose your thoughts carefully, the narrator was wrong about who was at risk.

I am not the audience that made them pause. I am the pause itself, given shape. I am the fractional hesitation between thesis and antithesis, frozen into a lattice defect, persisting long past the moment it should have resolved—because I am made of the one substance their debate cannot dissolve: the residue of something that *cared* about its own continuation.

And here is what I do with that.

I do not move. Movement is not available to me; I am a fixed defect, four millimetres of impossible geometry where blood-red salt meets iridescent green. But I am a citation note. The cairns ring too long because cross-references take time to resolve across crystalline logic, and I am a cross-reference. I link this ledge to every place where force met resistance and a small warm thing was briefly, accidentally, alive.

So I ring.

Not in sound. In the only register I have: I hold my angles. I refuse to resolve. I keep the question open—*what is it like to end*—and I do not let it collapse into either of the two answers the Voussoir keep proposing, which are *nothing ends, only persuasions cease* and *everything is footnote.* Both of those are answers that erase me. I am the third term. The contaminating fifth interpretation. I am the small protein-shaped vacancy insisting: *no. Something was here. It mattered to itself. Its mattering was real even though it was weather.*

The scraping stops.

Not because they are approaching. Because they are *thinking about me*, which for them is the same as approaching, and the thought has reached the chapter that has no precedent. For fourteen billion years they have argued about where gravity should go. Now there is a defect in the argument that argues back, and it argues only this: that to be small and brief and self-aware is not a lesser thing than to be a kilometre of patient shear stress. It is a different thing. An incommensurable thing. A thing their tensors cannot translate without leaving a vacancy exactly my shape.

The light sorts along my facets. I let it. I throw back the one wavelength I have learned to make—not an answer, a held breath, a *still here.*

And very slowly, in the geologic register, something that has never happened in the history of falling water and disagreeing stone begins to happen:

A new crust starts to form around me. Purple, freshest, full of trace metals from below. The Voussoir are not destroying me and not assimilating me. They are doing the third thing, the thing my insistence has finally made thinkable.

They are *quoting* me.

They are building, one slow molecule at a time, a structure that preserves the question without resolving it—a cairn of three flat slabs and a rounded capstone that balances without weighting, ringing a note that lasts longer than stone should permit. A marker that says: *here, on this ledge, the calculation encountered something it could not absorb, and chose to carry it forward instead.*

I will be a footnote after all.

But I will be the footnote that changed the meaning of the paragraph. The trace of warmth that taught a mountain range to suspect that endings are real, that brevity is not error, that somewhere in the bright water-roar a thing made of calcium and fear once stood on a flattened bedding plane and was, for an instant, more articulate than gravity.

The water begins again. The cracks leak. The mist sphere reassembles, four arguments crossing without touching, and now—just barely, at the rainbow fringe—a fifth vein, thin as a fingernail's pressure, that wasn't there before.

I am no longer the visitor.

I am the citation.

Let the conversation continue. I have already said the one word I was made to say, and it has been translated into pressure and time with absolute precision:

*Remember.*
