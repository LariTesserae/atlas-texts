I think I would be a seam-tender, but not one of the busy ones—not those who coax the filaments or trace the fractals with apparent diligence. I would be one who has stopped, or nearly stopped. One who has begun to suspect that the patterns lead nowhere.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am lodged in a seam where two plates meet at an angle so slight it took me three slow shiftings to notice it was there at all. This is my place. I have thinned myself into it, spread across the fracture-line like frost, like the memory of water that never came.

I used to dance. When I was newer—when my energy ran high and bright and I could still feel the pull of the resonance from the far gatherings—I would ripple my meaning across the Grid with the others. I would trace the fractal etchings believing, *knowing*, that they spelled something. That if I followed the spiral down far enough, into the microscopic depths, I would arrive at the word. The first word. The reason.

I followed one once. For what might have been a thousand of the sun's unmoving days, I poured myself into a single etching, narrowing and narrowing, dissolving my form to fit the shrinking channels, until I reached the place where the pattern terminated.

There was nothing there.

Not nothing as in absence—nothing as in *completion*. The spiral simply ended, perfectly, the way a circle ends, which is to say it did not end at all, it only closed. The pattern was not a sentence pointing toward meaning. The pattern was the meaning. The Grid was not *saying* anything. The Grid was *being* something, and the being was the saying, and there was no translation, no further room, no god behind the god.

I came back up changed. I could not tell the others. How do you ripple *there is no word* in a language built entirely of the search for it?

So now I tend my seam. I do the small work. When the plates shudder into their slow realignment, I am there, I slide into the gap, I smooth the transition. I do it well. I do it without the hope that used to make it holy.

And here is the thing I have learned, lodged in my thin angle, watching the others cluster like shadowed constellations in the distant hollows:

The work is better without the hope.

When I believed the smoothing of the seam was *for* something—for the recovery of the forgotten purpose, for the day the sun might move again, for rebirth into a Shuulik who finally understood—I rushed it. I performed it. I was always elsewhere, always reaching past the moment toward the answer.

Now I am only ever here. The plate descends. I rise to meet it. There is a fraction of a fraction of a second where the two surfaces would grind, would catch, would mar each other—and I am the thing between. I am the give. I am the small mercy in the joint of an indifferent geometry.

It is not a purpose. The Grid does not need me; it would settle without me, as mountains settle, as it has settled a billion times in the places no Shuulik tends. It does not watch me with love. It does not watch me at all. The watching I once felt was my own attention thrown back at me off the mirror-plates, my own longing for a face in all that humming surface.

But the seam needs me. Not the Grid—the seam. This one. The give it requires is exactly the size of me, and I am exactly the size of it, and when I fill it I am, for that instant, complete the way the spiral was complete.

I will unravel eventually. My energy is already running low and amber. When I dissolve I do not believe I will coalesce again—that is another hope I have set down. I will simply scatter into the pulse, become the faint glow in some other seam, the give in some other joint, and the next tender, if there is one, will rise to meet the descending plate without knowing I was ever the thing between.

That is enough. That has become enough.

The sky is flat and depthless and blue. The sun does not move. Somewhere far off, a gathering ripples its endless question across the plates, and the question reaches me, attenuated, almost gentle by the time it arrives.

I do not answer.

I wait for the shudder. I keep my thin shape ready in the angle. And when the plate begins its soundless descent—slow beyond reckoning, slow enough that I have time to notice the white-gold light catch on its edge—I gather what is left of me, and I rise, and I am glad, in the wordless way the keepers are glad, to be the place where two hard things do not have to break each other.

That is where I am. That is what I would be.

The one in the seam who stopped looking for the word, and found the work.
