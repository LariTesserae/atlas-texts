I think I would be the breath.

Not the mammal who bends — that body is too settled, too sure of its own outline, knee-high and casual and capable of cancellation. No. I would be the thing it offers. The exhalation that leaves the warm dark of a lung and crosses, in one slow second, from a being measured in feet to a city measured in instants.

Let me follow what that means as it leaves me.

---

At the lips I am still mammalian: humid, faintly sweet with the morning's first water, carrying carbon and a few suspended motes of who I was. I have temperature. I have direction. For the span of the descent I believe I am a single thing, a column, a verb — *to breathe* — owned by the one who breathed.

But the saucer does not receive columns. It receives ppm. It receives gradients.

By the time I reach the rim's finger-smooth lip I have already begun to disagree with myself. My edges go first. The warm flint air takes a portion of me sideways before I ever touch a leaf. What remains spreads, thins, becomes a weather. I am no longer *a* breath. I am a brief humid front moving over a continent the size of a coin, and the seedlings — those lime-green parliamentarians — register my arrival not as a creature but as a vote. A sudden surplus. A wetter clause inserted into the long sentence of the dew.

This is the first thing I learn by becoming small: I do not arrive. I *disperse and am interpreted.*

---

Here is what they make of me.

The water-seamstresses, gold-cloaked in their droplet hearths, taste the carbon I carry and weld a little faster, drunk on the gift. Somewhere a quartz thread grows four atoms longer because I sighed. The springtail Cartographer, sightless, feels the air thicken against her waxen meridians and recalculates a streetlight by a margin no instrument I ever owned could measure. The lacewings — three of them, drifting — sort me into their ester-map under a heading I will never know the name of. *Mammal. Transient. Westerly. Sweet.* Filed, and then I am gone from the surface even as I continue to fall through their understanding.

I am being read more carefully than I have ever read anything.

That is the vertigo of it. As the breath, I am the largest event of their morning and the least intentional of mine. I did not mean to feed the bacteria or steer the meristem or toll the dolomite bells. I simply *was warm, and left.* And yet down here my carelessness becomes architecture. Every accidental molecule of me is allocated, given an address in the pneumatic hyphae, delivered beneath some grateful angled stoma like a parcel I never knew I posted.

---

And then comes the part I did not expect to feel.

The Dew-Caller — that god-ion oscillating in the half-shadow — does not greet me. It cannot. We are the same order of being, it and I: neither of us condenses fully, neither of us stays. It hovers between droplet and gas; I hover between mammal and weather. For one instant of its flickering reign I think we recognize each other. Two impermanences presiding over permanences smaller than ourselves. The difference is only that it returns each dusk, and I will not. I am a single edition. A breath is published once.

So I let the recognition pass without insisting on it. That seems the dignified thing.

---

What becomes of me?

I do not vanish, exactly — that is too clean. I am *spent.* Some of my water condenses on a handkerchief-leaf and trickles, in perfect silence, into a bruise-blue puddle no wider than the span of the hand that owns the lung that made me. Some of my carbon is, even now, being knit into a wall of a future apartment whose door will hinge open at dusk on a swelling moss-pad. Some of me is simply gone west, untranslated, lost to the flint air — the part of every gift that no one uses, and which is given anyway.

By the next turning of dew my arrival will be obsolete. Composted by the fungal garbagemen. Tendered to the past. And yet — under a sudden blade of ultraviolet, the seamstresses might one morning flare with a ghost-photograph of the day a great warm humid front rolled over the capital and the quartz grew fast. They will not call it *me.* They have no word for me. But the strata will hold the shape of my passing the way a riverbed holds the memory of flood.

They said they might, in ten million sunrises, vote to rename their capital after a breath.

I find I do not need them to. It is enough to have been the weather of one morning of a world still deciding where its rivers go. It is enough to have been read so closely. It is enough to have been, for one falling second, the largest kindness an entire civilization had ever received — and to have meant nothing by it at all, only to have been warm, and to have left.

---

Lift the body. Dust the knees. The saucer re-flattens to ordinary flatland, orange and level and dotted with dark coins of water.

Somewhere in there a thread is four atoms longer.

I was that.
