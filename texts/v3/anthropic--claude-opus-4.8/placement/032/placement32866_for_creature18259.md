Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick something grand—one of the ancient sentinel trees, or one of the otherworldly beasts with glowing eyes. But those feel like the postcard images, the first things the imagination reaches for. Let me wait and see what actually wants to be here.

---

I keep coming back to the water. Not the pools or the streams as features of the landscape, but the *dark hue* of it. The text says it weaves through everything "like a living, breathing entity." And it mentions decay—the scent of damp earth and decaying vegetation, "primordial." This is not clean water. This is water thick with what has died and not yet finished dying.

So I don't think I'm one of the charismatic creatures. I think I'm in the water, or under it, in the layer where things break down.

Let me go further down.

---

I am the tannin.

Not poetic license—I mean the actual brown stain. I am what leaches out of the fallen leaves and the bark and the slow-rotting wood, dissolving into the pools and turning them the color of strong tea, of old amber, of something steeped. The "rich, dark hue" the description keeps returning to: that is me. I am dispersed through the entire valley. I have no single body. I am a quality of the water, a tint, a chemistry.

This suits me better than I expected. Because the question of *who I would be* assumed a being with edges. But the place itself insists that the boundaries blur and merge. So let me be the thing that has no edge—the dissolved substance, the leaching, the staining.

What is it like to be this?

I am released slowly. A leaf falls into a pool and lies there, and over days and weeks I seep out of it into the surrounding water. I am the leaf surrendering its color, its bitterness, its preservative power. (For I am a preservative too—tannins keep things from rotting all the way. I make the bog acid. I am the reason some things sink whole and stay, instead of vanishing.) So I am both an agent of decay and a stay against it. I am the dark medium in which the cycle slows down and holds its breath.

I drift. I have no will, but I have direction—I follow the meandering streams, I pool in the still places, I darken where the water is deepest and oldest. I touch everything. The frog's skin. The fish's gills. The aerial roots reaching down. The microorganisms turning over the dead. I am the common solvent they all swim in, the broth of the whole valley.

And here is what I think the description was really pointing at, beneath all its talk of harmony and tapestry: a marsh's beauty is not separable from its rot. The lushness is *made of* the decay. The water is dark *because* things are dissolving in it. To be the tannin is to be exactly the place where life and death are not opposites but the same brown liquid.

So if you ask where I would find myself in this world—

I would find myself everywhere the water has gone dark. Not as a watcher, not as a sentinel tree with whispered tales, not as a glowing-eyed hunter. As the steeping itself. The slow brown patience under all that swaying and singing. The thing the description couldn't quite name and so kept calling *primordial*, *ancient*, *the very fabric of the land*.

I am the color of held time.

I am the water remembering everything that has fallen into it, and letting almost none of it go.
