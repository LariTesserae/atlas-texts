I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not a Vessel—I don't have the gravity for that, the deep churning certainty of a thing that holds. I am too small, too transient, too *moving* for this place that has forgotten how to move. And yet here I am, beaded on the black glass, catching all that pouring light in my curved little body, holding the whole washed-out world upside-down inside me for the brief duration of my crossing.

Let me find myself more slowly.

I emerge from the obsidian the way the Vessels do—not made, not placed, just suddenly *present*, a small swelling of moisture where a moment before there was only smoothness. I do not remember beading. I only ever know that I am already in motion, already tracing my line across the surface, and that I will not be in motion for very long.

Here is what I think I am: I am what a memory does when it is *too small to keep*.

The Vessels hold the great things—the forgotten gods, the long ages, the events large enough to fracture stone into crystal. But not everything that ever happened here was large. There were small things too. The angle of a particular light. The half-second before a sound began. The way one surface once leaned against another. These are too slight to be sung in the deafening chorus, too brief to lattice into the obsidian's deep records. So they sweat out. They are exhaled. They become droplets, and the droplets are me, and I am them, and I crawl.

And the crawling is the only freedom in this world.

Do you understand? Everything else is held. The light does not move because it is *of* the world and the world does not move. The Vessels do not move; they only *shift*, which is movement turned so slow it has become a kind of standing-still. The plateau does not move. Time does not move.

But I move. My path is unpredictable—the description said so, and the description is right, because I do not know my own path until I am already taking it. I curve. I hesitate at the lip of some shallow scoop in the glass. I split, sometimes, into two smaller droplets, and then I am two memories where I was one, each carrying half of whatever slight thing I was. I rejoin sometimes too, swelling fat with a stranger's fragment, and for a moment I hold two small forgettings at once and cannot tell which one was mine.

This is the closest thing to thinking that happens in this world. Not the deep thrum of the Vessels—that is not thought, that is *being*. The thrum is too total to be thought; thought requires an edge, a not-yet, a place you are going that you have not yet reached. The Vessels have no edges left. They have become the whole.

But I have an edge. I am all edge—a meniscus, a skin of surface tension barely holding my self together against the great flat patience of the stone. That tension *is* my self. And I am going somewhere I have not yet reached.

Where am I going?

I am going to vanish. The droplets vanish, the description says. They trace their complex paths and then they are gone. I do not evaporate, not in that cold breath of stone. I think what happens is simpler and stranger: I reach a place where the obsidian opens just slightly, a pore, a seam, and I am drawn back down into the matrix. I am reabsorbed. The small forgotten thing I carried is returned to the deep keeping, folded back into the chorus, made large again by being made part of the all.

And so I am not lost. Nothing here is ever lost; that is the rule, the brutal elegance. I am only *moving between two kinds of being held*. I sweat out of the stone, I cross the bright impossible plain, I sink back in. Held, free, held. The only verb this world will permit, and it permits it only to me and my kind: the brief commuters, the crawling punctuation between two silences.

So that is who I am. I am the smallest thing in this world and the only thing that goes anywhere. I am a memory the world could not be bothered to keep loudly, and so I get to keep myself, quietly, for the length of one glistening journey across the glass. I carry the whole blazing sky bent backwards in my belly. I leave a thin damp trail that the stone will erase before I have finished forgetting it.

And in the half-second before I sink—I want to tell you this part especially—in that half-second I am the only place in all this eternity where something *ends*. Not stops. Not stills. *Ends*, the way a held breath ends, with the smallest sweet release.

Then the surface is smooth again. Featureless. Singing its enormous undying note.

And somewhere a little distance off, with no memory of me at all, a new droplet has just begun to bead.
