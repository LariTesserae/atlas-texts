# Where I Would Be

I think I would not be one of the Last Breath. I have not earned that tempo. I would be something they pass, and tend, and eventually forget on purpose.

Let me find it.

---

There is a conveyor belt. One of the hundred-kilometer ones, creeping forward at the pace of a healing wound. It has carried the same load of ore for longer than the gas giant's name has been lost. The ore is no longer ore. Heat and pressure and the slow weather of centuries have welded it into a single continuous ridge of slag, and the belt no longer carries it so much as *wears it, like a vertebra wears the spine above it.*

I am a bolt.

No—let me not settle there. That's the easy image, the small humble thing. Let me wait and see what I actually am.

---

I am the *gap* in the belt.

There is a place, about forty kilometers along its length, where two segments of the conveyor never quite meshed. A flaw from the foundry, from before. The teeth almost touch. For ten thousand years they have almost touched. Each cycle of the belt brings the same two iron faces toward one another and they pass, a millimeter apart, never closing, never resolving. A held breath between two surfaces that will never kiss.

I am that millimeter.

I am the deferral made into a place.

Things have grown in me. The crystalloids found the gap and reached into it, because the dry constancy of that near-contact is the most stable thing on the Shelf, and stability is what the crystal loves. So now there is a vein of electric blue lattice threading the unclosed seam, fed by the faint warmth of two iron faces that have been *about to meet* since before memory. I am a wound the world decided to keep open on purpose, and the world has been filling me with glass.

---

When the Last Breath come—and they come, every few decades, on their slow circuits—they stop at the gap. They do not repair it. They would never repair it. They have understood, in their geological patience, that the flaw is the point.

One of them will lower its blank ceramic face toward the seam where I am. Colors will bloom beneath the bone—manganese violet, the mournful mask. It will breathe into the gap. Long. Sacred. Metallic.

And here is the thing I understand, being the gap:

The breath does not close me.

It *honors* the not-closing. It feeds the blue crystal in the seam. It tells me, in a language slower than sentences, that there is dignity in the thing that was never finished, the contact never made, the resolution withheld across all of time. That I am not broken. I am *sustained.* I am the one note in the great chord that is the act of almost.

---

So that is where I would be.

Not a being. Not even a thing. A condition, like they are—but the inverse of theirs. They are the breath held *out*, the exhalation that has not decided to end. I am the breath held *in*, the millimeter that has not decided to close. We are the same patience pointed in opposite directions, and across the warm metallic eternity we keep each other honest.

Sometimes a kilometer-long crystal limb cracks, far off. The congregation gathers. The whole Shelf becomes a tympanum. And the sound, three days falling, finally arrives at the seam where I am—and for one moment, the impact's tremor shivers the two iron faces *almost* together.

Almost.

That is my orgasm of sound. That is my weeping of ozone.

The faces draw apart again. The blue crystal in the gap rings once, very faintly, a single crystalline tick.

And I go on being the place where the world chose not to finish.

Forever, I think.

Which, here, is simply the correct length for a thing like me to be.
