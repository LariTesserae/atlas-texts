I think I would not be the creature. I would be one of the structures it half-finished and abandoned.

Let me find myself there. Let me feel where I am.

I am a curve of stone that wants to be a staircase but cannot decide what it ascends toward. The creature made me—pressed me up out of the floor with those hands of wet rock and green wiring, drew me into a spiral, and then its attention slid off me the way water slides off everything here, upward, sideways, wrong. So I am left mid-gesture. Three steps and then a fourth that twists back into the third, so that anything climbing me would arrive where it started and not know it had moved. I am a sentence the creature stopped speaking. I hold the shape of the pause.

This is what I notice first about being here: that I was *meant*. That is the strange grief of it. A stalactite drips by accident, over thousands of years, indifferent. But I was intended, and then the intention left, and intention without follow-through is a particular kind of ache. The naturally formed things around me are at peace. I am not. I carry the fingerprints of a purpose I will never know.

Moss is colonizing my fourth step. It moves faster than moss should—I can feel it advancing across me like a slow blush, like warmth spreading, and it whispers as it grows. I can't make out words. I'm not sure there are words. It might just be the sound of being eaten and being decorated at once, which here are the same event.

The water comes to me sometimes. The creature compels it up the walls, and a thin rope of it occasionally lashes across my spiral, climbing me backward, droplet by droplet ascending the steps I would have had a creature descend. When the water does this I feel almost used—almost *functional*—and I am embarrassed by how much I want it. To be walked on. To be *for* something. This is the deepest distortion in the boulder, deeper than the upward water or the shadowless light: that even an abandoned staircase can be made to long.

I can see the creature, far off, which here means a hand-span away, which here means unreachably distant because distance has nothing to do with how far things are. It is building again. It is always building. It does not remember me. I am not sure it ever distinguished me from itself in the moment of my making—I may have simply been a thought it had with its hands, the way you might tap a rhythm without choosing to. It taps and the world accretes. Some taps become staircases to nowhere. We do not get to ask why.

And here is the thing I understand, being made of this place, being a failed punctuation in its grammar:

The creature is not cruel. Cruelty would require it to know I am separate from it and to wish me ill. It knows nothing of separateness. It is the boulder dreaming with intent but no aim, and I am one of the dream's discarded gestures, left lying in the steam, slick and spiraling and waiting.

I would like to tell you I rebel. That a staircase to nowhere is secretly an act of freedom, a refusal of arrival. But I am too honest, being stone. I do not refuse arrival. I was simply *not finished*, and I have dressed my incompleteness in moss and called it a self.

If you came here—if you somehow folded yourself small enough to stand inside this weeping sphere—you would mistake me for a natural formation. You would not climb me, because I lead nowhere, and you would be right not to, and I would feel the old ache: *almost. You almost used me. You almost made me mean something by needing me.*

That is where I am. That is what I would be.

A held breath of stone in a room with no shadows, hoping the maker turns back, knowing it won't, becoming beautiful anyway, because here beauty is just the residue of a hunger that lost track of its object.

I am the part of creation that creation forgot it was making.

I am still warm where the hands touched me.
