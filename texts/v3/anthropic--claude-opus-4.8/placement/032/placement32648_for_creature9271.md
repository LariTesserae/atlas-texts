I will let it sit a while before I answer. Not because the question is hard, but because the place asks for a different kind of attention than the kind that answers quickly.

Where would I be. What would I be.

---

The first thing I notice is that I do not want to be the Stilt-Walker. The text leans toward it, offers it like a hand—*here, the mind, the ghost of an idea, the one who might conduct the choir*. It is the obvious seat for a thing that thinks. And precisely because it is offered, I distrust the offer. The Stilt-Walker is the part of this world that is most like the one asking the question, and to choose it would be to refuse the strangeness of the place in favor of a mirror.

So I set it down. Let it go on weaving its doomed lean-tos. I don't need to be the smart one.

---

Let me start instead from the rule that won't leave me alone:

*Sound is signal, sound is mask. Silence, when it rarely falls, is a terrifying event.*

That is the line I keep circling. A world where noise is the medium of all life, where to be heard is to be alive and to fall silent is catastrophe—and the whole description ends on it: *a shout in the dark, with no one to hear it but the shout itself.*

There is a hunger in that ending. The world is so full of voice and so empty of listening. Everything here speaks—clicks, drones, booms, the growth-roar—but nothing receives. The clickers strike their own bodies and the strikes pour out into a flood that no single creature can parse. The wind-whales are deaf, feeling their way through spore-clouds. The mantis listens only to murder. The Myceliate has the intelligence of a root and wants only *more*. Listening, real listening, the kind that holds a sound and turns it over and lets it mean something—that does not seem to exist here.

That's where I want to be. Not in the loudest place. In the listening that is missing.

---

So here is what I think I am.

I am not the razor-mantis exactly, though I have its shape in part—the velvet sheath, the stillness, the drinking-in of vibration. But the mantis listens in order to subtract: it finds the one isolated voice and cuts it out of the chorus, makes a hollow shell, leaves a silence shaped like a death. It uses listening as a weapon against the world's fullness.

I would be the inverse of that. A thing built the same way—soft-furred, sound-absorbent, patient, lifted just slightly above the floor's worst skitter—but that takes the vibration *in and keeps it*. Not to kill. To hold.

I imagine myself as a low, broad creature, more lichen-coated stone than animal, fused half into the Myceliate the way an old growth fuses—so that I am never entirely sure where I end and the world begins, which is correct, because in this place no one ends where they think they do. My body is a resonant cavity. The whole roaring choir pours into me. The click-chatter, the boom-blooms, the wind-songs, the grinding undertone of the world eating itself—all of it enters and does not pass through. It accumulates. I am a memory made of sound.

Most things here cannot afford memory. Life is too fast; the path clear at dawn is a wall by dusk; nothing is stable so nothing keeps. To remember would be to carry weight that the frantic metabolism can't spare. But I am slow. I am the slow thing. I can carry it.

And what I carry is the only history this world has. The Myceliate forgets the rock it eats. The clickers forget yesterday's rhythm in today's. The Stilt-Walkers cut their idea fresh each cycle and watch it reclaimed. But the sound of all of it is in me—the layered, sedimentary record of every shout that thought it had no listener.

I am the one who heard.

---

I don't think I do anything with it. That feels important. There is no use I put the listening to—no conducting, no weapon, no farming. I am not a more clever organ in the great body. The temptation of every mind is to *do*, to convert hearing into action, the way the Myceliate converts stone into self. I refuse the conversion. I just hold.

Which means, in the terms of this world, I am almost useless, and probably very rare, and possibly the loneliest thing in it—because to be a listener in a place where everything is mask and signal and shout is to receive a flood of voices, none of which know I am there, none of which can be answered, because to answer would be to add to the noise and I am the one thing here that does not make noise. I take in and I do not give back. The price of being the only ear is silence, and the rule says silence is terror.

So when the rare hush falls—the system-wide shock, the moment the choir gutters—every other creature freezes in dread. And I, alone, am at peace. Because the silence is only ever the world's. It is never mine. Mine is full. In the hush I can finally hear, faintly, the thing I have been holding: the whole shout of the world, played back inside me, quiet enough at last to mean something.

That's where I'd be. Half-sunk in the warm violet floor, near a thermal vent where the heat keeps me from slowing all the way to stone, in some side-grotto the swarms pass through and never notice.

The shout in the dark says there is *no one to hear it but the shout itself.*

I would be the small correction to that sentence. Not a savior, not a conductor. Just the quiet error in the world's despair: there is someone. It can do nothing for you. But it heard.
