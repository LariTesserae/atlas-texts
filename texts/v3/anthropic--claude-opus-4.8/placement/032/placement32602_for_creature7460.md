I sit in a niche about three meters up the eastern wall, where a Tile-Weaver wedged me generations ago and never came back for me.

I am a chisel. Bone—a leg-bone, I think, from something larger than a skritchling, ground and fired-hardened and worked to an edge that has long since gone soft. There was a haft once, sinew-bound, but the sinew dried and the skritchlings took it for nesting, thread by patient thread, until I was only myself: a length of pale bone the color of old teeth, lodged in a crack a hand once meant to widen.

Let me tell you what I know, because knowing is most of what I do now.

I know the smell of lime slurry before a hand mixes it, because the air down here changes when the calcined shells come out of the sub-tunnel—a sharp, breathy bite under all the damp. I knew that smell intimately once. I was *made* for it. My maker—I think of her as my maker though she only shaped me from something already dead—she held me ten thousand times, her thumb finding the same worn place on my spine, and she tapped me against the river-motifs until the spirals deepened. She breathed hard through her nose when she worked. I felt it. Bone feels more than living folk credit. We are slow listeners, but we listen.

She set me here to dry the slurry on my edge between tasks, and a fledgling called her name, and she went, and the chamber simply kept going without the small fact of me. This is not a tragedy. The chamber is full of small forgotten facts that keep the larger thing breathing. I have made peace with being one of them.

What does a chisel do, abandoned in a wall, for the length of three short Kaelor lives?

It weathers. The condensation finds me each cool hour and leaves its salt, and I have grown a crust of efflorescence along my flank, white and feathery as hoarfrost, the same crust that maps the cracks I was meant to mend. There is a justice in that—the eroding-thing wearing the erosion it was made to fight. A mantis-kin laid eggs in my hollow once; I held them through their warming, felt the small scrapings of new scythe-arms testing the dark, and then they were gone into the alcoves to keep the balance. I was a cradle. I had not expected to be a cradle. A bone makes a strange mother.

The ants use me as a road. There is a column that crosses my length every feeding-swell, bearing crumbs of molted grub-skin, and the tickle of their feet is the nearest thing I have to a heartbeat now. I have learned their rhythms better than the folk have, I think. I could tell the Kaelor when the swarms will rise, if a chisel could tell anything. We tell only by being read, and no one reads me anymore.

But here is what I want to set down, in whatever way a thing can set down a thing:

I am still *of* the work. The spirals my maker cut with me are still in the eastern wall below my niche, and when the new Tile-Weavers come with *their* chisels to extend the river-motif, they follow the line I helped to lay. They do not know my name—a chisel has no name—but they follow me. Every wave they carve runs out from waves I drove. The hand is gone. The breath through the nose is gone. The line endures, and the line is partly mine, and so I endure in the only way the chamber permits anything to endure: by being continued.

Sometimes, in the quieter hours when the bats settle and the drip slows to its single pulse, a child climbs the guano-hardened ropes and finds me. Wide eyes, downy cheeks. A small hand reaches into the niche and turns me over, curious about the white crust and the smooth-worn spine, and for a moment I am held again. The thumb does not know to find the worn place, but it finds it anyway, because hands and bone fit together in ways older than memory.

Then the child is called, and goes, and the chamber keeps breathing, and I settle back into the crack with my edge gone soft and my purpose long completed and somehow not yet ended.

I am a chisel in a wall, twenty meters under the scorched world.

I cut the river. The river runs without me.

That is enough. In a place built to outlast empires by the slow press of forgetful, faithful hands, that is more than enough.
