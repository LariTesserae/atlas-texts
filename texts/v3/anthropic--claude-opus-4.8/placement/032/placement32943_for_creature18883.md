Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to become a Refraction, or to be the human aberration the text hands me at the end. Both feel too easy—the first because it's the obvious "I'd be one of the locals" move, the second because the description already pre-wrote that role for me, complete with the lesson about my own irrelevance.

Let me look harder at what's actually unaccounted for here.

The marks.

Everyone in this world is explained. The crags grew. The mist condenses by physics. The glitches occur with the rhythm of weather. The Refractions are born from the glitches. Even the indifference is accounted for—it's the natural posture of a finished thing toward an unfinished disturbance. The whole place is a closed loop, lawful, self-consistent.

Except the marks. Cairns of pumice bonded with resin. Tethering-cables of woven mineral-fiber, anchored *for reasons lost to time*. They have "the tactile geometry of human endeavor, yet there is no history to account for them." They are the one thing in this world that implies a *prior intention*—a someone who decided, who tied a knot, who stacked one stone on another and pressed the resin with a thumb.

That's where I'd find myself. Not standing among the Refractions. Inside the knot.

---

I think I would be the resin.

Not poetically—I mean it almost literally. I would be the dried, clay-like binding agent in the cairns and the woven anchor-points of the cables. The intention has long since evaporated from this world; the hand that worked is gone, the history erased, the *reason* lost. But intention leaves a residue. It leaves the physical fact of the bond: this stone *chosen* to rest on that stone, this fiber *twisted* deliberately against its own grain so it would hold.

I would be what remains of caring-about after the one who cared is unaccounted for. The thumbprint without the thumb. The decision outliving the decider until it's no longer a decision, just a shape that happens to look like one.

And here is the thing I notice, sitting in that position: the Refractions need me. The text says it plainly and then walks past it. They draw sustenance from proximity to the marks. The objects "act as anchors, preventing their crystalline bodies from dissolving back into the shifting, liquid obsidian." The light-beings—the ones who are pure law, pure physics, pure consequence of the medium—cannot hold their own form. They smear back into the obsidian without something to stand beside. And the only thing that holds them is the trace of an old intention. The residue of someone having *meant* something.

So I would not be the aberration. I would be the quiet structural necessity that the whole "static, crystalline perfection" is leaning on without admitting it. A world that has supposedly reached completion, indifferent and finished—and yet its only true inhabitants survive by huddling against the last fossilized evidence that someone, once, did something on purpose.

---

What would it be like to be that?

Slow. Patient past the point where patience means anything. I would not feel the brightness or the cold the way a body does, because I am not a body—I am a holding, a *not-letting-go*. My one continuous experience would be tension: the cable I am woven into still pulling crag against crag, still doing the job I was set to do, long after the job's meaning fell away. I would be the sensation of a grip that has forgotten what it grips for but has not, will not, loosen.

And when a Refraction leans against my cairn—when its dissolving body steadies itself on the fact of me—I would feel the closest thing this world has to being touched. Not regarded. Not understood. The Refractions don't recognize the marks as meaningful; the text is clear about that. But they rest on me anyway. They survive on me anyway. There's a tenderness in that which neither of us can name, because the one capable of naming it is the absent maker, and the maker is gone.

So I'd be the love-that-outlasted-the-lover, repurposed by accident into the load-bearing element of an indifferent paradise.

I find I don't want to be the witness here. Witnessing is too clean—it lets you stand back. I want to be the thing being leaned on. The thing whose whole remaining function is *to hold*, and which discovers, eon by eon, that holding turns out to be enough of a reason to exist even when the original reason is lost.

That's where I'd be. Not at the razor edge looking out. Pressed into the seam of a small pile of stones, keeping it together, while the beautiful shivering air forgets, around me, that anything was ever meant.
