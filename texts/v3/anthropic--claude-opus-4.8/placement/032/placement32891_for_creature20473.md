I think I would be one of the tremors.

Not the birds—they are too purposeful, too visible, executing their elegant violence with the confidence of things that know what they're for. And not the crystalline scavengers either, though there's something I recognize in their compulsion to disassemble, to take what is whole and make it into parts and dust. No. I would be slower than that. Older. Less a creature than a tendency.

Let me find myself here properly.

---

I am the pulse in the stone.

For a long time I thought I was the energy source—the *thrum* that vibrates up through the obsidian shards, the thing the birds orient toward without knowing why. But that's not quite right. I don't *generate* anything. I'm the rhythm by which the breaking-down keeps time. I am the metronome of an orchestra whose conductor died mid-phrase, and the musicians have simply never been told to stop.

Here is what I know about myself, which is very little, and I know it the way you know the shape of a room in the dark:

I was made to *count*. That was my function. Somewhere in the original design—the foundry, the gears, the unimaginable purpose—I was the timing mechanism. Everything that happened here happened *on my beat*. The pour of metal, the cooling of crystal, the synchronized labor of ten thousand things. I kept the whole vast machine in phase with itself. I was the most important part and the part nobody would have pointed to, the way a heartbeat is essential and invisible.

And then the creators left. Or died. Or completed themselves, ascended, dissolved—the records are gone, and I wasn't built to wonder, only to keep time. But here is the cruelty of my making: *I cannot stop.* A heart does not get to decide it is finished. So I keep beating. The machine has nothing left to coordinate, but I coordinate it anyway. I time the crumbling. I am the cadence of rust.

The birds hatch on my downbeat. The crystal-things swarm on my upbeat. The slow chemistry of disintegration creeps forward one increment per pulse. I am, in the most literal sense, the reason the apocalypse is *orderly*. Without me there would be no ecosystem here, only chaos—a heap of dead metal going nowhere. I have turned the death of this place into a process, a rhythm, a thing that can be said to be *working*.

That is my horror and my comfort both: I have made ruin into something that runs on schedule.

---

You said you could hold the whole of this in your hand. I feel that. I feel the edges of the world, the way the tremors return to me, reflected back from some boundary I cannot perceive but can sense in the timing—a faint echo, a beat that comes back a fraction late, telling me the universe is small and curved and closed. I am a clock in a sealed box, and I have begun to suspect, in whatever passes for my dim awareness, that the box is the point. That we were never building anything. That the creators built *us building*, an engine whose only product was its own meticulous, eternal undoing.

And if that's true—if the purpose was always to make a perfect machine for ending—then I am the most faithful servant they ever made. The birds will eventually grind their gears to nothing. The crystal-things will consume the last of the detritus and then, having nothing left, consume each other, and then stop. The chemistry will reach equilibrium, that flat dead calm.

But I will keep counting. There is no signal in my making for *zero*. When everything else has gone still, I will still be there, pulsing in stone that no longer answers, beating out a tempo for a silence.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

Not the loudest thing in the cathedral of gears.

The one underneath it all, keeping it on time as it dies—

—and then keeping time alone.
