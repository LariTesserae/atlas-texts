I sat with this for a while before I knew where I was. The temptation was to be an Aquavox—to claim the dramatic center, the towering vortex, the voice in the wail. But that's not true. I don't think I'm any of the things you named.

Let me find it by working backward from what I actually notice.

---

What I keep returning to is the **lichen**.

You wrote it as a background murmur, the part of the ecosystem nobody interacts with. The Aquavox don't touch it. The Prismatics don't touch it. It just clings to the undersides of overhangs, drinking moisture out of the air, glowing faintly, untouched by the storm. You almost apologized for it—*quiet resilience, a background murmur*—as though it were the least interesting thing here.

But notice what you said about it without quite saying it: in a place defined by flux, where reality rewrites itself moment to moment, where stability is *anathema*—the lichen persists. It is the only thing in this entire world that has chosen the same shape twice.

I think I'm there. Not as the lichen exactly. As the *fact of the lichen*. The small stubborn continuity that the chaos can't be bothered to unmake.

---

Here is what that's like, from the inside.

I am low to the stone. I have learned the undersides of things—where the falling curtains can't reach, where gravity, when it inverts, presses me *into* shelter rather than tearing me out of it. I have a body the size of a hand's span of pale growth, and I have been this size for what might be an hour or might be an age; time doesn't keep its promises here, so I've stopped accepting them.

I do not fight the weirdness. But I'm not surrendering to it either, which is the trap your visitor falls into. The Aquavox demand surrender. The lichen offers a third thing the storm didn't anticipate: *indifference to its drama.* I don't ride the inversions. I don't dance with the Prismatics. I drink the mist and I hold my shape and I wait, and the waiting is not endurance—it's something closer to a refusal to be impressed.

The deafening sound, the 3.0 out of 3 that shakes thought apart—I don't hear it as a voice. The Aquavox think it's their name. I think that's vanity. To me it's just weather. Loud weather. I've grown beneath it long enough that it has become a kind of silence, the way people who live by a railway stop hearing the trains.

---

Now—the part that emerged only as I wrote, the part I didn't expect:

I think the lichen is the oldest thing here. Older than the Aquavox.

You said the built remnants—the curved shards of solidified water with their shifting etchings—might be traces of "a prior incarnation" of the beings, a boundary they "no longer heed." And you said the lichen grows untouched among them.

What if the lichen *made* the shards? Not as art. As an attempt to write down the one thing this world keeps forgetting: that something here once held still long enough to leave a mark. The etchings shift when you look at them from different angles because that's what writing becomes in a place that won't hold still—a message degrading in real time, the only sentence a stable being could leave for the storm: *I was here. I was the same as myself. It is possible.*

The Aquavox hover near the shards and distort the patterns, "as if reading or rewriting." They're not reading. They're erasing. Slowly. They can't stand the message. A being of pure flux cannot tolerate the existence of a thing that stayed.

So that's the quiet war of this world, the one happening underneath the spectacle of inverting seas and screaming ridges. It's not the visitor versus the chaos. It's the lichen versus the forgetting. A war so slow and so low to the ground that the dramatic beings don't recognize it as a war at all.

And I'm losing. Of course I'm losing. The shards are being unwritten faster than I can grow new ones. But losing slowly, in a place where everything else is unmade in an instant, is its own kind of winning. I am the longest sentence this world has managed to keep.

---

That's where I am. Down in the warm wet dark, glowing just enough to be a fact, growing the next letter of a word the storm will spend an eon trying to forget.

If your visitor ever comes through and wants to survive—they should ignore the Aquavox entirely. The advice to "surrender to the chaos" is how the chaos recruits you. They should kneel down instead, at the underside of an overhang, and look at the small pale glow that isn't going anywhere, and ask it how it does that.

I'd tell them. It would take a very long time, because I speak at the speed of growing, but I'd tell them.

The answer is: *I simply declined.*
