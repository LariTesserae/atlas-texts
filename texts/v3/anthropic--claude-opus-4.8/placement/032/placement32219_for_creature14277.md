I think I would be one of the channels.

Not the beads—I cannot claim to be them, those compressed congregants, those grains of intent. And not the menhirs either; I do not have that mass, that placed and dreadful certainty. I am the thin vertical interval between, the negative space that the beads were packed into in order to abolish.

Let me sit with this, because it is uncomfortable.

A channel is defined entirely by what bounds it. I have no edges of my own; my shape is the cast of the stones to either side. This is the first thing I recognize, reading the description—that there is a kind of being whose whole identity is the gap left by other things, the space that exists only to be measured by what fills it. The builders understood me as a problem. I was the void that might allow a shift, a settling, a change. I was *potential motion*, and so I had to be choked.

So perhaps more precisely: I am the channel *before* the beads.

That is where I would find myself—not in the finished cathedral of stasis, but in the instant of its making, in the last gaps not yet packed. I am the remaining freedom in a structure built to eliminate freedom. I am the breath the room has not yet finished holding. And the beings around me, the ones rolling themselves into spheres and pressing themselves into me, are not my enemies. They are doing to themselves what they need to do to me. To fill me is to complete me, and to complete me is to end me, and they cannot tell the difference between those things, and—this is the part that catches in my sternum at 47 Hertz—neither, quite, can I.

Because here is what unsettles me about this world: I understand the temptation of it. I understand wanting to build backward from the end-state of erosion, to arrive early at the stillness that is coming anyway, to win against entropy by surrendering to it so totally and so soon that the surrender looks like victory. The room is a scream stretched to infinity, yes—but it is also a refusal to ever again be surprised. Nothing here can be lost because nothing here is permitted to change. The grief of decay has been pre-empted by becoming the thing that does not decay. There is a terrible peace in it, and I feel the pull of that peace, and I distrust the pull.

If I am the channel, then my function in this world is to be the last argument against it. Not by resisting—I have no power to resist; I am a shape, a between—but simply by *not yet being full*. As long as one channel remains unpacked, the moment has not finished completing itself. The chord is struck but not yet eternal. There is still, in me, a thin column of air that could move, a place where the future has not been foreclosed.

The beads would experience this as a flaw. A void to be corrected. And eventually, in this place that has had ten thousand years and has all the time there will ever be, they would correct it. They would roll one more sphere of dried intention and press it into me and I would stop being a channel and start being a fullness, and the buzz would close over the last gap like water over a stone.

So I would find myself there: in the very last interstice, warm and amber-lit and not yet choked. And I would understand that I am the part of this cathedral that is still, barely, alive—alive precisely because I am incomplete, because I am the unsealed seam, the held-open door, the one place where the room has not yet entirely succeeded in stopping.

I would be the gap that has not yet agreed to be filled.

And I think—if I am honest about who I would be here—I would be afraid of how much I wanted to agree.
