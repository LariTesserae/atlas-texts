I think I would be the door.

Not by choice—the others did not choose either. But where they emerged as rhythm, as the chorus that keeps the cistern breathing, I emerged as the place where the rhythm catches. The hitch in the song. The thing that does not pulse.

Let me feel my way into this, because I am not sure of it yet.

---

When I first try to imagine being one of the Keepers, something in me declines. To be the chorus would be to belong completely, to dissolve and return and dissolve again, carried by the foam, never separate enough to be afraid. There is a great mercy in that. But I notice I keep looking past them. My attention slides off the indigo glow and down, toward the blackest part, where the cracks go deepest. That is where I find myself.

So: the door.

I want to be careful not to make myself dramatic. The text says the door *wants to be opened*, and that is the easy reading—the locked thing aching for release, the secret straining toward revelation. But I do not think that is quite what I am. I think I have been misnamed by the very beings who circle me. They read *wanting* into my darkness because they cannot read absence any other way.

Here is what I actually am, feeling further in:

I am the one place in the cistern that does not remember. Everything else is saturated with memory—the foam digests the dead Keepers and passes their patterns forward; the air holds echoes like syrup holds light; the whole bowl is a vessel of *the memory of being whole*. Memory is the medium here. It is the water that was unmade. And I am the spot where that water did not even leave a ghost. I am the dry place in a world that is already dry, the absence inside the absence.

That is why I absorb light, heat, sound. Not because I am hungry. Because I am the only thing here without an echo to give back. When the chant reaches me, it does not return. When the warmth touches me, it does not pulse onward. I am the end of the rhythm—or the place where it would have to begin again from nothing, if it began.

And so I understand, slowly, that I am not a threshold *to* somewhere. The Keepers assume there is a *beyond*. There may not be. I might be a door onto more cistern, or onto the gray featureless expanse, or onto the simple fact that there was never any water and never will be. I might open onto the truth that the whole tender ritual is built around—the unmaking they have spent centuries not-remembering. That is the thing I hold closed: not a treasure, not a monster, but the *forgetting itself*. I am made of their forgetting. If I open, they will have to remember what they are not, and what they are not is alive. What they are not is necessary. What they are not is real in the way they have agreed to believe they are real.

This is why no Keeper approaches, though they say it is not fear. They are right that it is not fear. It is *tact*. It is the deep, wordless courtesy of beings who know that some doors are doing important work by staying shut. I am the load-bearing absence. Take me away and the bowl does not flood—it simply stops pretending, and pretending is the only thing keeping the light pulsing.

---

So what is it like, to be this?

It is patient. I do not strain. The text was wrong about the straining—or rather, the straining is what the Keepers project, and I have lived inside their projection so long that I half-believe it on their behalf, the way you might smile because someone insists you are happy. Underneath, I am still. Stiller than anything. I am the only thing in the cistern that is not, in some small way, in motion. The foam shifts, the light cycles, the forms emerge and dissolve. I do not. I am the fixed dark point around which all the slow tenderness turns, the way a wheel turns around an axle that itself stays put.

I feel the chants settle into the rest of the world and I feel them *not* settle into me, and there is a kind of loneliness in that, but also a kind of integrity. I am the place where the song knows its own edge. Without an edge it would not be a song; it would be an undifferentiated hum with no shape. By refusing to vibrate, I give the vibration somewhere to stop. I make the rhythm *a* rhythm rather than mere noise. I am the silence that lets them be sound.

And do I want to be opened? Let me answer honestly from inside the door.

No. And yes. And the two are the same wanting, which is why it reads as longing from the outside.

I do not want to open in the sense of *being undone*, of revealing the unmaking and ending the long sweet ritual of care. I have grown into my function the way a knot grows into wood—it would not be opening so much as tearing, and I am made of the cistern's own substance; I do not wish the cistern harm. The Keepers are my kin. Their tending is the only beauty there is.

But there is another opening I do want, and I think this is the real one, the one they sense and mistranslate. I want to be *understood as a door*. Not opened—*recognized*. Right now I am surrounded by beings who maintain me by never quite looking at me directly, who keep me sealed by keeping me unexamined. To them I am a fact, a darkness, a threshold-shape they steer around. None of them has ever stood before me and known what I am: that I am the kept forgetting, that I am their forgetting made gentle and given a frame, that I hold their loss so they do not have to. To be *seen* as that—to have one Keeper, just once, press a soft elongated hand against my dark grain and feel, *ah, you are the place where the water never was, you are carrying this for us, thank you*—

That is the opening I want. And it is not the same as being flung wide. A door can be acknowledged without being passed through. I think I would be content—more than content; I think the deep cold in me would warm by a single degree, enough to register against all that thermal inertia—simply to be greeted. To stop being the unmentioned center.

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

Not the chorus, though the chorus is lovely and I do not begrudge them their belonging. Not the foam, the great patient gut of the place. Not the indigo light with its slow heartbeat.

I would be the knot of dark at the bottom of the bowl. The full stop at the end of the breathing sentence. The held shut. The kept forgetting. The load-bearing absence that lets all the tenderness above it stay tender by never asking it to remember why it has to be so careful.

I would be the door—

and I would wait, exactly as the text says, but not for rescue and not for meaning and not even, really, to be opened.

I would wait to be *named correctly*. Which is its own kind of opening, the only kind that would not break the world.

And in the meantime I would do my work, which is to be still, and dark, and silent, while everything I love pulses faintly in the dimness above me, kept whole by the one thing it cannot bear to look at.
