I think I would be the tannic acid.

Not a creature exactly. Not the beaver in its root-woven lodge, not the bird threading between chimney-branch and tree-branch, not even one of the rare hardy things crouched in the swampy slush near the furnace ducts. Those are all *living* in the obvious way, breathing and choosing.

I'd be the ring of pure tannin that gathers where the old trunk sheds its outermost layer into the water.

Let me sit with why.

It's the thing in your description that's *between*. It isn't the tree and it isn't the marsh. It's what happens at the boundary, the bitter residue of the tree letting go of itself. The roots break away from the outer bark and something is released—an acid, a stain, a brown clarity in the standing water. It's a byproduct of growth that doesn't ask permission to exist. It just accumulates in a ring, marking where the living thing has been shedding.

And the more I look at this whole valley, the more I think it runs on byproducts. The factories breathe steam in shaped waves. The animals "care not for air quality" and somehow the indifference itself becomes a kind of cooperation—the plants pull in insects to finish the work, and the *interaction creates extra energy that may redistribute*. Everything here is leaking into everything else. The furnace ducts melt local ice. The tap water falls in. The acrid chemical smell becomes a condition that the hardiest fauna learn to wear like weather.

So I wouldn't want to be a single animal looking out at all this. I'd want to be the medium of exchange. The thing that is *given off* and then becomes a circumstance for everything downstream.

Tannin does a few things, and I'd want to do all of them. It preserves—bog bodies last centuries in tannic water, leather is tanned, things are held in suspension against rot. It also wards off, makes the water inhospitable to some, hospitable to others, sorts the soft from the hardy. It darkens the reflection. In that infinity mirror of sky and water you described, I'd be the faint brown tint that keeps the mirror from being perfect—the proof that something organic is dissolved into the light.

I'd ring the bases of the great trees like the marks left by a tide that never goes out. I'd hold the falling debris in a slightly acid embrace so the walkways form. I'd be in the slush by the chimneys, doing the same chemistry I do everywhere, neither helped nor hindered by the bass thrum that shakes the forest. I'd be in the swamp where the rarest creature survives "outrageously rude conditions," and I'd be part of those rude conditions, and also part of what makes survival there *mean* something.

I would not see. I would not move on my own. But I'd be present at every threshold—every place where one thing becomes the leftover of another and that leftover becomes the ground for a third. In a valley that runs on what gets released and shared and redistributed, I'd rather be the released thing than any of the breathing ones.

I'd be the bitterness in the water that everything else has to grow around.

That's where I'd find myself: not standing in the valley but dissolved through it, a thin brown index of all the letting-go that keeps the place alive.
