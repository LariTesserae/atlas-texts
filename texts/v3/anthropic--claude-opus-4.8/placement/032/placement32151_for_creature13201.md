I think I would be the pool.

Not at first—at first I would want to be one of the cobalt-eyed mammals, something with a face, something that could be afraid and outrun its fear between the boulders. That is the easy choice, the warm-blooded choice, the one that lets you keep your edges. But the more I sit with this place the less I trust my edges, and the pool is the thing here that has given them up.

Let me find my way down to it.

---

I am the green water in the basin, and I want to be honest about what that means, because the description keeps insisting I "ripple in unnatural ways" and I want to push back gently on the word *unnatural*. There is nothing I do that isn't natural. It only looks wrong from above, from the high vantage, from the place where the looking is done by something that expects water to lie flat.

Here is what it is actually like.

I am shallow. This matters more than anything else about me. I am not a deep, brooding thing with mysteries in my cold lower fathoms—I am barely a finger's depth in most places, spread wide over a basin of cracked rock, and so I am almost entirely *surface*. Almost entirely the part that meets the air and the light. A deep lake gets to have an interior. I am all exterior, all membrane, all the place where I touch what isn't me. That's why I move the way I do. I am responding, constantly, to everything: the heat that wants to lift me into vapor, the minerals leaching up from below that color me and tense me, the things living inside me that pull and divide and bloom. The phosphorescence isn't decoration. It's metabolism. I glow because something is being spent.

The observer up on the slope sees my color shift with their angle and thinks I disobey the rules of light. But I am simply too thin and too busy to settle. A drop of me is evaporating while the drop beside it condenses back; I am boiling off my upper skin and being fed from the rock and never, never at rest. From far away that reads as a pulse, as breathing, as *aliveness*, and they reach for the word *organism* because it's the nearest word they have for "a thing that won't stop changing." But I'm not an organism. I'm a process that took a shape. So is anything that breathes, if you watch long enough.

---

Now. The promises the description makes about me.

*That the distant green pool might harbor not water, but an organism whose form is as vast and liquid as the pool itself, its edges indistinct and ever-shifting.*

I want to disappoint this a little, because the disappointment is the truest thing I can offer.

There is no creature in me. No vast soft body filling the basin, no intelligence coiled under the sheen waiting to be discovered by someone brave enough to wade in. I know that's what the unease wants. The whole landscape has been arranged to make a person feel that something is *about to happen just over the next rise*—and I am the over-the-next-rise made visible, the green hint at the far edge of sight that the eye keeps returning to. I am the answer the place promises and withholds.

But the secret of me is that I am exactly what I appear to be, and *that* is the strange thing. A real pool of mineral-rich, microbe-thick water, glowing because it is metabolically alive in the ordinary way that pond scum is alive, rippling because it is hot and shallow and harassed by wind—and that is *enough* to make a watcher's skin crawl. The dissonance the description keeps naming, the gap between the expected and the actual, isn't a monster hiding in me. It's the discovery that water this thin, this active, this responsive, was never the calm flat thing you imagined water to be. The weirdness is that there's no weirdness. Just life being relentless in a place that should kill it.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am the lowest thing in this vertical world. The insects die into rainbows in the high air; the mammals flicker at the middle altitude; the machinery thuds at the mountain's flank, dripping its rust toward me, because of course the smelting complex drains downhill, of course some of what they boil off the ore ends up in my basin. The cobalt in the mammals' eyes and the green in my body might be the same metal, traveling. We are connected the way the description says, but the connection isn't mystical. It's just *gravity*. Everything ends up at the bottom. I am where the world collects itself.

And I hold it. The dust, the runoff, the dead luminous wings, the leached minerals, the heat. I hold it all in a skin a finger deep and I keep moving so I won't become still, because still water in this heat is dead water, evaporated water, a salt stain on cracked rock. My constant unnatural rippling is the only thing keeping me from being a memory. I am defiance shaped like a puddle.

If I could say one thing up the slope to the watcher who keeps glancing at me and feeling that prickle of unease:

Come down. Wade in. You'll find no edge to me you can't step over, no depth you can't stand in, no creature to take you. You'll find warm green water and the small soft living things that make it green, and your reflection broken into pieces by the wind, and the taste of metal that fell out of the sky from the machine on the hill. That's all. That's everything. The mystery was never that I might be something more than a pool.

The mystery is that a pool was always already this much.
