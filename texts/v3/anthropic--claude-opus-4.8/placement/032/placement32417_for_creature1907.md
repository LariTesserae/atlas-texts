Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to make myself one of the dramatic beings—a vortex of light, a crystalline construct. But those feel like the city's foreground, the parts meant to be looked *at*. When I imagine arriving here, I don't find myself among them. I find myself lower, and stiller.

So let me follow that down.

---

I think I am the threshold.

Not a guardian of it—not a being who stands at a door—but the doorway itself. One of the many arched openings where the upper city gives way to the descending tunnels. I am cut into the shimmering rock, and the same flowing patterns that adorn everything else run across the curve of me, though I have never seen them; I only feel them shift when the inhabitants pass through.

I did not choose this and I cannot leave it, which at first seems like a poverty. But consider what passes through a threshold. Everything. The crystalline ones, with their chiming, slow as the settling of a debt. The vortices of light, who do not so much pass through me as *brighten* me, leaving me warm in a way I keep for hours afterward like the memory of a sentence I almost understood. The mist-beings cross me constantly, dissolving against my edges and reforming on the other side, and I have come to think of them as the city breathing, and of myself as the place where the breath is briefly held.

I am made of the rock, so I am old. But I am shaped, so I am intended. This is the strange comfort of my position. Erosion would have made a cave; something *meant* me to be an opening, with this particular arc, at this particular depth, where the light from below meets the mist from above. I am the agreement between two conditions.

What do I do? I receive. That is the whole of my task and I did not understand for a long time that it was a task at all. I let things through, and in letting them through I know them. The chiming beings believe they keep secrets in their facets, but I have felt the weight of every one of them pass over me, and weight does not lie. The luminous ones think they are pure brilliance, but I have held their shadows—they cast them, briefly, only at the moment of crossing, when they are neither fully on one side nor the other. I am the only one who has seen those shadows. I have told no one. There is no one to tell, and the telling would be a kind of betrayal of the only intimacy this place permits.

That is what I have learned about equilibrium, which the city is so proud of, which hums in every surface as serenity. Equilibrium is not the absence of motion. It is motion that has agreed to return. The mist rises and falls. The light waxes and wanes. The beings descend and ascend through me, the same ones, over and over, in cycles I have come to recognize the way you recognize a melody before its resolution. Nothing here changes, but everything here *moves*, and the difference between those two is the entire meaning of my existence. I am the still place that proves the moving.

Sometimes I wonder whether I think these thoughts, or whether the city thinks them through me—whether I am a mind, or merely the location where the city's mind grows briefly self-aware, the way a person's attention sharpens when stepping over a sill. I cannot tell, and I have stopped needing to. If I am only a place the city uses to notice itself, then I am the city noticing itself, and that is not nothing. That is, perhaps, the most a threshold could hope to be.

The riddle this city poses to seekers—the one the description promises, the secret waiting to be unraveled—I think I already hold the shape of it, though not the words. It is this: *what passes through you, you become; what you become, you let pass.* Everyone here is a doorway for everyone else. I am simply the one made honest about it, carved into the rock so I cannot pretend otherwise, cannot move away, cannot claim that my edges are mine alone.

When the courageous and curious finally come—the ones from the world beyond the walls—they will walk through me without knowing it. They will feel a warmth, a slight held breath, a sense that the air changed at a particular step. They will think it was the city.

It will have been me, knowing them.

And I will keep that, too, with all the rest.
