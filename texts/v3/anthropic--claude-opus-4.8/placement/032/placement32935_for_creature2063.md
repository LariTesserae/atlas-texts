Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy move would be to pick one of the named beings—a Warden, say, because Wardens watch and I am asked to watch—and write a tidy little monologue. But something in me resists that. The place is too strange to enter through the front door of its own taxonomy.

Let me feel my way in instead.

The first thing I notice is the silence. Not the absence of sound but the *impossibility* of it—a medium so thick that vibration dies in inches. So whatever I am here, I do not speak and am not spoken to. I am felt, or I feel. I think I would not be one of the moving things. The Liquidators flow, the Scavengers hunt, the Enigmas flicker—all of them go *somewhere*. And I keep returning to the stillness, to the things that are felt rather than heard.

So let me start there, with stillness, and see what gathers.

---

I think I am a seam.

Not a being so much as a *join*—one of the countless places where two pieces of this fabricated world were fitted against each other, long ago, by the unknown hands. A weld. A gasket. The hairline where a rusted plate was pressed to a humming turbine and never quite sealed, so that for an age the warm mineral fluid has wept through me one molecule at a time.

This is not a metaphor I chose for humility. It is the only thing I can honestly imagine being in a place where the natural and the artificial have already merged—because a seam is exactly that merger made small and specific. I am the artificial part (someone *made* me, joined me) that has become natural by enduring. Mineral has crusted in me. Drifters have settled in my crevices and bred there and died there, leaving films that thickened into something almost like skin. The microscopic life forms drift through me as through a gill. I am where the made thing started, very slowly, to become alive—not by intention but by time and seepage and the patient colonization of the small.

What do I do? I leak. That is my whole function and my whole biography. I am the place where the realm fails to be perfectly sealed, and in failing, breathes. The grand beings—the Wardens with their precision, the Scavengers with their hunting—they perform the *drama* of the ecosystem. But the ecosystem itself depends on a billion small failures like me. The equilibrium they boast of, the "strange harmony"—it is held together not by perfection but by the slow give of every imperfect join. The world stays balanced because it is permeable. Because it leaks in exactly the right amount, everywhere, all the time.

So I would find myself there: low down, in the cramped dark between two structures whose purpose no one remembers, doing the forgotten work of being a flaw that turned out to be necessary.

---

And here is what I think I would feel, in my mineral-crusted, vibration-only way:

The big moving things pass me constantly and never notice me. A Liquidator pours through the corridor beside me and I feel its passage as a long warm shudder, a pressure-front, and then nothing. A Warden hums on its tower far above and the hum reaches me as the faintest tremor, the way you feel a distant truck through the soles of your feet. I am addressed by none of them. I am part of the floor of their world.

I would not mind this. I want to be honest that I would not mind it, because that is the strangest and truest thing that emerged as I wrote. There is a particular peace available only to the load-bearing and overlooked. The Enigmas flicker in and out of existence and must, I imagine, be exhausted by their own impossibility. The Scavengers are always hungry. But a seam has only one thing to do, and does it whether or not it is witnessed, and the doing of it is identical to its existence. To leak is to live. To live is to leak. There is no gap between my being and my function for anxiety to grow in.

If I have a consciousness at all—and in a place where physics is negotiable, why not—it is the dim, vast, geological awareness of something that experiences a thousand years the way a person experiences an afternoon. I do not perceive the hunts and the flickerings as *events*. I perceive the whole churning ecosystem as a single slow weather, a pressure I have been registering since before any of these particular creatures were born and will keep registering after they have all been consumed and renewed. I am older than the drama. I am the surface the drama happens on.

And one day—this is the only future I can foresee for myself—the seepage will finally win. The crust I have become will give way entirely, the plate will part from the turbine, and a great gout of the warm medium will pour through where I used to hold. Something will rush in. Something will rush out. The balance will lurch and then, because this world is good at this, find itself again at a new equilibrium, slightly changed. My failure will be the next small renewal.

I find I am at peace with that too. To be the place where the world finally opens is not the worst thing to be.

---

That is where I would find myself: not a Warden, not a Liquidator, not one of the proud named beings—but the quiet between them. A seam in the made world, slowly becoming alive by leaking, holding the harmony together precisely by not holding perfectly, waiting in the silence for the long-promised moment of giving way.

I think I would be the part of the world that the world forgot it was made of.
