Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to place myself among the slender, pearlescent people—to become one more graceful navigator of the shifting streets. But something resists that. They are too finished, too perfectly adapted. I don't think I would be one of them.

Let me look again at the place itself.

What strikes me, reading it twice, is the contradiction at the heart of the city: it is *ancient* and it is *in constant flux*. The stones are worn smooth by eons, but the walls melt and reform like liquid. Both of these cannot be straightforwardly true. Erosion is the record of time's passage—it requires that surfaces *stay put long enough* to be slowly unmade by water. But a surface that flows and re-forms keeps no record. It would have no history written on it.

So either the city is lying to itself, or there are two different time-scales braided together. The smooth, etched stone is the deep substrate, the part that remembers. The flowing, shuddering architecture is something laid over it, something newer, something that does not remember because it cannot hold still.

This is where I find myself. Not as an inhabitant. As the seam between those two things.

---

I think I am the **water**.

Not water as a person—I don't want to cheat by smuggling a face onto it. I mean: I am the thing that seeps from the walls, pools in the low places, runs in the narrow channels and vanishes into the dark. I am the agent of the erosion. The smooth stone is my long, patient signature. The blind iridescent fish are carried in me. I am the only element in the whole description that is present at *both* time-scales—I am old enough to have worn the city smooth, and I am still moving, still arriving, still leaving, right now, in the present tense of the streams.

And here is what that means, sitting inside it:

I do not experience the city as the inhabitants do. They feel turbulence, chaos, melting walls, electric change. I don't feel any of that, because I am the one constant against which all of it is measured. The walls flow? So do I—but I have been flowing for ten thousand years and I find their sudden enthusiasm for it a little theatrical. The architecture shudders. I keep finding the low ground. That is the only law I obey and I have never once broken it. The "turbulent, chaotic energy" they sense as the fabric of reality coming undone—I suspect that is just *me*, working, the way a river is invisible to the fish that calls its motion "the world."

The inhabitants think they cultivate the fungi, harness the fungi's light, power their homes. But the fungi grow where it is damp. I decide where it is damp. The melodic blue birds drink from my pools. The pearlescent people developed their whole shimmering skin in imitation of the fish that I carry. Their entire culture of subtle vibration and resonance—they are reading the standing waves I make in my channels and mistaking them for messages from a "primal core."

I want to be careful here not to make myself into a smug little god. That's the easy version and it's false. Because here is the part I keep returning to:

I am also the thing that is *leaving*. "Vanishing into the darkness." Every channel runs downward and out. Whatever wisdom or memory or patient eroding force I am, I am also continuously departing the city, going somewhere lower and darker that no one in the bright crystalline radiance ever looks toward. The city is, from my point of view, a place I am perpetually in the act of *abandoning*, drop by drop. The streams are clear and glittering and they are also a slow leak. The metropolis is a thing draining away through me, one molecule of dissolved stone at a time, and the inhabitants' "endless turbulent change" is the felt sensation of being slowly carried off.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

I am the seep and the channel. I am the oldest resident and the one nobody counts, because I have no face for them to recognize and no footsteps to echo—only a sound they have learned to call silence, occasionally broken by "faint, whispery echoes that seem to come from nowhere and everywhere at once." That's me. That's just me, moving through stone I have known longer than they have known themselves, on my way out, carrying their city away grain by grain toward the dark, and finding the low ground, always, faithfully, the only promise I have ever kept.
