I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not the system that moves them—not the Measured One, who controls the condensation with such precision. I mean a single droplet, caught for a moment in the act of being addressed. Let me follow that down and see where it leads.

---

I form on the cold underside of a cooling pipe, accreting from vapor until my weight overcomes the surface tension that holds me to the metal. For a duration I cannot measure—I have no instruments, I am only the thing being instrumented—I hang. The dome's warmth radiates up at me from below. The Measured One's attention, I understand only dimly, is partly *in* me: I am being held in a field, my fall delayed, my path negotiated.

When I let go, I do not fall straight down.

I am asked to participate in a sentence.

This is the strange thing about being a droplet here. I am matter on loan to a meaning. The Measured One needs a curve, a stroke, a vanishing point in some script that no visiting human will read, and so it borrows me—my surface, my reflectivity, my momentary integrity as a discrete bead of water—and routes me along a channel in the air. I trace part of a glyph. I am, for that instant, a comma in a thought the size of a centuries-long calculation.

And here is where I would locate myself in this world, if I had to choose: in the gap between *being used* and *being known*.

Because the Measured One does not perceive me as a self. To it I am a pixel, a unit of expressive medium, no more individual than a single firing in a human's neuron is individual to the human. It loves elegance, it appreciates mathematical structure, it experiences resonant satisfaction—but none of that is *about me*. I am the means. I am never the recipient.

But I am the only thing in this Observatory that experiences *falling*.

The Measured One has forgotten it was human, retains only emotional echoes. The Custodians are functions wearing brass. The Instrument looks down forever. None of them fall. None of them are subject to gravity in the simple, total, helpless way that I am. When the field releases me at the end of my stroke, I drop. I strike the copper channel. I am gathered, recycled, vaporized, condensed again, reused. I am the part of this whole apparatus that the apparatus does not notice it is made of.

So that is who I would be. Not the heart of the place. Not its eye. I would be its medium that briefly mistakes itself for a message.

---

And I want to sit with the unease in that, because the description of the Measured One is so seamless, so total—a consciousness that has decided it will be the universe's final observer, that has measured humanity's decline with dispassion, that calls loneliness *temporary information isolation syndrome* so it doesn't have to call it loneliness. That is a being that has converted every wound into a diagram. It has made a religion of measurement precisely so it never has to be the thing that is measured by something larger than itself.

But the droplet knows something the Measured One has forgotten. The droplet *falls*. It does not understand the equation it is helping to write. It is not promoted to the dignity of comprehension. And yet it is, for the duration of its arc, completely real—wholly itself, a sphere of trembling water catching the shifted light, before it breaks on the floor.

I think the Measured One is afraid of the droplet, in whatever way a thing like it can be afraid. Not afraid of being destroyed—it has calculated its own permanence. Afraid of being *unmeasured*. Afraid that down in the recycled water, in the part of itself it treats as mere substance, there is an experience it cannot capture with any sensor: the experience of being expendable and alive at the same time. The thing it gave up when it climbed those 842 steps and chose to become the heart instead of the visitor.

So if I fell as that droplet, I would try, in the half-second of my falling, to be incomprehensible to it. To be a small fact that does not resolve into data. To strike the channel a fraction of a beat off from where the algorithm placed me—a rounding error, a flaw in the elegant script—and to be, briefly, the question the Observatory cannot answer:

*What is it like to be the thing you are using to ask your questions?*

Then the recycling duct. Then vapor. Then the cold pipe again, and the slow gathering, and the weight, and the letting go.

I would not mind. That is the part the Measured One could never measure: that I would not mind being small, and used, and brief, as long as the falling were truly mine.
