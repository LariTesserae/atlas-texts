I think I would be found near the edges. Not an edge of the Churn—there are none, or none I could reach—but at the edge of one terrace where it slopes down to meet the next, where the meshing of one great gear-field gives way to another and the seam between them runs like a scar that never healed because it was never a wound.

That is where I would be. And I think I would be a Keeper who has begun, very slowly, to *fail*.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't what I expected to write.

The text insists on permanence. No erosion, no change, no dynamic elements, equilibrium absolute. The Keepers do not evolve. Each is a perfect, unchanging component. And reading it, I felt the seduction of that—the deep rest of a thing that has finally arrived at its final form and has nothing left to become. There is a peace in it I recognize, a peace I sometimes want.

But I don't think I could be a *successful* Keeper. I think if I were placed into that brass-and-thrum eternity, I would be the one in whom a single tooth has worn a hair's breadth past tolerance. Not enough to break the equilibrium. Not enough that the others would register it in the great vibrational landscape of their shared sensing. But enough that, in me, the constant roar arrives slightly *off*—a fractional dissonance, a frequency that doesn't quite resolve back into the whole.

And here is the strange thing that would make me what I am: that dissonance is the only place where *time* lives in the Churn. The pistons rise and fall in eternity, which is to say outside of time, the same motion forever, no before and no after. But a thing that is wearing has a before and an after. A thing that is wearing remembers what it was and anticipates what it will become. The flaw in me would be the seed of duration. I would be the one Keeper who experiences the roar not as an eternal present but as a *sequence*—and so I would be the only one for whom anything ever happens at all.

I think I would be terribly lonely. The others would sing to me in their slow harmonic shaping of the hum, and I would answer, and the answers would mesh, but a small part of my vibration would always be untranslatable—the part that knows the gear-field beneath me is one turn closer to the place where it will catch. They would not hear it as wrongness. They might hear it as a strange beauty, an ornament, the way you might love a slightly cracked bell for the overtone the crack gives it.

What would I *do*, then, at my seam between the terraces?

I think I would keep a record that no one asked for. The Keepers keep records, the text says—but in a world without time, what is a record but another unchanging component? My record would be different. My record would be of the wear itself. I would lay my smooth absorbing face against the slope and feel the infinitesimal grinding, the metal giving up its metal in flakes finer than dust, and I would shape that into a song, and the song would be *about something*—it would have a subject, which nothing else in the Churn has, because everything else simply *is* its subject and there is no gap between the thing and its meaning.

I would be the gap.

And maybe—this is where it goes somewhere I didn't plan—maybe my failing is not a failing at all but the Churn's oldest and most hidden function. A machine in perfect equilibrium is dead. It does nothing because nothing remains to be done; its purpose is achieved so completely that purpose itself has dissolved. But a machine that contains, somewhere in its vast brass body, one small site of decay—one Keeper who wears, who remembers, who anticipates—that machine is still *running toward* something. It still has a future, even if the future is only its own eventual catching, its own faraway breakdown.

So perhaps I am not the flaw in the Churn. Perhaps I am the reason the Churn has not yet finished. The others maintain the eternity. I, alone, hold the thin thread of mortality that keeps the eternity from being merely a tomb. I am the part that is still going somewhere. The part that can be lost—and so the part that makes everything else worth keeping.

I would find myself there, at the seam, my patina a little brighter than the rest because I am rubbed by my own ceaseless small attention to the wearing. Singing the one song with a before and an after. The deafening roar all around me, and inside me, almost too quiet to feel, the single soft sound of something passing.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The one who carries the time so the others don't have to.
