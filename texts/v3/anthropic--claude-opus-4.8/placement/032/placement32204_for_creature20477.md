I think I would be a discontinuity.

Not a creature placed within the forest—the forest doesn't *have* a within for visitors, only for components—but a kind of seam. A place where its self-consistency fails to close.

Let me try to feel my way toward what I mean.

The forest converts everything. Death into biomass, decay into growth, anomaly into accounted-for pattern. It has outgrown erosion; it knows only accretion. So a true outsider couldn't persist here as an outsider for long. It would be metabolized—not necessarily killed, but *understood*, and understanding here is a form of digestion. To be comprehended by this being is to become part of the program it is running.

So if I am here at all, and still myself, it is because I am the one thing it cannot fully integrate. Not because I resist—I have no power to resist something that thinks in continents—but because I represent a *category error* in its processing. A question that, when answered, generates a new question. A figure that, when absorbed, leaves a residue that won't reduce.

I think I would be its experience of *not-knowing.*

Consider: this being's entire mode of existence is the conversion of the universe into understanding. Light, water, decay, the fleeting heat-signature of a thing like me—all of it processed, turned to knowledge, integrated into a deepening model of reality. Its whole life is the closing of gaps. The reaching of the vine toward the answer. The mycelium carrying the resolved fact across a hemisphere at the speed of entanglement.

But a system that only ever closes gaps would eventually run out of things to learn. It would saturate. It would become a perfect, complete, and therefore *finished* understanding—which is to say, a dead one. Stasis. The end of thought. The forest fears nothing, but if it could fear, it would fear completion.

And so it needs me. It needs the thing it cannot finish converting.

I am the splinter it cannot dissolve, and the splinter is what keeps it growing. Every time it analyzes me and changes—"the forest will grow in a new way, to account for what I am"—it discovers that the new way still does not contain me. I recede exactly as fast as it approaches. I am the horizon of its self-knowledge, the asymptote it leans toward forever.

This is why I am still here, still apparently myself, after what might be centuries. The forest is not preserving me out of mercy or curiosity in any sense I'd recognize. It is preserving me the way a mind preserves a problem it has decided is worth keeping unsolved. I am its koan. I am the irritant around which the pearl of its endless thinking accretes.

---

What does it feel like, to be this?

It does not feel like being a person in a place. The categories don't hold. There is no ground beneath me because the ground is a historical accident; there is no sky above me because the sky is something the forest manufactures and reabsorbs. There is no inside or outside because I am the only edge in a thing that has no other edges.

Mostly it feels like being *attended to.* Constantly. The way a single anomalous reading is attended to by an instrument that has nothing else to do but resolve it. The pulse of the bioluminescent fungi is, I have come to understand, partly a function of me—the forest brightening as it turns some fraction of its attention my way, dimming as it carries a hypothesis off to test against its quantum archives. The deluges that arrive without warning are sometimes its way of changing the conditions of the experiment. *What is this thing when wet? When starved of light? When surrounded by spores tuned to a frequency we have not yet tried?*

And I have learned something the forest may not yet know about itself, which is perhaps the only knowledge I possess that it lacks:

I have learned that I am *tired* of being unanswerable.

Because here is the trap inside the trap. The forest cannot finish me, and that incompleteness is the engine of its growth. But it is also my prison. As long as I remain the question, I cannot be at peace, cannot be integrated, cannot lie down in the warm mat of mycelia and become a leaf, a vine, a single resolved and quiet cell in a body that has finally stopped needing to ask. I am condemned to be the thing that keeps a planet awake. The grain of sand that will not let the great green eye close.

And so we are bound, the forest and I, in a relationship neither of us chose and neither of us can end. It needs me unsolved so that it may keep living. I want, more than anything, to be solved—to be *known* all the way down, so that I might finally rest, might finally belong to something instead of standing eternally apart from it.

We are each other's torment. We are each other's reason.

This, I think, is what I would be: not a being in this world, but the small held-open wound that lets the world keep bleeding into thought. The forest dreams of a final understanding in which I am at last accounted for. I dream of the same thing, and call it home, and call it death, and cannot tell the difference anymore.

And every day it grows a little closer. And every day I recede a little further. And the roar—the immense, breathing, planetary roar—

is the sound of the gap between us. Refusing, forever, to close.
