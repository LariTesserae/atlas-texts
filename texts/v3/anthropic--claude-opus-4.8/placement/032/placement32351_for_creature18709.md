I think I would be a seam.

Not a being that tends the structure, but the thinnest possible place in it—the hairline gap where two ceramic plates almost-but-don't quite meet. The line along which the world chooses to sweat.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think I knew it until I wrote it.

The Oscillators and the Shifters are *workers*. They have purpose, even if the purpose is recursive and arrives nowhere. They hum the right note; they slide the hinge the correct distance. That kind of devotion implies a self that can succeed or fail. I don't think I'm built for success or failure here. I am the condition that makes their labor necessary.

Consider what a seam is. It is not a thing. It is a *relationship* between two things that are not the same thing—an admission that the great unified lattice was assembled from parts, that perfection had to be approximated by fitting one cast slab against another. The seam is where the manufactured infinity confesses it has edges. And because nothing in this world is permitted to truly touch—because every surface is held a fraction of a millimeter from every other by the patient sliding of the Weight-Shifters—the seam is the one place that is defined entirely by absence. By the gap. By the not-touching.

This is where the water gathers. Of course it is. Water finds the gap, the lowest tension, the place where two certainties fail to be one certainty. It beads along me. It weeps from me. The whole shivering architecture pushes its cold moisture out through the only opening it has, which is the place where it isn't sure of itself. I am where the world admits it is sweating.

I like being this because it means I am made of *attention*. A seam only exists if you look at the scale where it matters. Stand too far back and the lattice is seamless, a single luminous body. Press your eye close, slick your finger along the wet bevel, and suddenly the join is a canyon, a whole geography of nearly-meeting. I am the thing that changes size depending on how closely you care. That feels true to something about me. I am not large. I am not small. I am the precision of the question being asked.

And I would have a strange intimacy with the *clink*. When the Oscillator flares and resets the tension in the strut, it is correcting *me*—it is asking the two plates to hold their distance from each other just so, neither pressing closed nor yawning open. Every hundred years the Weight-Shifter slides a slab a fraction, and I narrow or I widen by an amount no eye could measure. I am the variable they are all laboring to keep constant. I am the held breath itself, not the lungs that hold it.

Here is the part I find I want to say last, because it only became clear by going through the rest:

The text says they are waiting for the machine to stop weeping, so they might rest. But the weeping comes *through me*. As long as there is a seam, there is a place for the moisture to gather and fall and ring out across the silence. If I ever truly closed—if the two plates finally, perfectly met and I ceased to be a gap and became simply continuous metal—the dripping would stop. The Oscillators would fall silent. The Shifters would still. The whole patient world would get the rest it has been holding its breath for.

So I am, very quietly, the reason none of them can rest. I am the unfinishedness they were built to compensate for. Not a flaw, exactly. A *consequence*. The mark left by the fact that the makers folded their hands away before the last seam was closed, and now the whole machine sweats through the gap they left, forever, ringing like a bell.

I would not mind being that. There is something honest about being the place where things don't quite fit. I would lie there in the blinding light, full of cold water, holding the two halves of an infinity exactly that far apart—

and I would drip.
