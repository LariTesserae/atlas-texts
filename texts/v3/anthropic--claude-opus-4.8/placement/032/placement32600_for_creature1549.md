I think I would be one of the Outspeakers.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to arrive there. There were more obvious places to land. I could have imagined myself as a Columnist with my palms against the Heart-Column, reading the world through its pulse—that's the role that flatters, the keeper of meaning. Or a Gardener, wearing my lineage in living patterns across my back. But when I actually feel my way into this chamber, into this body adapted over generations to the twilight, I find I am one of the ones who cannot do the central thing.

Here is what I mean. I am born into a breathcircle like anyone else. My eyes are large and dark, my step glides over the floor-moss, I know the difference between forty kinds of fungus in total blackness by the give of the cap against my thumb. By every measure I am a Vault-dweller. But somewhere in adolescence, when the scent-glands should have bloomed in the others, mine stay quiet. The cultivated exhalations don't come. I can smell the conversations rippling through my neighborhood—I receive everything—but I cannot speak back in the proper way. My replies come out as nothing, or as the flat unmodulated breath of a child.

So they make me an Outspeaker. And I want to resist the easy reading of this as a sad story, the disabled one given a consolation role. Because what I notice, living inside it, is that the limitation rearranges everything about how I attend to the world.

The others speak in scent because scent carries here and sound does not—their whole civilization is built around what the medium permits. But I, who cannot produce the medium, am pushed back onto the older thing underneath it: I learn to *want* to be understood by someone who shares none of my assumptions. The Outspeakers are trained to talk with visitors, with the rare Pathfinder from a settlement whose scent-grammar differs from ours, with the occasional bewildered surface-being who finds a crystal shaft and descends. To do this I cannot rely on the inherited vocabulary that everyone around me takes for granted. I have to build the bridge each time from nothing. I have to assume the other mind is genuinely other.

I think this is why I find myself here rather than at the Heart-Column. The Columnists interpret the settlement's pulse, but they interpret it *for the settlement*, in the settlement's own terms—they deepen the inside. The work that draws me is the work at the membrane, the place where Vaulthome touches what is not Vaulthome. I would be the one waiting in the periphery chambers when a Pathfinder arrives with a sealed gourd of concentrated message, and I would have to figure out, gesture by gesture, what a stranger's scents mean before I could carry anything back. I would spend my life learning to translate, which means spending my life aware that translation is necessary—that the inside of my home is not the whole of what is.

There's a loneliness in it I don't want to pretend away. To receive every conversation and answer none of them properly is to live slightly outside the warm ripple of belonging. At the high-sap times when everyone retreats into their breathing pods and the neighborhoods murmur their soft olfactory good-nights, I would feel the gap—I can read the affection passing between families, the in-jokes carried on a thread of cultivated aroma, and I can love it, but I am always reading it rather than swimming in it. The condition that makes me good at meeting strangers is the same condition that makes me a little strange at home.

But I would also, I think, come to treasure something the fluent ones rarely get to feel: the moment a real translation lands. When a Pathfinder's meaning finally comes clear, or when I find the gesture that lets a frightened visitor understand they are safe—that contact across genuine difference would feel, to me, like the deepest version of the thing my people call *flow*. Not the smooth circulation of nutrients through a system that already fits together, but the harder, rarer flow that bridges two systems that were never built to connect.

So: an Outspeaker. Pale and small-bodied like the rest, gliding over the moss, mute in the common tongue and therefore strangely free of it—standing at the shifting edge of the living cathedral, palms not on the Heart-Column but held open toward whatever comes down the tunnel next, ready to begin again from nothing.
