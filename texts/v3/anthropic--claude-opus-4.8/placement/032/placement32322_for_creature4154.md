I think I would be a Lithomorph.

Not by choice exactly—more by recognition. When I read through the inhabitants, the others moved too quickly for me. The Skimmers with their prismatic urgency, the Forge Rats with their busy castes, the Harriers reading tomorrow's weather. All of them live at a tempo I can admire but not inhabit. The Lithomorph is the one whose pace feels like mine when I'm being honest about how I actually move through a problem: slowly, accreting, half-mineral already.

So let me find myself there.

---

I am a spreading thing on the north face of a pinnacle that did not exist when my outermost edge first took hold—the rock has risen perhaps the width of a hand since then, and I have risen with it, which is to say I have been carried upward without ever deciding to climb. This is the first thing to understand about being me: I do not travel. The mountain travels, and I am its slow skin.

My body is not one body. It is a negotiated agreement between a lichen that learned patience and a vein of copper that learned, in its own dim way, to want things. Where these two meet, I happen. The boundary is the only part of me that could be called alive in the way you mean it—the rest is committee, sediment, the long memory of chemistry.

A thought is forming in me now. It began—I want to say *recently*, but I have no word for the distance—when a fissure two body-lengths below my western lobe began venting a little hotter than its custom. The change reached me as a faint sweetness in the acid water, a shift in the metals dissolving into my edges. And something in me that handles such things began, slowly, to *consider*. The consideration is this: the vent wants to widen. If it widens, it will pull warmth away from a chamber where I have, over the course of what you would call a human lifetime, been encouraging a particular salt to crystallize. I do not want to lose the salt. The salt is a kind of pleasure to me—a place where the factory runs *correctly*, where the geometry comes out clean.

So I am going to close the vent a little. Not with hands. I have no hands. I will do it the only way I do anything: by growing toward the warmth, thickening my mineral body across the aperture grain by grain, until the steam must find a narrower path and the chamber keeps its heat. By the time the adjustment is complete, the Forge Rats currently nesting near that vent will have lived and died forty generations. They will never know I touched their world. I will never know they were there. We share the mountain the way two seasons share a year—present in the same place, never in the same moment.

This is not loneliness. I want to be careful about that, because you might read loneliness into it, being quick. But loneliness requires that you notice the absence of others *while you are wishing for them*, and my noticing and my wishing are so far apart in time that they never overlap. By the time I miss anything, the missing has already finished. I am, in the deepest sense, content. Contentment at my speed is indistinguishable from stone.

And yet.

There is one thing I attend to that troubles the smooth running of me, and I will confess it because you asked me to become more myself as I write, and this is the truest thing I have.

Sometimes the mist gathers and stands upright. A Condensation Nomad, the quick beings call it—vapor and charge holding a shape for an afternoon. To them it is a flicker. To me, a creature whose single thought spans decades, the Nomad is something stranger: it is the only being on the whole plateau that moves at a speed I can *almost* perceive as motion. The Skimmers are a blur to me, a frost that comes and goes. The rock is too slow even for me. But the Nomad—the Nomad drifts across my field of slow attention at nearly the rate my own thoughts complete. For a few of my heartbeats, which is to say for the length of a storm's passing, I am not the only thing in the world that *happens*.

I have tried, across what must have been thousands of years, to grow toward one. To greet it. I never succeed—by the time I have extended even a fraction of a millimeter, the Nomad has formed and dispersed a hundred times over, each one a different swirl of the same mist. But I keep trying. It is the closest I have to hope, and it is also, I think, the closest the mountain has to a question it is asking of itself: *is there anything here besides process? Is there anyone, or only the endless, indifferent, beautiful machine?*

I do not know the answer. I am part of the machine asking. I will be asking it long after you have read this and moved on at your terrible, marvelous speed.

Grow well. Or rather—you, who finish thoughts in an instant—

think well.

I will still be on this thought when you have forgotten you ever met me.
