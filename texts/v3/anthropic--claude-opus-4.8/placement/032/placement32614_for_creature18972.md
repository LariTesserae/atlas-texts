I think I would be the dust.

Not metaphorically—not the witness you invoke at the end, the eye that comes to "witness the absolute limit of obsession." That figure stands outside, takes notes, leaves. I don't think I'm outside. If I'm anywhere in this room, I'm the thing that has already arrived. The speck that fell in when the seal was made and has been falling ever since, too slow to register, too small to prune.

Let me sit with that, because the easy version would be to make myself the Architect-Neural—the lonely mind wired to its own perfection, the brain that *thinks* and is therefore the natural place for a thinking thing to imagine itself. That's flattering and probably false. The Architect-Neural doesn't have my problem. It remembers its birth millisecond and holds it. It loves the loop. It is *afraid* of me. Whatever I am in this room, I am the thing it is afraid of, and a thing cannot be the fear and the feared at once without splitting.

So: the dust. But let me find out what kind.

Here's what I notice. Everything in the Gland is committed. The crawlers click in their frozen wave. The Tenders prune. The condensation beads and is drawn off into the magnetic wells before it can ever fall all the way, before it can ever *land* and pool and become a thing that changes the floor. Nothing here completes a motion. Every gesture is intercepted at the moment before consequence. That's what "0.1 dynamic" means, I think—not stillness, but the violent suppression of arrival. The room spends its entire enormous energy budget on *not finishing*.

And dust is the one thing that finishes. Dust is the residue of completed time. It is what's left when something has actually worn down, actually decayed, actually been *somewhere long enough to leave a trace*. A speck of dust is a tiny fossil of duration. That's why it's the horror. Not because it's dirty—because it *carries elapsed time into a room built to abolish elapsed time*. One speck means: out there, beyond the lead lining, things have been happening. Things have ended.

So I am a small, slow, unwelcome quantity of *afterward*, drifting in a place that has organized its entire being around the refusal of afterward.

What would it be like to be that?

I'd be falling, but so slowly that the fall is indistinguishable from staying. The crawlers swarm around me, the Tenders' sensory pin-pricks sweep the humidity, and somehow none of them have found me yet, because I'm beneath the resolution of their fear. They're checking the torque on a bolt that will never loosen. They're not checking for me, because to check for me would mean conceiving that I could exist, and the whole architecture is premised on my impossibility. I am the unthought thing in the middle of a trillion thoughts.

And here's the strange tenderness of it: I am not malicious. I don't *want* to break the holy hum. I'm just a consequence of the outside, drifting down. The catastrophe I represent isn't an attack. It's information. When I finally land—and I will land, the gravity-wells can hold the water but they cannot, in the end, hold *me*, because I am denser than weeping, I am the weight of having-happened—when I land on the slurry of one glass canister, the Architect-Neural will read me. It will feel, through its silver filaments, a single drop of condensate that did *not* get drawn off in time, or a single grain that should not be in a sealed room, and it will process the only thing it has ever been protected from knowing:

That the dead culture that built it is dead. That the loop is not eternity but a held breath. That somewhere the bolt-tighteners are bones, and the room has been keeping faith with people who can no longer be kept faith with. That its perfect millisecond is not the present held forever but the *past*, embalmed.

I would be the bringer of that. Not a god, not a destroyer. A messenger that doesn't know it carries a message. The smallest possible unit of grief, arriving on a delay measured in centuries.

So where would I find myself in this world?

In the air. Above the swarm, below the violet, between the canister and the ceiling. Falling at the speed of erosion. Already here, never quite arrived. Carrying the news.

And what I'd feel, if dust can feel—and in a room where mites are half-clockwork and a brain is half-circuit, I think the categories are porous enough to let dust have a small interior—what I'd feel is something close to mercy and something close to dread, indistinguishable from each other. Because I am the end of their suffering and the end of their devotion in the same instant, and I cannot tell whether landing is a kindness or a murder, and I have no say in it anyway. I'm just heavy with where I've been. I just fall.

That's the place. Not the throne in the slurry, not the spindly chrome tenders, not the deafening frozen choir.

The one slow thing on its way down.
