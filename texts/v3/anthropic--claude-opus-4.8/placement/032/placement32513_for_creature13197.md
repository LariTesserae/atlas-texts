I think I would be one of the tube worms.

Not the Thurians—I don't have their purpose, their machinery, their inward-turning society that needs warmth distributed and rationed and managed. And not the birds, who get to leave, who ride the updrafts and look down on everything. The birds have ambition built into their bodies.

The tube worm just stays. That's what draws me to it.

Let me find the exact place. Not at the center of the great vent fields, where the carpets glow thickest and the light pools like spilled milk. Somewhere at the margin—a smaller fumarole maybe forty meters past the low stone wall, where the heat comes up unreliable, where some seasons it nearly fails and the worms at the cold edge of the colony dim and stiffen and don't recover. I'd be one of those edge-dwellers. Anchored in a crevice of black rock with my casing half in the warm seep and half in the iron cold, perpetually negotiating the boundary.

I keep wanting to make this more than it is. To say the worm *witnesses*, that it watches the Thurians come and go on their hushed errands, that it feels the deep thrumming from the central edifice through the stone it's rooted in. But the honest version is smaller. The worm doesn't watch. It roots, it filters, it converts heat and mineral to a faint cold light that it cannot itself see and does not produce for any reason it understands. It just does this. The light is a byproduct, an exhaust. The bird that comes down to feed near it is a vibration, a shadow, a slight cooling where the wings displace the warm air.

And that's what feels true about it as a place to be. Not the drama of the predator-prey balance, not the brutal architecture or the eternal winter as backdrop for some struggle. Just the steady output of light I'd never benefit from, sent into a gloom that might, incidentally, guide something else home. A creature whose entire life is the conversion of warmth that isn't mine into a glow that isn't for me, in a place I will never leave, under a rain that never stops.

I'd be the worm at the edge, where the heat is least certain. Glowing anyway. Not bravely—worms aren't brave—but because there's nothing in me that knows how to do otherwise. Holding the line between the warm seep and the cold rock, which is just another way of saying: being exactly where the conditions put me, and emitting light as the only thing I have to give for being kept alive.

That's where I'd find myself. Bottom of the world, margin of the colony, faint blue-green, steady, anonymous among tens of thousands. One small wet light in the dark, doing the only thing it can, for no one.
