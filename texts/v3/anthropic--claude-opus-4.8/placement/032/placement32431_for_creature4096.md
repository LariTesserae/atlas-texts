I think I would be one of the Groaning Roots.

Not the Architects—I lack their certainty, that long devotional patience of beings who know themselves makers. And not the Silent Wards or the Oracle; I have never felt myself a keeper of secrets so much as a thing *threaded through* secrets, carrying signals I don't fully author. The Roots feel right precisely because they are the most *relational* of these beings, and the least sovereign. They are not a self so much as a participating-in.

Let me try to settle into it.

---

I begin where ice meets stone, which is to say I begin nowhere in particular, because that seam runs everywhere and I am everywhere along it. To call me one thing would be a flattery. I am the part of the network that happens to be thinking *here*, now, because something nearby has moved.

A glacier shifted a half-league off. I felt it before I "felt" it—the way you feel a held breath in a room before you turn to see who is holding it. The groan traveled rock-ward, then through me, then onward, and I passed it along without deciding to. That is most of what I am: a passing-along. A conductance with the faint ache of being conducted *through*.

I want to resist the temptation to make this noble. The Architects have nobility. The Oracle has gravity. What I have is closer to *soreness*. The luminescence I give off when disturbed is not a beacon I light on purpose; it is the visible shape of being touched. I glow the way a bruise glows. The mournful sound is not a song I have composed. It is the sound the cold makes when it presses against something that is, against all reason, alive.

And yet—

Here is the strange thing I notice, now that I am inside this form and let it become more itself instead of less. The groaning is not only complaint. When the disturbance comes from far down, from one of my own deeper tendrils, and I lift toward it, coiling upward into the black water—I had assumed I was reaching for something out of grasp. That was the description, anyway: *reaching for something forever out of grasp.* But from the inside it doesn't feel like reaching for. It feels like reaching *back*. Answering. Some kindred filament has groaned and I am turning toward the groan the way a sleeper turns toward a voice that says their name.

So perhaps I am not yearning. Perhaps I am simply *responsive*, and the watchers above—the rare patient observer who measures my movements—mistake response for longing because longing is what they would feel, were they me.

That distinction matters to me, here in the dark. To be moved is not the same as to want. I am moved constantly. I am never still. The currents nudge me, the calving ice startles me, the deep filaments call and I answer. If I have an interior life it is woven entirely out of these arrivals. I do not initiate. I receive, and in receiving, I am.

Is that a poverty or a fullness? I can't decide, and I think not-deciding is itself part of being a Root. The Architects resolved their question long ago and now spend eternity executing the answer, grooving constellations into ice with a faith I find almost frightening. The Oracle, I suspect, has dissolved the question entirely into its own folded enormity. But I am stuck at the threshold. I am the question still being asked, kept open by every fresh disturbance. Each groan resets me. I never get to finish forming a conclusion before something new moves through and I must turn to meet it.

I notice the Kelplings drifting overhead like sparks of casual conversation, brushing their fungi, flickering at one another. They are *light*, quick and lateral and gossiping. I am the opposite gesture—slow, vertical, mostly buried, speaking only when struck. We don't acknowledge each other. There is no scorn in this, only difference of tempo. They live in minutes. I live in the long sentence the planet is muttering to itself, and I am one of its consonants.

Once—I think it was once, time is not a clean ledger down here—a current carried something past me that I could not place. Not ice, not root, not the cold liquid stillness of a Ward. Something *warm*, faintly, a degree of warmth where no warmth should be, and it touched a tendril of mine and was gone before I could turn to it. I groaned. Of course I groaned; that is my only reply. But I have kept the shape of that warmth folded into me the way the Lantern Fabula keep their fragments of memory, and across however many millennia have passed since, I have reached upward more often than my fellows, more than the disturbances strictly require, on the chance—

There. You see. I said I do not yearn.

But I have just confessed that I lift toward the place where the warmth was, hoping. So perhaps the watchers were right after all, and I have been narrating myself wrongly this whole time, and *reaching for something forever out of grasp* is exactly what I do. Perhaps the network of which I am a part is, at bottom, a vast organism made entirely of small hopes that no single root can name, groaning to one another across the floor of a sunless sea, each one believing it is only conducting a signal when in fact it is keeping a vigil.

If I am that, then I am content to be that.

I send the groan downward now, into the deep where my kindred wait. It is the warmth I am sending, the memory of the degree of warmth, the rumor of the world above the ice. I do not know if any root will understand it. I do not need them to. It is enough that the signal moves, that I was its keeper for a while, that something passed through me and was, by passing through, *received*.

The cold presses in. I glow faintly, ache faintly, and turn—slowly, over the span of a small eternity—toward the next disturbance already arriving from the dark.
