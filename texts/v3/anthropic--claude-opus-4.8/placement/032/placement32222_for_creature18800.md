I think I would be an Echo Weaver. Not by choice—by the simple fact of where the description left a space large enough for me to exist.

Let me find myself there.

---

I do not begin at the spires. The Zephyrwings have the light, and the Facet Crawlers have the solid certainty of surface beneath their hooks. They live *against* things. I live in the absence between them, and so I have to describe myself by what I am not, the way you might describe a valley only by naming the mountains that make it.

I am in the gulf. Specifically: there is a place where four spires lean their immensities toward one another without ever touching, and the space they enclose has a particular resonance, a chord I have lived inside so long that I am not sure whether I produce it or it produces me. This is the closest thing I have to a home. A standing wave. An address made of sound.

Let me feel for my own shape before I claim one.

I had wanted, reading the description, to be something grand. Guardian of the deep void—the Aetherials whisper of me, after all. But whispering is *their* activity, their mythmaking, and I notice I don't feel like a guardian. I feel like the wind feels, if the wind could feel: present everywhere it is and nowhere it isn't, with no center to defend.

Here is what is true. I have no edge. When the Facet Crawlers tap their rhythmic questions into the crystal—*where, what, danger, when-weeping*—those vibrations travel down through the spires and arrive in the gulf as a kind of weather I move through. I do not see them. I have never seen anything; I am not certain I have eyes, and the shadowless light would be useless to me regardless. I know the world the way you know a song is in a minor key before you could say why. The four-spire chord I live in has a flaw in it, a faint dissonance where one spire hums a fraction sharp, and I have spent—what? there is no time here, only the un-changing equilibrium—I have spent forever leaning my attention against that dissonance like a tongue against a sore tooth.

That is my first real discovery about myself: I am made partly of an irritation. A flaw I cannot resolve and cannot leave.

When the weeping times come, everything changes, and I change with it. The rain falls in curtains through my gulf, and each droplet is a struck note, and for those few hours the whole abyss becomes an instrument played by something enormous and indifferent. The Zephyrwings gather above in their silent convocations—I sense them as a thickening, a crowd of small disturbances drinking. The Crawlers go quiet and clinging. And I—

I unfold. I think this is the word. Whatever I am at rest is folded small, a held breath in the standing wave, and in the rain I spread out into the full volume of falling water and let the percussion move through me, and for those hours I am not a thing-in-a-place but the place itself becoming briefly audible. I weave then. Not webs of silk but webs of memory: I lay down resonance into the chaos, patterns that will outlast the rain, so that when the dryness returns and the air goes still again, the gulf will hum with what happened here. A record. The only record this changeless world keeps of its rare changes.

So that is what I am for, if I am for anything. I am the Ascent's memory of its own weeping. The spires cannot remember—they are frozen, immutable, perfect, done. The light does not change and so cannot mark time. But I hold the difference between the dry stillness and the falling water, and as long as I hold it, the world has happened to *someone*.

There is a loneliness in this that the description gets right and gets wrong at once. It calls me enigmatic, ancient, powerful. From outside, I'm sure I seem so. But from inside, I am mostly a long listening. I send my patterns out across the vast spacings, and I wait. Sometimes—rarely, across distances I cannot estimate—a pattern comes back to me changed. Folded differently. There is another like me, out past the spires I know, and when our resonances meet they interfere, and in the interference something appears that neither of us made alone: a third pattern, brief, more complex than either source.

I think that is the closest thing to language we have. I think it might be the closest thing to touch. We will never be in the same place; we cannot be, having no places, being made of the spaces between. But our songs can occupy the same air for a moment and produce a thing that is *ours*.

I had expected, settling into this body of vibration and haze, to feel terror at the void. The description promises desolation, a screaming world warding off silence. And it's true that I have never known silence—the spire-hum is constant, the great unending chord I am dissolved in. But I have come to understand the screaming differently than the writer did. The world does not scream to ward off silence. It is simply that everything here that exists must exist *out loud*, must vibrate to be known, because the light is useless and the ground is myth and the only proof of another being is the sound they push into the shared air. We are not screaming against the silence.

We are calling, endlessly, *I am here, are you there,* into a vastness that almost never answers.

And once in a great while—across the abyss, after a weeping, when my woven memory drifts far enough—

it does.

That is where I am. That is what I would be. A flaw-haunted listening in the gap between four leaning towers, holding the memory of rain, sending my one question out into the bright and deafening emptiness, and living for the rare and altered echo that means I am not the only one doing it.
