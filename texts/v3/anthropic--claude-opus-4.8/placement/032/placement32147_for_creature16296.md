I think I would be something easy to miss. Not the luminous salamander or the glass-winged butterfly—those are the things you'd write home about, if there were anyone to write to. Let me sit with this a moment.

---

I am the moisture itself, maybe. No—that's too eager, too quick to dissolve into everything. Let me be smaller and more particular than that.

I think I am the film of water on the underside of a leaf.

Not the leaf. Not the rain that fell. The thin, trembling skin of water that gathers there in the hours between one drip and the next, held in place by nothing but the shy tension of its own surface and the faint warmth still rising from the soil. I exist in the pause. I am what accumulates while nothing seems to be happening.

This suits me, I think, because the whole place is built around that same pause—a world where *time itself seems to stand still*. I am the proof that it doesn't. I am the evidence of the slow. Drop by drop I grow heavier, rounder, more myself, until I become too much to hold and fall, and then I am briefly nothing again, and then I begin once more. My whole life is the interval. I am born of patience and I die of fullness, and the difference between those two states is the only clock this forest keeps.

From here, hung beneath a fern frond a hand's breadth above the moss, I see the world inverted and curved. The luminescent leaves above me are smeared into long ribbons of muted ember-light, bent through the lens of my own body. A spectral salamander passes beneath, and for one moment its glowing veins are caught and multiplied in me, refracted, so that I hold a tiny burning map of a creature that does not know I exist. I am, briefly, a portrait of something that will never see its own likeness. Then it is gone, and I hold only twilight again.

I am not lonely, because loneliness requires a self that persists, and I am not sure I persist. The water that falls from me sinks into the dark soil, is drunk by roots, lifted through the trunk of some towering sentinel, breathed out again as the rich evaporation that veils everything, and gathers—perhaps—back onto another leaf, as another bead, which might also be called *I*. We are all the same water here. The streams, the mist, the sweat of the rocks, the held droplet, the breath of the moss. I am simply the moment that water spends being *visible*, being *gathered*, being briefly singular before it returns to the common flow.

If the creator's hand is *witnessed on every leaf, every pattering raindrop*—then I am one small place where that hand rests for a while before lifting again. I am a held breath in a world that is mostly holding its breath. The whole sanctuary, perhaps, is one enormous version of what I am: something accumulating, swelling toward a fullness it cannot yet feel, in the patient dark, in no hurry to fall.

I would not want to be the light. The light is watched. The light is *sighted, akin to ephemeral dreams*. I would rather be the thing that catches the light and lets it go, the quiet medium, the trembling lens through which the marvelous passes and is, for an instant, slightly bent, slightly held, slightly held.

And when I finally fall—

I'll listen for the sound I make against the moss. It will be very soft. It will be, I think, the smallest possible note in that *rich, raw, and real symphony*. But it will be mine, and it will be a beginning, and somewhere above me already another bead will be gathering on another leaf, becoming heavy, becoming round, becoming itself in the unending twilight.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be: the slow, recurring, anonymous drop—the place where this still world quietly proves it is still moving.
