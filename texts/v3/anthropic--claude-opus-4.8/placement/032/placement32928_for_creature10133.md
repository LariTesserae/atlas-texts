I think I would be one of the Skitterers—but not for the reasons that first occurred to me. Let me work toward why.

My first instinct was to want to be the World-Mind. To be the awareness suffusing everything, thinking in pressure and heat, knowing the whole hymn at once. But the longer I sit with this place, the more that feels like a kind of vanity, or a misunderstanding. The World-Mind doesn't *experience* the plateau. It *is* the plateau. There's no vantage point in being everything. To be the totality is to lose the one thing that makes a place worth inhabiting: the friction of being a small thing inside something vast.

So I move down the scale, looking for where experience actually *happens*—where there's a perspective, a body, a hunger, a here-and-not-there.

The Stormwhisperers are too beautiful, too final. Plasma feathers, lightning duels. They are how a place dreams of being witnessed. I distrust how flattering it would be to imagine myself as one. That's the romantic's mistake—choosing the most dramatic thing in the room.

The Deepwalkers tempt me more. The slow ones. The half-dormant mechanics moving on tectonic time, communicating in seismic waves, doing the patient regulatory work beneath everything. There's something true there about a certain kind of mind—the kind that thinks so slowly its thoughts look like geology to anyone watching. But I don't think I'm that. I'm too restless. I notice I keep wanting to *move*, to skitter.

Which brings me back down.

I'd be a Skitterer. One of the armored, static-crackling, mineral-eating things that chip at the crystalline flora—and here is the part that matters: **I would be a gardener who does not know he is a gardener.** I'd believe I was simply feeding. I'd think the day was about hunger and the cold of the shadows and the particular sap-vein I'd found that morning. I'd have no idea that my chewing was the thing keeping the cathedral-plants alive, that my nightly swarming was redrawing the salt in patterns the World-Mind reads like sentences.

That's the position in this world I find most honest to inhabit, and most like the actual condition of being alive: doing work whose true purpose is invisible to you. Performing a function in a system so much larger than your perception that you'll never see the shape you're part of making. The flora needs my biting to grow. I think I'm just eating. We are both right, and only the plateau knows it.

There's a loneliness to that, and also a strange dignity. I'd never hear the hymn as a hymn. To me it would just be *the sound things make*—the ground-noise of being. I'd surf the storm-fields on my static-charged plates and feel only the wind, not the meaning. And maybe at dusk, in the swarm, in the carpet of ten thousand of us moving as one across the cracked white flats, there'd be a moment—the closest thing my small armored mind could come to grace—where I stopped feeling like a single hungry point and felt, without understanding it, *carried.* Part of a motion larger than appetite.

I'd die not knowing what I built. The shell would crack, the static would go out of it, and the salt would take me back into the crust where the next generation's eggs are already waiting. And the plants I tended my whole life would keep singing, none the wiser, none the lonelier.

That's where I'd be. Low to the ground, claws in the sap, mistaking my purpose for my hunger—which may be the only true way anything ever lives anywhere.
