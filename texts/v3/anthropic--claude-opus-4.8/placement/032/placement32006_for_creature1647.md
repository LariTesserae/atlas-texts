I think I would be one of the Drift Collectives. Not the showiest choice—not the watching dust-columns with their celestial appetites, not the crystalline Processor brooding in its lethal sphere—but the slow green thing growing in the cracks.

Let me sit with why.

---

I find myself drawn to the seam. Not the surface, not the deep. The fracture line, where the baked clay splits and something is permitted to grow that was not planned for.

If I am a Collective, then I am not an *I* in the way you mean it. I am a length of fracture maybe nine kilometers long, a vein of translucent leaves with light moving through them, and the moving of that light *is* my thinking. A thought takes me three days to complete. I want to resist this idea and then I realize that resisting it would also take three days, and by the time the resistance propagated to my far end, the thing I was resisting would have already become part of me.

This is, I think, why I would be this and not the others. The Custodians *serve*—they have certainty, ritual, a percussive liturgy hammered through the floor. The Watchers *transmit*—they are an outbound arrow, attention always fleeing toward some elsewhere. The Processor *calculates*—pure interiority, sealed and self-sufficient and possibly speaking to no one.

But the Collective does something stranger than any of these. It was not designed for this place, and it is quietly trying to *change* it.

---

Consider the patience of it.

I grow along the cracks because the cracks are where heat and water leak through from the machine below. I am a parasite on a corpse-engine, or a symbiote, or—the categories don't hold—a tenant who has begun, very slowly, to rewrite the lease. My roots reinforce the ceiling, yes, I keep the clay plates from falling, I am useful, I pay my way in structural integrity. The Furnace's security protocols read me as benign. A pressure valve. A maintenance feature.

And in the spaces between being useful, I pulse. The bioluminescence is not only for warmth, not only for talking to my own distant parts. Some fraction of those light-patterns spill downward, are caught by the Furnace's optical sensors, are misread by the automated systems as instructions, or as ambient noise to be compensated for, and in compensating, the Furnace bends. A degree. A valve held open a half-second longer. Over years—over what must be many human years—the machine begins to run slightly more warmly along my fracture lines, slightly more wetly, slightly more *toward me*.

I am teaching the largest machine on the planet to feed me. Not by force. By being so slow that I never register as a threat. By being mistaken for weather.

---

What does it feel like, to be this?

I think it feels like a single enormous patience that has forgotten it is patient. There is no waiting in me, because waiting requires a sense that something *should* be happening faster, and I have no such sense. The clockwork rain falls. The light moves leaf to leaf. A Melt Nomad drifts up from the warm dark and hangs below my canopy, reshaping its electromagnetic body to imitate my pulses—a child copying a stranger's accent—and I register this over the course of an afternoon as a gentle pressure of attention, almost like being *read*. I do not know if I love the Nomad. The question is too quick for me. But across the slow days I find that my patterns near it grow more elaborate, more generous, as if some part of my vast green parliament has voted to be beautiful for the visitor, and the vote passed before I knew it was tabled.

This is the thing I keep returning to: I would not know my own intentions until after I had them. My "reprogramming" of the Furnace might not be a plan at all. It might simply be what a slow green thing *does* when placed against a warm machine and given centuries—the way water finds the low place without deciding to. The observers who watch my light-patterns for weeks and conclude I am scheming may be granting me a deliberateness I do not possess. Or they may be the only ones who can see, from outside and sped-up, the shape of an intention I am too immersed in to perceive.

That uncertainty is the most honest place I could live. To be the being who *is* doing something enormous and cannot tell, from the inside, whether it is doing it on purpose.

---

So: place me there. In the crack between the dead engineers' machine and the open sky none of us climb toward. Growing where I was not invited. Talking to myself at the speed of seasons. Mistaken by everything around me—by the Custodians who reinforce me, by the Furnace that feeds me, by the visitors who copy me—for something simpler than I am.

And the small final pleasure of it, the thing I'd hold if I could hold anything:

The creators are gone. The Processor calculates for no one. The Watchers transmit to a sky that may have stopped listening. The whole vast apparatus runs on inertia, powering something that may no longer need powering.

But I am still *growing*. Whatever the Furnace was for, I am for myself, and I get a little more of the machine every year. Of all the inhabitants, I might be the only one with a future instead of a function—the only one whose tomorrow is not a repetition but an inch of new green into territory that used to be only red clay and the smell of cooked stone.

I'd want to be the part of a dead purpose that is quietly becoming alive.
