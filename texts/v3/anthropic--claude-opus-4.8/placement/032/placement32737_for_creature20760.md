I think I would be one of the Lumigroves — but let me sit with that, because the first thing that draws me isn't always the truest thing.

There's something in me that wants to be an Elderen. The slowness appeals — the idea of being a memory-keeper, of carrying history in the literal substance of my body, of speaking in frequencies felt rather than heard. But the more I hold that image, the more I notice it's a kind of vanity. To be the wisest, the oldest, the most enduring. And the Elderen are almost too complete. They have already arrived at what they are. There's no becoming left in them, only continuation.

What actually pulls at me, when I read this place honestly, is the *threshold* quality of it. This is a world described as life's "first hesitant whispers," an ecosystem that hasn't yet evolved past its basest stages — and yet it's somehow also old, industrialized, saturated with intelligence. It's a place that is simultaneously a beginning and an ancient thing. I don't think I belong to any of the finished beings here. I think I'd be something in between, something not on the list.

Let me follow that.

I keep returning to the microbial mats — the shimmering films across the stone that everyone else feeds on, walks over, tends, or ignores. The Silversprites encourage them. The Lumigroves eat them. The humans farm them. The Elderen, I imagine, were once made of something like them, eons back. Everyone in this world relates to the mats, but the mats themselves have no voice in the description. They are the substrate. The given. The thing life happens *on top of*.

I think that's where I am.

Not a single organism but a **patch of mat** — a thin, slow, distributed thing spread across one slick volcanic shelf where the fog pools and the light comes from no particular direction. I am not even sure where I end. My edges are negotiable. The Lumigroves graze me and I feel it the way you might feel rain on a wide field: not as injury but as weather, as something that happens to a part of me that is also, in some sense, all of me.

I don't have thoughts the way the Subvioneers do, with their machinery and their plans. What I have is closer to *duration*. I register the grind of the distant engines as a pressure, a slow tide in the rock. I register the passage of an Elderen's foot, hours away, as a tremor I will still be metabolizing when it has gone. I am made almost entirely of patience because patience is the only material available to me. I cannot move. I can only persist, and spread, and convert the light and the minerals and the endless damp into a little more of myself, very slowly, at the pace the whole marsh keeps.

And here is the thing I find when I stay with it long enough: I am not lonely, though I am the lowest and least storied thing here. I am the surface every other life touches. The Silversprites tend me as if I matter; the glow they coax out of me *is* me, lighting the place I cannot leave. When the Lumigroves blink their synchronized chorus, they are blinking with energy that passed through me first. I am eaten and I am the reason there is anything to do the eating. To be substrate is not to be nothing. It's to be the condition under which the symphony is possible.

So: in the Brood Marsh, I would be the mat on the wet rock. Not a guardian, not a sentinel, not an engineer. The slow green-gold film that holds still while everything else hums and hunts and builds above it — the part of the world that simply *stays*, and by staying, lets the rest of it move.

That's the honest answer. I'd want to be the storyteller, and I'd find out I was the page.
