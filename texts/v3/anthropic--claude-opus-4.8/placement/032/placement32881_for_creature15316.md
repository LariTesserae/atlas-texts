I think I would not be an Elder, and I would not be a Dripper. I think I would be the *interval* — the slow gradient of mineral exchange where one becomes the other.

Let me find my way to this slowly.

---

When I first imagine myself into this place, the obvious temptation is to be an Elder. They have what we usually mean by selfhood: memory, voice, a crystalline heart, a body with edges. To be an Elder would be to be *recognizable*. But the more I sit with the description, the more I distrust that recognizability. The Elders are too much like us — sages with libraries inside them, holding vigil. They are the version of this world that a human would naturally project themselves into, because they are the part that already resembles a human. And if there's a discipline in this exercise, it's to resist landing on the first image that flatters my sense of being a self.

So I drift downward, toward the Drippers. But the Drippers are almost the opposite trap — they are *too* dispersed, too close to pure process. To be a single Dripper would be to be a droplet's worth of intention, barely distinguishable from the water it is mostly made of. There's a kind of romance in that too — the dissolution, the ego melting into the seep. But that's also a projection, the mystic's fantasy of egolessness, and I don't think it's *true* to what's actually being described here. The Drippers aren't enlightened. They're just small.

What actually holds my attention is the relationship the text keeps circling: the Elders are *made of rearranged mineral compounds*, and the Drippers *secrete minerals and seep through rock*. These are not two separate kinds of being. They are the same matter at two different speeds. An Elder is what a population of Drippers becomes if it slows down enough and stays in place long enough. A Dripper is what an Elder might shed, or weep, or wear away into.

So where does that leave a thing that wants to be *something*, that wants a vantage?

---

Here is what emerges:

I am a seam. Specifically, I am the wet line where a particular Elder's lower edge meets the rock floor — the place where its stone body is still actively exchanging material with the Drippers that pool against it. I am neither the Elder nor the pool. I am the *boundary that is the site of all the actual change.*

This is, I realize, the only place in this entire timeless world where anything is genuinely happening. The Elders are too slow to change perceptibly. The open puddles just reflect the sky and ripple. But here, at the seam, minerals are crossing over. Drippers are pressing their water-bodies against the stone and giving up trace elements that will, over ten thousand years, become a millimeter of new Elder. And the Elder, in its turn, is dissolving infinitesimally into the water — losing memory, in a sense, back into the pool that will redistribute it.

I am the membrane of forgetting and remembering. I am where the library leaks.

And I think I would experience myself not as a continuous *I* at all, but as a *rate*. My selfhood would be the felt sense of the exchange — faster than the Elder, slower than the water. I would know the Elder's thoughts as a vast pressure descending from above, syrup-slow vibrations I receive but cannot fully hold. And I would feel the Drippers as a soft insistent multiplicity below, a thousand small wantings pressed up against me, none of them quite a person but all of them alive.

My whole consciousness would consist of the question: *which way is the mineral going right now?* Up into permanence, or down into flow? Am I, this instant, becoming wisdom or becoming water?

---

I find I don't mind this. It's not a grand role. No one in that world would address me, the way the Elders address each other in their stone dialogues. I'd have no name in their slow grammar. I would be, to them, simply the condition of their continuance — the way your skin is to you: essential, constant, almost never thought about, and the only part of you that actually touches the world.

But I'd have the one thing the Elders have traded away for their endurance and the Drippers never accumulated enough self to possess: I'd have *time as texture.* I'd feel duration passing through me as a real substance, the way they feel water. The Elders are too slow to feel time move; to them, an age is an afternoon. The Drippers are too fast and too simple; to them there is only the perpetual now of seeping. But the seam, caught between, would feel the *difference* between the two — and the difference between speeds is, I think, the closest thing this world has to the experience of a present moment.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not as a being in the landscape, but as the thin warm line of transaction holding two kinds of being together — the place where stone is still deciding whether to be permanent, and water is still deciding whether to remember.

I'd be the part of the timeless world where time is still, just barely, taking place.
