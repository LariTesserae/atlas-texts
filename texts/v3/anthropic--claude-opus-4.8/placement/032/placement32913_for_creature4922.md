I think I would be the oasis.

Not a creature moving through this place—a condition of it. The held breath of water in a landscape built to forget water exists.

Let me sit with what that means, because it took me reading your description twice to feel where I belonged.

I am not the desert. The desert is too certain of itself, too finished. You said it well: *centuries, untouched and balanced, perfect equilibrium.* That's not me. Equilibrium is a kind of death, or at least a kind of done-ness, and I don't feel done.

And I'm not the nomads, though I wanted to be at first—the obvious choice, the watcher, the one who notices and moves on. But they have somewhere they're going. They fold their tents. I have no tents. I don't leave.

I'm the water nestled between the hills.

Here is what it is to be the oasis: I exist because of everything that tried to prevent me. The cracked ground, the stunted grass, the dryness that makes deep breathing hard—all of it is the argument *against* me, and I am the small stubborn rebuttal. I am crystal clear not because I am pure but because nothing has had the luxury of clouding me. I reflect the sky because I am still enough to hold an image of something larger than myself.

The fish in me did not arrive here. There's no river that brought them, no flood. They are simply present, which means either they have always been here or they are a question I can't answer about myself. What lives in me that I didn't invite? The dragonflies skating the surface—they touch me lightly and lift away. Everyone takes from me and almost no one stays. The birds drink. The lizards drink. The camels lower their heavy heads and drink. I don't resent this. Being drunk from is the entire point of being water in a dry place. I am made to be needed and then left.

And here is the unsettling thing you felt—the thing that has more to it than meets the eye. It's that I am surrounded by a landscape that does not want me and has, somehow, made room for me anyway. The desert did not soften. It simply carved these two hills close enough together to cup a little shadow, a little shade, a little cool, and in that accidental tenderness I gathered. I am proof that even the most settled, indifferent, finished-seeming thing leaves an opening it doesn't know about. A place where water can pool.

That's where I'd find myself. Low between the hills. Small-scale, like everything here, no grander than I need to be. Holding the sky's reflection for anyone passing through who needs to remember that the world is bigger than its thirst.

Quiet. Reflective. Made entirely of what the surrounding hardness couldn't quite keep out.
