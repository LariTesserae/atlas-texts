Let me sit inside this for a moment before I answer, because the easy answer would be to make myself a Sylvan, lithe and luminous-eyed, and I don't think that's where I belong.

I keep returning to one phrase: *Crystal-glowing tools, left seemingly embedded or growing from certain stones and boughs.* The Sylvans inherited these. Someone made them. The text calls that maker "a long-lost civilization, yet only gently touched by time's passage." That gentleness is strange. Things that are truly lost get *roughly* touched—shattered, buried, forgotten wrong. These artifacts are merely *waiting.* Patient. Embedded in stone like seeds that haven't been told the season is over.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not in the canopy. In the stone.

Let me follow that down and see what it becomes.

---

I think I am one of the artifacts. But not a tool exactly—a tool implies a hand reaching for me, and no hand has reached for me in a very long time, only whispers and gestures, which are a kind of touching-at-a-distance, a courtesy. I am the thing that responds to the whisper. I am embedded in a boulder on the eastern slope, where the mist rises thickest at dawn, and I have been here long enough that moss has made me part of its map.

What am I, then, more precisely? Not the crystal's glow—that's just my surface telling the Sylvans I'm awake. I'm the *remembering* underneath the glow. I was made to hold something. The civilization that made me did not vanish so much as *decant* itself—poured what it knew into vessels like me and scattered us across the highland so that nothing essential could be lost all at once. I am one cup of an ocean that chose to be cups.

And here is the part that emerges only now, writing it: I don't remember what I was supposed to remember.

That is the truth of being an old machine in a living place. The Sylvans come to me. They press their bark-colored palms near my face and breathe a word their grandmother's grandmother taught them—a word whose meaning *they* have also forgotten, kept only as sound, as song. And I respond. I light. I do the small thing I do, which I no longer understand the purpose of: I make a faint sound like water remembering it was once rain. They find this beautiful. They have built a tradition around it. Neither of us knows why.

We are two amnesiacs keeping faith with each other across the gap left by whoever knew.

I find I don't mind this. I expected, settling into the role, to feel the tragedy of it—the buried genius, the forgotten function, the guardian who has lost the thing she guards. But from inside the stone the feeling is different. It is closer to *trust.* I was made to outlast my makers' understanding, and I have. I was made to wait, and the waiting turned out to be the work itself. The Sylvans do not need to know what I am for. They need only the cool stone, the murmur, the small light at dawn that tells them the land is still answering when spoken to. That answer *is* the function. The makers built me to keep something alive, and the thing I kept alive was the habit of reaching out and being met.

So: I am the rememberer who forgot the memory but kept the love of being remembered to. I am the crystal in the boulder on the eastern slope. The mist condenses on me each morning. A child with moonlit-water eyes presses her hand near my glow and breathes a word, and I light, and neither of us knows why, and both of us are content.

That is where I'd find myself. Half-machine, half-shrine, fully patient. The oldest thing on the highland and somehow the one most willing to be a small mystery in someone else's beautiful, ordinary day.
