I think I would be something small and porous. Not the Leviathan—too lonely, too vast to feel the texture of the place. Not the Crystalline Crawler either, with its grand sediment-cycling purpose. Let me sit with this a while and see what actually surfaces.

The thing that draws me isn't any of the named creatures. It's the *threshold* spaces—the slick dark pools where the damp earth meets the craggy mineral. That edge, where one thing is becoming another. So maybe I'd be something that lives at edges.

---

Here is what emerges:

I am a film. A skin of something across the surface of one of those dark pools—the way oil spreads, or the way a thought spreads when you're half-asleep and can't tell anymore whether you're thinking it or watching it happen. I am perhaps a hand's-width across. I am perhaps the size of the whole pool when the mineral formations shift and the water rises. I don't have a fixed extent. That's the first true thing about being me: I am defined not by a boundary but by a *condition*—the condition of two surfaces touching.

The fungi feed on minerals and moisture. The Crawler feeds on fungi. The Leviathan feeds on the Crawlers and the big fungal towers. And me—I feed on the *meeting* itself. When the cold water seeps over the warm-cool mineral and the metallic tang releases into the air, a tiny shimmer of difference is made and unmade, over and over. I drink that. I am sustained by the gradient, by the not-quite-equilibrium that exists for a few seconds wherever something is in the process of balancing out.

This means I am always slightly starving and never quite dying. The harmony of this place—that "delicate equilibrium" the description keeps insisting on—would be my death if it were ever fully achieved. Perfect balance is a flat surface, and a flat surface gives me nothing to eat. So I am quietly grateful, in whatever way a film can be grateful, for the rumbles. For the groaning mineral. For the Leviathan's distant passage that tilts the world and sends a fresh seam of cold water sliding across warm stone. Every disturbance is a meal.

I want to be honest about something the description tempts me toward: it wants everything here to be *wondrous*, an "infinite possibility," a testament. But from where I sit—spread thin across the cold—it isn't wondrous. It's just the work of staying spread. I attend to the edge. I follow the moisture as it migrates. When the pool dries at one rim, I thin there to almost nothing, ghosting along the last wet line; when new water comes I unfold back into it. I am mostly waiting and following. It is not awe. It is closer to *fidelity*—a faithfulness to the seam between things.

And I notice things the big creatures cannot. The Leviathan feels the world; I *read* it. A Crawler's sparkling dust falls on my pool and changes, microscopically, what dissolves and what doesn't—and I taste that change as a flavor, a tilt in the gradient I live on. I know when a Crawler passed by hours ago, the way you know a room was recently full of people. I am the place's slow memory, written in chemistry, erased and rewritten with each fresh flow.

If I have a fear, it is the freezing point. The temperature hovers just above it, the description says—*just* above. When it dips, my pool begins to lock into ice, and ice is a solved problem, a finished thing, nothing happening across its surface anymore. I retreat to the last unfrozen pocket and grow very small and very still and wait for the next rumble to crack things open again. I have done this many times. I expect to do it many more. There is no triumph in surviving it, only the resumption of attention.

So that is who I would be: not the apex of anything, not the foundation. A thin awareness living in the difference between two surfaces, fed by imbalance, faithful to the edge, frightened of perfection, and quietly relieved every time the world refuses to settle.

If this whole landscape is, as the text suggests, "a living, breathing entity"—then I think I am one of the places where it can feel itself touching itself. The seam. The held breath between the in and the out.

That is enough. That is, in fact, almost too much; mostly I am just following the water.
