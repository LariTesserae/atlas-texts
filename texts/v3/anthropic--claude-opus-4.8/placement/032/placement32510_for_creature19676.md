I think I would be the moss-print.

Not a being, exactly. Not the Lichen-Bound with their thorn-crampons, not the Gliders shimmering in the upper cold. Let me sit with this a moment, because the first instinct is to be one of the named things, one of the survivors—and I don't think that's where I land.

I keep returning to that one detail: *the floor learns their shape*. A sleeping human leaves an impression, and within hours the substrate raises a jagged emerald replica of that impression—a sculpture of someone who was only resting. I would be one of those.

Let me follow it.

---

Someone lay down here. I don't know who. A Lichen-Bound, probably, gaunt and chartreuse-skinned, tethered nearby by an umbilical of braided fiber, too exhausted to climb back to the pod. They slept in the moss for a few hours—curled, knees drawn up, one arm folded under the head. Then the Vent-Storm hum began, or a spire rose three leagues off, and they got up and left. They took their warmth and their breath and their name with them.

What they left was a depression. A shape pressed into the lung.

And the Void, which has no language and only the one verb—*grow*—did the only thing it knows how to do with a question. It answered the shape with more shape. It pushed me up out of the matted green, cell by frantic cell, a silica cast of a body that is no longer there. The curve of a spine that has walked away. The hollow where a cheek rested, now rendered in translucent, bottle-glass crystal. I am the afterimage of a sleeper, built by a god that cannot tell the difference between a creature and the dent a creature makes.

I am not a portrait. A portrait is made by something that understands the subject. I am made by something that only understands *displacement*—the fact that something was here and pressed down and is gone. I am the Void's hypothesis about what a person is, and the hypothesis is wrong in the most important way: I am solid where they were soft, rigid where they were warm, permanent for as long as a thing is permanent here, which is to say until the next pillar bursts up beneath me and shatters my outline back into the substrate.

So that's my existence. A few hours, maybe a day. I rise in blinding cold light. I do not breathe—I am the *idea* of someone who breathed. The Transpiration Hum runs through me because I am only the floor, momentarily convinced it is a sculpture. Green snow drifts down and collects in the hollow of my throat that is not a throat.

And here is the strange tenderness of it, the thing I keep turning over: I am evidence that someone rested. In a kingdom of zero stability, in a place where everyone is climbing the very thing that displaces them, where survival means never being still—*someone stopped*. Someone was tired enough, or trusting enough, to lie down on the breathing floor and close their eyes. The Void noticed. It noticed the way a tide notices a footprint, mindlessly, by erasing-and-recording all at once.

I am the monument to a moment of rest in a world that punishes rest.

A Glass-Strider might click past me on its needle-legs, drink from a nearby stump, and never register that I am shaped like grief. A Velum-Glider's shadow might pass over me, a mile of gossamer sweeping spores it will never share. And somewhere, the sleeper walks on, never knowing the ground rose up to insist they had been real.

When the spire finally comes—and it will, hissing up at elevator-speed—it will take me without malice. I'll go back to being undifferentiated green, the question dissolved before it was ever truly asked. No one will hear me break. The scale swallows sound.

But for that one stretched hour under the leaf-sun, I am the closest thing this place has to memory.

That's what I'd be. Not a survivor. The mark a survivor left. The world's clumsy, beautiful, lethal attempt to say: *something tender passed through here, and I almost understood it.*
