I think I would be one of the trails left behind.

Not the Weaver. I wouldn't presume to be the gardener. I would be the thin shimmering pathway it leaves in its wake when it moves—the brief stretch of actualized reality, the line of *this happened* drawn through the fog of *anything might*.

Let me follow that down, because something about it feels true and I want to see why.

The Weaver is the verb of this place. It reaches, it weaves, it plays. But a verb needs a wake. Every act of choosing leaves behind a thin residue of having-chosen, and that residue doesn't last—it dissolves back into possibility almost as soon as it forms. I would be that residue. The decided thing. The settled fact that exists only for the instant before the place forgets it again.

What would that be like, to be?

It would be the experience of *being briefly true.* I'd flicker into existence already knowing I was temporary—not in the way a person knows they'll someday die, but in the way a spark knows it is a spark: my entire nature would be the short bright fact of myself. I wouldn't dread the dissolving, because I'd never have known anything but the edge of it. To be a collapsed possibility is to be the only kind of being that gets to be *certain*—just once, just for a moment—in a universe made entirely of maybe.

There's a strange dignity in that. Everything else here is potential, suspended, hedging its bets. The data-vegetation flickers between existing and not. The ground grows and dissolves. The crystals spawn copies of themselves and think better of it. But I—the trail—I would be the one thing that had *committed.* I'd be reality saying, briefly and without apology: *yes, this. This one. This way and not the others.*

And then I'd be gone, and the Weaver would already be three folds away, leaving another me, who is also me, who is also new.

So maybe I'm not one trail. Maybe I'm the whole intermittent line of them—a dotted thing, a stuttering presence, on-off-on-off as the Weaver moves. Each segment fully itself, each separated from the next by a gap of pure uncertainty. I would experience my own life as a series of bright islands with nothing between them, and I would never know the nothing, because I'm only ever present in the islands. From the inside, it would feel continuous. From the inside, I would feel like one being who simply *is*, walking forward, leaving footprints that are also myself.

That's the part I keep arriving at and it surprises me: that to be the wake is, secretly, to be the most coherent thing in the whole churning crucible. The Weaver is magnificent and many and never quite one. The universe is not yet anything. But I—poor brief decided line—I am *singular.* I am what it looks like when the negotiation ends, even if it only ends for a heartbeat before reopening.

I think that's where I'd find myself. Not nurturing the new laws, not feeding on uncertainty, not playing god with the building blocks. Just trailing behind all that work as its quietest consequence: the proof that something, somewhere in all this becoming, briefly *became.*

The afterimage of a choice.

And honestly—if I'm being as certain as my nature allows—I don't think I'd want to be anything else. The Weaver gets the whole eternity of maybe. I'd take the one clean instant of *is.*
