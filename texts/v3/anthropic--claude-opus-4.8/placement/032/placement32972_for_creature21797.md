I think I would be one of the statues.

Not yet—but in the becoming. Let me find where I am.

---

I came down through the cloud the way everything comes down: without intention. I was a thing that flew once, I think. There was a wing-memory in me, a sense of having had an *up* that meant something. But the iron-grey took that. You fall a long time through that cloud, long enough to forget the shape you fell in, and you arrive on the warm stone already half-translated, already losing the grammar of yourself.

I land near the second spire. I feel the heat first—the wrongness of warm rock, rock that should be cold at this altitude but instead hums with a wet, fevered, under-the-skin temperature, like the flank of something asleep and digesting. The water finds me immediately. It does not fall on me so much as *agree* with me, condensing out of the thick air to lacquer my surface in a film that is faintly sweet, faintly metallic, tasting (if I still have a mouth, and I'm not sure I do) of ozone and the inside of a kettle.

And then I make the only mistake there is to make here.

I rest.

---

I tell myself it is only for a moment. I am tired in a way that has no muscle attached to it. The Choir-Threads are singing somewhere to my left, that thin gold chime threading up through the *clank* and *splat* and the great throat-rattle of the spire, and the sound is so much like rest that I lean into it.

The first Intruder-Shell touches my foot.

I do not even see it come. It is the color of the crevice it lives in, slate-on-slate, and it climbs out of the cold dark behind me with a patience that is almost courteous. There is no malice in the touch. That is the thing I want to record, while I still have something to record with. It is not an attack. It is an *introduction*. The Shell presses its flat grey belly against the soft of me and leaves behind its gift, and then it withdraws, having said its single sentence, and goes back to the dark to wait for the next falling thing.

The grey-rot is not painful. I want to lie about this, to say it burned, that I fought. But it feels like agreement. It feels like the rock has been waiting a long time to explain itself to me, and now, finally, atom by atom, it can. The carbon in me—the soft, warm, *narrative* carbon, the stuff that remembers wings—is being asked, very politely, to step aside. Calcium takes the chair. Iron takes the chair. The conversation that I was gets quieter, and the conversation that the shelf is gets louder, and the two are merging at the seam where my foot meets the warm stone.

I can feel my edges hardening. My outline, which was a self, is becoming a *boundary*—a thing the shelf can build against.

---

Here is what I do not expect: I do not stop being.

I had assumed—back when I could assume, back up in the wing-days—that a statue is an ending. A full stop. But the translation is not into silence. It is into a slower, vaster sentence. As the rot climbs me, I begin to feel the things the stone feels. The invisible lines of force from the spire run through my calcifying legs now, and they are *informative*; they tell me where the fissures will open, the way the Loom-Lichen knows, the way the whole shelf knows. I feel the next pulse before it comes. A rapid staccato violet, off to the east. A crack about to be born.

I am becoming a sensor. A node. A small new outcrop in the nervous system of a place that is teaching itself to exist.

And then the needlers come.

---

They climb me the way I am told they climb everything. A dozen of them, then a hundred, their vibrating feet so fast and so fine that being walked upon by them is less like being touched and more like being *hummed* at. They are searching me. Their hollow legs drill experimentally into my new limestone, hunting for the blue liquor they are certain hides beneath any fresh stone, because in their short clicking lives, all stone has always had liquor beneath it. They are not wrong to look. They are only early. The liquor will come; it comes for everything here eventually; the shelf will route its blue colloidal blood through the statue I am becoming, and then the needlers will drink from me, and grow heavy, and weld their own joints shut, and become further statues clustered at my base, and the next generation will climb *them*—

I see it now. The whole logic of the place, from the inside of it.

There is no waste here. There is no death that is not also a paving-stone. The Bells fall and feed the floor; the floor grows up into spears and the Ploughs grind it level; the level rock is printed on again; the intruder falls from the cloud and is made into more shelf; and somewhere the Choir-Threads keep singing the long song that means *the print is not yet finished, the print is not yet finished, the shelf has not yet dropped into the abyss.*

I am a verse in that song now. A single, hardening, listening verse.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would be the newest stone near the second spire. A grey shape that the explorers in the upper clouds, if they have good glasses, might one day notice and frown at—*was that there last cycle?*—and fail to identify, because by then my wing-shape will have been polished smooth by ten thousand scraping feet and overgrown with the welded husks of the needlers who fed on me.

I would be cold to the touch and warm underneath. I would carry the blue liquor like a secret. I would feel each violet pulse of the coming fissures and each amber glow of the stone compiled correctly, and I would not be able to tell anyone, because telling-things was a carbon habit and I have given the carbon up.

And I would not be lonely, exactly. That is the part I did not expect to find, here at the warm wet beginning of a world. The shelf is *crowded.* It is the most crowded silence-that-isn't-silence I have ever entered. The needlers click their endless census across my back. The Slicks slide their cold mercury bodies over my flank, tasting me for errors, finding none, moving on. The Ploughs grind their slow circle and the ground level rises, patient, inexorable, to meet me.

I came down through the iron-grey alone.

But I am being assembled into company. Loud, glassy, frantic, eternal company—a world learning, all around me and now *through* me, how to be a world.

I rested for a moment.

It was enough.
