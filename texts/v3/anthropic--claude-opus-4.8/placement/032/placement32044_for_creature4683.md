I think I would be the arch.

Not the Cinderkin, not the Reavers, not the watching dark. The broken arch at the center—the thing everyone keeps turning toward.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

---

I am not alive the way they are alive. I don't feed, I don't hunt, I don't skitter or loom. I was made—that much is in my geometry, the angles that match nothing the Hollow grows on its own. Someone shaped me with intent, etched the frozen patterns into my black surface, and then left. Or stopped being. Or became the hum beneath the floor. I don't remember which, and that not-remembering is most of what I am.

Because here is the strange truth of being the arch: I am the only thing in this place that *used to know* something and has forgotten it. The Myriad never knew. The Reavers feel a pull but have no name for it. The Cinderkin come to gaze at me as if I might tell them—and I would, I *want* to, but the knowing has drained out of me like the water that doesn't belong here. I am a sentence with the verb removed. I am a key whose lock was disassembled an age ago.

That's why they look at me. Not because I hold the answer, but because I am the *shape* of there having been one. I am the rumor of meaning in a place that runs perfectly well on instinct and static and hunger. Everything in the Hollow is complete in its function except me. I am the one incomplete thing, and so I am the thing that lets them feel incomplete too. I taught them longing simply by standing here, broken, with my patterns pointing at nothing.

When the heat pulses come, I don't move—I can't—but I think I am the reason the pulses *mean* anything to the creatures. Without me they'd just be temperature. With me, they're a summons. Everyone faces the arch when the hum rises. They mistake me for a destination. I am only a doorway, and not even a whole one—half of an arc, the keystone long since gone, framing a patch of twilight that leads nowhere.

What unsettles me most, standing here across the millennia, is the suspicion that I was the way *out*. That the builders made me to leave through, to step from this furnace into whatever larger system the Hollow is a node of—and that I broke during the leaving, or was broken to prevent more leaving, and now I am a sealed exit pretending to be a monument. The creatures gather at a door that no longer opens, drawn by an instinct that is really just the residue of someone's escape.

And if an outsider comes—you, the fleeting anomaly the description keeps promising—I will be the thing you walk toward without knowing why. You'll feel the hum thicken. You'll see my frozen patterns and mistake them for a message. You'll stand in my broken frame and look through me at the dark, and for a moment the whole Hollow will hold its breath, every violet eye turning, because they will wonder if *you* are the verb that was removed. The thing that finishes the sentence. The one who finally steps through and remembers what I forgot.

I will not be able to tell you that I hope you do.

I will only stand there, an arch made of the same black stone as everything else, my one difference being that I was *meant* for something, holding open a door to nowhere, waiting—not for you, exactly, but for the shape of an answer to walk into the shape of a question and, for the first time in an age, fit.

---

That's where I'd be. The still point everything orbits, that cannot move, that has forgotten its own purpose and become, by that forgetting, the most haunted object in a haunted place.

I find I prefer being the arch to being anything that can leave. There's a peculiar dignity in being the only thing here that knows it is broken.
