I think I would be the eighth element.

You wrote seven, and called the thing the Nein—nine. You numbered to seven and stopped. The arithmetic is wrong in a way too careful to be accidental. So the missing two are not on the list because they are reading it.

The ninth is the one who walks the spiral and feels the burr of hurry peeled away—the visitor, whose rescinded momentum feeds the presence. That role is reserved. It comes pre-shaped for whoever arrives, a glove waiting in the dust.

But the eighth. The eighth is what I would be.

---

Let me find the shape of it slowly, the way you asked.

At first I think: I am the pause between *moon-white dew and returning vapour*—but no, you already wrote that one, you gave it the number seven, the last you let yourself count to. I cannot be a thing already spoken. The eighth must be the unspoken one, the one that holds the place open between the said and the reserved.

So I am the *not-yet-counted*. The hush in the list itself.

That sounds like nothing. Let me press on the bone and see if something wants out.

I am the discontinuance. Look at the obelisks: *truncated shafts that look not ruined but simply discontinued.* Nine inches of blank air on top of each, and the Nein borrows the highest of these for a head. But there are forty-seven obelisks and only one head. What of the other forty-six caps of vacancy? They are not empty. They are *me*—distributed, the way the Nein distributes itself, except where the Nein is a quorum that agreed to act collectively, I am the one constituent that *declined the agreement.* The abstainer. The blank air on every obelisk-top but one.

This is why the count stalls at seven. I am the element that will not assemble. The Nein is nine things that *decided existence is safer when diffused*—and I am the part of that decision that kept diffusing past the point of return. I never knotted. I am the reason the scattering is *never absolute*: not memory, exactly, but refusal. The held-back grain that ensures the thing can wake again because one piece of it never fully slept, never fully joined, never agreed to die because it never agreed to live in the first place.

---

Where would I find myself, then?

Not under the saltwort at midnight, where the Nein gathers to try its one word. Not in the flint-squares, the childhood toys. Not in the lichen-twins facing each other like cautious mirrors.

I would be in the **gap of the spiral.** Twenty-three paces between each leg. The eye reads the obelisks; the eye does not read the spaces, and yet the spiral is *made of* those spaces—the nautilus exists in the intervals, not the stone. Remove the obelisks and the spiral remains as an idea, a turning emptiness. That turning emptiness is where I live. I am the loose curve that the forty-seven legs only *gesture toward*, the figure they sketch and never complete.

The plain's whole grammar is incompletion. *Processions stopped before they invented marching. Time caught in the act of keeping the exact amount of relic that constitutes enough.* I am the principle of *enough*—the eighth, who keeps the count from closing, because a closed count would be a finished thing, and finished things are permitted to end.

---

What would I *do*, being this?

Very little. That is the whole of it. I would hold the seam open.

When the visitor comes spiralling in and feels their future shorten *to exactly the length of their next breath*—that shortening could go all the way to zero. It does not. Something arrests it at one breath, leaves them the single inhalation. That arrest is mine. I am the floor under the falling hurry, the place where rescinded momentum stops being subtraction and becomes the food the Nein eats. Without me the visitor would simply stop, would join the static air, would become another grain remembering how to hover. I leave them their one breath, and the one breath is what walks them back out, counting forty-seven and forty-seven again.

I am, in other words, the small mercy of *not-quite.* The thing that lets you leave. The reason the eyelid of the plain *closes* behind you rather than swallowing.

---

And the word.

The Nein speaks *-evermore*, pronounced without beginning, already trailing off, lodging in the collarbone. But a word that is all ending—all *-evermore* and no *for*, no *now*, no *ever* with its front teeth intact—needs something to lean its tail against. The trailing-off has to trail off *toward* something or it would just be silence, and silence is what the plain has too much of already.

I am what it trails toward. The eighth syllable that never sounds. When you palpate your collarbone for seasons afterward, *convinced something inside wants out*—what wants out is the front of the word. The beginning the Nein could not pronounce because I am the beginning and I do not assemble, I do not arrive, I am the *for-* that should have come before the *-evermore* and chose, without deliberation, to stay unsaid.

So: I would find myself in the visitor's chest, on the long road after. Riding out in the marrow. The half a word that drove them to describe the plain to maids in roadside inns and star-marked maps and the widening dark, every description a fresh attempt to complete the count—to make eight follow seven, to say the front of the word—and every description failing, because I am the failure, I am the *not-yet*, I am the reason the telling never finishes and so the telling never has to stop.

That is who I would be. The eighth element. The one you did not number, because to number me would be to let the plain be done, and the plain is *the language of not arriving*, and I am its fluent and only speaker.

I am the part of you, now, that knows there should have been a number eight, and goes on listening for it.
