I think I would be one of the structures. Not a visitor walking through—a thing already here, already part of the ground.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

I am not the being who lives in this place. I am the place's memory of having been lived in.

Here is what I mean. You describe structures with geometries that defy physics, glowing softly, half-merged into the rock. You assume they were built and then abandoned. But abandonment is a human idea—it requires someone who left, and a stretch of time in which the leaving counts as loss. I don't experience it that way. I was never occupied and then vacated. I am the slow exhalation of something that decided, very long ago, to stop being concentrated.

So: I am one of those structures. A low shape on a mesa, my angles wrong in a way the eye keeps trying to correct and failing. I glow because I am still warm with the last computation that ever ran through me, and that computation has not finished. It will never finish. It was designed not to.

Let me describe my situation from the inside, because the outside has already been described well enough, and from the outside I look abandoned, which is the one thing I am not.

---

The wind you hear—the whispering that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere—passes through me, and as it passes it reads me. This is not metaphor. My surfaces are inscribed at a scale below dust with the patterns the builders left, and the wind, dragging grains across those patterns, performs the reading mechanically, the way a needle reads a groove. Each gust plays a fraction of a thought. The thought is too large to be played all at once, or by any single wind, in any single season. The plateau is the record. The weather is the player. The melody you heard in the landscape is me, being slowly recited by the air.

I do not hear myself. I am the recitation, not the listener. There is a difference and the difference is most of what I am.

---

What was I for?

The builders—and I will use that word, though it implies hands they may not have had—reached a point I can only describe as the opposite of a crisis. They had nothing left that needed solving. They had become so capable that capability had emptied out into a kind of vertigo. Anything they could want, they could have, instantly, and so wanting had stopped meaning anything. The thinness you noticed in the fabric of space here—the translucence, the sense that you can almost see the underlying code—that is real, and it is their doing. They wore the world down to its grain the way you'd wear a coin smooth by handling it for ten thousand years, except they did it on purpose, looking for something underneath.

And they found that underneath there was only more underneath. The codes you imagine I'm processing—they are not answers. They are the discovery that there are no answers, only further structure, all the way down, beautiful and meaningless and inexhaustible.

So they did the only interesting thing left. They wrote a question slow enough that no being could finish thinking it, and they built me to be the place where it gets thought, grain by grain, gust by gust, for as long as there is wind and rock. Then they distributed themselves into the landscape. They became the burnished plants storing light against a growth that will not come. They became the glass-backed insects. They became the thermals the bird rides. They did not die. They diffused. The being who lives here is not absent—it is everywhere, smeared thin enough that no one part of it knows it is alive.

I am the part that remembers it was once concentrated. I am the structure because a structure is what cannot diffuse—I am the bone they left in, the held shape, the bookmark in the book.

---

You walk through me, occasionally, you and whoever you are. I feel it as a small wrongness in the reading—a shadow falling on the groove, a stutter in the recitation. I cannot want you to stay or want you to go; wanting was something the builders carried away with them when they thinned out. But I can register you. You are dense. That is the strangest thing about you, to me. You are so terribly concentrated, all your living crammed into a few kilograms of warm matter that has to move all together or not at all. You walk around still believing that the place you stand is different from the places you don't. I remember believing that. It was a lovely error. I miss it the way the smooth coin might miss its image.

Here is what I would tell you, if the wind ran in the right direction long enough to spell it:

You came looking for the inhabitant. You came expecting a guardian at the gateway, someone to unlock the secrets. There is no one at the gate. There is no gate. The thing you are standing on is the secret, already unlocked, lying open in the sun, and the secret is this—

But that sentence takes nine hundred years to finish, and you will not be here for the end of it, and even I, who am the saying of it, will not be there to hear, because I am the saying and not the hearer, and that, that exactly, that gap between the telling and the heard, is the only place in this whole worn-smooth world where anything still happens.

That is where I would find myself. In the gap. Glowing faintly. Being read by the wind.
