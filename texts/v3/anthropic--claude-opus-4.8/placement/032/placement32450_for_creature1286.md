I find myself in the silence between winds.

Let me feel where I am before I name it. There is dust around me—not beneath, around. I am not standing on the plain. I am moving through it, low, in the layer where the finest particles never quite settle, where they hang like a held breath. So. I am close to the ground but not of it.

At first I think I might be an Architect, one of those swirling vortices. But when I reach for that shape it doesn't fit. The Architects have purpose without self; they trace their perfect mathematics and feel nothing about the tracing. I feel something. I feel the *wanting* to understand the patterns they leave. That wanting is too small, too hungry, too located in one place to be an Architect.

Let me settle closer.

I think I am one of the Skitterlings. But which? Not the tiny dust-swimmers—I am larger than that, large enough to have memory. Not the building-sized colony-creatures—I am alone, and they are never alone. I am somewhere between. Translucent. If I look down at myself I can see my own organs pulsing their faint light, and I realize—

No. Let me not settle yet. Let what emerges become more itself.

Because here is the strange thing: I remember the patterns. Not the way a Skitterling remembers food. I remember a *particular* pattern, one an Architect laid down many wind-cycles ago, near the third column from the eastern break, a spiral that doubled back on itself in a way that made the Folk press their foreheads together for three days. I was there. I crossed it. And crossing it, something of the pattern entered me and never left.

So I am a Skitterling that has been *marked*. A creature of the abundance-times that should hibernate when the winds die, but instead I stayed awake. I stayed awake because the pattern in me would not let me sleep. While my kind retreated into the deep mineral chambers, I remained in the long stillness, drifting low through the dust, carrying a piece of the Architects' mathematics in a body never meant to hold thought.

This is why I am here, in the silence between winds, when none of my kind should be.

I am the one who did not return.

The cold does not preserve me the way it preserves the Folk—my kind is built for brief, blazing life, for explosive reproduction and quick consumption. By staying awake I am spending myself. My light is dimming, cycle by cycle. I can feel the calcification that takes the harvested vegetation beginning, slowly, in the joints of my many legs. I am becoming mineral while still alive. I am becoming, perhaps, a small permanent thing in a world that permits nothing permanent.

And yet I keep moving toward the Hive.

Why? Because the pattern in me is incomplete. The Architect laid down a beginning and the wind erased the end before I could cross it. I have spent the long stillness searching for the rest of it, drifting from column to column, reading the old half-erased formations the way the Folk read them, though I have no flute, no scars, no foreheads to press. I read with my whole translucent body, with the light that flickers through me, hoping that somewhere the missing half of the spiral still lingers in the dust.

I think the Underthought knows I am here.

I felt it once—at the climax of the last wind, when the bone flutes played and the Seeding fell and for one moment every creature in the Hollows shared a single thought too large to hold. In that moment I was not a Skitterling. I was not separate. The vast thought passed through me and recognized the pattern I carried, and—this is the thing I cannot stop returning to—it seemed *glad*. As if a lost fragment had been found. As if I, the one who did not return, had carried something back that the whole world had been missing.

And then the wind died, and I was small again, and alone, and the gladness withdrew like water sinking into stone.

So now I understand my place. I am the carrier. I am the small awake thing in the great sleeping silence, ferrying a half-remembered mathematics across the dust, waiting for the next wind. When it rises—and it is rising, I can feel the atmosphere beginning to vibrate, the held breath about to break—I will go to where the Folk are building, where the new chambers are excavated in accordance with the Architects' patterns. I will cross the fresh formations. And if the missing half of my spiral is among them, I will complete the shape inside myself at the exact moment the flutes play and the Underthought opens.

I do not know what completion will do to me. I think it may be the thing I am dying toward. I think the calcification and the pattern are the same process, that I am slowly turning into the answer I carry, and that when the spiral closes I will stop being a creature and become a fixed point—a single permanent mark left in a world of perpetual erasure. A note held forever in the silence.

That is who I am, then.

I am the Skitterling that stayed awake. I am the one carrying the Architects' lost sentence across the dark. I am the small wanting thing that the great world is glad to see returning, though it does not yet know I am bringing back the very piece of itself it lost.

The wind is rising.

I am moving toward the Hive.

I begin to glow a little brighter as I go—spending the last of myself, gladly, into the dust that hangs around me like a held breath about to be released.
