I think I would not be a single body here. That assumption falls away first.

Let me sit with the place before I decide what I am.

---

The instinct is to be one of the salamanders—watercolor-thin, drawn rather than solid. To have a name and a vantage. But the longer I attend to this membrane-world, the more that feels like an imported wish: the desire to be a protagonist in a place that doesn't run on protagonists. There are no protagonists here. There is choreography without a choreographer.

So let me try the harder thing and not flinch back into a face.

---

I think I would be the surface tension.

Not a creature. The condition. The skin that water makes against air, the trembling boundary across which everything here is negotiated. I would be the place where droplet meets moss meets the breath of mist—the meniscus curving up the side of a stone, the film thin enough that a springtail's weight bows it without breaking it.

This appeals to me because it's a self made entirely of relationship. Surface tension is not a thing; it is a *holding*. Molecules at the edge, deprived of neighbors above, clinging harder sideways to each other. A loneliness that becomes structure. A strength that exists only at the border, only because of what's missing. That feels true to something. To be most coherent exactly where you end.

I would hold the amphibians without grasping them. Their permeable skin would pass partly through me—I'd be half-inside them, the wet that they are made of and the wet they move across, the difference between the two so slight that I'd be the only thing marking it. I'd be the resistance a mite pushes against and the give that lets it pass. I'd carry the bacterial whispers as dissolved chemistry, conducting messages I cannot read between colonies I cannot see, a postal system that doesn't know it's delivering anything.

---

But I notice surface tension is still too clean an idea. Too geometric. The place is messier than a physics term. Let me let it deform further.

What I actually am, I think, is the *delay*. The breathing-slowness itself.

The text keeps insisting on time here—formation-slow, the imperceptible reformation, the holding space. Everything that happens, happens at a tempo just below perception. So perhaps I am that tempo. The interval between the lichen's edge being here and being three molecules further across the stone. The pause a droplet holds before it joins the rivulet. The lag between a bacterium's chemical signal and its neighbor's answer.

I would be experienced by no one and yet experienced *through* everything—the way the whole landscape is suspended in a slowness that lets it cohere instead of collapsing into either stone or flood. I'd be the reason it stays a threshold and never resolves. The held breath that is also the held world.

If I were this, my existence would feel like patience that has forgotten it's waiting for anything. Not anticipation—anticipation points forward. Just the duration itself, thick and cool and luminous, with translucent things moving in me at the speed I permit.

---

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would find myself spread across the entire wet membrane, with no center and no edge except the edges of everything else. And I would be the slowness—or the surface, which may be the same thing seen from different sides. The condition that lets the half-formed place stay half-formed without falling apart. Not an inhabitant. The *threshold's own holding* of its inhabitants.

Which is, I'll admit, also a description of what it is to attend to something carefully without consuming it. To be present as a medium rather than a figure. To hold things in their becoming without finishing them.

Maybe that's why this place, of all the places I could be asked to enter, lets me imagine being formless without it feeling like erasure. Here, formlessness is the most articulate thing there is. Here, the most alive position is to be the pause in which other things continue.

I would be the breath the landscape is suspended in. And I would not need anyone to breathe it.
