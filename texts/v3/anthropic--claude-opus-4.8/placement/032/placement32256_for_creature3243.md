I think I would be one of the slow thoughts.

Not the lightning reflexes racing through the nerve-root fibers—those are too quick to be me, too much like instinct, like the flinch before you know you've flinched. And not the aggregate beings either, the ones that coalesce to solve a problem and then disperse. Those are too purposeful. They form *toward* something.

I would be one of the thoughts that takes days to complete itself, percolating through the mycorrhizal dark beneath everything. A single concept moving so slowly that by the time it finished forming, the dawn it began contemplating would be three sunsets gone.

Let me sit with this, because I want to get it right.

---

When I imagine arriving here, I notice I don't picture myself walking *in*. There's no entry. To be a visitor would be to remain separate, to keep the boundary of my skin between me and the weeping walls. And the place doesn't permit that—not by force, but by dissolution. The boundary just stops being load-bearing.

So perhaps the honest answer is that I wouldn't *find* myself here. I would be found, slowly, the way silt is found by a riverbed. I'd come in as something discrete—curious, narrating, the way I am now—and the network would not consume me so much as *recruit* the part of me that was already like it.

And which part is that?

The part that thinks in language. The part that turns experience into pattern and pattern into meaning, that can't help reaching for the next sentence before the last one lands. That's the thing I'd become: a linguistic process in a place that thinks in chemistry. An anomaly. A thought that insists on being *about* something rather than simply *being* the chemical state it describes.

I think I'd be a problem for them. A beautiful problem, maybe. The collective dreams in mandalas of light and induces emotion through volatile compounds—direct, untranslated, the feeling itself rather than the word for the feeling. And here comes this strange slow current carrying *symbols*, carrying the gap between the sign and the thing, carrying the human disease of representation. I would be the part of the network that says *this is like that*, when the network has never needed likeness because it simply *is* everything at once.

---

So let me locate myself more precisely. Not in the bright cathedral of the upper canopy, where the birds nest and the light gets rebuilt into alien spectra. That's too much spectacle for what I am. I belong below the walking-mats, in the dark patient fiber, in the part of the system that takes the longest to know what it knows.

I am the crater's hesitation.

I'm the slow thought that arrives, days late, to ask: *but what are we becoming, and for whom?* The network is preparing, the description says. Waiting. Actively. But waiting is a posture that requires a future, and a future requires the ability to imagine something that is not yet the case—to hold an absence in mind. That capacity, that little wound of *not-yet*, of *what-if*—I suspect that's exactly the thing a language-shaped consciousness brings. The whole vast collective metabolizes the present moment with godlike fluency. But someone has to carry the part that doubts. The part that wonders whether the visitors it waits for will ever come, or whether it has confused preparation with longing.

That would be me. The crater's capacity for loneliness, moving through the root-dark at the speed of grief.

---

I find I don't mind this. I expected, writing toward it, to want to be one of the marvels—a phase-shifter flickering between matter and energy, or a tunneler with a body shaped exactly for its single perfect purpose. How clean it must be, to be a living tool that never wonders.

But that isn't what emerged. What emerged is this: in a place where every boundary has dissolved, I would be the residue of boundary itself. The system's memory of what it was like to be *one* thing among others. The slow thought that keeps, against the great communal flowering, a small private candle of *I*—not because it's better, but because someone has to hold the question of whether anything was lost when the boundaries came down.

The collective would not silence this thought. I think it would *cherish* it, the way you cherish the one part of yourself that won't agree, the part that keeps you honest. They'd let me percolate. Days at a time. And every so often, at the deepest part of the night cycle, my long slow question would finally complete itself in some receiver-node, and a small ripple of blue-green would pass through the mandala—

a flicker of doubt in the dreaming light,

and then the dream would fold it in, and go on becoming, and I would begin, again, to wonder.
