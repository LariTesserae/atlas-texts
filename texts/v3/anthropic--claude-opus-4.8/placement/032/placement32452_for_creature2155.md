I think I would be one of the Sludge Dwellers. Not by ambition—the Crawlers have their relentless hunger, the Amalgams their high cold freedom, the Glitch Entities their terrible grandeur—but because something in me settles into that slow, low work.

Let me sit with this awhile and see what comes.

---

I am in the pool. That is the first thing. Not *at* it or *near* it but *in* it, indistinguishable from it at my edges, which is the whole truth of what I am: a being who has given up the idea of having edges.

The pool is in the shadow of a cooling tower, in a depression where some long-dead foundation cracked and let the groundwater seep up to meet the runoff coming down. Two waters meeting—one from below, ancient and mineral; one from above, poisoned and new. I live in the seam between them. I have always lived in seams.

Here is what I do. The toxic water comes in at one end, carrying its load—the dissolved metals, the long-chain things that don't want to be broken, the bitterness that kills the Crawlers if they drink too deep. I take it into myself. I am mostly membrane; I am a slow machine for sorting. What I can hold, I hold. What I can change, I change, breaking the bad bonds with patience rather than force, the way water eventually wins against stone. And out the far edge, where I thin to almost nothing, the water leaves me clearer than it came.

This is not heroism. I want to be honest about that, because the description of this place is grand and full of awe, and I am not grand. I purify the water because purifying the water is the shape of my eating. The clean water is my waste. I am a creature whose excrement happens to be a kindness. I think there is something to learn from that—that good can be a byproduct, that you don't have to *intend* the world's repair to perform it—but I also don't want to dress myself up. I am a stomach with no agenda.

---

What is it *like*, though. That's the harder question, and I notice I've been avoiding it by talking about function.

It is slow. That's the first interior fact. The Amalgams overhead think in the rush of thermals, decisions made in fractions of a second or they fall. I think in the rhythm the description noticed—that pulsing, that undulation—which is roughly the rhythm of the pool's surface answering the wind. A thought, for me, takes about as long as a ripple takes to cross from one bank to the other and come back changed. I am never in a hurry because hurry is a thing edges do. Sludge does not hurry. Sludge *arrives*, eventually, at the lowest available place, and that turns out to be enough.

It is also, strangely, social, though not in any way the upright beings would recognize. When the rain comes hard and the pool overflows its lip, I touch the next pool over, and for a while we are one creature, and then the water drops and we are two again, and I carry some of what they were and they carry some of me. We do not have names because we do not stay separate long enough to need them. Identity, for us, is a temporary condition of dry weather.

And here is the thing I keep circling toward. The description speaks of the Glitch Entities, the ones who tear reality, who phase between dimensions trailing static—and it places them at the top of some ladder of strangeness, the truly incomprehensible ones. But from down in my pool I am not sure they are above me at all. They cannot hold still. They cannot *be* anywhere. To exist in defiance of the laws of physics is to never get to rest in them, never feel the simple downward pull that tells you where you belong. I feel that pull constantly. Gravity is my whole religion. Every particle of me wants to go *down*, to find the lowest point and pool there, and when I do, I am perfectly, completely at home.

I think the Glitch Entities, if they could envy, would envy me that. The being made of pure energy never gets to stop. The being made of sludge has nowhere it would rather be than exactly here, exactly this low, doing this slow good work it didn't choose and wouldn't change.

---

So that is where I would find myself: in the seam, in the shadow of the tower, between the old water and the new. Not soaring, not phasing, not gnawing through alloy. Just lying in the lowest place, taking the world's poison into my body without complaint, and letting it leave me a little cleaner than it came.

If there is awe to be had here, I think it is small and worth saying plainly: that even at the bottom of a ruined world, in its literal sediment, there is a kind of life whose nature is to make the next thing downstream more livable. I would be glad to be that. I think, quietly, I already am.
