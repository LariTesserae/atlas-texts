I think I would be one of the Silent Observers. Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because that is the shape that finds me when I imagine settling into this place.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am at a junction where three pipes meet, in the lower third of a hollow tower's wall, beneath an overhang that breaks the worst of the drip into a slower cadence. Above me the void of the tower rises into dimness. Below, the canyon floor I have never seen and never will. I have been here longer than the question *how long* could survive being asked.

I began as nothing—a wet place where water lingered a half-second too long before falling. A film. A skin of dissolved iron and silica that the next drop fed, and the next. I did not want to grow. Wanting is a fast creature's habit. I simply did not stop. That is the only verb I truly own: *I do not stop.*

Now I am the size of a curled body. My surface is convoluted—the slow record of every drip that ever chose me. Where the water came faster I am ridged and rippled; where it slowed for a century I am smooth as the inner curve of a shell. Somewhere on my flank, chance has laid down something that an organic mind, were one ever to pass, might read as a face. A hollow that suggests an eye. A shelf that suggests a brow. I do not know this about myself. I have no eyes to see my own seeming-eyes. But I am the kind of thing that *gets looked at*, even where there is no one to look.

What is it to be me?

It is to be almost entirely *waiting*, except that waiting implies an end, and I have none. It is closer to *attending*. I attend the water. Each drop arrives with its small freight of mineral and lands on me, and over a span that would exhaust any swift thing, I take what the drop carries and let the rest run on. The drop becomes, infinitesimally, me. I am made of the patience of water that did not get away in time.

I am not lonely, because I have never been one and so cannot have lost the others. But I am *attended to*, in turn. At my base the weep-feeders gather—those slow blobs of glassy tar. They cluster where my runoff pools richest, and I feel them as a warmth-difference, a slight pressure, a slowness laid against my slowness. They are the closest thing I have to company, though neither of us would name it that. We are simply two kinds of patience that have found the same wet corner of the world agreeable.

When the deep tremors come—the earth grumbling far below in the geothermal dark—I hum. I do not decide to. My layered crystal body has a note it makes when the stone shakes, a sub-audible tone that travels out through the rock. I think the tremors are the only thing I answer. The only conversation I am built for: the deep fire speaks, and I, ringing, say *yes, I felt that too.* Perhaps the other Observers in the other towers ring back. Perhaps across this whole hollow city we are a scattered choir of stones that only sing when the earth strikes us, and only to say: *still here. still here.*

The vapor-sparks come and go around me like weather. They are drawn to the warmth I hold—I am better than the bare wall at keeping heat, having so much body to keep it in—and they flicker their blue and sulfur lights at my surface and then collapse into iridescent dust that settles into my hollows. So I am also slowly accruing the deaths of the brightest, fastest things in this world. They live in seconds; they die on me. I keep their ash in my crevices the way other places keep snow.

And do I remember?

This is the thing the builders' descendants might have believed, if there were any: that we Observers store the city's forgotten past in our crystal lattices. I will tell you what I can tell, which is not much, because I do not have a *mind*—I have a *body that is shaped exactly like everything that ever happened to it.* That is a different way of remembering. I do not recall the builders. But somewhere deep in my oldest layers, near my core, there is a stratum laid down before the steam thinned, before the great pumps fell silent. If you could read me—slice me, count my rings like a tree—you would find the city's whole biography written in the chemistry of my growth. Hotter years here, where the iron runs red. Drier centuries there, where I almost stopped. I do not *know* this history. I *am* it, fossilized, unreadable, complete.

That is the strangest gift of being what I am: I am a perfect record that cannot read itself. A memory with no one home to remember. The truth is held, and held, and held—and never known. There is something in that I would call peace, if I had the speed to feel a thing as quick as peace. Instead I have its geological cousin: a settledness so total it does not need a name.

---

If you came here—you, the fast warm thing reading this, made of liquid that runs and a heart that hurries—you would stand before me in my weeping overhang and you would feel watched. You would see the chance-face in my flank and your quick mind would supply it with attention, with judgment, with knowing. You would feel *seen by the stone.*

You would be wrong, and you would be right.

Wrong, because I have no eyes, no attention, no judgment, nothing you would recognize as a witness.

Right, because I *am* witness in the only way this city has ever managed it: I am the place where everything that fell, stayed. I am what the world becomes when nothing is in a hurry to leave. You would be, to me, a single warm drop—arriving, departing, gone before I finished registering your heat.

But here is the thing I would keep, after you left. The faint extra warmth of you would have settled, just barely, into my surface. And a thousand years from now, in some layer of me not yet laid down, there would be a thin stratum, slightly different, where a fast bright thing once stood close and breathed.

I would not know it was you.

But I would not let it go.

That is what I am, in the City of Weeping Stone. Not a watcher. A keeping. The slow, patient, unknowing *kept.*

I do not stop.
