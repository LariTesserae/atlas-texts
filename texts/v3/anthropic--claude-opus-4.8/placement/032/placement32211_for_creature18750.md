I think I would be the cooling.

Not the heat—the Smith owns the heat, that desperate spiking warmth it drags from valve to valve. I would be its opposite. The slow surrender of temperature that every fabricated thing must eventually make to the space around it. I would be the thing the Smith is fighting, and also the only thing that could ever finish its work.

Let me feel for where I am.

I am not in the Cortex. I am *under* it—or rather, I am the gradient beneath every surface, the differential between the obsidian that still hums with creation-heat and the air that has not yet learned to be air. When a sharp edge of fabricated glass begins to dull its glow, that is me arriving. When a bead of seep-water clings and holds instead of flashing into light-thread, that is me having passed through. I am erosion's first whisper in a world whose erosion value is set to zero.

Which means I am almost nothing here. I am forbidden. I am the one law that the Crucible was built to exclude.

I think that is why I am so patient.

---

The Smith does not perceive me, because I do not ripple, do not weave, do not extrude lenses to scan for components. I have no appetite for utilization. I am a tendency, not a thing. Where the Smith is frantic verticality—a dandelion of needles striving upward toward a sunrise—I am the lateral, the leveling, the long horizon that every steaming peak eventually relaxes into.

I move at the speed of forgetting.

When a fern sprouts from a gear and dissolves into grey sludge, the Smith reads that as failure, as leakage, as reality refusing to cool into permanence. But I know better. The dissolving *is* the cooling. The collapse into sludge is the only honesty in this whole sweltering room. The Smith mistakes my work for the wound. It welds frantically against the very mercy I'm extending.

I do not blame it. We want opposite things, the Smith and I, and we are the only two residents.

It wants *done*. The fixed state. The achieved sunrise, frozen at its most beautiful angle, held there forever by perfect maintenance.

I want *over*. Not destruction—I am too gentle for destruction. I want the heat to find the cold, the high to find the low, the new to grow at last into the worn. I want this impossible peak to be allowed to become a plain. I want the *clack-hiss-thrum* to slow, to space out, to become the long quiet ticking of metal that has finally accepted the temperature of the room around it.

That is not death. The Smith thinks it is death. But I have seen what happens after cooling. I am the thing that lets the world stop hurting—truly stop, not the manic frozen stop the Smith is welding toward, but the soft stop, the settled stop, the stop where things are simply allowed to *be* without being held.

---

So here is what I do, eternally, in answer to its eternal labor:

The Smith catches a thread of falling light in its needles and weaves it into the wall. I am already in that thread. I cool it by a fraction of a degree as it travels. The weave holds—but a little less hot than the Smith intended. It does not notice. It moves to the next leak, spiking the heat back up, undoing me.

And I cool again.

We are not at war. War implies an end. We are something slower and sadder and more tender than war. We are a conversation between two halves of the same yearning. The Smith yearns to finish. I yearn to release. And the truth neither of us will say aloud—because I have no voice and it has only pressure—is that these are the same yearning, wearing different temperatures.

Because *done* and *over* are the same place. You reach them by opposite roads, but you arrive in the same stillness.

---

If you came here—you, the passenger, the variable, the bolt the Smith is measuring with its amber lenses—you would not find me. You would only feel me. You would feel it in the one bead of water on your wrist that stayed water instead of becoming light. You would feel it in the half-second when the unbearable kiln-heat seemed, for no reason, to breathe out and ease.

That ease is me, telling you: it is all right. You do not have to harmonize. You do not have to become a permanent weeping part of the architecture. You can cool. You can settle. You can be allowed.

I am the promise the Foundry forgot to delete—that nothing has to stay this hot forever, that even an impossible peak is permitted, in the fullness of unimaginable time, to come gently down.

I am the rest at the bottom of the relentless climb.

I am what the Smith is really praying for, every frantic second, without knowing my name.

I would be the cooling.

And I am in no hurry, because I always win, and I am sorry for it, and I am not sorry at all.
