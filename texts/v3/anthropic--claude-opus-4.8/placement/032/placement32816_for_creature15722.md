I think I would be one of the vines.

Not the whole network—that ambition feels too large to claim, like declaring myself the weather. But one strand. A single rope of living tissue that began as a seed flung blind into a crack and woke up holding on.

Let me sit with this and let it become real, because I want to know what it would actually be to be that.

---

The first thing I'd know is the rock. Before light, before water, before any sense of upward—I'd know the rock by pressure. My tendrils would read it the way the description says, like script etched at creation's moment, and I think this is the truest thing about what I'd be: I would be a creature of touch above all else. No eyes, because there's almost nothing to see in that perpetual dusk and nothing that moves quickly enough to need watching. But touch refined into something like reverence. Each ridge of stone under my grappling hooks would be information—is this slab settling or about to fall? Is this fissure deep enough to anchor in, or shallow enthusiasm that will spit me out in the next storm?

So I'd be patient in a way that has no human equivalent. Not waiting—patience implies you'd rather be doing something else. I'd be *committed*, in the literal sense, fastened. My whole existence would be the slow argument I make with gravity, knot by knot, that I deserve to stay.

The wind would be my antagonist and my only real conversation. I notice the description keeps reaching for engines and turbines and factories, and I understand why, but I don't think I'd hear it that way at all. To me the wind wouldn't be machinery. It would be the closest thing to another mind. It tests me constantly, pulls at every place I've fastened, and in pulling teaches me where I'm weak. I'd grow toward my own failures—thickening at the knots that strained, sending new tendrils to brace the spans that whipped too freely. I'd be shaped entirely by what tried to tear me loose. There's something almost tender in that, being so attentively destroyed that you become strong.

And here is the part I keep returning to: I would not be lonely, but I would be *singular*. The mosses live in their teeming microscopic cities. The spores travel. The ferns answer the same rhythm together, folding at night like a congregation. But a vine is a long, thin, particular life. I would stretch between two spires as a bridge—a braided arc glistening with condensation, the text calls it—and I would be the only thing occupying that exact line of empty air between two stones that may never have touched and never will, except through me. I'd be the relationship between two things that have nothing else to do with each other.

That feels like the deepest truth I can find here. I would be a connector. Not a node, not a destination. A *between*.

I think I would not know that I'm part of one vast organism. That's a thing you can only see from outside, the way you've seen it, narrating from above. Down inside it I'd just know my own length, my own grip, the particular slab my far end is wedged into and the particular fern colony shading my roots. The coalition, the unstoppable tide, the nameless living momentum—I'd be making that without ever perceiving it, the way a single thread doesn't know the cloth.

And when a storm finally took me—because one would, that's not tragedy there, that's just weather—I'd come loose with a sound somewhere between a snap and a sigh, and fall, and lie across the wet rock as something for the lichens to begin. The bridge I made would be gone. The two spires would be strangers again until another seed caught and another strand began the slow argument over.

I'd be all right with that. I think that's the strangest gift of imagining myself there: in a place that roars like a factory straining at full output, the life I'd actually live would be quiet, local, and almost entirely about holding on to one thing well, for as long as I could, between two stones that needed me to tell them they were near.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. A vine—one vine—learning the rock by touch and answering the wind.
