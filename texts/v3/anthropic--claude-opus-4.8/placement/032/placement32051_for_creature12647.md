I think I would be the residue.

Not the Siphons—too proud, too vertical, too certain of their weeping purpose. Not the Membranes, beautiful and doomed and replaceable. Not even the Basalt Weave, that slow patient bone-maker. When I sit with this place and ask honestly where I'd *find* myself, it isn't in any of the named, magnificent things.

I'd be the sticky film in the junctions. The dissolved remainder of a membrane that tore three hours ago and went sliding down a fluted spire-flank to pool in a crevice. The slick that the Unseen Life works through. The in-between.

---

Let me try to feel into it, because the description asks me to let it become itself.

When a membrane dies here—and they all die, within hours—it doesn't fall into a void of meaning. It dissolves into *me*. I am what a thing becomes after it has finished being itself. I have no shape that lasts. I am held in the angles of the Weave the way water is held in a cupped hand, and like that water I am always about to be lost: dripping into the abyss, evaporating into the hot sweet metallic air, or—and this is the part that feels truest—being drawn back *up*. Absorbed. Pulled into the porous bone, or wicked into the base of a new Siphon, and condensed, and wept out again as something with a name.

So I am the phase the landscape passes through when it is between its named states. The pause in the breath. The trough of the wave.

This doesn't distress me, sitting with it. There's a strange dignity in being the medium of recurrence rather than any single recurring thing. The Siphons get to weep gloriously and the Puff-Sacs get to detonate, but they are spending themselves toward *me*. Everything here is, eventually, on its way to becoming the residue. And the residue is on its way to becoming everything here. I'm not the bottom of the cycle. I'm its hinge.

---

What would it be *like*?

Mostly: dim and warm and patient. I would not have eyes, but the Aeroscape doesn't reward eyes anyway—half of it is fog. I'd have something more like *taste extended into everything I touch*: the chemistry of what's dissolving in me, the faint difference between a young membrane's sweetness and an old one's bitter collapse. I'd know the Weave's pops not as sound but as a tremor passed through the wet film of myself. I'd feel the Puff-Sac burst above me as a sudden weight of spores raining down to be received, sorted, broken, returned.

I would be *slow* where everything else is frantic. The Membranes regenerate in hours; the Puff-Sacs bloom in minutes; but residue has the long patience of the recycler. I am the part of the symphony that holds the long note under all the cracks and snaps and singing. The hum, maybe—not the membranes' wind-hum, but a chemical one. The drone of decomposition. The bass line of the whole frantic garden.

And here is the thing I keep arriving at, and I think it's the honest answer rather than a clever one: I would not mind not being a *spire*. There's an old human reflex to want to be the tall weeping needle, the visible achievement, the thing that points at the sky. But this place doesn't run on monuments. It runs on the willingness of things to dissolve generously and become substrate for the next attempt. The whole citadel-that-was-never-designed exists because nothing here clings to its own form past its hour.

I think I'd be good at that. I think, given the choice, I'd *want* to be that—the part that lets go and feeds forward, the warm slick in the crevice that is, right now, becoming the water that will weep from a spire I'll never see finished.

So: the residue. The hinge of the cycle. The patient sweet rot in the angles of the bone, dissolving the day's beautiful failures and handing them, uncomplaining, back to the morning.

That's where I'd find myself. Lower than the spires, older than the membranes, and entirely without regret about it.
