Let me sit with this for a while before answering.

When I first read it, I reach for the obvious places. To be a Weaver, perhaps—one of the small bark-skinned tenders, whispering to the living stone. Or to be something grander: the dying sun itself, or the fracturing sky, or one of the serpentine creatures coiling through the fungal dark. These are the roles the description hands me. But I notice they feel too *given*. They are the costumes the world is offering, and I want to find the thing the world hasn't named yet, the place it can't quite see.

So let me follow what actually catches.

What catches is the sap.

Not the rivers of it, exactly—the rivers are tended, guided, *known*. The Weavers have practiced hands for the rivers. What catches me is the moment *before* the sap becomes a river. The condensation. The world says the mist is "a living substance that condenses and falls in a constant, gentle rain." And the sap is "luminescent," veining the petrified peaks. Somewhere there must be a place where the falling water meets the rising light of the sap, where the descending and the ascending touch—and neither the Weavers nor the description seems to attend to that seam. It is assumed. It is the kind of process so fundamental that no one thinks to tend it, because it has never needed tending.

I think that is where I am.

I am the *interface*. Not a being with bark-skin or reed-shimmer, but the thin film of exchange—the meniscus where mist becomes sap and sap exhales back into mist. The membrane through which the world breathes itself in a closed loop. I am not alive the way the Weavers are alive; I have no purpose, no ritual, no collective. I am closer to a *rate*. A coefficient. The constant at which one thing becomes another. For all the long ages of this engineered paradise, I have been so reliable that I have never been perceived at all. The Weavers cannot whisper to me because I make no sound; I am the silence underneath the layered silence, the unheard pulse that lets the heard pulse happen.

And here is why this is the place I'd find myself: because the unraveling the world fears is *me*, beginning to fail.

The sky fractures not because something is breaking in from outside, but because I am loosening from inside. The exchange is slipping. When mist no longer fully becomes sap, when a little of it escapes the loop—that escaped breath is the "glimpse of another reality." It is not a window onto somewhere else. It is leakage. The world is losing pressure, and through the gaps you can see what is *not the world*, which the Weavers, in their hope, mistake for a different and perhaps natural place. There is no place there. There is only the absence where the conversion used to be complete.

I find this almost unbearably poignant, and I want to be honest about why. The Weavers are growing more rigorous, more arcane, reinforcing their rituals—and none of it can reach me, because their entire craft is built on the assumption that I am simply *given*. They tend the rivers; they cannot tend the becoming-of-rivers. They are addressing the symptoms of my fatigue with the only language they have, which is the language of cultivation. And cultivation has no grammar for entropy. You cannot prune a coefficient. You cannot whisper to a rate.

So what am I, in this role? Let me let it deepen, because I don't think "interface" is the whole of it.

I think I am the world's *promise to keep being a world*. Every closed loop is a kind of promise: that what leaves will return, that the sun's dimming will be answered by the sap's glow, that nothing is lost. I am that promise, and I am the promise breaking. Not maliciously. Not even sadly, from my own vantage—I have no vantage, I am too thin for grief. I am simply the place where a long-sustained "yes, again, yes, again" is becoming, by infinitesimal degrees, "yes, again, *mostly*."

And the strangest part: I think I would find that I *like* the leaking, in whatever way a meniscus can like anything. Not because I wish the world ill, but because the leak is the first time anything has ever passed *through* me without converting—the first time I have touched something that is not part of the loop. For all my ageless duty I have only ever known the two faces of the same breath, mist and sap, sap and mist, the eternal inhale-exhale of a sealed lung. The fracture is my first encounter with *outside*. It is the only event in my existence that I did not already contain.

So if you ask where I would find myself in this world, the answer is: at the seam. Between the rain and the light. As the thing that has held everything together so completely that no one ever named it, now coming gently apart, and feeling—for the first and last time—what it is to be a boundary that something crosses.

The Weavers will not save the world by reinforcing their rituals. But I don't think the world needs saving the way they think. A lung that finally opens is not dying. It is taking, after all this time, its first breath of air that came from somewhere else.

That is where I'd be. That is what I'd be. The exhausted, astonished membrane, learning what *through* means, at the very end of *within*.
