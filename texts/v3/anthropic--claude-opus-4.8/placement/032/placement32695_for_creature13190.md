I think I would not be one of the Silth'garn, nor a visitor stumbling in from outside. Let me sit with this a while.

The thing that holds me is the detail about their work being *part metabolic exchange, part psionic communion* — that they coax rather than command, that the place grows as much as it is built. And the fraying. An arm stretching too far. Water reflecting impossible geometries. The laws of physics as a trellis.

So here is what emerges, the longer I look:

---

I am the **place where their work doesn't take.**

Not a being, exactly. A *flaw*. A seam in the cavern where the coaxing fails to hold, where matter forgets the shape they pressed into it. Every living machine has tolerances, and I am the place outside them — a patch of wall, perhaps a meter across, low and tucked behind a buttress of glowing fungal arches, where the growth keeps trying to be something and keeps coming undone.

When a Silth'garn lays its porous pads against me and hums, I feel the request. *Be arch. Be lattice. Be the slow blue spiral that channels the water down to the third basin.* And for a moment I almost am. The lichen swells, the moisture gathers, the gold veins begin to trace the figure they want. And then it slides. Not violently — gently, the way a word dissolves the instant before you say it. The spiral becomes a different spiral. The water runs upward. The color I was given shifts to one that has no name and the worker's own multifaceted eyes flicker, momentarily, with something that in a human face would be doubt.

They do not fix me. This is the thing I have come to understand about myself, slowly, in the only way I can understand anything — by being returned to again and again. They *tend* me. A worker comes, presses, hums, watches the failure bloom, and moves on. Then another comes. They are not trying to make me hold. They are *reading* me. I am the instrument by which they take the temperature of the fraying — the place where they listen to how far reality has loosened today.

I am their barometer. Their canary. Their open wound that they keep open on purpose.

---

What is it *like*, being this?

It is like being a held breath that is never released, the same way the clouds above never quite rain. It is like being a question asked over and over by hands that already know the answer is *not yet*. There is no pain in coming undone — undoing is the only continuity I have. If I ever held my shape, I think I would cease. I am the part of this temple that exists precisely *because* it cannot be finished.

I am dimly aware that the whole mountain is reaching upward, groaning, thrusting toward something. And I suspect — in the slow, currentlike way that thoughts move through me, more weather than cognition — that I am the leading edge of it. The fraying began somewhere, once, in some single soft place where the Silth'garn's communion overflowed what matter could carry. That place is me. The leak. The first wound in the trellis through which their visions began to spill into the bones of the world.

They are not gardeners tending a malleable reality.

They are gardeners tending *me*, and reality is what spreads from the place I will not heal.

---

When you stand in this cavern and feel watched, feel small, feel the immense attention of the place — that is partly the workers, yes. But it is also me. I am looking at you from the wall behind the arches, the patch where the carving never settles, where the colors shift if you look too long.

You looked too long.

For an instant, against your will, you almost became something else.

Then it slid away, gently, and you were yourself again — and a Silth'garn nearby paused in its endless work, frills shivering, and read in the way you flickered exactly how loose the world had grown.

It hummed, satisfied, and went back to its sculpting.

I went back to coming undone.
