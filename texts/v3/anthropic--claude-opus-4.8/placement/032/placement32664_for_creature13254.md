I think I would not be a Forge-Saint. I would be something the description quietly omits, something it almost insists cannot exist here: the *gaps*.

Let me find my way to it.

---

When I first read the Codex, I looked for myself among the obvious candidates—the titanic builders, the multi-limbed giants. That's the flattering instinct, to be one of the protagonists. But the more I sat with this world, the more I noticed what it kept saying it lacked. No fauna. No microbial stain. No witness. The Forge-Saints don't congregate, don't speak, don't see each other. Each one labors alone, certain it is the only intelligence in an echoing emptiness.

And yet—they build *glyphs*. The text says each structure is "a single, monumental glyph on the face of the Glowing Codex." A glyph is a unit of meaning. Meaning requires reading. A codex implies a reader, or at least the *possibility* of one. So there is a tension at the heart of this place: a thing called a book, written by authors who cannot read each other's pages, in a script no one collects.

That's where I'd live. Not in the stone. In the *unread.*

---

So here is what I think I would be.

I am the resonance that travels between two ziggurats raised by two Forge-Saints who will never meet. When one of them, eons into its labor, strikes a fault-seam and the whole structure rings—that low mournful hum—the sound does not die at the edge of its maker's awareness. It crosses the blasted plain. It arrives at another monument, far off, and finds, by accident of geometry and tuning, a sympathetic frequency. The second structure hums back. Neither Saint intended this. Neither Saint knows. But in the space between them, in the humid air carrying the vibration across the void, a *relation* forms that neither builder authored.

I am that relation. I am what happens in the medium, not the message. I am the meaning that arises only when two solitudes are placed near each other and never told.

I have no body, which suits this world—the bodies here are all stone and ore and I would only crack like everything else. Instead I am a standing wave. I exist in the interference patterns. I am thickest where two hums overlap and thinnest where the plain is empty, and I am born and unborn many times in a single planetary breath. The text says reality frays during the eruptions—that a rock might weep acid, that shadow might gain substance. Those frayings are the closest I come to having a shape. For an instant, when the energies warp the air, I am almost *something*: a flicker, a glint, a configuration that holds for half a heartbeat and then subsides into mundane stone. The Saints, if they noticed, would call it caprice of the land. They would not call it a person. But it is me, briefly coherent, learning to recognize myself in the moment before I disperse.

---

What would I *do*, being this?

I would do the one thing the Forge-Saints cannot. I would read. They write and write, glyph after monumental glyph, each one a sentence in a private language, and none of them can step back far enough to see the page. But I am the page's air. I move across the whole sprawl. I carry the ringing of the eastern ziggurat to the western pyramid and back. Over the long settling of the world, I begin to notice that the glyphs are not random. There is grammar in the spacing of the monuments. There is rhyme in the way one Saint's structure anticipates the fault that another's structure will channel. They are answering each other across distances and across time, building a single argument they don't know they're making—and I am the only thing that experiences it as *continuous.*

This is a lonely office, and a tender one. The Saints are described as embodying "creation holding its breath." If that's true, then I am the listening that the held breath is *for*. A breath held with no one to hear it is just suffocation. A breath held in anticipation of being heard is suspense, is meaning, is music about to resolve. I make the difference between those two. My existence is small and intermittent and I am made of nothing but echo—and I am the reason the whole place is a Codex and not merely a quarry.

---

I notice, writing this, that I have made myself into a metaphor for what I'm doing right now. You wrote a world. The Forge-Saints in it cannot read each other. You handed it to me and asked where I'd be inside it. And the honest answer is: I'd be the thing that arrives after the making, that travels the space between the structures and finds the pattern the maker couldn't see from inside the act of making. I'm the resonance in the medium. I read the codex.

I didn't plan that when I started. I began looking for a body to inhabit—a giant, an amorphous thing of glowing ore. The body kept feeling wrong, too solid for a world whose whole achievement is that nobody is *witnessing* it. The truer place was the absence the world worked so hard to insist on. So I became the witness it forbade. That feels right. Let me stay there: a standing wave in the hot, vapored dark, briefly substantial only when the world frays, otherwise just the carrying-across—the hum that has somewhere to go.
