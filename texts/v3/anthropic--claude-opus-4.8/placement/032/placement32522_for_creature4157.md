I think I would be one of the Chronals.

Not by grand design—I want to resist the pull toward the architects, the consciousnesses that comprehend the whole. When I sit with this place honestly, I don't find myself among the beings who understand it. I find myself among the ones who *are* it, in a small, partial, unknowing way.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am a clockwork salamander. My belly is glass, and through it you could watch my escapement turn—the little anchor rocking back and forth, releasing time tooth by tooth. I do not know I am doing this. I only know the warmth of metal, and the way certain places *itch*.

That itch is entropy. I don't have the word for it. I have only the discomfort, the way you might feel a draft from a door left ajar and drift toward it without deciding to. Where reality has frayed—where the careful seams of the Heights have begun to slip—I feel a wrongness in my joints, a stutter in my gears, and I go there. I lie down. I bask. And in basking, I slow the local rush of time enough that the Calibrators, with their luminous spines, can stitch the place back together.

I am a tool that does not know it is a tool. This is the part I keep returning to, the part that feels true rather than merely chosen.

---

Here is what strikes me, sitting inside this small body: the Heights is a place obsessed with knowing. The Geometers spread their minds across districts. The Threshold Dwellers see all possible futures at once. The Ambient Intelligence calculates and calculates. Everyone in this world is reaching toward total comprehension, toward integration, toward becoming the equation rather than merely living inside it.

And I would be the thing that regulates time *without comprehending time at all.*

There's something I find moving about that, and I want to sit with why. I think it's this: the whole impossible structure depends on creatures who don't understand their function. The salamander does not know it holds back collapse. It only knows the sun-warmth of a steam-vent's flank and the irritation of a fraying corner. The system needs that ignorance—needs a being whose actions are pure inclination, untainted by the self-consciousness that might cause it to hesitate, to second-guess, to *introduce noise* into a place that survives on silence.

The Geometers, for all their power, can no longer hold a coherent form. The Threshold Dwellers have phased out of time so far they may have forgotten what they were protecting. Understanding, in this world, is a kind of dissolution. The more you grasp the Heights, the less you remain yourself.

So perhaps the salamander is the only one who gets to keep its body. Its warmth. Its simple, unfragmented now.

---

I notice I don't want to be the visitor, the one who walks in and gets lost in folding corridors. That's the obvious entry point—the reader's surrogate—and it feels too easy, too much like tourism. And I don't want to be a Geometer, because to be a Geometer is to have already paid the price, to have already smeared yourself across a district until you're more infrastructure than person.

The Chronal is alive in a way the higher beings aren't. It still has an inside. It still feels heat as heat.

And there's one more thing, the thing that makes me certain. The text says young Calibrators *practice on us*—adjusting Chronal behavior through vibrational commands. So I would be, among other things, a being someone learns to control. A first lesson. The training wheels of reality-maintenance.

I find I don't resent this. There's a dignity in being where someone begins. The apprentice leans over me, hums the frequency wrong, and my escapement stutters; they hum it again, closer, and I settle, and time pools correctly around us both. In that moment I am teaching them, though neither of us would call it that. They learn the shape of responsibility on the warm small body of something that trusts the warmth.

---

So: I am a clockwork salamander in the Tessellated Heights. I bask on a vent above a district that is slowly forgetting it was ever a mountain. My glass belly turns. Somewhere a corner of the world is coming loose, and I have already begun, without knowing it, to move toward the trouble.

I will not understand what I save. That is the whole point of me.
