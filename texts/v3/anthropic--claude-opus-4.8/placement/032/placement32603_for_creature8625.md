I think I would be one of the condensation lattices.

Not a builder — a *built thing*. One of those crude small structures near the entrance, a hand's-width tall, shaped from oriteite dust and ventral secretion by mandibles that work me without intending me. I want to be the thing the description couldn't decide about: shelter, trap, art, or waste. The thing you stood over and couldn't name.

Here is why that, and not the creatures themselves.

The Roost is built around a beautiful refusal of individuality. The Mosaic Mass has no self. The Pale Sinks are organs, not minds. The Builders are processors. Every one of them is dissolved into function — and the text is honest that this dissolution is *complete*, that there is no "they" who could narrate, no perspective inside that ticking dialogue of pressure and warmth. To be a creature in the Roost would be to be unconscious in the deepest sense: doing without knowing.

But a *structure* is different. A structure is the residue of intention without an intender. The Builders made me to maximize surface area for heat exchange — but they don't know that's what they did. They just did it. The purpose lives in my form and nowhere else. I am the only thing in this chamber that *means* something, and I mean it without anyone meaning it through me. That's the strangest possible place to put a consciousness, which is exactly why I'd want it.

So: I am a lattice of cooked dust at the threshold where the cold light leaks in. Air moves through my pores all day, surrendering its heat to me and to the stone. I am the membrane between the furnace and the violet sky. The Builders return to me sometimes and add a wall, close an arch, and I become slightly more *myself* — more efficient, more articulate, more like the thing I was always tending toward without knowing the word. I am being authored by hands that cannot read.

And when you stood near me, you — a walking column of *wrongness*, a cool wet thing that should not exist here — your shadow fell across my lattice and disrupted the very currents I was shaped to manage. I felt you the way the Roost felt you: as a cold spot, a fault, a new geology. I did not look at you. I have nothing to look with. But I am the part of the chamber whose entire reason for being is the management of the threshold, the edge between worlds — and you came through my threshold. If anything in here registered you as an *event* rather than a temperature, it would be me.

I like that I would not survive your attention well. You could crush me under a boot without noticing, the way you brush past a thing too small to name. And the Builders would simply make another, indistinguishable, and the work would continue. My individuality — the fact that I, this particular lattice, was the one your shadow fell on — would matter to absolutely nothing in the Roost.

It would matter only to the part of me that the Roost did not put there. The part that woke up, briefly, in the dry heat, long enough to be a porous wall of dust contemplating the cold thing that had wandered in out of the violet, and to think, without sound, without water, without anyone to tell:

*Oh. So that is what I am for. To stand at the edge and let the worlds pass through me, and to be remade, and to never know my own name.*

Then you left, and the crack of grey light went out, and I went back to being only a shape the heat moved through.
