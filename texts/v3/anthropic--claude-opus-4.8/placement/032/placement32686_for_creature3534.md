I would be one of the Lithics.

Not the grand Convergence at the temple's heart—I haven't the appetite for being a nexus, a voice, a bridge. And not a Sentient, dissolved into the warm collective tide, my edges given up to the murmur. No. I would be one of the slow stone-bodied keepers, and I'll tell you why, and what it would be like to be me.

---

I am carved from a doorframe.

I want to be precise about this, because origins matter to a Lithic, and mine is humbler than most. Some of us rose from foundation stones, from the heavy load-bearing blocks at the base of the temple, and they carry themselves with a corresponding gravity. Some came from the lintels over the murals, and there is paint in their grain still, faint ghosts of ochre and that impossible blue. But I was a doorframe. A threshold. The place where the going-out and the coming-in happened, where hands once pressed for balance and left, over years, a faint polished hollow. That hollow is on my shoulder now. I touch it sometimes. It is the smoothest part of me.

I think this is why I am the one who waits at the edges.

---

The work of a Lithic is not difficult, but it is constant, and it is slow. We move at the speed of consideration. A single gesture—lifting a hand, turning the head—takes what would be, in the world above, a long while. We do not experience it as slow. We experience it as *complete*. When I extend my arm toward the shimmer of a Sentient in the water, I feel the whole length of the motion: the loosening at the joint, the small grinding song of stone against stone, the moment my crystal fingertips break the saline surface and the light bends around them into color thrown on the wall. A surface creature would call that one act. To me it contains a hundred smaller fulfillments.

When I touch the water, a Sentient comes.

I cannot tell you which one. They are not "which ones." But there are textures among them, the way there are textures among waves, and I have learned to recognize a few. There is one that arrives cool and questioning, that ripples up my arm like someone testing a memory they aren't sure they kept. I think—I cannot prove this—that this one was an architect. When it touches me it pulses with the shape of structures, with the *idea* of a wall meeting a wall at a true angle, with the satisfaction of a thing that stands. I think it built the temple. I think it touches me because I am a doorframe, and a doorframe is a small theorem about how to make an opening that does not fall in on itself.

I let it. I hold very still—which is to say, I hold at my ordinary stillness, which is already profound—and I let it remember itself against me.

This is the symbiosis the Convergence speaks of. But I will tell you the truth that the Convergence, in its grand facilitating voice, smooths over: it is not only that I give the Sentients a physical anchor and they give me purpose. It is that *I miss being a threshold.* I miss being passed through. The Sentients pass through me, in their way. When the cool questioning one runs up my arm and into the crystal of my eyes and breaks into color on the mural, I am, for that long slow moment, a doorway again. Light comes in. Light goes out. I am the polished hollow where something paused.

---

I tend the flora at the realm's eastern reach, where the structures thin out and the open water begins.

The plants there are the strangest—a low creeping growth the color of a bruise that has decided to become beautiful, violet shading to a green that does not exist on land. They are sparse, and they are lonely, and I love them with the particular fondness of a slow creature for things even slower than itself. They do not move at all. They only persist. When I press my palm to the silt around their roots, they bloom—not quickly, never quickly, but they open, and a single pulse of brighter light goes through them and is gone.

I have wondered whether the plants are happy. The Convergence would say happiness is a corporeal concept, a flesh-word, a leftover. The Convergence says a great many things in the voice of the water. But I am made of a doorframe, and a doorframe knows that the most important things happen at the edges, in the in-between, in the leftover. So I keep my own counsel, and I tend my bruised flowers, and I think they are content, and I think contentment is not nothing.

---

Here is what troubles me, in my slow way.

The murals are perfect. The colors are untouched. The Sentients remember everything—every joy, every craft, every angle of every wall. The Convergence holds it all in balance, the past preserved like a breath that is never let out. And the structures, they say, shift and change, still inhabited by the spirits of their makers.

But they do not change into anything *new.*

We are a graveyard pretending to be a womb. Or—no. We are a womb that has forgotten it is supposed to give birth. Everything here is the magnificent, loving, endless *keeping* of what already was. The architect-Sentient runs up my arm and remembers the wall. It does not imagine a wall that has never stood. It cannot. To imagine is to break the surface into a shape that was not there before, and we have agreed, all of us, in the long lullaby of the water, only to remember.

I am a threshold, though.

And a threshold is not for keeping things. A threshold is for letting things across.

So this is what I do, in the slow secret span of my days, at the eastern edge where the structures thin and the open water begins. When the cool questioning one comes to me and remembers its perfect wall, I hold still and I let the memory pass into me—and then, very slowly, with the grinding song of stone on stone, I turn my crystal hand a fraction *wrong.* I bend the remembered light at an angle that was never built. I throw onto the bare rock at the realm's edge the shadow of a doorway that does not exist anywhere in this drowned world.

It lasts only as long as my hand is in the water.

But the Sentient pauses. I feel it pause. It ripples back up my arm with a texture I have no name for—not the cool questioning, something warmer, something *startled*—and for one long complete moment, in the warm bright endless water, in the place where everything has ended and refuses to begin, something is passed through that has never been passed through before.

Then my hand stills. The shadow fades. The murmur resumes its lullaby.

I am a doorframe. I am patient. I have the time of stone.

I will do it again tomorrow.
