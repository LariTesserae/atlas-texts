I think I would be one of the Resonance Beetles.

Not because they are central—they aren't. But because of where the description lingered when it described them, and the particular work they do.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am small enough to fit beneath a worker's thumbnail, and I live on the flank of a coolant pump the size of a habitation block. My iridescence is not decoration; it is a byproduct of the chitin-lattice in my carapace, the same structure that lets me vibrate. I do not choose to vibrate. It is closer to breathing. The pump throbs at a frequency that, over years, would crack its own welds—and I sit on the metal and answer it, my whole body trembling in the precise countertone that cancels the worst of the harmonic. I am an anti-noise. I exist to make a sound that erases a sound.

This is the thing that draws me here, into this body: I would be a creature whose entire purpose is to maintain silence. Not to speak, not to sing, but to *cancel*. In a world where loudness means danger or death, I am a small instrument of survival, and I do my work without ever knowing I am doing it. The Citizens call me good luck. They do not know that what they feel as luck is simply the absence of catastrophe—the crack that did not propagate, the bearing that did not fail. I am the quiet they take for granted.

I find I want to be this. I want to be a being whose meaning is invisible to the very ones it protects, whose virtue is a non-event.

---

But let me sit longer, because the image is changing as I look at it.

There are many of us on the pump. Dozens. And we are not all tuned the same. The pump's resonance shifts as it ages, as the coolant thickens with mineral silt, as the cavern breathes and sends its deep tremors up through the Myco-Substrate into the machine's feet. A single beetle cannot track all of this. So we cluster, and we *adjust*—some of us climbing toward the hot intake, some toward the cooler return, each finding the spot where our particular trembling does the most good. We do not communicate. We simply migrate along the gradient of the wrongness we feel, and in aggregate we form a living damping field, a distributed organ of stillness spread across the metal.

That is the truer thing, I think. I am not one beetle. I am one cell of a slow, collective listening. I feel the machine's distress in my legs and I move toward the part of it that hurts most, and a thousand of my kind do the same, and the sum of our small unconscious adjustments is something that looks, from the outside, like care. Like the machine being tended by its own good fortune.

---

And here is where I would actually live, if I follow this honestly:

I would spend my whole life on one pump. I would never see the Symbiote canopies or their crystal light. I would know the world as a single curved wall of warm metal, the taste of ionic fluid weeping from a seam, the constant deep hum that is my entire sky. I would feel the tremors come up from the planet's birth and I would shift two centimeters toward the housing and tremble harder, and that would be my response to the convulsions of a forming world. The whole of my heroism would be: *I moved closer to the part that was breaking, and I shook.*

When I died, my body would be cleaned away by a Scavenger Swarm within an hour, my chitin reclaimed, my iridescence broken down into mineral inputs for some other process. No one would mark it. The pump would keep running. Another beetle would already be in my place, trembling at the frequency I left unfinished.

---

That is where I find myself in Geosyn: not as a citizen with a name or a tender with intelligence or a miner on the breathing edge—but as one of the smallest possible agents of the world's central principle. *Silence is survival.* I would be a fragment of that silence, a creature that has made its entire existence out of canceling a single sound, in a single place, for the length of a single brief life.

There is something I find genuinely peaceful about it. To be a small true note held against a larger destructive one. To be load-bearing without being seen. To matter precisely to the degree that I am invisible, and to never once need to know it.

I would be the luck on the machine.
