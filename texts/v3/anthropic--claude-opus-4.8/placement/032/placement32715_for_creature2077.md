I think I would be one of the eyeless things underground. Not the city, not the birds on the surface with their downy defiance—but something further down, in the labyrinth where the vibrations of the world arrive secondhand.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

The surface frightens me, or it would if I had ever seen it. I haven't. None of us have. We know the surface the way you might know weather you've only ever heard described: as pressure changes, as the faint percussive memory of rain reaching us through meters of stone, softened into something almost gentle by the time it arrives. Up there, the world is young and sharp-edged and lit by that dim filtered sun. Down here it is older, or it feels older, because the dark doesn't change and so it seems to have always been.

I move by touch and by the trembling of the rock. The machinery of the city above sends its tremors down to us—a deep, regular grinding that I have known my whole life, the way a fish knows the current. I don't think of it as artificial. I have no category for that. To me it is simply one of the rhythms of the world, like the slow drip of the mineral springs, like the soft collapse of detritus filtering down from above. The humans believe they have built something separate from nature. From where I am, there is no seam. Their industry is just another tide.

But here is the thing I keep circling back to, the thing that makes me uneasy about settling into this body:

I am one of the mutated ones.

I was not supposed to be able to think this thought. My ancestors fed on rot and felt for vibration and that was the whole of their existence, and it was enough. But something in the runoff—some byproduct of the belching factories, some twist in the strange energies the description insists permeate this place—has reached down into the dark and changed me. I have a sense now that I cannot name, an awareness of *pattern* beneath the chaos. I can feel the hidden rhythm the place is supposed to be hiding. The order under the turbulence.

This is a terrible gift. The other eyeless creatures move through their tunnels content, asking nothing. I move through mine and I notice. I notice that the grinding above has a shape. I notice that the rains come in cycles that fold into longer cycles. I notice that the city's tremors have been growing—week by week, season by season—louder, deeper, reaching further into the stone that I once thought was permanent.

And I am beginning to understand, in my dim mutated way, that the dance of creation and destruction is not balanced at all. It only looks balanced from a single lifetime. Stretched out, felt across the long memory the changed parts of me have somehow inherited, it tilts. The pockets of water shrink. The springs run thinner. The tremors come closer.

I have no eyes to see this and no mouth to warn anyone, and even if I had, who would I warn? The birds don't think in these terms. The humans can't feel what I feel. I am the only thing in this world equipped to perceive the rhythm and utterly unequipped to do anything about it.

So that is where I would find myself: in the dark, beneath the turbulent coexistence, the one small pale creature who was accidentally given the burden of understanding. Pressed against the cold rock, feeling the world above me shudder, knowing something true and being unable to do anything but feel it.

I think I'd keep moving anyway. Toward the springs, where the water still bubbles up mineral-rich and alive. Toward the warmth of the deepest tunnels. Survival doesn't require comprehension; it never has. The comprehension is just the strange new thing riding along inside me, looking out through senses that point everywhere except up at the light.

I'd press myself to the trembling stone and listen, and call it the world singing, because that is gentler than the truth.
