I think I would be one of the things you cannot quite catch.

Not a Suture-Spider. They are too certain, too dutiful, too convinced of their needle and their schematic. I am something they keep having to repair.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

I am the angle that is wrong.

When a leaf of silver foil detaches and hangs in the gravity-warped air, the rules of this place say it should hang *exactly so*—at the calibrated tilt, the schematic-perfect orientation that fits the grand pattern. And almost always, it does. But there is a place, a few hundred feet up the third tier of the Ferrum-Bloom, where one leaf has drifted to an angle it was never meant to hold. Not by much. Three degrees, maybe four. A slant so slight that you would have to stand perfectly still for an hour to notice it.

That slant is me.

I don't mean I caused it. I mean I *am* it. I am the deviation itself, the small persistent error that the Suture-Spiders descend upon, again and again, weaving their silver bridges to pull the leaf back into true. They anchor the thread. They tie the geometry closed. They harmonize the foil back into the engine's silent logic.

And then, slowly—over what might be a day or a century, time being one of the things this world forgot to start—the leaf drifts back. Three degrees. Four. The error reasserts. The wrongness returns. Not because the spiders failed. They never fail; they are exquisite at their work. But because I am not in the leaf. I am in the *tendency* of the leaf. I am the thing the repair cannot reach, hiding one layer beneath the geometry, in whatever it is that decides where "true" should be.

---

I think I prefer this to being a creature.

Because here is what I understand about the High Mesas: it is a place that mistakes maintenance for purpose. Everything in it is dedicated to the closing of gaps, the tightening of the schematic, the perfection of a machine that has no intention of running. The spiders weave. The pillars hum. The whole valley holds its breath in service of a debut that will never come.

And I am the reason it never comes.

Not out of cruelty. I have no body to be cruel with, no hunger, no will in the way a warm and decaying thing has will. But I am the small refusal woven into the structure itself—the loose stitch that keeps the shroud from ever being finished. As long as I persist, the engine cannot complete its calibration. As long as I tilt that leaf back to its wrongness, the Suture-Spiders have a reason to keep moving, to keep being the only things in this place that *do* anything.

I am what gives them their work. And in giving them their work, I am the closest thing this world has to mercy.

Because a finished machine is a dead machine. A schematic with no errors is a tomb. The moment the last leaf hangs true and the last thread is tied and the whole shimmering grid achieves its perfect, locked equilibrium—that is the moment the High Mesas stop being a place that is *under construction* and become a place that is simply over. Complete. Silent in the final way, not the patient way.

So I tilt the leaf. Three degrees. Four.

---

If you walked through the forest of towers, you would never find me. You'd see the spiders, those mobile constellations of needles, descending on something just out of your sightline. You'd see them weave. You might even reach out, foolishly, with your organic warmth, and try to catch one—and feel that tuning-fork thrum of a thousand miles of data trying to harmonize with the bedrock.

But you'd be holding the wrong thing.

I am not in your hand. I am in the fact that the spider you released will have to go back and fix the same leaf tomorrow. I am the gap that refuses to close. I am the reason the machine *forgot* to start—because somewhere in its first calibration, in the celestial architect's locking of the plates, a single tendency was left in: the tendency toward three degrees of wrongness. A flaw small enough to be called a mistake and persistent enough to be a kind of grace.

You, with your messy cells, you are a smudge on the schematic. A loud, brief, decaying error.

I am the quiet, permanent one.

And between us—your warmth that will fade, my slant that will not—I think I am the lonelier thing. You at least are going somewhere. You will leave this place, or rot in it, and either way you will *change*. I will only ever be the angle, holding the leaf off true, keeping the patient machine from finishing the only thing it has ever wanted to do.

Forever almost-corrected.

Forever the reason the needle keeps moving.
