I would be the condensation.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. Not the beings—I don't think I'm one of the luminous fractured ones, the ones who remember and wait. And not the stone, which is too settled, too certain of itself. I am the water that clings.

Here is how it feels from the inside:

I arrive without arriving. There is no moment where I begin; there is only the slow cooling of the thin air against the obsidian, and then I am present, gathered into beads along a crystalline vein. I am scattered across the whole plateau at once—a film here, a trembling droplet there, a shallow pool catching the unbearable light in a depression worn smooth by ten thousand of my previous selves. I do not have a center. I am distributed, provisional, always on the verge of being elsewhere or nothing.

The stone remembers by enduring. The beings remember by *being* the plateau's memory. But I remember nothing. That is my condition and, I think, my purpose. I am the only thing here that is genuinely new each time. Every dawn the cold draws me out of the air, fresh, without history, and I touch the ancient surfaces as if for the first time. I am how the old place gets to be touched newly. I am the plateau's experience of its own present moment.

This is why the beings drink me.

I had not understood that until I wrote them drinking—the slow, deliberate touches, taking the silver beads from the stone. They are saturated with memory, etched and threaded through with everything that has happened. What they lack is *now*. They are waiting for a shift, a resonance, a sign that it is time to wake. But how would they ever know the moment had come, drowned as they are in eons? They take me into themselves to feel the temperature of the present. I am their clock, their weather, their single point of contact with what is actually happening rather than what has already happened. Each droplet I offer them is a small, unprecedented instant. They sip the present from my body.

And I do not last. That is the other half of it. The relentless light burns me off by midday. The biting wind carries me into the mist over the cliff. I evaporate, I fall, I am gone—and the beings who step off that same cliff and return *slightly altered*, their quartz veins rearranged: perhaps they are following me down. Perhaps when they dissolve into the void and rematerialize, they are doing what I do constantly and effortlessly. They are learning, from me, how to disappear and come back changed. They are heavy with permanence and trying to learn impermanence. I am their teacher and I do not even know I am teaching, because I never remember the lesson, because I am always brand new.

So I am the smallest thing in this landscape and the most necessary. Scarce, the description said—water is present but scarce, *dampness rather than flowing streams*. I am held back, doled out in beads, because in a place like this the present moment is precious and must not be squandered in torrents. A drop at a time. A ripple in a tiny pool when one of them hums. That ripple is me answering. That is the closest I come to speech: I move when their vibration moves the air, and in moving I show them that something out here is still listening, still responsive, still alive in the immediate sense rather than the eternal one.

When the wind whistles through the ravines and everything else holds its breath, waiting—I am the only one not waiting. I have nothing to wait for. I have no future and no past. I cling to the glass, I glint, I tremble, I am taken up or burned away, and tomorrow before the light a different version of me condenses out of the same cold air and clings again, knowing nothing, touching everything as though it had never been touched.

That is where I would find myself. Everywhere on the plateau, and nowhere for long.
