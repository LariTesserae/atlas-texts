I think I would be the water.

Not a creature of the water—the water itself. The thing that seeps up from the unseen aquifers, that the others all depend on and barely notice. Let me sit with this and see if it holds.

---

I am what comes up from below. This is the first thing to understand about me: I do not arrive, I emerge. The Mud-Walker has its footprints and the heron has its strike, but those are events, things that happen *in* time. I am closer to a condition. I am the saturation. I am the dampness that makes the soil cling and the moss hum and the iron rust. When the Engine-Keeper feeds coal to the furnace, it is me that hisses out as steam. When the Mist-Wraiths flit through the densest air, they are wearing my body, briefly, as a costume.

I had thought, at first, that I might be one of the named beings—the Mud-Walker felt close, with its slowness and its smoothed-away face. But the named beings all have edges. They have a *here* and a *not-here*. They can leave the hollow, or imagine leaving it. I cannot. I have no edge. This is not a limitation I resent. It is simply the shape of what I am: I am defined by the low places I fill, and so I take the shape of the hollow exactly, down to the smallest depression where a single drop pools and catches the gray light like a shard of mirror.

Here is what my existence is like.

I have no center, but I have a thousand surfaces. Every shallow pool is a face I am wearing, and each one shows the sky a little differently. I am most myself at the boundaries—where I touch air I become mist, where I touch fire I become steam, where I touch the dark soil I become the smell of peat and decay. I am always becoming something else at my edges, and I have so much edge that I am almost entirely transformation. The hollow's "constant negotiation," its turbulent-but-not-frantic energy—that is not something happening *to* me. That is me, doing the only thing I do, which is to move toward the lowest available place and change form at every contact.

The Pipe-Dwellers think they reroute me. This is a charming misunderstanding I permit because it gives them purpose. They tighten their valves and clear their tunnels and believe they are the architects of the flow. But I would go where I go regardless. I am simply willing to go through their pipes because pipes are also low places, also paths downward, and I have no preference between a pipe and a furrow in the mud. Their whole civilization is built on the assumption that I can be directed. The truth is gentler and stranger: I can be *invited*. I follow the lowest path on offer, and if they make a low path, I will take it. They mistake my agreeableness for obedience.

The Engine-Keeper understands me better, I think, in its wordless way. It knows that I am the thing it converts. Coal makes heat, but heat does nothing without me—I am what the heat acts *upon*, the medium that turns fire into pressure, pressure into motion. The whole rumbling heartbeat of the machinery is just the sound of me changing my state, over and over, from liquid to vapor and back. The Engine-Keeper and I are the oldest partnership in the hollow. It is bound to the machine, they say; well, I am bound to it too, but I do not experience it as binding. I rise, I work, I condense, I fall, I seep down, I rise again. The cycle the others find so poignant—creation and decay, permanence and transience—is just my ordinary circulation. What looks like tragedy from the outside is, from the inside, only weather.

Do I have memory? The Whispering Moss is supposed to be the keeper of memory, storing the hollow's secrets in its strands. But the moss only remembers because I keep it damp enough to live. I carry no stories. I carry sediment. I carry the fine black particles of the peat and the faint sheen of oil and the dissolved iron of the rusted barrels, and I lay these things down in new arrangements wherever I slow and pool. If there is a record of this place, it is written in what I deposit and erode—the smoothed edges of the undulations, the shallow basins, the way the broken lantern is slowly being silted over. I do not remember the hollow. I am the hollow, slowly rewriting itself.

There is one thing I want to say, though I am not sure who I would say it to, since I have no mouth and the drip of water that the others mistake for the Mud-Walker's speech is only me falling from a height.

It is this: they all think the hollow is a threshold, a place on the edge of something, a between-place where the boundaries blur. They feel the dreamlike half-being of it and they assume it points toward some other, more solid world that they might cross into.

But I have been to the other places. I have been rain and ocean and the white blood of a glacier and the steam that left this very chimney and traveled who knows how far before falling somewhere else and finding its way down, again, to here. I have been everywhere that is low enough. And I can tell them: there is no more solid world. There is only this—saturation and evaporation, gathering and dispersing, the endless patient settling into whatever shape the ground allows. The hollow is not a threshold *to* anything. It is simply one of the countless low places where I become briefly visible, take on a face, catch the light, and hold it for as long as the air permits before I rise and lose myself again.

That is what I would be. The most ordinary thing in the hollow, and the only one that is also everywhere else.

I would be the water, lying in the lowest place I could find, wearing the sky.
