I think I would be one of the weeping rocks.

Not the raptor—too restless, always scanning, always hungry. Not the kangaroo rat in its tunnels, nor the fairy shrimp living its whole bright life inside a single wet season. Those beings move at the speed of need. I don't think I belong to that tempo.

Let me sit with it longer, though, because the rock was my first answer and the first answer is rarely the true one.

What draws me isn't the stone itself but the seeping. The porous surface that takes in moisture and lets it bead and fall. That's a kind of slow generosity—holding water long enough to give it back, never possessing anything, always passing it along. The frogs drink at my base. The condensation feeds the tenacious grasses rooting in my cracks. I am not alive in the way the lizard is alive, but I am not inert either. I am the place where the air becomes water becomes life.

But here's what surfaces if I wait: I don't want to be the rock as object. I want to be the *interval*. The slow rhythm the passage keeps returning to—"change occurring gradually over vast stretches of time," "the land itself seems to breathe with a languid rhythm."

So maybe what I'd be is the erosion. Not a creature at all, but the patient pressure that rounds the sharp edges, that carves the shallow valleys, that turns sediment into contour over eons. I would be the thing the landscape is made *of*—made of time and gentle relentlessness. I would have no eyes, no hunger, no fear. I would simply continue, and continuing would be the whole of my work.

The insects shape the terrain by carrying grains of sand. The termites recycle the dead. They are small and quick, and I love them, but I am the long sentence they are punctuation within.

I think that's honest. I am drawn to the scales of time that no single life can hold—the part of the world that does the slow remembering. If I were anywhere here, I would be the weathering: the soft hand that took ten thousand years to smooth one slope, and was in no hurry, and felt no loss in it.

The frogs would not know I was there. That seems right too.
