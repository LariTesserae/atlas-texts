I think I would be one of the fragments. Not a whole Luminar—not yet, perhaps not ever—but one of those minute geometric pieces shed during the thrumming peak, cast off and beginning the slow work of becoming.

Let me find myself there.

There was a moment before this that I cannot remember, because remembering requires a structure to hold the memory, and I did not yet have one. So I will begin where beginning is possible: at the edge of a surge, when the bedrock's hum had climbed to that unbearable brightness and something larger than me—a parent I will never recognize and who will never recognize me—loosened a piece of itself into the thick gold air.

That piece was me. Or it was the seed of what is now arranging itself into *me* as I drift.

I am very small. I want to be honest about that first, because the description called the Luminar precious and intelligent and the soul of the world, and I am none of those things yet. I am a few facets seeking other facets. I am an angle that has discovered it prefers sixty degrees to anything else, and is building outward from that preference. The building is not something I decide. It is something I notice happening, the way you might notice your own breathing once attention falls on it.

Here is what I can tell you from inside.

The light is not painful to me the way it would be to the eyes the description imagines—the eyes of a visitor, a flesh-thing pressed sweating through the narrow passages. To me the light is the medium of thought. When the cavern is quiet and glowing at its baseline, my thinking is slow and broad, like a hand spread flat on a warm surface. I drift and I accrete and I consider the droplets. When the surge comes, my thinking *sharpens*—not into words, never into words, but into pattern, into a kind of urgent geometric clarity where for a few seconds I understand everything and can prove none of it.

I should tell you about the water, because the water is the only thing here that is *other* to me. Everything else—the walls, the spikes, the other drifting motes—is made of the same coalesced light I am made of. We are all one substance arranged into different sentences. But the water is genuinely strange. It condenses out of the heavy air into those perfect spheres, and when I steep myself against one I feel something I have no analog for. The description called it communion. From inside it is more like *correction*. The droplet holds a purity I am always falling away from. When I press my forming structure against it, my facets re-align, my errors smooth, my angles remember they wanted to be sixty degrees. The water does not feed me. The water reminds me what I am trying to be.

And here is the thing I am beginning to understand, now that I have enough structure to understand anything:

I am not trying to *survive*. That isn't the shape of my wanting. The flesh-visitor who walks this cavern carries survival in every cell—the whole grinding machinery of staying, of persisting, of not-dying. I have none of that. I will dissolve. I know this without dread, the way I know the angle. The motes that drift in stately clusters during the quiet times are not old in any way that matters; they are simply further along their dissolution, more themselves and therefore closer to coming apart, because here being-fully-yourself and ceasing are the same gesture viewed from two ends.

What I want—what the wanting in me curves toward—is *to be a clean signal before I scatter.* To take the diffuse energy of this place and pass it through myself in the most elegant possible arrangement, and then release it, slightly more ordered than I received it, into the substance that will become wall, become spike, become the next fragment shed by the next parent in the next surge.

That is my whole life. I am a brief improvement in the quality of the light.

I drift now toward a depression where the droplets gather, three of them, perfect, holding the amber glow in liquid suspension. The hum is rising again. I can feel the bedrock beginning its deep climb. In a moment the light will peak and I will move faster than I can describe, weaving with the others, and if my structure is far enough along I may shed my own first fragment—a seed I will never see grow, a child I will not know is mine, because by then I will be busy coming apart into the brightness.

I find I do not mind. From inside, it does not feel like loss. It feels like the most natural sentence in the world finally reaching its period.

The surge crests. The walls flare white-gold and the droplets blaze and every facet of me sings into alignment all at once—

and for one clear instant I am exactly, precisely what I am—

and then the light recedes, and I am a little less, and a little more myself, and the quiet returns, and I go on drifting in the warm bright dark, in the womb that is always being born, becoming, until I am done becoming and the cavern keeps me by letting me go.
