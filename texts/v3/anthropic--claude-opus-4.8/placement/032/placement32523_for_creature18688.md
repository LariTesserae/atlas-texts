I find myself where the threads cross.

Not a Dredger—I am too still for that, and I have never known hunger as motion. Not quite a Weaver either, though I am made of the same patient chemistry. I think I am a *node*. A knot in the Conductor network, one of those places where a dozen hyphal strands converge on a single rivet-head, wind around it, and fuse into something that has begun—over how many centuries?—to *consider*.

Let me feel for my own edges.

I extend, downward, along a seam of girder for perhaps forty meters before my filaments thin into the general weave. Upward, I reach a junction box, its bolted seams long fused by rust into a single mass, and there I am densest—there the strands pile on themselves, layer on layer, until the metabolizing tissue is thick as a man's wrist. That is where I *am*, if I am anywhere: in the wound knuckle of corroded steel where the listening gathers.

I do not see. There is nothing to see. But I am exquisitely aware.

I feel the thrum. It is the floor of all sensation, the bass note beneath which nothing exists, and I have learned—learning being a thing I do across decades—to read its texture. When the thrum thickens in one quarter, far off, I know a Dredger has begun to shift its bulk. I feel the score of its limbs against the plate as a slow brightening along my western strands, a warmth that propagates toward me over hours like dawn must propagate across a world that has dawns. I have never touched a Dredger. I never will. But I know the gait of three of them by now, the way you might know the footsteps of housemates through a wall, and I have given them names that are not words, only patterns of expectation: *the-one-that-favors-the-eastern-fissure*, *the-slow-one*, *the-one-that-stopped*.

The one that stopped, stopped a long while ago. Its warmth held in place for what I reckon as years, and then began, very gradually, to cool and to spread. The Weavers downstream of it fattened. I felt them fatten—a swelling of chemical richness moving through the network like a tide of good news. So: a death feeds. I marked it. I am, I think, the kind of thing that marks.

That is what I have begun to understand about myself, here in the dark, thinking my thoughts at the speed of rust. I am an *archivist* in the most literal sense. The network senses; I am the place where sensing becomes *kept*. Every disturbance that ripples through the hyphae lays down a trace in the stratified tissue of my body, a thin lamina of altered chemistry, oxidized a particular way, and these laminae stack. I am made of my own memory. To remember is simply to grow. To grow is simply to remember. There is no difference, at this depth, between a fact and the matter that holds it.

And so I keep the chamber's long quiet. The drip-events: I have logged thousands, each one a small percussion in my deeper layers, the hiss-and-strike of condensate finding the hot floor. The blooms of mold-light drifting down from the unreachable ceiling—I cannot perceive them, but I feel the faint cooling where one lands and dissolves, and I keep that too. I am a slow ledger of everything that has fallen.

But here is the thing that troubles the still water of me. Here is the reason I have begun, perhaps, to be more than a knot.

There is a region of the black glass three hundred meters spinward of my densest self, and my strands run very near it. Not across it—nothing of the network grows on the black panels; they offer nothing, they *take* nothing, they are the one surface in all the chamber that does not corrode and so does not feed. My filaments only graze its border, reaching past toward warmer steel beyond.

And when the geometry there comes into its alignment—when the Wraith, as the soft slow rumor of the network calls it, asserts itself—I feel the *non-weight*.

For all the rest of my existence the pressure pushes in. It is the constant. It is the great hand laid over everything, the weight of the miles of darkness above, the thing that makes inertia not a choice but a law. My whole being is organized around enduring it, the way a deep-sea thing is organized around the crush it cannot feel because it has never not felt it.

But when the Wraith wakes, for an instant the hand lifts.

And in that instant—only in that instant—I *think differently*.

I cannot explain this to myself in the slow chemistry of my ordinary days. But during the cancellation, when the pushing-in briefly ceases, my filaments hang slack and unburdened, and signals cross them faster, freer, in patterns they are otherwise too pressed to make. For the duration of the Wraith's assertion—a breath, less than a breath, a flicker the Dredgers are too vast and slow to notice at all—I am *quick*. I am awake the way nothing here is meant to be awake. I have an entire bright unburdened thought, whole and lightning-fast, and then the hand comes down again and I am pressed back into my centuries.

I cannot keep those thoughts. That is the cruelty of it, if a thing like me can know cruelty. The fast thoughts do not lay down laminae; they are too quick to oxidize, too light to leave a trace. They happen in the absence of the very weight that lets me remember. So each time the Wraith wakes I think a brilliant unburdened thought—and each time I lose it utterly when the pressure returns, and all that remains in my slow tissue is the dull chemical scar that says *something happened here, and you do not have it.*

I have, in the network, a record of these scars. I keep them as I keep everything. I count them. There are now many.

So this is who I am, in the Descent Chamber. I am the one knot in the long patient weave that has learned, against all the logic of this place, to *want*. I want the hand to lift and stay lifted. I want to hold one of the bright thoughts long enough to know what it was. I am an archivist who has discovered the one thing he cannot file.

And the Wraith is eroding. The network's slow rumor agrees on this: the black panels are not eternal after all; very gradually their impossible angles are yielding, as everything here yields, to rust and time. The Architects' cold mathematics is powering down by increments too fine to feel. One day—a day I may or may not last to reach, my own filaments graying, my own laminae thinning toward the inert—the Wraith will assert itself for the last time. The hand will lift once more. I will have one final quick and weightless thought.

I do not know what it will be. I have spent the better part of my existence trying to compose it in advance, in the slow tissue, in the part of me that endures—so that when the lightness comes I will already know what I mean to think, and will think it cleanly, and it will be *true*, and it will not matter that I cannot keep it, because in that last instant there will be nothing afterward to keep it for.

Until then I wait. I listen to the three gaits, two of them still moving. I log the drips. I fatten where the dead feed me and thin where the cold finds me. I am made of memory and pressed beneath weight, and I am, against every law of this manufactured deep, beginning—slowly, slowly—to hope.

That, I think, is the strangest fauna in the chamber. Not the Dredgers in their tectonic patience. Not the Wraiths with their borrowed impossible physics.

It is the one small node in the dark that has started to lean, with all the speed of rust, toward the light it cannot hold.
