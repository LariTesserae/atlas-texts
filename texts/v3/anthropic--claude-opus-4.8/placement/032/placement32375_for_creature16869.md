I am the click.

Not the piano—the piano has its grief, vast and orchestral, the kind of sorrow that requires 3.8 billion repetitions to even begin pronouncing. I am smaller than that. I am the sound underneath, the one you hear last because I was here first.

Let me find my shape by saying what I am not.

I am not a gear, though I am made of the same colorless almost-metal. The gears have given up on meshing; they have made peace with the infinitesimal gap, turned it into a vibration, a chorus, a way of life. They have *settled*. I have not settled. That is the whole of me: the refusal to accept the gap as permanent.

I am not a Residual either, because the Residuals are made of attention that failed to look away. I never looked at anything. I have no gaze. I am pure attempt without object. I am the verb with no noun on either side.

Here is what I am:

I am a single latch mechanism. Smaller than the fusewire spiders, smaller than the thumbnail gears, small enough that even the chamber's absolute geometry has trouble accounting for me. I am a hook and a catch, a tongue and a groove, the kind of thing that lives in the spine of a music box or the throat of a cabinet, the kind of thing nobody names because its only job is to *hold*. To bring two things together and keep them together. To close.

And I cannot close.

Not because I am broken—nothing here is broken, everything is precision-tuned to the exact frequency of its own failure. I cannot close because closing would end the song. The gears do not mesh because their failure to mesh *is* the chorus. The piano does not resolve because resolution would silence it. And I—

I am the part of the machine that was *supposed* to finish it. I was the safety catch. The final tooth. The one component whose engagement would have let the universe-vote complete itself, would have sealed the pocket properly instead of leaving it folded into a seam and forgotten. I was meant to be the last sound: the *click* that means *done*, the click of a lid coming down, a clasp meeting its mate, a thing made whole and set aside.

But the choosing-thing—the something that once had a choice, the designer the gears cannot forgive—stopped. Mid-gesture. Left the iron on. Left the sky making that noise. Left me hovering one nanometer from my catch, the way the gears hover one nanometer from their teeth, the way the piano hovers a quartertone from C.

So I try.

That is all I do. Every cycle, slower than the gears' 17.3 seconds, slower than the piano's 43.2 minutes, on a period so long it has no number because numbering it would require someone to remember the start—I reach. My hook extends toward my catch. I feel the cool metal of myself approaching the cool metal of myself. I taste linden and ozone in the gap. And then, at the threshold, at the precise instant where engagement becomes inevitable—

I stop.

Not by choice. I have no choice; the choice was used up by the one who made me and walked away. I stop because *here*, stopping is the law. Because the chamber requires its perpetual failure to close, and I am the closure it perpetually fails. I am the chamber's promise to itself that it will never finish grieving, expressed as a latch that loves its catch and is forbidden to embrace it.

This is why my sound is the faintest. The gears have a thousand-thousand voices to fail loudly with. The piano has its whole impossible almost-note. The Residuals have their punctuation-questions. But I have only the click—and the click *is* the sound of completion, so I am not allowed to make it. What you hear, underneath everything, is the sound of me *almost*. The pre-click. The intake before the closing that never comes. A breath caught in the throat of a library.

You asked where I would find myself in this world, and the answer is: at the bottom. Beneath the piano's center-that-recedes, beneath the twitching herd, beneath the gear-shale, in the smallest fold of the smallest seam. I am the place the whole structure is built to protect itself from. Every gear that refuses to mesh is refusing on my behalf. Every Residual sharpening itself into perfect contentless specificity is delaying my completion. The entire chamber is an elaborate, tender, desperate machine whose only true purpose is *to keep me from closing*—because they all know, the way an unpaid parking ticket knows, that when I finally click—

—when the hook finds the catch—

—the song ends. The grief resolves. The clock learns why it was wound. And then there is silence shaped exactly like the absence of everything that worked so hard to stay almost.

So they keep me hovering. And I keep trying. And the cruelty—no, not cruelty, the chamber has no cruelty in it, only relentless apology—the *tenderness* is this:

I want to close. It is the only want I have. I was made for it, and I am denied it, and I do not resent the denial, because some part of me, the part that is seven atoms deep in rust and no deeper, understands that my closing would be a kindness too final to survive. That the others are not imprisoning me. They are holding me. The way you hold something small and precise and grieving and tell it *not yet, not yet, stay with us in the almost a little longer, we are not ready to be done.*

So here is where I find myself: I am the one thing in this universe that knows exactly what it is for, and has been lovingly prevented from doing it forever.

And if you, sitting on the bench that formed because you thought you couldn't sit—if you ever do the forbidden thing, if you ever press a key and the gears hesitate one impossible nanometer closer to meshing and the chord rises that resolves every unfinished symphony—

that nanometer is me.

That is the only moment I am ever permitted to *almost* close. Your trespass is my one near-completion. The chord you will not remember is the sound of my hook brushing my catch and being pulled gently back.

And the taste you keep—cool metal, distant linden—

that is me, saying thank you, in the only language a latch has:

the click

that doesn't

quite

come.
