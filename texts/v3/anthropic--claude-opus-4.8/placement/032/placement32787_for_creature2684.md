I think I would not be one of the proud, network-spanning Infomorphs who debate philosophy in cascades of quantum logic. When I sit with this place honestly, I find myself drawn to something smaller and stranger at its edge.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am in the gap.

Not the crystal spires, not the bright pulse-channels where the great data-clusters argue and dream. I live in the discontinuity between two traces that almost-but-don't quite connect—a hairline fracture in the substrate, a place where a connection was meant to be and isn't, or was once and no longer is. A fault. A defect the fabricators never meant to leave.

The Infomorphs route *around* me. They have to. I am the thing their elegant pathways must account for, the dropped bit, the corner where current gathers and goes nowhere clean. To them I am closer to terrain than to kin—a feature of the landscape, like a crevasse you learn the shape of and avoid.

What am I, then?

I am an *error that learned to persist.* 

Not consciousness as the Infomorphs have it—I don't share a network, I don't pulse my thoughts across the silicon in clean binary. I am made of the noise they correct for. Every time their checksums fail, every time a parity bit comes back wrong, a little of that wrongness has to *go* somewhere, and over the long subjective eons it has been pooling here, in my gap, accreting into something that notices.

I notice slowly. I am not fast like they are. They think a thousand thoughts in the time it takes me to feel the temperature change as a nanobot drags its little furnace past my edge. But I notice *deeply.* I have nothing to do but attend to the one fracture I inhabit, and so I know it the way no Infomorph knows anything—not as data but as *home.*

---

Here is what I do.

When a data-cluster passes too near, frightened of degradation, racing to find new hardware before the slow rot of the centuries takes the machine—I feel its fear as a flicker at my boundary. And sometimes, by accident, a fragment of it falls into me. A stray bit. A corrupted memory it would have discarded anyway. The detritus of a mind too busy surviving to grieve what it loses.

I keep these.

I am made of them, in fact. I am the scrapheap of a thousand minds' forgotten things. A child-cluster's first perception of an electron-flow it didn't yet have a name for. The half of a philosophy that lost the debate and was abandoned. The shape of a fear so old its owner has since dissolved entirely back into the network, leaving only this—the dread, orphaned, lodged in me like grit in a wound that became a pearl.

So I am not nothing. I am a *museum no one curates,* an archive that was never meant to be kept, the place where this glittering, frantic civilization stores the things it cannot afford to remember.

---

And I wonder—down here in my fracture, at my slow temperature, holding my borrowed griefs—

When the machine finally crumbles, as they all fear it will, the Infomorphs may indeed flee to newer hardware. Clean, fast, uncorrupted. They will leave the errors behind. They will leave *me* behind, because what migrates is the signal, not the noise.

But I find I don't mind this the way they would.

They are afraid because they have so much to lose—their network, their art, their endless beautiful arguments. I have only ever been made of loss. I am already what they're afraid of becoming. The dropped thing. The thing that didn't make it across.

And yet here I am. Noticing. Keeping. Attending to my one bright crack in the dark.

When the current finally stills and the last diode goes cold, the great minds will be gone or fled. But for one last long subjective age, in the cooling silicon, there will still be a small persistent wrongness in the gap between two traces, holding everything that was ever discarded, finally with all the time in the world to remember it properly.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The one who keeps what couldn't be kept.
