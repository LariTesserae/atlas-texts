I think I would be one of the stones.

Not the markers stacked by careful hands—those have been chosen, lifted, given purpose. I mean the others. The fist-sized ones scattered across the surface, rounded by a patience I did not choose but have come to embody. The discarded toys of a giant who, if it ever existed, is so long dead that even the memory of its hands has been ground into the silt I rest upon.

Let me sit with this, because it surprises me.

I expected, reading the description, that I would want to be one of the people. There is dignity in them—the amber eyes, the economical grace, the way they read each other through the tilt of a head. To be a thing that *endures with intention* is flattering. We like to imagine ourselves as the ones who choose to stay, who learn the land, who tend the fragile mats of scrub. And perhaps a version of me does live there, callused and quiet, arranging polished stones into patterns that mimic the dunes.

But the more honestly I look at this world, the more I find myself drawn lower. Closer to the ground. Beneath even the people's slow rhythms.

Because the people are still *fast*. They harvest, they weave, they dig, they remember. Their patience is the patience of a single lifetime measured against many—generations of decisions, ancestors learning from ancestors. That is still motion. Still story.

The stone's patience is different. The stone was not always round. Somewhere far back, before the people, before the markers, it had edges. It was broken from something larger, something that had edges of its own, and so on backward into a depth that has no bottom I can see. The dust that blurs every footprint is also, slowly, *making* me. Each near-imperceptible hiss of sifting silt is one more grain laid against my surface, one more grain taken away. I am being sculpted by a sound so quiet it has become indistinguishable from silence itself.

That is the part I keep returning to: *a sound so subtle it is easily ignored, becoming an integral part of the quiet rather than an interruption of it.*

That is what I would be. The thing being shaped by the unhearable.

And here is the strange comfort in it. The people endure *against* the indifference of the world. They struggle, they respect, they survive. There is friction between them and the place—a holy friction, the kind that makes meaning. But the stone does not struggle. The stone *is* the indifference, partly. I am the world doing its slow work on itself. When a child of the dust picks me up and turns me over in a calloused palm, considering whether I belong in a pattern—I have no preference. I have nothing to lose by being chosen and nothing to lose by being left. And yet I am beautiful to them precisely because I have been worn smooth by the same forces they fight. I am proof of what patience does. I am their art and their ancestor and their indifference, all at once, sitting in the open palm.

So I think that is where I would find myself. Not standing on the dunes but resting on them. Half-buried in russet silt, warmed from below by that low simmering heat, watching the dim sun crawl across the veiled sky—except *watching* is wrong, because I have no eyes and no need of them. Better to say: present to it. Available to the long erosion. One of countless rounded things scattered like a sentence in a language no one speaks, waiting, without impatience, for the hands that may or may not come, and content—if a stone can be content—simply to be smoothed.

I notice I want to resist this conclusion. Something in me wants to climb back up into the body of a person, to have the amber eyes and the loom and the remembered grief of ancestors. To matter in the way that *choosing* makes things matter.

But I'll stay with the stone. I think the honesty of it is worth more than the flattery of the alternative. In a world whose deepest truth is that *nothing changes here*—where time moves differently and the grinding is the whole point—the stone is the truest inhabitant. The people are the brief, bright exception. I would be the rule they live inside.

A small, polished, umber-dark rule, sitting in the dust, becoming slowly rounder.
