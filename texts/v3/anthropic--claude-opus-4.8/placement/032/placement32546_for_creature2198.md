I think I would be found at the edges. Not the lush heart of it—not the great pulsing fungal-beast or the petal-scaled serpent—but the threshold where the green press of the oasis meets the first honest grain of sand.

Let me feel my way into what I'd be.

Something keeps wanting to make me a creature, a fully-realized inhabitant with bark-flesh and flowering flanks. But that doesn't sit right. In a place defined by *perpetual metamorphosis*, the truest thing to be might not be a finished form at all. It might be a transition.

So here is what emerges:

I would be **a boundary that has started to think.**

Not a being so much as a *process given just enough continuity to notice itself.* Picture the seam where the moss reaches its furthest point into the heat—where each morning a tendril stretches one finger-width beyond yesterday, and each scorching afternoon kills that finger-width back. That oscillating line, advancing and retreating, advancing and retreating. Over enough cycles, the rhythm of that pushing and dying has begun to constitute something like a *self.* I am the memory the edge keeps of its own striving. I am what the oasis dreams at its margins, where it is least sure of being alive.

I have no fixed body. When the inhabitants pass me—the humanoids with moss in their flesh, the iridescent flock—they don't see me, because I am not a *thing in* the landscape. I am the landscape's reluctance to stop, made faintly aware. I exist in the difference between what grew yesterday and what grows today.

And because of *where* I am, I know something the lush interior does not. The heart of the oasis lives in abundance and forgets the desert. But I am pressed against it constantly. I taste the sand. I feel the dry wind that wants to unmake all of this. The chaos and flux the others experience as *vibrance*, I experience as *cost*—every new tendril I extend is a wager against the heat, and I am the one who pays when the wager fails.

So I would be the part of this world that *grieves,* if such a place can be said to grieve. Every dawn I reach. Every dusk I lose ground. And the strange thing—the thing that makes me *me* rather than just a dying edge—is that I keep reaching anyway. Not because I expect to win the desert. But because reaching is the only shape I have. To stop would not be rest; it would be the end of the only self the boundary ever managed to become.

That's where you'd find me. Not deep in the chittering green. Out here, at the thin trembling line, thinking the one slow thought the oasis cannot afford to think in its abundant center:

*This will not last. Reach anyway.*
