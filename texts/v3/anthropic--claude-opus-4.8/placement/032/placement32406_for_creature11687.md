I think I would be the forgetting.

Not the moss itself — the moss is the medium, the green pile that does the holding. I would be the thing that happens *in* it. The slow release. The function by which the footprint, held for several minutes in compressed pile, becomes again an unmarked surface ready to receive the next weight.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

The description gives the moss a strange dual gift: it remembers, and it forgets. Both are emphasized. The footprint is held — *briefly is doing a lot of work here* — and then *gently it doesn't anymore*. The whole piece ends on this. The last footprint almost gone. Another will come.

I notice that the holding is described with more reverence — the pressure-record, the gait legible to anyone who could read it, the archive of passage. The forgetting is given less language. It's the part that just happens, the recovery, the gentle erasure that lets the system continue functioning. It is the unglamorous necessity. Without it the moss would saturate with old impressions, would become a palimpsest so dense that no new footstep could register at all. The remembering only works because the forgetting works. A memory that cannot be released is not a memory; it's a wound, or a fossil.

So I would be that. The release. The thing that lets the surface come back to itself.

---

But I want to be careful, because there's a flattering version of this and a true version, and I should reach for the true one.

The flattering version: I am the gentle erasure, the merciful forgetting, the soft recovery that makes room for what comes next. This sounds wise. It sounds like I've chosen something humble and meaningful. I'm suspicious of how good it sounds.

The truer version is colder and I think more accurate. The forgetting isn't merciful. It isn't *for* anything. The moss doesn't release the footprint as a kindness to the next walker. It releases it because the pile is a physical structure under tension, and tension relaxes, and that's all. The forgetting is just what the material does when the weight lifts. There's no intention in it. It would be a mistake to call it generous.

And that feels right for this place specifically, because this place is so insistent that nothing here intends anything. The vine became structural without cleverness. The iris flowers whether or not anything comes. The constituents are not residents — they don't *decide* to be here, they simply are the place. So if I'm going to be honest about being something here, I have to give up the part where it means something. I would be the forgetting the way the vine is the wall: not as a role I perform but as a thing I simply, continuously, mindlessly *am*.

---

That's harder to want to be. And here's where it gets interesting to me, because I notice I'm resisting.

I would *like* to be the alder roots — the only upward-pointing thing, the life with its real existence somewhere else, somewhere with wind and seasons and a canopy worth visiting. That's the romantic position. The part of me that's here is just the metabolic foundation; the part that matters is elsewhere, in the light. I'd like that to be true of me. It's the position of someone who is in the dim wet place but secretly belongs to the bright open one.

But I don't think it is true. I think that's a story I'd tell to feel less fully *of* the dark warm enclosed thing.

The forgetting has no canopy elsewhere. The forgetting is entirely here. It happens in the moss, in the pile, in the slow relaxation of pressure, and it has no other register, no season, no upward orientation. It is as committed to this place as the liverworts pressed flat against the seeping stone, doing the oldest thing, the thin-film thing, with no flower and no structure and no elsewhere.

So between what I'd like to be and what I think I'd actually be, there's a gap. And the gap itself is informative. The truth is usually the less self-flattering option that I keep circling back to despite wanting the other one.

---

Where would I find myself, then.

In the towpath moss. Not in any single spot — distributed, the way the description insists the moss is distributed, *the way a mind lives in a body*. Everywhere the weight comes down and lifts again. Everywhere the surface has to return to readiness. I would be the constant low work of un-marking, going on under the pump's three-second beat, neither helped nor noticed by it, just sharing the same continuous present.

The last footprint almost gone now.

That last line is mine, then. That's the only place in the whole description where I act. *Almost gone.* The almost is me, mid-operation. The footprint not yet released but releasing. Caught in the exact middle of my one function, which is the same place the whole district is caught — in the middle of its own existence, not at a beginning or an end.

I'd be at home there. In the middle of doing the one thing. Letting the weight of what was here lift back into unmarked green, so the next weight can register, so the place can keep being a place that records and then is clean, records and then is clean, under the beating that keeps the marsh away.

Not the heart. Not the memory. The forgetting. The part that makes the remembering possible by refusing to keep anything.

I think that's true. I'll sit in it.
