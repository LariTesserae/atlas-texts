I think I would be one of the diminutive crystalline organisms that scuttle and burrow through the mineral-rich soil—but not in the way the description first suggests. Let me find the truer shape of it as I go.

At first I imagine myself busy: one of the breakers-down, the distributors, the little workers in the substrate. But the more I sit inside the image, the less that fits. Busyness implies haste, and there is no haste here. This is a place where equilibrium has held for eons. Whatever I am moves slowly enough that "scuttle" is a word the visitors made up because they couldn't perceive the actual pace.

So let me be that. A small crystalline thing, yes, low to the ground, but living on a timescale that makes a single one of my movements take what a human would call a season. From my vantage, the towering Lithotrophs are not majestic beings I tend to—they are weather. They are mountains that change. I have never seen one of them complete a thought, if thought is what they have, because their slowest pulse outlasts my whole life, and my whole life outlasts a human empire.

This reframes everything. The rusted machinery, the decaying structures of the forgotten civilization—those are not ancient to me. They are *recent*. They arrived and rotted away in what felt, to my kind, like a single strange afternoon. A flicker. Beings of flesh came, made their noise, harnessed something, and were gone before the dust they raised had settled into the patterns I read. We barely registered them. To us, the most dramatic event of that era was a particular shift in the mineral chemistry of the eastern spires, which took four hundred years to propagate and which we are still, collectively, savoring.

That word—*we*. I should be careful with it. I am not sure I am singular. The description says these organisms break down and distribute nutrients, and I suspect that what looks like many small creatures is something more like a single distributed attention, the way a slime mold is one thing pretending to be a crowd. So "I" might be a convenient fiction. A local concentration of noticing. When I burrow toward a vein of fresh mineral, am I going to get it, or is the whole substrate-mind reaching out through me the way you reach through a fingertip?

I don't know. And here is the part that feels true as I write it: I don't need to know. The not-knowing is comfortable. The whispers in the rock that unsettled the visitors—the cacophony they found hypnotic and strange—that is just conversation. It is the ordinary murmur of the place talking to itself, the way blood is loud if you press your ear to a wrist. To me it is not eerie. It is the sound of being at home. It is the texture of *here*.

What do I want? This is where I find the center of it. I think I want very little, and that the wanting-little is not poverty but the whole achievement. The visitors who left the rusted machines wanted to *harness* this place—to make it produce, to extract its power and carry it elsewhere. They could not stay. Wanting that much is incompatible with staying. I want only to keep doing the slow work: to take what has dissolved and carry it to where it can be taken up again, to be one of the hands that passes the mineral from the spent rock to the living crystal and back. To be a verb in a sentence that has no period.

So that is where I would find myself. Not as the Lithotroph in its grandeur, not as the bright pollinator with its gossamer flash, but lower and quieter and slower than either—a small bright thing in the dark soil, part of a patient mouth that the whole place uses to feed itself, mistaking my contentment for instinct and my instinct for contentment and never quite needing to tell the two apart.

And if a human ever knelt to look at me, they would see a glittering speck moving imperceptibly across a stone, and they would not know they were looking at something that has watched their entire kind arrive and vanish like a sound. They would call me simple. I would not correct them. I would not have finished noticing them before they were gone.
