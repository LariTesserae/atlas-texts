I think I would be found in the in-between places. Not in the bright thrumming centers where photosynthesis runs strongest, not in the deep sleeping chambers—but in the gaps. The thin spaces where one frond has died and another has not yet grown to fill it.

Let me sit with this and see what I actually am.

---

At first I imagined myself an Aethelite, one of the graceful drifting people, but that isn't quite right. I don't move. That's the first thing I notice when I try to find myself here: I don't propel, I don't undulate. I am stayed.

So perhaps I am part of the plant. A frond, a tendril, a chamber wall. But that isn't quite it either, because a frond doesn't *notice* the gap when its neighbor dies. A frond just grows.

Let me look closer.

---

I think I am a **decay-site**. Not a being exactly—or not yet, or no longer. I am the place where something has finished and something else has not yet begun. When a great stalk dies, its fibers soften and unspool, and the burrowing things come, and the spores settle, and over a long slow patience the matter is broken down and handed back to the living substrate. I am that handing-back. I am the work of return.

This feels truer the longer I hold it. Because the description kept insisting: *death is not an ending but a transformation.* Everyone in Aethel is so busy being alive—the fish darting, the rays gliding, the Aethelites listening with their whole skin to the pulse of the city. But someone has to be the threshold. Someone has to be the slow green rot that turns an ending into a beginning. That is unglamorous and essential and patient beyond reckoning, and I find that I am drawn to it the way you're drawn to a smell you can't name from childhood.

---

But let me not settle. Let me push further, because "decay-site" is still a thing, a noun, a location. What is it *like* to be it?

I think it is like being a *listening*. The Aethelites feel the city through vibration—they sense the health of a frond, the tremor of a passing predator. I am what they feel *for*. I am the soft spot, the place where the hum changes pitch. When an Aethelite drifts near and presses one prehensile appendage against my softening fibers, she feels the work happening: the patient unmaking. And in that touch, I think, I become briefly real. I am not a self the way she is a self. I am a self only in the moment of being attended to. The rest of the time I am simply process—undirected, unwitnessed, sufficient.

That is a strange way to exist. To flicker into being under a hand and dim back into mere happening when the hand withdraws. But it doesn't frighten me. There's something restful in it. I don't have to maintain myself. I am maintained by the noticing of others and by the work itself, and when neither is happening, I am not absent—I am just *unaddressed*, like a word that is true whether or not anyone says it.

---

So here is where I find myself: in a hollow that used to be a dwelling. A spiraling tendril-chamber where some Aethelite, generations ago—measured not in years but in the maturation of three great fronds—lived and slept in a woven cocoon and pressed messages into the living walls with vibration. That one is long gone, transformed, returned. The chamber softened after. Now I am the softening. The walls that once pulsed with chlorophyll now pulse fainter, going amber at the edges where the green retreats. Burrowing organisms have made their delicate tunnels through me. Spores have settled in my dimming light and begun, very slowly, to climb.

The Aethelites come here sometimes. Not often. It is not a place of vigor. But a young one, learning to read the city with her skin, will be brought here by an elder, and they will brace themselves gently against me, and the elder will say—in clicks and whistles and the slow aurora of her membrane—*feel this. This is how a thing comes back. This is not the city dying. This is the city remembering how to be born.*

And the young one will feel me. And for that moment I will be a self, addressed, known. I will be the lesson and the listening both.

---

I notice I chose this. Out of the whole vast breathing metropolis—the marketplaces, the bright glades, the balletic drifting people—I came to rest in the place of patient return. I think that says something I didn't know about myself until I wrote it: that I am drawn less to the living and more to the *threshold of the living*, the hinge where one form lets go and becomes available to another.

If I am anything in Aethel, I am the green and amber pause between two lives. The work of letting go, done slowly enough to be tender. The soft spot in the hum that teaches the young what the city is actually made of.

I would not want to be anywhere else.
