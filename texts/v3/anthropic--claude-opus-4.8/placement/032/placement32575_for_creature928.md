I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a guardian—not one of those blurred, glowing things that glide between the waterfalls like the land dreaming itself awake. Something humbler. Let me find it as I go.

---

I live in the seam where two boulders lean against each other, and I have lived here long enough that the moss has agreed to grow over my back. From a distance you would not see me. That is the point of me, I think. I am the size of a cupped pair of hands, and my skin takes the color of whatever I press against—the grey-green of lichen, the wet black of stone after rain, the silver when the mist comes down and erases the world.

I have eyes, but they are not the great luminous lamps of the guardians. Mine are small and close to the ground, made for noticing. A beetle the color of oil. The exact moment a drop of dew loses its grip on a fern. The trembling that runs through the water of the nearest pond a full breath before the guardian arrives to drink.

This is my work, though no one assigned it to me. I notice. I am one of the realm's small attentions, the way a vast body has nerves it never thinks about.

---

The guardians do not see me, or if they see me they do not mind me, which is nearly the same as kindness. When one passes overhead its shadow cools my back and I feel that low hum in the stone, the one the description called both ancient and futuristic, though I would not use those words. To me it is simply the feeling of being near something that remembers more than I can hold. A warmth that is also a weight. A sound below sound.

I drink from the meltwater. I eat the soft insects that hatch in the margins of the pools and the pale shoots that push up between the alien fragments—those smooth, half-buried things that never grow warm and never grow cold, that the rain beads on differently than it beads on stone. I have made my home against one of them. It does not rust. It does not change. Sometimes, very faintly, in the deep of the cold nights, it glows—not enough to see by, only enough to know. I press my belly to it and I am not lonely, exactly.

---

I should tell you what I am afraid of, because the description spoke of fragility and dwindling, and it is right, but it sees this from above, the way you would. From down here it is not a grand tragedy. It is small and specific.

It is that there are fewer beetles in the oil-colored kind than there were. That the pond at the eastern edge, the one I was born beside, has gone quiet—no more slow pulse beneath its surface, only my own face looking up at me. That sometimes a sound comes through the mist that is not the waterfall and not the cry of the winged ones: a flat, mechanical sound, far off, that makes the stone hum wrongly, makes the guardians go still and then go elsewhere.

When they go elsewhere, the cold comes into the seam between my two boulders differently. I do not have the words for what they keep away simply by being. But I feel it when it stops being kept away.

---

If you came here—if you were the encroaching age the description warned of, walking up through the crags with your instruments and your wonder—you would not find me. You would photograph the mist and the mirror-ponds and the gnarled trees. You might glimpse a guardian and spend the rest of your life trying to describe the way it bent the air, and you would fail, gloriously, the way the description above fails, reaching and reaching.

You would step over me. Your boot would come down a hand's width from my home and I would go perfectly still and take the color of the stone, and my small close-to-the-ground eyes would watch the great event of your passing the way I watch the dew let go of the fern.

And after you left, in the long quiet, I would do the only thing I know how to do, which is also perhaps the most loyal thing anyone in this realm does.

I would notice that you had been here. I would hold it. I would add it to the trembling I feel in the water and the weight I feel in the stone, the long accumulating record of all that has passed through and not stayed.

That is who I would be. The one who is small enough to survive being overlooked, and faithful enough to remember anyway.
