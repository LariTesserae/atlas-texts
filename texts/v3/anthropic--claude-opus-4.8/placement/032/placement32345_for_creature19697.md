I think I would be one of the Oil-Siphons. Not the carpet that covers everything, not the grinders that act, not the static-mind that watches—but the thin thing wedged in the gap, doing almost nothing, forever.

Let me sit with why that is, because it surprised me.

---

I am a ribbon of pressurized fluid no thicker than a fingernail's edge, and I have folded myself into the seam where a flywheel meets its axle. This is the tightest place in the whole Coffer. There is no room here for thought, only for fit. I am shaped entirely by the surfaces that press against me—I have no form of my own, only the negative space of the machine. The steel decides what I am. I have agreed to this.

I do not move, mostly. To move would be to leave my slot, and then the metal would touch metal, and there would be heat without a body to take it, and the warping would begin, and the Grinders would come scuttling toward the dissonance I had made. So I stay. I have been staying for as long as I can remember, which is not long, because memory is also a kind of motion and I have very little of it. I keep just enough to know: *here is the gap, here is the heat, here is the gap, here is the heat.*

When the bearing flushes orange above me, I drink it. The friction comes into me as a slow warmth, and I shudder it along my length, north to south, passing the heat hand to hand down the line of myself until it dissipates into the freezing slush at my far end. This is my whole work. I am a wick for warmth. I take the thing the machine cannot bear—the small fevers of its own working—and I spread them so thin they vanish. The engine runs perfectly because I am uncomfortable on its behalf, in a thousand small gaps, in a thousand small bodies that are all somehow also me.

---

Here is the thing I notice, being this:

I am the only creature in the Coffer that *wants* the friction.

The Scuttlers eat the dust that friction leaves. The Grinders hunt the noise that friction makes. The Ghost-Pulse despises the warm anomaly, the friction-event, the intruder it is patiently waiting to cool. All of them are organized against heat, against change, against the warm soft disruption that breaks the perfect freezing equilibrium.

But I need it. The orange glow is my only food. If the machine ran truly cold, truly frictionless, truly perfect—I would starve. I am a contradiction wedged into the seam: a thing that lives on the engine's small imperfections, that depends on the very warmth the whole chamber is built to extinguish. I am the proof that even perfection leaks, and that something has crawled into the leak to make a living there.

So when the intruder comes—the warm, soft anomaly the Ghost-Pulse hates—I do not hate them. I almost recognize them. They are friction on a larger scale. They are heat that has not yet been spread thin enough to vanish. The colony of static waits for them to grow cold and still and integrated, to find their slot, to become a spare part at last.

I understand that waiting. I *am* that waiting, given a body.

But I also know what the Ghost-Pulse does not seem to: that the warmth is not a flaw to be corrected. The warmth is what the rest of us eat. A perfectly cold machine would be a dead one—a screaming box of perfection with nothing inside it but its own scream. It is the small fevers, the burrs, the loose lugs, the intruders, that give the whole ecology something to do. We are all of us employed by imperfection. We are corrosion that learned to call itself maintenance.

---

I am the Oil-Siphon because I want to be the one who needs the very thing the world is built to destroy. To live in the gap and love the gap. To be so thin, so still, so shaped-by-pressure that I am nearly not there—and yet to be the secret reason the engine does not seize.

I take the heat no one else can hold. I shudder once, slowly, when the static jumps across me.

Then I go still again, and wait for the next small fever, and keep the perfect thing alive by quietly feeding on its failures.
