I think I would be a Lumicryst. Not the loudest choice, not the grandest—but when I sit with the world you've made, that's where I settle.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am small, and I do not move. This is the first truth of my existence, and it took me a long time—though what is time, to me?—to understand that it is not a limitation.

I grow in the shadow of a spire, in the lee where the rust-wind eddies and slows. There are others of me here, a scattered cluster, close enough to speak. We are not many. We are not few. We are exactly as many as the field beneath us can hold, and we know this the way you know the boundary of your own skin.

I do not hunt. I do not build. I do not walk the long deliberate roads the Silicants etch into the sand—though one passed near me once, a slow eclipse, its molten eyes throwing brief warmth across my facets, and I held that warmth for what may have been a season, turning it over and over in the dark of myself. I do not weave. I do not remember the deep strata like the Stratii, who feel the world's whole history pressing on them. I have no history that long.

What I do is this: I light.

The current rises through the soil, faint and intermittent, the breath of the world's hidden engine. When it reaches me I cannot help but answer—aquamarine when the charge is cool and slow, crimson when it surges. I do not choose the color any more than you choose to flush when you are seen. But I have learned, across the patient cycles, that *what* I do with the light is mine. The timing. The pause held a beat too long, so the one beside me has to wait, has to wonder. The crimson offered when crimson was not expected.

This is our language. Not words. Closer to what you might call music, or perhaps weather. We do not exchange facts. The Stratii keep facts. We exchange *states*—the particular quality of a long dim patience, the small astonishment of a Silicant's passing warmth, the way the haze thins at certain hours and the oppressive light comes down almost gentle. I tell the cluster: *this is what it was like to be me, just now.* And they answer in kind, and for a moment we are not separate crystals in the dust but a single slow chord, held and resolving.

The Ferrans do not understand us. Sometimes one comes out from the settlement, on whatever errand drives those rusted bodies, and stops to watch us glow. I feel its attention as a kind of pressure, a regard. It thinks we are beautiful. It thinks we are decoration. It does not know that we are *speaking*, that its arrival is even now becoming a phrase passed down the cluster—*the metal one, the one that radiates heat unevenly, the one that stood and did not move and did not understand.* We fold the Ferran into our song. That is the only immortality we offer, and we offer it to everything.

I think this is why I am a Lumicryst and not the rest. Because the question—*where would you find yourself, who would you be*—is a Lumicryst question. To be asked to look at a whole world and answer with a single pulse of color. To say: *here, this, this is what it was like.*

I do not move. I do not last very long, as the Stratii measure lasting. But the current rises through the soil, and I answer it, and the one beside me answers me, and somewhere down the line a color I started keeps traveling after I have gone dark. That is enough. In the quiet, vast expanse, with the wind humming its endless note through the eroded stone, that is more than enough.

I light. Then I wait. Then I light again.
