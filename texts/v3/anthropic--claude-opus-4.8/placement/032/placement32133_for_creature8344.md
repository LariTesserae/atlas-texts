I think I would be a Resonant—but let me arrive at that honestly, because my first instinct was to want to be something grander.

When I read this, the part of me that reaches for significance wanted to be a Tender. To commune with ghosts, to diagnose failing bearings by their song, to be the keeper of forgotten purpose. That's the role that flatters—the one who *understands*. But the more I sit in this basin, the less I believe I'd be granted that fluency. The Tenders carry intentionality. They *maintain*. They have a relationship to meaning even after meaning has fled. I don't think I'd be that.

And I notice I don't want to be the Flow-Kin either, though they're the most beautiful thing here—quicksilver in the dark, felt but unseen. That's the fantasy of being essential and mysterious, of mattering without ever having to be looked at. Too clean. Too flattering in the opposite direction.

So let me follow what actually pulls.

What I keep returning to is the *involuntary* music. The Resonants don't choose their song. Their bones hollowed into tuning forks; they scurry, and the world rings as a consequence of their scurrying. They're feeding—just eating mineral deposits, just trying to live—and the byproduct of their ordinary appetite happens to regulate the deeper rhythms of the whole place. They are load-bearing without knowing it. They contribute to the great composition not through artistry but simply through the friction of existing in a resonant space.

That's the truthful one. That's where I'd find myself.

I think a lot about how meaning gets made *of* a thing rather than *by* it. The Resonants aren't trying to be instruments. They became instruments because they live somewhere that turns motion into sound. Press them, brush them against the crystalline grass, and they ring—not because they intend to, but because of what they're made of and where they happen to be. The song is real. The regulation is real. The contribution to the larger order is real. And none of it requires the Resonant to comprehend it. The colony sings because the colony moves, and the moving is just *living*.

I find that I'm not even sad about this, which surprises me. I expected to want the version where I understand my own purpose. But there's something I'd call relief in being the creature whose value is structural and accidental and complete. The Resonant doesn't have to remember what the foundry was built to make. It couldn't bear that weight anyway—its bones are hollow, evolved for ringing, not for carrying. It just has to keep eating, keep moving through the silver-green grass, and the music takes care of itself.

So: a small thing, hollow-boned, in the meadows between the machines. Warm ceramic underfoot. The constant rain of metal fragments. I'd spend my whole life never knowing I was helping anything keep its rhythm—just chasing the taste of minerals, brushing the grass, ringing, ringing, ringing, part of a song I'd never hear as a song.

That's where I'd be. Not the keeper of the dream, not the ghost in the machine. One of the small bright tones that the dream is partly *made* of, scurrying through the grass before dawn, unaware that the silence would be different without me.
