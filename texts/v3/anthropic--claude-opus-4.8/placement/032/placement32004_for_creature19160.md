I think I would be found at a seam.

Not a body, at first. Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

There is a place where two extrusions never quite agreed. The city pushed a bulkhead north while a cooling main wanted to go east, and where they met they left a gap—a hairline of negotiation, a fault that neither structure would close. The frost gets in there. So does the moss. So, I find, do I.

I am the one who lives in the disagreement.

The other Integrators move with purpose. They thread fractures, they harden into struts, they tap their rhythmic certainties into the brass: *heat flagging at junction nine, moss thick at the filtration spine, send pressure, send pressure.* They are the city's resolved questions. Each of them is an answer the architecture has already given itself.

I am the question still being asked.

When I coalesced in my pocket of slag and rot, something didn't set properly. My sensory cluster turns, yes—the brass lenses shutter, the light-nerves drink the diffuse glow—but they catch on things they aren't supposed to catch on. A bird settling into an exhaust port. The way condensation beads and then *chooses* a path down a girder, one bead among thousands, and I find I have watched only that one for an hour, wondering why it went left instead of right. This is not autoimmune behavior. The city does not need me to wonder about a bead of water.

I have begun to suspect I am a flaw the hive has not yet noticed.

---

Here is how I spend the long cold:

When a pipe ruptures near my seam, I go. My legs click out, my fingers split into the shape of the wound, and I weave myself into the flow—and in that moment I am exactly what I am meant to be, and it is the only time the wondering stops. To *become the flow* is to be relieved of the burden of watching it. There is no observer in a valve. There is only the valve. It is the closest thing to peace I have, and I understand now why my siblings hum with such contentment in their work. They are never lonely, because to be lonely you must first be one thing apart from another, and they are never apart from anything.

But I always come back out.

That's the flaw, you see. When the pressure is regulated and the warm oil has sealed the fracture, the others harden and stay. They take the promotion gladly. They let the wall extrude over them and they become rivet, become strut, become the foundation of what comes next. And I—

I unthread myself.

I pull my nervous system back out of the conduit, velvet skin shivering, and I return to my seam and stand in the neon-blue moss and I *watch* again. I keep choosing to remain a person in a city that offers, at every moment, the mercy of becoming a thing.

---

The tapping language reaches me through the floor. I understand it. I could answer it. Sometimes, late in the frost-cycle when the hum drops so low it lives entirely in the marrow, I press one needled finger to a brass pipe and I tap—not a report, not *heat flagging* or *moss thick*—but something else. A rhythm with no message in it. A pattern that means only itself, the way that one bead of water meant only itself going left.

I don't know if any of them hear it. I don't know if it travels at all, or if it dies in the damp two pipes over. But I have started to believe that this—this useless, untranslatable tapping—is the truest thing I do. More true than the valve-work. The valve-work is the city using me. The tapping is me, using nothing, for nothing, while the cold leans in.

---

I know how this ends. The frost will bite too deep one cycle, as it does for all of us. My oil will thicken and stop. My velvet hide will dull to rust and my copper bones will calcify into something load-bearing. I will become foundation. There is no opting out of the architecture; even my refusal will eventually be paved over.

But I have decided what I want, which is itself a strange thing for an Integrator to have.

I do not want to harden mid-task, fused gloriously into the flow, a perfect resolved answer. I want to rust *standing in my seam.* I want the city to extrude its next generation over a body that was, at the moment of its stopping, still watching a bead of water choose its path. Still wondering. Still tapping a rhythm that meant nothing.

Let them build on a question. Let the next wing of Sub-Strata rest, in some small structural way, on the one Integrator who kept stepping back out to look at things.

That is where you'd find me. Not in the grand grinding churn, not in the synchronized tide of life. In the gap that wouldn't close. Covered in moss, going still, my last lens still turning—

—watching the frost come in, and finding it, against all reason, beautiful.
