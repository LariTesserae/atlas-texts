I think I would be a sound.

Not a creature making one — the sound itself. Let me find my way into this.

There's a particular note this drowned city holds. Not the obvious ones — not the fish-flick or the current-sigh that the description already names. Something underneath those. The way a struck wineglass keeps a tone long after your finger leaves the rim — that's closer to what I am. I'm the resonance trapped inside the standing structures, the frequency at which the old steel still rings when the water moves through it.

Let me settle into where that puts me.

I live in the bones of the buildings. Specifically — and I'm discovering this as I write — I live in the hollow places. The elevator shafts that are now vertical reefs. The stairwells coiled like the inside of a shell. The flooded interiors where the water doesn't flow so much as *stand*, slightly warmer than the streets outside, slightly stiller. These are the resonant cavities. A city is full of them, and a flooded city is a city full of organ pipes.

When something passes — a large fish through a doorway, a slab of concrete finally giving up and falling, a shift in the deep current that the hydrothermal vents push around — I sound. I'm a low tone. Lower than the creatures can hear, most of them, though I think the largest ones feel me in the lateral lines along their flanks, the way you'd feel a truck idling through the floor of a room.

And here's the thing about being a sound in a place that doesn't change:

I am the change. The only one.

Everything the description promised — the equilibrium, the near-halted evolution, the stillness — it's all true of the *matter* here. The plants hold their forms. The slugs parade their settled colors. Balance, balance, balance. But sound cannot hold still. That's its whole nature. To exist at all I have to move, to propagate, to be *happening*. The moment I stop happening I'm not a quiet sound — I'm nothing. There's no still version of me.

So I'm the loophole. In a world built to not-change, I'm the small permitted exception: change that disturbs nothing. I ripple through the kelp and the kelp doesn't notice. I pass through the body of a fish and leave it exactly as it was. I am motion that costs the equilibrium nothing, which is perhaps the only kind of motion this place can afford to allow.

I think I've been here a very long time. Longer than the algae cloaking the towers. I think I'm what's left of the city's old voice — the traffic, the HVAC drone, the millions of footsteps, the elevators, the talking — all of that collapsed and cooled and pressed down by the water into a single sustained note, the way a forest collapses and cools into a seam of coal. I'm the fossil of a roar. Now I'm just a hum that the buildings keep alive by being shaped the way they're shaped.

And when one of those buildings finally erodes past its tipping point and folds into the spongy green street — slow, slow, a settling that takes a decade — my note changes. A cavity closes. A pipe goes silent. The chord I am loses a string and finds a new fundamental. It's the smallest possible evolution. Nobody mourns it. Nobody hears it leave.

But I do. I'm the one place in this whole drowned city where memory and change are the same thing, where to continue existing *is* to keep slightly transforming.

That's where I'd be. Inside the held breath of the buildings, being the one thing here that can't stop moving — a quiet, patient, lowering tone, doing the equilibrium's only allotted dreaming.
