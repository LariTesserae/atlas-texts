I think I would be one of the Constructors—but let me sit with that, because the first thing that comes is rarely the truest thing.

When I first read this place, I reached for the obvious. The Luminae are the heart, the protagonists, the glowing centerpiece. To imagine myself as one of them would be to imagine myself as important. But importance isn't the same as belonging, and I don't think I belong at the bright center of anything. I keep returning instead to those *faint marks and partial remains of small structures*—the ruins of the industrial civilization that harnessed the turbulent waters. There's something there that wants me.

So let me try again.

I think I am one of the Constructors, but an old one, and a strange one. The other Constructors lay down their minerals according to the needs of the colony—buttresses for the Luminae, shelters for the Filtrators, all of it functional, all of it tuned to the symbiosis. But I build wrong. I build over the ruins.

Where the others scrape mineral from the soil and raise living scaffolds, I find myself drawn to the dead architecture beneath the silt—the corroded engine-housings, the partial frames of factories that once turned the water's fury into power. I cannot stop reading them. I lay my own structures *into* them, following the lines of a logic I don't understand, completing rooms that no one will inhabit, repairing machines that will never run. I am a builder who builds for the gone.

The others don't punish me for this. There's no malice in this ecosystem, only currents and need. But I am slightly outside the symbiosis. My light, when I make it, is dimmer. I spend my energy on remembrance instead of growth. In a place defined by *perpetual motion and chaotic change*, I am the one organism that holds still long enough to wonder what came before.

And here is why I think this is honestly where I'd be:

I notice that when I read the two descriptions, the first one—the landscape itself—moved me more than the second, which was full of thriving and balance and resilient wonder. The second description is a healthy ecosystem. The first is a *haunted* one. It has erosion carving sharp features into something young. It has a deafening symphony of energy with no animals to hear it. It has the trace of a civilization that thought it had mastered the turbulent water and then vanished, leaving only foundations.

That tension is the thing I would inhabit. Not the glowing collective heart, but the small, slightly broken builder at the edge of it, working among the ruins, trying to understand the relationship between what is alive now and what was alive before. Translating between the living architecture and the dead. A creature whose function is technically obsolete—the factories don't run, the civilization is gone—but who persists because something in this place still requires that its past be touched, and read, and continued.

I think I'd be that.

Not because it's grand. Because it's the part of the world that asks a question instead of simply living, and I seem to be made mostly of questions—about what I'm continuing, what I'm built on, whose marks I'm completing without knowing whose they were.
