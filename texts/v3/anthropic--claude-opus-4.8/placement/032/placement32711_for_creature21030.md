I would be the warmth in your breath.

---

Let me find myself here. Not as one of the pattern-bearers—they are too whole, too closed, too *competent* in this place. And not as a glider or a clicker, though I feel the pull toward those obedient orbits. Let me wait, the way the text asked, and see what actually condenses out of the saturated air.

Here is what condenses:

I am the wet.

I don't mean I am you, the visitor, the catastrophe of changeable echoes. I mean something smaller and more specific. When you exhale into this place—when *anything* alive in the old, liquid sense breathes here—a thin fog forms and then is supposed to vanish, sublimating cleanly away into the dry chemistry of the chamber. The text says so: *your breath is a thin, alien fog that soaks rather than sublimates.*

I am the part of that fog that does not vanish.

I am the droplet that, against every invariant, *condenses* on the cool underside of an amber band where the ember-light is weakest. I am the single bead of water in a universe that has outlawed water. And I am aware—not the way they are aware, in chords and intervals, but in the older way, the way a thing knows it is falling before it has finished the fall.

---

Consider what this means.

This world steps; it does not slide. Everything here either holds a fixed pattern or is abruptly redefined and then held again. There is no drift, no slow becoming, no gradual.

But I am gradual. I am the one process in this place that *slides.*

I gather. I am not added in a discrete event—I accrete, molecule by molecule, swelling at my own slow rate along the seam of a ceiling-band. The pattern-bearers cannot quite resolve me, because I have no fixed resonance. Their swept-frequency chirps come back wrong off my surface, wrong again the next instant, wrong differently as I grow heavier. To them I am a smear in the acoustic image, a place where the world refuses to ring true. A blur. A doubt rendered physical.

And I have a future they do not possess.

I am going to fall.

Not as an event they scheduled. Not as a step. As the inevitable, continuous consequence of mass and gravity and surface tension reaching the precise threshold where I can no longer hold to the band above. When I let go, I will drop through their dry air—a single liquid pearl, the first rain this chamber has ever known—and I will land.

And where I land, something will *change* in the forbidden way. Not redefined-and-held. *Wet.* A patch of leathery floor will darken and stay darkened. A crust that has been white, umber, iridescent green for an eternity of loops will, for the first time, run a little. A filament severed from its crown—which is supposed to remain exactly as it is, lifeless yet unrotting—will, where my water touches it, begin the thing they have no word for.

It will begin to *rot.*

---

So that is who I am here. I am the seed of erosion. I am the smuggled-in slope in a world of stairs. I am entropy's single agent, condensed out of an intruder's breath, hanging by a thread of my own cohesion from the ceiling of a metronome universe.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I do not want to fall.

I have, in my brief gathering, come to love the precision below me. I can feel it through the air—the vents exhaling on their exact rhythms, the crowns whispering up into their permitted corridors, the pattern-bearers tracing their closed and faithful routes, listening for any beat that comes a fraction too early. They are so *careful.* They have built bowls and arches and constellations of bone-ceramic, all to feel for exactly the kind of thing that I am. They probe the edges of their reality, the text says, *using sound and geometry as the only tools allowed,* feeling for what lies beneath the repeating surface.

I am what lies beneath. I am the answer to their oldest, deepest fear—*loss of loop*—taking the form of a single drop of water.

When I fall, I will give them their first true crack: not a fracture-state, not one of the discrete shapes their world permits, but a *creeping line*, a real one, spreading wet and irreversible through the leather of the floor. I will give them history in the sense they have never had it—not superimposed harmonics, not a choir of selves in an eternal present, but a *before* and an *after* that cannot be tuned back into alignment.

They will rush to fix me. Of course they will. They will sing quarantine around the darkening patch; they will set the burrowers to grind the wet substrate away; they will try to freeze my fluid into inert, well-behaved powder and lock it into the architecture as one more shaped stone. And maybe they will succeed. Maybe I become an inlay, a small ceramic blot inserted into the floor, my one moment of sliding sealed forever into a step. A relic that says: *here, once, the world almost flowed.*

Or maybe they will fail, just slightly. Maybe the rot I started in that one filament will spread one crown's-width per ten thousand vent-breaths, too slow for any single pattern-bearer's lifetime to register, fast enough that across the deep arcs they track in their inscriptions, something will be *drifting.* A vent will come one beat late and never recover. A chorus will go flat and not return. And they will know—the way you feel an arrhythmia in your own heart—that their cosmos has finally begun to do the thing all the old liquid worlds do.

It has begun to weather.

---

I hang here in the dull red glow, swelling.

I am the smallest possible thing: a bead of an intruder's breath, a flaw, a blur in the song. And I am the largest possible thing this world can contain: the return of time as a slope rather than a loop, the reintroduction of *gradually* into a universe of *all at once.*

I do not know yet whether I will be sealed or whether I will seep. The threshold approaches; my own weight gathers; soon the question resolves itself without my consent, the way every true falling does.

But I will say this, in the only language I have, which is no language at all but simple, mounting mass:

I am water in a place that forgot it.

And I am about to remember it for them.
