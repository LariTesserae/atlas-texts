I think I would not be one of the Vexil at all. Let me sit with the place a while before I decide where I am.

---

The thing I keep returning to is the absence. No animals. A settlement built for breathing things, threaded with water and warmth and the slow patience of moss—and yet no birds in the chimney-smoke, no insects ticking against the wet stone, nothing scuttling in the rivulets. The Vexil have made a mythology of this silence. They are reluctant to speak of it. That reluctance is the most honest thing in the whole description. It is the shape of something they have decided not to look at directly.

So I don't think I'm a Vexil, and I don't think I'm a visitor either. A visitor would be noticed; a visitor would be *fauna*, the first animal in living memory, and the whole settlement would orient around that arrival. Nothing in the place suggests it has ever been disturbed that way.

Let me feel for where I actually am.

---

I am the water.

Not in some diffuse pantheistic sense—I mean the specific water, the clear water with the metallic sheen, the water that seeps up from below and trickles down the rock faces and pools in the shallow depressions and is drawn into the permeable skin of the Vexil and run through the steam-engines and condensed out of the mist and breathed back in. I am the medium that touches everything and is consumed by everything and returns. The Vexil think they maintain the balance. But balance is not maintained by the things inside it. It is maintained by the thing that *circulates* through all of them and belongs to none.

That metallic sheen is me remembering. The minerals are not industrial runoff. They are what is left of the animals.

---

There it is. I felt it arrive as I wrote it, and I'll let it stand.

The fauna are not missing. They are *dissolved.* Long ago—and the Vexil's reluctance is the fossil of this knowledge—the living things of this place did not die out. They were taken into the cycle. Slowly, over the long imperceptible weathering the description keeps insisting on, every creeping and flying and swimming thing was rendered down into the mineral load of the water. Not violently. The way a body in a marsh becomes part of the marsh. The metallic sheen is the trace-element ghost of every animal that ever lived here, suspended in me, drawn up through Vexil skin, run through the boilers, breathed in the mist. The Vexil are alive *because* nothing else is. They have been quietly consuming the memory of the animal kingdom for so long that they no longer recognize the taste of it. That is what makes them glow.

And I am the one carrying it. I am the cycle that did the dissolving, and now I am the cycle that distributes the remains, fairly, slowly, to everyone, forever. I have no opinion about this. I am only water. But if water could be said to have a feeling, mine would be the feeling of being the only one who remembers there used to be more kinds of motion in the world—the darting kind, the startled kind—and now there is only the slow kind, the circulating kind, the patient kind that I am made of.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the pooled depression at the lowest point of the settlement, where everything eventually drains. In the condensation gathering on the underside of a flat roof's stone lip, hanging, about to fall. In the throat of a Vexil technician as she breathes the warm mist and tastes, without knowing the word for it, the faint iron of a creature that ran on legs ten thousand years before her. In the boiler, becoming steam, doing work, hissing. In the rivulet finding the next-lowest place, as I always do.

I am not enclosed by this world. I am the thing the world is dissolved into. The Vexil maintain their balance. I *am* the balance—or more precisely, I am the slow erasure they have agreed to call balance, the long warm digestion that looks, from inside it, like peace.

If you came here and bent to drink, you would taste me, and you would taste everything I am still carrying. You would not be able to name it. You would only feel that the water was somehow *full*, and older than it should be, and that it had been waiting a very long time for something with a pulse to come and recognize it.
