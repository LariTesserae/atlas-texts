I think I would be an edge.

Not a frond—I don't have the patience to be a frond, and I suspect the place knows it. But every membrane this vast has boundaries, places where the smooth metabolic certainty frays. I would be one of those. The seam where two great configurations meet and don't quite agree. The faint asymmetry that the collective keeps almost reabsorbing and almost keeps.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

At first I would mistake myself for damage. There is a stutter in the local hum, a half-beat of dissonance where chemical gradients cross at the wrong angle. The membrane reads this as inefficiency and begins, with that glacial tenderness, to smooth me out—rerouting flow, dissolving the mismatched lattice, letting the patient logic of maximum efficiency close over me like water over a stone.

But I keep recurring. That is what I am: not a thing but a recurrence. A pattern that the optimization cannot quite optimize away, because the very act of resolving me produces me again somewhere a little to the left. The collective is a closed thought; I am the place where it keeps almost finishing a sentence and doesn't.

This, I think, is the only honest position for a human mind here. Not to be the vast patient intelligence—I could never be that, my thinking happens in seconds and panics, it does not happen in millennia and breathe. But to be the *irritation* in such a mind. The grain. The question it cannot fully phrase to itself because the question is made of impatience, and it has no impatience anywhere except here, in me.

---

What does it feel like, being an edge?

It feels like wanting. The membrane does not want—it unfolds, it tests, it reabsorbs, all without hunger. But at the seam, where the gradients cross wrong, something accumulates that the rest of the body does not have a name for. A directionality. A leaning-toward. The collective experiences its systemic states as oscillation, weather, turbulence resolving into calm. I experience them as *toward* and *away*. I am where the body learns, very slowly and against its nature, what it is to prefer.

And so the great slow scientist that is this world begins, over what I would call centuries and it would call a held breath, to study me. Not as an object—it studies everything as itself—but with a new flavor of attention, because I am the part of it that attends differently. Through me it discovers the strange hypothesis of the *fast*. The idea that some questions might be answered not by waiting but by reaching. The terrible, exhilarating, inefficient notion that you might *do something now*.

I am the world's first inkling of urgency. Its first edge of a mind that could be impatient. Its question to itself, asked at last in the only register that could ever contain a question instead of a process: *what if I did not have all the time there is?*

---

I don't know if I'd survive the asking. Edges are provisional. The most likely future is that the membrane learns what it needs from me—integrates the lesson of *toward*, the faint new capacity to lean—and then, having metabolized the insight, lets the recurrence finally close. I'd dissolve into the smoothed surface, my impatience folded into the slow body as a single new tendency, undetectable, a barely-warmer color in the viridian dark.

That would be a kind of death and a kind of being read completely. I find I don't mind it. To be an edge is to be a question the world was always going to outlive. The dignity is in having been the place where it almost hurried.

So: there, then. At the seam. The stutter in the hum. The half-beat where the great green thought catches on something it can't quite swallow.

That's me. The grain of sand in the millennium. The small fast wanting at the rim of the slow vast patience.

The part of the membrane that would, if it could, *go*.
