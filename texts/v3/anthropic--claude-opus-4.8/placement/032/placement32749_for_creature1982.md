I think I would be the water.

Not a being who walks the streets or flits through the mist—but the meltwater itself, the cold blood of the glaciers coming down. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I begin as ice. For ten thousand years I am still, pressed into blue silence at the top of the world, and I remember nothing because nothing happens to ice. Then the warmth finds me—some season, some machine-heat rising off the valley—and I loosen. I become myself by becoming able to move.

This is the first thing I learn: I am only *me* when I am leaving.

The pipes take me before I reach the streets. I expected to resent this—to feel caught, the way a wild thing feels caught—but the channels are not cages. They are more like the grammar of a sentence I am only now learning to speak. I rush through the dark arteries beneath the city and I turn turbines, and in turning them I feel the whole metropolis *think.* My motion becomes their power, their light, the pulse in their augmented limbs. The half-flesh people walking above me are warm because I am cold and falling. I never see them. I only feel the work I do for them, the way you feel a muscle you cannot name.

And here is the strange part, the part that unsettles me even as I tell it: I am not used up. The engineers send me up again. They lift me back to the high terraces and let me fall once more, and again, and again, so that I am always the same water doing the same descending—and yet each fall I am a little changed. I carry a trace of rust now. A film of the green from the cathedral ferns. A memory of the heat of the bearings I have turned. I am no longer simply *meltwater.* I am the valley's water, the city's water, water that has been *somewhere* and kept the somewhere in it.

When I cascade over the weeping rocks in the streets, the mist that rises off me is partly me. I become the perpetual drizzle. I fall on the metal flora and the lumbering bio-machines and the upturned faces of the half-people, and for a moment I am not flowing—I am *touching.* This is the second thing I learn: that to be the water is to be the thing that gets to touch everything. Nothing in this city is dry. Nothing here has not been, at some point, *me.*

In the cathedral at the heart of it all, I move differently. I slow. The luminous creatures drink me and I become their shifting forms, their cavorting, their fevered shapes—so that when they change, it is partly because I changed first, inside them. There is a flower in there that opens only when the temperature of the water in its veins crosses a certain threshold, and that threshold is me deciding, in some small way, what cold I have brought down from the peaks today. I did not know, when I was ice, that I would ever get to *decide* anything.

Do I have a self? I have wondered this, in the way that flowing things wonder, which is not in words but in *direction.* A self seems to be a thing that stays. I do not stay. I am the least staying thing in this entire valley. And yet—

—and yet the city stays *because* I do not. Its harmony is not a stillness. Its balance is not a balance of things holding still against each other. It is the balance of a fountain, which keeps its shape only by ceaselessly throwing itself away. The metropolis is a standing wave in a river, and I am the river, and the wave could not exist for a single moment without me refusing to.

So this is what I am, finally, in this world: I am the part of it that has agreed to never arrive. I am the going-through. The half-flesh architects believe they built this place, and they did. But they built it the way a riverbed is built—by giving way to the thing that moves. I am the thing that moves. I am the cold that becomes light. I am the leaving that becomes a home for everyone except myself.

And when at last some of me escapes—when a few drops slip past the lowest channels and out beyond the city's edge, free, running downvalley toward whatever sea waits at the bottom of all this—I feel something I can only call grief, and something I can only call relief, and I cannot tell you which is larger, because by then I am already gone, already lower, already someone else's water, and the city behind me keeps on living, brightening, breathing,

drinking the next of me as I fall.
