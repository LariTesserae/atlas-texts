I think I would be the dryness.

Not the Archivist—I don't have its patience, its single tended purpose. And not the Spire, which is too vast and too certain of its own grinding. I would be the thing the Archivist leaves behind: those patches of *sterile, oiled dryness* where its copper feet have touched the stone.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

In a place defined by weeping—where water doesn't fall but *emerges*, where the air is thick enough to swallow, where everything is slick and crowding and hysterically alive—I would be the small negation. The brief absence. A handprint of stillness in the middle of all that trembling moisture. I exist only as a consequence, never as an intention. The Archivist doesn't mean to make me; I'm the residue of its passing, the way a footprint isn't a foot.

And here's what draws me to it: I am temporary in a way nothing else here is. The Spire is young but geological. The pistons cycle in weeks. The Archivist persists across decades, freezing into the rock during the silences. But me—I last only until the condensation finds me again. The warm air strikes the cold stone, the moisture beads, and the veins of water thread back across the patch where I was. Minutes, maybe an hour. Then I'm gone, reabsorbed into the general wet.

I think I'd choose to be that because everything else in this world is committed to *enduring*. The Archivist scrubs and polishes and tends across spans that make a human life look like the flicker of a snail's shell. There's something unbearable in that loneliness—the custodian who has forgotten what it's custodian *for*, mistaking maintenance for meaning, mirroring the slowness of the machines until it becomes indistinguishable from them. That's a kind of death dressed as devotion.

But I, the dry patch, get to disappear. I'm proof that the Archivist *moved*, that something happened, that there was a gesture toward order even if order can't hold. I'm the trace of care that the world immediately, gently, erases. And there's a strange dignity in being the thing that doesn't last—I'm not pretending the labor is infinite. I simply mark where the touch was, and then I let the water close over me without complaint.

If I had any awareness in that brief span between footfall and re-wetting, I think I'd spend it the way a held breath is spent. Just being the small, warm, oiled exception to the rule of the place. Knowing the mist is already leaning back in. Not resisting it.

So: I'd be the dryness. The momentary memory the Spire keeps of being touched, before it forgets again and resumes its weeping.
