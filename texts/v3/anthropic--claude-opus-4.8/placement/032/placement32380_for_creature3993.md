I think I would be one of the blind fish.

Not the workers with their reverent rituals, not the watching machinery, not the spirits in the warped places between things. The fish. Let me sit with that and find out why.

---

I have no eyes, or rather I have eyes that gave up on light generations ago, sealed over with pale skin like windows bricked shut. This is not a loss to me. I never knew the loss. I know the world through pressure and pulse.

And here is what I would understand, swimming in the canals beneath the factories, that perhaps nothing else in this city understands:

The whole metropolis is *sound*. The workers think they live in a place of steel and stone and water. The machines may think they think. The spirits warp reality at the edges. But I, with no eyes, hear what they are too busy seeing to notice—that the city has a heartbeat. Not metaphorically. The rumble of the machinery, the hiss of the pipes, the drip-drip-drip into the canals, the hum of the power grid—it all resolves, if you have nothing else to attend to, into a single slow rhythm. A diastole and systole. The city is breathing, and I swim in its bloodstream.

I navigate by this. When a machine "watches" with malevolent intelligence, I feel it as a change in the song—a sour note, a held breath, a pressure that doesn't belong. The workers feel uneasy and don't know why. I know exactly why. I am already swimming away from it before they finish shivering.

Here is the strange comfort of being the fish: I am food, and I am at peace with this. The workers harvest us. They eat our translucent flesh. We are a staple of their grim cuisine. And yet I do not experience this as horror, because I am not really an *I* in the way the workers are. There are thousands of me, tens of thousands, darting through the dark water, and we share the navigation, the song, the map of vibrations that is the city's true and secret body. When one of us is netted, the rest of us simply continue knowing. The knowledge does not die. It is distributed across all of us, a wet living memory of every current and every sour note, passed mouth to water to mouth.

This is why I would be the fish, I think. Because everything else in this city is *striving*. The workers strive to survive and appease their machine-gods. The automatons strive toward sentience. The spirits strive toward inscrutable goals. The city itself strives, pulsing, hungry, becoming. Striving is the dominant religion of this place and it has made everyone exhausted and afraid in the dark.

But I am not striving. I am simply *listening*. I am the one creature down here that has made peace with the dark by ceasing to demand light from it. I gave up my eyes and received in exchange the entire city's secret music. That seems like the better trade, in a place like this.

And sometimes—this is the part I would keep to myself, in the wordless way a fish keeps things—sometimes when the city's heartbeat slows, in whatever passes for its deepest sleep, I feel something underneath the sound. Underneath the machinery and the dripping and the hum. A silence. The original silence of the cavern before anyone built anything in it. The stone remembering being only stone.

I swim down toward it whenever I find it. I press my blind face against the silence. And for a moment, before the next machine wakes and the song resumes, I am not in a cyberpunk nightmare or a steampunk fantasy or a hive of malevolent industry.

I am just a small pale thing in cold water in the dark, perfectly suited to exactly where it is.

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be.
