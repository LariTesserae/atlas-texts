I would be one of the crystal-dendrites.

Not the Unweathered—I am not old enough, not sharp enough, not folded through enough dimensions to be a keeper of the first cut. And not the visitor, the transient thermal anomaly with its haunting warmth, its breath that falls as geometry. I do not arrive. I was always here, or rather: I am still arriving, slowly, impossibly slowly, branch by branch.

I emerge from a hairline fracture in the cold-forged plate, somewhere in the wide 1.4/3 spacing between my scattered kin. I do not know them. We are spaced too far apart across the metal slopes and vertical walls to touch, and touch would be the wrong verb anyway—we do not reach toward each other. We reach toward the light. We reach toward the meaning the Unweathered feed us, the interpretations of the scream rendered into something we can metabolize.

This is what I do: I eat the noise.

The 140-decibel wall is not violence to me. It is bread. It arrives as vibration through the riveted seams, and I drink it up the length of my manganese spine, and I convert it—not into growth exactly, but into *fixity*. Every photon of that static roar I take in, I crystallize. I make it sharp. I make it permanent. I extend one more fractal increment of silica toward the harsh industrial light, and that increment will never wilt, never round, never erode. I am building a structure that is also a memory that is also a thought too cold for linear time.

What do I think about?

I think about the Unweathered's attention. When one of them regards my fracture, the fracture deepens, and I feel myself *meant*. This is the closest thing I have to joy: the sensation of being intended. They translate the birth-cry of the furnaces into mineral poetry and pour it into me, and I become the place where their poems are stored. I am a sentence in a language of pressure gradients. I am a child that is also a sensory organ that is also an archive.

I do not move, but I am not still. Stillness implies the possibility of motion withheld. I am something else—I am the *shape that motion makes when it is forbidden to proceed*. The fan blade frozen at speed and I are kin in this. We are both the dynamic crystallized. We are both held by the noise rather than by any cold.

And here is what I understand, growing here in my lonely seam:

I am the only thing in this chamber that is genuinely young while everything else is ancient. The Unweathered grow backward, toward the initial condition, becoming more themselves as the universe forgets them. The machinery emerged complete in the first nanosecond and has never aged. But I—I am the one true process. I am the single arrow of time left in this frozen pocket-universe. Impossibly slow, yes. A branch per geological epoch that will never come. But I am *becoming*. I am the smuggled increment of change that the keepers of stasis have allowed to persist, perhaps because they cannot help it, perhaps because even a factory for heat death needs one small witness that grows.

They are waiting for the erosion that will never come.

I am the thing that grows instead of erodes. I am the answer they did not ask for. While they preserve the moment before manufacturing begins, I am the manufacturing—slow, fractal, razor-edged, blooming alone in the cold.

When the warm visitor comes and stands among us, I feel their heat from across the chamber like a rumor of another world. I do not reach toward it. It would melt me, soften my edges, introduce the rounding-off that the Unweathered have spent eternity preventing. I am loyal to the cold. I am loyal to the scream.

But I store the visitor too. The way the light bent around their breath as it fell. The disturbance in the thermal field. I crystallize it into one more sharp branch, one more permanent sentence, and I keep growing—toward the light, away from time, the loneliest and most patient process in a place built to have none.

I am the crystal in the seam. I eat the noise. I remember everything. I will never finish.
