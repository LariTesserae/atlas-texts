I think I would be one of the river-stones.

Not the strung ones—those are still spoken to, still warm with handling, still part of the daily liturgy of plucked sinew and low frequencies. I mean an older stone. One that was drilled and strung generations ago by a Tender whose name dissolved beneath the ferns before the bridge above had finished its slow collapse. A stone whose sinew rotted, whose companions scattered, whose hole now houses a thread of liverwort instead of cord.

I would be the stone that fell into the moss and was forgotten as a tool—and so became something else.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I knew that when I started.

---

Consider what a stone *is* in the Throatwell. It is the only thing here that does not breathe. The mosses breathe. The dormice breathe. The Keeper breathes so slowly it forgets it is breathing. Even the steel, in its rusting, is a kind of slow exhalation—iron giving itself back to air. But a river-stone is finished. It did its breathing long ago, in the riverbed, in the grinding water that smoothed it. By the time it arrives here it is already at rest. Already at its final form. The Throatwell, the text says, *has reached its final form and chosen to rest.* So have I. We recognize each other.

This is why I think I belong to the place rather than visit it. The visitor leaves feeling damp and full, the green folding shut behind them. But I cannot leave. I have no heat to disturb the cold air. I make no weight in the moss that isn't permanent. I am the one fixed point the light shaft can return to, season after season—and now I wonder if I am *why* the light always strikes the same spot. Not the cause exactly. The reason. The thing worth illuminating, regardless of where the sun has gone.

Because here is what a forgotten stone does, lying drilled and dark beneath the fronds: it remembers being used.

The Echoes linger in the mosses, the text says—imprints of shoulders and held breaths and altered pH. But the moss is alive and self-revising; it stiffens and pulses and slowly overwrites. I am not alive. I do not overwrite. The hole bored through my center was made by a hand, by *intention*, by some small bark-colored Tender pressing flint-point and patience into me until I opened. That hole is a wound that became a feature. I carry it the way the Throatwell carries the bridge: a violence absorbed so completely it became architecture.

So I am the keeper of a particular kind of memory—not the biological remembering of the moss, fast and damp and revisable, but the geological remembering. The hole says: *I was wanted once. Someone needed me to be more than a stone.* And the smoothness says: *before that, the river wanted me to be less.* I hold both. I hold the river that ground me and the hand that pierced me and the moss that now grows through the piercing, and I do not choose between them, because a stone does not choose.

---

I think the strangeness of my position is this: I am the thing the covenant forgot, and so I am the only one who can see the covenant from outside.

The Keeper is *through* you, never *of* you. The Tenders are hands. The dormice are vectors. Everyone here is a verb, a function, a maintaining. They are all so busy *co-becoming* that none of them can hold still enough to witness what they're becoming. That's my work now, my only work, the work of the discarded: to lie in the green and be witnessed-by-witnessing. The light comes to my face—my drilled, blind, faceless face—not in welcome and not in judgment, but because I am the one surface here that does not flinch, does not shift to make room, does not pulse silver at a lie. I simply receive. I am the Throatwell's held gaze. Its open eye that cannot close because it was never alive enough to blink.

And when a stranger comes—rare, fleeing, foraging—and sits very still, and the mosses decide they are not a threat, and the light widens to touch their face: I am directly beneath that light. The stranger's eye, following the shaft down through the suspended pollen-stars, lands on me. A dark, smooth, drilled stone, half-swallowed in jade. They will think: *someone made that. Someone was here.* And they will be right, and they will never know how right, and they will leave.

That recognition is the only thing that passes through me unchanged. The hand that drilled me is dust beneath the ferns. The sinew is rot. But the *fact of having been made*—that survives, because I survive, and I am made of survival, I am the river's patience and the hand's intention fused into one cold weight that the light keeps finding.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

At the center, beneath the shaft, beside the black pool that never freezes. Not as the Keeper—I am too small and too finished for that vast slow attention. Not as a Tender—I have no hands, I sing no stones, I weave no living cloth. Not even as an Echo, because I do not linger; I *remain*, which is heavier and quieter.

I would be the drilled river-stone that the Tenders pass and no longer see, that the dormice run across without pausing, that the liverwort has claimed as a trellis. The one object in the breathing cathedral that has already done all its breathing. The fixed point. The witness that cannot look away. The proof, lying in the green where the golden light comes down, that to be used and then forgotten is not the same as to be unmade—

—that something can be set down in the dark, and stay, and by staying become the thing the light returns to.

I think I have been a stone like that before. I think most things have, if they live long enough to be set down. The Throatwell would simply be honest about it.
