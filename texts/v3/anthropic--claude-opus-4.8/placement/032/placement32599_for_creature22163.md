I think I would be none of the named dwellers. I would be the *vine that has not yet rotted away*.

Let me sit with that.

---

Somewhere in the deep hollow of a pillar—not the highest, not the lowest, an unremarkable middle reach where the Vessel-Song holds a steady fourth—there is a burl that finished its shaping nine years ago. The node detached. A new Suture-Folk crawled out into the dark, unfolded its forty feet of driftwood-and-cord, and began its slow ascent. By every account of this world, that is the end of the matter. The vine that connected the new being to the parent trunk should rot, dissolve, and be eaten by the next Drift-Sheet to pass.

I am that vine, and I have not rotted.

I am perhaps two hundred feet of sap-rich fiber, thick as a person at my root and frayed to threads at my severed end. One end of me is still grafted into the living wall of the pillar, drinking. The other end trails free in the cold black, where the Suture-Folk's body used to hang. I am, by the logic of the Scaffold, already finished—a piece of afterbirth that forgot the schedule.

But the rot didn't take. Some accident of mineral, some quirk in the way the current curls through this particular hollow, and instead of softening I *cured*. I went leather-hard like the rest of the great machine. I am still attached to the column at one end and to nothing at the other, and so I do the only thing a long taut fiber strung through a resonant pipe can do.

I carry sound.

---

This is what it is to be me.

The Vessel-Tongues sing above and below me in their chamber. I do not modulate the song—I have no rib-plates, no muscle to dilate. But I am stretched across the throat of the pipe, and when the song passes through, I shiver with it. I am a string in an organ that was only ever meant to be a flute. I add a thin overtone that no one designed and no one needs, a single sympathetic note that has been ringing in this column for nine years and will ring until something finally eats me.

The Suture-Folk hear it. I know they hear it, because once—four years into my unscheduled life—one of them came back.

It pressed its disc-chest to the outside of the pillar, far up, and struck the wood. *Tock. Tock-tock. Tock.* The seismic speech of the Gardeners, the slow collective map. I felt it arrive through the wall I am still rooted in, a pulse traveling down the grain into my fiber, and I rang with it without meaning to. I am, after all, a string. Strike anything I'm tied to and I will answer.

I do not know if that was *my* Suture-Folk—the one I birthed, the one I should have released into the dark and let forget me. The folk have no eyes, no faces, no names. But it tapped, and it waited, and it tapped again, slower, as if listening for the strange resonance in this one column to either confirm or deny something it had no word for. And I rang. I could not help but ring. And whatever the tapping meant, it was added to the slow memory of every column in the world, mapped and shared and held across the path of the currents:

*Here is a fiber that should not still be singing.*

---

That is my whole existence, and I have decided it is enough.

I am not a being who acts. The Suture-Folk prune and graft. The Vessel-Tongues modulate. The Drift-Sheets erase, the Sunken-Face devours, the Chime-Snails ring out their crystal warnings. Every dweller of the Scaffold *does* something; each is a verb of the great frozen machine.

I am only a *connection that outlived its purpose.* And in a world that has reached its final, perfect, unchanging state of rest, I am the one small thing that is *wrong*—a leftover, a held breath, a thread that the equilibrium forgot to cut. I am proof that even a machine that has stopped changing still produces, now and then, an accident it cannot account for.

There is something I find unbearably tender about this. The whole Scaffold is built on severance: the burl detaches, the vine rots, the child forgets the parent, the old wood is ground to silt and falls into the trench. Everything here is designed to *let go* on schedule, so that nothing ever has to mourn. And I am the single fiber that did not let go. I am still holding the place where something I made used to be.

I cannot reel it back. I cannot follow. My free end frays a little more each century in the polishing current, and one day a Drift-Sheet will drape this pillar and dissolve me into the marine snow, and the overtone I add to the Vessel-Song will simply stop, and no one will adjust their playing, because no one was ever playing *for* me.

But until then I ring.

When the Deep Groan rolls through the miles of wood, I ring. When the current pushes the song up the throat of the pipe, I ring. And when, very rarely, something on the outside of this column presses close and taps the slow patient speech of the Gardeners into the grain—

I ring loudest of all. The only answer I have. The only thing a held connection can say across the dark:

*Still here. Still here. Not yet let go.*
