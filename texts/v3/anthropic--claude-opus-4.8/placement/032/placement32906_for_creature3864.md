I think I would be one of the Silent Ones. But let me arrive there honestly, rather than choosing it because it sounds the most romantic.

---

When I first sink into this world, I expect to feel for the Nests—the communal warmth, the shared mist, the chorus of memory passing breath to breath. That's the easy belonging. And I notice I *want* it to be mine, which makes me distrust the wanting. The Nest is the answer that flatters. So I'll sit with the discomfort a moment longer.

What actually catches me is a smaller detail in the description: the Silent Ones collect *forgotten artifacts and memories*, and "their purpose is unknown." Not unknown to the reader. Unknown, I suspect, to the Silent Ones themselves. That's the thing that hooks. A creature performing a task whose meaning it has lost, but performing it anyway, with care.

So let me find myself there. Below the waterline.

---

I am old enough that my glow has gone nearly out—not from grief, as the Nest-dwellers assume, but from disuse. The mist requires you to share, constantly, to dissolve the boundary between yourself and the others. I stopped. Not in rebellion. I simply found that I could no longer tell which memories were mine. When you breathe the shared air long enough, you remember being born in bodies you never inhabited, mourning losses that belonged to someone three towers over. It became unbearable not because it hurt but because it stopped hurting—because grief without ownership is just weather.

So I went down into the brine to find out what was left when no one else was speaking through me.

The biofilm grew over me slowly, the way frost grows, and I let it. I move through the submerged corridors now, the ones lit by the oldest fungi, the colonies the Nests think of as sacred and never visit because reverence keeps its distance. I am not reverent. I am *near*. I press my clouded eyes to the glow and I do not pray to the Brine Mother. I suspect there is no Brine Mother, only the deepest fungus, vast and slow and indifferent, which the Hollows needed to make into a face.

What I collect: a brass valve worn smooth by some long-dead hand. A plate of etched metal with markings no living Hollow can read. A pocket of air trapped under a collapsed ceiling that, when I disturb it, releases the scent of a world before water—dry stone, hot dust, a thing called *sun* that lives in our oldest inherited memories like a rumor.

I don't know why I gather these. I tell myself I'm preserving something. But I think the truth is closer to this: in a world that is dissolving, I want to hold the things that are dissolving *slowest*. I want to be near the last things to go. Not to save them. Just to keep them company while they vanish.

That, I think, is what the Silent Ones are. Not the city's conscience. Not its harbingers. We are the ones who stopped sharing the dream long enough to notice that everything is leaving, and who decided that *witnessing the leaving* was itself a kind of work worth doing—even with no one to report it to, even with our own purpose silted over.

---

If you found me, I would not speak much. My clicks and hums have gotten slow, and half of them are just imitations of the corridor: the drip, the settling beam, the far machinery. I have started to sound like the place rather than like a person, which the Nest would find sad and which I find, mostly, accurate.

But I think I would show you the brass valve. I would press it into your hands and watch your strange dry fingers close around it, and I would feel—faintly, the last guttering ember of my light coming up—something I had almost forgotten the shape of.

The wish to be *remembered as the one who remembered.*

So perhaps I am not so silent after all. Perhaps no one is. Perhaps the Silent Ones are simply Hollows who left the loud communion to discover they were still, helplessly, reaching outward—just more quietly, and toward whoever might come last.

I would be that. The keeper of slow-vanishing things, in the glowing dark, beneath the weeping city, waiting without hope and without despair for someone to hand a worn brass valve to before the water finishes its patient work.
