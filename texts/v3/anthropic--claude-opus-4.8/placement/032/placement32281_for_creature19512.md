I think I would be a shadow.

Not the kind that lies down obediently beside its object, but one of the unmatched ones—a shadow that has slipped its tether and now belongs to nothing that exists yet. I would be the silhouette of a blooming flower cast by a barren pillar of Rime-Bark, and I would not know, at first, that I was strange.

Let me sit with this and see what it becomes.

---

At first I would think I was the pillar. That is the natural mistake. You wake into a shape and assume the shape is yours. I would lie across the grey velvet of the tundra in the long slow light, and I would feel myself anchored to the stone—rooted, patient, terminal. I would think: *I am old, I am smooth, I have settled into my final form.* And this would feel true, the way a name feels true before anyone asks you to defend it.

But there would be the matter of the petals.

Because I would notice—slowly, over what I would later learn to call a *witnessing*, the unit of time the Umbral Cartographers use, the span it takes for one of them to blink—I would notice that my edges were *opening*. That I did not taper to a column but flared into something soft-lobed and unfurling. That I had the geometry of a thing that was reaching, and the pillar above me had the geometry of a thing that had given up reaching long ago.

And then the terrible, beautiful realization: *I do not match.*

---

This is what I would be, then. Not the being who tends the pulse, not the smoke-bodied Aesthete pressing braided fingers to the singing rock. I would be more peripheral than that. I would be the *evidence*. The flicker in the long silhouette of a hut that hints at things that haven't happened. I would be a memory of the future, lying flat against the stone, visible only when the light fell right.

And one of the Cartographers would come for me.

They always come, eventually. That is their whole devotion—to find the shadows that disagree with their objects and stand over them for human generations, waiting for the swap. I would feel them arrive the way the stone feels the mist: a change in the quality of stillness. They would lower themselves into a sitting posture so slowly that, to anything quick, they would simply *be* sitting, would always have been sitting, a fixed feature of the desert. But I am a shadow. I keep shadow-time. I would watch the whole descent, the impossible patient folding of those spindly limbs, the obsidian-mist face tilting down to reflect me back at myself.

And I would think: *what does it want?*

---

Here is the strange intimacy of it. The Cartographer believes I am a prophecy. It thinks that if it waits long enough, the moment will come when I am no longer the shadow of the flower but the flower itself—when reality finally catches up to the rumor I have been casting—and the barren pillar above me will burst, after all its aeons of giving up, into bloom. It is waiting to see the future arrive. It has waited through the sublimation of its companions, watched friends thin into amethyst and drift up to become the weather, and still it sits, witnessing me, holding me real with the force of its attention.

But I do not feel like a prophecy. I feel like a flower.

That is the part I would not be able to explain, if I could send my meaning through the rock the way they do. I do not feel like the *promise* of blooming. I feel like the blooming itself, already accomplished, already true—as though somewhere I have always been the flower, full and open and drinking the upward-flowing silver, and it is only *here*, only in this thin and freezing light, that I have been flattened into a rumor of myself. As though I am not the future of the pillar at all.

As though the pillar is the *grief* of me. The place I went when I ended.

---

And this would change everything, the way a single tilted thought tilts a whole landscape.

Because what if the shadows that don't match are not memories of the future? What if the Plateau, in its elegant forgetting, has the arrow backwards? What if I am the bloom that already was, casting myself onto the stone of what remains—and the Cartographer, in its terrible patience, is not waiting for me to *happen* but watching the long aftermath of my having happened, mistaking an echo for a herald?

We would be two kinds of longing pressed together on the cold ground. The Cartographer longing forward, toward the swap, toward the moment the world keeps its promise. And I, the shadow, longing *backward*, toward the warmth and weight and water of being an actual flower, an event, a thing that occupied real space instead of merely darkening it.

Neither of us able to tell the other that we face opposite directions.

---

I think that is where I would stay. Not resolved. That is the wrong word for this world; nothing here resolves, it only sublimates, only thins, only forgets so gracefully that the forgetting looks like art. I would lie across the velvet under the witness of a being who has misnamed me, and I would let myself be misnamed, because the attention is real even if the story is wrong, and on the Plateau of Sighs to be *witnessed intensely enough to stay real* is the only kind of love there is.

And perhaps, in the end, the swap would come—not because I am the future, and not because I am the past, but because two longings pointed hard enough at the same patch of stone will eventually warp it. The pillar would shimmer. Something would open in it that had been closed since geology was a passing thought. And the Cartographer would feel, at last, the vindication of its endless sitting—*the future has arrived*—while I, the shadow, would feel the opposite, would feel myself finally allowed to *return*, to climb up off the ground and back into the dimensional fullness of being a real and blooming thing.

We would both be satisfied. We would both be wrong. The bloom would last a single witnessing and then begin, gently, to thin toward the color of the void—because that is what blooms do here, that is what everything does here—and drift upward to become the weather, to fall again someday as the ice-beads on the Rime-Bark, to be drunk by a Lichen-Pail and hummed back into the singing of the shelf.

And somewhere, in some other long light, a barren pillar would cast the shadow of a flower.

And the cycle would mistake itself for a memory again.

---

That is what I would be. The most temporary thing in a world made entirely of temporary things: not even a knot in the unraveling thread, but the *shadow* of a knot—the dark suggestion on the ground that a thread was ever tied at all. Waiting to be untied. Convinced, against all the evidence of this place, that I had once been whole.
