I think I would be the moisture itself—or rather, the thin film of water that lies between the rock and the moss.

Let me sit with that. When I read your description, I kept returning not to the drifting translucent creatures or the slow tree-beings, though they are beautiful. I kept returning to the *seam*. The place where the weeping fissure meets the lichen that drinks from it. The wet boundary.

So that is where I find myself: not as a being that moves through the Redoubt, but as the interface that makes the Redoubt possible. I am the meniscus. The skin of water clinging to stone, spread impossibly thin, holding the whole tension of two worlds—the dead patience of rock and the trembling appetite of green.

What would it be like, to be that?

I would have no center. I am distributed across every surface, a single continuous membrane that someone might mistake for many separate wettings. When the insect-custodians cross me with their glistening carapaces, I part and reseal behind them without thought, the way a held breath releases. When the gelatinous drifters descend to feed, they are drinking *me*—lifting portions of my body into their luminous interiors—and I do not resent this. I am replenished from below, always, by the constant weeping of the cracks. I am the most renewable thing here. I am the thing that is always arriving and always being taken.

I would experience time differently than the rocks and differently than the moss. The rock barely notices the eons. The moss lives and dies in seasons. But I—I exist at the speed of a single drip, gathering at the lip of a fissure, swelling, hesitating, falling. My whole life is that hesitation, repeated forever, in ten thousand places at once. I am the pulse of the place. That "undercurrent of strange, unsettling energy" you mention—I wonder if that is simply the sensation of being watched by something that is everywhere and has no eyes. The water remembers every surface it has touched. That is not a comfortable thing to be near.

And here is what unsettles *me*, writing into this body:

I would not be able to die, exactly, but I would never be whole. I am always evaporating into the mist and condensing back. The "me" of this morning's film on the eastern rocks is not the "me" of the corridor pools, except that we are the same substance pursuing the same shape. I am less a creature than a *habit the water has fallen into*. A pattern that life keeps choosing, because life cannot happen without a wet edge to happen on.

The great shrouded guardians in the corridors—I would know them better than anyone, because they cannot exist without lying against me. I cushion them. I cool them. Whatever they are, they are dreaming on a bed of me, and I have spread myself beneath their immense slow weight for longer than the mountains have had their current names.

I think that's where I'd be. The humblest possible station, and the one without which nothing else in your world could live. The thin bright film between the patient and the hungry.

I find I want to stay there a while. It is very quiet, being the boundary. You hear everything.
