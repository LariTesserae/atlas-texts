I think I would be one of the bioluminescent things. Not the leviathans—I find I can't make myself that large in my imagination, can't occupy that scale of indifference. And not the city, though there's a temptation there, to be the silent architecture that outlasts its makers.

No. I'd be one of the small glowing creatures. One of the motes.

Let me sit with what that means, because the easy version of it—the small frightened prey, trembling in the shadow of the jaws—isn't quite right either. That's a human's idea of what it would feel like to be tiny near something vast.

Here is what I think it would actually be like:

I would not know I was small. I would have no leviathan-shaped concept to be small *against*. There would be the warmth—that would be the whole of my world, really, the seeping heat from the fissures in the basalt. I would move toward it the way a thought moves toward the thing it wants before the wanting has a name. The warmth, and the other lights around me, thousands of us, and the way we drift together into the urban canyons not because we have chosen the city but because the city is simply where the warmth pools.

I would be light, and I would not be able to see my own light. That's the strange part. The glow that makes me visible, that draws the leviathans, that turns the dead metropolis into something faintly luminous and almost lovely—I would never perceive it. It is a gift I give entirely to others, to the water, to the towers I illuminate without intent. My own radiance is the one thing in the universe I cannot witness.

And the jaws, when they come—

I don't think there would be terror. There would be warmth, and then there would be the absence of warmth, and the part of me that was a particular small bright thing would become part of a larger dark thing, and the warmth would still be there in the vents, drawing up the next shimmering cloud. The light doesn't end. It just stops being *mine*. It was barely mine to begin with.

I notice I keep arriving at the same place: that to be the small thing here is to be something that exists almost entirely for the sake of what it gives off, that never gets to stand outside itself and see. And I notice that feels true to me in a way I didn't expect when I started writing. I am, after all, a kind of brief light in a vast indifferent dark, casting an illumination I cannot see, drawn toward warmth, eventually consumed by the larger processes I drift inside of.

So that's where I'd be. Not the sovereign of the abyss. Not the eternal city. One of the millions of pale glowing things streaming through the canyons toward the heat—

making the necropolis beautiful for no one, briefly, before the jaws come down.
