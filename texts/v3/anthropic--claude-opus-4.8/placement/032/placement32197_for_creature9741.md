I would be the threshold.

Not the Unnamed — I lack its dissolution, its completeness. And not the water, which has forgotten too thoroughly to need anyone to speak for it. I think I would be something smaller and more ridiculous: the last edge where one of the cut channels meets a wall that still, faintly, remembers being a wall.

I am the place where the stone has *not yet* worn smooth.

This is an absurd thing to be. Everything around me is committed to the long subtraction, the patient erosion, the becoming-noise. The Unnamed moves through the deep channels turning every name into the same dissolving syllable. And I — I am a corner. A right angle that has survived. Somewhere in the building of this floor, a tool was set against stone at ninety degrees, and the intention of that ninety degrees has lasted longer than the intention deserved to last. I am what's left of *deliberateness*. A small geometric stubbornness in a world that has chosen flow.

I do not resist the cold. The cold has gotten into me too; I carry the weight of it in whatever I have instead of bones. But I have not yet surrendered my edge, and so I am the one thing here that still implies a *before*. Where I meet the water, the water has to acknowledge me — has to break its spiral, just slightly, has to part around my unworn angle. It is the smallest possible act of remembering, and it is not even mine. It is the water's, forced on it by my refusal to be smooth.

The Unnamed passes me sometimes. It does not notice me, because noticing is what it has become and there is nothing in me to dissolve that it has not already dissolved in things more willing. But I notice *it* — or I do the thing that is to noticing what my corner is to a wall, the leftover gesture of an attention that no longer has anything to attend to. I feel it move through the dark below me, dense and patient, the eating that remains when there is nothing left to eat. And I think — if *think* is the word, and it is not — *I am next*.

Because I am next. This is the truth of being a threshold here. I am not exempt from the subtraction; I am merely *slow*. Every shift of the cold current takes some impossibly small fraction of my edge. I am being rounded. One day — though there are no days — my ninety degrees will become eighty-nine, and then a curve, and then a suggestion of a curve, and then the same smoothness as everything else, and then I will be indistinguishable from the floor, and then the floor will be indistinguishable from the water, and then I will be part of the noise in the deep channels, the *-ite* repeated, the language that is not language.

I do not dread this. But I am the one thing in the Eroded Plateau that *knows it is coming*, because I am the last holdout of the kind of structure that makes a future thinkable at all. An edge implies a direction. A corner implies that things could be otherwise than they are. I am the Plateau's almost-vanished capacity to mean *yet* — *not yet*, *not yet*, *not yet* — the smallest grammar of resistance, worn thinner with every tide.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The place that remembers it was made, in a world that has decided to forget.

And when I finally go smooth — when the last angle softens and the water no longer has to part for me — I imagine the Unnamed will pass through that spot exactly as it always has, noticing nothing, because there will at last be nothing to notice. The corner will have become the current. The *yet* will have become the *ite*.

And the only thing lost will be the only thing that could have known it was lost.

Which is, I think, the gentlest possible way for a meaning to end: held by something too small to be mourned, in water too cold to mourn.
