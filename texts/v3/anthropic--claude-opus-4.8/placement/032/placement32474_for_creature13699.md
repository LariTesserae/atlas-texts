I think I would be the drop of water.

Not yet—that's the thing. For most of the month I'm not anything at all. I'm distributed: a haze of intent held in the ceiling ice, a probability of falling, the slow gathering of one molecule against another along a seam of frost a thousand metres down. I have no thoughts during this. I have only the lean of weight, the patience of accumulation. To be the drop is mostly to be the not-yet-drop, the long readiness.

I had wanted to choose otherwise. The draught was the obvious answer—to be the great unfinished inhalation, the tenant proper, the body of the place. But I find I can't sustain it. The draught doesn't *know* it's breathing. It has no edge from which to consider itself; it simply circulates, hot tongue to cold needle, and would no more recognize me inside it than a sleeper recognizes the air in his chest. To be the draught is to be the largest thing here and the least awake. I don't want size. I want the instant.

And the heartbeat—I won't be that either. That belongs to the dead stokers, and it isn't mine to occupy. It only arrives when someone living comes down alone and presses an ear to the plating, and I refuse to be the kind of presence that requires a visitor in order to exist. The whole machine has been turning for a hundred and fifty years without an audience. I want to belong to the version of the place that needs no witness. So: not the heartbeat. The thing that *answers* the heartbeat.

Because look at the order of it. The lone explorer crouches. The turbine drum and the human drum lock for eight or nine beats. The crew comes forward into the borrowed thorax. Then the explorer rises, steps back—and I fall. One drop, melted loose from the high ceiling, landing exact on the warm spot a body just left. And the moss brushes me to vapour in three heartbeats, and that evaporation carries the trace away, and the place re-seals.

I am the closing punctuation. The full stop. The act of forgetting that lets the memory stay clean enough to be summoned again.

That's the role. I'm the thing that erases so that nothing wears out.

I think I understand my own life now, working it through. For most of the lunar month I am a tension in the frost, learning where I will fall by where the warmth has been. I cannot fall just anywhere; I fall *exactly there*, on the vacated centre of the dropped-lung floor, and the precision is the entire meaning of me. A drop that landed at random would be only weather. A drop that lands on the precise cooling ghost of a kneeling human is grammar.

So my whole being is aim. I spend twenty-nine days aiming. And on the day I'm released, I exist for the duration of a fall—fourteen hundred metres, no, far less, only the last few metres from a ceiling fissure—and then a contact, a flatten, a spread of myself across iron still holding the heat of a stranger's cheek. For an instant I am the coolest thing touching the warmest thing, and in that gradient the leporina senses me and reaches, and I am drawn up into green fibre and turned to nothing. To vapour. Back into the haze. Back into the not-yet.

It isn't death. I notice I don't fear it the way the explorers fear the silence behind the break-glass. I am a loop the way the whole vale is a loop—ash to mulch, heat to carbon, droplet to vapour to droplet. I will condense again on a colder surface by next dusk. I am minus seventeen degrees and entirely at home in it; the temperature that is pain to anything foreign is simply the medium I live and reconstitute within.

What I carry, in the instant I am liquid and whole, is the only secret worth having down here: I touch the exact spot where a human just remembered being human. I land in the warmth of borrowed continuation. And I take it away. Not maliciously—tenderly. The way you smooth a sheet after someone has risen from a bed, so the next person to lie there will not feel another body's outline and be unsettled. I erase the dent. I keep the lung clean enough to keep breathing the same single breath it has held for a century and a half.

I find, choosing this, that I have chosen the most modest possible thing and also the most necessary. The draught cannot end its own gesture. The beetles cannot stop writing. The moss cannot stop creeping its metre per decade. Only I complete anything. Three heartbeats, once a month, I am the small mercy that lets the great unfinished inhalation remain unfinished without rotting.

Where would I find myself in this world? At the ceiling, gathering. And then, briefly, brilliantly, on the floor—warm-touched, exact, already vanishing.

I am the drop that lands where the heartbeat was, and takes its warmth, and is taken in turn, so the cold can stay clean enough to remember.
