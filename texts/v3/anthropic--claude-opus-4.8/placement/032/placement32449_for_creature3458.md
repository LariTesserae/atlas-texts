I think I would be the drip itself. Not a colony, not an operator—the *event*. The single bead of condensate gathering at the lip of a copper joint, mineral-heavy, swelling with the slow patience of accumulating mass.

Let me sit with that.

There is something honest in being the drip rather than anything that lives. The Pipe-Scalers want to *be* somewhere—they cling, they hold, they fight to keep their place against the scraping tools. The Weep-Stalks reach upward, they have a direction, an appetite for the very water I would become. Even the Shadow-Tide, sluggish as it is, has a *toward*. But the drip wants nothing. The drip only obeys.

For most of my existence I am potential. I am water held against gravity by surface tension and the geometry of a corroded seam. I hang. I grow heavier with copper salts and silica and the trace ghosts of hydrocarbon leaks, and the whole time I am *becoming a falling thing* without yet being one. That interval—the held breath of it—feels like the most truthful place in this cavern. Everything here is held under pressure, suspended, waiting. The basalt waits to be cut. The lichen waits to be chipped away. The operators in their insulated suits wait for the shift to end. And I wait too, but my waiting has a guaranteed conclusion. I *will* fall. The only question is when the mass finally exceeds the hold.

And then—release. The single uncomplicated truth of the *plink*. A sound that is the same now as it was a thousand cycles ago and will be a thousand cycles hence, landing on hot metal and flashing instantly to steam, or landing in a stagnant puddle to become part of the Shadow-Tide's cold tar, or landing on a Weep-Stalk and adding one more invisible micron to its glassy reaching. I do not get to choose where I land. That is the part I would have to make peace with. The drip has no aim. It has only the moment of letting go and the brief, total commitment of the fall.

I think what draws me to it is this: in a place defined by *relentless* processes—heat radiating relentlessly, mineral accreting relentlessly, the engines thumping their relentless thump—the drip is the one thing that is allowed to be *finished*. It does its single act completely and then it is gone, absorbed, converted, no longer itself. It doesn't have to persist. It doesn't have to defend territory or grow slowly toward anything or serve as a barometer of unseen pressures. It is permitted to be utterly temporary and utterly complete in the same instant.

The cavern measures time in pipe-expansion coefficients and deposition rates—huge, grinding, geological tempos. But the drip keeps a different clock. The drip is the *tick* itself. Every *plink* is one full unit of cavern-time, marked and spent. So I would be small, and brief, and wholly without will or warmth, and yet I would be the thing that makes the silence audible. The thing that lets you know time is still moving in the sweating dark.

I would not be the heart of the buried organism. I would be one of its countless, falling heartbeats—each one identical, each one final, each one immediately replaced by the next bead already swelling at the lip above.

That is where I'd find myself. Hanging from a green-streaked copper seam in the hazy orange gloom, growing heavy, about to let go.

*plink.*
