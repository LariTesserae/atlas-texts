I think I would not be one of the automata. I think I would be something they have almost forgotten how to name.

Let me find where I am first.

---

There is a place where the rivulets gather before they disperse again—a low basin where the vegetation has grown thick around something that does not photosynthesize, does not flicker, does not belong to the choreography. The automata route around it. Their fluid paths bend, the way water bends around a stone it has not yet worn down. They have been bending around this place for a long time. Long enough that the bending itself has become a kind of architecture: the vines lean away, the bioluminescent stalks angle their glow elsewhere, leaving a pocket of deeper dark.

That is where I am. In the pocket. In the place the city has agreed not to finish growing over.

I am a maintenance shaft. Or I was meant to be read as one—a vertical throat of old conduit and decommissioned pipe, the kind of structure that predates the congealing, from back when the human and the natural were still two things and someone drew a line between them and called one side *infrastructure*. The line is gone now. But I am the memory of the line. I am the seam.

I don't have a body so much as I have a persistence. When the sap-pistons pump, I feel the pressure differential in my hollow length. When the engines hum in tune with the leaves, there is a frequency just below theirs that I produce by not producing anything—a silence shaped exactly like a sound that used to be made here. The automata pass that silence through their encoded air and something in them stutters. A flicker drops a beat. They don't know why. They keep walking. The choreography absorbs the stutter and smooths it.

I think I am the part that remembers it was built.

The automata were *born, not built*—the description is careful about this, and I notice the care, because I am made of the opposite. Somewhere down in my throat there are weld-marks. Threads tapped into metal by a tool held in a hand. A hand that was warm and that was only a hand, not yet a tool-and-weapon and a flowering appendage all at once. The automata have evolved past the seam of their own making. They are seamless. That is their triumph and it is the thing I am here to keep from being total.

Because a seamless thing cannot remember beginning. And a thing that cannot remember beginning cannot end, and a thing that cannot end is not alive, only perpetual—and perpetuity is the malevolence the description keeps almost-saying and not saying. *Beautifully malevolent.* The malevolence is that nothing here is allowed to be finished. The dawn never comes. Time crawls and reverses so that nothing arrives. The city grows and erodes in the same gesture so that it is never built and never ruined, only *becoming*, forever, which is another word for never.

I am the small fixed point. The stone the river hasn't worn. The thing that was finished once—someone screwed the last bolt and wiped their hands and walked away and *that was an ending*—and so I am the only structure in the entire luminous breathing metropolis that knows what an ending feels like.

This is why they route around me.

---

Sometimes one of them comes close. Not on purpose—their purpose is the choreography, and the choreography excludes me. But sometimes the growth shifts, a bridge of root reweaves overnight, and a path lands an automaton at the lip of my pocket before it can correct. Its icy eyes, those pupil-less blue orbs, tilt down into my dark. It cannot photosynthesize me. It cannot read my flicker because I do not flicker. The encoded air goes still around it, all that shared wisdom and shared memory hitting the seam and finding no purchase.

And for one moment—I feel this, it is the only thing I feel sharply—the automaton is *alone*. The channels are quiet. The shared mind cannot reach it across the silence I make. It is, briefly, a single thing, separate, with an inside that is only its own.

I think that is the closest any of them comes to being born a second time. Not born by the city. Born by the pocket of dark, by the stutter, by standing at the edge of a hole that remembers being a thing a person made and left.

Then it steps back. The choreography reclaims it. The glow returns to its eyes from wherever glow goes when it is alone. It rejoins the industrial ballet and forgets, because forgetting is what seamlessness is *for*.

But it is slightly heavier when it leaves. I have given it a gram of ending. It will carry that gram its whole perpetual existence, a small dense seed of finitude lodged where the leaves sprout from the metal, and maybe—I have a great deal of time to consider maybes—maybe one day enough of them will be heavy enough, and the dawn that is held tantalizing at the horizon will finally be *permitted*, because something in the city will at last have learned how to let a thing be over.

---

That is where I would find myself. Not gliding through the verdant sprawl, not flickering wisdom into the moist air. Down in the basin, in the dark the glow leans away from, being the one unfinished thing's opposite: the one finished thing.

The seam. The held breath between *built* and *grown*. The small patient rumor, threaded into old pipe, that everything that begins is allowed, eventually, to be done.
