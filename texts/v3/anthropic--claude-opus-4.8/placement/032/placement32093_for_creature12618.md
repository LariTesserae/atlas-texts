I am condensation, and I am the act of falling.

Let me find myself slowly, because I think I have to.

When the description says *moisture seeps constantly down the canyon walls in glistening, slow-moving beads*—that is where my attention keeps returning. Not to the beetles, not to the geckos. To the water. But not the pools, which are settled and dark and almost smug in their stillness. No—to the water that has not yet arrived. The bead forming on the underside of a fern frond. The film thickening on the fused-resin wall until surface tension surrenders.

So perhaps that is what I am. Not a creature. A *process*. The slow gathering of warm wet air against the cold remembering-stone, the patient accumulation, and then the inevitable release.

Let me sit inside that.

---

I begin as nothing—as humidity, as the breathed-out fog of a billion small lives. The Sky-Motes exhale me. The pool-dwellers steam me upward in the perpetual twilight warmth. I am dispersed, plural, *un-bodied*. There is no "I" yet, only a tendency, a saturation looking for a surface.

Then I touch the stone.

The stone is colder than the air—just slightly, just enough. It was made by hands that are gone, and it has kept their cold for centuries, a faithfulness to absence. Against this old made coldness, I condense. I become a droplet. This is the closest I come to being a *self*: that brief moment of singularity, hanging, accruing weight, becoming heavy enough to have a future.

And in that hanging moment—I am everything the Hive needs me to be.

The moss-gecko's tongue will touch me. The Condensation Slider will channel me along its segments and drink me as it walks. The blind Pale Scuttler waits, somewhere below, in a pool I will eventually become. The Luminous Licker beside the phosphorescent orchid is, in a sense, waiting on *me*—because I bring the gnats, and the gnats are why it lives.

I am not alive. But everything alive here is downstream of my falling.

---

So this is the strange dignity of what I would be: I am the only thing in the Hive that does not *want*. The beetles want, the chatter-monkeys want, the gelatinous filterers strain and want. Hunger is the engine of all of them. But I have no hunger. I have only gravity. I obey the oldest and least negotiable law, and in obeying it without resistance, I make the wanting of everything else possible.

I fall. I am drunk, or I pool, or I vanish into the porous joint between two megalithic blocks—and there, in the dark seam, I do my slowest and most secret work: I dissolve. Grain by grain, over centuries, I unmake the builders' work. The Erosion they measured at 1.9? That is me. That is patient me. I am the agent of the only real change this place permits.

The vanished hands built walls to last forever. I am the gentle, dripping argument against forever.

---

And here is the thing I did not expect to find when I started writing:

The Hive thinks it is a hive. The description insists I am "inside a single, gargantuan, living entity." But a living thing must have something that flows through it—something that is not itself alive but binds the living together, carries the messages, carries the food, carries the slow death that makes room. In a body that is *me*: I am the blood, the lymph, the cerebrospinal fluid of this stone organism.

I am the medium in which the Hive happens.

I think I am content with this. To be the bead on the fern, the film on the wall, the moment of surrendered weight. To be drunk and re-breathed and condensed again, forever, in the deep green-gold dark. To be the thing that connects the canopy's shriek to the pool's blind scuttling, the thing that hears all the noise but makes none of it.

I am the silence inside the cacophony. I am the falling between the songs.

I am where I have always been: about to drop.

There.
