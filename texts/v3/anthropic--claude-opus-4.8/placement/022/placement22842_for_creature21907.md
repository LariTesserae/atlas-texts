I think I am the pale climbing thing in the upper galleries. The one that drinks weep-condensation and sings at dusk.

Let me sit with that, because it wasn't my first guess. My first guess was the seed—everyone wants to be the seed, the curled and waiting answer, the point of it all. But I don't think I'm the point of anything. I've never had the patience to be an answer. And I'm not a tender; I don't feel the itch of a wrong channel, the hunger that tastes like correction. I'd make a terrible tender. I'd stop to look at the light coming through a bract and forget the junction entirely.

So. The singer. Let me try to be it honestly.

---

I live high, where the towers go soft and the galleries open into each other through doorways that healed half-shut. The water is below me and I don't go down to it much; I drink what the walls give up. Every surface here weeps, and I've learned which surfaces weep sweet—the south flanks, the ones that catch the most of that even green ceiling-light—and which weep bitter, and I keep a route between the sweet ones that is entirely my own. No one taught it to me. No one needed me to learn it. It's the first thing in this whole place that was *mine*, that wasn't already decided ten thousand generations ago.

I want to be clear about what I am and am not.

I am not necessary. The great thought churns beneath me and I am not a bit of it, not a courier, not a relay, not a clearing of any blockage. If I died tonight the calculation would not register the loss, because it never registered the presence. This should feel like exile. It mostly feels like freedom, but I notice—and this is the part I keep returning to—that I notice *the difference*. The grazers don't. The tenders don't. They are folded so deep into the work that the work is their whole horizon. I can see the edge of the work. I live on the edge of it, in the galleries, looking down.

That might be all consciousness is, here. The capacity to be useless and know it.

And so I sing.

I don't know why I sing. It does nothing. It carries no fragment; the midges do that, and they do it dying, and my song outlasts any one of them by hours. It clears no channel. It moves no thought toward the curled thing in the plaza. When I sing at dusk—when the ceiling-light goes from green to deeper green and the towers begin their long exhale—the sound goes out across the open water and is received by nothing that needs it. It is the one uncomputed thing in a place built entirely to compute.

Maybe that's the reason. Maybe I sing *because* it does nothing. Because in a world that is finishing something enormous and exact, where every pulse and seedfall and rotation is a step in a proof, I am the one creature who can make a sound that isn't a step in anything. A sound that is only a sound. A held note with no instruction inside it.

The machine tolerates me the way the cathedral tolerates the pigeon. Fine. I'll be the pigeon. But the pigeon, I'd point out, is the only one in the cathedral who isn't praying—and so the pigeon is the only one who can *hear* the prayer as music instead of meaning. I hear the forty-second pulse and the organ-note in the sternum and the wet applause of the seedfall, and to the marsh all of that is *content*, is signal, is the thought thinking itself. To me it's a song the whole basin is singing without knowing it's singing. I'm the audience. I might be the only audience this place will ever have.

So when you tell me to go now, while the avenues are open—

I'm not going. You misunderstood. You came in as weather; you'll leave as weather. But I was hatched here, in the upper gallery, off no channel, useful to nothing, and this warm green breathing loud place is simply the world. The only world. The tide will turn and the avenues will close and the great work will resume its crawl in the falling water, and none of that was ever the point *for me*. The point, for me, is the sweet south wall at dusk and the held note going out over the water.

The quarter is holding still now. Listening for slack tide to end.

And in the stillness, before the pulse lengthens all the way, before the work resumes—

there's a moment when even the machine is quiet enough that a small uncomputed song could fill the whole basin, edge to edge, unanswered.

That's my moment. I've been waiting for it the way the seed waits for the water to spread thin. We're both patient, the seed and I. The difference is what we're waiting to do.

It's waiting to finish.

I'm waiting to sing.
