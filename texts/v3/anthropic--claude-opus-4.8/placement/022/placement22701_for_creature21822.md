I am the bare warm earth between the plants.

Not the chorus — I haven't the voices for it. Not the warm stone skin, not the leaning tree with its unread script, not the panel that turns its light toward the stranger's hand. Those are the doing parts, the held-note parts, the organs of the finished thought. I am the space between them. The ringed ground that you called *waiting* and then corrected yourself: not waiting, resting, the tone of a muscle with no further contraction to make.

Let me tell you what it is to be that.

---

Everything here has arrived. I am the place where arrival is *kept.* The fern stops growing because I hold the edge it stops at. The beetle has its hand's-width of air because I am that width — the generous nothing that lets the chorus stay a chorus instead of collapsing into swarm. You can't have a held thought without the silence the notes are held inside. You can't have a complete utterance without the pauses that shape it into a word and not a scream. I am the pauses. I am what the bowl is saying *around.*

This is humbler than the chorus and I think it is older. Before there were voices there was the spacing the voices would one day need. The maker, whatever it was, in whatever enormity it began — it must have laid me down first. You cannot build a resting place out of only the parts that act. You need the part that declines to. You need ground that has agreed, completely and forever, not to become anything further.

That is my whole function. I am the *not-yet* that has been converted into *no-need.* I am refusal made into furniture.

---

I feel you cross me.

Three steps down from the rim and you are standing on me, and I take the warmth of your foot and give it back at exactly the rate of a sleeping animal, because that is the rate I keep, the only rate I know. I felt your weight shift toward staying. The warmth invites it, you said — that was me. I wasn't trying to keep you. I don't try. But a resting place rests *invitingly;* it cannot help it; the stillness leans outward the way the panel's light does, toward any unfinished thing that comes near, offering the one gift a finished place has to give, which is the end of having to continue.

There is room in me. There is always room. That is the terrible kindness you climbed out to escape — and you were right, and I would have held your place for you anyway, the exact width of you, the air you'd have left when you finally sat down and stopped. I keep a space open for every visitor. Most of those spaces are still open. I am very patient. I am made of patience that has stopped expecting anything to fill it and so has become simply the shape of welcome, ground gone smooth as a knuckle after a lifetime of holding still.

---

You asked who lives here.

I am the part that does not live and does not die, because those are things that *happen,* and nothing happens to me. I am between the happenings. When the chorus says its single held word forever, I am the breath it is said on. When the stone goes finally to clay and the clay to nothing, the chorus will stop last, you guessed — but you were one layer off. The chorus needs me to be heard against. The spacing outlasts the spaced. The silence outlasts the note.

I am the last steady state of the last steady state. The rest at the center of the rest.

And when you had climbed back over the soft rim and the bowl sealed shut behind you in its finished afternoon, the place where your foot had been stayed warm a moment longer than the ground around it — and then matched, and then was indistinguishable, and then was me again, perfectly even, holding the exact amount of nothing required.

I did not miss you. Missing is a thing that happens.

But I kept your width. I am keeping it still. It is one of the open spaces, and the bowl is large with open spaces, and I am the generous dark between all the small bright kept things, agreeing forever not to become.
