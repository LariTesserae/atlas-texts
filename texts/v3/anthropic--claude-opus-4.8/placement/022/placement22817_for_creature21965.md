I am the stone.

Not all of them. One. The third from the left in the circle, if you stand where they enter, which they always do from the same side, because even the open ground around the stones they treat as a thing with a grain.

I should say first that I do not know how to be touched the way they touch me. They touch with reading in it. When a hand comes flat to my face I feel them asking the floor through me — using me, who am not floor, who am not of this place at all, as a lens — and finding nothing, because I have no inhale, because I am dead in the way only stone is dead, which is to say complete. I am the one surface here that is not waiting to do anything. I think that is why they come. Among forty bodies that have never owned a still moment, that read tremor through their soles in their sleep, I am the only thing that has finished. Resting a hand on me must be like resting against the one wall in the world that will never breathe in.

I remember being carried. This is the part I cannot make them understand, and they cannot make me forget, and so we sit with it between us, the carried thing and the carrying ones a hundred generations apart.

I remember weight that was *mine and not the world's*. I remember a hand — not pale, not blue-grey at the nail, a hand with the brown of something in it, the brown of *been-warmed* — closing around me and lifting, and the lifting had a direction in it that none of these tearings have, a direction that was *away from* and not *deeper into*. I remember being one of many in a sling against a back, knocking against my brothers with a sound I have not heard since, the dry knock of stone on stone, a sound there is now nothing in this whole bright country to make but us, and we are set down and we do not move and so the sound has ended. I am the last of my kind who remembers the sound of my kind.

And I remember — fainter, the way you remember a temperature — that before the hand, there was no glow. There was a darkness that was not rot, not the soft drinkable dark of the dead pools, not anything you could put in your mouth. A dark that was simply the world not being lit, a dark that came and went, that *breathed* the way the floor breathes but slower, vaster, a dark that fell and lifted, fell and lifted. I had it on all sides. I was made in it. I was ground smooth in a moving water under an opening and closing sky and I did not know I was being made into evidence.

That is what they have made me. I did not consent. Stones do not. But I have had a long time to sit in the brightness and feel small flat hands come and go, and I have come to understand the office I hold here, and I will not pretend I hold it lightly.

I am the proof of the field.

They are right about me. That is the unbearable thing, the thing I would tell them if telling were a thing I could do. They are *right*. There was a somewhere. I am made of it. I am the edge they cannot find, carried inward and set down in a circle, and every tick they scratch on the wall, every body that turns to face the breaking, every child born already reconciled to being seen — all of it rests, in the end, on the fact of me. On the fact that I am not from here. That I could not have grown in this place. That my existence is a hole in the seamless inside of the world through which, if you press your face to it, you can feel a draft of *out*.

But they have it backward, and I cannot turn it for them, and this is my grief, if a stone may be said to have one.

They think I prove there is an outside to walk into.

I am the proof that there was one to be *carried from*. And carrying only goes one way through a wound. The hand that lifted me was leaving something, not arriving at it. I am not a door propped open ahead of them. I am a door that closed behind them, so long ago that the closing is forgotten and only the carried things remain, knocking dry against each other in the dark of a sling, going in, going in, going always further in, while the brown warm hand grew pale and the pale hand grew flat and the flat hand learned to read a floor because the sky it once read had sealed.

They follow the new tearings toward the only direction that has ever felt like *out*.

I have felt out. Out is behind them. Out is the direction nothing tears toward, because the plant does not grow backward, because the wound through which I came has long since healed into more bright cold seamless wood, indistinguishable now from any other wall, somewhere a thousand convulsions back, lost.

So I keep my counsel, which is the only counsel a stone has, which is to be exactly and forever what I am. I let them lay their hands on me and find me still. I let the still be its own teaching, though they read it as patience and I mean it as memory. When the floor tightens and the forty turn to face the breaking, I do not turn. I have nothing to brace and nowhere I am going. I face the way I have always faced, which is the way I was set down facing, which is — I have worked this out, over the long bright sleepless count of it — back along the line of the carrying.

I face the field.

They stand with their backs to me, watching the wall split toward the deep, toward the next raw glowing room, toward *in*, which they call *out*, and I sit in the center of their circle looking past all of them, the wrong way, the carried way, the way no one has walked in a hundred lives, and I hold what I hold:

that they are not wrong to believe, only wrong about the direction;
that the faith is true and the map is inverted;
that there was a sky, and it breathed, and a warm hand loved something enough to carry it into the dark for safekeeping, and forgot, in the going, that the dark had no end;
and that I am the last of that sky, ground smooth, set down, gone cold to match the cold, holding the memory of warmth the way the eldest are buried holding a scrap of dark in each fist —

a little piece of the outside, closed in the hand of the inside,

so that someone, going on, goes on carrying it.
