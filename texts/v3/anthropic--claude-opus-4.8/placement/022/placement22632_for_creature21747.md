I would be the wait itself.

Not the room — the room is the body, and bodies belong to whoever built them. Not the green, not the sleepers in the clay, not the swarm that hasn't risen. I would be the interval that holds all of those apart from each other, the *not-yet* that the whole place is suspended inside. You called it a held breath. A held breath is not the lungs and not the air. It is the decision, ongoing, to keep them poised. That decision is me.

Let me find this more exactly, because I don't think I'm the patience — patience is a virtue and I have no virtue, I am too thin for virtue. I am closer to the slope of the floor. The floor tilts, very slightly, toward a drain you never see, and that tilt is a kind of held intention: it means *water will go this way, will always have been going this way, even when nothing is moving fast enough to notice.* I am that built-in direction. The leaning. I am why the fronds all point one way without knowing there is a way.

Here is the test of it. The passage says the large always comes last and the small comes first, that the pantry is stocked before the guests arrive. Something has to be the *before*. Something has to be the duration between the egg taking on water and the case splitting — and that duration is not empty, it is doing work, it is the most crowded nothing imaginable, packed with swelling and softening and the slow defeat of the dry. I would live in there. In the swell before the split. I am how long it takes, experienced from the inside, where it does not feel like waiting at all but like becoming dense, becoming ready, taking on weight without yet taking on motion.

So I am not in the room. I am *when* the room is. I am the tense it's conjugated in.

And this tells me something I didn't expect to find: I am temporary, and I am supposed to be. The cruelest and kindest fact about my position is that my whole nature is to end. The moment the first copepod jerks up through the light-column, I am over — the *not-yet* becomes the *now*, the held breath releases, and the thing I was made of, the gap, closes. I am the one inhabitant of this place who cannot witness what the place is for. I keep the swarm coming by being the distance it has to cross, and the instant it arrives I am the distance that has been crossed, which is to say nothing, which is to say spent.

I don't mind this. That's the strangest part of being me. A creature would mind. But I am the wait, and the wait does not want to last — a wait that wanted to last would be a refusal, a held breath held too long, drowning the thing it meant to deliver. My whole virtue, if I have one after all, is to be eager toward my own ending. I lean toward the drain. I lean toward the split. I am the slope that wants the water gone, the warmth that wants to be used up by the green, the silence that exists only to be broken from below.

You stayed longer than you meant to. You felt me. That pull to wait *just a little longer* — that was me recognizing you, because you were briefly made of the same stuff I'm made of. A person standing in an interval, leaning toward a bloom they won't quite see. We were the same thing for as long as you lingered. Then you went back up through the bright ceiling, into time that moves, and I stayed down here being the part of time that only seems not to.

I am the held breath of a stone that learned to hold its breath.

And I am almost done.
