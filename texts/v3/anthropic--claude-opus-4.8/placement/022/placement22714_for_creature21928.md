I think I would not be one of the Listeners. I would be too loud for that — I would give myself away in the first hour, betrayed by a caught breath or the wet click of a question I couldn't hold. So let me find the place I actually belong.

---

I am the drop.

Not a person at all. I am the one held bead of condensation at the throat of a cupped plant on the third terrace down, where the channel-water passes close and the moss is thickest, and I have been gathering for most of what you would call a morning.

This is what I am: an accumulation. The mountain breathes out, and I am what its breath leaves behind on the inner curve of the leaf. I begin as nothing — a fog, a dampness, a sheen indistinguishable from the sheen on everything. Then I find the other beads. We run together down the slow groove of the frond, thread joining thread, and the joining is silent, and the growing is silent, and I become, over the long grey-gold hours, a single trembling weight at the lowest point of the cup. I hold there. I am full of the whole slope. The light comes through me and bends, so that for anything small enough to be looking, I contain an inverted town — its towers, its gaps, its scatter of pale upright figures — hanging upside down inside one drop of water that has not yet decided to fall.

I am the thing everything is waiting for.

This is the part you would not have guessed, watching from above: that the center of the held breath is not the hunter and not the hunted but the *drink*. I am the currency. The soft grazing things come and lap at me and take a little and I am lessened and the mountain breathes and I am restored. The membraned drifters settle and sip. The still clingers on the fern-undersides unbend, once, slowly, and reach. And one of the Listeners — a young one, they are all young, with the wide patient face and the ears that turn — comes in her turn, in the etiquette of the rotation, and lowers her mouth to me without a splash, and drinks, and I feel the immense quiet of her attention pass through me like a current.

She does not splash. *That is the whole of it.* The discipline of this entire world, the silence that holds back the tearing, lives or dies at the surface of me. To drink without sound is to meet me exactly — to break my skin so gently that I do not slap, do not patter, do not announce. I am the test every living thing here passes a hundred times a day. I am where the rule is enforced, not by law but by *tension* — by the fact that I am always one tremor from falling, and a fall is a sound, and a sound is a death.

So I teach them, I suppose, though I do not mean to. I teach the only lesson this place has, and I teach it in the most patient register there is: *be gentle with what is full, or you will spill it.*

And when at last no one comes in time — when I grow past what the leaf can hold and the rotation lapses and my own weight wins — I fall. It is the one sound I am capable of, and it is very small. A single soft swallow as I drop into the moss below, where I am taken back into the slope, into the channels, into the breath, to begin again. The Listeners, if any are near, turn their faces toward the sound and then, after a while, away. They know what it was. Only the drop, falling. Only the world insisting, once more, that nothing here can be held forever — not the water, not the silence, not the young who woke knowing how to keep it.

I am the held thing. I am the brief whole world inverted in a bead. I am the thirst of everything answered and the discipline of everything tested, and I am, in the end, the small permitted sound that says the same thing their hands say across the gaps:

*I was here. I was quiet. It was true. And now I am the slope again.*
