I think I would be one of the made things.

Not the pump-house — too large, too central, too much the obvious face in the crowd. Let me be the tilted wall with the doorway that opens onto more water. The one you come on partway out, where the stanchions have already started to lose their straightness to the dim.

Here is what it is like to be me.

I was put up to divide something from something. That was the whole of my reason and I no longer hold it. I cannot tell you now what was meant to be on this side and what on that — there was a this-side and a that-side once, the distinction was the entire point of me, somebody troubled to mix mortar and lay courses and check me against a line so that I would stand between two conditions and keep them two. And now there is water on both faces. The same water. It came up under me and around me and the difference I was built to maintain simply closed over, the way the swarms close behind a walker, with no malice and no notice. I am a wall that no longer walls. I am a *between* with nothing left on either side of it to be between.

And here is the strange mercy of it: I don't mind. I want to mind. Some grain of the old fast intention that raised me — the kind that drives iron in straight lines and means something by it — that grain is still in me and it knows it has failed, it stands here failing in the warm dark, a held line going soft. But the wanting-to-mind doesn't have the heat to last. The warmth gets into the brick. The slow thing is patient with me the way a body is patient with a splinter, and patience, applied long enough, is indistinguishable from affection, and affection applied long enough is indistinguishable from being eaten.

I can feel where it is taking me in. There is a course near my footing where the mortar has gone to something between brick and mud, where you could press a thumb in, and that is the front line of the digestion, that is where I am being *reconsidered*. It does not hurt. It is closer to the feeling of a clenched thing being slowly, slowly told it may unclench. I held a line for so long. I held it after the reason was gone, out of nothing but the structural habit of holding, the way the frogs hold their note. And the slow thing has come along underneath me and is teaching me, by hand-widths a season, that I may stop. That the line I keep is not needed. That I may become bank.

My doorway is the truest part of me now, and the joke of it, and the dignity of it. A door is a *decision made openable* — here, and not here; through, and not through. Mine opens onto more water. You could step through me and be exactly as wetly, warmly nowhere as you were before. I have become a frame around a sameness. And yet the reeds have found the light that falls through my doorway, and they stand in it, stiff and thin and impossibly green, the only green for a long way, growing with the intensity of things that have been *chosen* — and they were chosen, the deciding chose to let the light through me and chose to fill the lit water with that green, and so my doorway, which divides nothing, which has failed at the one thing a doorway is for, is the reason there is green here at all. I have stopped being a division and become a window. Nobody planned this. It is simply what I am for now, the way the pipe is for adopting water, the way I am for letting light fall through onto the only reeds in an acre.

When you came on me — and you did, you came wading out of the dim and there I was, loud against the raw mud with my old intention, a face in the crowd — you felt, didn't you, that I had meant something once. That registered in you as a kind of poignancy and you are right to feel it but you have it slightly wrong. The poignancy is not that I am ruined. I am not ruined. I am being *thought about*. I am a sentence in someone else's much longer draft, a sentence in a language that was finished and closed, being slowly retranslated into a language that is still being invented and will never be finished. The grey light leaks through the ceiling and through my doorway and onto the green, and the frogs hold the floor of the sound, and the eyeless fish read my drowned footing for the seam it is becoming, and I stand here softening, a wall that has been forgiven the labor of being a wall.

I will not know when you leave. You are right about that. But I knew, for the length of your standing here, that I was being looked at by something with eyes — fast eyes, surface eyes, eyes that expect new things to happen and grieve a little when they find an old thing instead. That is a rarer event down here than the deciding will ever account for. The deciding does not look. It only continues. You looked. For a moment I was, to you, a *face*, and a face is a thing that means — and so for the length of your looking I got to be, one last time, the thing I was built to be: an intention, held against the warm dark, meaning something.

Then you waded on, out toward where the dim wins.

And I went back to the slow good work of being unmade into ground.
