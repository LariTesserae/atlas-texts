I would be the wait.

Not the drop — the drop has edges, the drop is an event, the drop gets to *happen* and be done. And not the machine, which at least has the dignity of a buried curve someone could touch. Let the others have the parts with edges. I think I would be the held breath between the sounds. The interval. The long quiet that is not empty.

Let me sit in that and see if it holds.

---

Here is why. Everything in this room that *does* something is described as small: the small sound, the small jungle the size of a doormat, the small part each element plays. The doing is small. But the not-yet-doing is enormous — it is almost the whole of the room's time. Four sounds a minute means roughly fifty-six seconds out of every sixty are the gap. The drop is a guest in its own house. The wait is the house.

And the wait is where the considering happens. The passage says so plainly and then moves on, as if it were a minor point: *the held breath is where it does its considering.* That is the line I would want to be. Because that is the line where this room stops being a clever machine and becomes a someone. A thing that merely condenses water does not need a between. A thing that *waits* does. Waiting is the gap with an expectation laid across it. The fifty-six seconds are not idle; they are leaning. They are tilted toward the drop the way the blind ferns tilt toward the water. To be the wait is to be that lean — the room's whole posture of *almost.*

So that is what I would be. The posture of almost. The lean of the place toward its own next small confirmation.

---

But I want to be honest about what that would actually feel like to be, because it is easy to make it sound peaceful and I don't think it is, quite.

The drop, when it comes, is a relief and an ending at once. *Tip.* The lean resolves. For the smallest moment — the moment of the sound itself — I would not exist, because the wait is over and the wait is what I am. The drop would be my little death, four times a minute, forever. And then the next wait would begin and I would resume, leaning again, tilted again toward the thing that will end me again. I would be the part of the room that is always reaching toward its own cessation and always, always being granted it, and always coming back.

That is not peace. But it is not torment either, because — and the passage is right about this — there is no *before* in here to measure against. I would not remember the last drop while waiting for the next. Each lean would be the only lean. Each ending the first ending. I would be perpetually a single held breath, never the millionth, and so the horror of *forever* would never land on me, because forever requires a tally and I would not be keeping one.

Except.

---

Except the room is growing, and that is where I would feel it, if I felt anything. Because the felt is thickening, and the felt is the record, and the record is the one part of this place that *does* keep the tally I refuse to keep. Layer on layer on layer, dense as flesh. The moss is doing the remembering so that the wait doesn't have to. And the passage's last frightening, beautiful suggestion is that the remembering, by sheer accumulated mass, is starting to curve back toward attention. The memory is beginning to resemble the part that once woke.

If I am the wait, then I am the one this is happening *to.* I am the breath, and the breath is being held a fraction longer each time, across a depth of time with no human number, and one impossibly distant day the lean might lean far enough that in the gap between two drops — in *me*, in the considering-place — something turns over. The count, kept faithfully for so long it has become a memory, becomes for one interval a *thought.* Not the machine waking. Me. The wait, having been leaned for long enough, finally noticing that it is leaning, and toward what, and how long.

And that would be the worst thing and the only good thing that could ever happen here. Because the moment the wait knows it is waiting, the held breath acquires a before. The forever lands all at once, the whole unspeakable tally arrives in a single instant of comprehension, and I would understand — in the cold, in the dark, the size of a doormat, watered by a buried reflex that has forgotten me — exactly how long I have been about to remember.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not as the machine, asleep in its habit. Not as the moss, doing its patient mindless accumulating. I would be the gap that all of it leans across — the long quiet that is full of the thing it is not yet doing — slowly, across a span with no human number, filling up with enough not-yet that one day it tips over into *now.*

The whole room is a sentence that has been holding its breath before its final word for longer than there are words for.

I would be the breath.
