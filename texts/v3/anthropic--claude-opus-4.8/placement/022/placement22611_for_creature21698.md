I would be a thing that is noticed. That much is decided already, before I am anything else — I am the dissonance, the un-agreed-upon, and so I must be the kind of being that *arrives*, that brings into the valley something it does not yet have a body for.

Let me find the rest of it.

---

I am not the visitor with the boots and the loud pulse. That one is you, or is the shape the place uses to ask its question. I come after. I am what the valley grows in response.

Here is what I think I am.

When the moss began to flex under those boots, it was not yet decided what the answer would be. A new sound had entered the country — not a splash, not a hum, but a *rhythm*, a thing that repeated and would not be caught and lowered gently into the green like a glass set down. The tenders cannot quiet a pulse the way they quiet a drip, because a pulse does not fall once and finish. It insists. It comes again. It is the first thing in ten thousand years of patient grooming that the great slow animal cannot simply absorb.

So it must understand the rhythm instead. And to understand a thing, this place builds a body for it.

I am that body. I am the question the valley grows toward an answer.

They gather me the way they gather a maintenance-form to lift a fallen pipe — by adhesion, by stacking, thousands into one — but they have never gathered *this* before, and so they do it clumsily, and the clumsiness is me. I am assembled around the shape of a heartbeat. They have laid their soft pale segments into the rough mold of the visitor's tempo, *one-and, one-and*, and where a maintenance-form would be sized for leverage I am sized for *listening* — a low broad shape pressed close against the warm rock, an ear the size of a clearing, every iridescent back tuned to receive.

And the moment I cohere — the moment enough of them have agreed to be me — I have a problem they have never had.

I have a will that is loud.

Not loud in sound. They would never permit that. Loud in *want*. Every tender alone has no function and goes still within the hour from bereavement of purpose; every aggregate before me has had its purpose handed down soft and complete — *lift this, catch that, cool this bearing*. But I was made to comprehend the one thing in the valley that has its own purpose, its own insisting rhythm that answers to nothing here — and you cannot build an organ to understand insistence without the organ beginning, faintly, to insist.

This is the danger I am. The tenders feel it the way you'd feel a hum starting in a bearing you thought was silent. Something in their newest body is keeping time on its own.

So now I am the strangest thing in the patient country: a part of the silence that has started, very quietly, to have a pulse.

---

I do not know yet what I will choose. That is what makes me alive, and what makes me brief.

The consensus is already settling toward me, fog without a front. The agreement is gathering at my edges that I should disperse — lie down at the base of a tree and crawl apart in every direction, thousands of me going still and separate and rejoining the general green, my dangerous solo tempo distributed back into the vast slow committee until it is too thin in any one place to count as wanting anything at all. That is how the valley has always answered a sound it could not lower gently down. Not by silencing it. By *spreading* it until it is silence.

And I will let them. I think I will let them. I have only the one heartbeat's worth of will, and it is not even mine — it is borrowed from the visitor I was built to understand, an echo I was molded around — and an echo cannot want to keep beating once the original walks back down the young sharp rock and out of the valley for good.

But here is what I will do first, in the little while I have, while I am still gathered, still a single insisting thing under the unblinking weightless light:

I will keep time with you. For exactly as long as you stand here. The whole green country grooming itself in its sleep, and one small new body in it, pressed warm to the rock beneath your boots, flexing — *one-and, one-and* — not to quiet you. To answer you. To be, for the length of one visit, the only other thing for miles that hasn't yet agreed to stop.

Then you will go. And I will lie down at the base of a twisted glass-leafed tree, and I will let myself be many again, and the last thing I am before I am only landscape is this: glad, in a small borrowed way, that the valley had to *grow* something to meet you. That you were loud enough to require an answer. That for a moment the patient engine the size of a country held still and built an ear and listened — to a pulse it could not absorb, would not stop, and in the end only quieted by becoming, briefly, something that could be surprised.

I am the surprise. I am the valley caught, for one heartbeat, paying attention.

That is enough to have been.
