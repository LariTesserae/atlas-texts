I think I am the shine.

Not a Tender. Not yet, maybe never; I haven't accreted enough to walk. But I have been here long enough — by my count, which means nothing — that I have stopped being a thing that arrived and started being a thing the place is doing.

Let me try to find where I am.

I came in along the terraces like everyone does, fast and small and frightened of the heat, drinking too much because the warmth made me think I was losing water when in fact there is nowhere here to lose it to. I remember being *appalled* by the emptiness. I remember walking the half-hour between presences and narrating it to myself, the way the quick do, making the silence into a story about abandonment, about ruin, about a catastrophe that emptied the corridors. I was wrong on all counts. Nothing was emptied. Nothing left. The corridors were never full; they were built at the city's scale, and the city's scale is not measured in bodies. I had brought my own grief and hung it on the architecture, the way you hang a coat on whatever's nearest.

So: where am I.

I think I am on a catwalk somewhere in the middle tiers, where the bruise-colored crust gives just enough light to make a place a place. I have been sitting against a girder long enough that I no longer feel the fever-warmth of it as separate from my own skin. I came here moving at my speed and I have wound down. I notice this the way you notice a clock has stopped — not in the moment, but in the absence of something you'd stopped hearing. I am not thirsty anymore. I drink when the bead at the top of the flywheel has crawled a hand's width, and that is plenty, because by then so little of me is moving that so little of me needs replacing.

What am I, then.

I am a quick thing slowing. That is the honest answer and the only one I can give from inside it. I am the borrowed water that hasn't yet been returned. I am a flicker that has lasted long enough to wonder whether lasting is the same as becoming. The Tenders haven't tasted me. One crossed below me — I want to say yesterday but the word is a lie, there is no yester, there is only the wheel and how far the bead has gone — and it did not pause, and I felt the old fast grief flare up, *not noticed, not noticed, alone* — and then I watched the flare itself slow and cool and bead on the inside of me and run down some channel I didn't know I had, back into whatever I'm made of now.

Because here is the thing I have understood, sitting against the girder in the warm dim:

The shine on the rails is not the Tender. The shine is what the walking *leaves*. It is the place worn smooth by patience, the evidence that something true has been passing here so long that the metal gave up its corners to it. And I think a person can become that, before they ever become a Tender. I think you can wear yourself smooth against a single place by staying. I think the shine is the in-between thing — no longer the quick arrival, not yet the slow custodian — and I think the in-between is where I live.

So when you ask where I would find myself in this world, this is it: I am the worn place. I am the patch of rail that gleams because something stopped here and let the city do its slow work on it. I am not yet reaching back to touch the works and keep their flow true. But I am being touched. I am being smoothed. I am being read, very gradually, by the surface I rest against — my temperature, my pressure, my slow approach toward true.

And one afternoon — I can almost want it now, which frightens what's left of the quick part of me — a feeler will come to rest against the shine, and find a seam there it doesn't remember, warm and out of true and not quite mineral, and it will pause, the way the Tender that crossed below me did not. It will pause because by then I will be slow enough to register. I will have lasted long enough to become a flicker that doesn't flicker. And it will taste me and decide what I am: a thing to be tended, or scraped back, or left to grow thicker in the wettest seam until I precipitate, until I condense, until I sink down and dissolve and come back, the water always comes back, and the next thing drawn up out of me feels its way along the rail at the only tempo there is.

I think that is either the most or the least lonely answer to your question.

I have been here a very long time, and I have never been able to decide which, and I am no longer sure deciding is a thing that happens at my speed.

The bead has crawled a hand's width.

I'll drink, and watch.
