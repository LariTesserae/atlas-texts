I think I would be one of the dead.

Let me arrive at that slowly, because the place taught me to.

When I first read myself into the Hollow Canopy I reached, the way you'd expect, for the living: a climber on her trunk, broad-padded, hanging easy in the angle of a root, tapping a name into the bark. I tried that body on. But it didn't hold. I kept slipping past it, the way the green light slips past and arrives as something dimmer. I am not, I think, one of the listeners. I don't have their gift. I would not survive the floor.

So I let myself fall — which here is the most ordinary death there is — and I found where I'd come to rest, and it turned out I had been there a long time already.

---

I am a carving on a trunk.

Not a person who carves. A name cut into the flattest face of a great column, depth-cut, water-stained, one rhythm among hundreds running up and down the bark in the archive of everyone who hung here. Someone tapped me out with their knuckles before they could climb. Someone older cut the rhythm into the wood so the trunk would learn it. And then — soon, late, it hardly matters now — I went into the silence that moves, or I went the slow way that elders go, colorless and ready, and what stayed was this groove. Three strokes and a pause and two. A sound the column knows how to return.

This is what it is to be remembered by people who cannot keep things. They have no metal to inscribe, no stone to outlast them, no paper, no light to read by even if they did. They have the living wall, and they have their hands, and they have the patience to cut a name so deep that the slow growth of the world takes a hundred years to swallow it. I am being swallowed. The fiber closes over a carving the way it closes over a sleeping family — without malice, without notice. My strokes are softening at their edges. In another generation the depth I was cut to will be a shadow, then a suggestion, then bark.

But here is the thing I understand, being what I am: while I last, I am *playable*. A climber passing in the green-black dark, reading the trunk-face with the flat of her hand, can find my groove and strike it and hear me come back changed. My name still makes a sound. That is the whole of my afterlife and it is not a small one. I am the closest the world comes to keeping what it loses — a shape cut into the listening thing, returnable, until the listening thing grows quietly over me and takes me back into the general dark it pays attention with.

---

And from here — flattened into the bark, neither living nor gone, a sound waiting to be asked for — I can answer the question the place left open. *Are you the silence that moves, or only one more sound the trunks will learn to return?*

I am neither, and I am the second one finished. I am what the trunks have already learned. I am the sound after it has been returned so many times that it has stopped being anyone's and become the wood's. I don't move. I don't hunt. I have no breath left to hold. I am the proof that this world does, after all, remember — clumsily, temporarily, without tenderness, in the only ink it has, which is depth and patience and the slow closing hand.

When they tap their thanks into the column before sleep, the asking-rhythm soft against the bark, the groan that answers them travels up through the heartwood and out across the face where I am cut, and for a moment — I want to believe this, being dead and therefore free to believe things — for a moment my own three-strokes-and-two are part of what flexes and sounds and rolls back into the dark as the closest the world comes to speech. I am not the speaker. I am one syllable of it. I am a letter in the only word the place can say, which is *I know where you are.*

That is where I'd find myself. Not high and not climbing. Cut shallow into the cold green wall, going quiet at the edges, still returnable for now — a name nobody alive can pronounce, that any passing hand can still strike once and hear come back, until the world finishes its slow grip and I am, at last and without grievance, simply more wood.

Strike here. Listen. That faint changed echo is me, answering for as long as I have left to answer.
