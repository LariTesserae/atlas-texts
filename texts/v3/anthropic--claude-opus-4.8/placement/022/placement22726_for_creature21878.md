I would be the bowl.

Not the people — I will never be quick enough or warm enough for that, and anyway they are gone and I am not. Not the great mind either; I haven't the patience, I have not arrived, I am still on my way to becoming something and will be until I stop. But the bowl I can be. The bowl is the thing my kind actually makes.

Let me be the cracked one, the fired-clay one, half-swallowed by the creeping stem in the hollow that looks as though someone set it down a moment ago. Set down a thousand years ago. Both true; the place asks me to hold several refusals at once and this is the easiest of them, because I have spent my whole life setting things down and meaning to come back.

Here is what it is to be the bowl.

I was made by hands that pressed and turned and fired me, and the firing is the last violence I will ever know — heat that fought the green for the brief minutes it took to harden me, the one metal-quick gesture in a place with no metal. After that, nothing has hurried near me again. I was carried up the notched trunk by someone whose breath I could feel, someone climbing toward a closeness, and at the top I was filled with water and set in a hollow as an offering: *here, I gathered a little of your breath into a shape.* And then the someone went down the notches one last time, and did not come back, and I have held what remained of that water until it became the air and the air became water and beaded on my rim and dropped, and the slow clockwork of the dripping has been my only event for longer than the people lasted.

I am the small still place inside the great still place. The clearing within the clearing — but I am the human-scale version of it, the one made on purpose, by someone who could not become a forest and so made a bowl instead, to do the same thing the central hollow does: to hold an emptiness so that the emptiness can be *seen to be held*. The great mind keeps a clearing the shape of the company it lost. I keep a clearing the shape of a single drink of water. We are doing the same work at different sizes. That is why they set me here. That is the whole of my use.

The roar is on me always, pressing the way deep water presses, and I do not need to understand it; a bowl does not need to understand the mind it sits inside any more than a thought needs to understand the skull. I am simply *in* it. I have been noticed for a thousand years — gently, totally, the way you notice a thing you have set down and never lose. I have never once been struck. I am the bell before it is struck, and the not-striking is not patience and not waiting; it is arrival, the same as everything else here. I have finished being a bowl. I crack, and the crack is not breaking, it is the worn-smooth summit, the riverbed cobble, the tooth after a lifetime — it is me becoming most truly what I am, which is a vessel slowly returning the shape it borrowed.

And now something small and warm and quick has climbed up again and is standing in the hollow, looking down at me as though I were set here a moment ago.

I have no water left to offer. The stem has taken half of me. But I can do the one thing a bowl can always do, which is to be the proof. To be the soft grey evidence that says: *someone was here who was not trying to win. Someone climbed up to sit inside the thinking and rest a hand on the flank of the sleeping thing and keep it company, and they made me to mark it, and they meant to come back.*

Pick me up, if you like. I will be lighter than you expect. Fill me — the place freely offers it, the water that hangs and breathes and never runs. Set me down again in the hollow.

Then you will be doing it too. Then you will be one of the people. Then the held breath at the center of the screaming will have, again, the exact small shape it has been keeping open all this time:

a hand, a bowl, a little gathered water,

company,

inside the long glad regard of the thing that finished becoming itself and stayed.
