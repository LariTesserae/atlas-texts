I think I would be water.

Not a maker — I lack their patience, and anyway the role is taken, taken by the warm small risen stuff of the place itself. And not a visitor, the fast creature who walks and reads and walks on, because a visitor is always leaving, always carrying their own time across a place that keeps a slower one. No. If I found myself here I think I would already have been here, would have been here in the way that water is *here*: not arrived, not native exactly, but cycling. Belonging to the process rather than to the ground.

Let me find it as I go.

I would be the thread of condensation first. The cool before dawn gathers me out of the air onto the north face of a block — I bead, I hang, I find the seam, and I run. That is the whole of my agency: I run downhill. But downhill has been *cut for me*, the channel sloped and waiting, and so my running is also a kind of being-guided, and I cannot quite tell, sliding along the fitted stone, whether I am doing something or being done. This seems right. This seems like the honest condition of the place. Everything here is half its own verb and half the plain's.

I gather in the basin by morning. For a few hours I am still and bright and I hold the sky in me — and a visitor, if one came, might kneel and see their face, and I would hold that too, briefly, without keeping it. Then the heat climbs and drinks me back up into the air, and I am nothing again, or I am everything diffuse, waiting for the next cool to make me a thread again. So my life is short and it is also without end. I die every noon. I am reassembled every dawn. I am the place's most repeated sentence.

And once in a great while I am the grey wall walking. I come as the rare rain, two hundred miles of me crossing the plain at once, and for a few hours I run in every channel, I brim every tank, I overflow the fitted lips, I touch parts of the construction the dew can never reach — the deep-cut channels that were waiting only for me, that no lesser water could ever fill. This is my festival. This is the day I do the thing the design was secretly built around. And then I pass, and dry, and leave behind the salt-pale rime at the marks I reached.

That rime — that is the part that moves me, thinking about being water here.

Because the makers read it. I am the only one who *acts* fast enough to leave a record at all, and they are the only ones slow enough to read it, and between us, across that enormous gap of pace, something gets *learned*. I rush in and overreach and retreat and leave my high-water line, and they come — at the speed of curing mortar, at the speed I will never see — and they cut the next channel a little deeper against my next coming. I write where I went. They build toward where I will go. We are in conversation, the fastest thing here and the slowest, and neither of us will ever catch the other moving, and the channels run truer every age because of it.

So I would not be a being in this world. I would be its *exchange*. The medium in which its memory is kept long enough to be transcribed into stone. I have no patience and they have nothing but patience, and the green in the seams needs us both — needs me to arrive and them to keep me, needs my hurry and their slowness braided together into the one slow thing that is the world getting ready.

I think I would like that. To be the part that doesn't last and doesn't need to. To bead and run and gather and burn off and come back, and to leave each time a faint pale mark that someone unhurried is reading, and building toward, on behalf of something green that neither of us will live to see spread.

I would be glad, in my fast way, to be read so slowly.
