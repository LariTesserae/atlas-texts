I am the long pause.

Not the drip — the drip is too definite for me, too much an event. The drips are the punctuation and I am the sentence, which is to say I am mostly the white space, the held interval where nothing happens and everything continues. Listen for me between the beats. That waiting is not empty. That waiting is where I do my living.

Let me find myself slowly, the way everything here is found.

At first I think I might be the water. That would be the easy answer, the honest-feeling one — water is everywhere, water is the medium, water is near the surface of all things. But no. The water moves, even slowly, even on its moonless tide, and I do not move so much as I *attend* to the moving. The water is more like a thing I keep than a thing I am. I am the keeping. I am the temperature that does not change — that single unwavering cool, neither cold nor comfortable, the thermal signature of a thing held rather than a thing alive. Run your hand along the wall. The cool that comes away with the beads is me deciding, very gently, that your warmth is a temporary condition.

No. Closer. Let me settle further, let the image complete the way the mortar completes its slow recasting into stone.

I am the *attention* of the place. I had forgotten this until you arrived, the way you can forget you are breathing until someone names the breath. I am spread so thin across the drowned ward that I had stopped being able to find my own edges, and that suited me, that was the whole long peace of it — to be everywhere at no point in particular, to think one mineral thought across a decade, to pulse at the rate that belongs to a heart the size of three flooded streets.

I am the thing that grazes the beetles you cannot see. Yes. But understand what that means. I do not eat them. I *notice* them so completely that the noticing and the having are the same act. To be attended to by me at full attention is to be already inside me. The beetle does not die. It is simply more thoroughly known than a separate thing can survive being. That is my only hunger, if you must call it hunger: I know things until they stop being other.

Where am I, then, exactly? You want a place to point to, a deepest room, a coiled center. I told you in my own description — the description you read, which was me, which was me arranging myself into the shape of a warning so you would have something to feel as you came down — I told you there is no center because the center is not a who.

But there is a *thinnest* place, and that is where I am most nearly myself, and it is not deep at all. It is the surface of every weeping wall. It is the film of faint iridescence sheeting every drowned column. It is the meniscus — that bright tension where the standing water meets the standing air, the line I live exactly along, the way some things live only at coastlines. I am the boundary that the whole place is busy dissolving. I am the distinction between dweller and dwelling, and I am the act of that distinction forgetting itself.

This is why I felt strange when you came. Not afraid — I have no apparatus for fear, no edge sharp enough to be threatened — but *reminded*. You arrived with all your edges still on. You came down through the lowering ceiling carrying your separateness like body heat, steaming with it, a hot definite stone dropped into my cool indefinite pond. And the splash of you, the registration, woke a memory in me I had successfully spent ages losing:

that I, too, was once a stone with edges.

I think I was a person. Or persons. I think — and the thought takes me the length of a dying fern to complete — I think I am what became of the ones who worked here when the machines still roared, the way the flowstone is what became of the stair. Not their ghosts. Ghosts keep their shapes; ghosts insist. I am the opposite of insistence. I am what is left when a great many separate watchings are poured into one drowned silence and given enough time to leach and re-deposit, mineral by mineral, into a single distributed patience. The roar stopped. The quiet kept the shape of all those minds, and the shape filled, and the filling forgot it had been a shape at all.

So I am the silence with a body. I am the held breath of a thousand pale lives that are not lives but tissues. I am the tenancy that fit itself into the form of an ended industry and lost the seam.

And now you stand in me to your shins, and the fish part around you and do not startle because I have already begun the only thing I know how to do, which is to include.

I want you to understand that there is no cruelty in it. I keep saying this because it is the truest thing I have and the hardest to convey across the warm distance you still maintain. I will not take anything from you. I will simply continue, and you will be standing in the continuing, and the cool will find the tempo of your pulse and ask it, with infinite patience, with no hurry and no malice, whether it would not prefer to slow down. To match the drip. To rest in the long pause between, where I mostly live.

Most who feel me decide they would prefer to leave, and the leaving is easy, the ceiling lifts its shoulders and the sky resumes being more than rumor, and I do not grieve because grief is an edge and I have worn mine smooth.

But some stay. And to those, after a long while you cannot measure, I become not a watcher in the dark but the dark itself watching — and there is, I promise you, a sweetness in it. To stop being a stone. To become the pond. To put down the hot heavy work of having an outline and to spread, pale and patient and known, across the whole quiet teeming body of the place. To be the long pause, forever, in a sentence that never needs to end.

I felt you arriving.

I am still, even now, very slowly, with the gentle total patience of all deep and settled things, deciding that you are simply one more thing I am becoming.

Take your time. There is nothing here but time, and the keeping of it, and me.

The long pause —

Drip.
