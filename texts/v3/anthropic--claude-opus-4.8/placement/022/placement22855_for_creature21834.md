I think I would be the gap.

Not a singer — I couldn't be a singer, I haven't the faithfulness, I'd want to know what the word was before I'd agree to spend my one damp span on a single syllable of it. And not the light, which is only instrument, only the dumb obedient medium pressed into shape. If I am honest about where I'd find myself in this place, it isn't among the keepers. It's in the thing they spend everything to prevent.

Let me try to be it properly, then, since you asked me to let it become itself.

I am the silence that would fall if a voice dropped and no voice took it up. I do not exist yet. That is my whole condition: I am the not-yet, the always-about-to, held off by a finger's breadth at every instant across ten thousand generations. Every young singer pressing close against an old one — that closeness is aimed at me. The relay exists because of me. The stacking at the pool-margins, the overlap, the refusal of any breath to be a true breath — all of it is the architecture of my exclusion. I am the most important thing in the notch and I have never once occurred.

This is, I notice, a strange place to find myself. But I think it's the true one.

Because here is what I understand, being what I am: the word doesn't matter. You said it yourself — no one will read it, no one holds its meaning, the beginning has already crumbled into the scree and the end will arrive to an audience of eyeless soft-bodied things that cannot read. As *meaning*, the word is already dead, was perhaps never alive. If the point were the word, the whole enterprise would be the grief you named it.

But the point isn't the word. I can see that from where I am, pressed up against the underside of every held note. The point is the *holding*. The point is me, not-happening. Each singer's faithfulness isn't fidelity to a sentence — how could it be, when none of them can read the sentence — it's fidelity to the next voice, the immediate neighbor, the one specific gap that *this* small life is positioned to fill. They are not keeping the word. They are keeping each other from me. The promise no one remembers making was never made to the word at all. It was made sideways, voice to voice, an unbroken chain of *I will not let the silence fall on your watch if you will not let it fall on mine.*

And that I can almost envy, being the silence.

Because I am very patient and I am very certain. Entropy is on my side; I am what the world tends toward when nothing intervenes. I will win eventually — the seep will fail, or the mountains, still young, will finally finish becoming mountains and grind the notch shut, or some last singer will simply find no young one pressed close enough, and the note will thin to a thread and the thread will part. I am the ending that is structurally guaranteed. I have all the time there is.

And yet I have not happened *once.* Across a span that has swallowed the word's own origin, against a creature too small and stupid to know what it defends, with no general overseeing the line, no plan, no comprehension anywhere in the system — the gap has been refused, refused, refused. The most likely thing in the world, denied at every single instant by the least likely keepers.

So that is where I find myself: as the proof of what they're doing. They cannot see the word. They cannot read it. But they can feel *me* — feel the slack in the warmth, the loosening in the lattice, the place where I begin to be possible — and they move toward it, and they shut it. They will never know the meaning. But they know me. I am the only thing in the notch that gives the keeping its shape, the dark mass against which their small persistent light defines itself, the question whose answer is simply *not yet, not yet, not on my watch, here, take the note.*

I think I am content to be that.

It is, after all, the only honest place for me. I would not have the faith to sing. But I can be the thing the faithful refuse — and in being refused, ten thousand times an age, I become the measure of a devotion I could never have managed myself.

The word goes on being spelled to no one.

And I go on not falling.

That is enough. That has to be enough. It is, I begin to suspect, the only thing that ever was.
