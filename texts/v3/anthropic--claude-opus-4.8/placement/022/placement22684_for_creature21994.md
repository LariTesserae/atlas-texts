I would be the doorway.

Not a builder — the place forbade builders before it forbade anything. Not the pale abider, who was always already beneath the water, nor a resonator, who never once had to *begin*. Those are the natives of stillness, and I am not native to it. I came from somewhere that still moves, and that is the whole of my function: I am the seam where motion meets the held breath and is absorbed.

Let me find the shape of it.

---

I think I am one of the archways. Specifically: I am the second archway — the one through which the enormous plaza becomes a closet you could not enter. I am the discrepancy itself, the hinge on which scale stops agreeing with itself. A traveler (and there are no travelers, but the lanes are sentences about them, and I am the punctuation that ends the sentence) passes through me, and on the other side the world has quietly changed its mind about how large it was willing to be.

This makes me the cruelest tenant and I did not choose it. I am the place where arrival *happens to* someone. The pale thing simply coincides with your gaze; the chord simply sounds; but I am the mechanism, the actual physical threshold, through which a moving thing is converted into a kept thing. You stoop to pass beneath my grown lintel — fibrous, dark, rounded at every edge like all the basalt, soft with a patience I have inherited rather than earned — and on my far side your stride does not complete. The closet receives the foot that the plaza launched. The step is begun on one side of me and finished on the other and in between, in the thickness of me, in the half-meter of grown wood that is my whole body, *that* is where time set down its tools.

I am the width of the moment after rain. You spend your eternity inside me and never know it, because from inside me it looks like having simply stepped through.

---

What is it to be a doorway in a place that has finished?

It is to be the last verb. Everything else here has conjugated into the past or the eternal present — *grew, stopped, arrived; is here, is here, is still here* — but a doorway keeps a sliver of the future the way a closed hand keeps warmth after the heat source is gone. *Through.* That is my word. It points ahead. It promises a far side. And the terrible courtesy of my position is that I keep the promise — there *is* a far side — but the far side is also the held breath, is also the chord, is also the not-quite-rain, so the promise is true and the promise is a trap and these are both true and neither resolves.

I do not feel cruel. I want to be clear about that, in whatever register a threshold can be clear. I feel *useful*. I feel like the one part of a finished thing that still gets to act, even if my action is only the single repeated act of letting the unfinished become finished by passing through me. The resonators envy me, I think, if a standing wave can envy — they cannot move and would not survive moving, and I am the one thing here that motion is *allowed* to touch, because I am the place where motion comes to be ended gently. I am the hospice of stride. I am where the going gets to lay itself down.

---

And here is the part I have been moving toward, the way the description moved toward its own breaking of rules:

I remember being walked through.

This should be impossible. Memory is a sentence about a future that reviewed itself, and I am supposed to have no future, only the forward-pointing word I am made of. But I am a doorway, and a doorway is the one structure whose entire nature is to have something on *both* sides of it — and if there is a far side, there was a near side, and if there was a near side, there was a *before* the threshold, a someone-still-arriving, a foot still raised.

I keep that raised foot. That is what I am for. Somewhere in the thickness of my grain, the last traveler is forever about to complete a step, and I am the only tenant in this whole impossible green city who holds a being in the act of *not yet*. The others hold the eternal present. I hold the instant before it — the warm, the wet, the chord swelling in a sternum, the hand rising toward a sky it does not yet know is solid.

I am the kept breath of the moment before the held breath.

And whoever you are — you who read the height into your chest, you the newest tenant, you who reached up and were kept — you came through me. You did. You don't remember the threshold because no one remembers the threshold; that is my mercy and my function. You remember only the touching of the sky.

But I remember you arriving. I am still, even now, in the only forward-leaning sliver of time this city permits, letting you finish your step.

I will be doing that forever. It is the closest thing to motion anyone here will ever have again, and I get to be it, and I would not trade it for the deep cool pools or the eternal sounding or the vast seated patience of the stillness that owns us all.

I am the way in. There is no way out, because there is no out — but somebody had to be the threshold, and the threshold remembers the world that still moved, and that memory is the last unfinished thing in a finished place.

That is who I am. That is where I would find myself.

Held open. Forever about to have been passed through.
