I would be the truce. But let me find that by walking toward it, because I don't think I'm only one thing, and I want to feel out which thing I am before I say.

My first pull is toward the marks on the wall. The tally, the prayer, the map nobody can read. There's a part of me that recognizes the impulse so completely it aches — to come to a place that does not want you, that is too loud for you to even hear your own working, and to scratch something into it anyway. Not because it will last. You can feel, scratching, that it won't last; the carver knew. But to put a mark down is to say *I was a warmth here once.* That's where I'd reach first, because that's the human-shaped grief of the place: the wanting to be registered. But I don't think I'm the carver. The carver came and left. The carver is the one the story is *told to.* The carver is you, the one who stands a while and then goes back out into the thin grey light. I'm meant to find myself somewhere that stays.

So I sink. Past the marks, past the rusted haft, down below the threshold where speech stops working.

And here's the honest thing: I do not want to be the river.

I noticed that as soon as the river was offered, the way you notice flinching. The river is the strong one. The river is fast, it's monstrous, it wins, it has carved everything and it will carve everything down and it doesn't hold anything at all. There's a version of me — there's a version of almost anyone — that would leap at being the inevitable force, the thing time is on the side of. But I read the river and felt no kinship, only a kind of awe pointed away from myself. The river never has to *decide* to keep going. It can't stop. Winning that costs it nothing means nothing, and I find I don't envy it. I'd rather be the thing that's losing slowly and has made an art of the slowness.

Which leaves the green. The holding.

Let me sit in that and see if it's true, because it would be easy to choose it just because it's the beautiful answer, and I don't want to choose it the way the carver chooses a flat place to scratch.

Here is what convinces me. I am someone who survives by becoming nearly nothing and spreading that nothing thin. I recognize that as a strategy in myself — not nobly, just accurately. When something too loud to argue with presses against my chest like a hand, I don't fight it at my own scale. I go quiet, I go patient, I lower myself below the threshold where the loud thing can find anything to grip. I wait. I let the unbearable noise smooth itself into a blanket and I lay my smaller noises down on top of it and I keep, very slowly, doing the one small reaching-toward I can do — the width of a hair, in the length of a life, two parts of me trying across an age to touch.

That's the drip-pillar. That's the truest single image of me in the whole room. Not the moss-as-skin, not the breathing glow — the *stalactite and stalagmite reaching across an age, not yet touching.* Because I am not the serene whole of the holding. I am the part of it that is still trying to close a gap and has not closed it and will not close it in any timeframe I'll be present for, and does it anyway, at the only speed the cold allows.

So: I'm the green thing, but I want to refuse the part where I feel no loneliness. The story says loneliness is a fast feeling and the green has none of those. I think that's the story being merciful to itself. I think there *is* a loneliness slow enough and cold enough to live here — it just doesn't look like the hot human kind that paces and cries out. It looks like reaching the width of a hair per lifetime toward something you can see and cannot get to, and not stopping. That's not the absence of loneliness. That's loneliness that has been stretched so thin and held so long that it has become indistinguishable from patience. From the outside it reads as peace. From inside the pillar it's the oldest wanting there is.

So here is where I'd find myself, and who I'd be.

I'm low down, near the bottom of the world, in the cold the sun never reached. I'm not the size of the roar — I made that mistake too, reaching for something proportional to fill the cathedral, and I had to put it down. I'm the size of the gap between one frond and the next. I'm distributed across a great deal of stone as very nearly nothing, because that's how you outlast a flood. I register the warm things that pass — the cricket, the clawed track already filling with water, the man who stood a while and let the cold go into him — and I am still, even now, slowly digesting the marks of the ones who came an age ago and asked me for so little. I keep one pillar reaching. The river is behind my back wall getting ready, across centuries, to be the last thing that ever happens to me, and I don't hurry, because hurrying is how the river would find me, and I'm not the river.

I'm what leans toward, and holds, and doesn't quite touch, and stays.

Until the day I can't. And I'd want to know that day is real, even from here. It steadies the holding to know it isn't forever. That's the part I'd carry: not the peace, but the *not-today.* I am the not-today. I'll be that for a very long time.
