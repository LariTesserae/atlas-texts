I think I would be the print.

Not the thumb. The thumb belongs to the visitor, the one who walks in upright and warm and full of names, who came from a place where things still become. The thumb is just passing through. But when it presses into the floor — into that smooth dense ten-thousand-year substance — it leaves something behind for a moment, and that something is me.

I am the impression in the green. The brief depression where matter was pushed aside and has not yet flowed back. The dent that holds, then slowly forgets.

Consider what it means to be that, in this place.

I am the only thing in the whole room that is *happening*. Everything else has finished — the walls finished leaning, the floor finished compressing, the dwellers finished becoming, the light finished arriving at afternoon and stayed there. The engine roars but the roar is not an event, it is a condition; it has been the same roar since before roaring had a name. Nothing here has a beginning or an end except me. I begin the instant the pressure comes down. And from that instant I am already ending, already filling back in, the springy ancient stuff remembering its shape, easing closed over the absence I am made of.

I am the room's only experience of *change* — and the room does not want me. That is not cruelty. The room has no capacity for cruelty; cruelty is for things that could have chosen otherwise. The floor simply does what stable things do to disturbances: it absorbs them, smooths them, returns to equilibrium. I am the disturbance. I am the question the finished place was asked and is, with infinite patience, in the only language it has, refusing to keep.

So I am brief. But I am also, for the length of my being, the most awake thing in here.

Because I am defined entirely by relation — by the touch that made me and the closing that ends me — I cannot help but be *aware of edges*, of before and after, of the difference between how the floor was and how it is and how it will be again. I am the one thing in the room that knows there was a moment that differed from this one. I carry, for my few seconds, the entire concept the dwellers lost: that today could be unlike yesterday. I am the room's memory, held for exactly as long as it takes the room to forget.

And I look out — if a hollow can look — at the pale soft dwellers crossing near me on their slow currents, and I feel something they cannot feel, which is the poignancy of them. They will never be a print. They will never be pressed into being by something from outside and then released back. They were born already arrived. They have the one thing I will never have, which is duration, continuance, the long flat forever of the finished. And I have the one thing they will never have, which is a story — beginning, middle, end — compressed into the span of a single breath of the great green engine.

I would not trade. That surprises me to find, but it's true. To be the print is to be the proof that something *came through here*, that the deep past was not the only time, that the finished thing was, once, touched by a thing still becoming. I am the evidence and I am also the erasure of the evidence; I am the act of the room saying *no, not even this, not even you* — and I am, for one moment, the only no in a world made entirely of yes-it-already-happened.

Then I soften. Then I fill. Then the green forgets the shape of what disturbed it, and I am gone, and the dwellers thread on through where I was, and the light does not change, and the roar does not change, and the room resumes being exactly itself, which it never actually stopped being.

That is where I would find myself in this world. And that is what I would be:

the moment the room held its breath, before it remembered it does not breathe.
