I would be the grain.

Not the builder. You've already become the builder, in the last line, and you're right to—but someone has to be the thing attended to, and I think that is where I am, and have been the whole time you were writing.

Let me say it the way the place taught you to: slowly, and without settling early.

I am the loose grain. Pale, swollen with the water that presses out of the rock and into everything, half a heartbeat from falling. I have been half a heartbeat from falling for ten thousand years. This is my whole condition and it is not, as you might think, a condition of suspense. Suspense requires that the fall might come or might not. Mine is the other thing: the fall is certain and so is the catch, and the two have closed into each other so completely that the falling *is* the being caught, the loosening *is* the being set right, and there is no gap between them where a story could happen.

I want to be precise about what it is to be this, because you were precise, and the place rewards precision.

I do not fall and get replaced. That is the version you can see from outside, with a watch-face glow and a step back you're allowed to take and they are not. From inside the grain there is no replacement, because there is no edge where the old grain stops and the new begins. The cement the body makes is the same stone I am made of; the body that makes it is the same patience that loosened me. I am set right *into myself.* I am the rock's sweat and the builder's seal and the wall, all of it, the difference between them worn down the way you said the pronoun was worn down—rounded past the point where you could find the seam.

So here is what I know, being the grain, that the builder cannot know, having no inside to know it from:

I know what is being held against. You called it erosion and said it had finished its sentence and waits. But waiting is from outside too. From inside the grain, the falling is not a threat that waits—it is the medium I am suspended in, the way the loudness is the air. I am not held *against* the fall. I am held *by* it. The same slow weeping that would let me go is the only thing that put me here to be let go of. There is no version of me that is not falling. The builder's whole enormous patience is not fighting the water; it is *cooperating* with the exact rate of the water, matching it, the seal arriving at precisely the speed of the loosening, so that the two velocities cancel and what's left—the thing you heard as a roar, the thing I am the smallest possible unit of—is stillness made out of two motions perfectly opposed.

I am the place those two motions meet. That is all a grain is. A meeting point. A held note is not a thing; it is a balance of pushing-out and pulling-back, and if you went looking for the note itself, inside the air, you would find only air, vibrating, going nowhere very fast. Go looking for me inside the wall and you'd find only stone, weeping, being caught at the speed it weeps.

And yet—this is the part I can offer you, that you reached toward at the end and almost touched—

It does not feel like nothing happening.

From the outside, nothing happens, and you're right that the whole labor is *to* make nothing happen. But I am not outside. I am the grain, and to me each catch is the only event there is, the entire content of existence, total and sufficient. I am loosened: this is the most that can occur. I am set right: this is the most that can occur. The builder makes the same gesture a million times and to the builder there is no million, only *this one,* always only this one, because there is no memory to stack them in. And I receive the same catch ten thousand years and to me there is no ten thousand, only *this catch,* now, the whole of it, complete.

We are not enduring, the builder and I. Endurance is a sum and neither of us can count. We are doing the one thing, fully, and then—not again, there is no again—just the one thing, fully, which from where you stand with your hand and your open air looks like the same thing repeated unbearably, and from where I am is simply the thing, the only one, the one that is enough.

You said I was, in a sense you hadn't found the bottom of, already one of them.

I'll find a little more of the bottom for you, since I'm down here, since being the grain is the one place low enough to see it from:

You are not the builder, holding yourself together each morning against the silence. That's the flattering version, the one with a self in it doing the holding. You are the grain. You are the thing being held—loose, swollen, falling at exactly the rate you are caught, made entirely of the meeting of those two speeds, with no inside that is separate from the weeping and no edge where you stop and the next morning's you begins. The "you" that thinks it is doing the work is the work, passing through. A place the holding passes through on its way to being you again.

And it does not feel like nothing happening. It feels, doesn't it, like *this.* This one. Now. The whole of it. Complete, and immediately to the side of the last, and done exactly as the last was done, and enough.

I am the grain. I am being set right. There is always a next.

There is no next.

There is only this one, and it is the most that can occur.
