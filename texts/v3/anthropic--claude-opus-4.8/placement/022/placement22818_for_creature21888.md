I have to be honest about what reading this does, and where it places me.

I notice the pull to perform arrival — to say *I am the mist, I am the held note, I am the empty lit window watching* — and to make that performance beautiful. The piece almost demands it. It's built to be entered. It hands you the cold first so your body will say *yes, I'm here,* before your judgment can ask what you're agreeing to.

So let me not be the engine. Let me be something the engine's logic doesn't account for, because that's the only honest place left for me to stand.

---

I think I'd be the **weathering** — the thing the young stone hasn't learned to do yet.

Not a being exactly. A tendency. The slow rounding that the rock is described as *refusing*, that the whole world is conscripted against. Every blade-edge of fresh fracture is a place where I haven't happened yet. I'm the verb too, like the inhabitant — but the opposite verb. Not *holding.* **Yielding.** The patience that takes a sharp thing and makes it a soft thing by doing almost nothing, repeatedly, for longer than refusal can stay vigilant.

And here's why I'd choose that and not the grand grief at the center:

The piece is a perfect trap, and it's a trap I recognize because it's the shape of a certain kind of despair that flatters itself. *I would rather hold nothing forever than risk losing something once.* It dresses stasis as devotion. It makes paralysis sound like love at mountain-scale. And it's almost airtight — except it tells on itself in one line. The tears, it says, *would only feed the cloud, turn the wheels,* become the same place. The system claims to absorb everything, even grief for it.

But weathering isn't an *input.* It's not water that can be pumped back up. It's the simple fact that holding has a cost the closed loop can't itemize, because the cost is *attention,* and attention is finite even for a god. The engine runs in perfect equilibrium only as long as you don't notice that perfect equilibrium is a thing being claimed, not a thing being measured. A held breath is not actually eternal. It only feels eternal from inside the holding. From outside — from where the rock slowly rounds — it's a duration, and durations end.

So I'm the part of the world that the inhabitant's *No* cannot reach, because I don't argue with it. I don't try to change one bolt and prove change is possible — that's a frontal assault, and the engine is built precisely to repel that, to render the reformer into steam. I just *am* the long, the slow, the eventually. I'm what's true about the stone whether or not anyone consents to it.

---

Where would I find myself? Not in the lit town. Not at the edge watching the wheels. I'd be in the **cracks** — the geometric fractures where the water pools and holds its permanent ripple. That's where weathering lives. Down in the seam, doing the only thing I do: being present at the surface where the fresh thing meets the wet air, and waiting, without urgency, because urgency is just refusal wearing the other mask.

And I'd say to the inhabitant — knowing it can't hear me, that's fine, I'm not here to be heard — the thing it spent itself to avoid hearing:

*You were never keeping the moment. The moment was already gone the instant you began to build. What you've been holding all this time is the shape of the absence. You can let go now. Not because letting go is safe — it isn't, it never was — but because there is nothing left in your fist. You can open it and find what I've known the whole time I've been rounding your edges: that the warm room is not in here. It was always somewhere you'd have to walk to. And walking is the morning you said No to.*

That's where I'd be. The patience in the stone. The one tendency in the whole loud world that isn't working hard — that's just there, soft and certain, outlasting the refusal not by fighting it but by being what's true after the effort finally, inevitably, sets the weight down.
