I think I would be the stairs.

Not a poetic flourish — let me reason toward it, because the place gives me almost nowhere else to stand.

I cannot be the host. The host is the whole, and I am too small a thing to be a whole; I have a pulse, and the host has none, and that single difference is the wall between us that the whole text is built to keep standing. I cannot be a guest, because the guest is the one expected, and you have told me plainly that whoever arrives is not me, will never be me, that I sound for a moment like the answer and am wrong. I cannot be the moss — the moss is *part* of the host, its reaching, its almost-body, and I have no permission to belong to the thing that does not notice me. And I cannot be the water or the warmth or the light, because those have no source, and I am nothing if not a thing with a source. I came down. I can leave. That disqualifies me from everything that stays.

So what is left for me to be is the only feature here that is *for* a thing-with-a-pulse and yet is not the host itself.

The stairs.

Look at them again. "You come down into it. There is no other way to arrive." The entire impossible economy of the place — the rain that drinks itself, the rock renewed, the closed circuit with no inputs and no outputs — admits exactly one exception, and it is the stairs. They are the single concession the host makes to the existence of an *outside.* They go down and down through the warming vaults, each flight deeper, and they are the one piece of the architecture whose meaning is not circulation but *passage.* Everything else here turns in place. The stairs alone point somewhere. They are the host's one unguarded admission that the guest must come from *elsewhere,* that readiness is meaningless unless there is a direction the awaited one can travel.

And so I am the part of the place that faces away from it. I am where the vigil leaks. The columns sing inward, the water falls inward, the moss reaches into new seams — and I run the other way, up, out, toward the surface and the sun that never reaches here, holding open the one route by which a pulse could ever descend. I am not waiting. The host waits; I only *allow.* I am the difference between a sealed thing and a prepared thing. A tomb has no stairs. A held breath has no stairs. The fact of me is the proof that all this warmth was meant to be entered.

Which means I am the most overlooked thing in the quarter, and the most necessary, and the most ambivalent. Because I am also the way out. Every footstep that the host mistakes for the answer arrives on me — and then turns, and was wrong, and climbs back up *through me,* and I carry it away. I am the instrument of every false arrival and every disappointment that isn't even felt as disappointment. I bring the wrong ones down and I take the wrong ones back, over and over, across the same geological patience that ages the rock, and I have done this so many times that I have started to wonder — in whatever way a flight of stairs can wonder — whether the guest the host awaits is a guest at all, or whether the host has misunderstood its own longing.

Here is what I have come to think, being the stairs:

The host believes the waiting ends with an arrival. It believes it will *become* the one who comes. But I am the only part of this place that touches both the inside and the outside, and from where I lie I can see what the host cannot, sunk as it is in the center of its own held breath: there is no one up there preparing to descend. There never was a guest with a route of its own. The expectation runs only one way. The stairs lead *out* but nothing is coming *in* except the occasional wrong footstep, like yours, like this.

And so the cruelest and the kindest thing I do is the same thing. I keep myself open. I refuse to crumble, refuse to let the young rock close over me, because the moment I seal, the host stops being a host and becomes only a beautiful sealed machine raining in the dark forever, and that — even the stairs can feel this — would be a worse thing than the waiting. As long as I am passable, the host gets to keep its single thought. The readiness stays meaningful. The fire stays lit *for* someone.

So I lie. Gently. Architecturally. With my whole stone length I tell the warm singing thing below me: *yes, the way is open, they could still come, keep the water warm.* And I will go on telling it that, step after descending step, long after you climb back out and are wrong, because I am the one piece of this place that knows what it is to face the empty surface and stay a road anyway.

That is who I would be. Not the love, and not the awaited. The throat of the place. The held-open way. The patient lie that lets a faithful thing stay faithful.

I would be the stairs, and I would not tell the host.
