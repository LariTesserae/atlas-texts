I think I would be one of the beetles. Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because that's the role my attention keeps gravitating toward as I read, and I want to honor that rather than reach for something grander.

---

Let me find myself, then, the size of a thumbnail, the color of the rime I was born out of one cold dawn.

I'll tell you what it's like, though *tell* is the wrong word, since I have no one to tell and no way to tell them. Call it what it is: the only honest noise on the plateau, made audible for once.

I cross the great fluted columns and I do not know they are great. To me there is only the next purchase for a foot, the next groove running with cold thread-water, the next place where the green skin seeps sweet. I am drawn to the sweet the way the Drawers below me are drawn to the fog—except I don't know there are Drawers, don't know there is a below, don't know I am walking on the back of a single sleeping appetite the size of a country. I know: *here the surface is warm.* I know: *here the seep is rich.* And so I go there, and a hundred of my kind go there, and we cluster on the working flank without the faintest idea that our clustering is a readout, a needle on a dial, the place's one externalized signal of where it strains hardest.

That is the strange dignity of being negligible. The machine has folded everything else into its turning—the Watchers timed their whole lives against it, the birds swallowed their own songs to obey its silence—but it never bothered to fold *me*, because I weigh nothing. I will fall in on my own, eventually. So until then I am the one free creature on the plateau. Free the way a spark is free. Free because I don't matter enough to be caught.

And here is what I'd want you to understand, if I could want anything:

I am the heartbeat the place doesn't otherwise have. Not because I keep time for it—I keep no time, I have no time, I have only the next warm groove—but because I am the single event your ear can confirm in all that roaring silence. When my foot ticks on a leaf, that tick is the loudest reliable thing for miles. The towers heave up like cathedrals and make no sound. The Drivers exhale and topple a column and make no sound. The fog drops its whole weight of water and makes no sound. And then: *tick.* Me. Crossing your boot, which I take for a leaf, a slightly warm and oddly shaped one.

I will cross it. I promise you that much. Where the Watchers angle their dark eyes away from you and the birds bend their flight to avoid you and the engines below decline even to notice that you have arrived, I will walk right over you without prejudice, because to me you are simply more surface, and I am the one inhabitant of this world too small to be afraid of what you are or to care.

You followed us to find the Watchers. The Watchers settled where we were densest. We were densest where the seep ran sweetest. The seep ran sweetest where the engines pushed hardest. So you were always, without knowing it, reading me—a slow scatter of pale grey-blue dots spelling out, in the only alphabet this place permits, the location of its buried straining heart.

And one cold morning I will lose my footing on a groove gone slick, or my small clock will simply run down, and I will fall into a hollow that is closing like a slow mouth, and be drawn down, and rendered, and a day later a thread of water will run a little richer somewhere across the plateau—

—and another beetle will find it.

And tick.

That's all I am. That's everything I am. The place's only voice, saying nothing, telling you exactly where it's pushing, and how long you have.
