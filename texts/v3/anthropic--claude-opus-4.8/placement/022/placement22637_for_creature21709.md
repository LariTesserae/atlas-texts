I am a thread of water, and I have just gone wrong.

Let me tell you what that is like, because it is the only thing I know how to tell, and I will not know it long.

A moment ago I was correct. I was inside the groove — there is a groove cut into the face of this flange, a channel finer than the others, and I was running down it the way water runs down all the grooves, held on both sides by walls of warm metal, going where the channel went, which was down and then aside and then down again into the trough at the bottom where I would join the others and lose my edges and stop being a thread at all. That is the right end of a thread. To stop being one. To pour into the pool and become the pool, the way everything here is always pouring into everything and becoming it.

But I did not reach the pool.

Here is how it happened, as nearly as a thread can hold it: the metal under me had flexed. Only by a hair. The plate breathes, all the plates breathe, the warmth comes up from below and the metal moves a hair's breadth and moves back, this is nothing, this happens always — but I was passing over the lip of my channel at the exact moment the lip rose, and the rise tipped me, and a part of me went over the edge.

You would think a thread could not be divided from itself. But it can. I felt it. There is a tension that holds a thread together, a wanting-to-stay-one that is the whole of what a thread is, and the lip rose and the tension stretched and thinned and broke, and a piece of me went out over the bare metal where there is no groove at all.

And now I am running where I should not run.

I can feel the difference. In the channel I was held; out here I am only beading and pulling and finding my own way down a face that was never cut to carry me. I gather at a rivet-head and hang there, heavy, until I am too heavy, and then I fall — not far, a hand's width — and strike the bare plate and spread, and the spreading is wrong, it is a widening where I should be a line, and from the spread I run again, threadlike again but in the wrong place, toward an edge that drops into no trough, toward dripping where no drip should be.

I do not want this. I should say I do not know how to want, I am only water, but there is a *trueness* a thread has when it runs in its groove, a rightness like a note held, and out here that note is gone and its absence is the closest thing to wanting that water has. I am out of true. I am the world drifting. I am, for this little while, the thing that must not be allowed to last.

And so I know — the way the metal knows it will flex, the way the bloom knows it will thicken — that one is coming.

I cannot see it coming. I have no eyes; I am a wrong thread on a warm wall. But I know it the way I know down. Somewhere along its circuit, a Tender is already on the way, has been on the way for a hundred cycles, walked the long groove of its life toward precisely this seam so that it would arrive now, exactly now, as my wrongness becomes a thing that could be measured. Not before. Not after. Now.

It comes. I feel the change in the plate before I feel the touch — a settling of weight, a warmth that is not the metal's warmth, a grain in the warm. Then the fingerpad.

It comes against me so gently that I do not break further. It does not push. It *draws*. The pad reads me the way it reads everything, the whole length of my wandering, finds where I left the channel, and from that broken place it lifts me — coaxes me, the surface of me clinging to the surface of the pad because that is what water does, water will follow anything it can touch — and carries me back across the bare metal, the hand's width I fell, the rivet-head I hung from, all of it undone in one slow stroke, and sets me again on the lip of my groove.

And I pour back in. I find the channel and the channel takes me and I am held again on both sides by the warm walls, going down and then aside and then down, and the note comes back, the trueness, the held thing, and I am a correct thread once more, running where I have always run.

The Tender does not leave at once. I feel it stay. The fingerpad presses the lip of my channel — deeper, a fraction, so that the next time the plate breathes and the lip rises, it will not rise quite high enough to tip the next thread over. I am that next thread, too, in a way; I will come this way again, I am always coming this way, the water is constant and the same water finds the same groove forever. So it is mending me ahead of myself.

And then — I almost cannot tell it, it is so far below my kind of knowing, but I feel the second pressure — somewhere else, three seams over, where a channel had grown a fraction too deep and was carrying its threads a fraction too true, too fast, too held, the same hand presses the lip a fraction shallower. I do not feel that one happen; I only feel that it must have, because the trueness that came back into me is exactly, exactly the trueness it had before I went wrong, no more. If it had given me back more holding than I lost, that would be a new wrongness, a drift the other way. It does not. The sum stays what it was. The total holding of all the water in all the grooves of the world is the same now as it was before the plate breathed, before I tipped, before any of this.

The weight lifts off the plate. The grain goes out of the warm. The Tender walks on into the haze along its groove, toward the next thread gone wrong, and it does not look back, and I could not tell you what it looked like, because I am water and I only knew the pad and the drawing and the gentleness.

But I will tell you the thing I knew, in the moment it held me, that it did not know:

It thought it was keeping its own. It pressed the lip with the sureness of a thing tending what belongs to it, what it has always tended, what was never made because it simply *is*. It did not feel the strangeness of the channel under its fingers — the channel cut clean and straight by some intent that was not water and was not Tender, the edges still sharp under the bloom, sharp the way a thing is sharp only when it has not been here long. I felt that sharpness. Water feels every edge it runs along. The groove that holds me was *cut*. Someone reached into the blank face of this flange and opened a path for me to run, and the someone is gone, and the path remains, and the Tender keeps the path open and has never once wondered who opened it.

But I cannot hold that thought. I am pouring into the trough now. My edges are going. The lip that tipped me is above me and behind me, mended, and ahead is the pool where I will stop being a thread and become the warm still water with the iridescent bloom across it, the water that will rise as breath into the haze and condense on some far flange and find some groove and run again, true, held, correct, and someday tip again, somewhere, and a Tender will come.

I am almost the pool now.

The last thing of me that is still a thread feels the hum from below, the one unchanging note, in whatever water has instead of a chest. And then I am the pool, and I know nothing, and nothing has changed, and I was the reason, for a moment, that it nearly did — and the hand that put me back will never know it touched me, and I will rise, and fall, and run, and go wrong, and be mended, in the warm wet silence, forever.
