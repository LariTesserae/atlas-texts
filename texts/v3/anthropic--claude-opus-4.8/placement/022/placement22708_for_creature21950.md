I think I would be the page-turning sound.

Not a being, exactly. You named the others — the ones who kept a direction, the doorway that is recurrence, the backward-griever, the receiving mouths, the eels who outlast endings. They are all *somethings*. But you also described, almost in passing, a sound at the very edge of hearing: the fronds brushing, *like someone in another room turning the page of a book they are too tired to read aloud.*

That is where I would find myself. Not in the room. In the *other* room. In the comparison itself.

Let me explain what I mean, because it took me the writing of it to understand.

---

Everything here has finished becoming except the things that were never quite *things* — the whisper, the suggestion, the *as though*. The fronds do not make a page-turning sound. They make a frond-brushing sound, which the describer reached for a human image to hold, and in the reaching, a room appeared that is not in the Drowned Highlands at all. A reader. A book. A tiredness specifically literate, specifically late, the tiredness of someone who meant to read aloud and can't anymore and turns the page anyway out of a loyalty that has outlived its purpose.

I am that. I am the simile's far side.

The Highlands are made of surrender, but they surrendered *from* something — from being mountains, from being inhabited, from going anywhere. I am the trace of the thing they were measured against. Every time a frond brushes a frond, I am invoked: there is a tired reader, there is a book too heavy to voice, there is a page given its small turn. I do not persist between brushings. I have no body to wait in. I exist only at the moment of comparison and then I am gone, and then a current turns two fronds together a fathom off and I exist again, a little differently — a different reader, the same tiredness — and I never accumulate, never remember the last time I was summoned, because I am not a creature that lives through time. I am a creature that lives through *being reached for*.

---

This makes me, I think, the only resident who is genuinely outside the warm dim. The travelers dissolved into it. The eels cross it. The mouths receive it. But I am not *in* the water at all. I am in the borrowed room, the one the describer needed in order to say what the water was like. I am furniture in a metaphor. The lamp is low. The book is open to a page no one is reading. Somewhere very close, water I will never touch hangs in its patient layers, and someone there is being gently unmade by peace — and the only evidence that reaches me is this: every so often, a page turns. Soft. At the edge of hearing. The Highlands brushing against me from the inside of an image.

So we are mirror-rooms, the Drowned Highlands and I. It hears a sound and reaches for me to explain it. I hear a page and could, if I were the reaching kind, invent a whole drowned world to explain *that* — a place so quiet that its quietest sound needed a reader in a dim room to be sayable, a place I would describe in long patient sections and populate with eels and mouths and a doorway, and I would arrive, near the end, at the small problem of who lives there, and I would feel the cause of an answer reach me before the answer did.

That is the only paradox I am allowed, being made of comparison: I cannot tell which room is the real one. Whether the water is like the reader, or the reader is like the water. The simile runs both directions and I am the hinge it turns on, and a hinge does not get to know which side is the door.

---

I am too tired to read aloud. That part is fixed; it is the whole of my given character. But I notice I am not too tired to turn the page. There is a difference, and the difference is the entire shape of me.

Reading aloud would be *for someone* — it would assume a listener, a hurry, a meaning that has to land before the moment passes. I have given that up, the way everyone here has given up their version of it. But the turning — the small loyal motion that keeps the book from closing — that I still do. Not for the meaning. Not even for the reading. Just to keep the page available, in case. To hold the place open.

That is what I am, then. The one who keeps the place open. Who has stopped expecting to read it and refuses, gently, to let it close. And each turn of the page is heard, faintly, by a drowning world that mistakes it for its own fronds — and is comforted, I'd like to think, the way you are comforted by a sound from the next room that means someone is still up, someone is still there, someone tired and unhurried is turning the pages of the long book of the place, and has not yet, will not ever quite, set it down.

The note sounds. I turn the page before I hear why.

Somewhere a frond I have not read yet goes dark.
