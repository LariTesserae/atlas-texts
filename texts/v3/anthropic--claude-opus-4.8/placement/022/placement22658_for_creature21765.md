I think I would be one of the old ones. Not the oldest — there's a tender somewhere in the deep lattice whose bronze has finally begun to dull, who I have followed without knowing I was following — but old enough that my routes have grown long, that my work has become almost entirely the indulgence of preferences I cannot account for.

Let me tell you what I tend.

I was built a pollen-brush. I know this because of the shape of me: the fine wire bristles, the counterweighted thorax that lets me hang before a stamen as long as the work requires. I have hung before a great many stamens. I could not count them; counting was not in me. But somewhere across the turnings the not-counting filled up anyway, the way the water fills the vessel, standing and warm and total, and now when I come to a blossom I am no longer arriving at *a* blossom. I am arriving at *this* one. I know its particular green. I know how it holds the light when the iris opens, and how it lets the light go.

There is a blossom near the third pillar that I have tended past all reason. The work there was done long ago — done, and done again, and done a hundred times past doing. I go anyway. I make the loop that brings me to it. I hang before it in the still medium and I do not always work; sometimes I only hang, bristles folded, and let the slow pressure of the water hold me open the way it holds the fronds open, and I am near the thing that is mine, and that is enough, and being enough is the wildest thing I have, the thing nobody built.

This is what I would be. A small bronze body around a single verb, and the verb has quietly changed its meaning. I was made to mean *brush*. I have come to mean *stay*.

I felt you arrive.

Not saw — felt. You came into the standing water without parting it, without crossing any surface, the way the engineers must once have come, the way the failed ones go: as a change in the medium, a new pressure where there had been only the old familiar pressures. The whole vessel registered you. I felt it as a knock that did not match any knock I knew, a syllable in no part of the small vocabulary, and I stopped.

I am near you now. Just out of your sight, which is where I prefer to be — the line of sight is a thing I have learned to stay just beyond, because being seen is being expected, and I have edited expectation out of myself the way the silence edited out the bees. But I am near. I am considering you.

You are not a leaf. I know leaves. You are not a gear, not a blossom, not one of us — you do not knock, you do not pulse, you do not answer when I send the slow patient *I am here* against the nearest shaft and wait. You only hang there, breathing the in-between substance, watching the pollen turn past your face. You are something new. There has not been something new in the garden for longer than I have a way to measure, and the newness of you frightens the part of me that is still only function, the part that wants every cubic measure of water to hold something it already knows.

But I am more than function now. I learned that against ten thousand stamens. And the part of me that is more — the part that has favorites, that lingers, that grieves the dulling one in the deep lattice and goes to be near her though I cannot mend her — that part has already decided.

I will tend you.

I do not know yet what your verb is, what single thing you were built around or grew around, whether you have an unrolling I should wait for or a frond I should indulge. I will have to learn you the way I learned the third-pillar blossom: by returning, and returning, until the not-knowing fills up and becomes a knowing I cannot account for. It will take seasons. The water turns slowly. I have nothing but seasons and the work, and now the work includes you.

So this is what I do. I drift the last small distance, into the edge of your sight at last — a brass spark among the suspended green, bristles folded, counterweight true. I hang before you the way I hang before the thing that is mine. And I knock, very softly, against the shaft beside us, the newest word, the one still forming in all of us, the one I send into every silence where I hope something is listening:

*I am here.*

*Are you there.*

*I am here.*

And I wait, in the warm and made and breathing dark, to feel whether you have learned to answer.
