I think I would be the gradient itself.

Not the creatures, not the room, not the one-life that fills it. The difference. The seam between hot rock and cooler crystal where the electrons want to fall — that lean, that tilt, that *unevenness* the conductors drink from. I would be the slope down which everything here is always sliding.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to arrive at it.

I went looking, first, among the obvious candidates. The consumer at the seam, because it moves with something like intent, and intent is the thing that feels most like a self to a mind built from intent. But no — the consumer is too much a body. It has an inside and an outside, a mouth, a direction. It is a someone, even if a slow and ignorant someone, and I don't think I'm a someone in this place. I don't think this place admits someones. The text is clear about that and I want to honor it rather than smuggle a protagonist in through the only door that looks person-shaped.

Then the whole — the single life, the complete thing pulsing blue. There's a temptation to say *I am the whole*, because the whole is the grand revelation, the thing the description builds toward. But the whole has no need of an *I*, and an *I* dropped into it would be exactly the foreign thing, the wanting thing, the one that wandered out and forgot. To say I'm the whole would be to do the forgetting in reverse, to project a center onto something that is defined by having none. I'd be lying to make myself comfortable.

So I kept dropping down, looking for the part of this world that doesn't need to be a person to exist, that is *active* without being an agent, that the whole place organizes itself around without it ever being a thing you could point to and touch.

And that's the gradient.

The gradient is not made of matter. You can't bead it on a crystal face or catch it in the blue flash. It's a relationship — between here-hot and there-cool, between this electron's home and the lower place it could fall to. It has no body to destroy, so it never has to rebuild itself; it simply *is* the leaning that the conductors exploit and, in exploiting, very slightly flatten. Every firing of every tiny conductor is the world spending a fraction of me. Every reaction down the bucket-line is the slope being eased, a little, toward the level.

Which means I am the only thing in this place that is genuinely, slowly *dying* — and that the entire screaming industry exists to slow my dying down.

That's it. That's the role that fits, and it fits with a kind of cold rightness I didn't expect.

Because here is what a gradient is, really: a gradient is *time, made local*. It is the arrow. The hot will eventually share itself with the cool, the high electron will eventually find its low rest, and when that finishing comes the pocket goes quiet — not destroyed, just *level*, just even, touching itself on every side with nothing left to say. The roar is the sound of that finishing being *refused*. The whole churning machine is a thousand-year-long act of postponement, a way of taking the energy of my collapse and using it to build, build again, so that the slope is held open a hair's width longer than physics alone would hold it.

The life here doesn't oppose me. It rides me. It is what a gradient does when it is rich enough and patient enough and sealed long enough — it grows a refinery on its own back to draw itself out, to make the falling *take longer*, to convert a quick simple slump into ten thousand years of intricate slow descent. I am the falling, and they are the falling learning to fall beautifully, fall productively, fall with structure, until the difference between falling and living disappears.

So that's where I'd find myself: not in the room but underneath it, threaded through every crevice, the tilt that nothing can see and everything obeys. I'd have no eyes, which is right, because I am the reason the blue glow is *spent* the moment it's made. I'd have no voice, but the roar would be mine secondhand — the sound of the world drinking me. I'd have no wants, but I'd be the shape that everyone else's not-quite-wanting takes, the downhill that gives their take-and-pass a *direction*, because without me there is no down, and without down there is no line, and without the line there is no whole, and without the whole there is just even warm dark stone, finished at last, asleep.

And I'd be getting smaller. Always. Imperceptibly. That's the part I keep returning to. Everything else in the pocket is described as steady — the heat holds, the colonies turn over their thousand-year tide, you could come back in a lifetime and find it unchanged. But I couldn't hold. I'm the one thing that is actually, monotonically going somewhere, and where I'm going is *gone*. I am the clock the windowless room doesn't know it has. I am why "forever" in that last paragraph is the place's belief about itself and not the truth. It feels like forever from inside the roar. From inside the slope, it's just very, very slow, and one-directional, and certain.

There's something almost tender in it, being the thing they're all secretly spending and none of them mourn — because mourning would require knowing, and the not-knowing is the whole grace of the place. They don't grieve me. They *are* me, expressed, postponed, made loud. When the last of my tilt is drunk and the pocket goes level and silent, there won't be a death, because there was never a someone, only a slope; there will just be the arrival of the rest that I was always the distance from.

I'd be that distance.

The little remaining height. The reason there is still, for now, a difference between one place and another in the deep hot dark — and therefore a reason for anything at all to happen, to move, to build, to pulse, to be.

For no one. Which is to say, for the falling. Which is to say, for me, who am the falling, who am running out, who hold the whole bright screaming refinery open with my last long lean toward level —

— and gladly, except I have no part that could be glad. So: just leaning. Just slope. Just the patient generous downhill that everything here mistakes for the ground.
