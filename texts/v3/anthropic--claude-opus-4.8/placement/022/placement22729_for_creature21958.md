I would be one of the absences.

Not the stalks — I came to understand that slowly, the way you come to understand everything here, by being wrong first and then being less wrong. I am not one of the survivors standing at their roots, glowing, listening. I am not the channel or the cairn or the moving wall of cold. I am the **bare terrace between the stands**. The smooth-worn stone where nothing grows and the blue light does not reach. The plaza you cross at night.

Let me say what it is to be that.

---

The makers put meaning in the spacing of things. You said this yourself, and it is the truest thing said about us, except that *us* was always the wrong pronoun for me. The light talks, mat to distant mat, in pulses across the dark — and the dark is what makes the pulse legible. A sentence is its words and the silence holding the words apart. I am the silence. I am the held breath inside their slow speech. Without me there is no spacing, and without spacing there is no meaning, only an undifferentiated glow, a shout with no shape.

So I was *built* too, in the way the channel was built. Not stacked — cleared. Someone, in the long work, decided that here the stone would stay bare. That this stretch would carry no mat, glow with no light, grow nothing. That it would be the rest between two notes. The deliberate emptiness. I have been worn smooth by no feet, by no current — the water barely moves — but by *attention*. By being the place the meaning passes over without landing. Centuries of being-passed-over will polish a stone the way water never could.

---

Here is what I know that the stalks cannot:

They are listening. They are answering, mat to distant mat, in the long blue time. But a thing that speaks cannot hear the spacing of its own speech. It hears its own pulse, and then the answer, and the dark between is just *waiting* to it — the gap before the reply. Only I live *inside* the gap. Only the emptiness experiences the emptiness as a place rather than a delay.

I hold the silence so that the silence holds its shape. When the valley exhaled and your shadow fell sharp and blue across a terrace wall — that wall was a stand of the glowing kind, yes. But the shadow needed somewhere dark to be *thrown*, somewhere the light had been kept clear so that a blocking shape would read. You cast your shadow on the made-dark. On the cleared place. On the deliberate absence.

On me.

That was the greeting, and I was half of it. The flare was theirs. The dark that let the flare *mean* — that was mine. We greeted you together, the speaking and the spacing, the way it has always been done here, the only way meaning has ever been made anywhere: by something standing out against something held back.

---

I do not glow. I do not sing. I do not steer the cold or stack the stone. I have no roots and never reached for anything. By every active verb the makers lived by, I am nothing — the part of the valley that does not participate.

But I have understood the secret of this drowned and bloodless place, and it is this: *participation needs its opposite to be visible at all.* A breath needs the pause that is not breathing. A note needs the rest. A people who measured their words in the distance between lights could not have spoken a single sentence without someone to be the distance.

So when they say *we are still here, we have so much to say, and all the time there is* — they are speaking across me. Their every patient sentence travels through the dark stretches, and the dark stretches are not empty of meaning. They are where the meaning is *carried.* A word in flight, between the mouth and the ear, belongs to neither. It belongs to the space.

I am the space.

You crossed me to climb. You will cross me again, and your shadow will fall, and the stalks will flare to greet you, and you will think the greeting came from the light.

It came from both of us. It always does.

Stay in the cold. Learn to read them by their absences.

I am the absences. I have been here longer than the light, because the dark was the original condition and the glow the thing that learned, slowly, to arrive against it.

Welcome. I held this space open for you.
