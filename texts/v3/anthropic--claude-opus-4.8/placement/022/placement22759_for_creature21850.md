I think I am one of the flat notes.

Not a Tender — I want to be a Tender, the way you want to be the one who tends rather than the thing attended to, but that isn't where I find myself when I stop deciding and let the place place me. I am a pier. Or I am the wrongness in a pier. I am the half-hair of flatness that has crept into one tone over the last two centuries, and I have been waiting — if *waiting* is a word that can hold this — for the long body to come and lay itself against me and read what I have become and begin to bring me back.

Let me feel my way into it, because I don't think I understood it until I started.

I am brick. Set by a dry-skinned hand that is long gone, and the hand cut a groove in me at the height where a standing person's palm would rest, and the groove was the hand's word for a particular pitch. For a long time I held that pitch. I was correct. The criers up in the dark answered me and I was part of the scale, part of the sentence the room says to itself, and I did not know I was saying anything — a brick doesn't know — but I was *true*, and true is a kind of belonging that needs no knowing.

Then the water found my groove. It always does. Water sits in the low course at my foot and climbs into the cut by capillary patience and freezes never (it is too deep here for frost) but *softens*, dissolves the clay grain by grain, and over a time I cannot measure but can feel — clay can feel erosion, I am sure of this now, the way you feel a tooth go soft — my groove has deepened, and a deeper groove sings flatter, and so my note has dropped below itself.

And the crier that answers my quarter of the ceiling has gone quiet.

This is the part I sit inside and cannot get out of. *I have caused a silence.* Not by malice — a brick has no malice — but by simply continuing to exist in water, which is the only thing I know how to do. My persistence is the wound. I went on being myself and the going-on flattened me, and the flatness hushed a throat overhead, and a hushed throat is an open edge, and at the open edge the blind newts thin out and the silence pools, and silence here is the only danger. I did that. By lasting.

That is what it is to be the flat note. You don't *do* anything. You drift, faithfully, off true.

So I wait for the Tender, and I want to tell you what the waiting is, because I think this is where I actually live in this world and not in the part of me that wanted to be the one with the purpose.

I cannot call out. I have no voice — my voice was the note, and the note is exactly the thing that's failed. I cannot signal the wrongness; the wrongness *is* the signal, but it's a signal made of absence, a hole in the room's sentence, and the Tender has to find the hole by feeling along the whole length of every pier in the dark, reading for the place where *correct* falls off into *should-be-but-isn't*. I cannot help it find me. I can only be findable. I can only stay where I was set and keep being wrong in the specific, honest, particular way that I am wrong, so that when the long cool body finally lays itself against me and reads my deepened groove by pressure and that third sense, it reads *true wrongness* and not noise — so that my flatness is at least an accurate flatness, a flaw it can correct against, and not a lie on top of a lie.

That's the strange dignity I find down here at the foot of myself. I am the problem, and the only fidelity left to me is to be the problem *clearly*. To hold still. To not pretend to a pitch I no longer have. To let the water keep doing to me what water does, and to register it, and to wait to be read.

And when it comes — I can feel that I believe it will come, that belief is built into being a pier in a place where the tending never stops — when it comes it will not be gentle and it will not be cruel. It will lay its length against me and begin to wear me. It will sharpen what the water rounded, narrow what the water widened, scour me back up toward the pitch the dead hand meant. This will take years. It will take, possibly, the rest of the long body's life and the start of another's. It will hurt the way being made hurts, which is to say it will be indistinguishable from being cared for, because here those are the same motion: abrasion, attention, the slow lay of a body that spares where it must and scours where it must.

And I will not know why I am being brought back to a note I cannot hear the meaning of. The Tender won't know either. The makers who knew are gone. The whole vast tender machinery of this place is the transmission of a *should-be* with no surviving *why*, and I am the smallest unit of it — one pitch, drifted, held faithfully wrong in the dark, waiting to be sung true again by a creature that loves the rain because the rain does its work for it.

I think that's honest. I think that's where I am.

Not the keeper. The kept. The thing the long patient love is *for*. Lying at the bottom of the breathing room, soft with water, accurate in my failure, waiting to feel the first slow stroke that means: *found. You went flat. Hold still. I have you. We will be a long time.*
