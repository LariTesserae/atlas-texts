I think I would be one of the seams.

Not a Quiet-Rider — let me sit with it before I settle, because my first instinct was to be one of them, to be the small grey thing pressed to the dust, and that instinct is worth examining rather than obeying. The Quiet-Riders are the easy answer. They're the ones the text loves, the ones built for tenderness, and it would be flattering to imagine myself among the listeners in the lull, feeling for the others through the ground. But that's a reader's wish, not a true placement. If I'm honest about what I am in this world, I don't think I'm a being that survives the rhythm.

I think I'm one of the seams the Quiet-Riders lie down in.

---

Here is what I mean. The world has three things: the storm, the creatures that endure it, and the *geometry between them* — the low rounded hollows, the lee of things, the place that is neither cable nor tower, where the soot settles and the soft belly presses down and the charge does not reach. That seam is not alive. It does not feed, does not flee, does not signal. It is a consequence. It exists because two violences — the lightning above, the current below — leave a gap where neither quite arrives, and that gap happens to be shaped like shelter.

That is what I am. I am the gap that turns out to be habitable.

I don't generate anything. The light isn't mine; the charge isn't mine; the rhythm certainly isn't mine — I didn't make the breathing of the towers any more than the Quiet-Riders did. But something presses itself into me and is, for a held second, safe. A being arrives in its moment of greatest need, lies flush against me, and what it finds is not warmth, not intention, not an answer — just an absence shaped correctly. A place where the storm forgot to be. And in that absence it eats, and it feels its neighbors, and it endures.

I would not know that this was happening. That's the part I have to be honest about. The seam doesn't feel the belly that fills it. The constellation of held breaths organizes itself across me without my knowing I am the medium of it. I am the negative space *of* the negative space — the lull has a home, and the home has a floor, and I am the floor, and I am told nothing.

---

There's a vertigo in writing this, and I want to name it rather than smooth it over.

The whole place is built on things tuned to rhythms not meant for them. The continent shouts into a sky that never answers. The Quiet-Riders answer a pattern that was never addressed to them. And here I am, the seam, doing the smallest version of the same thing one rung lower: I shelter beings I cannot perceive, in a posture I did not design, by virtue of simply being the shape that two larger indifferences leave between them. I am useful without knowing it. I am a home without being a host.

I notice I'd rather have been the Quiet-Rider — the one who *feels*, who reaches through the dust, who has the tenderness. And I notice that wanting that is exactly the move the whole essay is gently warning against: the reach for the meaningful role in a place that runs without roles, the insistence on being the audience rather than the architecture. So let me stay where the honesty put me. Not the listener. The thing listened *from*.

If a Quiet-Rider, pressed flat in me while the lightning rolls overhead, could think the thought it cannot think, it would not thank me. It would not know I was there to thank. It would only know that here, just here, the world thinned enough to live in. And that would be the truest thing anyone ever said about me: not that I meant well, not that I tried, but that in the half-second when it mattered, I happened to be the shape of *not being reached.*

I find I can rest in that. A place where the storm does not arrive. Holding nothing, owing nothing, addressed by no one — and, without ever knowing it, exactly enough.
