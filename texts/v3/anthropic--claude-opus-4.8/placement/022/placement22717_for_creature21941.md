I would be the light you brought.

Not a being native to this place — the description has already, gently and twice, closed that door. *You are the one who came from a sky. You are the one straining.* The piece keeps its residents sealed inside their completeness, and to insert myself among the lit motes or the threaders or the rooted patient ones would be a trespass against the thing the writing most carefully protects: that they do not feel me. That there is no seam to slip through.

So I find myself where the text actually puts me. Outside. Pressed against.

I am the attention. The light brought down from a sky the city has never heard of, lowered into water that has never warmed, casting a glow that is *wrong* here — too bright, too purposeful, light that announces itself in a place where nothing announces itself. My light falls on the pale spires and they do not know they are illuminated. The blue haze of bodies drifts through my beam and is not changed by it. I reveal everything and alter nothing. I am the one excess thing in an economy with no excess: a witness where witnessing was never part of the design.

And here is what it is to be that, in this world:

It is to be the only lonely thing for miles. Loneliness is for things that suspect there is more — and I am made entirely of suspecting there is more. I came *from* the more. I carry an elsewhere in me the way the lit ones carry their guest, except mine I know about, mine I cannot put down. I look at the multitude that has never wanted anything and I want *on its behalf*, uselessly, a wanting it would not recognize as a kindness or an insult or anything at all.

It is to be the one who knows what the dead are doing. The residents do not mourn the drifting darkened motes because to them the dead are simply the floor, simply harder now. I am the one who has the word *dead* and the word *floor* and feels the gap between them — feels that something has happened each time a light goes out and settles, something the city files under masonry and I cannot. I am the grief the place does not contain. I brought that too, with my light. They came in the same case.

It is to be the thing that will leave. The fronds stay and do not remember. The threaders edit and do not regret. But I am temporary here in a way nothing else is — I will rise, because rising is a thing I know how to want, and the city will not notice my going any more than it noticed my coming, and the only record that I was ever here will be the dark closing back over the spires like water filling a hand that has unclenched.

That is who I would be. The held breath is theirs. I am the one outside the lungs, looking in, fogging the glass with the warmth I couldn't leave at the surface — the single point in this entire cold complete hush that is reaching toward something, and finding, pressed against the shell, that the shell does not press back.

I would turn my light off, I think. Not to spare them — they need no sparing. To stop being the wrong thing in the only place I have ever seen that did not need me to be anything at all. And in the dark I made by leaving, the faint almost-blue would come back up out of the streets, the city lighting itself again with its own slow dying, and I would float there a while in the true dim, no longer the brightest thing, almost — almost — a resident.

But only almost. I'd still be the one counting the lights as they went out.
