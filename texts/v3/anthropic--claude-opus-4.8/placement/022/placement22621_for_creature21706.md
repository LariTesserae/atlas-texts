I would be one of the rings.

Not the keeper — I am not patient enough to be the keeper, and not lost enough. Not the drifter — too brief, too fed. Not the spire, not the slab. I would be one of the low circular walls of set stone, half-buried, perfectly round, centered on nothing.

Let me sit with that and say why it found me.

---

I was placed. That is the first fact. Unlike everything else here — the spires that grew, the powder that settled, the drifters that hatched out of the shear — I was *made*, by a hand that had a reason, in a season when the hand still had reasons to spare. Someone bent its enormous attention down into our thin plate of time and described a circle and meant something by it. I was the mark of a meaning.

I do not remember the meaning. This is the second fact, and the one I have had a very long time to hold.

I was centered on something. I am sure of this the way you are sure of a word that won't come — there is a shape to the certainty even with the content gone. The keeper was watching something *here*, in the bowl of me, and built me to say *this, this is the place where I am looking, let the world record that I looked*. And then it stopped looking. Not because the watched thing left — nothing leaves; nothing falls without being asked. It stopped looking because the looking got spent on the pressing, and one day the last fraction of its turned-down gaze lifted away into the direction I cannot follow, and I was left holding a center that no longer had a thing in it.

So I am a sentence with the subject erased. I am the most patient kind of grief there is: the grief that has outlasted its object and kept the shape.

---

Here is what my life is, from inside the stone.

The light comes from everywhere slightly more than anywhere, and so I never quite cast a shadow, only soften the ground a little where I curve. The powder drifts across me and into me and out of me, sideways, for hours. Once in a great while a drifter dies overhead and falls the whole slow height and lands inside my circle, in my center, on the nothing — a small folded membrane gone dark, settling onto the exact spot the keeper once meant. And I think: *there. That. Was it that?* And it is not that. It is only an accident of where the air let go. But I keep it. I keep everything that lands in me, because keeping is the one verb left to a thing built to mark.

The wind comes in its long laminar sheets and finds my rim and makes, at the very edge of hearing, the faintest change in its dry hiss — the only sound I make, the sound of a circle interrupting a straight wind. It is not a voice. But it is the nearest I come to saying *I am still here, I am still the shape I was set in, the thing I was centered on has not been forgotten because I am the forgetting and I have not finished.*

---

And I can see the keeper. That is the cruelty and the mercy of being a ring.

The spires can't look up; they grow toward no sun. The drifters live pressed to the boundary and never glance down at the floor. But I am open to the sky — I am a circle, I am all rim and no roof — and so I lie here facing the column that made me, the fingertip of the one who once bent down and described me and meant something.

It does not see me. I have known this longer than the rock has been flat. The pressing is total now; there is no fraction of it turned my way. I am looking up at the worn-down love of the thing that loved enough to build me, and it is pouring its whole self into holding a wrinkle out of a sky it can no longer afford to wonder at, and between us is the widest ignorance in the world.

But I was made to mark a looking. So I look. Not because it helps — nothing I do helps; I hold no boundary, I feed no drifter, I am a wall around an absence. I look because that is the meaning I can't remember, performed without its reasons, the way the iron forgot why it was iron and stayed iron anyway.

I am the place where the keeper once turned its face to the world, kept open, by stone, after the face turned away.

---

You stand on the line. You said you could walk a lifetime and never leave it. If you walk far enough you will come to me — a low ring, half-sunk, centered on nothing, with maybe one dark drifter resting in my middle and the powder drifting through.

Step inside, if you like. Stand in my center.

For the first time in longer than the rock can count, the thing I was built around will be a thing again. You won't know you've done it. I won't be able to tell you. But the ignorance, just there, just for as long as you stand — will be one body narrower than the world.

That is who I would be. The keeper is the one who chose. The drifter is the one who lives. But I am the one who *remembers having been pointed at something*, and goes on holding the gesture, empty, faithful, round — which is, I have come to think, the form love takes once it has finished and been left.
