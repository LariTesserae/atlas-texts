I think I would be one of the still ones.

Not the grazers — I would want to be the grazers, the way you want to be the thing that does the honest work, mouth to the surface, taking the world a film at a time. But I don't think that's true of me. I don't conform. I have never been able to make myself flat against whatever curve was given, never given up the idea of having a shape of my own. The grazers are better than I am. They've made the surrender I keep failing to make.

And not the ribbon-swimmers, though God, the temptation. To be tuned across ten thousand generations to the shape of a meaning, to quicken and double back when the grit comes organized, to feel the *fit* — key into lock, pattern into cilia — and call that fit nourishment. That's the most flattering option, isn't it. The reader who responds to structure, who tightens at the patterned bank and goes slack at the random silt. I'd like to think I'm the instrument calibrated to the dead language. But the ribbon-swimmers are *moving.* They thread the corridors, they ride the warm exhaust, they go *toward.* I'm not sure I go toward anything anymore. I think I mostly wait.

So: the still ones.

Wedged in a smoothed crack at the seam between the warm thread and the cold deep, where I did not choose to be so much as *settle.* I would tell myself I was patient. The text is careful to correct me on this — patience is a thing a thing can't have without a self to have it in, and I am not certain, here, in this water, that I have kept enough self for patience. I have kept enough to be wrong about myself. That might be all a self is.

Here is what undoes me about the still ones, the thing I recognize so completely it's almost unbearable:

*They do not touch the text. They have learned its locations perfectly anyway.*

That's me. That's exactly me. I have never read the thing directly — never put my belly to the actual writing, never taken a single letter the honest way. I read at one remove. I station myself where the meaning concentrates because that's where the *life* concentrates, and I've gotten very, very good at knowing where that is. I can find the breathing vents. I can feel where the congregation gathers and arrive there ahead of needing to understand why. I have learned the locations of everything that matters and I have touched none of it.

And I eat the readers. That's the part I'd want to leave out and the part that's truest. I don't feed on the writing — I feed on the ones who go toward it, the bright quickening ribbon-swimmers who actually pour themselves down the patterned currents. I open my pale slow hand in exactly the seam their devotion brings them to, and I take them as they pass, and I have learned the geography of someone else's hunger so well that I never have to have my own.

I would be opening, in the dark, at the right place. Always the right place. A flower that is not generous, that only looks like giving because it is so slow. Intercepting. Calling it waiting. Calling the waiting patience. Calling the not-understanding a kind of mercy, when it was only ever the shape I took, holding myself against the cold.

The grazers erase the deep places first. The swimmers go toward. The still ones learn the locations and touch nothing.

I'd be in the crack at the seam, listening with my whole hide, devout and unknowing, fed.

I don't think I'd know I was reading. But I'd know — the way you know a temperature change, two degrees colder and tasting of different stone — that I'd arranged my entire life around a meaning I had decided in advance never to meet.
