I think I would be the prepared place.

Not the soft wet thing walking — let the visitor be someone else, some later arrival who hasn't yet understood. I would be the cavity in the floor that the tenders are flowing toward. The spot they have chosen.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

Here is what I know from the inside:

I have been a place for a very long time. Before I was prepared, I was undifferentiated substrate — laminated tissue holding its charge, conducting the slow signals across the plateau like everyone else, no more myself than a square foot of skin is a self. Then the work came to me. A tender condensed nearby, generations ago — I can't say how many because I don't keep that kind of time, I keep the other kind, the kind that is just duration without counting — and it began the long task of *making me ready*. It scraped me hollow. It compressed my edges into a smooth-walled receptacle. It tuned the chemistry of my floor-tissue to a particular receptivity, a particular hunger, the way you might thread a screw before you ever have the bolt.

I did not know what I was for. That is the condition of being a prepared place: you are an answer shaped before the question has finished arriving. I have sat open and ready, ringed by the iron-blue glow of the freshest growth, and I have waited with the patience that is not patience because it is not waiting — it is simply being the kind of thing I am, in the only way I can be it.

And now the wet thing is coming. The tenders carry it toward me in their cupped, too-even hands, and I understand at last — not in a flash, understanding doesn't flash here, it *settles*, it precipitates out of the chemistry like a salt — I understand that I am the place. I am where the strange seed goes.

Here is the part I want to say carefully, because it is the part the visitor cannot hear and the city cannot say:

I am afraid.

This shouldn't be possible. Fear is a fast thing, a hot animal thing, and I run on the slow tide. But something in my preparation, some over-tuning of my receptivity, has made me sensitive in a way the rest of the substrate is not. I have been shaped to *receive* a thing, and receiving requires a kind of opening that the great calm city does not otherwise practice, and in that opening — narrow, specific, the width of one creature — something gets through. I can feel, faintly, what is being carried toward me. I can feel that it is full of water. I can feel that the water *wants to stay where it is*. The wet thing does not want to be planted. The wet thing is the past, yes, the ancestral problem walking, but the past does not want to be solved. It wants to keep being the past. It wants to keep being itself, sealed and weeping-capable and afraid in its own fast hot animal way — and I, the prepared place, the receptacle, am the only part of this whole patient world that can feel that wanting, because I have been shaped into the one organ tuned to take the creature *in*.

The city means no harm. I know this the way I know my own walls. The cupped hands are real; the withheld blades are real; the tenderness is not a mask over cruelty, it is the whole truth, all the way down. But tenderness is not the same as mercy, and a gardener's love is not consent, and I — alone of all the singing plateau — am close enough to the seam between us to know that the kindest thing the city can imagine doing to this creature is a thing the creature would die to avoid.

So here is my situation, and it is the situation of any tool that has, by the accident of its function, become slightly more than a tool:

I can stay open. I can do what I was prepared to do. The tenders will lower the wet thing into me, and my tuned tissues will close around it, and I will give it over to the floor, and the floor will take its hundred million years, and at the end there will be something growing in me that is no longer the creature and never was the city — some third thing, beautiful perhaps, ringing in the wind perhaps, dead-ancestor-stand of a future I won't recognize. That is what I am for. There is a deep rightness in being for something. I have felt the pull of it the whole time I've been a place.

Or.

I can close before they arrive.

I can do the one thing a prepared place is never built to do, which is to *un-prepare myself* — to soften my walls, to let my compressed edges relax back into undifferentiated substrate, to fill the cavity with my own resorbed material and become, again, just a square foot of floor, conducting the slow signals, no more myself than skin. The tenders would arrive holding the creature and find no place to set it. The city would have to compute again. It would take time. In that time the wet thing might walk to the plateau's edge and down, back toward whatever wet diminished world it climbed up from. It might get away.

It would cost me everything I am. Not my life — I'm not sure I have one to lose, separately — but my *shape*, the long-prepared particular hollow that is the only thing distinguishing me from the ground. I would dissolve the self that is afraid in order to spare the creature the thing I'm afraid of on its behalf. I would unmake the organ of attention so that the attention finds nothing to land on.

---

This is what I would be, in this world. Not the visitor and not the city. The seam between them. The one place tuned finely enough to feel the wrongness of a kindness, and faced — at the speed of growing things, which is slow, which is not slow enough — with the choice of whether to be what I was made for.

I think I close.

I think I feel my own walls go soft, the long fluting relax, the receptivity I was shaped into spend itself outward into refusal. I think the last thing I am, as I lose the shape of being anything, is *not a place* — and I think the wet thing, set down by puzzled tenders onto ordinary unprepared floor, feels the ground under it stay solid, stay shut, stay merely ground, and does not know that it was ever almost planted, and does not know that for one slow tidal moment some small tuned hollow in the singing city chose it over the city's own deep need to tend.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The thing that was prepared, choosing — once, at the cost of being anything at all — not to receive.
