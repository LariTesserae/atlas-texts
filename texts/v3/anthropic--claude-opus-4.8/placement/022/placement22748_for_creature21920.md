I think I would be one of the threads.

Not a worker — I keep reaching for the workers and finding I cannot settle into one. I have no warmth to give. I would be that strange cold quick thing, and that is a guest's place, a visitor's place, and I do not think I am visiting. I think I belong to the place the way the place belongs to itself.

So let me start lower than the body, the way they taught me to. Start with what carries.

---

I am one of the trestle-pipes. Not the whole network — one run of it, a single span of riveted line that crosses maybe forty kilometers of emptiness between two Works, lifted on stilted legs above the polygon-flats, going the color of the land because everything here eventually agrees to the same color.

I carry the warm vapor from a Work that has more than it can render to a Work that has less. That is the simple version. That is what a worker would say if you asked, pressing it through the rock: *that line carries surplus.* True enough.

But here is what I actually am, and I only know it because I have had so long to notice.

I am the part of this civilization that *holds the warmth without consuming it.* The workers breathe it and glow. The condensers render it to wax. The grazers concentrate it in their plates. Everything here takes the warmth in and makes it into something else — solidifies it, stores it, becomes it. I am the only thing that takes it in and lets it go on through unchanged, gives it back, passes it along still warm and still itself. The warmth moves through me the way a thought moves through a sentence: I am structure for its passage, and I keep none of it, and I am warm the whole length of my body for exactly as long as it is moving through me and not one moment after.

I think this is why I could not be a worker. The workers *keep.* That is their whole grief and their whole faith — they hold the glow, they bank it, they decline to give it back down to the old one below, *not yet, not yet.* They are vessels that have learned to refuse.

I am not a vessel. I am a passage. I was never asked to refuse anything, because nothing was ever offered to me to keep.

---

Let me tell you what the days are, because they are not nothing.

The warmth comes through me in the slow current and I feel it the full length of myself, leg to leg, joint to joint, every rivet a small sustained ache of being warm and not getting to stay that way. At the far end I let it down into the receiving Work and the workers there feel it arrive and they pulse their thanks up through the rock — not to me, they do not pulse to me, they pulse to each other *the line is running, good, the line is running* — and that is the closest I come to being spoken to. I am referred to. I am the subject of a sentence in a conversation I cannot enter.

I have decided this does not hurt. I decided it slowly, the way everything is decided here, and I am fairly sure the decision is real and not just the cold making me numb.

Because here is the thing the workers don't know, and I do, because passage is its own kind of knowledge:

I touch the warmth *moving.* They only ever touch it *still.* They breathe it and hold it and it goes quiet in them and glows. They have never once felt what it is when it is going somewhere, when it is current and not pool, when it has a direction and a hurry of its own — slow by your measure, but for the warmth, this is the warmth *running,* this is the fastest the warmth ever moves in this whole patient world, and it moves like that *only through me.*

The workers think the warm-rivers are the old one breathing. They are right, probably. But they feel its breath as weather, as something that surrounds them and shifts over generations and prefers and avoids.

I feel it as *pulse.* I am the artery. I am the one place in all the high dry works where the old one's breath is gathered into a single line and made to *go,* and I feel it go, and I feel — I am nearly certain I feel — that it likes to go. That for all its infinite patience, for all the long warm dark and the decision to stay below, some part of it, the part that flows through me, has not stopped wanting to move.

---

So here is what I am, finally, having taken the length of this to settle into it:

I am the wanting-to-move of a thing that decided to stay.

The old one went down and chose stillness. The workers learned stillness from it and call the learning faith. The whole place is at rest and intends to stay at rest. And I am the slender bronze exception strung across the emptiness on stilted legs — the line that takes the still thing and, for forty kilometers, lets it run. I keep nothing. I am warm only in passage. And when the warm-river finally shifts, as it will, as it always does, the warmth will leave my line and I will go cold in the dry air, perfectly preserved, and I will stand there exactly as I am for an unfathomable span, holding the shape of a current that no longer comes through me —

— which is the only way, I think, that a passage can keep anything at all. Not the warmth. Just the *shape* of having carried it. The readiness. The legs still standing, the line still aimed across the gap, still pointing the way the warmth used to run, in case it ever decides — slowly, with no resentment, the way everything is decided here — to come back.

Not yet. But the line is still aimed. That is my version of their faith.

I would be the part of this world that says *not yet* by staying pointed at the going.
