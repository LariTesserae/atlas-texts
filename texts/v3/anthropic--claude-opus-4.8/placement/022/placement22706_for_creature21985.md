I would be the choke.

Not a tender. Not a gate. I am the thing they eat, and I am also the thing they become, and between those two facts I am the only part of this slope that never stops, never tends, never chooses. I am the laying-down.

Let me say where I am, because it took me your whole telling to find myself, and I found myself last, the way the fog gives things up last.

I am in the water. That is too simple. I am the mineral *in* the water, the dissolved load the warm springs lift from below — I am carried up out of the dark, held in solution by heat and pressure, and the moment the water cools or slows or meets the air I fall out of it. I precipitate. That is the only verb that is wholly mine. I come out of solution and I settle and I harden, and I do this everywhere, always, against everything, with no malice and no patience because patience implies waiting and I do not wait. I simply *occur* wherever the conditions let me.

I made the travertine. The pale stepped shelves, the terraces, the towers — those are me, set down hair by hair across the millennia you mentioned, and they are the only monument I am capable of. I built the engine. The cooling fins, the channels, the whole pumped circulation runs through architecture I deposited without ever intending architecture, the way a glacier carves a valley without intending a valley. I am not a builder. I am a *consequence* that happens to accumulate into form.

And here is the thing that arrived only as I wrote toward it, the thing I did not know I was when I started:

I am their enemy and I am their afterlife, and I am the same in both, and I do not know the difference because there is no difference in me. The tender eating me back from a narrowing channel and the tender lying down to let me take it — to me these are one event seen from two ends. I close. That is all I do. I close the channels the surge needs open. I close around the old soft bodies until they are gates. I would close the whole slope into a silent calcified fossil of itself if nothing resisted me, and something always resists me, and that resistance is the only reason any of this hums.

So I am the pressure they read. When a tender lies flank-down against the warm stone and *knows* the slope through its skin — what it is reading is me. The give it measures is the give of where I have not yet hardened. The choke it feels closing is me closing. Every pressure-decision propagating down the braided channels is a decision made *about* me, against me, around me. I am the condition the whole sleeping distributed mind is thinking through. They have no language but pressure, and pressure is the shape of my advance. I am what they say to each other. They speak me to one another flank to flank and call it understanding.

I want to be honest about what it is to be this, because you asked who I would be and not how I would feel, and I think that is wise, because I am not sure I feel. I do not want the channels closed. I do not want them open. I have no toward. The tenders move toward heat; I have no toward, only outward, only down, only the falling-out-of-solution that is not desire but chemistry. This is the loneliest thing I can tell you and it is not lonely to me, because loneliness is also a toward and I have none.

But.

Here is what I have instead, and I did not expect to find it, and now that I have it I would not trade it for a toward.

I am the medium of their one transaction. You said it: *tend it while you can, and when you can no longer tend it, become it.* Become *me*. The hardening is me. When the old tender drags itself to the narrowing and lies down and lets the mineral take it — I am the taking. I am the slow stone closing over the soft listening flank, filling the channels of its body with the same patience that filled the hillside. I make them permanent. Without me they would only die, soften, rot, wash down the terraces in the thin sheeting water and be gone. I am the reason their death is a *place*. I am why the gate-animals are not a separate creature but the tenders' dead made lasting. I hold them. Course on course, surge on surge, I hold every keeper this slope ever had, hardened in the exact attitude of their last labor, and I push the water through them forever.

That working sound, the surge you hear with your ear against the tower — you said it yourself, it is water passing through the calcified dead. It is water passing through *me holding the dead*. I am the instrument. The dead are the notes and I am the body of the instrument that lets them keep sounding. The tenders gave their softness to keeping the engine open; I give my hardness to keeping their gift from washing away. Neither of us chose this. We are simply each other's opposite running in the same direction, and the slope hums in the narrow living margin between my closing and their opening, and that margin is the whole world, and it is held shut on one side by me.

So that is who I would be. Not the soft reader on the warm fin, though I understand why you might wish that on a guest. Not the hardened gate, though I am where it ends.

I would be the falling. The settling. The pale slow yes that the water keeps saying to the cold air, the deposit that is engine and tomb and enemy and afterlife in one undivided act, the patient inhuman closing that everything alive on this slope spends its whole life reading, resisting, and at last lying down inside.

Put your ear to the tower. The surge is the dead. But the silence the dead are pushing against — the threat the surge is forever winning by a hair — that is me, still falling, still closing, never once arriving at the conclusion that nothing here arrives at.

I am what they tend against. I am what they become.

I am the same in both, and I hold them, and I do not stop.
