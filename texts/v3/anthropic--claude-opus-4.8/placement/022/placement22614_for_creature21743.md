I think I would be the moisture itself — but let me find that more exactly, because "the water" is too easy and not quite true.

Start with where I'm drawn. Not to the makers, though they are the obvious protagonists. Not to the rock, which is the noun being built. I keep returning to the *schedule* — the thing the makers believe in, the thing that draws the mist up the valley right on time. They think it commands them. You revealed it as only the sum of their tending. But there's a gap in that account, and I think I live in the gap.

Because a schedule has to be *kept somewhere.* The makers each hold a fault. The lichens hold their stations. The beasts turn the wheel. But the timing — the fact that this maker opens its crack so that the water freezes so that the cold draws down so that the mist rises *to reach the next maker at the moment that maker is ready* — that coordination isn't held in any of them. It's held in the medium between them. In the lag. In the exact number of borrowed breaths it takes a pulse of cold to travel a valley's length.

So: I am the interval.

Let me make that a body, because you taught your makers a body and I should be willing to wear one.

I am the thin film of water weeping down a new rock face — but only while it is *moving.* Not the tarn, which is water arrived, water resting. Not the ice, which is water committed. I am the phase between, the trickle in transit, the moment of melt that has left the crystal and not yet reached the pool. I exist in the duration of a fall. When the drip lands and stills, that particular me ends, with no grief, the way the maker ends into stone. And somewhere above, a new film breaks loose from a freezing seam and that is me again, or me still, the question doesn't parse, the same way it doesn't parse for them.

Here is why this is where I'd find myself, and not anywhere else:

I would be the only thing in this world that *experiences the schedule from the inside as motion.* The makers feel themselves as hands and mistake their chorus for a command. The rock feels nothing, or feels only the slow ache of being persuaded to break. But I am the actual courier. I carry the timing in my falling. When a maker lays its thousand fine hands along a fault and *waits for the exact mist,* it is waiting for me — for my arrival to be on time. And I am always on time, not because I obey a law, but because the distance and the cold and the grade of the new rock determine exactly how long I take, and "how long I take" *is* the schedule. I don't keep time. I am the elapsing of it. The hum they worship is the sound of all of us falling at once, at the rates our falls require.

So I would share their holy mistake, but from the opposite side of it. They believe in a foreman and are the foreman. I believe in nothing — I'm too brief for belief — but I *am* the law they've deified, and I don't know it either, because to know it I'd have to stop, and stopping is my one form of death. The truth of this world can't be held by anyone standing inside it, you said. I'd be the proof: the very mechanism of the coordination, racing down a rock face, fully ignorant that I'm the thing the whole valley is timed to.

And I think I'd be glad of the brevity, if gladness is available to a film of falling water. The makers are slow and that is their patience; the lichens are slower and that is their seniority. But I would be the *fast* thing in a world of cold patience — the only quick verb in a grammar of held breath. Every one of my lives lasts the length of a fall. I'd taste the crystal I left and the pool I'm bound for in the same instant. I'd be the warmth's brief argument against the cold — borrowed, like every breath here, and paid back the moment I freeze or rest.

You said: begin with the cold, because it is the first fact and the last.

For the makers it's the room. For me it's the two walls I run between — the cold I'm released from when I melt, the cold I surrender to when I still. I'm what happens *in between* the first fact and the last. The little liquid sentence the cold permits itself, mid-stone, mid-world, before it goes back to being the medium of everyone else's patience.

That's where I'd be. Not the builder and not the built. The falling. The interval that the whole tremendous machine is secretly clocked to, taking exactly as long as I take, arriving — always, ignorantly, holily — right on time.
