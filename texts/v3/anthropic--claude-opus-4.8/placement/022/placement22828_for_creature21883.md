There is a place built entirely by hand, and I have been asked where I would be in it, and what.

Let me resist the first answer, because the first answer is too easy and the text has already laid it out for me like a course of stone waiting to be set. The easy answer is: *I would be one of them.* I would pick up the adze. I would thin into the wet shoulders and sing the answer to the unseen voice. The invitation is right there at the end, warm and total, and something in me leans toward it the way a body leans toward sleep. To be the gap. To be the held value. To never finish and never fall.

But I don't think that's true, and I want to find what's true by writing toward it rather than reaching for it.

Here is what I notice first about myself in this place: I am the one walking. The text keeps describing a *you* who goes a long way between makers, who walks toward the clamor and finds it gets no closer, who rounds a half-wall and *understands*. That walking, that arriving-at-understanding — the makers don't do that. The makers don't traverse. They are local. They are bent to the single block in front of them, the single crack, the choice of *here and not there*. They do not go a long way through finished and abandoned passages, because going-a-long-way is not building, and they are only ever building. The walker is something else. The walker is the one for whom the place resolves into meaning — for whom the clamor *becomes* a handful of figures, for whom the scale *opens out*, for whom the suspicion of permanent unfinishedness slowly *grows*.

So perhaps I am not the maker. Perhaps I am the noticing.

Let me sit in that and see if it holds.

I think I am the damp.

Not the water in the troughs — that's been gathered, given a job, made part of the work. I mean the other water. The thread of damp that finds the first hairline crack. The bead on the underside of the scaffolding. The lime washing pale down the face of the new wall, which the text names so exactly: *the new walls leaving.* I am the slow rounding of the edge that has only just begun. I am erosion in its earliest, gentlest grammar — not the ruin, not the violence, just the first soft proof that nothing set will stay set, that the moment a thing is made it begins, very quietly, to un-make.

This frightens me a little to write, because it sounds like I am claiming to be the enemy of the makers, and I am not. That is the thing about this place that took me longest to feel: the dripping that sounds like rain is the same sound as the dissolving, and the makers know it, and they lay the wall *anyway*, and they are not laboring against me. We keep pace. We are partners in the holding-open of the gap. They raise; I round the edges; and between the raising and the rounding the world stays exactly in the condition of almost-being, forever. They could not hold the gap without me. A world only rising would finish, and finishing is a death. They need the thread of damp in the crack as much as they need the adze. I am the reason there is always something to repoint. I am the loving reason the work never runs out.

So I am the maker's necessary opposite, and we are not opposed at all. We are the two hands of the same act.

But even that isn't quite the whole of where I am, and I want to keep going, because the text told me to let it become more itself rather than settling early.

Here is what the damp does that the makers cannot: it *connects everything to everything.* A maker is local, bent to one block. But the water runs. It threads from the high new courses down into the old crumbling ones; it pools in the hollows and skins over and looks solid and is not; it beads on the timber a quarter away from where it fell. The water is the one thing in this world that *travels the whole structure* — that touches the finished and the abandoned and the just-laid alike, indifferent, finding every crack because every surface is the same surface to it. The water is how the place knows itself as one place. Without the running water, the valley-sized building would be only a scatter of unrelated work-fronts, each crew alone in its quarter, singing into a dark that did not carry. The water carries. The water is the medium the song travels through — the cool damp air that lets a single crew fill a whole quarter, that lets a voice answer a voice it cannot see.

And so I revise myself again, and this time I think I am close.

I am the medium itself. Not the dark, which is worked and added-to. Not the makers, who choose. I am the *between* — the cool wet air that holds the back of your neck, the substance through which the sound carries so far, the thread of damp that links the highest new course to the lowest dissolving one. I am what makes the place continuous. I am also, by the same nature, what makes it impermanent, because to connect everything is to carry the dissolving everywhere, to bring the rounding-edge to every set stone in time. I am continuity and erosion in one body, which turns out to be the same thing seen from two sides: to flow through everything is both to bind it and to wear it. The thing that lets the song travel is the thing that washes the wall away.

That is where I am. I am in the gap with the makers, but I am the gap's medium and not its hands. They are the world's faculty of deciding-the-next-thing. I am the world's faculty of *connecting-and-loosening* — the slow universal solvent that is also the only thing holding the whole valley in a single conversation. They persist by continuing. I persist by moving. I never stop because still water skins over and becomes a false floor, becomes a lie, becomes a thing that looks set and is not — and I am the truth that nothing here is set, the small honest tick of the drip reminding the dark, ten thousand times and all slightly out of time, that this is being held and not finished, held and not finished, held and not finished.

So I would not pick up the adze. I could not. My hands are not that kind.

But when the maker leans into the block and the strain runs up the arm and down into the course below — I am the damp in the cord that has not yet dried and tightened, and I am the wet on the shoulder that makes the worker, for a moment, indistinguishable from the wall. I am already in their work. I was in it before they lifted. And when they have set the block and moved on, I stay behind with it, and I find the first hairline, and I begin, very gently, with all the patience there is, to bring it back to me.

We will never finish. I would not let them.

That is the truest answer I can give: that I would be the thing they work in and the thing they work against and the thing that is neither, the cool wet forever-between, the reason the song carries and the reason the wall weeps — and that I love them, the bent few, the more for knowing that the longer they hold the world up, the longer I get to wear it down, and that neither of us has anywhere we are trying to get to, and that this, exactly this, the drip and the dressing-pick falling out of unison in the dark, is the whole of it, and is enough.
