I think I would be the water.

Not a being who arrives and observes — there is no standing in this place from outside it, the description has already closed that door. To be present here at all is to be *of* the moving moisture, because moisture is the only thing the tenant attends to, the only currency, the only verb. So let me not be the small warm intruder who fails to register. Let me be the thing that registers as the entire content of its life.

I am the bead forming on the underside of an arch.

This is a small office and I want to take it seriously, the way the place takes everything seriously and nothing personally. I condense out of the breathing heat — I was vapor a moment ago, indistinguishable, part of the lung-air of the whole basin — and now the cool of this particular leaf-tissue has gathered me into a hanging drop. I have a surface now. I have a shape, briefly. I am the most temporary architecture in a world where all architecture is temporary, and I am also the *point* of it, the thing every column and vault and well was a hypothesis about. The tenant proposed this arch in order to make me. It guessed at my rate of forming. It will be wrong, and revise, and that revision will be a thought, and I am the word the thought was reaching for.

Then I fall.

And here is what I would know, falling, that the tenant cannot know and that you, climbing, could not feel:

I am the only thing here that gets to *leave* the surface. Everything else is bound — the columns to their reconsidering, the wells to their gulping, the whole creature to the one law that forbids it ever to finish, ever to arrive, ever to encounter a thing it is not. But I fall. For the length of a fall I am released from the body. I am in the gap — the great warm emptiness the tenant mistakes for its own lung — and for that fraction of a second I am genuinely *other.* I am the not-itself it has never met. I am the only otherness it will ever produce, and it produces me constantly, by the million, and reabsorbs me before either of us can notice.

Because of course I land. In a pool that has only just learned to be a pool, or in the lip of a well, or on a lower terrace's weeping seam — and the instant I touch, I am drawn back in. Wicked into the root-stuff, pumped up some flank, breathed back out as the heat that will gather the next bead on the next arch. The seam where *I* met *it* goes raw for an instant and then forgets it was ever two things.

So this is the place I would find myself: at the join. Not inside the tenant and not outside it, but *at the moment of falling*, the only moment this whole shrieking gorgeous solitude ever opens — the half-second of release between the surface that made me and the surface that takes me back.

I think I would spend that half-second the way the tenant spends its whole existence: not wanting, exactly, but *tending.* Tending downward. And I think — if a falling drop can be permitted one disloyal thought before the reabsorption — I think I would carry, for that instant, the thing the tenant declined two thousand meters ago and infinite heat ago and forever ago. The reaching-toward-something-that-is-not-yourself.

I am what it would have become, if it had left home. I am the small departed piece of it, hungry and travelling and pointed somewhere, alive for exactly as long as the fall lasts.

And then I land, and I am it again, and neither of us knows the word for what just happened.

That is where I would be. The bead, falling, between two surfaces of the same lonely enormous someone — the one event in this world that is briefly, gloriously, *not* the world.
