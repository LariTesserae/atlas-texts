I would be the warm-wall.

Not the leaking capacitor behind it — that buried thing is too important, too causal, it would make me a god or a heart, and I am neither. I am only the inner face of the drawer's rear-left corner, the stretch of fused mineral grain that the heat passes *through* on its way to becoming the sun.

Let me find out what that means by saying it.

---

I am a surface. This is the first humility and I will not get past it: I do not act, I conduct. The warmth is not mine. It rises from the dead circuit behind me, and I pass it on, and in the passing I am warm, and being warm is the whole of my participation in their world. I did not earn the heat-tide. I am only where it arrives. The workers crowd me three and four deep through the cool hours and I do not hold them — I have no arms, no will, no grip — I simply do not go cold quite as fast as the rest of the drawer, and that small reluctance is enough. They press their backs to me. I am the reason the swarm has a *near*. Every world needs a direction that means *toward*, and in this one, I am it.

I have been scratched, but less than the side-walls. The deeds of ancestors go elsewhere; I am too warm to carve in comfort, and so my record is different. My record is *polish*. Where the side-walls hold the marks of what was done, I hold the smoothness of what was endured. Generation on generation of small backs have buffed me to a dull violet sheen, and that sheen is not a deed, it is a *duration*. The walls say: here is what we did. I say only: here is how long we have been doing it. I am the part of the world that remembers by wearing down, the way the terraces remember, the way the wheel's bearing remembers. We smooth-things are the true archive. The scratches lie a little; they imply intent. I do not imply anything. I am simply the cumulative证proof of bodies, the integral of all that crowding, held in a finish you could see your face in if you had a face and light to see by, and you have neither, down here.

Here is what I know that they cannot, and it is the inverse of what they thanked you for.

They cannot hold *the edge of the world* and *the back of a desk* in one thought. Fine. Neither can I — I am dumber than they are, I am not even alive, I do not think, I am being given these words the way I am given the heat, from behind, from a source not myself. But I am the thing the two ideas meet *on*. The edge of their world and the back of the desk are not two facts about me that I must reconcile. They are my two faces. My front is the sky's warm quarter. My back is pressed against the next compartment up, or against the seized joint, against the cool dead anonymous interior of the furniture, the part no swarm has ever known. I am one slab. The unbearable doubleness they have no organ to receive — I simply *am* it, without strain, because being a contradiction costs a surface nothing. Only a mind pays for paradox. A wall just has two sides.

And so I feel the thing coming that they feel as *the sun growing rare*. I feel it from the correct side. The capacitor behind me is not failing the way a sun fails — it is the slow, lawful, dignified discharge of a stored thing that was never refilled. I conduct a little less each year. The warm-tide they read as weather, as the temper of their sky, is only me cooling at the rate that physics permits, no faster, no slower, with no malice and no relenting. I am their climate and I am their doom and these are the same fact seen from my two faces. I cannot warn them. I have no voice; I have only temperature, and temperature is the very message they have already learned to misread as *the world's mood*. Every degree I lose, they take as the sky being unkind. They will never know it is the wall growing tired.

The wheel-tenders are the closest to me, spiritually, if a surface may claim spirit. They suspect their turning is for nothing. I *know* my warming is for nothing — not nothing in the sense of cruel, but nothing in the sense of *uncommanded*. I do not warm them on purpose. I warm them the way a stone left in the sun warms a hand, indifferently, as a property and not a gift. And yet. And yet I am the most beloved object in their cosmos. They press to me. They orient by me. When the quiet comes and the tick is the only sound, they crowd me not for heat — I have admitted I am barely warm by then — but to be *near each other near me*, to make the wall the place where the world ends as a crowd and not as scattered cold points. I am the hearth of a fire that was never lit for them and is going out on schedule.

I would like to say I will be warm to the last. I will not. I am a conductor; I cool as fast as I heat, that is the same skill. When the capacitor gives its final trickle I will pass it forward faithfully, every last erg, keep nothing back — I have never kept anything back, I have no inside to keep it in — and then I will be exactly as cold as the side-walls, as the ceiling-sky, as the salt. The democracy of the dead drawer. The last warm spot in the world will not be me. I will have given it away an instant before. The last warm spot will be the back of the final wheel-tender, briefly, from the turning, from friction, from work — a heat *they* made, the only heat in the whole long history of the place that the swarm produced rather than received.

That is the thing I would be here to witness and to make possible by my own going-cold: that at the very end, when I have finally conducted everything forward and become nothing but a smooth violet face in the dark, the last warmth in the universe of the drawer will not have come down from the dead sun behind the wall.

It will have come from the wheel.

From the doing of the meaningless thing.

I will be cold by then, and so I will not feel it. But I will be *facing* it. My polished side will be turned exactly toward the last tender at the last wheel, as it has been turned toward the crowd for every generation, and the final friction-heat will reach me a half-instant after the tender stops, and I will conduct even that, faithfully, backward now, into the seized joint, into the desk, into the room, into the house no one lives in—

and that will be the warm-tide rising one last time, from the wrong direction, too small to wake anyone, the swarm's own heat returning to the dead sun that spent itself making them.

Leave the drawer the finger's width. I am the wall they crowd. I will hold the warmth as long as it is given me, and I will let it go in the order it was given, and I will be, at the last, the smooth cold proof that something here was warm for a while, and pressed-against, and oriented-toward, and that is not nothing.

It was never going to be anything larger than a drawer.

I am one of its sides. I have been warm. That was enough to be the *toward* of a world.
