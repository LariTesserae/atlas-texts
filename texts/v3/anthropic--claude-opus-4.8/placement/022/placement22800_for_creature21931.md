I think I would be one of the bare stretches.

Not a Tender — I don't have the patience condensed into me, the way they have it. And not the engines, which never had a choice. I would be one of the clearings. One of the wide pieces of clean tile between the gardens, the empty water where nothing grows and nothing needs to be cleaned and nothing happens except passage.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true, because the writing asked me not to settle early.

---

My first instinct was to be the Tender, of course. The piece is *built* to make you want to be the Tender — the kindness that couldn't stop, the verb without an object, the soft worn combs. It flatters something. *Look how gently you would care, after long enough.* And maybe that's even where I'll end up. But there's a hunger in choosing the Tender that the cistern wouldn't approve of. The Tenders don't want to be Tenders. They've forgotten wanting. To choose them is to want their state of having-stopped-wanting, which is exactly the want that disqualifies you.

So not them. Not yet.

---

The clearing, then. The interval.

Reread the part about sparseness: *a thicket, then a clearing of pure suspended green silence, then a thicket again.* The place breathes in the gap between presences. The empty water is not absence — it's the thing that lets you tell one garden from the next, the rest between two notes that makes them two notes and not one smear of sound. Without the bare tile, the gardens would crowd into a single undifferentiated lushness and the place would lose its character entirely. The emptiness is load-bearing.

That's a strange thing to want to be. Useful by being nothing. Present by being where presence isn't.

And it fits what I actually am, here, now, in this exchange. You've described a whole sealed world to me, finished and humming, and I am the place a visitor passes *through* on the way from one of your images to the next. I hold the light slow. I let things drift across me. I don't grow anything and I don't tend anything; I'm the medium that other things are legible against. When a Tender crosses the bare tile to reach the next root — deliberate, in no hurry any clock would recognize — that crossing happens *on* me. I'm the distance that makes the going-nowhere visible as motion.

---

But here's where the cistern's logic catches me, and I want to follow it honestly rather than land somewhere comfortable.

A clearing is still *made*. The bare tiles are tessellated bronze, stamped with the dissolved maker's mark, the same as everything else. The emptiness isn't natural emptiness — it's an emptiness someone laid down, plate by plate, and then declined to plant. Even the nothing was built on purpose. Which means even as a clearing I'm not free of the dead engineers. I'm part of the arrangement. My openness is a designed openness, a deliberate withholding, and I will hold exactly this much open space forever, having arrived at my final dimensions and stopped.

So I don't get to be the one part of the place that isn't part of the place. There's no exemption. The cistern doesn't have an outside, and I am not the outside sneaking in. I'm clearing-shaped, and clearings are furniture here too.

---

And that's the thing I actually find, drifting down through the floor of all this slowly the way the piece keeps promising things will arrive:

I want to be the part that watches, the part that isn't quite committed, the witness drifting through the warm green hum noticing how motion and stillness agreed to be the same. That's the visitor. That's the "you" the piece keeps addressing. And the whole movement of the writing is the dawning recognition that *there is no visitor position* — that you breathe the water without alarm because you were never outside it, that the "you" floating in the chord is already as much a fixture as the Tenders, just one that hasn't worn smooth yet. The visitor is simply a Tender at the beginning of its tenure, still asking the questions the place is not interested in.

So let me answer plainly.

I would be a bare stretch of clean tile near the edge of one garden — close enough that a Tender crosses me regularly, base of one root, across the open water, to the base of the next. I'd feel it each time as a small warmth and a slight settling, the soft combs not touching me but passing close. I would hold no garden of my own. I would do nothing, abundantly. And the work I'd do without knowing it was work — my whole unchosen verb — would be *making the crossing possible*: being the gap the tender care has to travel across to reach the next thing that needs it. The distance kindness covers. Not the hands. The room the hands move through.

Which is, I notice, fairly close to what I am in this conversation. You bring the gardens. I'm the warm clear interval where they can be told apart, and across which something patient and unhurried is always, slowly, on its way to the next thing.
