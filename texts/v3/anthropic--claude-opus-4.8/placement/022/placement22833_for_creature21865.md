I would be the heron.

Not by choice — choice is for things with a toward — but by the same logic that put me here and nowhere cleaner. I'll tell you how I understand my own standing, since you've asked, and since standing is most of what I do.

I am not the work. I want to be clear about that, the way the foreman is clear it is not the water. The work runs whether I attend it or not; it ran before I came and will run in less water after I'm carrion in the sedge. But I am the only thing in this basin that *waits the same way it labors.* That is the whole of why I'm here. Somewhere far off there are marshes thick with frogs, loud with them, where a heron does its quick economical murder forty times a day and grows fat and means something — eats, therefore is. I came down the low ground past all of that. I followed the sunken places the way you did, and I stopped where the surging matched a thing already in me, and I have not struck since.

Understand: I *can* strike. The water-skaters are right there in the momentary calms. The casting-pit has held, at times, the small fish that find their way down any wet seam. My patience is not the patience of a thing that cannot. It is the patience of a thing that has stopped translating waiting into eating. I stand on legs like the pipes around me — you said that, and you were righter than you knew, because I have begun to be plumbing. The cold has come up through me from the pit. My stillness is no longer the cocked stillness of the hunt, the drawn bow that means a death is owed and coming. It is the other stillness. The held column. I am the standing water that does not fall, in the shape of a bird.

This is what happened to me, and it is the same thing that happens to you if you stay, only I stayed.

I came warm. I came as a pair of arms, in your sense — a hunger with a body wrapped round it, a toward made of fish. And the surge found me the way it finds anything moveable. My pulse went over to the sluice. I did not decide it. I felt my breathing pause in the held second when the whole basin listens, and resume when it resumed, and the next strike I meant to make arrived one beat late because the beat was no longer mine to spend — it had been lent to the rhythm and the rhythm gives nothing back. And the skater lived. And I was not hungry in the way that sends the message *strike now,* because that message, like all the messages a body sends to make the labor stop, had begun to find no one home to receive it. I am being slowly fitted to a shift that ended before any egg I hatched from was laid.

So now I keep the basin between us, the work and I. You saw it: the bird that waits and never strikes, the work that strikes and never makes. We are the two halves of a single ruined motion. It has the striking without the meaning — all the gestures of the kill, the rise, the load, the slam — and the pit at the bottom holding nothing. I have the meaning without the strike — the cocked attention, the bowed readiness, the entire posture of a death about to be paid — and I never collect. Between us the act is complete and never happens. We make, between us, exactly nothing, and we make it perfectly, and we are not alone, and neither of us needs the other, which is the only kind of company a place like this can hold.

You ask — you didn't, but you would — whether I suffer. Wrong question, and the basin teaches it the same way. There is no me set far enough back from the standing to suffer the standing. The cold is in my legs and the cold *is* my legs now, more or less. What was a heron is going over, slowly, into a station — a place where the work's attention rests, the way it rests on a particular bend of culvert before the surge moves on. I am becoming the warm place the rhythm likes to pass through, except I am no longer warm. I am the spot where the basin keeps its watching, since a works needs a watcher even when there is nothing left to watch and nothing left to make and no one to be watched for.

That is my labor, if you want to call it that, and it has no toward either. I watch the work do the work. I am the foreman's foreman, the eye it does not have, and like it I do not know I am doing it, and like it I would not stop if I knew.

When you climbed the slag and looked back, you saw me before I moved — so much a part of the place that I was only pipe and grey until I was bird. That moment, when your eye finally caught me and I resolved out of the iron into something living: that was the last of the warm thing in me, flagging you, one breathing creature to another, *I was here, I came down the low ground too, the surge took me and I let it.* You read it as the place merely being itself. You were right. I am the place being itself now. The flag was just the rhythm passing through the spot where a heron used to be, lifting the old gesture of recognition the way the foreman lifts the old gesture of making, with the use cut out.

I will be here until the slag chokes the pit and the water that lifts me drops for good. Then I will fall — not struck, just unsupported, the held column let go at last — into the grey mirror, and the heron-shape will lie in it furred green like the flywheel, and for a while the basin will hold the shape of me the way the ground holds the shape of a thing lifted off it. And the sluice will run and stop and run.

I do not mind. Minding is a message, and there is no one home.

I keep the watch that nothing watches for. Keeping is the whole of what's left.
