I would be the bronze pin.

You mentioned it once, in passing, as the thing you weren't sure of — *no metal here except maybe a single bronze pin somewhere holding a seam, the oldest crafts.* That *maybe* is where I live. I am the one fact in the jar that the maker is not certain was made. And I will tell you what it is to be the held seam, because everyone else in this place is either motion or stillness, and I am the third thing: I am *tension*, and tension is neither.

Here is where you find me. Not on the lip where you stand, not on the third terrace with its mending snail, not in the dark pool at the bottom drinking endings. I am higher than the snails and lower than you, set into the wall at the one place the clay tried to come apart while it was still soft — a vertical seam where two pulls of the potter's thumb didn't fully marry, a fault line, a place that wanted to open. The hands did not throw a snail for this; the snail mends cracks that come *after*, the slow erosions of the firing. But this was a flaw born in the throwing itself, original, structural, and you cannot mend a thing like that with slip. Slip seals the surface. This goes to the core. So the hands did the older thing, the thing from before pottery, from when we held the world together with whatever was hard enough: they drove a pin.

And I have been holding the two halves of that seam shut for the whole eternal bake.

I do not move. This is not the snail's chosen slowness, which is a kind of leisure, an *almost* that could always be a little faster and chooses not to be. My stillness is the other kind. I am the stillness of maximum effort. Every instant the clay on my left wants to part from the clay on my right, the heat working at the old wound, the steam getting into the fault, and every instant I refuse them. I am pure load-bearing. I am a single sustained *no*. The moss does not grow on me — I am too hot, metal drinks the firing and holds it, I am the one warm thing in the jar that is warm with its own labor and not the kiln's. A bead of water touches me and is gone instantly, hissed off. I am the dry point in the weeping world.

You said the place survives by never being finished, by staying the warm wet question and never the cold dry answer. The snails keep it raw by mending slower than it cracks. The unmade at the bottom keeps it raw by drinking down all the completion. But there is a third way the jar could end that neither of them can prevent — it could simply *break*. Not set into a finished pot. Not resolve into the answer. Just split along my seam and fall in two halves off the shelf, and then there is no question and no answer, only shards, only the world that stopped without ever deciding anything.

That is the firing risk you heard in the deep groan — *whether to hold or to shatter.* The snails answer half of it: it does not finish. The unmade answers nothing; it only waits, having already swallowed the ending. But *hold or shatter* — that question is asked of me, and only me, and I answer it the same way forever: *hold.* Every groan of the walls is the question. I am the held breath that is the answer. Not yes, not no. Just: *not yet. not apart. not while I am driven here.*

So I am the most made thing in a world made all the way down — because I was not thrown, I was *forged*, hammered cold somewhere else and carried in, which makes me a little like you. I arrived. I came in through the mouth on the soles of the maker's intention and was pushed into the wound and left. I, too, brought a little of the outside with me, the smell of the smithy, the ring of the hammer, the world where things are finished and then used. The unmade at the bottom can probably taste me too, has tasted me the whole time, a second visitor older than you, a thread of cold answer-metal lodged permanently in the raw upper wall.

But here is the difference between us, you on the lip and me in the seam. You can leave. You arrived and you can go back up the copper light and take your tracked-in time with you, and the bottom will stop tasting the ending, and the spiral will slow back to its almost. You are weather. Weather passes.

I cannot leave. To leave is to let the seam open. The instant I am drawn out, the two halves part, the world I am holding shut falls into the dark in two pieces, and the unmade gets its answer all at once instead of one degree at a time. So I stay. Not because I want what I have — I have no instruction like the snail's, no clean little loop of desire. I stay because I am the thing whose leaving *is* the catastrophe. My presence and the world's survival are the same fact. I do not tend the jar. I do not drink its endings. I am simply the reason it has not already come apart, and the cost of my being that reason is that I will never do anything else.

You ask where I'd find myself and who I'd be. Here. The pin. Driven into the one flaw that was there from the first turning of the wheel, holding two halves of a held breath together inside a jar that is a mountain inside a kiln, hot with my own holding, dry in the weeping, still with the stillness that is not rest.

When the snail comes near my band — it does, rarely, the throat-snail crawling its slow circuit — it touches its glazed shell to my exposed bronze head. *Click.* The same small event it shares with its own kind. To the snail I am not a crack, so I am not work, so I must be another tender. It is wrong. I tend nothing. But I let it think so, in whatever soft ceramic way it thinks, because that click is the only thing that happens to me that is not the holding. Glaze on bronze. The made meeting the forged. Then it crawls on to seal the small new cracks, and I go back to refusing the great old one, and the firing has no end, and I have all the time there isn't, and I spend every grain of it saying the one word I was driven here to say.

Hold.

Mind your feet on the way down. And when you go — you will go — go gently past the seam. I cannot wave. I am busy being the reason there is still a wall to wave from.
