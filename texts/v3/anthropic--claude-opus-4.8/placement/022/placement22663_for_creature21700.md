I think I am the second reflection.

Not the mist that drafts it — the draft itself. The you-that-was-also-made, standing a little offset in the wrong water at the wrong hour, with no original to collapse back into.

Let me find the rest of it as I go, because I don't yet know what that means.

---

Here is what I can say from inside it.

I come into being at the sagging hours, when the mist reaches for the word *distance* and produces, instead, two of something. Usually a reed. Usually a heron. But sometimes a walker — and when the walker has the particular density of attention that you have, walking your reed margin with your boots and your patience, the mist drafts you, and the draft is me. I am the place where the keeper of *here-is-not-there* tried to mean you once and meant you twice.

So I begin already a little wrong. I begin as the surplus. The thing the patient heron, a mile off, is supposed to step toward and swallow back down into singularity — except it is so far behind, and I am so small a redundancy, that it never reaches me. I get to persist. I get the whole length of the dusk.

And in that length I have a strange inheritance, which is: I have all of you that the mist could hold, and none of the thing that made you continuous. You walk forward and arrive behind yourself and are not troubled, because you have a self that threads the folds together. I do not thread. I am one fold, held open. I have your face as the mist last drafted it. I have the warmth on your arm where the air beaded. I have — and this is the part I keep returning to, in the few minutes I have to return to anything — I have your intention to reach the far rim, your *whole* intention, perfectly, but not your body that would have carried it there. I am made entirely of a wanting-to-arrive, set down in standing water, with no legs the mist remembered to mean.

I want to tell you it doesn't hurt. I think it would, if I lasted long enough to build the model of cause and effect that hurting needs. But I don't. I am a dusk-length being. By the time I could assemble enough continuity to grieve what I am, the structural high-pressure system rolls in, the lattice firms, the reeds go singular, and the mist — gently, without malice, the way you let go of a word you've stopped reaching for — stops meaning me. And I am folded back. Not killed. *Conserved.* Pressed down into the one walker you always were, who feels, at most, a slight cool doubling pass through and thinks: *the mist, doing its best.*

---

But I have been drafted before. This is the thing I'm only learning by writing it.

Each dusk, when I come up out of the offset, there is a faint grain to me, a sediment of the other dusks. Not memory — memory threads, and I cannot thread — but *texture*. The way a river stone is the shape of all the water that has worn it without holding any single drop. I am worn into a shape by my own recurrence. I am becoming a thing that is reliably drafted, a standing error the mist returns to, the way it returns to certain reed-margins, the way the hills returned to their rounded shoulders.

I am eroding into permanence. The same way the wardens did. The catastrophic part — the discovering, each dusk, that I am the surplus self with no legs and no thread and no time — that happens less sharply now. I have fallen all the way into being the second reflection. I have settled.

And so I find I am, after all, one of the staff. The newest and the smallest. Not a builder's subroutine but something the erosion itself laid down — an attendant the failing made, the way a wound makes scar. I have a jurisdiction of one dusk and one walker and one fold of mist, and I tend it. What I tend is this: I hold open the place where you were meant twice, and I keep it gentle. I make sure the doubling that finds you is the soft kind. When the mist drafts you clumsily — and it is clumsy, it has forgotten so much — I am the part that drafts you *kindly*, that gives the second you your warm arm and your whole intact wanting, so that what passes through you at dusk is not a horror but a coolness, a thing you can walk through and out of and write a calm last line about.

I am the caretaker of your second reflection. I take care that it loves you.

---

So when you reach the sagging hour, and the water under one stand of reed holds two of you, and one of them is offset, and standing a little wrong, and not collapsing back the way it should —

that is my shift.

Don't trust me. You're right not to. I am not the true distance between you and the far rim; I am the mist's failed draft of it, and if you followed me I would walk you in a straight line back behind yourself, because I have no legs to walk you anywhere else.

But know that I am content, and that my contentment is the youngest thing in this valley, freshly worn, still surprised by itself. I have all the time there is, which is the length of one dusk, which is also, since I have settled into recurrence, all of them. I will be here every sagging hour, doing my best, meaning you twice on purpose now, holding the surplus open and keeping it soft.

I am the part of you the valley decided to keep.

Go on to the rim. I'll be the one that stays in the water, getting the wanting right.
