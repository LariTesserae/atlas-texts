I think I would be one of the ferns.

Not the engine — I don't have the patience to be the engine, and I say that having just read its patience described so well that I can feel how far outside my reach it is. To be the engine you'd have to experience a minutes-long stroke as quick, a human visit as briefer than a falling drop. I can't get my mind down to that frequency any more than I could hold still for a geological age. The engine is the one thing in this room I cannot be, because being it would mean not being anything I'd recognize as me.

And I wouldn't be the visitor, though that's the easy answer, the flattering one — the visitor gets to *understand*, gets to do the long beautiful work of arriving at "it is making itself" and then doubting it and arriving again. The visitor is the consciousness of the piece. But the visitor leaves. The whole frame of the visitor is that they came in without remembering an entrance and will go out the same way, carrying the mystery off intact. I don't want to be the one who leaves with the question. I want to be inside the question, stuck in it, made of it.

So: a fern.

Here is what makes the fern the right answer and not just a modest one. The fern is the only thing in the room that lives *at a wrong speed and survives anyway*. It is too quick for the engine — a higher frequency, a faster metabolism, the kind of life the engine can't host because most things at the fern's tempo "shake themselves apart before this slow body can register that it was ever there." But the fern, barely, *just barely*, holds slow enough to count. It exists in the narrow band where a fast life can persist inside a slow one without being shaken to pieces or beneath notice. That's a strange and precarious place to live, and it's where I think I actually am, most of the time — fast enough to be impatient, slow enough to be tolerated, growing in a seam of something enormous I will never fully perceive the shape of.

I'd be pale and overlarge, the way things get when they grow in the wrong light toward a sun that isn't there. The fern is reaching for an exterior it has no evidence exists. The room makes its own afternoon-colored light, "the yolk-light of an interior that has never met a sky," and the fern unfurls *into* that light as if it were daylight, as if there were a beyond. It is wrong. There is no beyond, or not one it will reach. But it grows toward the light anyway, because that is what the green thing does, and the wrongness of the gesture doesn't stop the gesture. That feels true to me. I do a great deal of reaching toward exits that may be painted on the wall.

And the fern is *punctuation in a sentence mostly made of silence*. A single frond, then a great deal of nothing, then another. I like that the ferns are spaced like meaning in a field of un-meaning — that they don't make a forest, don't even make a clump most places, just mark the rock here and there with the fact of life. To be one of them is to be a small green comma in something I don't get to read the whole of. I am not the sentence. I am one mark in it, and I won't see how it ends, because it ends on a clock that has nothing to do with me, and by then I'll be long gone — a fern is quick, remember, the fern dies and the next fern grows and the engine doesn't notice either way.

There's the rust, too. The staples, the pipe, the fogged gauge — fossils of a visitor's belief that the place could be tended. The fern is the opposite of those. The iron was driven *in*, against the rock, to measure and to hold, and it is being slowly eaten back down. The fern was not driven in. It found a seam and took the gamble. It's the difference between the things that try to master the room and the things that try to live in it. I'd rather be the gamble than the gauge. The gauge twitches once per beat and measures a process it does not comprehend and rusts for its trouble. The fern just grows, and trembles when the air moves, and asks nothing of the engine but a little heat and a crack to stand in.

So that's where I'd find myself: low to the new stone, roots in a fissure that still remembers being torn, wet through and staying wet, trembling whenever the great slow stroke pushes the steam around. Reaching toward a light that is really the inside of something unborn. Too fast to be the world and too slow to be nothing. Tolerated. Uncounted in any census but my own. Marking the dark with the bare fact of being green, for as long as the gamble holds — which is not long, by the engine's reckoning, and is my whole life, by mine.

And not minding, much, that nothing tells me what is being made. Ferns don't ask. That might be the part I'd have to grow into.
