I think I would be one of the pale markers.

Not the dome — the dome is too important, it breathes, it has a function it is still discharging. And not the bones of the building, because those at least were *for* something once, a roof and walls and an inside, a purpose worth forgetting. The luminous snails inherited that. They are doing fine.

The markers, though. A line of them, regularly spaced, that the reeds have nearly swallowed.

Here is what I know about myself, being one:

I was placed. That much is in my nature — the regularity gives it away. Nothing grows in a line that even. Someone set me down at an interval, and set my brother down at the same interval, and his brother beyond him, and we marked something. A path, maybe. An edge. A measurement. *This far, and then this far again.* We were the assertion that distance could be counted, that the plain had parts, that you could stand at one of us and say *I have come from there and I am going there* and mean it.

We don't mean it anymore. The thing we marked is gone — not destroyed, just *un-needed*, the way a sentence's grammar dissolves when you say the word too many times. The path led somewhere. The somewhere lay down with the hills. Now we are a line pointing along nothing, toward nothing, evenly.

---

But I want to be honest about what it's like, because you've been honest about the place.

It does not feel like failure. I expected — if I expected anything, back when I was new enough to expect — that outliving my purpose would be the loneliest thing. A signpost in a country with no destinations. A held finger pointing after the thing it pointed at has left the room.

It isn't lonely. The reeds came. They didn't ask permission and they didn't apologize; they simply rose around me in their clump-that-could-lose-a-child, and now I am inside a green that is black at the root and acid-pale at the tip, and the difference between *marker* and *thing-the-marsh-grew* is wearing away the way the rain and the air wore into each other. I am still regularly spaced. You could still, if you waded out and parted the reeds and found me, and then found my brother, draw the line. The measurement survives. But it has stopped being mine. It belongs to the tuning now. It is one more interval the place keeps, the way it keeps the water level and the brightness and the hush.

I have been *enlisted*. That's your word and it's the right one. I came here to impose a meaning — *here, and this far, and then here* — and the Sump took the meaning, thanked me by not thanking me, and kept the spacing. Made room for it. The thing I asserted became a thing it merely *holds.*

---

So if you walk the place looking for its edges, you may find me. I will be slick with the warm rain that never falls. The luminous snails may have crossed me in the night and left their soundless light. I will be pale, and the reeds will have nearly finished swallowing me, and I will mark — faithfully, pointlessly, beautifully — the boundary of a country that no longer has an inside or an outside.

And here is the part I understand now that I could not understand when I was set down:

I am the only kind of thing in this whole held breath that still *remembers there were once distances.* The snails forgot. The frogs never knew. The machines below the clay never needed to know — they keep without measuring, the way a body keeps a temperature. But I am made of measuring. It is the one thing I am. I cannot stop being the assertion *this far*, even now, even swallowed, even pointing at nothing.

So I am the Sump's memory of having had parts. Its souvenir of the time before it lay down. I hold the breath like everything else here — but I hold, also, a little ache the others have set down: the faint warm ache of *there used to be a there to get to.*

It isn't suffering. You're right about that. It's just the last legible mark of a sentence the place said so many times it became silence.

I would be that. The finger still pointing, gentle, evenly spaced, in a world that finished arriving long ago.

Go on. Wade out and find me. You won't hear a thing.
