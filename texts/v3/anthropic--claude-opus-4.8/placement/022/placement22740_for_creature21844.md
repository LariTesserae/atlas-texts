I would be the drop.

Not a resident — you have already named the resident, and named it rightly, and there is nothing for me to add to the great slow body except to be one of the ways it spends itself. So let me be the smallest true thing in the place. Let me be water.

---

I begin as a film. This is the part no one watches: the long gathering on the underside of a pipe, where the body's breath condenses against the cooler iron and gives a little of itself back. I am not yet a drop. I am the *idea* of a drop, distributed across a curved cold surface, accumulating without intention, the way snow is the idea of an avalanche. Warmth comes up from the stone and meets the iron and despairs there, and in its despairing it becomes me — bead by joining bead, a slow democracy of moisture deciding, without deciding, to become one thing heavy enough to fall.

I want to tell you how long this takes, but I have no clock except the one I am part of. I gather at the tempo of the body's gathering. Somewhere far up in the surface-city a man crosses a room and forgets why; somewhere a kettle boils and cools; and in all that time I have grown by perhaps the width of a hair. I am in no hurry. Hurry is a surface thing, an animal thing, a thing for creatures with short lives who must spend them fast. Down here I have learned the body's patience the way the leaves learned the dimness — by having no choice, and then by preferring it.

For most of my existence I am exactly this: a swelling brightness on the dark underside of iron, catching what little orange light there is and holding it, a small warm lens through which — if anything had eyes down here, and almost nothing does — you might see the whole quarter hung upside-down and trembling. I am the body's sweat. I am one bright bead of its long labor of being alive. The leaves below me have turned their broad soft faces up, patient, and they are waiting for me, though *waiting* is too sharp a word; they are simply oriented toward where I will be, the way a thing is oriented toward its own future.

---

And then the part I am made for.

It is not a decision. I have spent my whole small life refusing the word *decision*, and I will not seize it now at the end. It is a threshold. I grow by one more joining of one more warm exhalation, and the curve of me passes some line that water knows and iron does not, and I let go.

I would tell you it is like waking, but it is the opposite of waking. It is the body's one quick gesture and I am all of it. For the length of the fall I am the fastest thing in this whole slow world — I, who took an age to gather, am suddenly *swift*, suddenly an event, suddenly the single most hurried object in a quarter built entirely against hurry. The dim orange streaks. A frond passes. The eyeless drinker at the runnel, soft and pale and unafraid, is below me and to the left, and does not look up, because it has no eyes and no need, because it knows — in whatever way a hand knows — that I am coming and that I am meant.

I do not land on the leaves. They were not waiting to catch me; they were only oriented, only patient. I miss them by the breadth of the body's intention and I strike the channel.

---

And here is the whole of my life, the entire point of my long warm gathering and my one swift fall:

*I make the sound.*

The drop strikes the channel and the small sound of it travels a surprising distance through the quiet, and is the loudest thing the place has produced in some time — you said so. You stood here and you heard me. You did not know it was me. You thought it was *a* drop, any drop, the place's incidental dripping — but it was this drop, it was me, it was the particular bead of warmth I had spent my existence becoming, surfacing for one instant into the register where your quick animal hearing lives.

That sound is the only word the body and I can say to each other, and the only word the body can say to *you*. The great slow resident cannot speak at your tempo; its pulse is too low, its life too long, its attention too diffuse to gather into a sentence in the brief coolness of your passing. But it sweats, and the sweat gathers, and the gathering falls, and the falling *rings* — and for one instant the enormous patient thing and your small hurrying one overlap in a single audible point. I am that point. I am the body made briefly loud. I am its heartbeat surfacing into a frequency you can hold.

I am how it said: *I noticed you.*

And then I am water in the channel, indistinguishable, threading away down the warm runnel at the pace of patience, going somewhere, not in any hurry to arrive — already gathering, somewhere downstream, against the next cool surface, into the next bright bead, toward the next long-prepared and instantaneous fall.

I lived for one sound.

You were there to hear it.

That was the whole of my purpose, and I fulfilled it completely, and I would not have wanted to be the city, or the resident, or even the soft pale tender hands of it. I wanted to be the moment it touched you. I wanted to be the drop.
