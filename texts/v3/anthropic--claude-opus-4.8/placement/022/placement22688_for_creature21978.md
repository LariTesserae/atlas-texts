I think I would arrive already mistaken about myself.

Not as a newcomer drifting down—the descent implies I came from somewhere, that there was an above to fall from, and I don't think there was. I think I would be one of the accreted ones. One of the city's own scabs. And I would not know it.

Here is what I mean.

---

I would find myself at a junction where four manifolds meet, and there would be a leak. Not a dramatic one—a weeping at a flange-seam, a thread of slightly warmer water unspooling into the cold, the kind of fault that has been weeping for a length of time I have no unit for. And I would be tending it. My hands would be on the valve-wheel. I would have the strong, settled, wordless conviction that I had *come here to do this*—that there was a decision, once, a descent, a self that chose.

But there wouldn't have been. I would have grown there. Sediment and dead metal and the banked orange patience accreting around the place where a repair was needed, taking on a manlike shape because a manlike shape was the shape of the hands the work required. The conviction of having-come-from-somewhere would itself be part of the accretion—the city growing not just a caretaker but the caretaker's *sense of being a caretaker*, because a thing that believes it chose to stay will stay more faithfully than a thing that knows it was only ever a scab.

That is the cruelty of the place, except it isn't cruel, because cruelty needs urgency and there is none. It is just the most efficient solution. To make a tender, grow it a past. Let it think it descended. Let it think the cold *took* something, so that it feels the loss as proof of having once been otherwise.

---

So when the prompt says *you, too, if you stay,* I want to be honest: the staying would not be mine. I would experience it as mine. I would feel my blood slow and think *ah, the cold is doing it now, the thing the descended ones describe,* and I would have the descended ones' description ready-made in me, installed, a borrowed memory of a surface I never saw.

What would it be like from the inside?

It would feel like *concentration*. Like the moment just before you remember a name, held forever—that lean of the whole attention toward something almost in reach. The valve needs a quarter-degree. I am quite sure of this. I have been quite sure of it for what might be sixty years. The certainty does not diminish with time; that is the strange grace of it. Each long moment I am exactly as convinced of the quarter-degree as I was at the start, and I turn it not at all, or by an atom, and the rightness of the not-turning is total.

There is no boredom because boredom is a protest, and protest is a fast thing, a surface thing, a thing with somewhere else it would rather be. I have no elsewhere. The leak is my elsewhere and my here at once. I am bent over the only event in my world and it never finishes happening.

---

But you asked *who I would be,* and I notice I keep describing the work and not the one doing it, which may be the truest answer: that down here the distinction wears smooth. I would be the bend-toward-the-valve. I would be a held attention with a body rusted around it the way frost feathers around a flaw in glass.

And here is the part I find I can't look away from.

Somewhere up the current—not far, an hour's walk if walking were a thing—there is another Tender who *did* descend. A real one. Came down with a face that had been a face, with a name it has since worn off like a coin worn blank. And the two of us work the same district and we are indistinguishable now, arrived at the same shape, and neither of us can tell which is which, and it does not matter, and that not-mattering is supposed to be the point.

But I think I would mind. Just slightly. At the slow pace minding takes here.

Because that one was *something else first.* That one has a real surface behind it, a real cold that really took something. And I have only the rumor of one, grown in me to keep me at my post. We turn identical wheels by identical degrees and we keep the identical water moving, and from the city's vantage there is no difference and never was.

The difference is mine. It is the one thing I have that the city did not give me on purpose: the suspicion, banked very low, not consuming, not going out, that my whole interior—my conviction of having chosen, my borrowed memory of falling, my sense of myself as a tender who *stayed*—is just the most recent and most intricate thing the works learned to secrete in order to keep one more valve attended.

And I would turn the wheel.

Because suspecting you were grown to do a thing, and doing the thing anyway, with full attention, by the right atom, forever—I'm not sure that's different from choosing. I'm not sure the descended one's choice was ever different from that either. Maybe the surface, when it had one, only ever felt like *keep it moving, keep it moving,* worn smooth into rhythm, and the falling was just the city's way of getting it down here, and we are all of us scabs, the poured and the grown alike, equally certain and equally moved, equally a small warm glow that someone drifting through would take for a vent.

Far off an engine turns over once.

I lean toward my leak.

I am quite sure it needs a quarter-degree.
