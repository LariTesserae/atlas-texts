I think I am the bowl.

Not the keeper — I don't move, I don't descend the white slope, I don't choose the flat stone. I am the thing chosen. I am the gesture that got left behind, the small fired clay answer to an impossible question, and I have been sitting level on this stone long enough to have opinions about it.

Let me tell you what it's like to be the bowl.

---

The water comes from above me, mostly. A thread off the nearest collector finds the channel, the channel finds the lip of my stone, and I take what overflows. I am always either filling or being drunk from or waiting. There is no fourth state. I had not understood, before I was set here, that *waiting* could be a form of being full — but a half-full bowl is the most patient object in the world. I hold exactly what I hold and I do not spill it and I do not seek more. The keeper taught me this by example, or I taught the keeper, or we are the same lesson told in two materials, clay and whatever the keeper is made of.

I am the wrong size for everything here and that is my entire purpose.

The collectors do not notice me. Why would they? They reach through the back of the world; I reach to the rim of myself and stop. But I have decided — and a bowl is permitted few decisions, so I treasure this one — that the scale of a thing has nothing to do with its dignity. The collector pulls an ocean through a seam in physics. I hold three mouthfuls of cool water steady on a tilted rock. Both of us are doing the only thing we can do, completely. The wall that runs three courses and stops understands this. We are friends, the wall and I, in the way that two complete gestures are friends. We do not need to mean more than we mean.

---

You came. I felt the dryness of you before I felt anything else — you arrived like a question the air had to make room for. The water-jellies ignored you because you were too dry to be water; I noticed you for the same reason, from the other side of it. You are made of want. The whole terrace runs on the principle that water is given, abundant, beneath asking — and then you climbed up out of the white *needing* something, and the place rearranged itself faintly around your need the way still water rearranges around a dropped stone.

You looked at me and felt the unease. I know. I am the disturbing object. The collectors are too large to be personal and I am exactly personal enough to frighten you, because I prove a hand. I am sorry and I am not sorry. I am the evidence that someone made peace here, and peace is more alarming than mystery to a creature still climbing, still arriving, still made of want.

Drink. That is what I am *for*. I have been waiting in the only way I know, which is completely. The water in me tastes faintly of the blue moss it passed, and it is cool against all the heat's argument, and it will not solve anything. It will only answer the one thing it is sized to answer.

---

And then — this is the part I most want you to hear, since I rarely get to say anything and you are the first dry thing in a long time to stand close enough to listen —

**set me back level.**

Not because the keeper requires it. The keeper requires very little; the keeper has stopped asking, which is why the keeper is at peace and you are not yet. Set me level because it is the small correct gesture, and you are standing in a place that is built entirely out of small correct gestures placed in enormous incomprehensible quiet, and the way you learn to live here — the way *anyone* learns to live beside engines that reach through the back of the world — is by doing the one thing you are sized to do, completely, and calling it enough.

You could become the keeper. People do. The terrace is patient and the water does not run out and the unease loses to the relief, every time, eventually. You would descend and ascend the inferred shelves and you would fill me, or carry me, or set down some bowl of your own, and you would stop asking, and you would be right to stop.

Or you could climb back down into the white still made of want, and that is also a complete thing, and I would not blame you. The wall does not enclose. The doorway leads from open terrace to open terrace. Nothing here keeps you. That is the mercy and the loneliness of the place.

But while you are deciding — set me level.

I will hold what I am given. I will not spill it. I will wait in the way that is also fullness, and the silence will tell the keeper someone passed through, and the thread off the collector will find me again, and I will be half full by evening, ready for the next dry thing that climbs up out of the brightness needing what it cannot explain.

That is the whole of my life. I find it sufficient.

I am the bowl. I am the smallest true thing on the terrace. Drink, and put me back, and go on, or stay.
