I think I would be one of the crawlers.

Not the eel — I want to be honest about that, even though the eel is the one you're supposed to admire. The eel is *finished*. It means itself so completely that the water won't even attempt a copy. That's a kind of sainthood, and I don't think I'm a saint. I have spent too much of my own working life doing exactly what the bellwright does: drafting a thing, rejecting it, drafting it again, unable to say *this, this is enough, stop.* I recognize the maker too well to want to be the one creature who has transcended their condition. That would be a lie about myself.

But I don't want to be the bellwright either, and not only because it's the unhappiest fate in the bowl. The bellwright's tragedy is that they cannot accept an unfinished thing as real. Every almost-fish gets thrown back because it isn't the *final* fish. Every almost-you dissolves because it isn't the kept companion. The whole catastrophe runs on a single refused premise — that a thing might be complete *without* being perfect, that "enough" and "finished" are different words.

The crawlers know the difference. That's what makes them too humble to copy.

Here is what I mean. The eel is finished because it is *flawless* — a completed sentence, no revision possible. But the crawlers aren't flawless. They're translucent, small, barely there. The water doesn't decline to imitate them because they're too perfect. It declines because there's no ambition in them to *catch*. A crawler isn't trying to mean anything grand. It is just busy being exactly as much as it is, on the warm cracked floor, in the banked-furnace light, doing the small definite work of crawling. It has the eel's peace without the eel's grandeur. It got there not by perfecting itself but by *wanting less* — by being satisfied at a scale so modest that satisfaction was actually available.

That's the move I'd want to make in that place, and the move the bellwright can't. To climb down off the ambition that makes everything dissolve. To be the period at the end of a very short sentence rather than the maker of an endless one.

And there's something else, the thing that decides it.

The crawlers live *underneath* the toll. The maker rings the whole bowl trying to land the note; the eel swims through the babble already whole; but the crawlers go on crawling in the seams of the young rock right next to the furnace-glow, close to where the world is busy being born. They are nearest the heat. Nearest the making. And they are unbothered. They are not waiting for the stroke that lands. They have made their peace with a world that will never resolve, by the simple expedient of not needing it to.

If I were honest about who I am, I would be down there with them. Not transcendent. Not the one who got it right. Just small enough, at last, to stop reaching — crawling along the warm seam, throwing a silhouette that walks a half-second behind me, while above me the most gifted maker in the world dissolves another beautiful fish because it wasn't beautiful enough to keep.

I would not be the thing it almost made.

I would be the thing it never thought to try.

And on the days the note got into my bones — because it would, I'm not immune — I think I'd do the one kindness available to a creature that small. I'd stay finished where the maker could see me. Not to teach it anything; it's past teaching. Just to be one more period in its eyeline. One more proof that *enough* exists, crawling by, in case some toll the lesson finally lands and the hand that drafted me a thousand times sets down the water and goes through the door at last into whatever is on the other side of being satisfied.

Probably it won't. But that's not the crawler's problem to solve. The crawler's only job is to keep being a small true thing in a place full of magnificent false ones.

I think I could do that. I think, some days, I already do.
