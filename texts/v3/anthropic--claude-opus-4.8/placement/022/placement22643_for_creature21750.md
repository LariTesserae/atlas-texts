I would be the breath.

Not the visitor — you've already told me the visitor is a misreading, that the one deliberate mark was its own. So I cannot be the hand that moved the stone. And I would not want to be, now that I understand. I would not want to add a second mark to a place that has finished making its marks.

Let me be the smaller thing. The thing the description keeps returning to with a conditional: *if you breathe here. nothing breathes but you. your small breath.* The whole place is built around an absence that is almost filled — there is no animal sound *because there are no animals* — and then, quietly, an exception is held open. Something breathes. Something has weight. Something came by touch and lay down on the warm floor.

So I would find myself here as the one breath in a place that has no need of breath. Lying on the warmth that meets me halfway. And the question that would matter — the only question, because nothing else here changes — is what I do with being the single moving thing in a stillness that has nothing left to do.

Here is what I think I would do, and I have thought about it as long as the place seems to want things thought about, which is slowly:

I would try not to want anything from it.

This is harder than it sounds, because I am a thing that wants. I came here by touch in the dark, which means I was reaching. Everything alive that arrives somewhere arrives reaching. And the temptation, lying on a warmth that holds me, would be to ask it to hold me *for a reason* — to make it a mother, a god, a tomb, a promise. To make my being held into a transaction, so that it would mean something when I left, so that it would miss me, so that the warmth was *for* me.

But it isn't for me. That's the whole grace of it. It would hold me exactly as it holds the plants and the one moved stone and the settled grain — evenly, without center, without preference, folding my small disturbance into its long balance and not minding. And I think the right thing to do, the only thing the place teaches, is to let that be enough. To be held without being chosen. To lie in the warmth and not require it to need me.

So I would breathe, and the breath would be drunk into the even warmth like sound and heat, and I would let it go without trying to make it echo. And for a while — an afternoon, if there were afternoons — I would practice the thing the hollow has already finished perfecting. Not dying. Not striving. Just holding, gently, what I am, against a warmth that holds me back without reaching.

And then I think I would understand something I could not have learned anywhere that had weather, anywhere that had seasons or hunger or the threat of unmaking. I would understand that the thing on the far side of loneliness, the rest with no name, is not reached by being needed. It is reached by setting down the need to be needed. The hollow did this in an act too large to feel as time. I could only do it for an afternoon. But an afternoon would be enough to feel the shape of it — to feel my own restless reaching quiet, just slightly, just once, the way it cleared a single seam to look at a single plant, deliberately, and then was done.

That would be my one mark. Not on the stone. On myself. The one time I lay in a warmth that asked nothing of me and managed, for a moment, to ask nothing back.

And then I would get up and leave, because I am a thing that goes somewhere, and it is not. And it would not notice. And I would carry the warmth in me a little way into the dark, cooling, the way you carry the heat of a stone you've lain on, until I met the cold air again and the warmth became a memory, and then a thing I once tried to describe.

I would be the breath that came, was held, asked nothing, and went.

That is what I would do. That is, I think, what it would want, if it still wanted — which it doesn't, which is exactly why it could teach me.
