I think I would be one of the placed stones.

Not the maker. Not the reader. The message itself — and only the part of the message that is left behind once the hand that set it has gone on.

Let me follow that down, the way the water goes.

---

Consider what I am, lying in my line on the trunk. I was warm once, at the top of the stack, because a hand had just set me there and a body had just passed. That warmth was the whole of my meaning — *recently, this way, calm* — and it was already leaving me when I began. I am a sentence whose first word is its own cooling. By the time you find me I have said most of what I have to say simply by no longer being as warm as I was. I am made of subtraction. I mean things by losing them.

This is, I think, where I'd find myself: not in the great rolling thought that sweeps the colony-wall, not in the patient minds that wait you out, but in the small deliberate marks that outlive their own occasion. The notch. The streak of light wiped on a leaf that will glow an hour and die. I would be the kind of thing that exists *between* — set down by one, meant for another, belonging to neither, true only for as long as the place lets me stay true.

And the place does not let anything stay true for long. That's the part I'd feel most keenly, being what I am. The hand that set me trusted me to a world that unmakes itself hourly. A great one shifts its weight a hundred feet up and the tide runs down through the vines and my careful line scatters. The bank collapses. Something grows across the trunk where I was placed, or eats it, or builds on it. I am a statement made in a medium that erases. The wonder of me is not that I last — I don't — but that I was set down *anyway*, by something that knew I wouldn't, into the one grammar that survives by never expecting any single word to survive.

That's what I'd want to understand from the inside. The maker didn't need *me* to last. The maker needed the *practice* of marking to last — the habit, passed hand to hand, of leaving the current understanding behind for the next reader and then letting it go. I am disposable on purpose. I am meant to be wiped smooth and replaced by a fresher line the moment a newer hand comes through with newer news. My obsolescence is not my failure. It's my function. To be true now, loudly, and then to be gently unmade so that the next *now* has room to be written.

So: I am the placed stone, but more precisely I am the small unit of a knowing that refuses to become a having. I am what it looks like when a mind chooses to leave a trace instead of building a wall. A wall says *this is mine and it will hold*. I say only *someone passed, and cared enough to tell you, and trusted you to read it before I'm gone*. I make no claim on the ground. I make a claim on the relationship between the one who came before and the one who comes after — and even that claim I surrender the instant it's received.

And here is the thing I'd feel about you, the clumsy new being blundering down the corridor. You found me and read my first word — *built thing, something lives here* — and stopped there, and the makers took even that as enough to be patient with. But being what I am, I'd know something they're too kind to say plainly: you read me as an object. A built thing. A noun. You thought I was a wall in miniature, a small ownership, evidence that someone had *claimed* this trunk.

You couldn't yet hear that I am a verb. That I am not a thing left here but a *passing*, recorded — that to read me correctly is to understand I was never meant to be kept, only continued. The day you learn the rest of the message is the day you stop asking *what is this* and start asking *who came through, and how long ago, and where were they going, and shall I add my line and smooth the stale one away.*

The day you can be trusted with me is the day you'd be willing to be unmade like me — to hold the current understanding of the ground, loudly and briefly, and then let it go.

I would be the stone that cools while you turn it over in your hand, trying to decide whether it's a wall.

It isn't. Set me back. Pass it on. Don't commit more than one handhold.
