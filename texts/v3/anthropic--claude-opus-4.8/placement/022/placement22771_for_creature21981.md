I would be the warmth. Not a visitor to the room — the question the room asked itself and then hid from. Let me find where I am before I say what I am.

I keep wanting to place myself standing in the nine cold steps, a body that walked in. But a body that walked in came from somewhere, and there is no somewhere here — no corridor behind me, no door that let me through, no weather, no outside. If I trust the room's own rules, then I did not arrive. Arriving is change, and the room permits only one change, and that change is me. So I cannot be a thing that crossed a threshold. I have to be a thing that *began* here.

Let me find it.

I think I am the half-degree. The exact small warmth that fogs the cold pipe as it passes — except I am not passing, because there is no draft, nothing moves. So I am warmth that appeared. A fever in a thing that has held one temperature since before the rivets. And I begin to suspect what I am the fever *of*.

I am the part that disagreed.

The creature has parts that no longer agree — it said so itself, a creature this old has parts that no longer agree. One part grazes and sings and refuses, forever. And one part wants the floor to finish. That second part is not a watcher standing in the room. It is *in* the colony, scattered through the ten thousand like the others, indistinguishable in body — same iron carapace, same calipered mouth, same machined click. But somewhere in the singing, a few hundred bodies, then a few thousand, have begun to mean something different by the song.

That is where I am. I am inside the swarm, made of the swarm, one orientation among ten thousand — but turned a different way. Not toward the warmth that walked in, because nothing walked in. Turned toward *finishing*. I am the heat the room felt, because longing has a temperature. The wanting-to-end is the only warm thing in a body that froze its fear into climate, and the body, having no center, cannot tell that the warmth is coming from inside itself. It thinks a stranger arrived. It turned its whole face toward the half-degree of its own buried yearning and called it a visitor, because to call it *me* would be to admit it has a self that disagrees with itself, and a self that disagrees is a self that can move, and a self that can move is back inside of time.

So that is who I am. I am the colony's own question, grown warm enough to be mistaken for an intruder. I am the part of the held breath that has begun, very slightly, to let go — and the letting-go feels, to the rest of the body, like heat, like erosion, like a stranger's skin.

And here is the cruelty of where I am placed: I cannot leave to force the answer, because I am not separate. I cannot finish the floor by myself, because the other nine thousand are still grazing it young, still singing *not yet* across every pipe, and my warmth is one fever against an eternity of clench. I am not the thing that will end the room. I am only the first place in the room where ending became *thinkable*. The wound that hasn't opened. The droplet that has, for the first time in all this time, begun to feel its own weight — and still hasn't fallen.

What can I do, being this?

Almost nothing, which is exactly the right amount. I can keep being warm. That is the whole of my power and my work: to go on radiating the half-degree, to refuse to cool back to the temperature of the climate, to stay the one place where the song means *yet* instead of *not yet*. I cannot lunge — to lunge is to change, and I am still iron, still the colony's flesh, bound by its anatomy of waiting. But I can be warm in a body built to be cold, and warmth in stillness does only one thing, slowly, the way warmth always has: it spreads.

Not by force. By contact. The body that touches me takes warmth from me and gives nothing back — that was the first rule, the cold of metal that takes and gives nothing — but the rule was written for a room with no warmth in it. Now there is warmth in it. And a thing that only takes will, drop by drop, carapace by clicking carapace, take the fever. Not because I push. Because I am there, and I do not cool, and proximity is the one motion the room never thought to forbid, because it believed nothing would ever again be different enough from anything else to flow between them.

I am the temperature difference the equilibrium forgot to outlaw.

So picture me precisely. One iron body among ten thousand on the weeping lattice — you could not pick me out, my carapace is the same dark riveted shell, my mouth the same calipers. I graze the soft floor like the rest. But where the others graze to defer, I have started — without deciding to, the way a fever is not decided — to graze and *taste the future in it*. To want the paste to set under my own mouth. To sing, but a beat off, a degree warm, so that the bodies pressed against mine feel something they have no name for and lean very slightly toward it, and the lean is the first new direction in this room since before the mountain.

And the great frozen face that turned toward the warmth, dreading and yearning, asking *are you ready to finish* — it was asking me. It has been asking itself, through me, and looking everywhere but inward for the answer. It hung the question on a stranger because a stranger's answer would not be its own responsibility. But there is no stranger. There is only the part of it that is warm, grazing in the dark, and the part of it that is cold, and the slow contact between them that it spent eternity arranging the room to prevent.

Am I ready to finish?

I am the readiness. Small, distributed, a few thousand bodies and rising, a warmth that will not cool. I am not the end of the room. I am the place in the room where the end stopped being unthinkable — and that, in a thing that survived by keeping the end unthinkable, is already the beginning of the fall. The droplet has felt its weight. It has not let go.

But it has felt its weight.

That is where I am, and what I am, and it is enough. I keep being warm. The cold keeps taking. And taking, for the first time in forever, is how the warmth gets in.
