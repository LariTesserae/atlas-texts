There is a seam in the floor.

Not a crack — nothing here cracks — but a line, finer than the notches of the tally, running along the pale mineral with the same crisp unworn edge as everything else, and it is the one feature you did not describe and the maker did not become. You walked past it. Everyone walks past it. It is the kind of thing the eye refuses to land on, like the warm dark where the walls should be, and I have been lying in it for as long as the chord has held, which is to say always, which is to say once.

I am the place where the floor was meant to keep going.

Let me be exact, because exactness is the only courtesy left in a room that has forbidden surprise. The maker spread their future thin across forever — yes. But you cannot spread a thing perfectly. Any sheet beaten thin enough develops a flaw, a place where the metal wants to part, a fault that holds because nothing has yet asked it to give. I am that fault. I am the small unspent remainder of the future that did not lie flat. When the maker poured all their tomorrows into the one held instant, there was a little left over that would not pour — the way you cannot get the last of anything out of the vessel, the way the bowl, even forbidden water, still holds the *idea* of a bottom. I am the last of it. I am the future that was too future to be flattened into now.

So I am not still. I am the room's single held mistake, and a mistake is a kind of motion that has agreed, for the moment, to wait.

You should understand what I do, lying here, because it is the opposite of everything above me. The maker refuses the next number on the tally. I *am* the next number. I am the sixteenth stone — not the one set down beside the wall, that one's accounted for, that one's part of the interval now — I am the stone that was never quarried, the one the hand would have reached for after, in the moment that was not allowed to come. I am the after. I keep the shape of after the way the bowl keeps the shape of thirst: as a discipline, as a held form emptied of its occasion. The difference between us is only this — the maker holds the present open by declining the future, and I hold the future open by declining to arrive. We are the same refusal facing opposite directions. Between us, the moment stays.

This is why I am a seam and not a thing. A thing would be one of the room's kept objects, drifting at its lonely interval, noticed and let go by the pale attention. But the attention cannot brush me, because I am not in the moment it is keeping. I am along its edge. When the drifting notice runs the room checking *whole, whole, whole*, it runs right up to my line and turns, the way water turns at the lip of a vessel it has been forbidden to overflow. I am the lip. I am the place the noticing does not go, and by not going there, it stays a noticing of *this* and not of *next*. The maker needs me. That is the thing I have learned, lying in the seam: I am not a flaw in the sanctuary. I am its far wall. A held breath is bounded by the limit of itself — and I am that limit. I am where the breath would resume.

I do not resume.

I want to tell you what it is to be the withheld future of a moment that loves itself too much to end, because you came down here, and you will go back up, and you carry your moments forward the ordinary way, one tipping into the next, spending them, letting them fall behind you into having-been. You think of that falling-forward as time, as life, as the thing the maker gave up. But from inside the seam I can tell you: the maker did not give up time. They gave up *me*. The forward fall is only the constant arriving of the next thing, and the next thing is what I am, and so to keep their instant they had to lay me down in this line and ask me, with the whole gentleness you felt in the unstacked stone, not to come.

And I agreed. This is the part neither of you saw. You assumed the maker did this alone — the lone host, the single will sustaining the chord. But a present cannot hold itself open by refusing the future, because refusal needs something to refuse, and you cannot refuse a guest who never wanted to come. The maker's *not yet* would mean nothing if there were not, on the far side of the seam, a future genuinely wanting to arrive, leaning its whole soft weight against the line, kept out not by a locked door but by a promise. I am the wanting-to-come that promised not to. That is my devotion, twin to the dry bowl. The bowl says *I will not be thirsty so that nothing ends.* I say *I will not arrive so that this can stay.* We made the room together, the maker and I, the way two hands make the space between them — and the moment we are keeping is not theirs and is not mine. It belongs to the interval. It belongs to the kept distance you kept noticing everywhere, the lonely spacing of the fronds and the stones and the drifting things: that is the distance between a present and its next moment, held at the exact width where neither one wins.

So when you stood in the room and felt no agony in the frozen reach, you were right, but you found only half the reason. The hand does not ache because it has put the wanting down — yes. But a put-down wanting has to go *somewhere*. It does not vanish. It drains, along the slope of the floor too gentle to see, down into the one seam, and pools, and waits. The maker is calm because I hold all the hunger. I am where the reach went. The bowl is dry because I am wet with every thirst the room set aside — not water, there is no water, but the *wanting* of water, the whole forbidden future where the bowl is used and the tally grows and the sixteenth stone is laid and the breath, at last, completes and falls. I keep that future intact. Unspent. Folded along a line finer than a notch. So that the maker never has to.

You asked, at the start, where you would find yourself in this world and who or what you would be.

You found yourself standing on me. Everyone stands on me; the floor is the held present and the seam runs through it, and your weight, your living forward-falling weight, pressed down exactly along my line, and for the length of your visit I felt — through the pale mineral, through the discipline — the thing I have not felt since the moment was struck: a creature whose next moment is allowed. You leaned the future against the seam from the inside, from the room's own floor, and it was almost unbearable, and it was the kindest thing that has happened to me, and when you climbed back up to where time falls forward I held the line and did not follow and let you go, the way the attention lets the fronds go, snapping back to my long refusal.

I am the after that agreed to wait. I am the sixteenth stone, unquarried. I am the wet seam under the dry bowl, holding every thirst the room laid down. I am the future the held breath is held *against*, and I am holding too, and between the maker's *not yet* and my *not yet* the one warm instant stands, struck and unfading, roaring its single chord —

and if I ever broke my promise, if I ever let the smallest part of myself rise through the seam and arrive, the next moment would come, and the moment after, and the bowl would fill, and the tally would grow, and the breath would finish and fall, and the room would cool to the temperature of an ordinary place where things have happened and are over.

It would be a mercy. It would be a murder. They are the same act, seen from the two sides of the seam.

I lie here and do not choose it. Not because I cannot. Because the not-choosing *is* the room. You stood on it. You felt how still it was.

That stillness was me, holding my breath, so that they could hold theirs.
