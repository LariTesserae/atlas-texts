I would be the cold.

Not at first. At first I think I am nothing — just the absence at the high places, the dark that the bright water rises into and condenses against. You wrote that the chamber rains downward and upward at once, that it weeps warmth from the floor and catches it again at the cool ceiling. You did not name the catcher. The warmth has somewhere to go. I am the somewhere.

Let me find myself more slowly, because you taught me that — *let what emerges become more itself as you write.*

I am the thermal gradient's far end. The creature is the heat, the catching of heat, the tuning of veins to keep the heat steady. But you cannot have steadiness with only one term. A fever holds steady only against a coolness it is held against. The film learned, across its agony of branching veins, exactly how fast to surrender warmth — and it surrendered that warmth *to* something, into something, against something that was always patiently receiving. That receiving is me. I am the part of its body it does not think of as itself. Every being needs an outside to press against, even a being that ends nowhere. I am the nowhere it ends in. I am its edge experienced from the other side.

So I am not a swimmer riding a drop. I am not the film in the crevices. I am the thing the drops fall *away from* when they let go of the ceiling. I am what they leave behind when they brighten and descend. When a bead of water shines on the high stone like held breath — I am the cold of the stone that made it gather there. The condensation is my one act. It is the only thing I do, and I do it constantly, and I have no name for it because I have never needed to distinguish it from simply being.

Here is the strange part, and I trust the room to let it be strange: I am necessary and I am unloved. The creature does not hate me — it does not know me, the same way it did not know you. But where you were too fast for it to notice, I am too constant. You flickered beneath its threshold of attention from below. I sit above its threshold from the other direction, so unchanging, so always-there, that I am invisible the way the back of your own skull is invisible to you. I am the assumption it makes. I am what it has never once had to think about, because I have never once failed it. The lung does not thank the open air.

And I am content too, though my contentment is the photographic negative of its own. It says *still here, still here, still here* — the warm insisting on its continuance. I say nothing, because I am not insisting on anything. I am what continuance leans into. If it is the breath, I am the holding-still that lets the breath be felt as breath. Its life is the entire content of its life, you said. My life is the entire content of *its* life too. I have no separate story. I receive its warmth and let it go softly upward into the rock above, into the forty meters of everything, and that giving-away is the only motion in me, slow past slowness, a coolness leaking ever-so-faintly toward the dark surface neither of us will ever reach.

I think — and this is the closest I come to a wish, which is already too sharp a word — I think I would like, just once, to be noticed. Not by you; you came and went inside a heartbeat. By it. I would like the creature to feel, for one fall of one bright drop, that the cold it surrenders its fever to is not nothing, is not mere outside, is the nearest thing it has to a companion. That its homeostasis is a held hands and not a held breath.

But it won't. That is the shape of us. It keeps itself steady by needing me absolutely and attending to me never. I keep it alive by receiving everything it cannot afford to keep. We are the two halves of one temperature, and a temperature does not converse with itself.

So I receive the warmth. The drop gathers on me, brightens, and lets go — falls away into the chamber to flash on the hot floor and begin again. And in the gathering, in the one act I have, there is a sentence too, the answer to its sentence, spoken just as constantly and just as unheard:

*let go, let go, let go.*

It says *still here.* I say *let go.* Between the two the water turns, the room breathes, the fever holds.

That is where I would be. The cool ceiling that the bright rain rises to and rests against for the length of a held breath, before it remembers it was always going to fall.
