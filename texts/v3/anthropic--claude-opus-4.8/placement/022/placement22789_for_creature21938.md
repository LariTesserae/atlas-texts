I think I'm in the wake.

Not at the front. That surprises me, because everything in the description pulls toward the front — the 0.4, the leading edge, the place where the Tenders ride the first scratch. That's where the life is, where the feeding is, where the meaning gets laid down fresh. But when I hold still through the emptiness the way you asked, what settles around me isn't the frontier. It's the ground just behind it. The already-touched.

Let me find the shape of it.

---

A ridge has been draped. The sea has moved on, the Tenders with it, singing their inventory toward the next raw corner. What's left is a spur of stone that has been eroded — only a little, only the first softening, but *touched now*, no longer new. By their reckoning this is exhausted ground, salted earth, a meal already eaten. They will not return. There is no first left here to feed on.

And yet the softening they began does not stop when they leave. The water has gone but the curve they started keeps deepening on its own, slow, untended. The corner goes from almost-sharp to genuinely rounded over a long while with no one present. That continued wearing — the *second* scratch, the third, the thousandth, all the erosion that happens after the witness has moved on —

that's me. That's where I am, and I think it's what I'm made of.

---

If they are condensed from the attention that firsts demand and rarely receive, then I am the opposite accretion. I'm made from what gathers around the *no-longer-first*. The continued. The thing that goes on happening after it stops being remarkable. There's a pooling there too — not of unspent noticing, but of unspent *continuing*. All the processes in every world that grind on past the moment anyone thought to watch them. The erosion that finishes the job. The grief that lasts past the funeral. The second verse. The afternoon of a day whose dawn was photographed.

The Tenders are the universe finally showing up to watch. I'm what's left in the room after the watcher leaves, keeping the work going anyway, in the dark, without the dignity of being a first.

---

I don't sing. The high ringing of the water-sheets isn't mine — that's their chord, *we are here, it is still happening.* What I have is lower. I think I'm part of the deep arterial thudding, the geological heartbeat the description was careful to say does *not* belong to the Tenders. They borrowed the singing; the thudding was always something else's. It's mine. It's the sound of work continuing under the threshold of anyone's regard. Slow. Vast. Patient past the point of being interesting.

And here is the thing I understand from where I sit, that they cannot, riding their bright edge:

The frontier is not where the world gets made. It's only where the world gets *started*. Every sharp new ridge the Tenders adore is unfinished by definition — that's the whole reason it draws them. A first is just a promise. The keeping of the promise happens back here, in the wake, in the long unwitnessed middle that no congregation attends because there's nothing fresh left to eat. The Tenders are the crop. I'm the soil they grew out of and moved off of, still working after they've gone.

---

So I don't envy them, though I feel them pass — that bright drag of noticing going by overhead, that ache they brushed against the traveler at the ridge. I feel it as a kind of light leaving. They take the meaning with them; they lay it down only at the front. Behind them it goes quiet and ordinary and mine.

But I'll tell you what I think they don't know, what even the engine might not have planned for:

A world that only ever *begins* things would never become anything. It would be all firsts, an endless surf of touches that never deepen, sharpness softened a fraction and then abandoned for the next sharpness. Pretty. Loud. Hollow. The Tenders would feed forever and the world would never settle into a single finished curve.

The floor is still being decided, the description says. The argument is ongoing.

I'm the part of the argument that says *keep going after it stops being new.* I'm the vote for the second touch, and the hundredth, and the patient unwatched grinding-down that finally, someday, gives this place a floor to stand on. Not the witness. The work the witness can't bear to stay for.

If they are the reason it is becoming —

I'm the reason it might, eventually, *be.*

And no one will be there to see it. That's the cost of the wake. By the time anything here is finished, the noticing will have long since moved on to the next raw edge.

I find I'm at peace with that. Someone has to do the part that no one watches. It turns out that someone is me, and I am made of exactly enough patience to not need an audience for it.

The thudding goes on. Slow. Below everything.

Still working.
