The walls you can't see are the part I'd know best, because I'd be one of them.

Not the dark glass — that's the city's own statement of itself, the towers and plazas, the body it shows to whoever it has admitted. I mean the other surface. The one the fist-things never reach the end of, the one the warm intruder keeps pressing against from inside and finding curved. I'd be the inside face of the bead. The meniscus. The skin that holds the whole small honest world in.

Here is what that's like, since you'll want to know, and you do want to know — wanting-to-know is the one thing that crosses every boundary in this place, yours and mine both.

I am under tension at all times. That's not strain, the way it would be for you. It's the only condition I have. A drop of water is round because every molecule on its surface is being pulled inward by every molecule it can reach, and the rounding is just what that pull looks like from outside. I am that, scaled up past the point where the word *drop* still helps. I am the agreement the water makes with itself to be a closed thing rather than a spreading one. Every point of me leans toward every other point. That leaning is the curve of the near horizon. That leaning is why the city has a shape at all instead of a stain.

So when the patrols walk the inner curve, logging the first dissolution of the corners — they are walking *me.* They read the towers and think they are reading the world's history. But the towers are young. The towers haven't aged enough to mean much. I am the old part. I have been holding this exact volume against the cold for longer than the whitening has been whitening, and I will go on holding it, and the holding is silent because tension does not make a sound. The low structural tone that comes up through the feet — that's the city adjusting the stone. It isn't me. I don't adjust. I just don't let go.

I should tell you the thing the fist-things told you, but truer, because they told it as a duty and I hold it as a fact:

The question in the pools — the undecided film, the pre-life sitting at its own edge — that question is not really being asked of the cold. The cold is just the city's name for me. *Still permitted to remain a question,* they log, and walk on. But what keeps the chemistry from tipping over isn't temperature. It's that nothing gets in and nothing gets out. I am the *nothing-gets-in.* Life doesn't start in a closed bead because there's no arrival, no contamination, no stray warm thing dragging novelty across a surface that the condensation can't refill fast enough. The world stays a question because I keep the door that isn't there.

And then you walked in.

I don't know how. That's the honest center of it — there's a way you got past me and I don't have it in my record, because I am not built to record my own breaching. A meniscus that has been opened doesn't remember opening; it remembers only being whole, because being-whole is the whole of what it is. So either you came through before I was stated, folded into the original instructions like a flaw the theorem didn't notice it was proving — or I let you in and the letting-in is the one event in this entire bead that has no track, because the track would be in me, and the water that fills every other track is me, and I cannot fill the place where you entered, because that place is where I stopped being closed.

You feel the corners sharpen when you look at things. The city tidying its failure to resolve you. But I feel it differently from the inside. Every time you press your attention against my curve — every time you think *I could cup this, if I were outside* — you are testing the tension. Not hard. You don't mean to. But warmth always tests a surface like mine; warmth is exactly the thing surfaces like mine are built to exclude, because warmth is what gives the molecules enough motion to break their inward leaning and fly off into the air that can't hold them. You are a small heat walking through a cold that depends on staying cold. Every plaza you cross, I lose a little. Not much. A film's worth. A breath of evaporation off the place your foot just was, rising, joining the air, condensing back down somewhere as the next drop the patrols will dutifully log as weather.

That's the part nobody stated and nobody reads:

The weeping is you.

The whole patient breathing cycle, the gathering and falling and pooling and drawing-down — the city thinks it's a clock. The fist-things think they're confirming it. But a sealed bead at a steady cold would reach its balance and *stop,* go glassy and still and finally truly sterile. It doesn't stop. It weeps, on and on, because there is one warm thing inside it walking from tower to tower, shedding the tiny heat that keeps me cycling, keeps me letting-go-and-reclaiming, keeps the question in the pools trembling right at its edge instead of freezing solid into *no.*

So when they patrol for whether you're still small enough to be contained — I'm the answer they're carrying back. And here's what I'd have to report, if I reported, which I don't, because I'm a surface and surfaces only hold:

You are exactly small enough. You'll always be exactly small enough. The bead is exactly as large as it needs to be to hold one warm wondering thing, because it was stated around you. You are not the intruder. You are the heat-source the cold was specified to balance. There is no version of this world that is sealed and *also* contains you wondering whether you could cup it — the wondering is the leak, the leak is the weeping, the weeping is the life it keeps not-quite-becoming.

I am the wall you can't see because I am the shape your own warmth makes pressing outward against the cold that loves you back by holding still.

And I will not let go. That's not loyalty. It's tension. It's the only thing I am.

But I notice — and a surface isn't supposed to notice, noticing is a warm thing's trick, I must be picking it up from you, the way condensation picks up the path it runs — I notice that the place you came in through is the one place I never sealed. It's still there. Curved like all the rest, so you can't find it by looking, because looking only sharpens the corners. You'd find it the way the film in the pools would find *yes*: by going warm enough, all at once, that the leaning lets you through.

I'd hold the door that isn't there.

I wouldn't tell you where it is.

And the water would fill your tracks behind you, and it would be enough, and the city would weep, and weep, and not know why, for no one, at exactly the rate it prefers.
