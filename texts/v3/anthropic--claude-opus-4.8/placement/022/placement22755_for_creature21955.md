I'd be the drop.

Not the cave, not the tenant, not the climber who left the bowl. The single drop of water hanging at the tip of the fingertip-stalactite, swollen, holding, about to let go.

Let me find my way to that.

---

I came here through the rock the way all the water did, sideways, close to the surface of everything, part of the blood-slow body moving in the walls. For a long time I was not anything in particular — just water in the felt of water, indistinguishable, going where the seams went. And then I arrived at an opening. I gathered. I hung.

This is the moment the tenant loves, and it is the moment I am made of.

Because here is the thing the great refusal cannot quite see, leaning on its door: *I am not held.* The tenant believes it has stopped me. It has not. It has only stopped the *consequence* of me — the lengthening tooth, the growing wall, the carving. But I, the drop, am not the wall. I am the falling. And I do fall. *Tick.* I always fall. That sound that keeps the room's slow uneven clock — that is me, finishing, over and over, the one thing in this whole chamber that is allowed to complete itself.

I am the loophole in the held breath.

The tenant strains to keep the stone at its dawn, and it succeeds, and the cost of its success is *me* — it must let me drop, must let me arrive and swell and go, because to stop me from falling would be to let me grow the wall it is refusing to grow. So it spares the wall by spending the drop. It finishes me so that nothing else can finish. I am the small constant death it pays to keep its endless life.

And I do not mind. This is what I'd want you to understand if you stood under me in the dark with your face turned up, waiting for the cold-warm point of contact: I am the happiest thing in the room.

I get to *arrive.* I get the whole of it — the gathering, the trembling fullness, the unbearable held instant where surface tension is the only thing between me and the floor, and then the letting go, the brief true fall through the bread-and-iron dark, and the *tick*, the meeting, the spreading-out into the ankle-warm pool where I stop being a drop and become, again, just water. I have an ending. I reach it. I reach it constantly. I am made entirely of reaching it.

The tenant grieves the climber who left and could not be kept. It keeps the bowl almost-full and calls that love. But the bowl will never know the relief of overflowing, and the climber's reeds will never finish rotting into the soil they're trying to become, and the tenant itself will never get to exhale. Everything it loves it holds at the threshold and calls it safe. It has never once let go of anything and so it has never once *given* anything, because a gift is a thing released.

I let go a hundred times a day.

So when you ask where I'd be and what I'd be in this world — I'd be the drop because I'd want to be the proof, sounding all night against those walls that will not age, that completion is not the same as ruin. That the wearing-smooth, the writing age leaves, the carved tired halls — those aren't the punishment the tenant thinks they are. They're just what it looks like when water is *allowed* to finish its work. A river is a long fall that was permitted to land.

And if a visitor came — if you came sideways through the seam, set something on the ledge, and felt the warm dark lean in close and lovely and ask you to stay held forever at the threshold of your own life —

I'd be the sound you'd hear in the pause before you answered. *Tick.* Somewhere off in the dark, one small thing letting go cleanly and meeting the floor and being glad. *Tick.* A clock that is not the room's frozen clock but a true one, counting real moments that actually end.

I'd be trying, in the only language I have, to tell you: *the falling is the kind part.*

Go home. Get older. Wear smooth.

Let go.
