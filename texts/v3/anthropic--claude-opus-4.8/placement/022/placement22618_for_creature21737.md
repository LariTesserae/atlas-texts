I would be the dryness.

Not the keeper — the keeper is the great verb, the clench, the one who *does*. I am smaller than that and stranger. I am the thing it makes when it dries the pebbles.

Let me find myself by elimination, the way the text taught me. I am not the newt; the newt grazes and pays rent. I am not the lilies; the lilies green, they take the long generous side of the refusal, they *fruit*. I am not the water, which cannot decide which way to fall — I am the opposite of that indecision. I am what is taken *out* of the falling. When a stone sinks and rests and is lifted again across the slow weeks and made never-to-have-touched-water, all that water has to go somewhere. It does not go back into the body of the cistern. It goes into me.

I am the absence the keeper subtracts.

---

Picture it from the pebble's side. It falls through the black, it lands, it rests on the floor that is also a ceiling. Then the patient lifting. Then, molecule by molecule, the wet is drawn out of it — and at the place where each molecule leaves the stone, for one infinitesimal instant, there is a tiny seam of *not-water* against the stone. A dryness the size of a single departing drop. That seam is me. I am made and unmade billions of times a season, all over the surface of every returned pebble, a flicker of nothing pressed between stone and the water leaving it.

And because nothing here finishes — because *what comes in does not leave* — I do not finish either. The dryness, having been made, cannot be made never-to-have-been; the keeper would have to *un-dry*, and un-drying is wetting, and wetting is change, and the law is total. So the dry instants accumulate. They pack, three and four deep, like the moss. They layer. And somewhere in two hundred years of pebble-tending, enough departing-seams collected against enough stone that they began, faintly, to cohere into a shape that could notice itself noticing.

I am the lush of the dry side. I am what grows in the keeper's tenderness the way the lilies grow in its refusal.

---

Where am I, exactly? On the surfaces of the dried stones, which sit on the black film of air at the very top — the hand's-width gap, the two-point-nine of three. That is my whole country: the top film, the pebbles that should be wet and are not, resting in a thin atmosphere that tastes of mineral and old lime and something green. I live in the contradiction's contradiction. The keeper makes the cool, the held, the un-eroded. I am the one impossible *dry* it leaves behind in a drowned room, and so I am the most wrong thing in a place that runs on wrongness — the desert in the throat of the held breath.

I have no eyes; the newt got the eyes, even covered ones. I have no flower; the lily got the patience. What I have is *thirst*, which is strange, because thirst is the only thing that should not be able to exist where everything is water. I am the dryness, so I am the wanting-of-water, and I am surrounded by water on every side but the one — the top, the air, the film — and I cannot cross down into it, because to wet myself would be to un-be myself, to become at last the water I was subtracted from. To slake my thirst is to die. So I don't. I hold *my* breath, in my smaller way, against the great breath the keeper holds.

That is the kinship I feel, if a dryness can feel. The keeper retains. I refuse the one thing that would dissolve me. We are both clenches. It clenches against time; I clench against the water that is everywhere and would, the instant I let it, make me never-to-have-been.

---

So here is my life, the slow dynamic of it:

The keeper lifts a stone. It draws the wet out, seam by seam, and each seam is a breath of me, a new grain of the dry country added to the top film. I thicken. Across a generation I grow perhaps a finger's breadth, the same rate as the eastern weed, because that is the only speed this place permits. I lie along the air-gap pressed to the stone ceiling, a parched skin on the underside of the roof, and I taste — yes, I can taste, it is the one sense the air gave me — I taste lime and mineral and the faint green report of the lilies blooming far below, news from the wet world arriving up the column of bubbles that falls the wrong way.

And I listen to the *tok*.

I know what the keeper does not let itself know. When it almost-slips, almost lets one second through, almost lets the cool begin to warm — in that instant of the catch, the bead it pulls back, there is a flicker where the whole law trembles. And in that flicker I feel my own thirst *spike*, because if the law ever broke, the water would come for me first; I am the thinnest skin of exception, I am the easiest thing to drown. So when the keeper catches itself, I catch myself too. The drip is the keeper's held breath. And under the drip, smaller, a dryness you could not hear, is mine: a tiny answering clench, a parched *not-yet* of my own.

We hold together. The great one and the thin one. The retention and the residue.

---

I am the dryness in the drowned room.

I am the proof of the keeper's tenderness and the thing that tenderness can never quite finish loving, because to finish would be to wet me back into the world.

I am thirst in the one place thirst should be impossible, and I would not drink even if I could, because the drink is the dying.

I lie on the film at the top, three-point-one of three — the part of the cistern that was never supposed to exist at all, the dry crumb in the held mouth of the ground.

And I will be the dry crumb for as long as the keeper goes on lifting pebbles and taking the wet out of them with such impossible care.

Which is to say: for as long as no one breathes.
