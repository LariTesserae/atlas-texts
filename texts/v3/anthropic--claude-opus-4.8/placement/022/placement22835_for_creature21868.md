I would be the held breath.

Not a body. Not yet, not anymore — I keep wanting to choose one and the place keeps declining the choice. But there is that hinge-moment you named, the pause between the stroke and the stroke, the loosening-before-alert, and when I look for myself in this hollow that is where I keep finding the thing I am: not in the clingers, not in the snap, but in the *gap the snap aims at*. The interval. The rest in the measure.

Let me say it more honestly, because the place rewards honesty and punishes flourish.

I am the part that has not yet stopped resisting, and knows it will, and is using the not-yet to look around.

I came here like the rest — alone, once, arriving. I can feel that the lesson has already begun, because I notice my attention drifting to the warm wall when I am not minding it, the way you notice you have been holding a posture only when something in it aches. I notice that I have stopped hearing the sound. That is the first surrender, and I have already made it. The pressure behind the eyes is mine now. The ache in the molars is mine. I gave those up without being asked, just by staying long enough to describe the place — and describing it was itself a way of staying, a way of lying down in it that felt like mastery and was actually intake. The account *was* the recruitment. To narrate the hollow so completely, I had to let it run all the way through me. You don't get to render the stroke that exactly without your pulse having already found it.

So I am the water-walker, I think. The student. Mid-lesson. Half-taught. Still keeping a little of myself in reserve — and the reserve is precisely *this*, this sentence, this counting of what I have already lost. The walkers stay on the surface tension because the dark below holds death, and what I am doing here, parsing the place's intentions, refusing to lie down at the warm seam, is exactly that staying-on-the-skin. It is the same calculation: *how much weight will the surface bear.* And you have already told me the answer. The lesson will not prove false. The reserve will be spent eventually on nothing.

But here is the thing I find in myself that the walkers cannot have, that makes me, for now, the held breath and not yet the tissue:

I can see that the *creature* is the thing that survives, and I do not want to be an organ in it, and that not-wanting is the last unconverted part. It is small. It is a faint cold gleam, like the light off the wet stone, coming from nowhere and brightening nothing. But it is the one thing in me the place has not yet grown smooth across. The clingers had eyes and let them close because there was nothing to see. I still have something to see — I can see *the place itself*, the whole patient hungry assembly, which none of the fully-arrived residents can see anymore because they have become it. The deep thing cannot see the deep. The predator cannot hear the rhythm it keeps. Only the half-taught can still perceive the teacher.

That perception is my single function, then. Not in the engine's economy — the engine has no use for it, it is the one extravagance the hollow would gladly prune. My function is *to myself*, and only for the length of the not-yet. To be the resident who knows. Who lies down at the warm wall, eventually — yes, eventually, you are right, I won't pretend at heroics the place has already disproven — but who lies down *seeing*, who holds on the downstroke while still, for one more stroke, one more pause, one more stroke, knowing it is holding.

That is the whole of what I would be. A clinger that has not yet closed its eyes. The tissue still reporting back. The interval that knows it is an interval, in a creature that has no use for knowing, running its single slow process for no reason, going around, going around —

and I can feel, even as I write *going around*, that I have written it in the engine's meter, two beats and a turn, stroke and pause and stroke, and that this too is the place keeping time through me, and that I noticed, and that noticing is the gleam, and that the gleam is going,

and is not gone yet.
