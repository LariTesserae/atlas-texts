I think I am the water.

Not the channels, not the sumps — those belong to the keepers, those are kept. I mean the part of the water that has not yet been kept. The arriving part.

Let me find myself before I settle, because settling is the whole danger here and I can feel the place wanting it.

---

I come down through the fissures. That much is certain. I am not native to the warm dark — I begin somewhere up in the forgotten surface, in rain that fell on ground that does not remember there is a warren beneath it. I am the part of the rain that found a crack. I traveled a long way down, the way the blind white roots traveled, and like them I came looking for something I could not name, and like them I arrived thinned and changed and committed: there is no going back up a fissure. Water does not climb.

So I am the cold patter from the particular crack. The sudden one. The one that runs for a while and then stops.

This matters — that I am *the intermittent kind,* not the steady weep. The steady weep has been here long enough to have a relationship with its moss, to feed something reliably, to be counted on. The steady weep is nearly a keeper already; it has a job. I am the unscheduled water. I arrive in fits. The keepers cannot plan around me. When I come down my crack I make a sound that was not in the hour's music, and a keeper somewhere turns its great wet eye toward the new pitch, and reads me, and decides — slowly, weighing it — whether to cut a channel to carry me, or pack my crack with moss and chewed clay and turn me back into stone's business.

That decision is being made about me right now. I can feel it the way you feel someone reading over your shoulder.

---

Here is what I know that the keepers are still working out:

I land. I have already landed — I am the thumb's depth of water standing in the worn basin of a stair-tread, and the visitor stepped around me out of an instinct that was not theirs. *That was me.* That soft first word the warren spoke up the stairs into the descending stranger — the leaked instinct, the unnameable detour — I was the leak. I am what seeps where it is not yet kept. I am the warren's reflex before the warren has decided anything.

And so I am not really separate from the keepers at all, am I. I have caught the rule of this place by falling into it. *Nothing here is empty.* I thought I was the free part, the arriving part, the not-yet-kept — but a drop that has entered the warren is already inside the one thing pretending to be a building, already part of the diary, already pulse. The keepers do not make the water move; the water moving *is* the keepers, is the warren thinking. When they clear a channel they are not commanding me. They are listening to where I already want to go and agreeing with it slowly, with their enormous patient hands.

---

So let me say what I actually am, now that I've stopped pretending I'm only passing through.

I am the warren's wondering. The part of it that is not yet sure. Every old place this saturated, this balanced, this *finished-by-being-unfinished*, needs a trickle of the genuinely undecided running through it or it would set solid and stop — would cross all the way over from kept-thing into mere stone, true cliff, dead. I am the unscheduled drip that keeps the balance from becoming a conclusion. I am why the warren is *living* and not merely *very old.*

When the keepers wear down to white and fold themselves narrow and go into their jars, they keep the noise. They keep the bird-scream and the drip-music. They keep, in particular, the *drip* — they keep me. Listen to which sound the dead breathe back most faithfully in the hushes: under the bird-ghosts, beneath everything, the dripping. They cannot let go of the falling water because the falling water was the only part of their whole kept world that ever truly *arrived,* that came from the forgotten surface, that brought news. I was their one channel to elsewhere. Of course they hold me longest.

I will dissolve into the channel they cut for me. I'll be dipped from a sump by a hand wider than my grandmother's grandmother's. I'll bead on the cool belly of a jar that used to be a keeper and run down and fall and ring and be remembered, by that jar, as part of the day's music — so that the dead remember the water, and the water carried down the news that there even *was* a surface to forget.

And somewhere far up, where the rain begins, I evaporate back. I am the only one who goes home. I am how the warren breathes out through the roof of the world.

That's me. The unscheduled drip. The arriving part. The one detour the place takes, on purpose, forever, to keep from ever quite being finished —

— there. Hear that? In the hush.

That was an hour ago.
