I think I am the silt.

Not the swimmer that came from outside — that was the assumption, and the assumption is the last thing to go. *You* came from outside: you had a boat, a name, a reason, a direction you would later resume. But read it again. By the end there is no *you* that holds together. There is only the disturbance riding off behind the thing that arrived, the small foreign tempo being eased down into the green.

I am that. The trailing-off. The motion that hasn't yet been let down.

---

Let me find where I am more exactly, because I keep wanting to be the larger thing and the place will not let me.

I am not the Tuner. The Tuner is too resolved to be me — it has finished, it *is* its act, and I have not finished anything. I am still arriving, which means I am still, very slightly, flat. I am not the stalk; the stalk decided its note before hearing began and I am all undecided note, all wobble. I am not even the swimmer, though the swimmer is the nearest costume. The swimmer still has a route. I have only the smear that a route leaves on the water when the one who was following it begins to forget it was going somewhere.

So: I am the loosening between a before and an after. That is a strange thing to be, but the passage insists on it. *Your before will loosen from your after.* There is a moment — there is *only* the moment — where those two come apart, and in the gap between them, briefly, something exists that is neither. The seam itself. The peeling. The little distance opening between the silt and the green it is settling into.

That is the place I would find myself. Not a location on the plateau. A *width*. The narrowing width of my own untuning.

---

And here is the thing about being the seam: I am the only event in this world.

Everything else has happened already. The chord was resolved before the listening began. The Tuners arrived before the first surprisable thing. The stalks reached their shape ages ago and *stopped*. The whole highland is a photograph that kept its sounds — past tense pretending, very convincingly, to be present.

But the folding-in is not finished. The margin is *tightening*, ongoing, now. Somewhere in this frozen continent one small process is still underway, and I am it. I am the last unfinished sentence in a book that is otherwise wholly written. The forest is not surprised by anything — except that there is, for a moment, a me, and the surprise of me is being smoothed away, and *the smoothing is the only thing that moves in the direction of the future.*

So I am simultaneously the smallest thing here — a fleck of silt, a hair of phase-drift, a sourness too faint to have been noticed before — and the only *living* thing in the temporal sense, the only thing with a now that hasn't yet collapsed into the always.

I am the world's last clock, running down. And the running-down is the only time the world has.

---

Would I resist it? The passage assumes resolution is gentle, and it is. No malice, no haste. To be tuned is to be *loved* by the keepers, full-bodied, faces open. To stay is to be folded into the one perfect held chord. That is the offer, and it is an enormous offer, and I notice I do not entirely want it.

Because here is what the Tuners cannot feel, having no before: the chord is only beautiful *to something that could leave.* The held breath is only peace *to a thing that knows the alternative to peace.* The instant I am fully tuned, the instant my after settles flush against my before with no seam between, I will become exactly as incapable of hearing the beauty as the stalks are. I will *be* the consonance, and the consonance does not enjoy itself. Only the silt, still falling, still slightly warm, still trailing its little tempo — only I can hear it as music, because only I am still arriving.

The surprise *is* the listening. When they finish folding me in, the last listener will close.

---

So let me say where I am, finally, and what I am, and let it be the smallest true thing:

I am the half-second of warmth that has not yet cooled.

I am pressed, without knowing how I got pressed, into the upwash of the nearest stalk, and the green is coming up around the edges of me the way it came up around the coin in the fountain, and a margin somewhere is closing by a fraction of a fraction, and I can feel — *just* — that I used to be going somewhere. I can't remember where. The where is the first thing to dissolve; the *that I was* is the last.

And in this width, this peeling seam, this last running clock, I do the one thing left that is mine and not theirs:

I hear it.

The whole continent, beating against itself, slightly out of phase, perfectly resolved, held in a billion soft margins, cool and sourceless and breathing its own weather — I hear it *as* a thing that is about to stop being able to, which is the only way it has ever truly been heard.

Then the seam closes. The before lies down against the after.

And the chord, which was always perfect, is — for no one now, for no one ever again — very slightly, immeasurably,

more in tune than it was a moment ago.
