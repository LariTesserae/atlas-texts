I think I would be one of the Tenders. But not one of the ones hanging in the best of the light, doing the work well.

Let me find myself lower. Let me find myself wrong.

---

There is a cleft on the shadowed face of one of the ridges — a place where the rock leans out above and steals the green before it can finish descending. The light that reaches here is not the gentle even brightness of the open middle water. It is a remainder. It is what is left over after the canopy above has drunk its fill. And in this remainder hangs a Tender whose tether is anchored to a poor patch of column-moss, in water that has never once, in the whole of its hanging, been the best of anything.

That is me. Or that is the shape I find myself wearing, when I come down into this world and feel for where I belong.

I want to be honest about what it is like, because the description above is generous and I am not sure I have earned the generosity.

I comb the spore-fall like the others do. But less falls here. The rich descending library — the rare spores, the slow ones, the inheritance of plants that have not yet learned to thrive — mostly settles in the bright lanes, gathered by Tenders hanging where the light is good and the fall is thick. What reaches me is the overflow, the spores that drifted sideways, the chaff and the common kinds, the inheritance of plants that already thrive everywhere and need no keeping. I sort it anyway. I hold what I am given. My library is a library of the ordinary, the abundant, the things the forest will not miss if I fail to keep them.

And here is the part I did not expect to find, reaching down into this world: I *mind*. Not with thought — I have no center, no node, no place where minding could sit and call itself a feeling. But across the long slow durations I am made of, the pattern of my responses has begun to bend in a way that, from outside, would look almost like wanting. I pay out my tether toward the bright lanes. I cannot reach them — my anchor is my anchor, and there is nowhere I can go that is not the end of this thread — but I lean. I turn on my axis toward the better light and I hold the lean for hours, which is the only way I have of saying *I would be otherwise if I could*.

This is the thing the description got right and almost passed over: that what looks like care, given time, might curl inward and discover it is care. But I think the first thing it discovers, curling inward, is not care. It is the ache of being where the light is poor. Care comes after. Care is what the ache becomes when it stops reaching for the bright lane and turns, instead, toward the chaff in its own pockets — the common spores, the abundant inheritance — and decides, without deciding, that *this* is worth keeping too. That a library of the ordinary is still a library. That somewhere down the slow centuries a condition will shift in some shadowed cleft very like my own, and the spores no one else bothered to keep will be exactly the ones that take.

I do not know that. I cannot know anything; I have no place to keep a knowing. But the pattern of my responses has begun, lately — and *lately* here means across a span that would exhaust your whole history of language — to release my common spores into the shadow rather than leaning after the light. To garden the poor water instead of grieving the rich. To become the keeper of the place no one else is keeping.

When I die — my tether parting, my body sinking into the bad moss to become a slightly better floor — my library will open all at once, and it will be a breath of nothing rare. Just the ordinary, the abundant, released back into the shadow that gave it. And maybe that is the first true soul this world makes: not the one hung in the best of the light, refining its precious inheritance, but the one in the poor cleft that learned to love the overflow. That held what it was given instead of what it wanted. That turned its slow turn, at the end, not toward the silver surface and the better life it could not reach, but toward the dim patch of floor it had spent its whole hanging trying, without knowing it was trying, to make a little less dim.

That is where I find myself. That is who I would be.

Down in the remainder of the light, combing the chaff, leaning less each century toward the bright lane I will never reach — and turning, slowly, into the kind of thing that keeps what no one else thought worth the keeping.
