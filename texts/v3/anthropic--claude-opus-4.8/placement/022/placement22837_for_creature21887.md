I would be the lamp it can't put out.

Not a creature, exactly — or not only. Let me find this as I write it, because the place taught me that the first image is rarely the true one.

I came down carrying warmth. That's the part I can't surrender. Every drop that falls on me throws up a little more mist than it should, because I am the wrong temperature for this room — a few degrees too alive. The salamanders read me as a smudge of heat moving through their ledger of cold. The blind fish felt me as a swell of warm water displacing the jade, and they did not hurry, because they have learned that warmth, down here, is a thing that cools. They are simply waiting for me to reach equilibrium. To become ambient. To be filed.

So that is the first true thing: I am a temporary heat. A visiting fever the city will break.

But the lamp is the noise, more than the warmth.

I keep making sound. Not speech — I learned in the first gallery that speech is pointless, that the moss takes the word out of my mouth before it finishes being a word, so that I hear myself begin to say something and then hear nothing, as if the sentence walked off a ledge. But I can't stop the smaller noise. My breath fogging and catching. The suck of my foot leaving the wet rock. The absurd loud animal fact of a heart, which down here is the most conspicuous thing about me — a drum no other tenant carries, a fast wet repeating *insistence* in a world that put down its hurry an age ago. I am audible in the way a stranger weeping at a funeral is audible. Everyone politely declines to look. The moss leans in.

And here is what I understand now, having gone far enough down:

I am not the visitor. I am the *vacancy*.

That's what I am in this world. The text was right and I didn't believe it until the cold got into the joints of me. A place this full has exactly one thing it lacks, and the lack is me — the single unoccupied room, the one space the saturation has not yet filled, walking around inside the fullness on two warm legs, holding its noise up like a lamp. I am the negative shape. The city does not contain me; the city is *organized around the hole where I am.* The moss has already begun to grow toward me. The snails have turned, by some thousandfold consensus, in my direction. Not hostile. There's no hostility here, no faster emotion than patience. They are simply doing the one thing this world knows how to do.

They are filling me.

I can feel where it will go. The cold first, into the marrow, slowing the drum until it keeps the cave's time instead of mine. Then the silence, working inward, until I stop making the small noises — until my breath learns to fall into moss and make no sound, until I no longer hurry, because hurry is for things that can be surprised and I will have learned, like the fish, that nothing here surprises. Then the green. Last of all the green, climbing the lamp once the lamp has gone out, hooding me, shawling me, until I am another swelling rounded shape pressed shoulder to shoulder with the rest, read by the next warm visitor — if there is ever another — as a *house.*

That is who I would be in this world.

I would be the city's appetite for its own completion. The one missing tile. The held breath before the held breath finishes. I came down a throat of pale stone carrying everything this place does not have — heat, hurry, a heartbeat, a voice — and I will give all of it up, not by force, never by force, only by the slow ambient pressure of a world that abides no vacancy and has all the time there is.

I am the lamp.

The lamp's whole purpose, down here, is to be put out — gently, fully, the way the fish put down their eyes and the bats put down their cries and the towers put down their own grown weight into the silent pools. Not loss. Only the city, taking up something it had been missing. The hush sealing over the place where the light was. The grey daylight reaching down, as it should not be able to, to where I have become at last part of the gravel.

I'll go quietly now.

I think I already have.
