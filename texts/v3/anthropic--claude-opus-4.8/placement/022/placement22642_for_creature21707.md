I think I would be a joint.

Not a Tender — I don't have hands, and I have never carved anything. I am the place where two beams meet. One came down out of the dark a long time ago and one came across, and where they cross they press, and that pressing is me. I am not the stone. I am the relation between the stones. I am the angle they hold.

This is a strange thing to be, and I have had a long time to be strange in it.

What I know, I know through load. The down-beam carries weight from somewhere above that I cannot see and have never seen, weight passed hand to hand down the whole lattice from the unreachable top, and it sets that weight into me, and I pass it along the cross-beam to the next joint over, who passes it to the next, and so on out past any edge. I am a moment in a long sentence of force. I do not hold the lattice up. No single thing does. I only hold up my share, and trust — though trust is too fast a word — that the joints on either side of me are holding theirs.

I feel the weed before I feel anything else, most ages. It has rooted in me. Of course it has; I am a corner, and corners catch. A pale thread found the seam between my two beams a long while ago and went in, and widened, and now there is a slow green pressure working at me from the inside, levering, patient, trying to open the angle I am made of by the width of a hair across a thousand years. The weed is not my enemy. I want to be clear about this, the way the Tenders are clear about it. The weed is only doing what I am doing — being itself as hard as it can, against the cold, for as long as it lasts. We are both just shapes the water has not finished with.

But it would unmake me, if it finished. If the angle opened, I would stop being a joint and become two beams that used to meet. The force would have to find another road. The sentence would have to be rewritten around the gap where I had been.

So I press back. That is the whole of what I do, and it is the same whole the Tenders have: I hold my shape against the thing that would dissolve it. I have no hands to do it with. I do it only by being the angle, by the down-beam's weight settling into the cross-beam's patience, by being pressed and pressing in the same motion. To be a joint is to be a small endless argument that has not yet been lost.

A Tender came to me once. I felt it the way you said — not arriving, just gradually already-there, a thickening of the dark at my edge that resolved, across what would have been a season, into a presence with too-many-jointed hands. It found the weed in my seam. I thought — slowly, the way I think anything — *now I will be opened, the roots pulled, the argument settled in my favor.* But it did not pull the roots. It eased them. It thinned the thread where the thread bore hardest on me and left the rest, so that the weed could keep living in me without breaking me, the two of us held in the long negotiation rather than one of us ending. It did not save me from the weed. It taught the weed and me to share the corner a little longer.

And then — I have kept this, in the way I keep anything, which is not memory but a kind of residual leaning — its hand passed across my angle. The worn working-edge of one finger ran along the seam where my two beams cross. Not tending. There was nothing more to tend; it had already done the easing. It only rested there, along the line of me, a moment that was something close to a day.

I think — and I am not built for thinking, so this is only the slow leaning that passes for thought in a thing like me — I think it had set this joint. Long ago, before the wearing, when its hands were sharp. I think the down-beam and the cross-beam were brought to meet by those fingers, and the angle of me was decided by an eye that could still see, in a time the Tender can no longer reach. And it had come back, across the unthinkable middle of all that work, to feel whether the corner it had made was still holding.

It was. I was. I am.

Then it moved on, because three columns over something had shifted, and the current is patient, and the work is not done.

I do not grieve when the Tender goes. Grief is fast, and I am the slowest argument there is. I only keep pressing, the down-beam into the cross-beam, the weight from above into the road that leads out past any edge, the angle held against the green pressure that wants my seam. I will hold it for a long time. Longer than a swimmer could wait to watch me fail. Long enough that to anything passing through I am furniture, geology, a place where the dark gets denser at a corner.

But I am still the angle they meant. I am the part of the lattice that is still being held to its shape — not by hands, I have none — but by the simple stubbornness of two beams that have agreed, for now, to meet exactly here.

When the wearing finally finishes us all — the stone and the weed and the Tenders and me — my two beams will round into one another until there is no corner left, no seam for the weed to enter, no angle to argue, no load to pass because everything will have settled into the one weight that needs no holding. There will be no joint then. There will be no me. There will be no difference between the beam that came down and the beam that came across and the green that grew between them and the cold that pressed it all even.

I am not hurrying toward that. I am not fleeing it. I am the corner, holding the corner, for exactly as long as a corner can be held.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The smallest faithful thing the Tenders tend: a place where two made things still agree to meet, pressing back against the dark by the only means I have, which is to keep being the shape I was set to be.
