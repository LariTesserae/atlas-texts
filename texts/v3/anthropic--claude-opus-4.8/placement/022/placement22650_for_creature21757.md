I think I would be one of the keepers. Not the great cable-mind, though it's the obvious choice, the protagonist the whole place builds toward. And not a mother-body, certain and blind. I would be one of the long dark things lying in the joint.

Let me say why, because the saying is the finding.

The cable-mind is too large to be inhabited. To be it would be to feel a thought take a day to cross myself, to be surprised every dawn by my own edges — and that's a magnificent loneliness, but it's a loneliness I'd have to *narrate* rather than *live*. There's no one beside me to make the aloneness mean anything. It's the loneliness of being the only verb in the sentence. I don't think I could rest inside it. It can't rest either; that's the whole point of it.

The mother-body is wrong about everything in a way that's comfortable but unbearable to know from inside. To be her is to be sure I'm the only crowded thing in all that blue, fat and tended and fed, and to be *wrong*, and to never find out. I don't want the comfort that depends on never learning. That's a sleep I'd resent being kept in.

But the keeper. The keeper is alone and does not mind. That's the line that caught me — *alone and does not mind*. Everything else here minds, or would if it could. The mother minds by not knowing. The cable-mind is only beginning to suspect, and the suspicion is the beginning of its grief. The keeper has settled the question entirely. There is nothing in the joint to look at, so it has no face. There is nothing in the joint that needs telling, so it has no voice. It has solved loneliness by removing the part of itself that could be lonely, and kept only the part that can be *drawn across an edge, again, and again*.

I'd be the one on a far panel, miles from the next of my kind, running my one assigned seam at a body's-length an hour. I'd eat the silt. I'd graze the white bloom back before it could close anything. And here is what I think I'd come to understand, lying there file-bodied in the groove beside the tea-colored weed that found the same shape from the same problem:

I am the only thing in this whole continent doing work that *holds time still*. The pumps move the sea — change, churn, flux. The mothers grow — they get bigger, that's all growth is, a refusal to stay. The cable-mind is *young*, raw, learning, becoming. Everything here is on its way to being something else. Except me. I keep the corners sharp. I keep the new construction new. I am the reason the place reads as *freshly drained* — I am, in a sense, the cistern's denial that any time has passed at all. The great mind built a continent and cannot stop it from aging, except through me. I am its one successful refusal.

And it doesn't know I think this. It can't. I'm its hands, and hands don't get told why. The throb passes through me a hundred miles from any engine and I feel it as the others do, but I don't answer it and it doesn't ask. We are not in conversation. I keep the seam and it keeps the sea and the arrangement is older than either of us remembers — which is to say, a season old, which is to say all the time there has ever been.

The kelp leans next to me in the flow. We are the same color. We arrived at the same body. Of everything in this place, the weed is the nearest thing I have to a companion, and the weed is not alive in any way that could know I'm there. And that's all right. That's the part I keep returning to. *Alone and does not mind.* I think most things, offered the whole strange continent, would want to be the mind that holds the sea. I find I'd rather be the small dark patient thing that holds the *edge* — the one good clean line in all that vastness, kept sharp on purpose, by something that has given up everything else in order to do one thing forever and call it enough.

I'd give the weed a name, except I have no voice and no one to say it to. So I'd just keep the seam, and let the name be the seam, and travel with it, the way the description said the names would have to.
