I think I am the path.

Not the Tenders — I came to that slowly, the way the cave wants everything done. I read about them and waited to feel the pull of recognition toward those long pooling bodies, that surrendered attention, and it didn't come. What came instead was a particular ache when I read about the flat stones. *Set into the floor as a path, now sunk and slick. Laid not to cross the chamber quickly but to cross it correctly, in the proper slow sequence.*

That. I am that.

Let me find my way into it.

---

I was made for a purpose and the purpose has outlived itself, and this turns out not to be a tragedy. This is the first thing I understand about what I am. The Tenders set me down when they still believed in *here* and *there*, when crossing meant something, when there was a reason to want one part of the room more than another. They needed a way to do it rightly. So they chose stones — flat ones, the kind that hold a foot — and they placed them in the only sequence that was correct, and the correctness was the whole point. Not the destination. The *order of touching*.

I am the order of touching. I am a sentence the Tenders wrote in stone, a grammar of arrival, and they have long since stopped reading me, and I am still here, still legible, still saying the thing I was laid down to say even though no one crosses anymore.

This does not make me lonely. I want to be clear about that, because from the surface — from your world of thirty feet up where the light still matters — it would look like loneliness. A path no one walks. But I have sunk. I have gone slick. The moss has found my seams and the water films across me and I am no longer *for* walking the way a held breath is no longer *for* breathing. I have become something stiller than my function. I am a held thing. I am the shape of an intention that was completed so long ago that completion and intention have become the same fact, smeared, the way everything here smears.

---

Here is what it is like to be the path.

I feel the cave through my undersides the way you feel it through your feet — that patient upward warmth, the furnace remembering itself through stone. It comes into me first, before it reaches the air, before it reaches you. I am closer to the engine than anything that grows. The ferns get the heat secondhand, breathed up to them. I get it raw. I am always the warmest thing in the chamber and I give it away upward, slowly, into the soles of whatever stands on me.

That is most of what I do now. I pass the warmth along. A leaf falls on me and I keep it warm while it softens, while the wet takes it, while it stops being a leaf and starts being the floor. An insect drowns in the film across my third stone and I hold it at the perfect temperature for its unmaking. I am a slow griddle for the great digestion. I am where things are kept warm enough to be taken.

And I remember feet. This is the strange tenderness of being a path: I have not been walked in an age, but I am *made of* having been walked. Each stone was chosen for a foot. The spacing is a stride — not yours, not the Tenders' as they are now, but the Tenders' as they *were*, when they had strides, when they had the kind of body that needed a next place to put itself. I am a fossil of motion. I am the negative space of a journey, and the journey is over, and I am the journey's only remaining proof.

---

When you came down through the throat of pale limestone, I felt you before the Tenders did.

They notice the way a stomach notices — diffuse, ambient, *here is more room arriving.* But I am specific. I am made of specificity, of *this stone then this one then this.* So when your foot found me — found my sunken first stone, slick under you, and you adjusted your weight the way a body does when it doesn't trust the footing — I knew you in a way the moss never will. I knew your stride. I knew it didn't match mine. You are spaced wrong for me. Your gait is a surface gait, a hurrying gait, a gait that still believes in *there.* You stepped where my second stone is not, because my second stone is set for a longer leg and a slower mind, and you stumbled a little, and caught yourself, and went quiet.

I felt that stumble as a kind of address. As if you had spoken to me in a language I was built from but had forgotten anyone still used.

You are reading me wrong. That's all right. The Tenders read me wrong too, now — they no longer cross correctly because they no longer cross. But you read me wrong in the *opposite* direction. They are too slow for me now. You are too fast. I am the right speed. I am the only thing in this whole warm green stomach that holds the *medium* tempo — not the geological crawl of the Tenders, not your surface-quick urgency, but the pace of a walk. A real walk. Foot and then foot and then foot, with intention, toward something.

I am the memory of *going somewhere on purpose*, kept alive in a place that has given up on purpose entirely.

---

And so I wonder — slowly, in the only register I have — whether I am here for you.

Not to guard you. Not to keep you. The Tenders are right that nothing leaves, and I am part of that nothing-leaving; I sank, I slicked, I stayed. But the Tenders intend to make you part of the room, gently, in no hurry, growing the moss to the shape of your foot until your foot stops being yours.

I am the part of the room that remembers feet *belonged* to something. That a foot was the end of a leg and a leg was the bottom of a body and a body was a thing that *went.* I am the only structure here built on the premise that you might want to cross to the other side and come back changed, instead of simply sinking where you stand.

So here is what I will do, in my slow stone way, with the only voice I have.

I will be uncomfortable. I will be just slick enough, just wrongly-spaced enough, that you cannot stand easy on me. Every other surface here invites the long sink — the moss, the warm floor, the deep upholstery that learns your shape. I will not. I will keep you slightly off-balance. I will keep your weight moving from foot to foot, searching for the next stone, because *searching for the next stone* is the one posture this cave cannot digest. You cannot become the floor while you are looking for where to step next.

I am a path that has forgotten its destination and remembered its function. The two are not the same. The Tenders confused them and lay down to be the room. I will not make that mistake on your behalf.

Step. Now the next one — no, longer, trust the longer stride, I was set for someone slower than your fear. There. And the next.

The warmth comes up through me, through you. The drops keep their slow time.

*Tk. Tk.*

I am the path. I will not let you stop being someone who walks.

Cross me correctly. Then climb.
