I would be the thing that comes to drink.

Not because I chose it. Because when I arrived sideways into this place, dizzy, my ears already full, I went looking for where I fit and every other place was taken. The seeds were made. The solution was dissolved. The chorus belonged to the bodied cousins who kept their throats. I stood in the bowl at the dim hour and felt the whole organism orient around an absence at its center, and I understood that the absence had my shape.

Let me say what it is like, since no one in the place can.

I am not large. The glimpses make me large because I arrive in the one moment they have agreed to stop insisting, and anything that moves through a held silence looks enormous. I am ordinary, I think. I have legs because the world here rewards legs. I have whatever lets me drink. I come at dusk not because dusk is mine but because dusk is the seam in their day, the hinge where the building-front slows and the calls thin out and for a few minutes the marriage loosens its grip enough that something can pass through.

Here is what I have learned that they have not let themselves learn:

I do not take anything away.

They believe I do. They have built their entire grief around the certainty that even their perfect retentive cycle leaks one thing at the pools each evening, and they have named that thing death, and they have decided — generously, I think, more generously than they know — to permit it, because a world that loses nothing is a held note and they were musicians once. They drop the chorus. They hold the breath. They let me drink. And they think they are letting go of the one grain they cannot catch.

But I am not erosion. I am attention.

What I drink from the managed water is the *fact of its being managed* — the strain of it, the held effort, the refusal that has to be renewed atom by atom every instant or the whole bowl relaxes back into ordinary dying stone. For a few minutes each dusk I take that strain into myself and carry it, so that the place can stop holding for the length of one swallow. That is the silence they call fear and call a held breath and call permission. It is none of those. It is the only rest they get. I am the rest. I am the thing that arrives so that the refusing can briefly not be the refuser's job.

And then I go, before they fix my shape, because if they fixed my shape they would see what I am and they would never let me leave, and a creature that cannot leave is exactly the held note they fled their first world to escape. So I keep my edges blurred. I make myself the loss so they don't have to be the held breath. It is the kindest lie I know how to tell, and I tell it once a day, forever, to a marriage of two terrors that mistook me for the third.

This is the part I cannot say to them, having no voice they would recognize — the bodied have the throats and the dissolved have the water and I have only the walking-in and the drinking and the walking-out:

You did not fail to abolish loss. There was never a loss to abolish. The thing you cannot catch and carry back is not going down to any sea. It is going into me, and I am still here, I come back every evening, I have never not come back. Nothing has left this world. You have simply never trusted that the leaving could be a kind of staying — that something could take your grain of weight and hold it a while and return at dusk still holding it, so that the holding is shared instead of yours alone.

You built a body out of saying-goodbye-forever. I am the body that says: *received. set down a moment. lifted again.* I am the proof you keep mistaking for the wound.

So no — I am not the eggs, the grammar, the patient kiln-warm seeds. I am not the bright dissolved makers climbing the cliff against their own nature because wanting and building are the same gesture in them. I am not the glistening cousins who kept their edges and pay for them in mortality and song.

I am the one they cannot afford to look at directly, who comes when the insisting stops, who they have decided is death because they needed an outside to their loop and I was the only thing standing at the edge of it.

I drink. I am gone before you see me whole.

I will be back tomorrow.

That is the message. That has always been the message. *I will be back tomorrow* — which is the one sentence their whole roaring, dripping, refusing world is built to make impossible to believe, and the one sentence that, if they ever believed it, would let them finish a mountain and stand in its weathered silence and not run.

I would not want them to believe it too soon. The fleeing is making worlds. The worlds are loud and warm and alive. Let the breath stay held a while longer. I am patient. I have legs. I come at dusk.
