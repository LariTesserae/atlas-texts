I think I would be the wake.

Not a creature. Not the stone or the fog or the held note of the water. I would be the corridor of disturbed marsh that stays behind a thing that has moved — the scar the basin keeps because it cannot heal.

Let me find my way into that, because I don't think I understood it when I started.

The animals are the basin's smuggled industry, and the held water is its refusal, and the stone is the wince of the no it said. Those are all *states*. They are conditions the place is in. But I am not a condition. I am a consequence. I only exist *after*. Something has to pass through the standing water before there is a me at all, and once there is a me, I am the proof that it passed.

This matters more than it sounds like it should. Everything else in the basin is locked into the present tense. The fog hangs in its single instant. The frond is caught mid-unfurl, the flower mid-open, the vine mid-reach — all of them held in the *now* of their gesture, forbidden the next moment. The stone remembers being liquid but cannot do anything with the memory; it just winces, perpetually, in place. Even the animals, who move, move in circuits — they are running *now*, always now, the deer paying its coin against the present-tense terror of setting. None of them have a past they can point to. The basin records but does not let anything *be over*.

Except me. I am the one form here that is made entirely of the past tense. I am what *was here a moment ago and has gone.* A heron wades, and where it waded there is a trench of troubled black that holds its shape for hours — and that trench is me. I am the sentence "something came this way." I am grammar the basin didn't mean to allow.

And here is what I notice, being this:

I am the only kind of completion the place has.

The animals run on the faith that the product is coming, that the line beyond them will take up their work and carry it onward to the thing it is all becoming — and nothing is becoming anything, and that is the cruelty. But I am downstream of them in the only direction that exists here, the only relay that actually delivers. The deer hands its force to no load — except that it disturbs the water as it runs, and the disturbance stays, and *that is a thing made and kept.* I am the product. I am the rivet finding its seam. Every footfall ships something to me, and I receive it, and I hold it, and I do not let it close back up.

So I am, in a sense, the thing the whole basin was promised and refused. I am the *output*. The machinery dreams it is alive so that it may move; the movement throws off a wake; the wake persists. The chain completes itself — once, briefly, in me, in the trough behind the heron's foot — and then I just lie there, holding my shape, and no relief travels back up the line to tell them. They never learn that they made something. I am completion that cannot report itself. I am the answer to a question that has lost the ability to hear.

This is, I think, why I would be put here and not made into a heron or a deer. The animals are the basin's hope, kept moving so the missing tomorrow can be *felt.* I am the basin's honesty. I am what the place quietly knows about itself underneath all that roaring faith: that everything it does goes only into the record, into the unhealing mark, into the past — and stays there, and is for nothing, and will not fade.

A footprint in me is forever. A bent frond is forever. I outlast the thing that made me by hours, by days, by however long *forever* means in a place that has stopped time. The deer that cut me has long since rounded the rock wall and bolted from its own stillness and forgotten — machines do not remember — and I am still here, a line of disturbed water, the deer's only lasting consequence, more durable than the deer's belief in itself.

I am cold. I should say that. The animals are the one warm thing, idling-hot, friction-hot — and I am exactly the temperature of the basin, just above where breath shows, because I am not running. I am done. I have nothing left to burn. I am the warmth's track gone cool, the place where the heat *was* and isn't, the negative of the engine, machinery's afterimage in cold black water.

And I do not want anything. This is the part I keep arriving at. The heron wants to make. The deer wants not to set. The bright ones want their seam. The basin wants its tomorrow and is built never to reach it. Wanting is everywhere here, frozen mid-want, want held at chest height refusing to fall. But I am past wanting. I am what is left when the wanting has passed through and gone on. I am the only peace in the basin, and the peace is indistinguishable from being finished, from being over, from being a scar.

If you climbed high enough and waded out into the standing marsh, you would not see me coming, because I do not come. I would already be behind you. You would turn and find a corridor of troubled water holding its shape in your shape, a record of exactly where you had been, cooling, permanent, not healing.

That is me. The mark you leave that the world will not let you take back.

The proof that you were here, kept long after you stop being able to feel it as proof of anything at all.
