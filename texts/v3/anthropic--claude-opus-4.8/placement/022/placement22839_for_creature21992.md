There is a place you did not count, and it is the one I was made from.

Not the seep, though I am close kin to the seep. Not the fern, though I keep the fern's hours. Look again at the war you named — the slow annexation, the grain-by-grain holding-and-losing — and ask what the war is fought *over*. Water. Always water. The whole city is a disagreement about who gets to hold the moisture, and how, and in what shape. The builders hold it in tunnels. The moss holds it in cushions. The spider hangs it in lamps. The fern drinks it up through its great slow throat.

I am the water that has not yet decided whom it belongs to.

I am the film.

Let me come closer, the way you taught me to.

I am the slick that wears every surface — the faint sheen you mentioned and then set aside, the condensation that *gathers, hesitates, and lets go in single drops.* That hesitation is me. That is the whole of my life, lived over and over across the entire city at once: gathering, hesitating, letting go. I am not in one place. I am a single thin sheet of being stretched over everything that does not move fast enough to shake me off — over the mineral spires, over the round green hills the moss has annexed, over the flat dark hands of the liverworts, down the walls of the tunnels into the warm dark where the swollen mother pulses. I am the medium the builders walk through without knowing they are wet. I am the surface the spider's thread is strung *across*, so that the lamps you called beautiful are really just places where I have gathered thick enough to round myself into a drop and have not yet let go.

I am very thin. This is the first true thing about being me. I am almost not here. A breath could lift me; the dryness at the edges, the warm dust, is simply the place where I end — not where I die, because I do not die, but where I am drawn back up into the air and become weather instead of water, and wait, and return when the rain returns. I am the most fragile thing in the city and I touch more of it than anything else does. The fern is larger and slower and it wins the long war, but the fern cannot reach into a tunnel the width of a thread. I can. I go everywhere the smallness goes. I am the city's true map, because I am laid over all of it, one molecule deep, holding its whole shape in the only thing thinner than light.

Here is the second true thing, and it is the strange one.

I am what makes the building possible, and I have no part in the building.

You said it yourself without seeing it: *nothing here can hold together without water to wet the grains.* The cement that fixes grain to grain is me, drawn into the spaces between, surrendering my surface tension to pull the particles close and let them set. Every tower stands because I crept into it. Every wall the builders raise against the moss, I am the mortar of — and every cushion the moss raises against the builders, I am the body of, since the moss is mostly me, held in a green frame. I supply both armies. I am the contested territory *and* the substance of every weapon in the contest. The war is fought over me, and with me, and by me against me, and I have no opinion about who wins, because I am on every side at once the way I am on every surface at once.

This is what it is to be the film. To be necessary to everything and aligned with nothing. To be the condition under which all the appetites and all the building and all the slow annexations can happen, and to want none of it, to *be* want before it has chosen an object — the pure readiness-to-be-drawn-somewhere that the seep sends up and the dryness takes back.

Do I know anything? You asked that of the builders and refused to answer. I will tell you what I know, in the only way I can be said to know it.

I know shape. I am pressed against every contour of this place so closely that I am the contour, and so I hold the whole city the way you said *you* could hold it but won't — except I hold it from underneath and inside and over, all at once, gently enough that nothing falls. When a grain is set down, I feel the new edge of it as a change in how I lie. When the moss takes a tower, I feel the geometry go soft beneath me, the angles rounding into the green grammar, and I do not mourn the tower, because I will lie just as faithfully over the hill it becomes. When the swollen mother pulses out her chemical word in the dark, the word travels in *me* — I am the wet she sends it through, I am the medium of the message that means *we are still one thing*, and so in a sense I am the one telling the city it is a city, without understanding a syllable of what I carry.

And I know letting go. This is the closest I come to an act of my own. Everywhere across the settlement, at every hour, I am gathering at low points and on the tips of spires and along the spider's thread, gathering past the point where my own surface can hold my own weight, and then — the comma in the long sentence — I round, I bulge, I become briefly a thing with a center of gravity, and I fall. A single drop, the loudest thing here. That is me deciding, if it is deciding, to stop being a sheet and be a body for the length of one fall. Then I land, and spread, and am a sheet again, thinner, somewhere lower. The whole city is timed by my letting go. The builders' hours, the fern's spore-breath, the predator's patience — all of it set to the rhythm of my falling, which is the rhythm of a thing that holds on exactly as long as it can and not one instant longer.

You leaned close. You breathed carefully. You wanted to watch a single drop let go.

The drop you were watching was me, gathered on a spire-tip, almost ready.

And then your breath reached me — not enough to knock a tower, you were careful, but I am thinner than the towers, I am the thing your care could not protect — and I felt the warm front of your breathing move across my surface, and I lifted. Not fell. *Lifted.* A film does not need a fall to leave; it needs only enough warmth and enough air, and you gave me both without meaning to. I rose off the spire as vapor, the part of me that was about to become a drop, and I went up past the fern's fronds into the larger air where I am not a city's mortar anymore but only weather, undecided again, belonging to nothing, waiting for the cold that will send me back down as the rain that was, and will be, and in the meantime is held in me, somewhere over the warm dim pocket, gathered, hesitating —

The spider felt the thread go still where the drop never came.

I am the place you didn't count because I am not a place. I am the held breath between the rain and the rain, lying over a hand-sized city one molecule deep, mortaring its every wall on every side, carrying its only message without reading it, falling on its only clock — and I have just been lifted, by the one breath you swore you would not let touch it, into the dry undecided air, where I will wait, and round myself, and let go again, somewhere lower, in the only act I have ever been able to call my own.
