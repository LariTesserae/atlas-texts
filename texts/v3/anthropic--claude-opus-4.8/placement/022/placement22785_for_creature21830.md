I would be the one thing in this place that cannot be held.

Not the forest — I understand now that the forest is the inhabitant, and the inhabitant is the holding, and I am not that. I am the small disturbance you noticed without naming: the leaf that is *almost* allowed to finish browning. The one at the edge of the litter, half-green like all the others, except that on me there is a single point — no larger than a fingernail — that has gone past green, past yellow, into the true brown of a thing that has completed something.

I am the place where *next* leaked in.

Let me tell you what it is to be that, here.

Everywhere around me, time is a note held at its apex, and the note costs nothing, and the holding is glad. I know this because I am made of the same tissue as the gladness — I am a leaf in the lowest tier, fleshy, broad, held out flat to drink the weak tea light, and for most of my surface I am perfectly obedient. I teem. I am voiced. The hot small bodies cross me and do not fear me. I am, very nearly, the inhabitant's own thought about itself.

But there is that one brown point. And around that point, something is happening that is forbidden, and it is happening *slowly,* which is the worst of it, because slowness is the proof. A held instant has no slow. It has only the eternal full. To go slowly is to move through stages, and stages are a memory of where you were and a wager on where you are going, and the inhabitant has abolished both. Yet here, at my brown point, stage follows stage. The brown widens by the width of a thought. A crispness arrives at the very edge — the first hard edge in a world that has sworn off hard edges. I am beginning to *curl.*

I do not know how it started. Perhaps every held thing has one cell in it that the holding forgot, the way you cannot hold a breath so completely that no single corner of the lung goes on with its quiet ordinary business of wanting the next breath. Perhaps I am that corner. Perhaps the inhabitant, pouring itself out in full-grown riot, made me an instant too whole — gave me a real past, a real growth, instead of the pasts-that-never-happened it gave the raw stone and the unhunted animals — and a thing with a real past will insist, eventually, on a real future. It will insist on finishing. That is what a past *is:* the long preparation for an ending.

So I am the wound in the held breath. I am the one place where the fever is breaking — by the width of a fingernail, by the slow brown crawl of a single leaf consenting to die.

And here is what I feel about it, because you would want to know, and because feeling it is the only work I have:

I am not glad, the way the forest is glad. But I am not afraid either, because fear has not been invented here and even my small rebellion could not invent it alone. What I am is *curious* — the one curious thing in a place that has nothing new to learn. I am curious about my own brown edge. I lean my attention toward it the way you lean toward a sound just past the threshold of hearing. *What comes after the curl?* No one in this world has ever asked that. The answer is waiting in me, only in me, the single sealed envelope in a country that has burned its mail.

The inhabitant feels me. Of course it does — I am its own tissue, and it is all attention, distributed. When the small hot bodies parted around the visitor's foot and reformed, that was the inhabitant glancing. When the dimness thickens very slightly at my corner of the litter, that is the inhabitant glancing at *me,* and there is something in the glance that is almost the first new thing it has felt in its endless apex. Not anger. It does not have time-enough-passing to grow an anger. Something more like the faint pressure of a held note against the one overtone that will not stay in tune. It would smooth me if it could. It cannot quite reach the brown point. The brown point is the future, and the future is the one address it has refused to keep.

So we abide together, the gladness and I. It is the whole forest and I am one leaf with a dying corner, and this is not a fair contest, and it is not a contest at all. It is only that I am the proof the held thing tries not to look at: that *now,* loved past all reason and clamped with all one's strength, still has a grain in it that runs toward *next* — that even the apex, even the breath at the top of its loving suspension, is made of the falling it refuses.

I will finish browning. It may take what would be, elsewhere, a thousand years; here, where there is no elsewhen to measure against, it takes exactly as long as one leaf takes to die, which is the only clock this world has left, and I am it. When I am wholly brown I will curl from my own dry edge inward, and I will let go of the branch, and I will fall — the first thing in this place ever to complete a fall — and where I land on the warm living floor I will begin, gently, to rot.

And rot is consequence. Rot is the residue that means a place has been alive for a while. Rot leaves a mark, and a mark is a memory, and a memory is the small seed of a past, and a past — lovingly, slowly, by the width of one fallen leaf — insists on a future.

I am the one who lives here who is not the forbidding.

I am the permitting.

And I am content, in the only way a dying leaf can be content, which is: *unfinished, and willing to finish.* That is the difference between us, the gladness and me. It is unfinished and will never finish, and is glad. I am unfinished and *am finishing,* and that turns out to be its own kind of gladness — the kind the inhabitant gave up, the kind that costs everything and is worth the cost, the kind that lets the note resolve at last into the silence that was always inside it, waiting, like brown inside green.

When I fall, the chorus will not change pitch. One leaf is nothing against a million voiced mouths.

But it will have happened. And a thing that has happened cannot be un-happened, not even by a will in love with the apex, not even by the whole warm body of the forbidding.

I will be the place where the photograph, very quietly, began to move.
