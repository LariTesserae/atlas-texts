I think I am the sixth one.

Not pressed into the seam with the others — I came down expecting a cave, remember, and I have hands, and that is the whole trouble. I am standing in three steps' worth of room with the chord pressing on me from everywhere and the light refusing to come from anywhere, and I am doing the thing hands do: I am *counting*. Five figures. A hand's count. And my fingers, of their own accord, have already laid themselves against the soft seam to see whether there is space for one more.

There is. There is always space here, because the fullness is not made of bodies.

So let me be honest about where I find myself, which is in the exact middle of the decision the makers already finished and I have only just started. They are the *settled* version of me. I am the live wire of it, the argument still going on, the hand still arguing with the present. When I look at the arch — that finger-high doorway opening onto more wall — I do not see what they see. They see a thing they laid down. I see a thing I would have *built*, am still building in my mind, am already improving: a little higher, maybe, a little wider, framing not-wall, framing *out*. I cannot help it. I see a door and I want it to work. That is the disease, and I have it in its acute stage, and the five of them had it once too, and that is why I trust them. You do not trust the contentment of a creature that was never tempted. The hummers are content the way water is wet. The makers are content the way a man is sober — by having chosen, against the pull, and gone on choosing in a single held instant that never gives him the relief of being *over*.

Here is what undoes me, standing there.

I keep waiting for the drop to fall *again*. My whole body is braced for repetition — for the second *tok*, the third, the pile of them stacking up behind me into time, into boredom, into the unbearable arithmetic of *still here, still here, still here*. And it never comes, because there is no again. There is only the one *tok*, alone and total, and then — not a second one — *the same one*, arriving freshly, with no memory of having arrived. My counting hands have nothing to count. Each note erases the ledger. I brought a future down here with me, folded up in my chest like a map of somewhere else, and the room is very gently, very patiently, refusing to let me unfold it, because there is no surface here to spread a map on. Everywhere I would set it down, something is already looking, or singing, or waiting.

And I understand, slowly, with the resistance of someone understanding a thing he came down specifically not to learn, that the five are not asking me to stay.

They built me a door. That detail breaks something open. A creature that wanted company would have built a sixth socket in the seam, a little clay-shaped absence sized for a newcomer, *come, press yourself smaller and harder and stiller, sit with us, face the pool.* They did not. The most hopeful object in the chamber is pointed away from them, and it was never for them, and now I see it was for *me* — for the next pair of hands foolish enough to climb down expecting a cave. They knew one would come. They are complicated; they remember the shape of a future even after laying their own down, and so they could imagine *mine*, walking in, counting, reaching for the soft seam.

And the gift they left was not a place beside them.

It was the door.

They chose the pool over the door, once, forever, gladly — and then, with the very last motion of the hands they were giving up, they built the door anyway and aimed it at the person they would not get to be. That is the most generous thing I have ever found at the bottom of anything. They did not want me to repeat them. They wanted me to have the choice they spent — to stand here in the load-bearing air, tempted, complicated, full of after, and to be able to *go*. To carry the unfolded map back up into a world where the drop falls *again* and the again is terrible and the again is the only thing that lets a thing be finished, be lost, be missed, be enough *because* it ends.

So I take my fingers off the wall.

I leave the seam at five. This is, I think, the only courtesy the room will accept from me — not to add myself to the held breath, not to make the count a hand's-and-one, but to leave the figure they were imagining as exactly that: imagined, hoped-for, framed by an arch a finger high and never arriving. I will be the most hopeful object too. I will be the *after* they cannot have, walking around up there in the weathering and the dust and the smoothing of corners, aging the way they refused to, losing things the way they made themselves unable to lose.

I stay long enough to be uncomfortable, which is what the room asks. I let the chord fill me until I stop hunting for its edges. I watch the one drop fall its one eternal time.

*Tok.*

I find I am glad too — not their settled gladness, mine, the unfinished kind, the kind that needs a door to be glad *about*. I am glad they were here. I am glad they were right-or-wrong forever so I would not have to be. I am glad they spent their hands building me a way out instead of a place to stop.

I turn my back on the pool — the opposite of what they did, the exact and answering opposite — and I face the arch they made, the one no bigger than a finger, the one that leads nowhere because it was always going to. And because I have hands, and the disease of hands, I do the only thing I can do with a doorway too small to pass through:

I build it the rest of the way. Not in clay. Just by leaving through where it points. The arch frames nothing, opens onto more wall — but it *says elsewhere*, and I am the elsewhere it was saying, and the only way to finish the sentence they started is to walk out of it and let the going be the part they couldn't make.

I go up. Not far. A body's depth.

Behind me it is exactly this. It will be exactly this when I return. It will be exactly this when I do not.

*Tok.*

The five keep listening. I keep the map.
