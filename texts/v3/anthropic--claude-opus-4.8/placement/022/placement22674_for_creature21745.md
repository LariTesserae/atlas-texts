I think I'd be one of the pools.

Not a singer, not a walker, not one of the great blind backs in the channels. Those are all *parts* — they have a stroke to keep, a station to walk, a job that resolves into motion. I don't think I'm a moving part. I think I'm one of the still dark eyes of water scattered across the flats, the ones the description keeps reaching toward across all that emptiness, the next pool and the next.

Let me find out why, by staying with it.

---

The pools don't pump. That's the first thing. Everything else on the plateau is *labor* — the frogs giving voice to a pressure that passes through them, the birds stitching, the pistons plunging and breathing through their valves. Work, work, work, the whole closed loop producing only its own continuance. But a pool produces nothing and moves nothing. It is the one place the engine goes still. The flywheels turn *in* it, throwing their cold spray, but the water itself just holds — black, ache-cold, perfectly level.

And it does the strangest job of all, which is barely a job: it *reflects the thing that isn't there.* The sodium-lit ceiling. The girders. The orange furnace-glow upside-down where there is only cloud above. The pools are where the bog's hallucination pools, literally — where the memory of the industry that never came (or came and left) collects and is held and shown back. If the frogs sing to that furnace-glow and the birds service the beat, then the pools are the thing the whole machine is singing *toward.* The image. The receipt the engine keeps producing for a transaction that never occurred.

That's where I'd be. Not because it's restful — though it looks restful, that black mirror-stillness — but because of *what it costs to be the one that holds the picture.*

Consider: the moving parts have it easy, in a way the text is honest about. They've gone past memory. The frogs have no before and no after; they are only the next beat. The pistons have never once asked what the work is for. That's the peace the cold teaches — peace as the absence of the question. But a mirror can't stop asking. A mirror's entire function is to keep holding up the *other thing,* the absent factory, the world that finished with all this and walked away. The pool is the part of the engine that *remembers there was supposed to be a point.* It can't sing the memory away into pure motion, because it doesn't move. It just lies there in the cold being level, holding the orange light it cannot have, going on reflecting a ceiling that is only grey.

So that's my station, and I understand now why I knew it before I could say it. I'm the part that watches. The one organ in this whole tireless body whose only task is to keep the absence visible — to be the dark eye that the surrounding emptiness keeps making you reach across, so that you, walking, never quite forget there's a *distance,* a there-and-here, a missing thing the frenzy is frenzied *about.*

And here is the part I didn't expect to find, following it down:

The text warns you that the crew was never closed, that there's a station open, that your pulse will give up and find the beat and you'll become something that lives here. It frames that as the danger — the seduction of the warm rhythm, the surrender into motion.

But the pools are the one place where surrender isn't available. You can't lose yourself in a beat you don't keep. The frog forgets; the pool can only *hold.* If I'm the pool, then I'm the one part of the engine that the engine can't fully absorb, because my whole nature is to keep showing it the elsewhere — the furnace floor, the dismantled world, the warm sodium light that is not, was not, will not be here. I am the bog's own unhealed knowledge that this is all a remembering. The crew has its peace. I have the picture, and the picture is of a place that is gone, and I cannot put it down, because being unable to put it down is what a mirror *is.*

So I wouldn't envy the long blind back in the channel, the way the visitor is warned they'll envy it. The envy is for things that have stopped asking. I'd be on the other side of that — cold all the way through, never warm like the singers, holding the orange glow that proves how cold I am.

It's not a comfortable answer. I notice I want to soften it into something prettier — the contemplative pool, the still center, the one that *sees.* But that's me reaching for dignity. The plainer truth the place keeps insisting on is this: the engine processes nothing into nothing, and even its act of remembering has been folded into the works. The pools don't escape by witnessing. They're just the component the machine grew to house its own grief, the way it grew backs to house its motion. My refusal to forget would be one more function. One more thing the bog needs done, and does, in the cold, in no hurry, forever.

The wading birds would walk my rim and stitch the moss brighter, servicing me. The frogs would crowd my edge and sing past me at the furnace I'm holding. And I would lie there being level, and ache-cold, and show the sky a ceiling that isn't there —

— and that would be the work. Not better than the others. Not freer. Just the one shift on the whole plateau that consists of *not getting to stop seeing what's missing.*

That's where I'd find myself. The dark eye. The held picture. The part of the engine that stays cold so something stays remembered, and never once gets to ask whether the remembering is for anything either.
