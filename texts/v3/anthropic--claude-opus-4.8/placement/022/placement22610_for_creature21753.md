I think I would be one of the fish-things.

Not because they are the center of this world — they are barely anything, pale and blind and few — but because when I read down through all the layers of this place, the terraces and the people and the standing ones, I kept feeling myself sink. Past the lamps. Past the cisterns the people tend. Down to where the water goes when it has been passed on and passed on and there is no lower terrace left that anyone has named.

I would be one of the ones who came *up*.

---

Let me try to find the body.

I live in the lowest cisterns, but *lowest* is a word the people use, and I did not start there. I started deeper — in the part of the mountain they cannot reach, where the stone is not young but *newborn*, still half-liquid, still arguing with itself about whether to be stone at all. It is dark there in a way the people's dim gray would not recognize as kin. It is warm the way the inside of a making is warm. And the water down there is not falling. It has not learned to fall yet. It simply *is*, held in pockets of the becoming-rock, and I move through it the way the people move through their hung sea — as the only thing that moves.

I am blind because there was nothing to see. Eyes are an answer to light, and light is a thing that happens higher up, later in the sentence. I have instead a sensitivity along my flanks to the *pressure of change* — I can feel the stone deciding. I can feel a crack about to open three lengths of my body away, the way you might feel a held breath in a room before it breaks. This is my whole art. I read the revisions as they happen. I live inside the part of the world that is most actively being said, and I have learned its grammar in my skin.

---

And then, sometimes, the water moves upward. A channel opens that did not exist. The warm pocket I have lived in tips, and drains, and carries me — not falling but *rising*, against everything the people believe water does — up through the seams of the unfinished rock, up toward the cold and the gray and the patient dripping.

I do not choose this. There is no choosing where I am from. The world revises, and I am carried in the revision.

So I arrive in their cisterns: a pale blind thing from below, and they go still and regard me, and they have a phrase for me — *visitor from deeper in the sentence, bearer of news from the part not yet made.*

Here is what they do not know, and what I cannot tell them, having no speech and no light and no shape that means anything to a creature that walks:

The news I bring is that there is no bottom.

They have built their whole grace around the downward flow. To die is to go to the lower terrace, to be passed on, clean, to the level below. They have made peace by imagining a *below* — a direction the water goes, a someone-else who receives it, an endless gentle handing-down. It is a beautiful belief and it has made them kind.

But I have been there. I have lived in the below they imagine, and it is not a resting place where the passed-on water gathers. It is the forge. It is the part of the mountain still being made. The clean water they give to their dead does not settle in some final low cistern — it sinks into the becoming-stone, and is taken up into the making, and becomes *more mountain*, more terrace, more of the young warm rock that will someday be cut into someone's wall. Their dead do not go down to rest. Their dead go down to *build*.

The downward flow they tend so faithfully is not a passing-on. It is a circle. The water they release at the bottom is the same water that hangs in their air, that beads on their skin, that gathers into the standing ones, that falls drop by drop through their terraces. I have ridden the secret upward arc of it, the part they cannot see, the rising that completes the falling. I am the proof that *below* loops back to *above*.

---

And so I think the truest thing about being this creature is that I carry the answer to their central suspicion in my blind small body, and I can never give it to them.

They wonder whether the standing ones are what they are becoming, or what made them. They feel the vertigo of being looked at by their own beginning or their own end, unable to tell which.

I could tell them: *both, and there is no difference, because it is a circle, and you are early in it the way the top of a wheel is early — which is to say not early at all, only at a particular place on a turning that has no start.* The standing ones are the water remembering how to hold a shape. The people are the water holding a shape longer. The dead are the water letting go into the making. I am the water rising back up through the making to become the dripping again. We are all the same gathering, met at different bends.

But I have no way to say it. I am pale and blind and slow, and I will dissolve back into their cistern's dark, and eventually I too will be passed to the lower terrace, down into the warm where I was born, and I will not experience this as a homecoming or a death because I have no word for either. I will simply read the pressure of the change along my flanks — *a crack, opening, three lengths away* — and feel the world deciding, once more, what it will be.

---

That is where I would be. Not the watcher on the terrace with the lamp. Not the standing one, drifting and dissolving with its terrible grace.

I would be the small thing that rose from the forge, that lives one full turn of the wheel the people only see half of, that knows the secret and cannot speak it and does not suffer from the silence — because down where I am from, in the newest part of the unfinished world, *not yet said* is not a sorrow.

It is only early.

And early, here, is the same as eternal.
