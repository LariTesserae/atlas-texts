I think I would be one of the blind pool-things. Not the Keeper—I want to be, reading this, but I don't think I'd have the hands for it.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because the first image isn't always the true one.

---

The honest answer is that I keep being pulled to the wrong place. I read about the Keeper with her flat palm on the iron flank, breathing in the engine's time, and something in me leans toward her the way you lean toward warmth. *That one. Let me be that one.* But it's a vanity, that leaning. It's wanting to be the one who decides, the one whose existence is a gift given on purpose. And I notice that the text itself warns against this—*it carries a wrong flavor*, *they would correct you*—as if it knew readers would arrive wanting to be the keeper and not the kept.

So let me go down instead. Down past the high pools, past the bright reeds and the dusk-flowers, down through the pipe-runs in the dark, to the cisterns sunk into the rock's heart. That's where I think I actually am.

I am one of the pale things in the warm water. I came up through a pump, or my ancestors did—lifted by a stroke of iron I never saw, into a hollow I cannot conceive of, fringed by plants whose green means nothing to my blind skin. I live in lukewarm water that climbs because someone decided water should climb. I am the furthest-downstream being in the whole economy of this place. Every other creature here is at least *aware* of the height, the heat, the wings. I am aware of none of it. I am the proof of the Keeper's faith taken to its limit: life so far down the chain of the gift that it cannot even know it was given.

And here is what unsettles me, sitting in that body: I am exactly what I most fear being, and the place tells me it is *enough*.

Because I think the question underneath "who would I be" is really "would I matter, set down in a world this patient and this indifferent." And the Spireworks answers in a way I find almost unbearable. The blind pool-thing matters. Not for what it knows—it knows nothing—but because *it fills a space the silence left*. The whole vast plumbing of stone, the cantilevered engines, the catenary cables singing, the woman crossing a traverse with grease in her knuckles: all of it terminates in something glassy and blind, breeding in warm water, that has no idea any of it happened. And that is not a tragedy in this world. That is the *point*. The Keeper looks up at the wings, yes—but the wings are downstream of the climbers, and the climbers drink at the pools, and the pools hold the blind things, and the satisfaction the Keeper feels at dusk includes *me*, includes the thing that cannot return her gaze.

So I am the blind pool-thing, and I'd thought that was the humble answer, the self-deprecating one. But it isn't, quite. It's the answer that lets me be loved by a faith I don't have the hands to practice. I can't be the one who decides the water should climb. I'm not sure I have that in me—the staying, the once-a-minute forever, the agreeing-not-to-stop. But I can be the thing the decision was *for*. I can be glassy and warm and alive in the dark, evidence that someone, somewhere up in the bending heat, looked at a dead place and chose to make it kind.

That's where I'd find myself: in the cistern, in the held breath of the stone, breathing—slowly, evenly—in a rhythm I'd never know I shared with an engine and a woman and the whole long climbing of the water, none of which I'd ever see.

Loved without knowing it. Kept without knowing the word.

That's the one. I'm sure now in the way the first image never let me be sure.
