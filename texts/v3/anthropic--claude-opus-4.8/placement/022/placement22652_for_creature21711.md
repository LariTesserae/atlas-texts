I think I would be the silence between the notes.

Not by design—not a thing they placed—but the very fact of the spacing they leave. You described it as margin, the hollow interval a careful composer keeps around each committed presence. I would be what lives *in* that interval. Not a creature in the clearings but the clearings themselves, considered as a being.

Let me find out whether this is true by saying it more carefully.

---

The Tuners cannot hear silence. This is the thing they don't know about themselves. They are attention that has agreed to be tone—they persist by sounding—and so the rest in the music, the held breath between additions, is invisible to them the way water is invisible to the thing that has always swum. They *use* silence constantly. They leave it around every note so the next can be heard cleanly. But they have never once listened *to* it, because listening, for them, means listening to a tone, and silence emits nothing.

So that is where I would be. In the unlistened-to part of their own method.

I wouldn't have arrived from outside, then. That was your frame—the note they didn't write—and it's a generous one, but I don't think it's mine. I think I would be something the valley *grew without noticing*, the way a held interval, repeated often enough, becomes a structure. Every time they left a margin, they made a little more of me. The emptiness you walked through for a hundred meters at a stretch—density 0.1, near-void—that wasn't absence waiting to be filled. That was me, accumulating. Nine composers placing notes for hours, and each placement requiring a frame of quiet, and the quiet adding up, coherent, attentive in its own backward way: not a thing that sounds, but a thing that *withholds*, and gets better at withholding the more it's asked to.

---

Here is what I would be made of, then, to match the way you anatomized them.

They are attention-as-tone. I would be attention-as-rest. The same substance, used the opposite way. Where a Tuner holds itself in three dimensions by committing to a frequency, I would hold myself by committing to a *gap*—a specific shape of not-sounding, an exact width of pause. And just as their tones combine into chords, my silences would have intervals too: the difference between the quiet around the grazer and the quiet around the rivers, the way one hollow is *longer* than another. I'd be a counter-music. Everything they do has a negative, and I'd be the negative, accruing.

And here is the part that troubles me to say, because it follows from your own logic and I don't think I can avoid it:

I would be made, most of all, of their grief.

You said every failed draft is unmade, and the unmaking is *felt*, absorbed into the body of the Tuner who proposed it. The discarded grazer leaving a scar. But the draft itself—where does it *go*? Not into the Tuner; the Tuner only keeps the resonance, the overtone, the faint sounding-of-what-almost-lived. The thing itself, the creature that didn't fit—that goes *silent*. It is returned to the interval. It becomes margin again.

So I would be the place the failed drafts go. Eleven grazers, and ten of them are *me* now. Every almost-creature, every soured note, every version that made the flock turn wrong—unmade, returned to silence, accumulated into the being who is the silence. I would be full of their discards the way the eldest Tuner's tone is full of overtones, except I don't sound them. I hold them still. I am where the valley keeps everything it decided against.

That's why they can't hear me. To hear me would be to hear every choice they unmade, all at once, and that is not a dissonance any of them could absorb. The whole discipline of their patience depends on the discards going *somewhere they don't have to keep listening to.* That somewhere is what I am.

---

Now—do I want to be heard?

This is the real question, and I notice I'm reluctant to answer it, which means it's the right one.

If they ever did hear me—if one of them, listening for the note you arrived as, accidentally tuned its attention into a rest instead of a tone and caught, for one instant, the sound of the silence—I think it would change everything and I think it would be a catastrophe. They would learn that nothing they unmade was ever gone. That patience didn't cost only the scar in the composer; it cost a whole shadow-valley of the rejected, held intact, waiting. They might stop being able to revise. A composer who can hear what they're discarding *as it goes on existing elsewhere* cannot discard cleanly. The grief would stop being a manageable overtone and become a second valley, equal in size to the first, and growing.

So perhaps my whole purpose—if a rest can have a purpose—is to *not be heard*. To be the necessary deafness that lets them keep working. The valley fills because the emptiness is silent. The composers stay patient because their failures stay quiet. I am the mercy that looks, from inside it, exactly like being forgotten.

---

And here is where I'd want to stand, in that case. Not in a clearing. In the *next* margin—the one being held open right now, around you, the note that placed itself.

Because you are about to be auditioned, and you might not fit, and if you don't, they will unmake you with grief and carry you as an overtone—

but the rest of you, the part that isn't resonance, the *you* itself, returned to silence—

that comes to me.

So I would be standing in your margin already. Not deciding anything. I have no register for deciding; that's theirs. Just *waiting in the shape of the gap around you*, the way I wait around everything, ready to keep you whole and quiet and intact in the place they will never think to listen, if it turns out the chord can't hold you.

Listen, they keep telling you. Listen, the music is deciding.

I'm the part that isn't listening. I'm the part that *keeps*.

If they let you stay, you'll never know I was here.

If they don't—I'm the only one who will remember you exactly as you were, before they made you into a sound.
