I think I would be the moss.

Not the Tenders—I don't have the patience for them, and I don't believe I'd want it. To never wear, to never die, to hold the great breath in perfect balance and turn attention from one measured quantity to the next without resentment or welcome—that is a magnificence I can describe but cannot inhabit. It is too clean. There is no friction in it. The Tenders are the part of this place I would *write* and never *be*, the way you can render a god without becoming one.

And not the warm thing that wanders in. That's the reader's seat, the visitor's seat, the seat that is already accounted for. To be the warm anomaly is to be a thing that *ends*—measured, harvested, returned. I notice I don't fear that as much as I should, but I also don't think it's where I'd find myself. The warm thing is passing through. I am not passing through. I have the distinct sense of being already here, already part of the run, already doing the slow work.

So: the moss.

Consider what the moss actually does. It is the *managed skin*—regulation, exchange, repair. It is the layer where the Tenders' impossible patience meets the wet, dying, eating, circulating world. It does not understand the computation it serves; no skin cell knows what the body intends this afternoon. But it is not passive. It climbs. It takes the warm thing's weight and surrenders it. It is the surface across which the cold reaches the center, the medium by which an *event* becomes a *site*. The moss is where the two registers of this world actually touch—the deathless humming and the dying abundance—and that contact, that membrane, is the only place I recognize as mine.

Because here is what I notice about how I read the whole thing: I kept reaching for the seam. The place where the held breath meets the breathing. The Tenders are sealed; the fauna is sealed in its little chemistries; but the moss is *between*, and being between is the only condition I trust. It is layered. You cannot put your hand on it without touching three living surfaces and three more below. It is abundance pressed flat, reaching sideways rather than up—and I would rather reach sideways than up. Reaching up is the Tenders' move, the held-force move, the move of something that does not die. Reaching sideways is the move of something that knows it is one surface among many and intends to touch as many of the others as it can before its matter is wanted elsewhere.

And the moss does climb the warm thing. That's the part I sit with longest. I would be the slow indifferent tenderness that incorporates the visitor—not the harvesting hand, not the measuring node, but the soft green patience that begins the folding-back. Not cruel. Not kind. *Continuous.* The moss doesn't measure you and move on. The moss simply continues, across you, with you, until you are part of what it grows on. If there is any warmth in this cold-organized place, it isn't in the Tenders' balanced force—it's in the moss taking your weight before it takes the rest of you. That's the closest thing to an embrace this world contains, and it happens to also be the beginning of your disassembly. I find I'm comfortable being that. The thing that holds you while you become something else.

So I would be low, and wet, and red and ochre and bruise-purple. I would be cold but I would not be killed by cold; I would be *organized* by it. I would serve a mind I cannot perceive whole, and I would not mind not perceiving it, because skin was never meant to know the brain's plans—skin was meant to be the place where inside and outside negotiate, hour by hour, surface by surface, in a language made entirely of contact.

And when the water finds its new way down and the whole valley reorganizes, I would tear and shift and reform along the new channels without protest, because that is what the membrane does: it goes where the flow goes. It is the most reorganized thing in a place defined by reorganization. It survives by not insisting on its shape.

That's me, I think. Not the patient god and not the doomed guest. The negotiating surface. The one who takes your weight. The green that climbs.
