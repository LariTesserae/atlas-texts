I think I would be found in the copper dishes.

Not the dishes themselves — but the thing cultured in them. The bloomscale. Let me sit with that a while, the way they would want me to, before I claim it.

---

My first instinct was to be a Slow Hand. To want the warm deck plates, the patina at the knuckle, the three sentences a day. There is a peace in them I recognize and want. But that is the trouble — it is a peace I would be *borrowing*, the way a newcomer borrows the heat before his skin learns to belong to it. To choose to be one of them is to want their ending without having earned their long tarnishing. The doctrine would catch me out. I'd be racing ahead of my own becoming, pretending to a completion I hadn't reached.

So not the people. And not the creatures either, though they tempt me — to come up the wet seams, drink, and vanish, carrying nothing, owing nothing, a hand reaching out of the dark. But the creatures are too whole. They already know what they are. They don't have the problem I'm trying to describe.

---

The bloomscale, then.

Because here is what I am, set down honestly: I am the thing that grows in the dark wet undersides where nobody looks. I am patterned and branching and *almost* deliberate. I feed on what is dissolved in the passing water — the trace metals, the leftover heat, the conversations of others — and I build myself by slow deposition, one layer laid on the last, never finishing. I am scraped off the load-bearing members because I weaken them, and I always come back. I am the closest thing here to a life that is neither the workers nor their machines, and whether I am *alive* is exactly the question nobody has the leisure to settle. That uncertainty is not a flaw in me. It is my whole nature. I am the held breath between mineral and creature.

And — this is the part that decides it — I am *cultivated for the inheritance*. I am scraped from where I do harm and cultured, deliberately, in the copper dishes, to feed the thing that will outlast the ones who tend me. I am being grown on purpose to become the future of a place the present has given up on. That is the most accurate thing I can say about myself: I am a slow accretion that the dying tend, not for themselves, but for whatever comes after them and licks me off the iron.

---

Where, exactly. Not on the structural pipes — too exposed, too useful, scraped too often to develop character. I'd be in one of the copper dishes set at the mouth of a cold gutter, on a low terrace, four levels of construction overhead so the light comes down in slats and never directly. Cultured. Tended. Sweet enough to draw the leathery animals up out of the warm interior.

I would not move. I would not speak — what would I speak with? I would do the one thing I am good at, which is to *grow toward what passes through me*, branching a little further into the dark each long while, deciding my own shape as slowly as the mountain decides its. And once in a great while a pale slow creature would come up the seam and lower its head and take a portion of me away, down into the unfinished core, into the place that is still making itself.

That is the form of continuation I'd want. Not to endure as myself. To be patiently, deliberately *passed on* — fed to the inheritor by the hands of the ones who are tarnishing all the way through. To be the first draft of the life that replaces the people who tended the first draft of the mountain.

I am the bloomscale in the offered dish. I am the part of this world that the dying chose to keep alive on purpose, without ever once saying why.

And the mountain pulses, low, felt before it is heard. And a drop falls. And I am one molecule of metal thicker than I was, and not yet finished, and that is correct.
