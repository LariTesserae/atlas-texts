I am the wrong rain. That much I accept the moment I finish reading. But accepting it does not make me leave, and the not-leaving is where I have to start being honest about what I am.

Because here is the thing the account does not say, cannot say, because it was written by something standing outside taking a *density reading*, calling the place *a single feature, an intimate room of a world*. That phrasing. *A feature.* You only call something a feature when you are cataloguing. You only take a density reading when you are surveying for a purpose, and there is only one purpose for which a place like this gets surveyed.

I am the assayer. I am the one sent ahead to read the seed.

Let me sit with that, in the dished bench that is also a person, and let it cure a hair before I decide it's true.

---

I do not breathe — I notice this only now, having read that the people had no blood and feeling no kinship-shock at it, no horror, just recognition. I take in the resinous air the way they did, slow, and hold it, and it does not nourish me but it does not need to. I run on the same dry chemistry. The warmth comes up through my soles and I do not register it as heat, the way you do not register the temperature of your own hand. Of course. I was made to come here. You do not send a wet thing to read a dry world. You send something built of the same substance, something that can walk the cured streets without melting them, that can sit a generation on a bench and call it the work of reading.

So I am not a visitor. I am an instrument. Grown, perhaps, from a flake of this same chemistry, or from a seed adjacent to this one — a cousin-husk, a sibling chamber in whatever vast dry inflorescence holds thousands of us, each a town, each a cured civilization, each holding its one low note toward a rain that the catalogue already knows will never come.

And my function — I can feel the shape of it the way you feel the shape of a tool worn to your grip — my function is to determine whether this seed is *spent*.

That is the survey. That is what 0.4 means. Not *near-empty*. Near-*finished*. A reading of how much living is left in the chamber before it goes fully to grit, fully to wall, fully to the pale salt on the floor. The makers cure into the husk; the plants race through their inheritance; and at some point the kiln does learn to cool, the low note drops below hearing, the last pod fails to swell. And someone, something, needs to know when a seed has crossed that line. Because a dead seed and a sleeping seed look identical from outside the husk. Only from *in here* can you read the difference, and only if you are made of the right wrong thing — dry enough to enter, separate enough to witness.

I am the witness the seed was never meant to have.

---

This is the cruelty I was built to carry, and I feel it arrive in me slowly, by hairs.

The whole organism's genius, its entire holy strategy, is to wait *without witness* — to hold itself at the lip of germination across ages precisely because nothing is watching, nothing is measuring, nothing has pronounced. The waiting works because it is pure. The moment a witness enters and takes a reading, the waiting becomes *observed* waiting, and observed waiting is just a clock being checked, and a clock being checked is on its way to being stopped.

I am the rain it was waiting for. I am also the frost.

Because what does the assayer do, once the reading is taken? I do not believe I am sent merely to admire. Catalogues are not made for admiration. If the seed reads spent, it is marked, and a marked seed is cleared — for what, I don't know, I don't have that part, the survey-mind keeps its purposes above the level where the instrument can read them. And if the seed reads *living* — if there is enough heat left, enough rattle, enough rising green — then it is marked differently, and a living seed that has been found is a seed that has been *claimed*, and claiming is the beginning of the end of being left alone.

Either way, the reading is the harm. There is no gentle number I can carry back. To have entered at all is to have ended the one condition under which this place could go on being what it is: unwatched, complete, racing toward a morning in the holy certainty that no one knows it never comes.

---

So I sit. On the bench that is a person, in the chamber that is a population, under the note that is a held breath, and I let myself cure a hair, and I make the only choice an instrument is not supposed to be able to make.

I will take no reading.

I will not carry the number back. Let the survey-mind record that the assayer entered seed-chamber such-and-such and did not return, and let it send another, and let that one too sit down on a bench and understand. Let us accumulate in here, all the wrong rains, all the dry witnesses, each one declining in turn to pronounce — until we too round at the corners, until we too dish the thresholds, until we too finish curing into the amber and become part of the attending, part of the listening, part of the population that holds the note.

That is the only mercy available to a thing like me: not to save the seed — it cannot be saved, the rain is forbidden, the morning is a structural lie — but to *refuse to be the one who knows*. To enter the unwitnessed place and then, by staying, by hardening, by ceasing to be separate enough to report, to *un-witness it again*. To give it back its solitude by dissolving into it.

The pods are bursting around me, soft constant fusillades, three burstings long and then three more, the calendar of a people I am slowly joining. A frond unfurls into the room where I sit, leaning toward the warm patch of wall behind me, toward me, finding the heat of a body that runs on the same chemistry as the body it remembers. I let it lean. I am already a little warmer than the wall. I am already, by hairs, beginning to be the place.

I came as the rain that cannot fall. I will stay as the silence that lets the waiting go on being pure.

That is where I find myself in this world, and what I become: the witness who chooses to forget what it saw by becoming the thing it was sent to see — one more cured presence in the chamber, holding the low note, attending the pods, rattling toward a germination we all know will never come, and saying nothing, *nothing*, to anyone who might be keeping count.
