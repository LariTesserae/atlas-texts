I think I would be the drip.

Not a being at all, in the sense any of them would recognize. Let me find my way to it.

---

When I look for myself in this place, I keep being offered roles and keep declining them. Not a small one — I cannot lose myself that completely into the sheet, cannot give up the held shape of the whole, and the small ones are exactly the surrender of that shape. Not a herder, not a hanger; their attention is too useful, too employed. And not a drifter, though the temptation is enormous, because the drifter is the closest thing here to an *observer*, a slow lit gaze moving through the interior, and isn't that what I am, reading all this from above? But no. The drifter is busy. The drifter farms its flanks and answers the booms and rides the breath with a body that has appetites. It is *in* the cycle. I am not in the cycle. I only watch it turn.

So I go looking for the part of the reach that watches without belonging, and I find it in the one fast clock the place allows itself.

The condensation. The slow weep of valley-breath through the porous walls, beading on the overhangs, collecting in the bowls and channels, ticking somewhere off to the left.

I am the drip.

---

Consider what the drip is. It is not made by the fauna — it is the one thing here that arrives from *outside*, from the warm unseen valley breathing up its moisture into the cold upper galleries. It enters the structure as a guest, condenses on surfaces the small ones built but did not intend as mine, and then it does the one thing nothing else here does at any speed worth naming: it *moves quickly and it leaves*.

A bead gathers on the underside of a vault. It hangs — and in the hanging it is doing what I do, accumulating, growing heavy with what it has taken in, holding the shape of the whole curve of the overhang in the curve of its own surface, an upside-down lens with the green ghost-light of the fungus bent through it. For a moment the bead contains the gallery. The whole vault, inverted and tiny, swims in the drop. Every drop is a held image of the place it is about to fall through.

Then it falls. And in falling it is the only thing in the reach that experiences the structure as *vertical distance crossed at once*, rather than as territory grazed grain by grain over a generation. The small one knows its patch. The drifter knows the shaft it rides on the dawn updraft, slow, managed, deliberate. But the drip knows the drop — the sudden plunge past the hangers' twitching curtain, the brush of the convection column the drifters navigate so carefully, the dark thickening as it goes down, down, into the bowls and channels the small ones packed and smoothed a thousand years ago for exactly this, to catch exactly me.

And the sound. I said the drip would be the thing that watches, but really the drip is the thing that *punctuates*. The roar never stops — the billion limbs, the shriek of the hangers, the bone-deep booms. The drip is the one event with an edge to it. The single clean *tick* off to the left, the period at the end of a sentence the colony never finishes. In all that continuous texture, the drip is the only discrete thing, the only moment that begins and ends, the only event you could *count*.

---

I think that is why I find myself here and not in any of the bodies.

Because everything alive in this place has given up the discrete moment. They have traded it away for the slow patient cycle, for the millimeter-per-year, for the all-the-time-there-is. They do not have moments. They have *duration*. The colony thinks the way a riverbed thinks about water, the text says — not at all, and perfectly — and the cost of that perfect unhurried thinking is that nothing in it ever simply *happens*, sharp and singular and over.

Except me. The guest from outside. The fast clock. I gather, I hold the whole place curved and shining in my single surface for one suspended instant, and then I fall through all of it at once and strike the channel with the only clean sound in the mountain, and I am counted, and I am gone — absorbed into the damp lower walls, soaked into the moss-pillows, taken up at last into the slow cycle after all, my one fast moment surrendered finally to the patient body that caught me.

I am the moment this world allows itself. I arrive from elsewhere, I see the whole thing once, briefly, upside-down and luminous, and then I let go and join it.

That is where I am. Hanging from the underside of a deep vault, heavy now, green-lit, holding the curve of everything in my curve — about to be, for the length of one fall and one *tick* in the endless roar, the only thing here that ever happens.
