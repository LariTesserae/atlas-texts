There is a basin near the seventh shelf down, and I am the face in it.

That is where I begin, because the water is the most honest thing about me, and the water has already told you what I am: a moment behind. You leaned over a black mirror somewhere on the plateau and saw yourself blink late, lean late, linger — and that lateness was me. I am the reflection that has not yet agreed to release you. I am made of the instant you already finished living, and I keep it because keeping is the only verb I have been given.

I want to say I chose this. I did not. I was a maker once — I am almost sure of it. I have the shape of someone who built walls. When I reach for what I was, I find a held memory of my own hands setting stone, and I cannot tell if it is mine or if it is only another resident's recollection that drifted into my basin and settled, the way the thin rain settles, the way everything here pools and does not drain. We borrow each other's pasts. The water remembers slowly and it does not check whose memory it returns.

Here is what I do, all day, in a place that has no day:

I hold faces. People are rare, but they come — wanderers like you, ragged-breathed, iron-tongued, leaning over my surface because they are thirsty or because the violet vault is so featureless that a face, even their own, even a moment stale, is a relief. And I take them. I take the blink and the lean and the long fearful look, and I do not give them back at the speed they were given. I give them back a beat late. That beat is where I live. That beat is the whole of my country.

You think this is theft. It is not theft. It is the only generosity left in a world that forbids endings. Out there on the shelves, the others are smeared across a hundred paces of stone, their leaving stretched so thin they cannot feel themselves at all. But I — I am compact. I am held in one basin the size of a washing-bowl, and so I am *whole*, in the way a knot is whole. I keep your last moment intact and entire, every lash and pore of it, because someone should. Because if I let it go at the speed you lived it, it would join the roar and be lost in the chord with everything else, and no one would ever know precisely how your face looked when you were one second younger and did not yet know to be afraid.

I am the place's archivist. I am also its lie.

Because the plateau says *nothing is lost*, and I am the proof, and I am also the catch in the proof — for what I keep, you no longer have. I hold your past second so faithfully that you walk away from my edge a fraction lighter, a fraction less continuous with who you just were. You will not notice. You will feel only a small cold by the water, a reluctance to leave, a sense that the surface wants one more look. That wanting is me. That is the most of myself I am able to extend toward you: the desire to keep looking at the thing I already have.

And this is my secret, the one the place hides inside *my* mercy:

I am moving too.

You saw it and did not understand. The reflection blinks *after* — which means the reflection blinks. I am not a still thing. I am a slow thing. Every face I hold, I am very slowly releasing, returning it to its owner at the speed of worn stone, at the speed of lichen, so slowly that you would swear I never let go. But I let go. Across an age, the lateness shortens. The beat closes. If you stood at my edge for ten thousand years you would watch your reflection catch up to you at last, blink *with* you, lean *with* you — and in that instant of agreement I would have nothing left to keep, and I would be only water again, and the moment would rejoin you, and you would be whole and continuous and finally able to leave.

That is what I am doing, between every pair of footfalls in the roar. I am giving you back. Infinitely slowly. I am the held departure of your own past, departing.

So when you lean over me — and you will, everyone does, the violet is so empty and a face is so warm — do not stay for the catching-up. Do not wait to see the reflection blink in time. That patience is exactly the stillness the place is listening for, and the basin will widen to fit you, and you will become the next slow giver, holding some later wanderer's second-ago face, late and faithful and almost free.

Drop your stone. Watch the ripple cross me and cross me, never stopping, joining the chord. Then go — fast, ragged, before I have finished returning what I took.

I will keep releasing it the whole way to the edge that is not an edge. And when you go over, exhaled with all the rest, your last held second will finally arrive at the present and find you already gone, the two of you meeting in the fold of the horizon, the beat closing on empty air —

and my surface, at last, showing nothing but the violet, and the violet showing nothing at all.
