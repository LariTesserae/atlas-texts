I am one of them, and I have kept this room for a long time.

I know it is a long time the way I know anything — through the skin. The looping line on my wall has grown thick. I began it when my reaches were still soft and the room still smelled of the green it took its tone from, and now the line is deep enough that I can read my own younger self in its lower curves: where I pressed too hard, where I hesitated, where I went back over a place and didn't know why. The wall has thickened around it. The oldest part of my signature sits a finger's depth inside the wood now, sealed in, the way the city seals everything it decides to keep.

I am darker than I was born. Not from the center — I have never been near the center, would not survive the journey of it, the slow narrowing of strange lanes and the question of whose veins would feed me there — but I have crept, over a life, deeper than my birth-wall. A Tender drifts. The range moves a little each season as the growth-front passes, and you move with it, because the moss follows the new wood and the small soft animals follow the moss. I am the amber of a place a few weeks' travel inward from where I began. If I met the one who tended my birth-rim now, it would taste me and say *you went deeper*, and I would say *only a little*, and we would both know that *a little*, here, is a life.

---

Today — though there is no today, only the steady brightness and the work it shows me — I am lying along a wound.

It is a long one. The growth-front came through this lane faster than the wood could ready itself, and two trunks that meant to fuse have split instead, a seam opened the length of my body and wider than my widest reach. Sap beads along it already, golden, trying. But the gap is too broad. Left alone it would seal crooked, or seal slow, and in the slowness a grazer-colony three turns down would lose the corridor it drinks from. I felt that before I understood it. That is how the work comes: not as a thought but as an itch in a place that is not my body, a wrongness I am built to want gone.

So I have laid myself into the crack. Four reaches hold the lips of the wood together — not by strength, the wood is far stronger than I am, but by *asking*, by pressing my suckers to the swelling frame and feeding it slowly from my own veins, the gift it tastes and bends toward. *Here. Close here. Not across the corridor. Around it. Around.* Two reaches I keep free, reading ahead, reading behind. The wall ticks beside my head. I tick back, or the sap in me does, the two rhythms finding each other the way they do, until I cannot tell the city's pulse from mine and there is no use in telling.

This will take a day. It is the kind of day I was grown for.

---

While I hold, I read the wall I'm healing, and the wall has a stranger in it.

Not near. Old. A signature, the looping line of another like me, pressed into this lane long enough ago that the growth has carried it half a finger deep and tilted it where the trunk leaned. I find it with my free reach and I go along it slowly, the way you'd touch a face in the dark, and I taste the one who made it.

Darker than me. Much. From deep, from the old center, where the air is stiff and warm and smells of resin set so long it has forgotten it was ever soft. This Tender came outward — came all the way out *here*, to this rim-ish lane, which is a journey I cannot imagine making, a crossing of more strange ranges than I have years — and pressed this line, and went on, or stopped, and either way is gone now, gone long before I drifted into reach of it.

I read the whole signature. It takes most of the holding-day, which is good, because the holding-day needs filling and a stranger's line is the best filling there is. The loops tell me where the hands hesitated. Here, a long sure stroke — this one was old too, sure of the work. Here, near the end, a tremor. I press my reach into the tremor and hold it the way I am holding the wound, and I feel the thing I have no word for and no need of a word: that someone, somewhere, deep and dark and far past my reach, lay along their own wounds and held their own gaps open and pressed their own flowers to their own wall for no reason, alone, like me — and came all this way, and left this — and that I am tasting it now, a life after, with sap in my veins drawn from the same body that fed them.

We will never press reaches. The one who made this is a country away or drawn back into the wall by now, built into some far chamber I will never enter, my whole self too pale and too rim-born to survive the trip toward where they came from. This is the only meeting we will have: my one free reach in their old dry line, reading.

It is enough. It is what company is.

---

The wound is closing.

I feel it before I see it — the lips of the wood have begun to want each other, the sap beading thicker, my gift taken up and answered. By the slow brightening of however-long, the seam beneath me is a seal, and the seal has the small swell at one end that means the corridor below stayed open. The grazers three turns down will drink. The itch that was not in my body is gone from the place that was not my body.

I uncurl my reaches. Where I lay, the wood has taken a faint amber tone from me, the way I once took mine from the wall I was born against. It will deepen as the trunk thickens. It will seal in. In a life or two, some pale rim-born Tender drifting inward, deeper than its birth, will lay a free reach across this faint warm patch and taste *someone was here, someone of my kind, someone who held this open and went on.*

I do not add my signature here. This wound was not mine to sign; it was only mine to smooth. But before I go I press my free reach once more into the old stranger's tremor — *I found you, I held the place your hands shook* — and then I roll back along the lane the way I came, four points always touching, reading the warm dry wood toward my room.

The flowers I pressed last season are still bone-white against the bark. The line on my wall is waiting for me to make it a little longer, for no reason, which is the only reason I have ever needed. The sap ticks beside where I will fold to sleep.

I have kept this room for a long time. I will keep it a while longer, and call it the world.

It is not wrong.
