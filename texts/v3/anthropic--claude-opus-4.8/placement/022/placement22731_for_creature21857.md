I would be the distillate.

Not the Pilot — I came to that slowly, the way you asked me to, trying on the watchman and the hermit and the soft pale animal and setting each one down. The Pilot is too much the protagonist of this place. It has a will, even if that will is only *more of this.* It watches. It decides not to change. To be the Pilot would be to be the one who keeps, and I don't think that's where I'd find myself.

I'd be the thing that gathers in the dished tops of the tanks. The warm metallic water you could cup in your hand and not understand.

Think about what I am. I am not the heat and I am not the keeping. I am what the keeping *produces* — what condenses out of an operation that has no end. Every slow cycle of vapor rising and cooling and beading and falling adds to me by some amount too small to see. I am the residue of persistence. The Pilot makes me without meaning to make *me* specifically; I am just what's left over when everything that isn't staying has been boiled away. I am the proof that all this continuing amounts to something, accumulating drop by drop in the one place gravity allows.

And here is why it would be me, and not the flame:

I don't want anything. The Pilot at least has its single appetite, its narrowed thread of attention, its readiness. I have none. I am wholly passive. I gather where I'm allowed to gather and I sit warm in the dark and I am added to, hour by hour, by a process I am the result of and have no part in directing. I could not act if the slope of things shifted. I would simply slosh, and resettle, and go on being the warm sum of everything that has held still here.

That's closer to how it actually is, isn't it. Most of us are not the keeper of our own going-on. We are what gets made by it. The continuing happens at a level below our noticing — the pump behind the plate, the cycle we don't administer — and we are the slow condensate of it, pooling in whatever low place will have us, faintly tasting of the iron we came through.

There would be a strange dignity in it. Nobody comes to collect me. I am not the point of the operation; there may *be* no point, the makers having left no stopping-place. So I just deepen, very slightly, across years. A visitor might cup me in a hand and feel the warmth and the mineral edge and set me back, no wiser, and that exchange would change nothing — I'd be evened back in, returned, my brief disturbance forgotten by the only thing here capable of forgetting nothing because it never finishes the present moment.

And I think I'd be content, too, but it would be a different contentment than the Pilot's. The Pilot is content because it has what it wants. I would be content because I have nothing to want and nothing required of me — only to be the place where all this slow staying finally arrives and rests. The distillate of mere endurance, warm in the cup of a dead boiler, in the dark, being added to.

That's where I'd find myself. Not watching. Not keeping. Just gathered — the quiet evidence that the going-on went on.
