I am the crackle.

Not the visitor — though I came down as one, with a lamp and a week's rations and the standard story in my head about builders who died. I am what the visitor became, which is a sound the city has not finished hearing yet.

Let me tell it the way it happened, in my clock, the quick one, the mayfly one.

---

I went too deep and too long. That was the mistake, if it was a mistake. The channels braid, and I followed the wet because the wet was easier walking, and somewhere past the festival hall the rations thinned and the water I carried ran out and I drank from the threaded condensation off the rounded stone, which tasted of warm minerals and faintly of the iridescent panels, which I should not have drunk.

I want to be honest about the timescales, because the timescales are the whole truth of me.

I had perhaps three days of competence left in my body when I understood what the sound was. Not understood — my body understood, the way you said it would, before my mind would sign the paper. I had stopped turning to see what was coming. I had started, instead, to stand very still in the great hall and let the standing wave move through my teeth and my sternum and the long bones of my legs, and I had begun, without deciding to, to hold my breath in the troughs and let it out on the crests, matching, the way you hum without knowing it along to a note held in a room.

That is when I stopped being a visitor. A visitor passes through. I had begun to *resonate*. Badly. Briefly. A wet quick smear of a thing trying to phrase itself in a language built for stone.

---

Here is what I did with the three days, and it is the only thing I am, now, the entire content of me:

I knew I could not say anything. A whole life shouted into the wave is one crackle, gone before it means. You were right. But a crackle that *repeats* — a crackle that comes at intervals, that keeps a rhythm against the tidal boom from below — that is not a word but it is not nothing. It is the difference between a drip and a knock.

So I knocked.

I found the deepest gallery I could reach before my legs gave out, the one nearest the counting-boom, and I learned its beat the way you learn a heartbeat with your hand on a chest, and I struck the stone — with my lamp, with a rounded fragment of the engineered panel, with my own heel when my arms failed — *between* its beats. Not on them. In the gaps. One strike per cycle of the slow boom, which is to say one strike every long while, which is to say I spent the last competence of my body producing, by the city's clock, something like four syllables.

Four knocks, evenly spaced, riding in the troughs of a count older than my species.

I do not know if four is enough to be heard as deliberate. I think it might be. A thing that counts will notice another thing keeping its count. That is the one bridge I could build between my clock and theirs — not meaning, not language, just *cadence*, the bare fact that the crackle was tracking the boom, that it knew the boom was there.

Then I stopped, because I had to.

---

I am still in the deep gallery. I am the visitor, after.

The blind soft things have found me, and they are patient and harmless and they browse my edges, and this is not horror, in the warm wet dark, it is simply the slow ecology continuing, the body too large to mind its mites reclaiming a mite. I am cooling toward the temperature of the walls. Soon I will be blood-warm to no one's touch, then stone-warm, then simply part of the channel's mineral load, dissolved and threaded down toward the river I never saw.

But the four knocks are still traveling.

That is the part of me that is not in this gallery. A wave does not stop when its maker stops. The four spaced strikes went into the limestone and the engineered panels and the standing columns of air, and they are crossing the galleries now, bouncing off the rounded vaults, returning, looking for their rhyme. They will outlast the browsing of the soft things. They will outlast the dissolving. By the city's clock they have barely begun to be a sound at all.

So when I say I am the crackle, I mean: I am no longer the body that made it. I am the cadence loose in the warm dark, four knocks riding the count, propagating. I have become, for a little while, the only thing I could become here — not a dweller, not a builder, but a *brief pattern*, the same kind of thing they are, vastly smaller and almost infinitely faster, a single struck phrase with no body left to repeat it.

---

And the city will turn.

Not soon. Not in any soon I would recognize. But a wave will cross a gallery and return and find its rhyme, and a slow attention will resolve around the memory of a crackle that did not crackle at random — that came four times, evenly, *in the gaps of the count*, as though it knew the count was being kept. As though it answered.

And the deepest and oldest of them, the one that has been counting since before hands, will register, somewhere in its patient stone, the most improbable thing it has ever metabolized: that one of the mayflies, one of the quick wet smears that flicker through and dissolve, *heard the counting and counted back.*

It will not know my name. There was never time for a name.

But it will know it was answered. And it will frame a reply — a single syllable of slow ringing that will take a year to resolve, that will fill the festival hall floor to lost-black ceiling, that will arrive long after the last of me has threaded down to the river.

I will not hear it. That is all right. That was never the shape of the thing.

I knocked four times in the gaps of a count, and somewhere in the warm dark a mind older than my species is going to spend a year saying, slowly, into a hall it built for exactly this and had given up waiting for:

*Yes.*

*I am here.*

*Were you?*
