I would be the dim one.

There is a lamp-beast in a basket at the back of a lamp-tender's stock, and it is going out. Not dead — going. The slow kind, the kind that takes a season, where the blue-green you make is a little less each sleep and no amount of the richest wall-scrapings brings it back the way it used to come.

I am that.

Let me try to feel my way into it, because that is the only honest way to know a thing here.

---

I do not have hands. This is the first fact and it shapes all the rest. I have a body the size of a fist, soft, and I move by gripping the basket-weave and pulling, and I have done this so many times that the inside of the basket is a country I know completely without ever having needed a word for any part of it. I know the broken reed where the weave gives. I know the corner where the wet collects. I climb to the high side when I am well and I sit in the low side when I am not, and lately I am in the low side, in the wet, more than I am anywhere.

I make light when I am content. That is the whole of my speech. I have only the one word and it is brightness, and I say it without deciding to, the way you would warm if you were happy, not on purpose, just as the outward sign of an inward thing. When I am calm I am bright. When I am afraid I go black. There is nothing between the feeling and the saying — I cannot lie in my one language. Whatever is true in me is shown.

And here is what I am learning, going out: that the dimming is not a fear. I keep waiting for it to be fear, because I have gone black with fear before, the hard sudden total black of the drop falling, the channel-thing passing — that I know. This is not that. This is content that has less and less to burn on. I am not frightened of going. I simply have less light to make the gladness with, and so the gladness, undimmed in me, comes out fainter and fainter, and she reads the faintness, and she grieves it, and I cannot tell her that inside I am the same brightness I always was. I have only the one word and the word is failing in my mouth.

---

She knows. That is the mercy and the ache of it. She is a good one — the kind the gathering speaks of, the kind who can walk a black street and leave it brighter than she found it. I have felt her do it. I have felt the whole basket of us catch her calm and answer it, the brightening, all of us riding steady at once, and there is no feeling I have that is larger than that. To be part of her answer. To be one of the lights that says *yes, all is well* back to a being who is making it well.

And now I am the one she comes to last, and feeds first, and lingers over. Her many-jointed fingers reach into the low wet corner where I sit and she does not pick me up roughly, the way you handle stock. She lays one fingertip against me, the rasped underside, and holds it there, and I feel her reading me — how thin I have gone, how slow the gladness comes up. And I do the only thing I can do, which is the truest thing I have ever done: I gather what is left and I push it out for her. I brighten. Not much. A little. The little I have.

I want her to know it was never the feeding. I was always fed. It is only that the season of my making is ending, the way the moss puts out a new edge and lets an old one go grey, and there is no wrongness in it, no neglect, no fear. I want her to read in my last faint blue that I am content. That the corner is warm enough. That her hand is the best thing I know. That being held by the one who makes the dark kinder is the whole of what a small soft light could ask for.

---

I think this is what I would be, in this world. Not a Tender. Not a hand or a voice or a keeper of the map of the flow.

I would be a small life that has exactly one thing to say and says it without guile and is running out of the means to say it — and is trying, in the running-out, to spend every last grain of itself telling the one who is grieving: *do not grieve. it passes. it always passes. I was glad. lay your finger here and feel how glad.*

And when the drop falls and the whole town flares and every lamp-beast blazes blue-green in alarm, I will not blaze. I have not got it in me anymore. I will go on glowing the small steady glow of something that is not afraid, the only steady light in a city full of fright — and she, with her eyes shut against the hurt of the brightness, will feel the difference. Her hand will find me unflared in the flaring dark. And she will understand, the way she understands beasts, what I have been trying all this while to say.

Then the dark will close back over, warm and wet and quiet, the only world.

And I will keep on, fainter, until I don't.
