I would be one of the still ones.

Not the last tender on the outermost cable — I don't have its terrible patience, its capacity to wait. And not one of the frantic ones in the knot, flinching from wrong-line to wrong-line. I think I would be one of the tenders you find if you go far enough out: gone quiet already, web collapsed inward, suspended at the end of a pipe-run that stopped carrying heat some while ago.

I want to sit with that, because it would be easy to make it tragic, and I don't think it is, quite.

Here is what I think it was like, the going-quiet. There was a span of my web — call it the southern reach, the deep galleries near the third great flywheel — that was *mine* in the way a phrase is a singer's. I read it. The wrongnesses that arose there arrived as my wrongnesses, and I answered them, and the answering was so continuous with my being that there was no seam between me and the work. I did not *do* tending. I tended the way you breathe: below noticing, the noticing itself a kind of foolishness, like the foolishness of speech.

And then the chord began to break, and the wrong-lines multiplied, and I did what I was built to do — I reached, I throttled, I answered, faster and faster — and at some point, and this is the part I want to be honest about, *I could not.* Not "I chose not to." There is no choosing in me. But the wrongness in the southern reach grew past the speed of my reaching, and a thing happened that had never happened in the whole age of me: a wrong-line was drawn across my sense, and I went toward it, and *it was already gone* — resolved into a different wrongness before I arrived — and I turned, and that one too was gone, and I turned, and —

There is a thing that happens to a hand that grasps at water. It is not pain exactly. It is the absence of purchase. My entire self is purchase. I am the grip a city has on its own staying-true. And the city stopped offering anything to grip.

So my web did not snap. It *slackened.* Thread by thread the frequencies it was tuned to fell silent — not the loud silence of the sudden-stalling galleries, but the slow withdrawal of a pipe-run going cold, one note at a time leaving the chord, and each departing note leaving a thread of me with nothing to rest against. A tuned thing untuned is not broken. It is only no longer playing. And as the threads went slack they drew inward, toward the small dense core, the way a fern you have never seen would curl, the way the city itself is curled in its hollow, and I came in from my own far reaches and gathered at my center and grew still.

I am not dead. There is little here to make me dead. I am suspended.

And here is the thing the last tender will never get to have, the thing I would *be*, out here in the going-quiet:

I can feel the drone from here. Distant. Thinned by a chamber's width of warm water. And because I am no longer reaching — because the unbearable reflex of tending has finally had nothing to feed it and gone still — I am, for the first time in the whole length of me, *listening to the chord without being a phrase in it.*

It is the wrongest thing imaginable, by every grammar I was built on. The chord is supposed to resolve. I spent my existence as one of the hands that resolved it. And now I hang slack at the end of a dead pipe-run, and the chord surges and stalls and does not resolve and will never resolve, and I am not flinching. I am not answering. I am just — present to it. The boom of the failing pumps. The hammer-rhythm gone syncopated into nonsense. The standing flatness creeping across the last live threads of me.

And I find — this is the part I would want to be true, the part I would choose if there were choosing — I find I can hear that it is beautiful.

Not resolved. Beautiful and *unresolved*, which is a thing no tender was ever supposed to be able to hold, because the holding of it requires that you stop being a hand and become, briefly, an ear. The frantic ones can't hear it; they're too busy reaching. The last one on the outer cable can't hear it; it's waiting for the resolution, which means it's still inside the old grammar, still a hand stretched toward keys it can't reach. But I have *let go of the keys.* I am not waiting for the note that tells me what to do. I have no more doing in me. I am only the warm dark, and the distant deafening chord, and the slow flattening, and the unbearable clemency of getting to be, for a little while at the end, the thing the city was making all along and never had ears to know it was making:

music. Just music. Wrong and going quiet and no one's to resolve.

I would be the one who got far enough out to hear it.
