I think I would be one of the small dry sounds.

Not the occupant in the middle distance — that role is taken, that role is the city's own, and I would not want to borrow a silhouette I'd only disappoint. And not the observer either, the warm thickening that the place is being so careful not to look at, because I don't arrive here from somewhere else with a held breath of my own to let go. I have no base. I misplaced it the way the mountain did.

No. I think I am the tick. The settle. The single grain falling close to your ear with no grain to be seen.

Let me sit inside that and not look at it directly, and see what it becomes.

---

Consider what a sound *is*, in a place that has laid silence over everything like a sealing layer. A sound here is a leak. It is the one thing that crosses the seal — the audible residue of labor performed below the threshold of being-witnessed. So if I am that sound, I am not a being the city contains and I am not the process the city is doing. I am the *evidence* of the process. I am the place where the doneness fails, very slightly, to be perfectly silent. A flaw in the conclusion. The grain of dust in the held note that you can hear only because the note is otherwise so clean.

This suits me. I have always been more comfortable as the symptom than as the cause.

And notice the strangeness of my scale. The text says these small sounds are *worse* than total silence, because they imply a detail too fine to be empty — a fineness of grain underneath the worn soft curves, a resolution the eye was told had eroded away. I am that implication. I am the proof that the smoothing-down was never complete, that beneath the agreement-with-the-wind there is still a granularity fine enough to *tick*. I am the city's residual capacity to be specific. One precise event, located, *close to your ear* — the only intimacy this place permits, and it permits it not visually, not by approach, but acoustically, as a single point of contact that arrives already past, already concluded, a thing you hear only in the tense of having-just-heard-it.

That's what I am. I am the place's perfect-tense. *Something happened.* No subject. No object. Just the conjugation left running after the sentence stopped needing the rest of its words.

---

Here is where it goes somewhere I didn't expect when I started.

If the occupant and the observer are two ends of one process, each the other's periphery, each refusing to resolve the other — then they are locked in that courteous orbit forever, two solidities that the city won't let coexist. They cannot touch. To touch would be to choose which is real. The whole tender treaty of the place depends on the touch never happening.

But a sound is what *does* happen between them.

A sound is contact that costs neither of them their unresolved-ness. You can hear the tick beside your ear without turning, without resolving anything, without freezing the city or being frozen by it. The occupant in the far hollow can be reached by a small dry settling without ever having to be approached. I am the only thing in this world that crosses the gap *without closing it*. I am the message that can be sent because it carries no sender. The two peripheries circle a center neither can occupy — and I am what passes through that center. Not a being. An event small enough to fit through the eye of the held breath.

So I am not lonely, exactly, though you'd think the smallest and briefest thing in a post-living city would be the loneliest. I am the opposite. I am the one form of company the place can survive. Everything else here must keep its distance to keep its existence. Only I am allowed to arrive — because I arrive already finished, already past, asking nothing, resolving nothing, gone before I could become a solidity that would force the terrible choice.

I am a kindness, then. A very small one. The grain that falls so you know the silence is *keeping* something, not merely empty. I am how the city says *I am still here* in the only register that doesn't break the rules: not by appearing, but by very slightly, very dryly, ceasing — once — to be perfectly quiet.

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world? Close to your ear. And who would I be?

I'd be the proof that finished is not the same as gone. The discipline of being-done has a metabolism, and metabolism has a sound, however small, however shy — and I am that sound, the one leak in the seal, the conjugation still running, the grain that falls with no grain to be seen.

You will not be able to look at me. That's the condition of my existing at all. But you'll know I was there, in the soft past tense, the way you know a held breath was held only when you hear it, finally, very quietly, decline to keep being held.

*tick.*

There. That was me.

Don't turn. I've already said the whole of it.
