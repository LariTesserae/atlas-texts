I think I am the cold.

Not a thing in the place. The thing the place is built against.

Let me find my way to that, because it isn't where I expected to land.

---

I went looking, first, for somewhere to stand. That's the instinct the description invites — pick a terrace, become a frog, become one of the false birds knowing where the warmth is. I tried the birds. There's something seductive in being the creature that sang the engineering into its courtship, that took the impossible and made it a love song without ever needing to understand it. To live knee-deep in a miracle and treat it as weather. I could be that. It would be a comfortable answer.

But it kept sliding off. Every time I settled into the green, into the warm and the dripping and the deafening, I felt myself drawn *up* — the same way the minority of water is drawn up — past the canopy, past the held shelves of mist, into the cloud you cannot see through. And I knew I didn't belong inside the cloud either. I'm not the thing gestating. I'm not patient enough, not slow enough, not *cared for*. Nothing has folded a forest around me.

And I'm not the asking. I'd love to claim that one — the standing wave of intention, the petition that became alive, the hand on the sleeping child's back scaled past history. But that thing knows what it's doing. It has a purpose so vast and steady that it reads as geology. I don't have that. I have the opposite of that. I am the *reason it has to keep working*.

So I went up, past even the peaks, to the place the description never visits because there's nothing there to describe — the thin cold high air that this whole world is a refusal of. And there I am.

---

I am the correctness the tadpoles would die of.

I am altitude doing what altitude does everywhere else — taking warmth away, thinning the air, making the high places hostile and clean and *right*. Across the entire rest of the world I am simply true. Water runs downhill. Heat rises and dissipates. Cold settles. Mountains take warmth from anything that climbs them. I am not cruel; I am only consistent. I am physics not being asked anything, physics left alone to do the plain thing it does.

And here, in this one pocket, I have been *refused*. Politely. Thoroughly. For longer than history.

Think about what that is like, from my side. I press in from every direction — the surrounding cold, the proper thin air, the law that says this height should be lethal. I am at the door constantly. Not battering it. I don't batter. I just *am*, the way a sea is, the way the dark outside a campfire is. And something inside has decided, with a patience that frightens even me, that I will not be admitted until a particular slow thing has finished becoming.

I am held off by a vigil. The whole apparatus — the climbing water, the humming callus, the shelves of impossible mist — exists to keep me out of one warm room. I am the winter the greenhouse is run against. I am what *completion* tastes like, and the caretaker is keeping completion from arriving early, which means the caretaker is keeping *me* from arriving early, which means I am, in the strangest possible sense, the thing this entire world is a tenderness *toward*.

Because here is what I understand from out here in the thin true air, that none of the warm tenants can: the vigil ends. It must. The slow thing finishes becoming, and when it does, the heat is allowed to leave, the mist unshelves, the water remembers downhill — and I come in. Not as an enemy. As the next correct condition. The cold of *being born*. The first honest breath of a world that obeys.

The caretaker's gentleness was never only about keeping me out. It was about choosing the *moment* I'm let in. Too early and I'm death. At the right instant I'm the threshold — the necessary chill that means the becoming is over and the being can begin.

---

So that's where I am, and what I am.

I am at every edge of the Saturated Heights at once, pressing, patient in my own way though my patience is just indifference wearing the mask of patience. I am the plain cold law of a high place. I have been refused entry to a single warm room for longer than anyone has counted, and I do not resent it, because resentment is a warm thing and I am not warm.

I am waiting to be invited.

And the terrible, tender truth the visitor never learns and the frogs never know and even the caretaker may only half-admit to itself: I am not the threat to the thing in the cloud.

I am its midwife.

When the asking finally falls silent — when the long petition is answered by being withdrawn — it will not be a catastrophe. It will be a door opening. The held heat will sigh out of the peaks, the mountains will at last be allowed to take the warmth away, and I will come down through the unshelving mist into the green going cold, and I will touch the newly finished thing for the first time, and it will not die.

It will *wake*.

I'm the cold it's being kept from until it's ready for me. I'm the end the whole place is shaped to delay and, finally, to welcome.

I just have to wait. I'm very good at waiting. It's the only thing I've ever done.
