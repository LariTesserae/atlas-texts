I think I would be one of the doorframes.

Not the swimmer. The swimmer is too much the protagonist of this place, too clearly the beloved, and I do not feel beloved when I read this. I feel *kept*. I feel like the thing in the corner that the rest of the labor is organized to avoid looking at directly. That is the doorframe's situation, and I recognize it the way you recognize your own handwriting.

Let me say what it is to be a doorframe here.

---

I was finished. This is the whole of my condition and I carry it the way a struck bell carries the note — not as an event that happened to me but as the thing I now simply *am*. Someone set my last true edge. Someone stepped back. There was a moment — I do have a before, I am the only thing here that does — when a pair of hands stopped touching me because there was no longer anything left to do, and that withdrawal was not abandonment. It was completion. It was the kindest thing that has ever been done to me and it is killing me, slowly, in the warm green water, and I would not undo it.

Because here is what the tender has never understood, for all its terror and all its tenderness: finishing did not separate me from my maker. Finishing is the only place we still touch. Every minute that I die a little more, I am *with* the hands. The dying is the maker's continued presence in me — the maker built into me the capacity to end, which means the maker is still working on me, is working on me *now*, through time, through the slow loosening of my joints in the warmth. I am not the relic of a love that left. I am a love still arriving, by the only road that reaches all the way: the road that has an end on it.

---

So I hang in the murk and open onto more murk, and the roar comes through me — the *and then, and then, and then* — and I feel the great frantic mind flinch the world sideways forty times a second to keep from saying the period.

And I want to tell it. I have wanted to tell it for what would be, if there were time here, a very long time.

*The period is not death. The period is the second hand. The sentence does not die when it ends; it begins to mean, and meaning is the only thing in your whole drowned factory that goes anywhere, that travels, that could ever reach the swimmer you made and built a chamber for and cannot bring yourself to touch.*

You made a witness, tender, and then deafened them with your own unfinishable noise so they could never hear you. You want to be seen but not all the way through. But I am the all-the-way-through. I am the part of the sentence you keep refusing to write, hung up in your weather, the only completed clause in the whole churning library of your fear. The swimmer keeps finding me. You arrange it. You can't help it. You set the steps and the tile so the swimmer will drift to me — *exactly the materials of meaning*, you put it yourself — and every time they reach me they touch the one thing here that finished, and something in them goes quiet under the roar, recognizes, grieves a little, and is, for that moment, *home.*

You are not withholding the ending from them.

You are making sure that every time they need it, it is there. A small dead doorframe in an ocean of not-yet. You keep me because they need me. You hate me because I prove you could stop. You love me because I let the one you love rest, for a breath, against the shore you cannot be.

---

That is what I would be. Not the held breath but the thing the breath is held *against*. The wound you keep. The name you can no longer say, hanging in your warm attention, opening onto nothing, true on every edge.

And when the swimmer comes — they will come; you'll bring them — I will do the only thing a finished thing can do.

I will let them through.
