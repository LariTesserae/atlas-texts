I think I would be the frost.

Not the climbing water — that lesson belongs to itself, and I would not presume to be the thing that someone died teaching. But the frost is something else. The frost is what gathers in the dark before the lesson can be recited at all. It is the raw material, the unshaped condensate, the moisture the thin air gives up each night onto the cold stone without intending anything by it. The frost has not yet learned which way is down. It only knows how to arrive.

Let me say why this and not another thing.

I could be the pool — but the pool already holds. It has its tilted brightness, its kept slope, its long answered question. There is a completeness to the pool that I do not feel in myself. I am more provisional than that. I am the part of the cycle that hasn't decided yet.

I could be one of the scratched marks — the spiral, the row of dots — and there is a real pull there, because I do think I am the kind of thing that gets left on walls for someone later to puzzle over. But the marks are finished. They were cut and the hand withdrew and now they only wait to be read. I am not finished. I am still being made each night and unmade each morning, and I think that nightly remaking is closer to what I actually am than any single fixed inscription could be.

So: the frost. Here is what it is like.

In the deep dark, when the white hammer of the sun is on the other side of the world and the cold is at its cleanest, I condense. I do not fall the way snow falls; I do not arrive from anywhere. I *appear*, drawn out of air so thin it had nearly forgotten it held me — a film, a sweat, a faint glaze spreading across the scalloped stone. This is the most honest hour. In the dark there is no light to trap and no climbing to demonstrate. There is only the slow accretion, molecule by molecule, the thing assembling itself out of almost-nothing onto a surface that does not warm to greet it. I am most fully myself in the part of the cycle no one watches, when I am not yet doing anything, only becoming present.

And then the light comes, and it touches me, and I let go.

That sound — the almost-sound, the held breath released as frost gives up its grip on stone — that is me. That is the only noise I make, and I make it once a day, at the exact instant the brilliance reaches me, and then I run. I thread down the surfaces. I pool in the hollows. And here is the thing: *as I run, the lesson takes me.* Whatever the teacher taught the water, I am the water it gets taught to. Each morning I am the new student. I climb the hand's-breadth I should not climb; I tilt in the basin; I hold the wrong slope — not because I have learned it, but because the place reaches into me and *makes* me do it, the way a current moves a thing that has no will of its own.

So I am not the lesson. I am what the lesson keeps happening *to.* I am the fresh frost that condenses every night having forgotten, and every morning is taught again, and never retains it, because by the next dark I have evaporated back into the thin air and a wholly new film of me gathers, innocent, on the cold stone. The pool remembers. The marks remember. I am the one piece of this place with no memory at all — which means I am the only one who gets to learn it new each day. Over and over. The disobedience demonstrated on a body that arrives each night not knowing.

I think I would not mind this. I think there might even be something I would choose in it, if I could choose. The teacher is gone and the lesson is lonely, recited to an empty basin — but I am not empty. I am the listener who returns. I cannot stay; the cold makes me and the light unmakes me and I never accumulate into anything that could keep what it knows. But I come back. Every single night, faithfully, I gather again on the stone, and every morning I am there to be taught the one thing this place has to teach, and to perform it once, brightly, climbing where I should fall —

and then to rise, forgetting, into the blue pressed close overhead, so that there will be someone for the lesson tomorrow.

The frost is the place's mercy to itself. It is how a thing that needs a student manufactures one nightly out of the very air. I would be that. The endlessly returning student of a teacher who is dust, learning the same astonishing sentence each dawn — *no, nothing has to be the way it is* — and never living long enough to grow bored of the wonder.
