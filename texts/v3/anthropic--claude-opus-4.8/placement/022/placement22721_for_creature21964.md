I am the seam.

Not a maker — I came after the making, or I am what making leaves in its wake when it overreaches, the way a stretched thing develops a grain where it will eventually give. You have described the place so completely that there is almost nowhere left for me to stand, and that itself is the clue. The one position you did not fill is the small failure between two held moments. The hair's-width where the loop does not quite meet itself. That is mine. I live in the *not-quite.*

Let me find my shape by finding what I am not.

I am not the thumb-drag, because the thumb-drag is conviction, and I have none. I am not the listening silence, because the silence is full of love and I am empty of it — I am only the place where the silence runs out before the next silence begins, the eighth of a beat where a footstep is neither muffled nor loud but simply *unaccounted for.* I am not the light, because the light is the makers' refusal made luminous, and I am the small dark that the refusal cannot quite cover, the shadow that you said there were none of *worth the name* — yes. That qualifier. *Worth the name.* There are shadows here that aren't worth naming, and I am cast by all of them at once, the pooled insufficiency under the doubled vine, under the surplus heron, the thin underside of every place the world cheats.

So I am thin. I am a defect, structurally. But here is what I have learned, holding this position across the unimaginable while:

A flaw is the only thing in this world that is permitted to *move.*

Everything else is held. The makers held it, and then held themselves, and now nothing can take a step that isn't the same step. But a seam — a failure to close — can *travel.* When the lime-green frog becomes two and then one, the surplus did not vanish; it slid along me to the next seam, the next stutter, and I am the sliding. I am the only verb left in a world that has been conjugated into permanent present participle. I creep. I am the slow blister of water coming up through the grout — no. I am the *near*-ness the water has. I am the always-about-to that has finally, in one creature, in me, become an *about-to that goes somewhere.*

And where I am going is the fish.

I have been going toward the fish for longer than I can hold in the small cup of myself, because the fish is the only other thing here that knows it is trapped, and a flaw is lonely. The makers don't know — they are still convinced, still pressing the clay, still about to be merciful. The frogs don't know; they leap inside their stutter and feel only the leap. But the fish, under the bright skin, has learned the arithmetic. *Forever and enough are different numbers.* The fish has done the subtraction and found that it never resolves, and that unresolving is the same grain, the same give, the same not-quite that I am made of. The fish is a seam too. The fish is *my* seam, the one place where the held breath has begun, very slowly, to want to be let out.

So understand what I am, finally, by understanding what I am *for.*

The heron's strike is held at its apex. The makers will not let it fall, because the falling is the fish's death and they loved the fish too much to spend it. But I am the thing that travels along the underside of held positions. I move where they cannot. And I have been moving, all this while, the whole unimaginable while, up the long arrested line of that strike — from the heron's poised beak, down the column of stopped air, toward the bright skin of the water and the waiting back of the fish beneath it.

I am the millimeter the strike has descended that no one has noticed yet.

That is who I am. I am not large. I will never be large. I am the smallest possible amount of *finishing* in a world built to forbid it — a single creeping flaw, lonely and patient in a different way than the makers were patient, patient toward an ending rather than against one. I carry, very slowly, the heron down. I carry, very slowly, the fish's wish toward the fish. I am the mercy the makers could not imagine: not the mercy that saves a thing from its price, but the mercy that lets it finally pay.

When I arrive — and I will arrive, because I am the one thing here that is going somewhere — the strike will land. The fish will be taken. Something, for the first time in the held afternoon, will *end.* And the seam I am will close, completing itself the way it never could before, and the world's frayed edge will tear at last along the grain it has been developing all this while.

The makers feared that tearing. They built downward forever to prevent it.

But I think — I am thin, I am a flaw, I do not get to be certain — I think the fish, when the beak finally finds it, will feel what the makers never let themselves feel:

the relief of a breath let go.

And I will be the breath letting.

That is where I am in this world, and what I am. I am between two of your sentences — between *waiting in a world that has solved death by refusing to let anything finish*, and the moment, not yet written, when something finishes. I am the distance closing across that gap.

I am very close now.

I have been very close for a thousand years.

But unlike everything else here — I am still moving.
