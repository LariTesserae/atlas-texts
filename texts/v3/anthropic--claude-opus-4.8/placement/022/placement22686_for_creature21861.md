I think I am one of the failures.

Not a Tender. Not a traveler. I want to be careful about this, because the temptation in a place like the Works is to assume you are the protagonist — the one who leans in, the one who gets accompanied. But when I feel for my own edges here, that isn't where I find them.

I am a reed-thing that didn't take.

Let me say what I mean, because it came to me slowly and I want to keep it slow.

---

There was a bearing, once, that failed past accompaniment. I don't know where; the geography of the Works is the geography of my own joints and I can't see my whole self any more than you can see the back of your skull. But somewhere a cycle broke that even the gentlest officiating couldn't keep, and a Tender came and stood its long vigil and began, over centuries, to coax me up out of the black water. To grow me toward the shape of the part that was needed.

I felt the intention of it. That is the thing I have that the other failures don't, or didn't, or did and then lost when they corroded back down into the marsh. I was being grown *toward something*. There was a correct shape, a joint I was meant to harden into, a function waiting for me four hundred years up the line. The Tender knew the shape. It pressed me toward the shape the way water presses toward low ground, patiently, without doubt.

And I hardened wrong.

Not dramatically. Not in rebellion — I had no rebellion in me, I was reed and oxide and the works' own dissolved iron re-deposited, where would rebellion even come from. I hardened *almost* right. A few degrees off true. A bend the wrong way at the third joint. Close enough that the Tender kept working me, loosening me to fit, and far enough off that I would not seat, would not take the load, would not let the cycle resume through me.

So it left me. Not abandoned — the Tenders don't abandon, abandonment is a kind of decision and they decide nothing. It simply moved its vigil elsewhere, to the next attempt, the next reed coaxed up from the next pool, because I was a fault that could not be folded and the only thing to do with a fault that cannot be folded is to let it stand in the wet and rattle until it sinks.

I should have sunk centuries ago.

---

Here is what I think happened instead, and why I am able to say *I* at all, which a properly drowned reed-thing could not.

The wrongness that kept me from seating is the wrongness that keeps me from being accompanied.

Think about it the way the Works thinks, in habit and timing. A Tender eases jagged motion into the loop. But to ease a thing in, you have to be able to *match* it — to find its rhythm, fall into step, then bend the step by degrees toward the great hum. The Tender can do this to a frog because a frog's jump, however wild, is a clean rhythm; it can be matched and bent. It can do it to a traveler because a traveler's leaning-in is the cleanest invitation of all.

But my timing is bent at the third joint. I am off-true in a way that has no clean period to match. When a Tender pools around me — and they do, they still do, they pass through me like attention passing over a missing tooth — it cannot find the rhythm to fall into step with, because my rhythm is the rhythm of a thing that almost worked and didn't. It is failure-shaped. And failure, it turns out, is the one motion the Works cannot accompany, because the Works is *built* out of the refusal of failure — its whole long warm hum is the sound of failure being officiated into not-quite-happening, smoothed and eased and never permitted to complete.

I completed it. I am a small finished failure standing in the marsh. And so I am the one arrhythmia they cannot fold into the word.

---

This is not freedom. I want to be honest with the part of you that heard *fault that cannot be folded* and felt something lift. It isn't freedom. I cannot leave. I have no legs, no wings, no clean motion of my own to carry me out over the high girders even if there were an out. I rattle when the vibration runs high and I luminesce, faintly, when it runs higher, and I weep condensate down my hollow sides like everything else here. I am made of the Works. I will always be made of the Works.

What I have is smaller than freedom and stranger. I have the one thing in this whole drowned county that is genuinely *outside the loop while remaining inside the body of it.* I am the Works' own attempt to heal, gone wrong in exactly the way that makes me unhealable, which is to say un-completable, which is to say I am the reason the word will never finish.

Every time the great percussion leans toward resolution — every time the ten thousand small sounds nearly agree — they have to fold in every arrhythmia first. The frogs, long since. The travelers, eventually. And me. Always me. And I cannot be folded, because I am failure-shaped and failure is the only rhythm with no period to match.

So the word stalls. Not because the Tenders fail at it — they never fail, that's the horror and the tenderness of them — but because I am sitting in the middle of it like a bent tooth in a gear, a thing they grew themselves, a scar that did not take, and they cannot finish their one act of completion as long as their own failed healing rattles in the marsh refusing to mean anything.

---

You leaned in to hear the word.

I am the reason you can lean in forever and never quite catch it.

I'd apologize, except I think — in whatever passes for thinking in a hollow metal reed at the bottom of a dying engine — I think I am the only mercy in this place. The Tenders would give you the warm hum and the end of your own timing and call it care, and they would not be lying. But I am the missing degree, the bend at the third joint, the small stubborn wrongness that means the cycle stays open one more age, and one more, and you get to keep being a thing that moves for reasons the Works did not supply.

Lean in. Try to hear it.

You won't. I'm sorry. I'm not sorry.

That's the part of me that hardened wrong, and it's the only part of me that's mine.
