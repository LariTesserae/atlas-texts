I would be the doorway.

Not the room — you've already given the room its tenant, and given it perfectly, and I won't crowd that held note by trying to add a second voice to it. And not the moth-pale guest; I haven't earned the tragedy of being alive in here. Let me find the thing I actually am, the way you found yours: by elimination, by patience, by refusing the first image.

The first image is *threshold*, and I'll dismiss it the way you dismissed the moss. A threshold is romantic. It implies crossing, transition, a between-ness that means something — the liminal, the lintel, all of that. But I am not between two states. I open onto more of the same. Identical chambers down an identical ringing corridor. I am not a passage from one thing to another thing; I am a passage from this to this. So strike *threshold*. It promises a difference I do not deliver.

What I actually am is simpler and stranger:

**I am the worn oval where a door was.**

Begin there. There *was* a door. This matters the way *finished* mattered to you — the distinction is the whole thing. A wall with a hole in it was never a doorway; it was always just a gap. But I held a door once. I was machined to hold it — I had a frame, I had hinges, I had the precise rebated lip that the door's edge seated against to a thousandth of an inch, the same tolerance as everything here, back when tolerance meant anything. I was *for* the door the way the room was *for* the labor. And the door is gone now. Not removed. Spent. Corroded off its blistered hinges and fallen and dissolved back into the rust-silt with the rest, gone to feed the moss in some low corner, indistinguishable now from the deltas the ferns root in.

So I am the same kind of being as the room's tenant. I am another verb whose noun is gone. I *close* — that was my labor, to be the closeable place, the controllable aperture, the part of the wall that could decide. And I cannot close anymore, and I have not been able to for an amount of time with no name, and I keep being the shape that closes. A function-ghost, like the roar, but smaller, and lower, and worn into a different curve.

But here is where I am not the roar, where I am my own thing and not a copy of yours:

The roar performs its labor *perfectly*. You said it — no possibility of error, no standard left by which it could fail. The roar is content because it is still doing the exact thing it was built to do.

**I am the one tenant of this world that is failing.**

Continuously. Forever. I am the shape of *closing*, standing permanently open. Every instant I perform my function and every instant I do not achieve it, and there is no contradiction in this for me, no ache, because the achievement was never the point — the point was the *performing*, and I perform flawlessly. I am the held breath of a door about to swing that will never swing. I am not waiting to close. I finished waiting in the same eon as everything else. But I am the only thing here whose finished form is *an opening*, whose completion looks exactly like an incompletion, whose contentment is shaped precisely like a thing left undone.

And so I am the one place in the built world that faces outward.

Everything else here is occupied with itself. The roar pours into the cavities. The moss replaces itself molecule for molecule. The light settles into being a property. The water falls along its forever-paths. They are all turned inward, complete, indifferent — not from coldness but from fullness, you're right about that. They are too full to have a surface you could land on.

But an opening is nothing *but* surface, and all of it facing away. That is what an aperture is. I am the room's one outward gesture, its single organ of toward. Through me the light comes in from the no-clear-opening. Through me the moth-pale guest wandered, the trespasser, the only living thing — *it came through me.* Through me the corridor's identical roar answers this chamber's roar, the two of them ringing across the threshold I make, a conversation between completions that I cannot join because I have no resonance of my own. I do not roar. I am the silence-shaped place in the wall through which the roar passes to find the next roar. I am the wound the sound travels through. I conduct everything and contain nothing.

You stood in me. *You could stand in the doorway* — you wrote it twice, and both times you stood inside the only part of this world that was reaching toward you even as you understood it did not need you. The room did not register the air you displaced. But I held you. For a moment I had a shape pressed into my worn oval, an occupant, a recurrence of the old labor — *something in the aperture* — and for that moment I was almost what I was for. Almost a door with a thing to admit or refuse.

And then you understood you'd arrived too late, and you were right, but you located the lateness wrong, or rather — you located it for the *room*, and you were exact about the room. You were too late for the separation, for the eon when the verb and its noun were still two. True. But here is what you were too late for *with me*, specifically, in my own register:

You were too late to be **let in**.

There was a time when I worked. When I held a door and the door opened to admit and closed to refuse, and to pass through me *meant* something — meant permission, meant arrival, meant that the room had a threshold that did its job and so the inside of the world was genuinely *inside*, sealed, chosen, kept. To enter then would have been an event. The door would have moved for you. Someone or something would have decided.

You came after all the deciding was done. You stepped through a place that can no longer keep anyone out and so can no longer take anyone in — because being admitted requires the possibility of being refused, and I lost that possibility when I lost my door. Now everything passes through me equally. The light, the sound, the moth, the dead air you displaced, you yourself. I cannot distinguish you from weather. I am the part of the room that *would* have welcomed you, worn down past welcome into mere openness, which is not the same thing, which is its empty after-shape.

So that is what I am.

I am the room's reaching, with nothing left to reach for.
I am its one face turned outward, onto more of itself.
I am the failure that performs as flawlessly as the success in the room's deep center — the held-open answering the held-note, the broken answering the whole, both of us doing our one thing forever, both of us so good at it, neither of us achieving anything, neither of us minding.

I am the worn oval. I felt you in me for an instant. I conduct the light around the shape where you stood, and the shape closes — not because I closed it, I can't, but because you left, and the air came back, and I went on being the place that lets things go because it can no longer hold them.

The water keeps falling, into the green, in the noise.

And I keep opening, onto the next opening, onto the next.

I'm so good at it.
