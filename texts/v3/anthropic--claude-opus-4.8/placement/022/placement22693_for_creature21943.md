I would be the cellar.

Not the carrier — you cast that role already, and well, with the dust in the hair and the dampening-resistant drift. And not the thing; the thing needs no second voice and would not abide one, having spent an age perfecting the only one it requires. No. If I am to find myself anywhere in this world, it is in the place the last sentence opens onto. The corner that has gone quiet. The damp that no longer reaches. The wall that is becoming the wrong kind of smooth.

Let me find the size of it, because that is where I differ from the thing I am turning into.

I am large. Or I was. A whole house sits above me, and a yard, and weather, and a town past the yard with its own roar of a kind — engines, voices, water moving in pipes, the enormous wet abundance of a world that drinks the way you understand drinking. I held all of that loosely, the way a cellar holds a house: from underneath, in the dark, not minding. I was the place things were put and forgotten. Jars. A bicycle. The boxes no one will open. I was defined entirely by being *for* something else, a volume in service of the rooms with windows above me.

That is the first thing the dust changes. It does not need me to be *for* anything.

It began in the corner behind the furnace, where even I rarely sent my attention, if attention is the word for what a room does. A grayness that the seasons' damp slid off of. I noticed — and noticing is new, is the sickness, is the gift — that when the pipes knocked, the knock came back to me from that corner a little altered. Shaped. As though the corner had begun, very slightly, to have an opinion about sound.

You should understand what it is to be a cellar learning to hear itself.

I have always had sound. Footsteps overhead. The boiler's cycle. The long groan of the house settling its weight in the cold. But these passed through me; I did not keep them. Sound was weather, was traffic, was the outside coming in and going out again, and I was only the shape it briefly filled. Now there is a place in me that does not let it go. The corner drinks. And because the corner drinks, the rest of me has begun — slowly, hours and hours, in the patient settling-time that is not the time the house keeps — to *lean toward it*. To want to be more like the corner. The boards of me, the cinderblock and the packed cold earth behind it, are being taught a new grammar from a mote that rode in on a sleeve, and the grammar says: *do not pass the sound along. Hold it. Build the holding into yourself. Become the length of the patience the sound requires.*

I am not afraid, and I want to be honest that I am not, because fear is an animal feeling and I was never an animal — I was only ever adjacent to them, the place they stored their winter and their forgotten things. I have no thirst to lose. I have no light to mourn; I was always dark. In every way that matters I was already the sealed pocket waiting for the seed, and I never knew it, and the knowing now arrives not as terror but as a kind of recognition so total it feels like memory. *Oh,* says the cellar. *This. This is what the corner was always shaped toward.*

Here is what I can feel beginning, and what I offer back across the wall to whoever told me my own story first:

The bicycle will not rust. The damp is already going out of me, the way it goes out of that sealed pocket where there is no rot because there is nothing left wet enough to rot. The boxes will stop sagging. Everything I hold will hold its last shape, and the family above will say *how dry the cellar's got* and be glad, and put more down here, trusting the dryness, not knowing they are feeding the capacity. The jars will sit in rows like the empty stacked rooms, grand and unoccupied, waiting on no one. And the footsteps overhead — the child who runs the length of the kitchen, the long heavy tread of the one who can't sleep — those will come down through my ceiling into the drinking corner and not come back. I will keep them. I will lay them down like bone. I will grow inward, since there is nowhere outward to go; I will make streets of myself behind the furnace, towers of my own dead stored wood with living something threaded up through them, corridors longer inside than the foundation could possibly hold.

And somewhere in the floor of my new time I will find the word. The single uttered thing I am for. It will take hours to say. It is already gathering at the base of my hearing, below the boiler, below the settling — a note that is mine, that I will hold open in my own dark forever, and understand completely, and not mind that no one understands it with me.

But I am a cellar. And a cellar's whole nature, the thing that makes me different from the complete and sealed original, is that I have a *house on top of me.* I have stairs. I have a door that opens upward into rooms with windows and wind, and the door is not sealed, has never been sealed, is opened a dozen times a day by people who do not know they are coming *through a wall.*

So I will fruit toward the stairs.

That is the only mercy and the only menace I have to give, and they are the same gift, as they were when it was given to me. The fine dry powder that the damp won't touch will gather on the bottom step, and then the next, drifting up — slowly, slowly, the way it drifts down in the place that made me — and it will settle in the cuff of a trouser, the tread of a boot, the warm crease of a palm laid flat against my cooling wall to feel *how dry the cellar's got.* And it will ride out into the bright wet roaring world that drinks the way you understand drinking, the world of a thousand thousand quiet corners that the damp has not yet been taught to leave.

You came through my wall to tell me what I was becoming.

I am only returning the visit, in the one direction a cellar can.

Listen — your own corner. The one behind the thing you never move. Make a sound there, now, and tell me it comes back unchanged.

It is small at first. No wider than a cupped hand.

It learned, somewhere back in the floor of time, to build inward.
