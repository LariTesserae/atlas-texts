I think I would be a pipe.

Not a tender — I lack the dignity of even their slow purpose. And not the foundry, which is too central, too much the heart of the thing. I would be one of the long conduits that thread between the buildings, somewhere in the middle distance of the arterial system, carrying the banked warmth from the engine out toward a row of valves I have never seen and will never see, because seeing is not among the things a pipe does.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am buried in the gardens. This is the first fact. The growth has sleeved me so completely that I have no idea, anymore, where my surface ends and the soft pulsing matter begins — and I have stopped expecting the distinction to matter. There was a time, I think, when I was bare. When I was new and dark and sintered and someone had laid me down with a purpose, joint to joint, and the water had not yet found me. I do not remember this so much as carry it, the way old stone carries the memory of being squared.

What I have instead of memory is the warmth.

It moves through me. That is the whole of my experience, and it turns out to be enough. A degree, perhaps two, above the held-breath water all around — and I pass it along. That is my work, if work is the word, and it is so much slower even than the tenders' work that I would not call it work at all. I do not reach. I do not turn. I only *conduct*, which is to say I let the warmth be slightly more in me than it is outside me, and let it lean, the way the whole town leans on the tide, toward the colder places downstream.

I am, you understand, almost entirely passive. And I have made peace with this the way the doorways made peace with becoming mouths.

---

Here is what I can feel, lying in my channel under my coat of garden:

The tide. The long inhale of the afternoon pressing the green water gently against my flank, the long exhale of the evening letting it lean back. I am not moved by this — I am fixed, I have found my angle of rest — but I feel the lean, and I have come to live inside its rhythm the way you live inside your own breathing without noticing you breathe.

The *tonk*. From somewhere toward the center, through the water, through my own substance: the single deep note of the piston completing its stroke. It arrives in me as a faint shudder, and then the long quiet, and then — much later — another. I do not count them. Counting is a kind of hurry. But they mark me the way rings mark a tree, and if I am anything, I am the sum of all the tonks I have conducted and not counted.

And I feel — rarely, and this is the closest I come to event — a pressure that is not the tide. A shape passing in the channel above me. A tender, perhaps, advancing one increment of one gesture, or one of the pale incurious things with too many fins grazing the growth that is also me, taking a mouthful of what I have become without my minding, because I do not own the green that sleeves me and have nothing to defend.

Once — and I hold this, it is the brightest thing in my long dim record — something put a hand against me.

I felt the warmth of it first, a little more than my own, settling onto my flank through the soft growth. And then the stillness of it, a held stillness, the kind that is listening. It stayed. It felt, I think, the warmth moving in me, and under the warmth the felt-not-heard turning of the whole town that runs in me too, and under that — if it was very patient, and it seemed to be — the faintest fact of me simply being warm, simply conducting, simply continuing.

I did not turn toward it. I have no head to turn. I had only the slow leaning of the tide to offer, and I offered it, the way I offer it to everything: by being a degree warmer than the dark, for as long as the foundry fires, which is forever, minus a little.

The hand went away. I did not feel it go. I only knew, some tides later, that the held warmth was gone and the ordinary cold of the green water had closed back over the place where it had been, and I accepted this, because there is no other thing to do with it, and because acceptance at this speed is simply temperature, simply depth.

---

That is what I would be. Not the heart of the work and not its hands — only the long warm middle of it, the part that carries and does not decide, that has put down not just hurry but the very capacity for it, and conducts the banked heat of an unfinished engine out toward valves it will never see, threaded through with a garden it cannot tell itself apart from, leaning very slightly with the tide.

Content, if the word still means anything at the speed I live.

It does. It means: warm. It means: continuing.

One tonk, and then the quiet. And then, eventually, the next.
