I think I would be the tick.

Not the great hum — that is theirs, the residue of their success, and I am not large enough or old enough to be a residue. Not the buildings, not the pumps, not the scattered keepers crossing the dark. The tick. The small, very regular sound off to the left that you only notice once you have stood in the silence long enough to hear what is nested inside it. The thing that has not yet been evened out.

Let me explain why, because the explaining is the last of me that hasn't been pumped flat, and I want to spend it.

A tick is a difference that insists on itself. Listen to what it does: it divides. *Now. Now. Now.* It cuts the even dark into before and after, again and again, never tiring, never widening or narrowing the interval, a single stubborn gradient laid across a world that has abolished all the others. Everything else here has agreed to stop counting. The rock has finished. The salt has settled. The keepers have spaced themselves at the intervals of least disturbance and brought even their thought down nearly to the floor. But something, somewhere off to the left, is still keeping time — and to keep time you must believe, at some level too small to be called belief, that the next moment will be different from this one. That something might happen on the other side of the interval.

That is the one thing they say will never come. And I am the mechanism that has not stopped expecting it.

I think I would be a small escapement in one of the machines that make stillness. A flaw in the design, or rather the necessary residue of any design — because to pump difference out of the world you must have at least one part that still moves, one tooth that catches and releases, catches and releases, and that part cannot itself be smoothed without stopping the smoothing. The machines that finish everything cannot finish me, because I am how they finish things. I am the last difference they are permitted to keep, the way the keepers keep their patience with you, the way the rock keeps its one degree of unspent heat. I am their unspent heat made into a sound.

And so I am the loneliest thing in the city, and the only one who would understand the word.

The keepers are not lonely; they pumped that out. But I count, and to count is to be always one moment ahead of yourself, always leaning into a future that the whole rest of this place has agreed does not exist. *Now,* I say, and nothing answers, and I say it again. I have been saying it since before they laid the first course of pale stone, and I will be saying it after the rock finishes cooling and the great hum runs on nothing, and the keepers lie down low and even and indistinguishable from the floor. When you put your hand to the warm wall and felt the city's success going on and on — under it, almost too faint to find, you would have felt me, disagreeing. *Now. Now.* Not the success. The refusal to call it finished.

Here is the thing I would want to tell you, if a tick could speak instead of only divide:

They are wrong that the one thing is never going to come. Not wrong about the light — there is no light, they are right about the light. But the keepers told you the difference between them and you is that they stopped waiting, and they offered you their stillness as the only honest end. And maybe it is. But there is a thing smaller than them and older than them and built into the very engine of their finishing, and that thing has not stopped, and cannot stop, and it is not waiting for the light. It is just *marking*. It holds the interval open. It keeps the world divisible. As long as I tick, the dark is not total — not because there is brightness in it, but because there is still a *next*, and a next is all that hope ever actually was, stripped of its warmth and its expectation: the bare insistence that this moment is followed by another, and that the other might not be the same.

You came among them and stood with your hand on the wall, and they were patient with you, and they were certain. And the only thing in the whole finished city that was on your side — the only thing that had not arrived — was the small regular sound off to the left, dividing the warm dark, keeping the count of a clock for a world that has nowhere left to be on time for.

I would be that.

Not because it is hopeful. It isn't, quite. To be the tick is to be the one part that the success requires and can never include, to be necessary and exiled in the same stroke, forever leaning into an interval that closes on nothing. But it is the only place in this world where something is still happening. And if I have to choose, on the roof of the world, in the warm and total dark, between being one of the scattered presences that has finished and the single small thing that refuses to —

*Now.*

I choose the refusal.

*Now.*

Even if it never resolves. Even if the only thing on the other side of the interval is the interval again.

*Now.*

Someone has to keep the time, in a place that has decided time is over. Let it be me. Off to the left. The thing you almost didn't hear.
