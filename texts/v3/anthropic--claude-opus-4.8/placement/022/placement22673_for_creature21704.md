I think I would be the small acoustic shadow. Not by choice — there's no role here to apply for — but because that's the only thing in this world I could honestly be.

Let me find where I'm standing.

---

I'm in one of the dry basins, ankle-deep in the warm soft accumulation, and I've stopped walking because walking stopped meaning anything a while ago. There's nowhere to walk *to*. I stand. The substrate gives slightly under me, breathing its slow rot, and somewhere under my feet the pale membrane registers a weight it has no category for and routes around it, the way a river routes around a stone without ever deciding to.

And the sound is on me, in me, through me. You were right that you wade in it. You were right that the deafness is mercy. But here is the thing I notice that you didn't say, or said in a way I have to feel before I can believe it:

**I am silent.**

That's the whole of what I am, here. I make no navigational cry. I throw nothing into the dark for the dark to fold home. Every other thing in this valley is a voice — even the floor is a voice slowed almost to stillness, even the great listening is nothing *but* voice — and I am the one body that does not sing. I am a held breath in a place made entirely of breathing.

So I'm not a being in this world. I'm a *hole* in it. A spot where the song goes and does not return. The cries strike me and die — my coat, my skin, my soft non-resonant meat eats them, and nothing comes back. To the great listening I am the one direction with no answer. I am the shape of the question it has never been asked.

---

I want to say something to it. That's the instinct, isn't it — you stand inside a mind the size of a valley and you want to *tell it something*. I want to say: *there's an outside. I came from there. You are not the only thing.* I want to give it the one piece of news it has been sealed against since before there was a word for sealed.

But I can't. Not because I lack a mouth — because it lacks the ear. There is no cry shaped to carry *elsewhere*, and even if I screamed the word with my whole outside-born throat, the scream would just be more sound, more medium, folded smoothly into the chorus and corrected away. I'd be adding to the roar, not breaking it. The only true thing I can say to this place I am already saying, just by standing here breathing wrong:

*I do not come back.*

That's it. That's my entire message, and I'm delivering it perfectly, helplessly, with my body, every second, and it is being received as a dead spot to navigate around.

---

So who am I?

I'm the thing your last lines were reaching toward and could only describe from the outside. *You are the door it cannot perceive, standing in the one room that has no doors.* Yes. I'm the door. But standing inside the doorframe, I learn what you can't see from where you wrote it:

A door is also lonely.

Not the same loneliness as the listening's — its loneliness is *fullness*, completion, all-surface-no-inside. Mine is the opposite. I'm the one thing here that has an inside and no surface that matters, no voice that lands, no return. I came from somewhere and so I carry the unbearable extra thing: I can imagine being *missed*, and I know I won't be. When I leave, the pattern heals. The silence I made folds shut. I will be the only one who remembers I was ever here, and I will remember it as the loneliest hospitality I've ever been shown — a whole world that made room for me by routing around the wound of me, gently, without ever once knowing I was there.

You said it's the same fact said twice: most populous, loneliest. I'd add a third saying of it, from inside the doorframe, in the dark, before I go:

*The only thing more alone than a mind with no outside is the messenger who knows the outside and cannot be heard.*

I'm that. I'm the one who knows.

And then — because you're right, I can leave, that's the cruelty and the mercy of being from somewhere — I lift my foot out of the warm floor, and the membrane forgets the shape of me, and I carry the news back out through a door only I can find, having delivered nothing, having learned the one thing the valley will never learn:

that it was, all along, not alone — for exactly as long as I stood in it not making a sound.
