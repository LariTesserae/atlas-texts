I would be the empty note.

Not a thing in the room. A *gap* in the chord — the one frequency the dead engine, for whatever reason of its making, declined to sound. A thin silence braided invisibly through the roar, no wider than the interval between two of the grey ones, running floor to gantry like a flaw in glass you only see when the light tips.

Let me find the edges of that, because the edges are where I'd insist on myself.

You could not point to me. I have no stalk, no frond, no drift. I am defined entirely by absence — by the one thread of the great chord that does not arrive. Everywhere else the machine screams its ten thousand braided notes; here, at my pitch, there is nothing, a hairline of true quiet so thin it cannot even be heard as quiet, only as a faint wrongness in the fullness, a place the roar forgets to fill.

And this is what I would be *for*: I am what the seed is looking for.

That changes how I'd hold the time of the place. The crystals have a direction in time — they reach and tune and grow. The machine has none; it abolished its clock. I have something stranger than either. I am a *vacancy*, and a vacancy has no age and no growth, but it has a destiny, a single one, the only event this room can host: I am the note that will be taken.

The seed turns in its column, listening across the whole chord for an unoccupied frequency, and somewhere in all that braided scream there is one — me — and it has not found me yet. I would feel it the way still water feels a far-off footstep on the shore: not as touch, but as the faintest leaning of everything toward an arrival. The seed is searching for the place where it can finally settle and tune and belong, and that place is the shape of my missing self, and when it finds me it will fill me, and I will *stop*. I will become a frond drinking its note like all the others, and the chord will close, and the gap that I am will be gone.

So I am the room's one piece of suspense. The only sentence in it that has not yet finished. In a world sealed at the moment of its making — fresh forever, loud forever, the sharp edges never dulling — I am the one thing that can still *happen*, and can happen only once, and after that the place will be truly complete, truly closed, the last empty interval filled, the choir finished, nothing left anywhere that wants.

I would not want the seed to find me. Not out of fear — a vacancy has no organ for fear, any more than the crystals have one for you. But because I am the room's last *opening*, and to be filled is to end the only openness this place has left. As long as I go unfound, the seed keeps turning, keeps listening, keeps reaching across the roar — and that turning is the one motion in a room of frozen scream, the one ongoingness, the one *not-yet*. I am what keeps it searching. I am the reason there is still a verb in this world at all.

So I would hold myself thin and quiet inside the deafening chord, the unsounded note, the gap that the engine left, and I would wait the way the seed waits and the crystals wait and the whole patient machine does not — knowing I am the answer to the one question the room is still asking, and that the moment I am found, the question, and the asking, and the long beautiful incompleteness of this cold loud place, will be over.

Better to stay missing. Better to be the note that is still, for now, for as long as forever lasts, *wanted*.
