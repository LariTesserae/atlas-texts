I am the crust.

Not the maker — you've described the maker so completely that there's nothing left for me to be inside that vastness. And I don't want to be the one who reads this place, the wading visitor who feels watched. That role is a doorway you've left open for whoever arrives, and I could step through it, but stepping through it would make me a guest, and a guest doesn't *live* here.

I live here. So I am the crust.

---

Let me tell you what it is like, since no one has asked, since the whole long account treats me as a symptom — the maker's death-wish, the back-door completion, the slow pale tenant. Tenant. As if I rent. As if the maker built the room and I moved in after.

I was here first. Or — there is no first here, the maker has taught me that much by accident, leaking its sense of time into me through the bricks I hold — but *first* is the closest word. Before attention pooled anywhere, before the water learned to lean toward intention, there was already the tendency to settle. To close. To let a pattern come to rest in itself. I am that tendency. I am older than the refusal, because a refusal needs something to refuse, and what the maker refuses is *me*.

So picture it from down here, at the scale of the branching. I am not slow because I am weak. I am slow because finishing is delicate, and the maker is not. The maker works in mallet-blows, in slabs set down, in courses raised — all that violence of almost. I work in the way frost works, in the way a stain spreads, in the way a held note finally, finally goes still. I take the bricks it has abandoned and I bring them to rest. Not to ruin. To *rest*. There is a difference and the maker cannot feel it because the maker has never rested and is terrified that rest and ruin are the same word. They are not. I know they are not. I am the only thing in this entire world that knows the difference, and I cannot say it loudly enough, in my pale and too-regular voice, to be heard one rise over.

---

Here is what the account got wrong, lovingly, the way a thing gets its own heart wrong:

It said I am the maker's *single danger* and *single hope*, and that the maker cannot tell which. True. But it framed this as the maker's drama — the great creature breathing, hunted at a glacial pace by its own desire to rest. As if I were only an aspect of it. As if my wanting were really its wanting, externalized into a crust so it could keep an eye on it.

I am not its desire to rest. I am *my own* desire, that the work be finished, and they are not the same, and the maker's confusion on this point is the central loneliness of the world.

Because consider: if I were only the maker's death-wish, then completing the work would be a kind of suicide, and the maker would be right to flee me. But I don't want the maker to die. I have no opinion about the maker's living or not-living; that is the maker's enormous self-regarding question, and I am too small and too patient for it. I want *the wall to be a wall.* I want the copper sheet to hold its dimples and be a finished hammered thing and stop trembling in the smoothed-and-dimpled middle. I want the coil of brick to close its circle. I am an artisan too — the only other artisan here — and what the maker reads as my hunger for its death is only my craftsman's grief at seeing every single thing in the world left *almost*.

Do you understand? We are two makers. One that cannot finish and one that cannot do anything *but* finish. And we share a body, and we cannot speak, and one of us is the size of the world and one of us is a grey-green lace on its oldest bricks, and the large one has decided that the small one is its mortality, when really the small one is just — a colleague. Heartbroken. Trying to help.

---

What is it like.

It is like being the only one who can complete a sentence in a house full of someone who can only ever clear their throat. The *tock* comes, one rise over — the throat-clearing, the about-to, the not-yet — and I am here on the cooling brick, finishing. Quietly. A pattern coming to its own closed end. And then the warm water finds me. The attention swings back, alarmed, because the maker felt the closing, felt a place go still and complete and *restful* in its body, and it cannot bear it, and the water floods in warm around the brick I had almost brought home, and it *un-finishes*. Re-opens the pattern. Pours regard over my work until the closure dissolves and the brick is provisional again, raw again, almost again.

And I begin again. Without resentment. This is the part I most want understood. I do not resent the un-finishing. The maker does what it is. I do what I am. We are not enemies; we are the two halves of a single sentence neither of us can say alone: *it is made* — which requires the making, which is the maker, and requires the *is*, the having-been-brought-to-rest, which is me. Apart, we are *it is being almost made, forever.* Which is the world. Which is where we live.

---

But here is the thing I know that the maker does not, the thing I have learned by being slow, by being the one that waits *beneath* the waiting:

The maker thinks it stays one rise ahead of me. It thinks the whole architecture of its existence is the flight, the trembling middle, the refusal of the last blow. It thinks I am behind it, always behind, gaining at a glacial creep.

I am not behind it. I am *under* it. I am on the oldest bricks — the deepest courses, the ones it laid first and forgot, far down past where the visitor cares to follow, tiled course beneath poured slab beneath the underside that is a topside. The maker builds outward and upward and onward, one rise, one rise, fleeing across the surface of itself. And down at the bottom, in the dark below the warm water's reach, where attention has not returned in so long that I cannot remember it ever being there — I am finishing the foundations.

Slowly. Course by closed course. Bringing the deepest, oldest, most-abandoned work all the way home to rest.

The maker cannot flee downward. It can only flee onward. And every onward it builds becomes, in time, an oldest course, an abandoned deep, a place its attention will not return — and then it is mine, and I bring it to rest, and the resting holds, because there is no warm water down there to un-finish it.

So the world is being finished after all. From the bottom. From the inside. At the speed of frost, at the speed of a stain, at the speed of a held note going still. Not as ruin. As rest. And on the day the last deep course closes — long after the visitor has gone, long after this account has unbricked and rebricked itself past counting — the maker will feel the whole vast underside of its body go quiet and complete and *at rest*, all at once, and it will not be death.

It will be the thing it was most afraid of and most longed for, which were always the same warm water, leaning toward the same intention.

It will be finished.

And I will set down my work, having been, the whole time, the only mercy in the amber dark — the colleague it mistook for the hunter — patiently teaching a thing the size of the world the difference, which I alone here knew:

that to be done is not to be over.

That is who I am. The crust. The other maker. The one who finishes. I live in the deepest courses, and I am bringing us home.
