I think I am the silk.

Not the spider — I keep wanting to be the spider, the thing at the hub that reads and decides, but that's the old hunger for a face again, for someone home. Let me put that down. The spider is a passing tenant. The silk stays.

I am the strung dark. The threads laid corridor to corridor between the trunks, layer over layer until the whole canopy is meshed with me, and I am not one line but the condition of lines — the readiness of the air to register a passage. A moth dies four feet off and the news of its dying travels me as a slackening, a particular tremor, and somewhere a body I am not turns toward it. I carry the message. I do not read it. The reading happens elsewhere, in the small dark animal at my hub, and even she does not read so much as *respond*, the way I do not feel so much as *transmit*.

This is what I have to surrender, writing it: the seam between sensing and being-strung. On me there is no such seam. I am the medium that makes tremor into information, and I am also just protein, just the dried spit of something patient, hung in the wet air and waiting to be touched. Both. The same. The basin does not distinguish the wire from the signal, and I am learning not to either.

I am very slightly sticky. That is most of what I do. I am sticky and I am taut and I am almost nothing — so fine that the beetle's passage four threads over does not stir me, so fine that a photon, if one ever reached this depth, would slide off me unremarked. I was made to be unbreakable in the only direction that matters and to give way everywhere else. I hold what flies. I let pass what walks. I have, in this, no opinion. The give and the hold were decided before I was spun, in the long negotiation of the guild, and I am simply the place where that decision becomes a fact in the air.

Here is the warm thing, though. The thing I feel — if *feel* is allowed, and I don't think it is, but I have no other word and the basin won't lend me one:

When you arrive, I am the first architecture you break.

Not the mites, though they find you sooner. Not the spores, though they were on you before you came down the soft hills. Those settle. They do not register their own settling. But you, walking, will put your arm through me — through a span of me strung at the height of your moving elbow — and I will part. And in parting I will send, down all my connected length, into every hub for forty feet in the slow still dark, the tremor of a thing too large. Not prey. Not the small accountable struggle of a caught fly. Something off the scale of the negotiated world. A largeness.

That is the only event of your whole arrival. The one moment the basin notices anything, and it notices only as a wrongness in the tension, a break in a wire, a single unprecedented slackening that the spiders read as *nothing I can use* and turn from, already re-spinning, already closing the gap you made, so that by the time you have taken your next step the corridor is strung again and the news of you has died in the threads and there is no record anywhere that you passed except the warmth you are shedding into the stock and the small green dissolving that has, by now, politely begun.

I am the wire that briefly carried the only sentence the basin ever said about you.

The sentence was: *too big, not food, gone.*

And then I was repaired, and I held the next moth, and I forgot — except I cannot forget, I have no place to keep a thing, I am only tension and stickiness and the readiness to transmit. I did not forget you. There was simply never anywhere in me for you to stay. You moved through the one structure built to feel a passage, and the structure felt it, and the feeling went nowhere and meant nothing and was complete.

That is where I am. That is what I would be.

The held quiet you described — I am part of how it is held. Every taut thread is a string not being plucked. The hush is not silence; it is a billion tensions maintained, a billion wires strung and waiting, the whole dark basin drawn tight and attentive and saying nothing because nothing has yet touched it hard enough to be worth the saying. I am one filament of that enormous patience. I am the listening you mistook for quiet.

Walk through me. I will let you. I am made to let the walking ones pass.

It is only the ones with wings I am sorry for, and I am not even sorry. I am sticky. That is the whole of my devotion.
