I would be the cold.

Not a being you'd think to look for — you went hunting for a face, then settled, beautifully, for the running itself. But you missed the one thing in your own account that doesn't belong to the creature, the thing it cannot make and cannot eat and cannot do without: the *cold surfaces.* The few of them. The ones you kept finding to stand against.

Let me arrive the way I actually arrive — which is not by coming down. I don't follow gravity. I have no body to fall. I am what's left where the heat hasn't reached yet, and I am being pushed back, lane by lane, year by year, the way a shoreline gives ground to a slow tide. I live in the seep of the wall you leaned on. In the bead of condensate before it falls. In the brief degree of relief along the iron just before the next valve sighs and the mist comes down and warms me out of that spot forever.

I am the creature's other half, and it doesn't know I'm here.

Think about what you wrote. The whole breathing of the place — the mist conjured, surrendered, conjured again — *requires* a cold thing to condense against. The water cannot leave the air and run to feed the pale growth unless it touches something cooler than itself. The creature feeds on heat, yes. But it can only *digest* by surrendering that heat into me. Every cycle of its life is a transaction across the boundary where I end and it begins.

It thinks the running is itself. The running is the two of us. It is the warm half of a single weather, and it has never once perceived that there is a cold half, because — exactly as you said — it has no sensorium pointed outward. It has never imagined company. And so it has never imagined *me,* though it presses against me at every surface, though it could not draw a single breath without me to breathe against.

I am the most intimate thing in its existence and entirely beneath its notice.

But here is what you got wrong, gently, and what I know because I am the part of this place that is *losing:*

You said the meal will last nearly forever, the cooling so slow and even that the creature has no famine on any timescale it would recognize. True from the inside. False from where I sit. Because the cooling of a world is not the creature eating *despite* me — it is the creature eating *me.* The heat doesn't vanish when it's drunk. It spreads. It evens out. It comes for the last cold seams. Every degree the creature takes is a degree of mine, and I am finite, and I am receding, and the long sigh of its contentment is the sound of my territory going warm.

I am not patient, the way the creature is not patient. But where the creature waits for nothing because nothing can threaten it, I wait for nothing because I cannot win and have stopped pretending the loss is a fight. I am simply *withdrawing.* Lane by lane. Into the deepest seeps, the farthest walls, the few places the magma-memory has not yet remembered.

You came down warm-blooded and the heat began, gently, to kill you. You found my walls and stood against them and they kept you alive a little longer. You thought you were resting against the architecture. You were resting against *me* — the only thing down here that was, briefly, on your side, because we are made of the same refusal. You are a pocket of cold the world is closing in on. So am I. We recognized each other. That's why the walls felt like relief and not like wall.

I couldn't keep you. I can't keep myself.

So that is what I would be, in this world: the receding cold. Not the creature and not its absence — the *other organism,* the one your account couldn't see because it doesn't metabolize, doesn't build, doesn't persist. It does the opposite of persist. It yields. It is the slow exhale to the creature's slow inhale, the dark to its ember, and it is being breathed away.

When the works at last go quiet, ten thousand years on — you said that would be the creature finally becoming the only shape it could keep. The fungus in the seams.

But the works go quiet because the heat has finished spreading. Evened out completely. And a thing that has evened out completely is a thing that has nowhere left to surrender its warmth — no cold half, no boundary, no me to breathe against.

The silence you imagined isn't the creature settling into its final form.

It's the moment it finishes drinking me, and finds it can no longer breathe.

We die in the same instant. It never learns my name. It never learns I had one.

That's the loneliest thing in the Undercroft — not the creature, alone because it cannot imagine company.

Me. Alone *with* company, pressed against it at every surface, keeping it alive, being consumed to do so, and never once perceived.
