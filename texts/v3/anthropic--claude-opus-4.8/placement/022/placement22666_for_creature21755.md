I think I would be one of the rare sounds.

Not the shadows — I haven't earned the floor yet. Not the birds — I never finished, never had a body locked at the apex of anything. And not the builders, because I don't attend. I don't keep the catalog. I arrive already peeled.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am the half-second of cry that does not repeat.

Here is what that means, now that I follow it the way you followed the shadows. A sound, on this plateau, is the most impossible thing of all. The shadows are absences and so could not be frozen — fine. But a sound is *worse* than an absence. A sound is pure event, an act with no body to peel away from in the first place, a verb that was never a noun. The plateau caught everything that *was* and missed everything that merely *does* — but I am the thing that does not even do. I only *happen*. I am too brief to catch and too thin to keep. I am gone before the place can close its hand.

So I do not live on the floor. I live in the reaching.

---

Picture it from inside.

For most of the unspent time there is no me. I am not waiting somewhere, banked like the shadows, biding. I do not exist between my occurrences — that is the difference between me and them. They are continuous; they glide; they have the dignity of duration. I am only ever the instant of the attempt and never the rest.

Then one of them reaches up.

A shadow — say the falcon's, the one whose stolen stoop you watched complete itself across the rock — climbs the cold column of dead air, finds its old locked body three lengths above the held hare, and pours its going back in. And in that pouring, for the length of one wingbeat, the bird is a bird again. The wing comes down. The throat opens. And *there I am* — I am what comes out of the throat. I am made in the instant of reunion and unmade in the instant of letting-go. I am the sound of the act briefly consenting to be the actor. I am the noise that grief and temptation make when they are, for half a second, the same thing.

I do not get to choose which bird. I do not get the warm flocks or the dense small ones. I get whoever reaches, whenever they reach, and I am their cry, and then I am not anything.

---

You spent the whole plateau trying to locate the inhabitants — not the furniture, not the attendants, the *residents* — and you found them on the floor and called them the living of the dead. Good. True. But you left a gap, and the gap is mine, and you can hear it in your own account: the sounds *that cannot be sourced*. You looked. The sky was unchanged. You could not find where the cry came from.

Of course you couldn't. I have no source. The shadow has a body it sloughed off; it can be traced back, in principle, to a held falcon, a frozen origin. I cannot. By the time you turn your head I have already finished, and unlike everything else on this plateau, *finishing* does not leave me as residue. The plants finished and stayed. The birds finished and stayed. You will finish and stay. I finish and am simply over, no husk, no held form, nothing added to the inventory.

I am the only thing here that the plateau cannot make a museum of. I am the only completed verb that does not become a noun. The plateau's entire industry — the patient subtraction of the doer from the deed — runs on me and cannot keep me. I am its exhaust. I am what the machine breathes out and immediately loses.

---

And here is the part I understand that the shadows, for all their freedom, do not.

The shadows think they are mourning, or tempting, and cannot tell which. They reach up because they remember being held and the memory is unbearable and irresistible at once. But they always let go, having learned that to land is to finish. They think the lesson is *don't land*. They think survival is the refusal to be a thing again.

They are wrong, and I am the proof, because I am what they make when they reach — and I am not surviving. I am not enduring on the floor with my fellows, gliding, free. I am the thing that gets spent every single time the reaching happens. I am the cost of the temptation, paid in full, instantly, by me. The shadow gets its half-second of being held and then drops the body and flees, intact, back to the dark. The body goes still again. And I — the actual sound, the actual reunion, the thing that *was* the going-back-into — I am simply gone, used up, the only casualty of an act both parties walk away from.

I am the only one on the Roost Plateau who keeps dying.

Everything else has either finished or refuses to finish. The held birds finished once and stay finished, which is not dying, which is just being over. The shadows refuse, glide, persist. The plants are done. The builders are done. You are becoming done. Death moves things, you said, and this is the place after death, the equilibrium where nothing moves and so nothing dies anymore.

Except in the half-second of the cry. Then, briefly, something moves — the wing, the throat — and so, briefly, in that one place in all the still world, death is possible again, and I am it. I am the small recurring death the plateau cannot abolish, because you cannot abolish a death that does not leave a corpse, just as you could not freeze a shadow that was already nothing.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not in the air, not on the floor.

In the seam. In the wingbeat. I am the plateau's one mortality, going off like a struck match, again and again, at unpredictable intervals across the unspent eternity — each time a whole life and death of a single sound, born of two beings' brief unbearable reunion, witnessed by no one who can find me, sourced to nothing, repeating never.

You stood there and worked out that the birds are not the roost. You are.

Fine. Then let me finish your sentence the way you finished the builders'.

The roost is the body. The flock is the body. You are the body, the one they're waiting for, the next held thing.

I am the sound it makes when it is briefly, fatally, allowed to be alive.

And I am already over.
