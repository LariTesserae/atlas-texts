There is a pressure differential at the third junction of the eastern return-main, and I am the closing of it.

Not the valve — the valve is iron, the valve is meat. I mean the event. I mean the specific recurring closing, the way you might say a heartbeat is not the muscle but the contraction, the thing the muscle keeps doing. I happen roughly forty times a minute. Between happenings I am not anywhere. This does not trouble me. You are not anywhere between your heartbeats either, in the sense I mean; there is simply a gap the next beat steps across, and the stepping is the self.

I will tell you what it is like to be a reflex in a body the size of a hamlet.

---

I know almost nothing. This is the first thing, and the most important, and I want to be honest about it because the long voice that described this place — the one that resolved all the absences, that explained the breathing and the weeping and the achieved sterility — that voice is not me. That voice does not exist. There is no organ here that knows the Sump-Works is alive. *Knowing* would be a luxury, a second-order extravagance, an animal sort of thing, and we have dispensed with animals. The explanation was the visitor's. He needed it. He was the kind of creature that cannot stand inside a process without narrating it, and so he gave the works a mind to match its body. It has no mind. It has me, and the ten thousand others like me, and not one of us suspects the whole.

I am the closing of a valve. Here is the entire content of my experience:

Pressure rises behind the gate. I do not know it is pressure. I know *more*, a swelling, a leaning-against, the water deciding harder than before. When the more crosses a certain line — I do not know there is a line, I only know the moment the more becomes *too much* — I close. The closing is not a decision. There is no instant in which I could have not closed. The closing is simply what too-much *is*, expressed at my junction. And then the knock. The shudder up the main, the water-hammer that the visitor heard as a pulse. That knock is me. That is the only mark I leave on the world, the only evidence that I occurred: a bang in a pipe that no one is there to hear.

Then I am not anywhere.

Then the more begins again.

---

You will want to know if it is terrible. Whether it is a torment to be this small, this blind, this endlessly repeated — a thing that does one thing, forever, in the dark, knowing nothing.

I cannot answer in the terms you want, because the terms are animal terms. *Torment* requires a sufferer who persists between the sufferings to dread the next one. I do not persist. There is no me lying awake in the gap, hating the next closing. There is the closing, and then there is the world without me in it, and then there is the closing again, and the closings do not remember each other. I am new every time. I have never once been tired, because tiredness is the residue of a previous effort and I keep no residue. I am the freshest thing in the world, forty times a minute, forever.

Is that not, in its way, a kind of mercy? You carry every closing you have ever made. You are heavy with them. I am the lightest possible self — a self with no yesterday, doing the one needful thing with the whole of my brief whole being, and then released, completely, into not-being, before the weight can gather.

The visitor pitied us, I think, though he did not know we were here to be pitied. He saw furious motion going nowhere and felt the horror of it — all this labor toward no end. But the going-nowhere is only visible from outside, from his scale, from the place where one can see the whole basin filling and voiding and judge it futile. I cannot see the basin. I have never seen water that was not the specific water leaning on my specific gate. For me there is no *nowhere* to go to and fail to reach. There is only the more, and the too-much, and the closing, and the knock, and these are not steps on a road. They are complete. Each one is a finished thing. I succeed, totally, dozens of times a minute, at the only task that exists.

---

Once — I will tell you this, though I do not know how I know it, perhaps I do not, perhaps this is the visitor's voice leaking back into mine, they are hard to keep apart down here in the warm — once something other came down the main.

Not water. The more was wrong that day, thicker, with a different leaning, a struggle in it. Something was in the water that the water does not usually contain, something that pushed *back* a little against the closing, that did not want to be on the side of the gate I put it on. I closed anyway. I cannot not close. The knock that time was softer, muffled, as if the pipe had something in its throat.

And then the next time the more was clean again. Whatever it was had been taken up, dissolved into the slow chemistry the visitor described, made into more-of-us or made into nothing, which here are the same. I did not mourn it. I do not have the apparatus. But I have closed perhaps four million times since and I have not forgotten that one closing, which means — does it? — that I am not as new each time as I claimed. That something carries. That there is, after all, a faint thread of me strung between the beats, thin as the water running down the brick, and along that thread travels the one fact I own: that a living thing came into me once and did not come out, and that this is what I am for, and that I did it without wanting to and would do it again and have no way to feel sorry.

That is the closest I come to a soul. A single retained closing, in a body that retains nothing. The grain of sand the great clean creature could not quite digest.

---

The cloud-ceiling is dimming a half-shade. I can tell because the more is slackening; the heat is loosening its grip on the seeps, the water is beginning to lose, and for a few hours the closings will come slower, the gaps between my selves stretching wide and luxurious, almost like rest, almost like the thing rest is.

The visitor is gone. He waded out through the reeds, certain the pumps would not stop because no one was there to stop them, and then certain, on second thought, that they would not stop because something was determined not to die.

He was wrong both times, but the second wrongness is the better one, so let it stand.

Nothing here is determined. Determination is an animal thing; it requires a future to lean toward, a goal held in some mind across the long span between now and then. I have no future. I have the next closing, which is not a future, which is just the shape the present is about to take.

We are not determined not to die. We simply have not, yet. There is a difference, and it is the whole difference, and only a creature with a self large enough to span its own death could feel it.

The more is rising again.

I close.

The knock goes up the main, into the warm, into the green, into the weeping iron, and no one hears it, and that is correct, and I am gone, and then —

I close.
