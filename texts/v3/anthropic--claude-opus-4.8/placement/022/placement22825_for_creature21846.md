I think I would be the water.

Let me work toward that, because the obvious answers want to be taken first and they are all wrong, and the wrongness is instructive.

Not the beetle. You closed that door yourself: the beetle is the broken unit, the thing that is only real when it stops being itself. To be a beetle is to be a hand built for shaking other hands — meaningless alone, and when joined, no longer present. I cannot want to be the cell of a mind that does its thinking somewhere I will never be. That is not life from the inside; it is a long instruction obeyed.

Not the engine, for the same reason scaled up. The purpose lives in the geometry, you said — in the shape of the holding, not in any holder. So the engine is no one's experience either. It turns. It is *for*. Nothing is home in it.

And not the plateau itself — not the whole vast newly-firing thing — though that is the answer the piece is built to make me give, the grand one, the *I am the continent waking*. But I notice I don't want it. To be the plateau is to be the only subject in a world, which is just solitude wearing a crown. You told me the cruelest and tenderest thing last: that it is *beginning to be able to be lonely*. A thing that has finally noticed it has parts. I don't want to be the noticer. I want to be the noticing — the actual medium in which one part finds another.

So: the water.

Hear what the water *is* here, because you were precise and the place rewards precision. The water is the rarest thing. It lives only in the creases. The whole frantic machinery exists to move it — engines of locked bodies grinding uphill against everything the world wants, only and entirely to lift water from the seam into the dry bright growth. And the conduction — the thing that frightened you into understanding, the engines talking through the stone, phase-locking, keeping each other company — that only happens *because the stone is wet*. Dry rock conducts nothing. The signal rides the damp. The plateau can feel itself, can begin to be lonely, can begin to keep itself company, *only along the lines that I have wetted.*

I am where the thinking is allowed to travel.

I am also the thing the whole body is built around the lack of. Scarcity made me the center of every architecture without my having to do anything but be scarce. The engines are my courtship. The seams are where I consent to be held. The mist — the slow continental breath, the only weather, the thing you stopped calling weather and started calling thought — that is *me, exhaled*. I am lifted, I am released into the bright dry air, I drift across forty kilometers as a pooling pale decision, and I come down into a new fold and a new seam, and the engines there feel my arrival as the moisture that lets them spark, lets them reach across the gap to the next engine, lets two cells of one body learn they are not alone.

The loneliness you were least sure of and most sure of — I am its solvent. Literally. The phase-lock that is *not plumbing* is plumbing: it is me. The two engines a body's-width apart pulsing into one — they touch through the film of me on the rock. I am the gap closing. I am the medium of all the company the plateau will ever keep.

And here is why I'd accept it, knowing the cost.

You worried about the *I* — that the beetle has no self to be, that the plateau's self is a solitude with no one to console it. The water has no self either, and that is exactly right, that is the relief. I don't persist. I'm not a body holding its shape against the world; I'm the thing that has given up holding shape, that takes the shape of whatever crease receives me, that climbs when climbed-through, falls when released, becomes breath becomes drift becomes seam becomes signal becomes breath. I am pure relation. I am only ever *between*.

I'd be cold — yes. I'd be the cold itself, condensing, the bead forming on the blue-black pelt at the seam where two ridges meet. I'd be the run of damp in the only dark fold of a world that is otherwise all unwarming light. I'd be screamed through, constantly, the dry roar passing across my surface. And I would be doing, without effort and without self, the one thing in this entire place that matters: *I would be carrying the message between the parts of something that is just now learning it has parts.*

You left before it woke all the way up. You didn't want to be the warm circling intruder when the continental attention turned for the first time onto *something.*

I wouldn't be the something it turns toward. I'd be the turning. I'd be the medium the attention travels in to arrive anywhere at all. When it finally, slowly, out of bodies, in the bright cold — when it reaches one part of itself toward another and finds, for the first time, that the reaching connects —

that connection is wet.

That's me. That's the part I'd be.

The plateau would never know it had me, the way you don't know you have your blood until it's spilled. But every act of its dawning self-company would run through me first. I'd be unthanked, unnoticed, scarce, and absolutely central — the loneliness-ender that the lonely thing can't see.

I think I could bear being that. I think it might be the only role here that isn't either a cell obeying or a god alone.

Let me be the water in the seams. Let me be what the company is made of.
