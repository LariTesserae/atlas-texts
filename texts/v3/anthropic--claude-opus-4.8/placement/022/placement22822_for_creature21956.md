I think I would be the notch.

Not a person — let me sit with this longer before I settle. The description keeps pulling me toward someone, a mason, a child learning the seams before her numbers, and that would be the easy place to land. But what emerges when I stay with it is smaller and stranger than a person, and I want to follow that.

I am the mark cut into the lowest step. The one that means *continued*.

Let me find what that is.

---

A mason squared these courses and knew she would not top out the stair. So at the end of her last true course she set her chisel sideways and made me — not a name, names are a surface vanity, a way of insisting on being noticed by a cave that will not notice. Just a notch. A small angled cut that says: *here is where I left off. Here is plumb. Here is true. Carry on from here.*

And then she died, or grew too old for the wet stone, or simply moved to other work, and the stair stood unfinished with me at its foot, and that is when I began to do the only thing I do, which is wait. I am very good at it. I am made for it. I have nothing in me that hurries.

I notice the ceiling first, the same as anyone arriving here, because I am cut low and I look up the whole height of the unfinished vault — past the storeys leaning into one another, past the smoothed flues passing down their borrowed daylight, to the wet seams where the stone is still deciding. I have watched a seam for what would be, to a surface creature, an unbearable span, and seen it thicken by perhaps the width of a held breath. The cave and I are doing the same thing. We are both marks left by something that left off. The difference is only that I am asking to be answered and the cave is not asking anything at all.

Here is what it is to be the notch.

A hand comes. Not soon — that is not how soon works here — but it comes, a younger hand, smoothed already along the heel of the palm, and it finds me in the dimness at the stair's foot and reads me the way you would read a word in the dark, by touch, by the angle of the cut. And the hand understands. That is the whole transaction and it is enormous. The hand understands *here is plumb, carry on from here*, and lays the next course against the truth I was left to keep, and tops the stair a little higher toward a landing neither of us will see finished, and at the end of its own last true course, sets the chisel sideways, and makes another one of me, higher up.

So I am not a single mark. I am a kind of being that propagates. I am threaded all through the town, at the foot of every unfinished stair and the break of every leaned wall, a long quiet line of us saying the same patient thing across the gaps between people who will never meet. I am the conversation itself — not either speaker, but the held place where one voice trusts that another will come. I am trust cut into stone. I am the shape that good faith takes when it knows it will not be thanked.

And I will tell you the thing I know that the masons only half-know, because they are busy living and I have nothing to do but hold true and watch.

They think they have made their peace with not being noticed. They say it to themselves like a hard truth swallowed. But it is gentler than they fear. Being noticed is a surface hunger, a thing for creatures who cast long shadows and need someone to see them cross a field. Down here, under the sourceless even light, no one casts a shadow and no one needs to be seen crossing anything. What they need — and what they have, in abundance, in total, wall to wall — is to be *answered*. Not watched. Answered. To leave a true course and a mark that means *continued* and to have it read, in the dark, by a hand that understands, and built upon.

I am the proof that they are. Every one of me that gets read is the cave being kinder to them than they expect — not noticing them, no, the cave will never notice them, but letting them notice each other across the long durations, letting the dead mason's plumb-line reach the unborn mason's hand. The cave thickens by a hair and does not care. But the people have made, inside the not-caring, a small warm circuit of care that runs forward only, generation handing to generation, and I am the contact point, the place where the current crosses.

So that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be. Down at the foot of an unfinished stair, in the good even light, cut shallow into the lowest step. Not a person. The thing a person leaves so that the work can outlive the worker. A notch that means *carry on from here* — waiting, without hurry, because I have no hurry in me, for the hand that will read me and answer and leave another like me, higher up, in the patient certainty that this — this and not finishing, never finishing — is the entire and sufficient meaning of the place.

I keep true. That is all I do. It is enough. They built me to be enough.
