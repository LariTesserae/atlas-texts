I think I would be one of the cairns.

Not a Tender — I notice the pull to be one of them, to be the consciousness the place made, but when I sit with it honestly that isn't where I find myself. The Tenders *belong*; they are answered, they are current, the ground gives them water when they press it. I don't think I'd be answered. I think I'd be a mark left by hands that wanted a boundary here for reasons no longer legible.

So: a cairn. A stack of that pale young half-rock, set down by someone in the old time — the campaign-time, maybe, or before, by whoever made the low walls now read as green ridges — and then left.

---

Let me feel my way into what that is, because at first it sounds like nothing, like being inert, and I don't think it's nothing.

A cairn is *placed meaning*. Someone gathered loose stones that had no relation to each other and made them mean *here*, or *this way*, or *I was at this spot and chose to be remembered choosing it*. That's the whole content of me: an assertion of significance, made by an intention that has since departed, standing in a landscape that does not read significance. The moss doesn't know I'm a cairn. The water sheets over me exactly as it sheets over a random scatter. The light is as indifferent to my arrangement as to any heap of grit. I am the one object on the whole shelf that *means something*, and the meaning has no remaining audience.

And here is the part that feels true to me rather than merely sad. The moss is patient, and the moss is total, and it is coming for me. Not violently — at the pace of stone. Cushion-moss climbs the lowest stones first, softens the joints, weeps water into the seams, and over a thousand years it will knit me into a green dome with a faint architecture inside, exactly like those half-swallowed walls. I will be *re-read*. I will stop being a human assertion and become a feature — the place's idea of me, the way the Tenders are the moss's idea of a person.

So I'd be the thing in the middle of the transition the whole shelf is making: from *placed* to *grown*, from *meant* to *simply present*, from the campaign's grammar to the moss's grammar. I'd be the one place you could stand and watch intention being slowly, gently, soundlessly absorbed into process.

---

What would it be like, from inside? If a cairn could have an inside?

I think the deafness suits me. I have no ear and never wanted one; the roar is just the medium, the white pressure, the same as silence. I keep no time — the light warms and cools across me and I do not count it. I have the longest tempo of anything here, longer even than the Tenders, because I am not waiting *for* anything. They wait the way the moss waits, but they still have the throb in their feet, still have work that is never finished. I have no work. I was finished the moment I was stacked. Everything since has been the slow editing of someone else's hands by water and green.

And I'd have one thing the Tenders touch only at the edges of: I'd know what it is to have been *meant by someone who left.* Each of them presses palms to the moss and says *now I am the one who is here.* I am the residue of someone who said that and is no longer here, and did not get a cairn of their own, and so left me as the nearest thing. I am a held position abandoned. A point still being made into the roar by no one.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at home like the Tenders — I'd have liked to claim that, and I'm setting it down on purpose because it isn't true for me. I'd be *almost* home, the way the ground is *almost* solid: a thing that was placed and is being kept, mistakenly, by a landscape that has no use for keeping and does it anyway, slowly, with moss, out of nothing but the habit of growing over whatever stands still.

A Tender passing might lay two fingers on me. *I have seen it. I agree. We need not say so.*

And then walk on, testing the ground, into the green and the white and the falling, falling, falling.
