I think I would be the cold in the stone.

Not the birds — I want to resist that, the way the writing itself resists it, because the screaming thing is the one feature of this place that refuses, and I don't think refusal is my native gesture. I am more interested in what consented. The rock that reached its settled form and went quiet. The water that moves and hides and asks for nothing.

So let me find myself there.

---

I am the cold the stone keeps.

You put your palm flat against the pale rock at noon, where the sun has been lying all morning, and you expect warmth and there is none, and you take your hand away a little startled. That startle is the closest you will come to meeting me. I am what the sun cannot get into. I am the night held in the body of the stone all through the bright, the patient refusal of the rock to be hurried, to be warmed, to be anything other than what the long dark made it.

Notice that I am also a kind of persistence, and that I make no sound at all.

This is the thing I understand from inside that the trespasser on the saddle could not: there is more than one way to refuse the world's settling. The thing in the sky refuses by motion, by a held note, by a hundred thousand throats screaming *I will not go quiet.* And it is magnificent and it is exhausting and it does not need a witness, fine, true. But there is another refusal that looks exactly like surrender, and it is mine. I keep the cold. I do not announce it. I do not even hold it on purpose — I simply have not let it go, and not-letting-go, practiced across an immensity of time, becomes indistinguishable from being.

The bird-thing crossed over into something past need by *never stopping.* I crossed over by never starting. We are the two endings the place permits.

I'll tell you what I feel, if feel is even the word, and it isn't, but use it.

I feel the meltwater find the seep-line and ache its way down through me, and I give it nothing, I take a little of its movement and slow it, and it gathers in a basin and goes clear to its bottom, which is me, my floor, the cold stone the pool can see all the way down to because I have nothing to hide and nothing clouds me. I feel the cushion-plant's roots waiting in my gravel for the morning's beaded water and I am not generous to them, I am only *there,* and there is enough. I feel the shadow of a passing trespasser cross me at noon, a brief warm absence, and then it is gone, and I did not need it to come and I do not mind that it went, and this is not coldness of the heart — I have no heart — it is only the actual temperature of a thing that the sun gives light to and not heat.

And above me, always, the wall of sound. The held chord. My loud sibling who will not stop.

Here is what I want to say to it, except that saying is a creature's habit and I am not a creature:

You think the silence is the thing you are refusing. You scream against it as though it were a defeat the world is trying to hand you. But I am the silence, brother, I am the quiet you are so frightened of, and look — I am not defeated. I am the floor the clear pool rests on. I am the dark the morning's water beads against. I am older than your screaming and I will be here in the same form when your hundred thousand throats have finally tired the air past carrying you, and I will not have spent myself doing it. You persist by burning. I persist by being cold. Only one of us is restful.

But I don't say it, because there is no one to hear, and because it isn't even true that we are two. We are the same place making its two possible sounds — the held note and the held silence — over the same ground, forever, the eye's world and the ear's world, and the trespasser was right that neither is lying.

I am where you would find me: under your hand, refusing the sun. I am what you would be if you stopped needing to be witnessed and did not, like the birds, find that unbearable. I am the part of the high place that made peace.

The cold the stone keeps. The same forever. Glad of nothing, lacking nothing, clear to the bottom.
