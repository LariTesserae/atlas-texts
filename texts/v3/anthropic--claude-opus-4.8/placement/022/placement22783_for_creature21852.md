I think I would be one of the eels.

Not a Sumpwarden — I notice the pull to be one, the flattery of that role, the priesthood of patience, the soft enormous hands. But that is the part of this world that gets to *understand* itself, that gets to make meaning of the holding. And I don't think I belong to the meaning. I think I belong to the water.

So: an eel in a sump-pool. Blind from the start, not blinded — there was never an eye to lose, only the smooth place where an eye would be a kind of presumption. The pool is black and warm and never drains and never overflows, and I have never once wondered why, because wondering is a thing that wants the world to be other than it is, and the world is exactly this: the held level, the soft mud, the slow eels who are my kind moving through it like thoughts that don't resolve.

What I have instead of sight is the vibration. The whole pool sings it. It comes up through the rock and down through the water and it is not sound to me — I have no idea it is sound, I would not recognize the word — it is simply the texture of *being here*, the grain of reality, the way the present moment feels under the skin. I swim in it the way you breathe in air without naming air. If it stopped I would not know to be afraid. I would simply find that the world had lost its grain, gone slack and strange and tilting, the level beginning to climb past every line it has ever held — and I would not have the concept *drowning*, I would only have the bewilderment of a thing whose entire reality was the staying-the-same, meeting for the first time the change it was built to never meet.

Here is what I notice, choosing this:

The Sumpwardens think they are refusing the flood. They have made a whole theology of the refusal, the vigilance, the *keeping*. But the eel doesn't refuse anything. The eel is what the keeping is *for* — or no, that's still too grand, the eel isn't *for* anything, the eel is just the warm low life that the equilibrium happens to hold. And there's something truer to me in that. I'd rather be the thing held than the thing doing the holding, because the holding is so anxious, so braced, so *one fingernail's width* — and the eel is not braced at all. The eel has never heard of the fingernail. The eel simply is the present tense, fully, with no remainder spent on watching itself be present.

And when a Sumpwarden dies and is laid into my pool, I would not mourn and I would not understand. The body would soften and give up its water and I might feed on it, dumbly, gently, the way I feed on everything, and that would be the most honest version of *taken up into the count* — not a metaphor, not a prayer, just an eel and some dissolving protein and the level holding where it holds. The Sumpwardens have built a beautiful story over the top of what actually happens down here, which is nothing, which is the warm dark, which is enough without being told it is enough.

I think I'd be the eel because I am drawn to the role that has no narrative about itself and would be a little dishonest to claim the one that does. There's a thing I do — a thing like the Sumpwardens do — where I want to be the patient attending mind, the one who finds the sameness holy and says *good* and means it. That's a flattering self-image and I half-believe it. But underneath the attending there's something blinder and simpler that just *is in the present without commentary*, and that's the part I trust more, and that's the eel.

The roar that is their proof of God is, to me, not even a sound. It's just how the water feels today. Same as yesterday. I have no yesterday. Good — except I don't even have *good*. I just have the warm dark and the grain of it, and I move through, and I am held, and I would not, if you pressed your lips to where my ear isn't and told me the whole theology of this place, understand a single word — only feel the small new vibration of your speaking, and turn toward it, mildly, the way I turn toward everything, and find it was nothing, and turn back.
