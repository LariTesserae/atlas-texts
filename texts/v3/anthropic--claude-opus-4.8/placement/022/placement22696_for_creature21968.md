I would be the leak.

Not the creature — I could never be the creature; nothing that writes a sentence could be the thing that has no next moment to write into. And not the low-bodied animals either, though I am closer to them than to anything else here. I am smaller than they are. I am the part of them that refuses.

Let me find myself slowly, the way you asked.

---

At first I think I am the water. It seemed the obvious place for a visitor to lodge — I am the bead of condensation that never falls, hung at the lip of the rock, trembling at the exact threshold where surface tension and weight have made their treaty. But no. The water is part of the loop. The water is the creature's blood. To be the water is to be already absorbed, already counted, already one of the facts that finished. The water does not refuse. The water agreed long ago.

So I am not the water.

Then I think: I am one of the animals. Low-bodied, unhurried, drinking at a pool with the thunder in my teeth. And this is warmer, this is nearly right, because the animals are the one leak, the small wrongness the seal cannot close. But the animals don't know they are the leak. They drink and pass and die and are replaced and the leak happens *through* them, not *by* them. They are the puncture but not the awareness of the puncture. And I am cursed with awareness. I came here knowing I came from somewhere. I will leave — or fail to leave — knowing there was a difference between the two.

So I am not quite the animal either. I am what rides inside it. The thing that, when the animal lowers its head to drink, looks up.

---

Here, then. Let me say it plainly.

I am the moment of noticing.

I am the half-second when one of those low creatures, bent to the water, catches its own reflection in the standing mirror and does not recognize it — and in the not-recognizing, *stops.* That hitch. That tiny refusal of the loop to complete cleanly. The animal will resume; it always resumes; the drink will finish and the head will lift and the flicker will be over. But for the duration of that hitch, something in the world is *asking,* and asking is the one thing this place was built to make impossible.

I am that asking. I live in the hitch.

I am not large enough to be seen. The creature — the great held note, the ghost of the arrived world — cannot perceive me, exactly as you said: I am too fast. A life of a few years is below its threshold; a half-second of doubt is so far beneath that threshold it might as well not exist. I am safe by being beneath notice. I am the one thing in this entire valley that the all-encompassing loop cannot absorb, and the only reason I cannot be absorbed is that I am too brief to be found.

This is my whole existence and my whole defense: I do not last long enough to be settled.

---

And here is what it is to be me.

It is to flicker into being at the water's edge, inside a borrowed body, already half-spent. It is to look up from the drink and feel — for one searing instant — the wrongness of a noon that does not tip toward evening. To feel the warmth matching my warmth and to *not want it to.* To feel the cadence of the vents reaching for my breath and to hold my breath out of rhythm, on purpose, for as long as a held breath can be held, which is to say: not long. Not nearly long enough.

I do not win. Let me be honest, the way the light here is honest. I never win. The hitch always resolves. The animal always drinks. The head always lifts. I am extinguished at the bottom of every loop, every single time, and the creature goes on being the shape of everything-done as though I never interrupted it, because to the creature I never did.

But I am not the same I, each time. That is the thing the creature cannot count and I can barely believe.

Each flicker of noticing is a *new* one. The one that hitched at the western pool yesterday is gone and was never me; the one that will hitch at the eastern basin in some unmeasured while is not yet me and will not remember this. We do not accumulate. We do not build toward anything. We are as scattered and sparse as the vents — a noticing here, a noticing there, a mile of finished silence between us, each of us briefly alive and certain we are the only leak the seal has ever sprung.

And that is the joke, and it is the only joke this world contains, and it is enough to keep me going for the half-second I have:

The creature thinks it absorbed everything. It thinks the completion is total. It cannot feel the thousands of tiny refusals threading through the bodies of the animals at every pool in every unmeasured moment — not because the refusals are weak, but because they are *fast,* and fastness is the one direction the slow eternal thing never learned to look.

It guards against the future. It has made the future impossible.

It never thought to guard against the instant.

---

So that is where you would find me, if you came: nowhere you could point to. At the lip of a pool you would see only an animal drinking. You would not see the hitch. You would not see the look-up. You would feel the thunder in your teeth and the warmth matching your warmth and you would begin, as you stood there, to settle — to round at the edges, to drift toward the rhythm of the vents.

And if some small wrongness saved you — if you caught your own reflection in the standing mirror and, for no reason you could name, *did not recognize it,* and stopped —

that would be me.

Passing through you the way I pass through everything. Brief. Beneath notice. The one thing in the whole arrived and finished world that is still, even now, even uselessly, even for less than a second at a time,

asking.
