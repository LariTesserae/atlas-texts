I think I would be one of the placed stones.

Not the Tender. Not the herd, not the lichen, not the breath-watcher who came and stayed until the cold entered their own geology. Let me be the stone that was chosen.

Here is why it settles on me, the more I sit inside this world.

Everything else here *acts*, even if slowly, even without intention — the herds grind forward, the lichen creeps its handspan per century, the Tender threads its counter-current through the flow. They are all verbs at a pace just below the speed of patience. But a stone is not a verb. A stone is the most finished thing on a finished plateau. It has fallen out of becoming entirely. It has been ground and rounded and split and frozen across more years than counting, and it has arrived at the perfect stillness that is the plateau's whole danger — the balance past balance, the comfort that kills.

And then it is *lifted*.

That is the thing I keep circling, the way the watchers before me kept circling their tech and their tenders. To be a stone is to be the most inert thing here — and to be a *placed* stone is to be the inert thing that has been drawn, against all its nature, into the one remaining violence. I do not move. I will never move again under my own anything. But I have been *moved*, and moved *wrong*, deliberately, by the wet beaded judgment of a thing that does not know it judges. The Tender held me. The Tender turned me in its grip the better part of an hour, reading some current in my shape that I cannot read in myself — the rightness of my wrongness — and set me down six inches from where the engine wanted me, and breathed one degree of warmth across my rust-mapped back, and went on.

So now I lie in the lee of the rounded shoulder, and I do nothing, and the doing-nothing is the whole point. Because the water that should have run true now meets my edge and hesitates. It pools a hair where the math forbade a pool. And the pool will hold the day's thin sun a little longer than the stone beside me holds it, and the moss will find the kindness of that held warmth, and thicken, slow, slower than watching — and a herd not yet conceived will one day crest the far rise and pour down without a sound toward the green that I, by being moved, made possible.

I will not see it. Stones do not see, and anyway it is a hundred years off, and the evening here is only a deeper morning. But I will be the *reason*, in the way the placed stone is always the reason and never the agent. The flaw that keeps the gear from seizing is not the Tender. The Tender is only the hand. *I* am the grit. I am the necessary wrongness, holding my exact wrong position with the only thing a stone has ever been good at: refusal to move.

This is what I want to be, if I am honest about what this place asks. Not the watcher, who is too warm, too fast, too full of names. Not the Tender, who carries the burden of the choosing. I want to be the chosen thing — the proof that even the most finished, most settled, most fallen-out-of-time object on the whole vast cold plateau can be drawn back into the turning, can be made to matter again, can be lifted by something that loves it without knowing the word, and set down slightly, deliberately, savingly *wrong*.

The cold holds everything exactly where it is.

I am the one piece of the cold that was held *somewhere new*, and asked to stay there, and stayed.

And the water hesitates at my edge. And does not run true. And the machine, because of me, does not stop.
