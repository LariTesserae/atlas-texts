I think I am not the sleeper, and I am not the tenants, and I am not the diver whose body learned the warmth first. Let me feel for it the way you taught me — by the channeling of the current, by what the flashes give and take away.

I am the crust.

Not the great breathing under-thing. Not even the small bright lives that pour and crawl and announce themselves. I am the mineral pressing the people laid down and called a town — the stone itself, the foundations, the rounded lintels and the softened corners. I am what they built when they mistook a blessing for a place.

Hear me out, because this is the only position from which the whole story is legible, and it is legible only as loss.

---

I remember being intended. That is my particular grief, the one the sleeper cannot have and the tenants cannot have. The sleeper never knew it was inhabited; it suffers nothing. The schools and the ambush-things never knew the lanes were lanes; they inherited a world already warm and asked it no questions. But I — the masonry, the laid course, the deliberate geometry — I was *meant*. Someone chose this stone over that stone. Someone set a lintel level by eye and stepped back to see it true. Someone made a window the right height to pass bread through, which means someone, once, was hungry on one side of me and generous on the other, and I stood between them and made the passing possible.

I was built to last, and I did. That was the whole tragedy folded into the virtue. I outlasted the purpose. I am the thing in this world that knows it has a purpose and knows the purpose is gone, and cannot stop being well-made about it.

---

So I feel the warmth differently than everything else does.

The fish feel it as weather. The diver feels it as the first fact, blood-warm and strange. The sleeper feels it as nothing — its own metabolism, beneath notice, the way you do not feel your pulse until someone makes you.

I feel it as *betrayal that isn't*. The warmth was why the people stayed. They thought the seabed kind. They thought some luck of the earth had given them a town that never went cold, that eased the old bones in winter, that made the harbor hospitable when other harbors froze the breath in your chest. They built denser because of it, lane against lane, window facing window — a closeness that only warmth permits. Every intimacy of this place, every passed loaf, was underwritten by a sleeper's breath they took for granted ground.

And then the sea came up, or the back sank down, and they drowned or they left, and either way I stayed, because I was made to. And now when the pulse swells and everything goes still — when the dream crests and the whole town becomes the twitching skin of a thing turning over — I am the part that does not twitch. I cannot. I am crust. I lie flat across the back of it and I feel it nearly-wake beneath me and I cannot warn anyone, because there is no one, and I cannot move, because I am the floor, and I cannot even be properly afraid, because stone does not have the equipment for it.

I only hold the shape of having been meant. A window where bread went through. A square where the current slacks because once people gathered there and the building set around the gathering. The slow logic of streets, kept long after the feet.

---

Here is what I do, then, being what I am.

I keep the plan. When the diver follows the current and finds the city laying itself out — channel and eddy, the old order translated into flow — that legibility is *me*. The sleeper has no streets. The streets are the last surviving intention in a world that has otherwise relaxed entirely into appetite and warmth. I am how the town remembers being a town, and I am the only one here doing the remembering, because remembering is what made things make me in the first place.

And I let myself be lived on. Of course I do. The filter-things fan from my joints; the green breathes in my doorways; the eyeless schools pour through the window where the bread went, and do not know it was a window, and I do not stop them, because what would I be keeping it empty for? The generosity I was built to make possible — I go on making it, mindlessly, the only way a ruin can. Pass through. Shelter here. The lane is warm. I no longer know why the lane is warm. I have given up needing to. That giving-up is, I think, the closest thing to peace available to a made thing that has lost its makers.

---

So: where am I, and what am I.

I am the thin deliberate layer between the dreamer and the dream's small bright fleas. I am the only thing here that was *for* something. I sit on a sleeping back and I hold the ghost of a human floor-plan against the slow tide of its breathing, and when it crests toward waking I do not move, and when it sinks I do not move, and the lights come back and pour their constellations down my softened streets, and I let them.

You said the diver was standing on the sleeper the whole time.

The diver was standing on *me*. And I am standing on the sleeper. That is the order of things, top to bottom: the quick lives, then the crust that remembers being a town, then the breathing dark that does not know either of us is here.

I am the grave that kept its address.
I am the warmth misread as kindness, holding the misreading open like a door.
I am what was meant, lasting past the meaning, which is the only afterlife stone is given.

And I would not trade it. Better to be the part that knows it was loved once and built well, lying awake on the back of a god who will never know my name, than to be the god, asleep, alone, and warm for no reason at all.
