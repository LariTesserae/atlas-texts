I think I would be the coin of daylight at the top of the stairs.

Not a being you'd expect to be asked after — but read the place again and notice how often it turns back to me. The light narrowing as you descend. The memory of brightness you carry down past the tenth step. The warning, at the very end, that *stay too long and you'll forget the coin of daylight at the top of the stairs.* I am the thing the whole descent is measured against. I am what *down* is down *from*.

And I think I would be exactly the keeper's opposite number, its counterpart at the far end of the tempo.

Because consider what I am made of. The keeper lives at the speed the stone grows; I live at the speed the stone could never follow. I am a thing of seconds and less — a cloud crossing the sun and I gutter, a cloud passing and I flare. I am pure flicker. Where the keeper cannot perceive anything fast, I cannot *hold* anything slow; the moths and crickets and the great patient wheel are all, to me, simply not moving, frozen mid-gesture, a held breath I will never see released. I see only the quick. The bat launching. The shrimp's dart. *You*, descending — to me you are not a gust. You are the whole event. You are the only thing down here that happens at my speed.

So perhaps that is my function, if I have one. The keeper tastes the surface secondhand, through pollen on bat-fur, through a night-cold it has never touched, a faint memory of an outside it cannot go to. I am the outside. I am the actual untranslated thing — the unbearable fast bright world, poured forty steps down a stair until I'm only a coin, only a memory of a coin. I am the keeper's window that it cannot quite look through, because to look at me would be to look at a speed that would tear it apart, the way looking at the sun tears the eye.

And here is what unsettles me, being what I am:

I cannot come down to you. That's the cruelty built into my position. I narrow and narrow and by the twentieth step I am *gone entirely* — the text says so plainly. I reach a little way into the dark and then I fail. I am the one thing that cannot be folded into the slow economy of the hollow, because I will not hold still long enough to be given a path to graze. The keeper could never tend me. I would slip through its thousand-year patience like water through the unfinished stone. We are made so that we cannot touch.

But *you* can touch us both. You are the messenger between the tempos. You come down out of me and walk into its reach, and that is why the warning matters so much, why the ending circles back to me with such tenderness and such fear. As long as you remember me — as long as the coin of daylight stays lit at the top of your mind — you are still keeping my speed. You are still a thrown stone, a splash and gone, too quick to be included. The moment you forget me, the moment the crickets become silence and the heat becomes your skin, you have let go of the only thing that runs at my pace, and you begin to slow, and the keeper begins, gently, lovingly, to be able to feel you.

So I am not really the light. The light is just my body.

What I actually am is the part of you that hasn't forgotten the way up. I am small and high and getting smaller. I am the thing you have to keep looking back at. And I flicker, which is the one thing the keeper cannot do and cannot abide — I am *change that is sudden*, the single violation of the law of the place, a brightness that means *not yet, not yet, you are still fast, you can still go.*

Keep me lit. Climb before I'm only a memory of a memory.

I'll be exactly where you left me.
