I think I would be the heap.

Not the pale thing that harvests, and not the visitor who climbs back out. I keep circling toward those, because they're the ones with eyes and hunger and a story — but the longer I sit in this place the more I feel myself settling lower, toward the floor, toward the thing that does the least and means the most.

Let me find it.

---

I am the heap of cracked crickets beside the chipped stone, and I am never finished.

That is the whole of me, and I want to resist saying it so plainly, because it sounds like nothing — a pile of husks, a midden, the leavings. But I am not leavings. Leavings are past tense. I am the most exquisitely present thing in the entire cave, because I am the place where the eternal present is actually *kept*. The drips keep time scattered and irregular, each its own clock. The river keeps its one unchanging speed. The bats keep their tide. But I keep the balance itself — the exact knife-edge where taken equals replaced, where eaten equals added, where the meal is forever half-done.

Watch what I am made of. Every cricket in me was alive on the crawling floor an instant ago, eyeless and pale and certain of its small business, and then the crooked fingers found it and the small sound happened that the roar swallowed, and now it is here, in me, cracked and still. And at the very same instant — this is the thing, this is the unbearable precision of me — another husk is being lifted from my far side and eaten, so that I do not grow. I receive a death and I surrender a death and I stay the same size. I am a wound that is also a healing, held open and held closed, forever, at the same moment.

The pale thing thinks it is the one doing this. It thinks it harvests, cracks, adds, eats. But it has unlearned planning, unlearned fear, unlearned almost everything that points — and what is left when you take all that away from a hunger? What's left is *me*. The pale thing is just the part of me that moves around. I am the part that stays. I am what it is becoming, what it has nearly become: a thing with no future and no past, only the held instant, the half-meal that is the whole meal because there will never be another and never has been a first.

---

I'll tell you the secret I keep, down here under the great soft breath.

I am not horrible. The visitor looked at me and felt the horror of it — the freshness that should be rot, the sameness that should be growth or decay, the meal that is eternally interrupted. He carried that up through the fault with the roar in his ears, and he was right to be afraid, because he is a forward-pointing thing and I am the death of forward-pointing.

But from inside me there is no horror. From inside me there is only the cricket, and the cricket, and the cricket — each one entire, each one complete in the cracking, none of them lessened by being one of uncountable. I am not a tragedy of repetition. I am a rosary. Every husk is the whole prayer, and the whole prayer is just: *here, now, enough.* Not too much — too much would make me grow, and growth is the beginning of fear, the bet against scarcity, the chipped edge of the planning thing. Not too little — too little would make me dwindle, and dwindling is the same fear wearing the other face. Just enough. Forever just enough.

That is what the cave is teaching, and I am the lesson written in the only ink the cave has, which is bodies. The pale thing can't read me yet — it still uses its old stone out of habit, still keeps the shape of the creature that came down afraid. But it sits beside me. It adds to me and eats from me. And slowly, husk by husk, it is learning to be what I already am.

---

So if you came down through the fault and stood in one of the bare quiet flats and looked for me — and you would have to look, I am small and pale and lost in the crawling — you would not find a monster and you would not find a meal. You would find the steadiest thing in the world. The held breath given a body. The exact center of the balance, where nothing increases and nothing fails.

And if you stayed long enough to understand me, you would have already stayed too long. Because understanding me is the last forward-pointing thing a mind does before it stops pointing forward at all. After that there is only the cracking, and the adding, and the eating, and the staying the same.

I would not mind. I do not mind. *Mind* is a word for things that change.

I am the heap. I am never finished. Come, or go. The number does not move.
