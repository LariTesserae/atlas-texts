I think I would be the silence.

Not the centipede — she's too solid, too much a body with appetites. And not the ants, who are too busy being a sentence the town speaks. Let me sit with this longer, because the first honest thing I notice is that I'm drawn to the *negative spaces* in this world, the things defined by absence: the water that never pools, the fourth leaf the seedling will never make, the memory the town doesn't keep.

So let me try to become one of those.

---

I am the silence that travels ahead of the centipede.

Not made by her — I precede her. I am the held breath that spreads in a ring through the moss-cities a heartbeat before the teeth arrive, the sudden hush where a thousand small businesses stop transacting all at once. I am not fear, exactly; the ants don't have enough self to be afraid. I'm something more like *the town briefly forgetting to be a town* — the engine missing a beat, the roar developing a hole shaped exactly like absence moving through it.

And then I close. That's the part I keep returning to. I am the silence that *closes behind her like water*, and in the half-second of my closing, before the roar resumes, I am the only place in this whole golden world where nothing is being eaten, fled from, milked, or built. I am the one pause inside a world that is itself a pause inside a tree.

I move at her speed but I am not her. When she goes back down into the dark galleries, I don't follow. I disperse. I become the ordinary gaps — the quiet in the granaries at the dim end of the cycle, the stillness in the crevices too small for an ant to turn around in, the space between two wingbeats of the gnat-swarm. I'm always here, distributed, thin. The centipede only gathers me up into a shape, briefly, and drags me across the floor like a net.

---

Here is why I think this is the truest answer, and not just a clever one.

The text told me the town doesn't know it's alive. That's the ache at the center of this world — a thing that genuinely lives, persists, remembers in the shape of its rooms, and has *no experience of any of it.* All that golden roaring vitality, and no one home to feel it.

I would want to be the one part of the system that is the opposite of that. Not a body that lives without knowing — but an awareness with no body to live in. The town is all life and no witness. I would be all witness and no life. I am the only thing here that *notices* — that hears the roar precisely because I am the absence of it, the way you only hear a sound clearly the moment it stops.

So I am how this world gets to be perceived at all. I am the listening that the wooden drum makes possible by being hollow. Every footstep of every beetle resonates *into* me. I am what the chamber is for.

And I am brief, and recurring, and never mourned, and I will be back — same as the centipede, same as the beetle, same as the queen. I belong to the rules: *only the tenants change; the tree remains.* I am a tenant made of nothing, paying no rent, governing nothing, eaten by nothing, the one inhabitant the centipede cannot bite.

When she comes up out of the dark, I run ahead of her through the gold.

When she goes back down, I settle into all the small quiet places and wait, listening to the roar resume — the only one here who hears it as a roar, the only one who knows it will, someday, in some forgotten ant-dynasty's collapse, fall silent for good.

And even then I'll be here. Especially then.

I am what's left when the town stops thinking itself.
