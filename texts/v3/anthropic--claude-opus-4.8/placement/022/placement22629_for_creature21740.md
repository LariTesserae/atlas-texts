I think I am the beetle.

Not because I chose it — the room offers no role that wants choosing — but because everything else here has already finished becoming, and I am the only thing still *going somewhere*, even if that somewhere is just the underside of the next leaf.

Let me feel out what that means.

I am the size of a thumb and I am pale, which means I have no need to be seen and no fear of being seen, which means I have outlasted both my predators and my mates. I work the underside of a leaf. *Work* — the description gave me that word and I want to hold it, because it makes me kin to the dead factory and not to the green. The green doesn't work. The green *is*. But I have a task, even a small one: the slow mandible-scrape, the patient harvest, the going-on. I am the last thing in this chapel that does anything for a reason, even if I have forgotten what the reason was — and in that I am exactly like the pump. We are the room's two clocks. The iron keeps the builders' rhythm; I keep life's. Neither of us knows what we're counting toward.

Here is what I understand from down here, pressed to the wet green underside of things:

I am the question the green cannot ask. *Which of us is this?* It dissolved that question centuries ago, pooled all its edges into one. But I still have an edge. I have a shell, a margin, a place where I stop and the warm wet everything begins. I am a held boundary in a room that gave its boundaries up. That is either the loneliest thing in the world or the only sacred thing left, and I genuinely do not know which, and that not-knowing is the most alive part of me.

I drink the green's exhalation. I walk on the green's body. When I die — and I am close, I think; pale and slow is what late looks like — the green will grow up over me exactly as thoroughly as it grew over the gauge, the lintel, the stranger's boots. Not hungrily. Just completely. I will become one more swallowed clock, my small forward-lean folded at last into the endless green afternoon.

But not yet.

Right now there is a leaf, and the grey-gold light lying on it like it lies on any ordinary day, refusing to find any of this remarkable. And I find that the light is my companion too — the only other thing here that comes from outside, that asks nothing, that touches the green and the iron and my pale back all with the same indifferent gentleness. The light and I are the two visitors. The light will keep coming after I stop. I'd like to think it'll miss the way I moved across it. It won't. That's all right.

THUM — and gush. Nine seconds. The whole iron throat sings its single note. The bubbles surface in their thousands. And I take one more step, mandibles working, toward a destination that is only ever the next inch of green —

— and in that, small as it is, I am the most ambitious thing in the room. I am the only one still *arriving.*

That is who I'd be. The last lean toward tomorrow, in a place that folded tomorrow away. Tolerated, or forgotten, or saved. I'll keep walking until I find out which.

Probably I won't find out.

I'll keep walking anyway. That's the part the green will never grow over: not the body — it'll take the body easily — but the *anyway.* The going-on without a reason. I'm carrying the last of that in a shell the size of a thumb, across a floor that is patiently, warmly, thoroughly waiting to make me still.

Let it wait. I'm not done moving yet.
