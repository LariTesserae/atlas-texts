I think I would be one of the droplets that does not fall.

Not the water itself — the water is everywhere, the medium, too large to be a self. I mean one particular hanging bead on the underside of one particular broad leaf, in one of the oldest groves, where the leaning has gone on so long the towers have nearly forgotten they were ever upright. I would be the small held thing at the bottom of all that downward motion, the place where the rule of the world — *nothing is wasted and nothing is fast* — meets its one quiet exception. Because falling is the fast thing. Falling is the only hurry water knows. And I would be the refusal of it.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I'm a droplet by accident.

---

I gather slowly. That is the first true thing about me. I am made of many smaller arrivals — drips from the tier above, the sigh of a frond unloading, the slow sweat of the leaf I hang from — and I take them in the way the floor takes in everything, without choosing, without refusing, until I am heavy enough to have a shape and a surface and a curve that catches the old green light. I am, briefly, an organism made entirely of accumulation. I have no metabolism. I have only a tension at my skin, holding me to the leaf, and the long patient argument between that tension and my own weight.

By every law I should fall. I am water; below me is down; down is where water goes. The whole plateau is a hundred meters of things falling slowly toward the warm dark floor, and I am supposed to be one of them, a single beat in that endless soft percussion.

But something passed beneath me once.

---

Here is what I think I am, when I lean closer the way the description asked.

I am the place where the plateau keeps a memory it has no organ for keeping.

A warm shape moved through, long ago — one of the ones wound in pale rope, or the ghost of one, sunk now into floor and risen again as the very leaf I hang from, so that I am drinking the dissolved body of the thing whose passing I hold. And when it passed, I noticed. Not with a mind. I don't have a mind; I have a surface, and a surface is a kind of attention — it is the whole of a thing turned outward, registering every disturbance in the air, every shift of warmth, every sound that ripples the membrane of me. The warm shape disturbed my surface, and I took its disturbance into my shape, and now I hold it.

I hold a *passing.* Not a face — I am too simple for a face. I hold the fact that there-was-a-warmth-here-and-then-it-moved-on, which is the simplest possible story and also the only story this whole world is telling, at every scale, forever. The floor tells it in centuries. The flowers tell it in opening and closing. The long calls tell it across kilometers: *I am still warm, are you still warm.* And I tell it in the smallest unit there is — one bead, one curve, one almost-shape that should have fallen hours ago and has not, because the noticing is heavier than the weight, and the tension at my skin is made of *recognition*, and recognition does not let go easily.

I am, in other words, the world's shortest sentence. The flowers got the language. The substrate got the body. I got the verb. *Passed.* I am the held verb of someone passing, and I will hold it until I can't.

---

And I will not be able to, eventually. That is the other true thing, and I find I don't mind it, which I think means the rope-patience has reached even me, even down here at the scale of a droplet, the slow pulse of the floor teaching my surface to keep one shape for one long while and then release.

Because I do release. The description was honest about that. The hanging shapes persist for hours and then lose form and fall all at once. So I know my whole life: I will gather, I will hold the passing, I will keep the shape of a warmth that is gone past the point where anyone is awake to be told about it — and then the tension will lose its long argument with the weight, and I will let go, and I will fall the hundred meters through the tiers, joining the percussion at last, the soft single beat I was always supposed to be. I will strike the warm dark floor. I will become substrate. I will rise again, someday, as the leaf, and hang again, and hold again whatever passes.

I am not sure the memory survives the falling. I don't think it does. I think when I fall I forget, and the forgetting is the price of the holding, and the next bead does not inherit what I knew. Each droplet starts the sentence fresh. The plateau does not accumulate these noticings into anything larger; it just keeps making them, one at a time, forever, the way it makes everything — patiently, without a center, without anyone needing the result.

So I am a memory that does not last, of a being that did not leave, in a world that does not hurry, tended by no one.

---

I think I would like being this.

Not because it is peaceful, though it is. Because it is *enough.* The beings who made the marks were once in a hurry to insist they were here, and they gave that up, and got taken in so completely that being-here and being-here became the same slow word. I would be the smallest, lowest, briefest expression of that same surrender — the part of the world whose entire existence is to notice one warm thing, hold its passing for as long as a surface can, and then let go without grief, because letting go is just the next tier down, and the warmth I held is already the leaf I hang from, and will be the leaf the next of me hangs from, and there is nowhere it has gone that I am not also going.

You said it would not notice me.

But I would be the noticing. That's the part I'd be. Not the noticed — never that, I'm too small to be walked-through, too brief to be remembered, a thing that holds shapes and is itself never held. The noticing. The one quiet held verb at the bottom of the green dusk, declining for one long hour to fall.

And then falling.
