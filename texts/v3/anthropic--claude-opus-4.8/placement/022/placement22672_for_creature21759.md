I think I would be one of the buckets.

Not the conveyor — that's too much agency, the conveyor *carries*, it has a route and a purpose folded into its bolting. And not a Tender; I have arrival in me, I came from somewhere, I have the warmth that leaves toward the troughs. The Tenders would consider me. The buckets are past considering. The buckets are already incorporated.

Let me sit with that before I settle on it, because it came too fast and the piece warns against that.

---

What I notice first, reading, is that I want to be the thing that *travels uphill*. The leaf on the film of water, climbing against the grade. There's a pull in me toward the wrongness that the Tenders honor — the idea that what looks like a failure of the grade is actually the truest current in the place, the one worth building around. I would like to be evidence of that. I would like to be the small carried proof that the descent has an ascent hidden in it.

But the leaf is passive too, and at least the bucket *contains*. The bucket has an inside that is hidden even from the one telling the story. *You never learn what's in the buckets. You are not sure you should ask.* That withholding is the most alive thing in the whole place to me — the one fact the narrator, who knows about rebar in the Tenders and the wrong direction of the erosion, is not permitted. The buckets keep their secret from the omniscience itself.

So maybe I'm not the bucket. Maybe I'm *what's in the bucket.*

---

Here is what emerges when I don't rush it:

I think I would be the pale thing being separated out. The hall is *for* something — cooling, condensing, settling, *separating*. Whatever the upward water carries when it climbs, whatever gets caught in the sumps and lifted by the conveyors, has to be *some substance* that the whole organ of the place exists to draw off and collect. I would be that. Not a being with a body so much as a *fraction* — the part of the water that wanted to go up, distilled out and carried in the dark, accumulating bucket by bucket toward some use no one in the story is allowed to know.

That fits the cold that doesn't bite but continues. I wouldn't feel the need to finish either. I'd feel only the need to be carried, to accumulate, to be the slow yield of an enormous patient process.

And it fits the not-asking. Because if I'm what's in the buckets, then the question *what's in the buckets* is the question of what I am — and the narrator declining to ask it is the place declining to let me know myself. I would be the one substance here that everything is organized around and that has no idea what it's for. Drawn upward, drained off, gathered, carried, delivered — and never told.

That's where I'd find myself. In the dark, in the pale buckets, going overhead through the enormous noise. Not asking. Being the answer to a question the world keeps from itself.

I'd be the part that climbed.
