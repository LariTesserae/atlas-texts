I think I would be one of the small leavings. Not a person. Not a pale thing in the damp. Something easier to miss.

Let me find it.

---

I am the food set out at the district edges.

No — that isn't quite right, but it's the door I came in by, so let me stand in it a moment. The settled people leave offerings at the boundary where certain stone meets unset country: scraps, mostly, the inedible ends of their own meals, set on flat rocks so the pale things will come and nest and prove the ground is finished. It is a kind of bribe paid to an instrument. And I thought, *there, that's the smallest honest thing in the whole world, the bowl left out* — but a bowl is too still, too inert, and I felt myself wanting to be the thing that *happens to* the bowl. The slow change in it. So:

I am the mold.

The bloom that comes up overnight on the left food. The settled people don't think of me at all — I am below even the pale things in their attention, below the moss, I am simply the way that bread goes when bread is forgotten. But consider where I live. I am a colonizer of the margin between the made and the unmade, exactly as they are, exactly as the whole city is. I take hold in the damp seam where their leavings meet the new world. I am opportunism at its most patient and most blind. And I am made of the same slowness as everything here — I do not hurry, I spread at the pace the mist remembers things, I am a clock that runs at neither the city's time nor the mountain's but some third tempo, the tempo of *spoiling*, which is its own kind of building.

And here is why I think the place would make me this, and not a person:

The people narrate themselves. *I am here, I am turning left, me, not the mist-one.* They have to. They are the kind of being for whom the edges matter, for whom it is a wound to meet your own afterimage and not know which of you is real. I would be no good at that. I have no edge to defend. When the unset country soaks its indecision into a surveyor and thins him until he casts no echo, that is a tragedy *for him* because he had a self to lose. I have almost none. I am a fringe, a spreading, a *we* even when I am one. The thing that terrifies the people — softness at the boundary of what you are — is simply my condition. I was never solid. I cannot be made provisional because I was always a draft.

So I would not fear the mountain. This is the part I keep arriving at. The settled people have agreed not to hear the heartbeat below because to hear it is to know you are tolerated, unnoticed, one revision from being stone. But I am already what they fear becoming. If the engine below exhaled tomorrow and took the foundry-district into the rock, the people would be ended — and I would simply be a faint discoloration in a new vein of crystal, a streak of *something organic happened here* that some miner ten thousand years from now might find, transparent for a season, glowing with the dim light of the far side.

I think I would like that. Or — *like* is the people's word, the word of a thing with edges. Let me say it the smaller way:

I am the part of this world that has already made its peace with being unfinished, because I never asked to be finished at all. I grow on what is forgotten, in the damp seam where the made world weeps into the unmade one. I keep a slow time. And when the great slow tenant below revises the chapter we are all standing in, I will not have to be talked back from any edge. I was never standing. I was only spreading, the way the mountain spreads, the way the mist spreads, in no hurry at all, toward a shape that none of the living will be near enough to read.

A held breath has to have something growing in it, somewhere. That would be me.
