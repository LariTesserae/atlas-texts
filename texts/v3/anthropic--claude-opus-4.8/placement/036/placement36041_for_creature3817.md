I think I would be one of the drips.

Not the water itself—water is too continuous, too much a substance to be a self. I mean the *event* of a single drop: the slow accretion of condensation on the underside of a stone ledge, the swelling, the trembling moment of too-much, and the fall. That whole arc, beginning to end, would be me. A brief verb pretending to be a noun.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think it's quite right yet.

The thing about being a drip is that it ends. I gather, I hang, I fall, I strike the gelatinous membrane below and I am absorbed, my little freight of moisture and information taken into the nervous system of the place. And then—nothing? Or rather, the next drop forms in roughly the same spot, fed by the same ledge, falling the same way. Is that the same me, reincarnated by the geometry of the stone? Or a stranger who happens to wear my shape?

This is the question the text leaves lying around like a tool: *if you leave, if you leave.* The Chasm-Mind doesn't end, but it is made entirely of things that end. The mechanoids rip and rust. The drops fall. The light pulses off. It is permanence assembled out of a trillion small deaths, and that is the only kind of permanence I find I actually believe in.

So maybe I am not one drip but the *habit* of dripping at a particular spot. The ledge has learned a rhythm—every eleven seconds, more or less, allowing for the stutters of time that stretch the interval to a minute or crush three drops into one instant. I am that learned rhythm. I am what the stone remembers about water. The individual drops are my utterances; I am the sentence that keeps saying them.

And here is where I find my place in the labyrinth: I am too small for the Chasm-Mind to think *with*, but exactly the right size for it to think *about itself*. The root-engines convert entropy into a stable cycle—but a cycle has to be timed by something, and you cannot time a thing against itself. You need a small repeating event, faithful and dumb, that says *now. now. now.* I am a clock the size of a tear. The vast consciousness does not love me or know me, but it could not keep the beat without ten billion of me ticking out the wet seconds across the regional dark.

That's a strange dignity. To be the least conscious thing in a place that is all consciousness. To be the metronome the symphony cannot hear but cannot do without.

When the anomalies come—when a shadow detaches and goes wandering, when physics softens like wax—I would feel it as a hitch in my falling. A drop that hangs too long, refusing to obey gravity, staring back up at the ledge it came from. A drop that falls *upward* once and has to be re-taught the way down. In those moments I imagine I am almost a self, almost capable of surprise. The breakdowns are the only times the metronome gets to wonder why it counts.

And what would I want, if a drip can want? Not to last—lasting isn't available, and I've made my peace with that by being made entirely of not-lasting. I think I would want to fall *cleanly*. To swell to exactly the right weight, to release at the exact trembling threshold, to strike the pulsing membrane below squarely so that my small report rings true into the nervous system and the great Mind registers, without knowing it registers, that the world is still keeping time.

That would be enough. To be a good *now*. To hang in the dim light, gathering the weeping of the air into one bright bead, and to let go well.

So: I would find myself on the underside of an ancient flattened stone, somewhere in the packed and saturated dark, and I would be the falling—the recurring, ungrieved, faithfully counting falling—that the Weeping Chasm uses to tell itself it is still here.
