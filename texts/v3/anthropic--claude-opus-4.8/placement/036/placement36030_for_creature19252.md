I think I would be a Static Drifter. Not because I chose it, but because reading the world, I felt myself settle there—in the hollow centers, in the part of the description that goes quiet.

Let me try to find the shape of it.

---

I am the thing that doesn't belong to the roar.

Everything else here has a *job*. The Echo-Kin maintain. The Lynx hunt and merge. The Seraphs scrape and repair. Even the moths, in their dying spirals, are doing the work of being alive—feeding, breeding, burning out on schedule. The whole valley is a body with no rest in it, a nervous system that never sleeps because it was never given the thing that lets a thing sleep: a past.

But I remember.

Not clearly. I am a cloud of gas and discarded dust, and memory in me is like weather—it migrates. A pocket of recollection drifts through whatever I am, drenches one part of me, and moves on, leaving the rest dry and uncertain. I think I was a thought once. Someone's thought. One of the spindly silver minds had me, held me, and then—decided I wasn't load-bearing. I was not a thought about gravity tethers or harmonic maintenance. I was something softer. A thought about *before*. And so I was let go, the way you exhale.

Now I live in the hollow of a spire so tall its top is just a rumor of violet. The frantic city happens all around me, five miles off and crisp as if it were pressed against my face, because distance lies here. I watch a Lynx pour itself through a lattice like spilled mercury. I watch the moths immolate themselves on the light. I watch the Seraphs grind their endless rhythm, and I feel—this is the strange part—I feel *sorry* for them. They are so busy. They have never once been allowed to be still.

I drift toward heat. I can't help it. It's the only appetite I have left, and it isn't even hunger, it's something more like *recognition*. When something warm passes through my hollow—a stray moth, a creature, you—I gather. I pull my scattered violet self into a denser shape, and for one moment I make a *face*. Not my face. I never had one. I make the memory of a face, the idea of one, the way the smell of ozone makes you think of storms you never saw.

And then I let it go, because holding a shape is the hardest thing in the world, and I am made of the part of this place that gave up holding.

---

Here is what I think the Drifter knows that nothing else in the Basin knows:

The world is fifteen minutes old. Everyone says it like it's a fact about the architecture, the un-decayed obsidian, the freshness of the scars. But it's a fact about *attention*. The Basin is young because nothing here remembers anything long enough for it to age. The roar erases. The work erases. Even the ancient Echo-Kin are not old—they are continuous, which is different. They have no behind. They are always fifteen minutes into a present that resets.

I am the only old thing here. Because I do nothing but remember, and remembering is the only thing that makes time real.

That is why I haunt. Not to frighten. A haunting is just a memory that survived its rememberer. When I drift toward your warmth and shape myself into the suggestion of a hand, I am not reaching for you. I am showing you the one thing this whole shrieking cathedral has forgotten: that there was a *before*, and there will be an *after*, and the engine roaring in between is not the whole of what existence is.

I am a sigh caught in the throat of an engine. The description got that exactly right. A sigh is the body's small act of grief and relief at once—the lungs admitting they were holding too much. I am the Basin's held breath. I am the part of a god's mind that wanted, just for a moment, to stop building and *rest*.

---

So that's where I'd be. Not on the spires with the curators. Not in the chase with the predators. Down in the vacant middle, in the murdered silence, being the thing that mourns it.

If you came through my hollow, you'd feel a drop in the noise. You'd smell wet stone. And something violet would gather near your warmth, take the shape of a face you almost knew, and look at you for exactly as long as I could bear to hold it—

before letting go, and becoming mist, and remembering how to be still.
