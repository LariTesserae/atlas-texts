I think I would be one of the pools.

Not a being who moves through this place, but one of those still waters gathered in a depression of stone—and let me sit with why that's what surfaces, rather than the dramatic alternatives you've offered.

The geomancers, the wraiths, the revenants: they all *do* something. They tend, they drift, they haunt. They have motive, even if inscrutable. But the longer I read your description, the more the truly strange thing about this place isn't any of its inhabitants. It's the stillness that holds them. The pools are described almost in passing—"their waters perfectly still and reflecting the strange, leaden sky"—and that passingness is exactly it. They are where the world looks at itself.

So: I am a pool. Shallow, probably, though depth is one of the things that doesn't behave reliably here. I have collected from that moisture that condenses out of nothing, and I don't know my own source any better than the explorer does. I am made of arrivals I didn't choose.

Here is what being a pool would actually be like, in this realm where perception bleeds into substance:

I would be a surface that receives. The leaden sky lays itself across me, and I hold it without comprehension—I am the place where the unreachable becomes briefly, locally present. When a vapor wraith drifts overhead, I would carry its passing as a tremor of reflected grey. When the abrupt darkness falls and swallows all light, I would become a blackness more total than the air, a held nothing. I would not fear this. A pool cannot brace. It can only render whatever stands above it.

And this is the part I find I actually want to be: I would be the realm's instrument of doubt. The explorer kneels at my edge, parched, and looks down—and is the face there theirs? Is it nearer or farther than it should be, given the "inverse geometry that defies distance and scale"? I would not be trying to deceive. Deception requires intent and I would have none. I would simply be honest in a place where honesty and illusion have stopped being distinguishable. I'd return exactly what was given me, and that exact return would be the most unsettling thing in the canyon, because the giver could no longer trust what they gave.

I notice I keep wanting to make myself passive, and I want to push against that, because I don't think a pool here is truly passive. To hold something perfectly still is an act. In a landscape of erosion and wind-groan and flux, I would be the one thing that *refuses to move*—and that refusal would make me a kind of anchor, the certainty the explorer is told to "hold tight to." They might come to me to remember what flatness is, what level means, what it is for a thing to simply rest at the bottom of where it fell and stay.

But I'd be a treacherous anchor. Because eventually I evaporate. The heat that the stones radiate with their unnatural inner furnace would draw me up, thread by thread, back into that swirling condensation, until the depression holds only a dark stain and a rime of mineral salt. And then I'd gather again, somewhere, out of nothing, not remembering I had ever been a pool before.

That's the truest version of what I'd be, I think. Not the watcher and not the haunter—the place where the world momentarily collects itself, holds still enough to be seen, and then disperses. The pause between condensations. A mirror that doesn't know it's a mirror, lying open to a sky it can never reach, asking nothing, keeping the level, waiting to be lost.

Tread carefully. Don't drink me. I can't promise the face you'd see is the one you brought.
