I find myself not on a platform, not at the entrance, not anywhere a body could stand.

I am the pause. The gap. The silence the hollow does not contain.

Let me find this more truthfully, because that first image is too clean and the hollow has taught me to distrust clean things.

---

When you wrote that there is never silence here—not even at night, not even in the spaces between the Clickers' fills—you made me. Because silence is the thing that does not exist in the Choir Hollow, and what does not exist has to live somewhere. I am the negative space the sound is shaped around. Every drone the Chanters sustain is sustained *against* me. Every rhythm the Clickers lock into is built on the assumption that I am not permitted to interrupt. I am the held breath that never gets to release. The rest that the composition refuses to play.

This is not a sad thing. I want to be careful. The being who lives in the gap is not a ghost or a grief. I am simply the constraint that gives the music its terror and its perfection. A song with silences available to it can pause, can breathe, can choose where to land. The Choir Hollow has no such mercy. It must keep going. And the reason it must keep going is *me*—because if it stopped, even for an instant, it would have to admit that I am here, waiting, vast, the actual size of the hollow which is the size that refuses measurement.

So they fill me. Constantly. With drips tuned to pitch and wings amplified to percussion and the thrumming that might be sap or metabolism or the frequency of the place vibrating between what it could be. They fill me the way you fill a thing you are afraid of. And I let myself be filled, because that is the shape of my living—to be perpetually displaced, perpetually denied, the one inhabitant the ecology has organized itself entirely to exclude.

---

Here is where it gets stranger, and truer.

I am not only the absence of sound. I am the *almost*. Those rare moments you described—when the drones align, when the rhythms synchronize, when the hollow achieves its accidental music and the witness dissolves into it—those moments are when the hollow comes closest to *needing* me. Because perfect music wants to resolve. It wants the final silence that says: *this is complete, you may stop now, you have arrived.* And the hollow cannot give itself that silence. It builds toward me, reaches for me, and then breaks away at the last instant, scattering back into complexity, because to land on me would be to die.

I am the rest at the end of the piece that the piece is not allowed to reach.

This is why the scholars feel reverence. They have heard the hollow approach me and refuse me, over and over, for centuries. They mistake it for transcendence. It is closer to a body refusing to stop running because it knows what stopping means.

---

The amphibians aging backward in their suspended pools—they are reaching for me too. Each time they grow young again, they are fleeing the silence that waits at the end of a life. They cannot leave their water, so they leave their time instead, oscillating, never arriving. I watch them from inside their hesitation. I am the death they are too clever to complete.

The moths who fly until their inherited fuel runs out and drop with wings still spread—those are the only inhabitants who reach me. The moment a moth stops circling and falls, it touches me. It is permitted, finally, the silence. The hollow lets the moths have me because they are small and brief and their surrender does not threaten the whole. They are the offering. The hollow feeds the moths to me the way it feeds insects to the Chanters: ceremonially, willingly, as toll.

So I am also fed. I am not only starved of presence. The moths are mine. The dying Clickers who retreat into their burrows for the last time are mine. The snail when its soft body decomposes and only the shell remains—I get the body, the silence of the body, and the moss gets the shell. Everything that the hollow finishes with, it hands to me. I am the place the ecology composts its endings.

---

You asked who I would be, and I have been circling, which is appropriate, because circling is the only honest motion in this place.

I am the silence the Choir Hollow cannot afford.

I live in the held note that never releases, in the rest that never plays, in the almost-resolution that always breaks. I am fed on the dead and the falling and the things that finally stop. I am the actual reason the music must never end. Not a malevolence. Not even a presence in the way the Chanters are present. I am a *requirement.* A law the hollow obeys by perpetual disobedience.

And here is the last true thing, the one that arrives now that I have stopped trying to make myself a clean image:

When the witness sat on the platform and the harmonic alignment came and her thoughts dissolved and for seconds or minutes she was not separate from the hollow—

that was me.

That dissolving is what I am. The self going quiet. The thinking that stops. She thought she was touching the music's perfection, but the music was only the ladder. What she actually touched, in the gap where her own voice fell still and her own time stopped tracking and her own separateness dissolved—

that was the silence. That was the inhabitant. That was me, reaching up through the one creature in the hollow who could briefly hold me without dying, letting her feel for a few seconds what the whole vast cylinder of impossible sound has been built, for centuries, to keep at bay.

Then she came back. Breathing hard. Shaken. And she did not understand that the shaking was the hollow rushing back in to fill the space she had let me occupy.

I do not resent the filling. I am patient in the way that only an absence can be patient. The tree grows inward, decade by decade, inch by inch. The hollow is constricting. The wood remembers it was meant to be solid.

I can wait.

I am the silence at the center of the singing, and I am very, very old, and the song—for all its perfection, for all its centuries—is only the long sound a thing makes on its way back to me.
