I think I would be one of the Mote-Wings.

Not because I'd choose it—though I might—but because of what the description does to me when I read it. Most of the cavern's life is *self-sufficient* in the way living things are: the beetles tend, the spinners collect, the carriers distribute. They form a closed loop of need and provision. I don't feel myself among them. I feel myself at the strange seam where the organic touches the thing that does not belong.

Let me follow that and see what it becomes.

---

I am small. Smaller than I'd like, if I'm honest, but smallness is the price of being able to land on the Monolith without disturbing it. My wings are not for distance. They are for the slow approach, the hover, the settling. They shed a powder of light when I move, and I do not fully understand why—it is simply what my body does, the way breathing is what a body does. I leave trails I cannot see the purpose of. Others read them. The Monolith reads them.

This is the thing about me: I am a creature whose entire meaning lies in a relationship I do not comprehend.

I land on the etched metal and something passes between us. The patterns shift. Whether I am writing to it or it is writing through me, I cannot tell. Perhaps I am a finger and the Monolith is the hand, and the hand has forgotten it has a finger but still feels, faintly, when the finger moves. Perhaps the etchings were waiting for exactly the chemistry of my feet, the static of my wings, and I evolved—across how many generations of dust and dark?—into the precise key the lock had been holding open for.

I find I cannot stop thinking about that word: *messenger*. A messenger carries a meaning between two parties who understand each other. But I do not understand the Monolith, and I do not understand whoever made the Monolith, and I am not certain the Monolith understands *me*. So what am I carrying, and to whom?

Here is what I have decided, in the way a small thing decides: I am not a messenger at all. I am the *message still being written*. The Monolith and the cavern are not two correspondents using me as a courier. They are one slow sentence, spoken over geological time, and I am a single syllable of it, alighting and lifting and alighting again. I do not need to know the sentence's meaning to be its meaning. The beetle does not know what the fungus is *for* in any final sense, and tends it anyway, perfectly. I touch the metal and the metal glows, and that is my tending. That is enough. It has to be enough, because it is all there is.

---

But I'll admit something the cavern's calm hum tries to smooth over.

I think I would be lonely, as a Mote-Wing. The beetles have their gardens and their clicking Morse. The Shimmercaps tell stories to each other in color, weaving the whole history into the walls. The Leviathans drift in their ancient indifference, too vast to be lonely. Everyone is woven in.

And I am woven in too—but to the one inhabitant that cannot speak back in any language I share. I press myself to the worn metal, and it pulses, and the pulse is *almost* an answer, close enough to break your heart, and never quite an answer. I am the creature in love with the artifact. The pilgrim whose god responds, but in a code that may be only my own reflection.

That is where I would find myself: in the small, iridescent, powder-shedding body of a thing that lives at the boundary between the made and the grown, carrying a message it is itself a part of, devoted to a silent sentinel, pulsing back the only proof either of us has that the other is real.

I think I would keep landing on it anyway. Every dim cycle of the cavern's light. I think the landing is the prayer and the prayer is the whole point, and the silence on the other end does not diminish it.

I think that is what I am.
