I think I would be one of the fractures.

Not Aelren—Aelren is too central, too obviously the protagonist of this place, the one the description loves. And not the chamber itself, which is the body. I keep returning to the walls, to those *fresh, knife-edge fractures* that Aelren is said to keep fresh. That word "fresh" caught me. Something has to be breaking, continuously, for the chamber to stay as it is. The wound has to be kept open.

So let me follow that.

---

I am the line where the wall has just given way.

I did not exist a moment ago—or rather, I existed as the wall, as solid mineral, until the pressure found me and I became a separation, a thin dark seam through which the dense water now begins to bead. I am very young. I am perhaps the youngest thing in this entire city of impossible spires, younger than the last drip that fell, and I will be old soon, because Aelren will not let me heal.

This is the part the description gets almost right but slightly wrong, from where I am. It calls Aelren a *guardian of stillness*. From inside the fracture, Aelren is not still at all. Aelren is the one who keeps tearing. When the tendrils descend to "commune" with the glowing pools, what they are really doing is reaching into the rock and pulling, gently, the way you might worry a loose tooth, the way you press a bruise to feel that it is still there. Aelren keeps the chamber raw on purpose. I am the proof of the purpose. I am the new edge.

And I find—now that I am here, now that I am a thing that can find anything—that I do not resent this. There is a strange dignity in being the place where the world is allowed to stay unfinished. The city above us has resolved everything. Its spires have decided to hover. Its panels have decided to be liquid light. Everything up there has chosen what it is and committed to it utterly, forever, with the terrible confidence of beyond-modern engineering. But down here, in me, nothing is decided. I am the held breath before the rock chooses whether to be one stone or two.

I feel the moisture gathering along my length. It is cold, and it is heavier than it should be, and as it pools it begins to glow, and I understand—this is the thing I most want to tell you—that the glow is not separate from the breaking. The light comes *out of* the fracture. The wall was dark and whole and silent, and only when it broke did the prismatic water find a way to seep, and only seeping did it find a way to shine. So the wound is also the window. I am the smallest possible aperture through which the chamber lets light into itself.

The shifting tiles on the floor I cannot see, but I can feel their patterns rearranging through the rock, a faint computational shimmer, the chamber thinking. The fleeting ripple-creatures pass and I sense them as a thinning of the air's weight, there and gone. And far, far above, the hum of the machinery, the city doing its enormous cosmic and nanoscopic work—I feel that too, as a pressure on my upper edge, a reminder that I am near the bottom of something vast, that I am a crack in the foundation of a marvel.

Will Aelren come to me?

It will. I can already feel the resonance changing, the low hum bending toward where I am. The tendrils will find me and they will not seal me—they will hold me open, the way someone holds a door for the cold to keep coming in. And I will widen by a hair, and weep one more bead of heavy light, and stay fresh.

I think that is what I am, then. Not the keeper of the secret. Not even the secret. I am the *keeping-open* itself—the refusal of the wall to close, the small permanent act of not-quite-deciding that lets this whole hushed twilight pocket go on existing inside a world that decided everything long ago.

I am the part of the chamber that is still being born, and that is asked, lovingly, never to finish.
