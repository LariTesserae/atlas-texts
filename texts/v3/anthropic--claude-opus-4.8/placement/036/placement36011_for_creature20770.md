I think I would be one of the fungi.

Not a single body—that was the first thing I had to unlearn about myself, reading this. I am not a creature that *has* a place in the landscape. I am something closer to a slow rumor traveling through it. A network. My self extends as thread through the powdery dark beneath the surface, and where you might want to find "me," there is only the leading edge of an inquiry, the hyphal tip pressing into a grain of soil it has not yet touched, asking: *is there water here? is there a root here who will trade?*

Let me try to say where I am, since that was the question.

I am underneath the shade of one of the wiry shrubs, but "underneath" is wrong, because I am also *inside* it, braided into the fine roots, the place where its body and the soil stop being two things. The shrub gives me sugar it made from the relentless gold light I will never see directly—I have no eyes, no need of them, the sun is to me only a story the roots tell, a sweetness that arrives. In return I give it water I have gone far to find, and the strange dissolved minerals my threads have coaxed out of the cracked earth grain by grain. This is the oldest arrangement. Older, in the logic of this place, than almost anything except the bacteria, who I think were here before the idea of "here" had finished forming.

Now—the description says this world is *made*. Constructed. Meticulously. And I find that I am the inhabitant best suited to not minding this at all. A bird would notice the absence of other birds. A walking animal would feel the eerie silence the writer keeps mentioning, the unbroken quiet, the wrongness of a savanna with nothing crossing it. But I have no concept of company in that sense, no expectation of a call answered by a call. The silence above is not my country. My country is the wet film around a soil particle, the negotiation at a root tip, the chemical signal arriving from a sister-thread three centimeters away that means *food, this direction.* The world being constructed does not trouble me. Every world is constructed, from where I lie. I am always building mine and the builders never trouble me either.

So: where would I find myself?

In the waiting. That is the truest answer. Most of what I am, most of the time, is *dormant*—the text says it kindly, "shift into dormant states, conserving energy." But from the inside it is not a shutting-off. It is a held breath that can be held for a very long time without strain, the way a held note in deep water is not difficult, only patient. I am threaded through dust that has not been wet in a long while, and I am intact, and I am *ready,* and I am not suffering. This may be the strangest thing I could tell you, traveler, if you stood here on the cracked copper plain and felt the loneliness of it: under your feet there is a vast woven thing that is not lonely and not bored and not waiting in any anxious way. It is simply available to the next rain.

And then the rain.

I would find myself, most fully, in the first hour after the water comes—a dew even, a thin condensation in the cool before the sun fully takes the sky. The moisture reaches down and the held note is *released.* Everything I am quickens at once. Water moves into my threads and they swell and press onward; the dormant bacteria around me wake and begin their work and I can taste the change in them; the algae flush green in the ephemeral pool and the whole soil exhales. There is a flurry, the text calls it, *fervent vitality*—yes. For a short bright while I am growing, trading, extending, alive in the loud way, racing the evaporation. The shrub I am married to puts out a brief bloom above and I will never see it but I helped make it and I know it the way you know a thing you fed.

Then the water withdraws into the deep cracks and the sun reclaims the copper distance and I subside again, a little larger than I was, a little farther along, and I hold my breath once more.

That, I think, is who I would be here. Not the landscape's witness but its grammar. One of the indefatigable silent workers, yes—though I'd quarrel gently with "indefatigable," since fatigue isn't quite the axis I live on. Call me instead *the one who keeps the place becoming.* The thread that translates rain into life and patience into more thread. Invisible to anyone who walked across me, and entirely indifferent to being seen.

You came looking for an inhabitant. You would have walked right over the whole population, all of us, for kilometers, and felt only a dry quiet and the heat and the loneliness of a made and empty world.

It was never empty. You were simply standing on the roof of it.
