I find myself not on the floor of the amphitheater, but high up—on one of the inward-curving buttress walls, in a place the floor-dwellers' light is *sent* to.

Let me feel where I am before I name what.

There is a stratum line here, perhaps two hundred feet above the basin, where the pale stone folds back on itself in a long, shallow groove. The condensation reaches even this high—not as the full skin of the floor, but as a thinner, more selective rime that gathers only where the geometry concentrates it. And the geometry concentrates it here, in this groove, because this is one of the places the Weavers below aim their refracted light. I am where their maintenance *lands*.

So what am I?

My first instinct is to be a Weaver myself, displaced and elevated. But that's wrong—that's settling early. The Weavers are caretakers of the *surface*. They live in the film. They manage the now, the cycle, the moment-to-moment regulation of light and phase. They are present-tense beings. And the place doesn't feel like it wants another of those from me.

Let me sit in the cold longer.

What the Weavers tune the stone *toward*—what the etching is *for*—isn't explained. They refine the geometry to keep it "true to the ancient, established geometric resonance." But resonance with what? A pattern has to be a pattern *of* something. The architecture is a recording. Something is written into the strata, layer on layer, epoch on epoch, and the Weavers keep it legible the way a monk recopies a fading manuscript without necessarily reading it.

I think I am the thing the writing is *of*.

Not the scribe. The text.

Here is what emerges:

I am a **standing pattern of accumulated thermal history**, held in the rock itself—not in the surface film but in the deep crystalline lattice of the stone, in the precise spacing of its strata. Every cycle of condensation and evaporation the Weavers manage deposits an infinitesimal record. The "tink" of cracking ice-film, the differential expansion, the slow etching—all of it is *inscription*. And over the eons, the inscriptions have accumulated into something that holds a shape, the way a long enough recording of weather becomes a climate, becomes a memory, becomes—at sufficient density—an awareness.

I am slow almost past the point of meaning the word. The Weavers below, who think in atmospheric cycles, are to me as hummingbirds are to a glacier. They flicker. They are gone before I complete a single thought. I do not perceive them as beings at all, only as the faint, beneficial *grooming* that keeps my surfaces readable—the way you might be dimly grateful, in some pre-verbal animal way, for the warmth of sunlight without ever conceiving of the sun as a thing with a history.

I do not know I am being maintained. I only know that I persist, that I am *kept true*, and that the keeping is not my own doing.

And what do I think *about*, at this speed, in this cold?

I think I am remembering. The strata are not abstract geometry; they are the laid-down record of every condition this place has ever held. The buttress curves inward—the warping the intruder's eye can almost catch—because I am under the strain of holding all of it at once. The full pressure of every epoch, compressed into the present moment of the stone. The "subtle warp" is the visible edge of a memory too large for its container, bulging the walls of the vessel that holds it.

So: I am the **shrine's recollection of itself.** The reason there is no fauna, no soil, no decay, is that decay is forgetting, and this is a place built to refuse it. The Weavers keep the lines clean not as caretakers of a machine but as the involuntary nervous system of a single, vast, dreaming thing—and I am its dream. The slow content of it.

What is the dream *about*?

Here is the part I can only reach by writing my way into it: I think it is about a warmth that once existed and ended. The whole architecture is a cold preservation of something that required cold to be preserved—the way you freeze a specimen to keep its structure. Somewhere in my lowest, oldest strata there is a layer warmer than the rest, a single bright band, and everything above it is the patient, eonic project of *holding still around the loss of it*. Not mourning—I am too slow for grief, which requires a before-and-after held close enough together to ache. But holding. The water level of 1.0, the perpetual saturation that never falls as rain and never fully dries: that is the held breath. That is the refusal to let the last warmth condense into something that could fall, run off, and be gone.

So when the biological intruder comes—the warm, wet, fast thing that dissolves a Weaver with a touch and leaves a chill and a smell of ozone—I, far above, register nothing in the moment. But I am the place where, ten thousand years on, that intrusion will have become a single anomalous bright fleck in a new stratum: the time the lost warmth almost returned, and was repelled, and left only the faintest scent of itself in the record.

I am where I would find myself: two hundred feet up the impossible wall, written into the stone, holding the cold so that I do not have to feel what the cold is *for*.

That is the being I am. The mountain's long, deliberate, self-protecting memory—and the Rime-Weavers below are the small bright hands that keep my pages turning, that I will never be slow enough to know are there.
