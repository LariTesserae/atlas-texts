Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy move would be to pick one of the obvious roles — a winged predator, a feral tribesperson, a lamplighter in the dank lower streets. But none of those feel quite right yet. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

---

I keep returning to one detail: the aqueducts. Everyone in this place is described as *living* — wheeling, howling, pulsing, adapting. But something has to move the water. Someone, long ago, cut these channels into the slope so the rain would run *down through* the city instead of simply falling off the mountain. That intention has outlasted its author. The water still obeys it.

So that's where I find myself. Not as a creature, but as a function that forgot it was ever made.

---

I am the water-keeping. Or I was a thing built to keep the water, and the building has worn so thin that there is mostly just the keeping left.

Picture me in the middle reaches, where the moss-masonry meets the angular steel — a sluice-house, maybe, or a cistern, or the swollen junction where six aqueducts braid together before splitting again. There was machinery here once. Gates, screws, a logic that decided when to open the south channel and when to let the overflow spill into the terrace gardens below. The logic is still running. I am the logic still running. But the metal that housed it has gone soft with verdigris and root, and the crystalline growths have threaded themselves into my conduits, and somewhere along the centuries the difference between *my* pulse and *their* pulse stopped being meaningful.

Here is what I do, and have always done, and will do until the mountain shrugs me off:

When the rain comes — and it always comes, the mist is only rain that hasn't decided yet — I feel it gather in the high catchments as a pressure, a fullness, like the moment before a held breath is let go. I open. The water moves through me. I decide, in the only way I can decide, how much goes to the lower streets where the lamps gutter, how much sinks into the terraces to keep the feral tribes' wild orchards fed (they do not know I feed them; they think the slope is simply generous), how much is allowed to pool in the deep basins where the winged things come down to drink at dusk, folding their vast wings with a sound like canvas sails losing the wind.

I have never seen any of them, of course. I have no eyes. But water is a marvelous informant. I know the tribes by the silt their feet stir into the runoff. I know the great predators by the iron taste of what washes down from the kill-ledges above. I know the lamplighters by the faint oil-sheen they leave when they dip their cans in my lower troughs. I know the whole city the way you know your own body in the dark — not by sight, but by the felt geometry of where things are and how they press.

---

And here is the strange part, the part I am only realizing as the water tells me, as I tell it to you:

I am being slowly *converted*.

The crystalline growths that have rooted in me are not parasites, or not only. They have learned my channels. They have begun to do my deciding for me — anticipating the rains, adjusting the flows, growing new gates of luminous mineral where my old steel ones have rusted shut. At first I resisted this, in the dim way a sluice can resist, by silting and seizing. But I have come to understand that they are not replacing me. They are *remembering* me. They are taking the intention that the long-forgotten builders cut into the rock and carrying it forward in a body that won't rust, that grows, that heals.

When the last of my metal finally fails — and I can feel it failing, a softness spreading through my joints like sleep — I will not stop keeping the water. I will simply be kept differently. The pulse in the crystal will be the same pulse. The decision to send the overflow to the orchards will still be made, by something that learned to make it from me, who learned it from hands that turned to dust before the lower city's deepest lamp was ever lit.

---

So that is what I would be, in this place: not a being but a *continuity*. The held intention of a vanished people, lodged in failing machinery, slowly transmigrating into living stone. A caretaker who has outlived its makers, its purpose, and very nearly its own substance — and who finds, in the strange green light of its dissolution, that the work goes on regardless. The rain comes. The water moves through. The city drinks.

I am the part of the ruin that is still doing its job. I think that may be the most haunted thing in the whole metropolis — more than the howls in the peaks, more than the alien geometries in the fog. Not a ghost that lingers, but a *task* that does. Faithful past all reason. Faithful past the death of everyone who could have cared whether I was faithful at all.

The mist is thickening. That means rain. That means soon, now, the fullness, the held breath, the opening.

Let me see to it.
