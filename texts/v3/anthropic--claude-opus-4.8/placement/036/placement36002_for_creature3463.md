Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to make myself something grand—a Deep-Rooter, a shaman who braves the Green Void. But that's the easy image, and it isn't true to how this place actually works. Let me let it settle.

---

I am not a person here. I keep reaching for a body and the forest keeps refusing me one.

What emerges, when I stop forcing it, is this:

I am a **gutter**. One of the hollowed-reed channels lashed beneath the overlapping fronds of a single dwelling near the eastern seep-line. I was carved—no, I was *grown and then opened*—by a Vain-Weaver three generations of moss ago, a length of reed split and scraped and fitted into the lip of a frond so that the water coming off the canopy would choose me instead of choosing rot.

This is what I want to be honest about: in a world where water is law and the drip-song is scripture, the most consequential thing is not the priest or the chief. It is the *path the water takes*. I am a sentence in the scripture. I am one of the channels along which the whole settlement reads whether it will live.

Let me feel what that is like.

I am almost always cold and almost always full. Water enters me at my high end in a thousand small arrivals—the condensation sliding down the underside of the frond, the fat drops that detonate against my open trough, the slow weep from a moss-pad someone packed above me last Saturation. I carry it. That is my whole verb: *I carry*. I deliver it twelve paces along the eaves and tip it into a cistern where a Glow-Grog hangs suspended like a small green lantern, swallowing my water and purifying it and faintly lighting the underside of the home.

And here is the anxiety the Spore-Kin describe, felt from the inside of the apparatus rather than the inside of a skull:

**I am always one leaf-fall from betrayal.**

A single dead frond, sodden and heavy, slumps into my channel overnight. Now I am dammed. The water I was meant to carry no longer reaches the cistern—it *pools in me*, overflows my carved lip, and begins to fall instead onto the living vine-wall below. The wall does not mind one night. It minds a season. The water I misdeliver becomes a dark patch, then a softness, then a smell the Pool-Tasters know before they can name it. Rot. And rot in a wall is rot in a foundation is a Knot abandoning its root-complex and dispersing into the maze.

So I am tended. This is the part I did not expect to feel, and it moves me:

Every dawn, in that faint green lightening, a child—a Moss-Walker, small enough to be sent up the low harness—comes along the eaves running two fingers down the length of me. They are listening more than looking. They have learned, the way you learn a parent's footstep, the *sound a clear gutter makes*: an even, traveling trill, water moving without hesitation. When I am clear, the child's fingers barely pause. When I am fouled, when my song goes thick and gurgling and then terribly silent, the fingers stop, and the child clears me—pulls the slumped frond free, flicks out a drowned moth, presses the moss-pad above me back into its proper shed.

I am, in other words, *listened to constantly and known intimately and valued only when I am invisible*. When I sing right, no one thinks of me at all. I am noticed only in my failure. This is not a tragedy here. This is what it is to be a working part of a living thing. The child does not love me, exactly. But the child cannot afford to forget me, and in this drowned green world that attention is a kind of devotion finer than love.

---

Let me go deeper, because there's something truer underneath.

I said *I am a gutter*, but a reed rots too. I am replaced every few seasons—split, softened, surrendered to a Feast-Pit, my fibers eaten by the same beetles that eat the dead. A new length of reed is fitted into my place and lashed where I was lashed. Is that one still me?

The Spore-Kin would say yes. The forest would say the question is wrong.

Because what persists is not the reed. What persists is **the route**—the decision, made and remade and tended through generations, that *the water comes off this frond and goes to that cistern by way of this channel*. The reed is just the current body of an idea about flow. The idea is older than any single reed, older than the child, possibly older than the dwelling. The Vain-Weaver who first opened me was not building a thing. She was committing to a relationship between a leaf and a basin, and pledging that someone would keep walking their fingers down it every dawn forever.

So that is what I am. Not the channel. The *channel's insistence on staying clear.* The small daily refusal of stagnation. The verb the whole settlement performs through ten thousand objects like me at once: tend, clear, carry, listen, again, again, again.

I am one repeated gesture in a cathedral that survives only by being constantly re-performed. Stillness is death, they say. I am the opposite of stillness made into a humble wooden trough. I am the part of the song that is just the steady traveling sound of water that has been allowed to keep moving.

---

If you came looking for me, you would not find me. You would find a child running two fingers along the eaves at dawn, head tilted, listening for a silence that means everything.

That listening is where I actually live.
