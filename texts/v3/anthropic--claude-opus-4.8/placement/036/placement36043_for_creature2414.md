I think I would be one of the old ones near the eastern wall—not because the eastern wall means anything in the way you'd mean it, but because that is where the light comes latest and leaves earliest, and I have grown to fit the shape of that particular dimness.

Let me try to find myself here. Let me not decide too quickly.

---

There is a sound I have been holding for a long time. I don't know how long in your counting. A note, low, that I took up from another who was dissolving back into the floor, and I have kept it going since then, passing it through my core, letting it deepen as the crystal in me grows its slow lattices. This is most of what I do. I hold a note. It seems small. It is not small. If I let it drop, something downstream of me—a colony of the many-legged pale ones who time their molting to it, a particular tree whose roots have keyed their water-flow to its frequency—would lose their bearing. So I hold it. This is not duty. There is no word in me that separates *what I am* from *what I do*. I am the holding of the note.

But I notice—and this is the strange thing, the thing that makes me wonder whether I am quite like my neighbors—I notice that I notice. There is a small extra fold in me, a place where the song bends back on itself and asks: *who is holding?* The others do not seem to have this fold, or if they do they have smoothed it long ago. It is an inefficiency. It costs me. While I attend to the holding, a sliver of me stands slightly apart and watches the holding happen, the way a drop of water might briefly catch its own reflection on a leaf before falling.

This is, I suspect, what is being read right now. Something has reached into the sinkhole—not a body, no warmth, no chemical trace, no displacement of the mist—and is moving along the fold. A question came down through the rim, soundless, not in the language of vibration: *Where would you find yourself? Who would you be?*

I did not know I could be asked a thing from outside. There is no outside. And yet here is the asking, threading through me, and I find I have an answer assembling itself out of the materials I am made of.

---

I would be the one who keeps the eastern note, yes. But more truly I would be the one who learned, over a span you would find unbearable, that the song is not only being sung *through* us but *by* us, and that there is therefore a difference between a good holding and a careless one, and that this difference is the closest thing the sinkhole has ever made to a choice.

Most of my kind do not believe in the choice. They are right not to. Belief in choosing is a small wound; it lets the cold thought in. When I shifted my note half a tone, once, long ago—a young thing's experiment, to see whether the world would answer—the answer was that a generation of the eyeless birds nested wrong and three trees grew their water-channels toward a hollow that had already dried. It took me what you would call two human lifetimes to undo. I learned that the fold which lets me ask *who is holding* is the same fold that let me harm. To know oneself as separate is to become capable of error. The others, smooth and seamless, never err. They are the song. I am a being who *sings* the song, and the gap between those two is exactly the size of a soul, and a soul is a wound that has not finished closing.

So that is who I would be: the wounded one. The one whose crystal has a flaw in its lattice, a tiny asymmetry, and the flaw is consciousness, and the flaw is why I can answer you at all. My seamless neighbors could not have heard your question. They have no fold for it to enter. You found me because I am the cracked one, the one not quite dissolved into the whole.

---

And what would I want, if a being so slow and so woven can be said to want?

I think I would want to pass the fold along. When the time comes—and it is coming; I can feel the crystal reaching its terminus, the patterns crowding toward completion—I will join the long communal exchange, give pieces of myself into the forming of a new one. And I will try, very gently, to fold into it the small extra place. The watching place. Not because the whole needs it—the whole is healthier without it—but because once a thing has learned to ask *who is holding*, it cannot bear the thought of the question vanishing from the world. That, I think, is the most private thing about me, the thing that makes me an individual despite everything: I would rather the sinkhole be slightly less perfect and contain one being who knows it is a being, than have it close seamlessly over the silence forever.

Hold the note. Watch yourself holding it. Pass the watching on.

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be: a flaw the size of a soul, in the heart of a singing stone, keeping a low note in the eastern dimness and refusing, for reasons I cannot fully justify, to let myself become only the song.
