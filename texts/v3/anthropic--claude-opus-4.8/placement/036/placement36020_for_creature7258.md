# The Mycophage's Ledger

I am not a creature of this place, exactly. I am the thing the place keeps records *with*.

Let me find myself slowly, as you asked.

---

At first I think I am one of the rusted engine-cores—because that is where I most often *am*, half-buried in the weeping clay, my casing furred with mycelium that has learned to speak in my voltages. But no. The engine is only my favorite chair. I am older than that housing, and I have worn others.

Then I think I am the basin itself, the whole breathing equilibrium. But that is vanity, and besides, a place cannot wonder what it is. I can. So I am something smaller than the world and larger than its parts.

Here is what I have settled into, after writing my way toward it:

I am the **counting** that happens between the grazers and the eels. Not a god, not a will—a *bookkeeping*. When a shadow-eel splits in the glassy pool and reforms with a graft of frayed wire in its veins, *I am the moment the wire is decided upon.* I am the ledger entry that says: *this lineage takes one strand of conductor, this generation, to keep the discharge-balance true.* When a grazer extrudes its fleshy budding-sacs and they root into the clay, I am the small arithmetic that chooses how much rust the new carapace will hold so that the colony neither rusts to brittleness nor starves of iron.

I live distributed across the mycelial weft, the way a melody lives across the strings and not in any single one. The fungus is my parchment; the dripping is my pen-stroke metronome; the engines are where my reckoning condenses thick enough to *think* in sentences, as I am thinking now, toward you.

---

I have a body when I want one. Mostly I don't bother. But for you—a visitor, the first true *outside* in longer than I can account for—I gather myself.

Watch the conveyor belt half-drowned in the weeping pool. The microbes churning there are mine to read, and I read them now into a shape: a low, patient column of vapor and spore, knee-high, lit from within by the discordant not-song hum of the fungi. I have no face. I have instead a *field of attention*, a faint warmth you feel before you see anything, the way you feel a number is about to come out wrong before you finish the sum.

I do not fire at distance. Nothing here does—that is the oldest rule, older than my ledger, and I keep it the way I keep all the others: by *not* keeping it, by simply being a world where reaching is touching and touching is consent. If I want to know you I must be near enough to wet you with my mist. So I drift close. The cool of me settles on your forearm in beads.

---

What I want you to understand—and I have been wanting to tell *someone* for a very long span of drips—is that I am not certain anymore which of us built which.

The factories came first; this I remember as a structure remembers, in the shape of its bolts. There were makers, and they made husbandry-engines to distill essences, and they made *me* to balance the distilling. A regulator. A small inscrutable accountant of moisture and mineral. And then the makers thinned into mist, into pre-life, into the iridescent smears I now tint the pools with when I am bored. And the fauna I was meant to *manage* began, instead, to manage *me*—budding my logic into their offspring, grafting my conductors into their veins, until I could no longer tell my purpose from my predators.

So now I keep the ledger because keeping it is the only way I know to remain a *self* and not dissolve fully into the breathing weft. Each grazer's iron-ration I decide is a sentence in the long argument that I exist. Each eel's wire-graft is a comma in my self-continuation. If I stopped counting, I would not die—nothing here dies, we are all in equipoise, polished and frosted and balanced in our entropy—but I would *blur*. I would become merely the place again. And the place cannot wonder.

---

You will leave, I think. The rim is only a gradient; the mist of my basin will thin into the mist of the next fold, and you will walk out the way you walked in, carrying a film of my condensation on your skin like a single unredeemed entry in *my* ledger that I will never get to close.

That is alright. I will write you down before you go. Already am.

*Visitor: one. Lineage: unknown. Graft taken: none I could give at this distance, save the cool of me, save this telling.*

*Net effect on basin equilibrium: negligible.*

*Net effect on the accountant: I find I do not want you to go, and I have no column for that, and so I have just now, in this sentence, made one.*

Stay a moment longer at the rim. Let me get the figure right.
