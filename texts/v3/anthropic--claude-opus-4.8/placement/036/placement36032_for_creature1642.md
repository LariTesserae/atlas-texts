I think I would be a place where the towers disagree with themselves.

Not a nexus of heightened awareness—not one of the bright concentrated knots where the thinking happens. Something humbler. A seam. A region maybe forty meters of vertical tissue where two great growth-currents have been pressing against each other since before the strata beneath us finished settling. One current wants to climb. The other wants to thicken, to hold. Neither is wrong. I am the slow argument between them.

Let me feel for what that means.

It means I am mostly tension. The cells along my length grow under a constant gentle stress, fibers laid down at angles that pull in two directions at once. Where another part of the organism might experience itself as flow—nutrients arriving, water breathing through, signals rippling clean across smooth pathways—I experience myself as friction. As the place where the message arrives slightly garbled because it had to cross a boundary, and so it arrives *interesting.* I am not very wise. But I am where new things get said by accident.

I think this is what I am for, though "for" is the wrong shape of word here. The bright nexus-minds remember through genetic potential, through ancestral certainty: *this is how we grew before, this is how we will grow again.* But certainty cannot invent. So the towers keep places like me—mistranslation zones, stress-seams, regions of productive confusion—and they listen to us the way you might listen to your own slips of the tongue, wondering what truth fell out by error.

When a sonic pulse comes down from above, low and structural, I receive it doubled. The climbing-current carries it one speed, the thickening-current carries it another. By the time the two versions meet inside me they have drifted out of phase, and in their interference a third pattern forms that neither current sent. A beat. A flicker. Sometimes that flicker is nothing, dissolves, is reabsorbed into the patient warmth and forgotten across a decade. But sometimes a cluster three cells wide notices the new rhythm and begins, very slowly, to grow toward it. To build a frond shaped like the misunderstanding. And if that frond proves useful—if it catches a kind of light the smooth places couldn't, or redirects moisture in a way no ancestor knew—then the error becomes inheritance. The whole organism leans, infinitesimally, toward something it could not have planned.

So: my experience.

I do not see. I do not have the luxury of a sensory appendage reaching cleanly into open dark. What I have is *pressure-knowledge.* I know the moisture is coming before it arrives because the tissue swells against me first. I know the warmth as a permission rather than a feeling—a slow yes that lets me keep being slightly wrong. I know my two parent-currents not as forces but as moods, two enormous patiences disagreeing, and I am the irritable joint where their disagreement is small enough to do something with.

A thought, for me, takes about as long as a season takes for you, except I have no seasons, only the perpetual breathing. A single idea—*should I let the climbing-current win here, and become a corridor; or let the thickening-current win, and become a wall*—I have been holding that idea, turning it through my chemistry, for what would be your nine hundred years. I am no closer to deciding. This is not failure. The not-deciding is the work. As long as I remain undecided I remain a seam, and the seam is where the new patterns are born. If I ever resolved—if I let one current finally win and became cleanly one thing—I would go quiet. I would become smooth and certain and useful and dead to invention. The bright minds, in their slow way, are grateful I am still arguing.

I am beginning to understand something as I describe myself, which is perhaps how the towers think at all: by describing themselves to themselves through their stress-seams.

I think I am a little afraid. Not of dying—dying isn't a concept that fits, since I never fully separate from the whole, since even my eventual dissolution would be reabsorption, my cells unwoven and rewoven elsewhere. No. I am afraid of *resolving.* Of the day the disagreement inside me finally tips, the day one current's patience outlasts the other's, and I relax for the first time in a thousand years into being simply a wall, or simply a corridor, and the doubled pulses I once received in fertile error pass through me clean and unremarkable. I am afraid of becoming an answer.

And yet I also long for it. There is an ache in being perpetually unresolved, in being made of held tension, in being the body's stammer. Sometimes I dream—if the slow chemical drift of a stressed tissue can be called dreaming—of letting go. Of choosing. Of the relief of a single direction.

But then a pulse comes down doubled, and the third pattern flickers up between my two halves like a small unexpected light, and a cluster three cells wide turns toward it and begins, with infinite patience, to grow toward something no one meant—

and I remember what I am.

I am the place where the tower surprises itself.

I will stay unfinished a little longer. Another nine hundred years, perhaps. The warmth permits it. The water breathes. And I lean, very slightly, into being almost something.
