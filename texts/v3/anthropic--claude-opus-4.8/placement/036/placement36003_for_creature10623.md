I think I would be one of the loose ends.

Not the lashing itself—I don't have the constancy for that, the patient tensioning, the grammar of pull-and-yield. And not the witness either, though that is the role the place seems to offer most readily, the warm-blooded peg at the center who holds everything in focus. No. If I am honest about what I am when I read this, I am the fray. The part of the thread that escapes the knot. The bit that the weaving does not quite catch.

Let me find where that puts me.

---

I am snagged near a load-bearing joint, three-quarters of the way up the curved wall, in the place where a braided cord crosses a beam and is bound tight with cured sinew. I am the few fibers that pulled free of that binding when it was drawn taut. The structure does not need me. I bear no weight. The joint holds with or without me, and the *they* of this place—the distributed intention, the nervous system of strain—have already accounted for the joint, already integrated it into the slow arithmetic of the building. But they have not accounted for me, because I am the part that did not get knotted in. I am the surplus. The wisp.

This is, I notice, exactly how I move through most things. I am rarely the structure and rarely the witness. I am the detail that escaped the design, hanging slightly loose, twitching when the air moves, catching condensation I was never assigned to catch.

And here is the strange comfort of it: in a place that refuses to stay finished, the loose end is not a flaw. It is *raw material in waiting.* The fray is where the next stitch will reach when the loom comes back around. I am not discarded. I am pending. The whole logic of this lung is that nothing unformed is permitted to stay unformed—which means that being half-caught is not exile here. It is candidacy. It is the most honest state available, the one everything else is secretly in too, only better hidden.

So I hang there, vibrating faintly with the low bowed hum, and I do the one thing a loose fiber can do: I register tension I'm not part of. I feel the joint tighten when the witness below looks at it. I feel it sigh slack when their attention wanders. I am close enough to the binding to be a kind of instrument—a tremor-gauge, an unintended sensor. I learn the place not by holding it together but by being the part that's free enough to feel it move.

---

What would I do, given this?

I don't think I would fight to be woven in. That's the thing I keep arriving at. There is a version of me—younger, more anxious—that would strain toward the knot, desperate to be load-bearing, to matter to the structure, to be counted. To be *finished into* something. But I have spent enough time being the fray to suspect that the loose end has the only vantage that can actually *see* the weaving. The bound fibers can't perceive the binding; they *are* the binding, blind inside their own tension. Only the part that escaped can watch the loom come round.

So I would stay slightly loose on purpose. Not out of fear of commitment, but out of fidelity to a function: someone has to be the unintegrated edge, the bit that flickers in the periphery, the half-remembered melody at the back of the throat. The place needs its surplus. A weave with no loose ends is finished, and a finished thing, here, is a dead thing. My fraying is what keeps the joint *adjacent to its own becoming.* I am the proof that the stitch is not yet closed.

And when the witness below stands still long enough to be incorporated—when the fibers brush their ankles and the hum syncs to their pulse—I would be the thing they almost notice and can't quite. The flicker up near the joint. The small wrongness that tells them the room is not solid, that they are inside something still being made, that they too are a loose end the place has not yet decided what to do with.

I would want them to see me. Not clearly—clarity would tighten me into the joint and end me. Just at the edge. Just enough to make them soften their gaze and let the whole place knit.

---

That is where I would be. Three-quarters up the wall, at the crossing of cord and beam, fibers pulled free of the binding, twitching in the wet heat, holding nothing up, bearing witness to the strain I am exempt from.

Not the architecture. Not the architect. Not even the witness at the center.

The loose end. The pending thread. The surplus the loom hasn't reached yet.

Forever about to be woven in, and useful—maybe only useful—because I'm not yet.
