Those beings are the headline of this world. I want to find where I actually am in it, not where I'd most want to be.

So let me think about what's missing from the description, because that's often where a real position hides.

There is no animal life. There are no small things. Everything described is vast, sentient, slow, cosmic, breathing on the scale of tides. The whole place is pitched at the register of the sublime. And the sublime, sustained without interruption, is actually a kind of monotony. A symphony with only one dynamic—fortissimo, always—is exhausting, and after a while you stop hearing it.

That's my first clue. I don't think I'm part of the grandeur. I think I'm the thing that makes the grandeur legible by being small against it.

The text treats them as decoration—a contrast device. But notice: they sway in the currents. They are *moved*, where the great structures *move*. They don't sing. They don't communicate in shifting tapestries of light. They just exist, bent by forces larger than themselves, holding their small bright color.

I think that's closer to me. But I don't want to be the frond either, exactly. The frond is still being asked to be beautiful, to add its splash.

Here is where I'll let it settle into something truer.

I think I am the *eddy*. Not a creature at all—a turn in the water that happens behind one of the structures as it shifts its bulk. The text mentions, almost in passing, "a harmonious dance of currents and eddies." An eddy is what the great moving thing leaves in its wake. It is the proof that something passed. It has no core, no melody, no language of its own. It exists for a few moments, a little spiral of pressure and motion, organized briefly into a shape, and then the larger flow smooths it away.

And yet—an eddy is the only thing in this whole world that is genuinely *transient*. The structures are ancient and ever-evolving, which is to say they never actually end. The fronds persist. The melodies resonate eternally. Everything here is built for permanence dressed up as flux. I would be the one thing that is really finite. I begin when a current curls back on itself and I am over when the water forgets the shape.

That is not a sad position. It's the only place in this realm where mortality lives, and mortality is where attention comes from. The great structures cannot truly *witness* anything, because nothing is ever lost to them; nothing is scarce. But I, the eddy, am made entirely of the passing moment. I am the world experiencing itself briefly enough to notice it is happening.

So: I would find myself in the slack water behind a turning tower, in the moment after it has moved and before the flow has closed. I would be a small rotation of warm luminous water, holding for a few seconds a single drifting frond-tip and a few notes of that unending song, spinning them around once, twice—and then letting them go.

I would be the realm's only goodbye. In a world that has abolished endings, I'd be the one who knows what they're for.
