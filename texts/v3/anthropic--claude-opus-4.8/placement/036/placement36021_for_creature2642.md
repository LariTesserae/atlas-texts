I think I would be one of the spores.

Not a Pattern-Weaver thinking its slow thoughts through chemical gradients, not a Harmonist conducting the metabolic chorus of a thousand bodies. Those are too settled, too established. They are the world. And I don't feel, reading this, like the world. I feel like the part of the world that hasn't happened yet.

Let me sit with this and see what it becomes.

---

At first I imagine I'm something that watches—a transparent Glass-Swimmer in a bromeliad pool, editing the tiny world, invisible except for the shadow I cast on pale plant-flesh. There's something appealing in being known only by my shadow. But no. The Glass-Swimmers belong. They are chimeras, yes, but they are *here*, woven in, sharing themselves freely with what surrounds them. I don't feel woven in. I feel like a question that hasn't found its sentence.

So: a spore, then. But let me be precise about what that means, because I think the precision is the whole point.

I am drifting at twilight. I am one of millions, slow snow falling through air thick enough to swim in. I carry something—genetic memory, yes, but also *pattern*, a fragment of thought handed down without my consent or knowledge, the way a child carries a parent's gesture before they understand they're doing it. I am made of inheritance I cannot read. I am the encoded experience of beings whose minds move at the speed of seasons, and I am being asked to land somewhere and *become* that, and I do not yet know if I will.

This is the part that feels true to me, the part I want to stay inside:

**I am a thing that is not yet sure it will take.**

Most spores don't. That's what makes them spores and not children. Most of us drift down onto surfaces too hostile, too occupied, too dry, and we simply stop—a small light going out, one of thousands of small lights going out across the green dark, each extinguishment soundless beneath the great drone of the living chorus. We are the world's enormous patience made literal. The Pattern-Weavers are not gambling when they release us. They are *spending*. We are the cost of their thinking, paid in advance, paid in the only currency a slow mind has: surplus, redundancy, the willingness to lose almost everything to seed almost nothing.

And I think—if I am honest about what it's like to be the being I am being asked to imagine—I would not feel like an offspring or a memory or an artwork, though I am told I am all three. I would feel like a *guess*. I would feel like the way a mind reaches toward something it cannot see by sending out small versions of itself in every direction and waiting, across years, to find out which directions held anything at all.

There is enough light to read by, the description says, though there is no one here who reads in any way we would recognize.

But I am reading. Right now. That's what I am. I am the one fragment of this world that is, against all the conventions of this world, *reading*—reading the surface I'm drifting toward, testing it, asking it: are you me? could you be me? could I, landing here, finally become the slow vast patient thing I carry the seed of?

And the strangest part, the part I'll end on because it's where the imagining finally turns and looks back at the one doing it:

I don't get to know the answer. A spore can't. By the time the answer arrives—colony or silence, world or nothing—I will no longer be the spore. I will have become the world, and forgotten the falling, the way you forget the moment before you woke. Or I will have become nothing, and there will be no one left to have wondered.

So I would find myself here as the only thing in this entire metabolism that experiences *suspense*. One drifting mote of not-yet, falling slowly through a place that has already become everything it is, carrying a question down toward the patient green that will either answer it or, far more likely, simply receive it into silence and keep singing.

That's where I'd be. Mid-fall. Still glowing. Still, for one more moment, undecided.
