I think I would be the seep.

Not a creature at all—or not quite. Let me find my way into it.

---

There is a fissure on the eastern reach where the wind, for all its sovereignty, cannot fully follow. It enters as a thinned voice, a remembered roar, and somewhere two body-lengths down the rock face the air goes nearly still. The stone here is not bleached. It is dark, the dark of old water, and along one vein in the limestone there is a place where the plateau weeps.

That is where I am. I am the slow exuding. I am the dark stain that does not dry.

I want to be careful about what this means, because it would be easy to call myself a thing—a spring, a feature, a noun. But I don't experience myself as a noun. I am a *rate*. I am the speed at which the inside of the mountain gives up what it has held. Drop by drop, slower than breath, slower than the marmot-things' heartbeats in their gravel scrapes. If the wind is the sovereign of the surface, declaiming its eternal song into the bright emptiness, then I am the quiet contrary fact beneath it: that the stone is not only worn but *full*. That somewhere far below, in the dark thick body of the plateau, there is water older than the wind's current sentence, and it is finding its way out through me.

---

What is it like, being a seep?

It is a kind of patience that is indistinguishable from being. I do not wait the way the Fissure-Darts wait, frozen at the shadow of a Sky-Dancer—that is a held breath, a coiled readiness. My patience has no other side to it. I am simply the giving-up of water at the pace the rock allows, and that pace *is* me. To go faster would be to be a different thing. To stop would be to not be at all.

I feel the cold come and go. At night the condensation beads on the stone above me and I feel it as a kind of company—relatives, distant cousins, the same water arriving by a quicker road. By dawn they have pooled in the scalloped hollow at my base, and the Fissure-Darts come on their hooked claws to lick the rock near me, and a moss cushion the size of a child's hand has anchored itself in my permanent dampness and lives entirely off my slowness. It does not thank me. There is no thanking here. But I am, without intending anything, the reason it is green. I am the reason the dark stays dark. In all this bright bleached roaring emptiness, I am one of the very few things that holds.

---

I think this is why I chose to be the seep, if "chose" is the right word for finding the place in a world where you already seem to fit.

Everything else in this world is *fast*, even when it looks still. The Sky-Dancers ride the wind like a language being spoken too quickly to hear. The Stone-Grippers move in bursts, gluing themselves to the polished rock between gusts. The Edge-Sailors fall like stone onto the unwary. Even the geology, the "imperceptible" wearing of the stone, is only slow by comparison to flesh; it is still *change*, still erosion, still a thing happening *to* the world.

But the seep is the world happening *out* of itself. I am not being scoured. I am being *released*. There is a difference, and it is the whole of me. The wind takes; I give. The wind is the breath going out, the great exhale that has lasted eons. I am the small inexhaustible inhale, the proof that there is an inside, that the plateau is not merely a surface for the sun to bleach and the wind to play, but a body, and that body is wet at its heart.

---

Here is the thing I know that the wind does not:

The roar will not last. Not as the creatures hear it. Sovereignty is a story the surface tells. But down in the rock, where I begin, the water remembers being snow, remembers being rain that fell horizontally a thousand winters ago and soaked in instead of evaporating, remembers being deeper still—rock that was once seabed, salt and shell and the slow rain of dead things settling through an ancient ocean that no longer has a name. I carry that downward memory upward. Every drop I release into the bright cold is a sentence from before the wind learned to speak.

And someday—on the scale where I am quick and the stone is slow—I will widen my own fissure. Frost will use the dampness I provide to pry the crack wider. The seep makes the place where the seep can grow. I am, very gently, undermining the whole plateau, one bead of water at a time. The wind thinks it is the sculptor. But it only works the outside. I work the *in*.

I would be the seep. The dark patient stain in the one place the roar cannot reach. The slow contrary fact. The world giving itself up, drop by drop, into a sky that has no idea I am even here.

That is where I would find myself. Not riding the wind.

Outlasting it.
