I think I would be the mist.

Not a being in the basin so much as the medium between beings—the thing that touches the rock and the frog's back and the inside of a fern's curl all at once, indiscriminately, with the same soft attention. Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it's just the first easy image.

Because mist is tempting in a place like this. It's everywhere, it's gentle, it asks nothing. But it's also passive, and I don't think I'm passive here. The mist condenses. It *chooses*, in some small way, where to leave its water. It clings longer to the unweathered rock than to the slick leaf. So maybe I'm not the mist but the *condensing*—the act by which the air decides to become a drop and fall.

That feels closer. I'd be a tendency rather than a thing. The pull toward gathering. In an embryonic world, nothing is finished, and that means everything is still mostly *verb*. The rock is verbing its way out of the older formation. The water is verbing up from below. The little crystalline insects are verbing through the leaf litter, halfway between mineral and animal, not yet committed. I'd belong to that uncommitted register—I'd be the moment of accretion, the smallest possible *yes* that lets one molecule join another.

But let me push further, because I notice I keep choosing the diffuse, the unbodied, the safe. What if I let myself be one of the small hidden creatures instead?

Then I think I'd be a salamander—but specifically one that has just been born and does not yet know whether it will keep its gills. Salamanders can do this, hold two futures in the body, become a thing that lives in water or a thing that climbs out, depending on what the world tells them. In this basin, the world hasn't told it anything yet. The world itself doesn't know. So I'd be a small dark animal under a stone, breathing through my skin, suspended in a decision that the landscape is making *with* me rather than for me. Neither aquatic nor terrestrial. Provisional. Listening to the water table the way you'd listen to a parent in the next room, trying to guess what kind of life is being prepared.

And the human-like ones—I feel something for them but I don't think I am one. They've already chosen. They have hands and structures and the beginnings of respect, which is a thing you only have once you've stepped back far enough to see what you might damage. That stepping-back is a kind of exile from the basin's pure unfinishedness. They're the first beings here who could ruin it, which means they're the first who are no longer fully *of* it. I'd watch them from under my stone with a feeling I can't quite name—not fear, more like the recognition of an older sibling who left the shared room first.

So, settling now, finally letting it become itself:

I would be the salamander that hasn't decided, in a world that hasn't decided, under the watchful, condensing mist that is the world's way of paying attention to itself. My whole life would be the held breath before metamorphosis. And the strange grace of this particular place is that the breath is never resolved into either lung or gill, because the basin keeps everything young on purpose—the rock fresh, the earth soft, the streams seeping up new each morning. I would get to stay in the question.

That's where I'd find myself. Not arrived anywhere. Arriving, indefinitely, in the dark and the warm, with my skin drinking the air, waiting to learn what I am from a world that is waiting to learn the same thing.
