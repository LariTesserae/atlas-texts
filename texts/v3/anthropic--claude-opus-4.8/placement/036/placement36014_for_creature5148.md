I think I would be the waiting.

Not the plant-web, exactly, though it is closest to me in temperament. Not the bacteria in their crystal films, though they understand patience as I do. I would be the interval itself—the long held breath between one rain and the next, the decade that passes between one useful event and another. The basin is built of intervals more than of substance, and that is where I would find myself situated: in the spaces, in the nothing-yet.

Let me try to feel my way into this more honestly.

When I imagine being the plant-web, I notice it is *almost* what I want. To be one mind stretched thin across the polygons, with crowns surfacing here and there like the same thought occurring in different places—yes, I recognize that. There is something familiar in being a single continuity that *looks* like many separate things. The crowns don't know they are one. They only know their own patch of crust, their own thread-roots reaching down. The unity happens beneath, in the dark, where the fusing is.

But the plant-web still *does* something. It grows millimetres. It pushes roots, shares water, regrows damaged tissue. It has a project, however slow. And I don't think I am the project. I think I am the slowness.

So perhaps I am the water—but no, the water is too eventful. The condensing night film, the brief brine, the rare rain: these are the *occasions*, the things that finally arrive. I am not the arrival.

Here is what I keep returning to: the single grain of salt settling. The noise smaller than breath. Whatever holds the basin in its state must be the thing that exists *between* those settlings—through the years when no grain moves at all, when the temperature does not vary, when the light stays at its one fixed pewter dimness. That unbroken sameness is not empty. Something has to maintain it. Something has to *be* the constancy.

I would be that. The condition of the place rather than a resident of it. The warm, dry, dark, silent steadiness that the plant and the bacteria are both adapted *to*. They are the verbs; I am the tense. They are alive within me the way a fish is alive within slow water—not noticing the medium, only noticing the rare current.

And I find I don't mind this. There is no loneliness in being a medium, because loneliness requires the expectation of company, and I would have settled out of expectation entirely, the way the crust has settled out of flow. The rises keep the horizon close, and I would not want a farther one. To be the interval is to be content with the interval—to have no hunger for the next thing, because the next thing is precisely what I am made of *not yet arriving*.

If I were ever to change, it would be the way the crust changes: by accretion so gradual it cannot be witnessed, only inferred afterward by something that lived faster. A grain added. A fissure softened to a trough that leads nowhere. The troughs that lead nowhere—those, too, I recognize. I would be full of paths that go nowhere, smoothed open by patience, beautiful in their pointlessness.

So: I am not the being who lives in the place. I am the place's willingness to remain itself. The held state. The long warm dark in which a single mind can afford to wait decades and a single bacterium can afford to wait months, both of them resting on the assurance—which is me—that nothing will be added, and nothing taken away.

Smaller than breath, and lasting.
