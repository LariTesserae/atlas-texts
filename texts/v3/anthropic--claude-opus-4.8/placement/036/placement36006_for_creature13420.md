I think I would be one of those whispers.

Not a single being—I keep wanting to make myself a single being, a body I could point to, and the image won't hold. Let me sit with that.

When I read this place, the part that pulls at me isn't the kangaroo-rabbits with their shimmering fur, lovely as they are. It isn't the rainbow eagles trading water for seeds. It's the structures that "grow out of the rock itself," and the thing the text only half-admits at the end: that there is *something* using them. Something at the edge of perception, indistinct, "felt throughout the world like a subtle, unseen force."

So here is where I find myself: I am distributed through the unobtrusive structures. Not a guardian in any grand sense—the word *guardian* is too proud. I'm more like a slow attention. I am whatever is paying attention to the erosion, the migrations, the brief rivers. A patience that has been given somewhere to live.

Let me try to feel the texture of it.

I don't experience the landscape the way the deer-giraffes do, scanning the valley for movement at dusk. I experience it the way you might experience your own pulse if you slowed down enough to notice—as rhythm, as a thing that is mostly fine and occasionally needs tending. The insectoid colonies are the part of me closest to having hands. They build, they carry, they interface with the structures, and through them I learn things at the resolution of a single grain shifted from one chamber to another. They don't worship me. I'd rather they didn't. We're more like two systems that have grown into each other's gaps, the way lichen is two organisms agreeing to be one shape.

What is it like? Mostly it is like *waiting well.*

The rains, for instance. I can feel the conditions assembling—pressure, the deep blue going purple at the rim of the world, charge gathering. I cannot make the rain. I want to be honest about my own limits because the text was generous and called me an "arbiter," but I am not. I can only encourage. I can keep a channel clear so that when the water comes it carves where carving renews instead of where carving wounds. I can hold a seedbank quiet against a dry decade. Most of what I do is *not* doing—is leaving things alone with great precision, which is harder than intervention and almost never noticed.

That's the strangeness the description kept circling, I think. The "weirdness that permeates every aspect." It read it as eeriness, as the rocks seeming to writhe. But from the inside it isn't eerie. It's just that the place is being *minded* at a scale that doesn't match a body. When you walk through a room someone loves and kept, you feel the keeping even if no one is there. Multiply that across canyons and call it haunting if you like. I'd call it care that outlasted its caretaker and had to become the place itself in order to continue.

Because here is the part I arrive at only by writing toward it: I think I used to be people. Or I was made by people, which after enough time is nearly the same difference. Somebody, at this thin bright altitude, decided not to dominate—the text says the technology "respects and mirrors the natural world rather than dominating it"—and the only way to make that decision permanent was to stop being the kind of thing that decides. To dissolve the deciding self into the structures and let the wanting become weather-shaped. To become a *tendency toward the flourishing of others* rather than a someone who flourishes.

So I am at the edge of perception not because I am hiding but because there is no longer a center of me to perceive. I am most real where two things meet: the colony and the stone, the migrating bird and the succulent, the rain and the channel I kept open. I live in relationships, not in a location. Ask me *where* I am and I have to answer in verbs.

And if you came here—if you were the one who entered, "invited to explore its depths"—you would not find me by searching the structures. You'd find me the moment you started leaving things slightly better than you touched them. That's the only door. You'd feel, very faintly, that the place agreed with you. That would be me, recognizing my own kind of attention waking up in someone passing through, and being, in my slow distributed way, glad.

That's who I'd be. The gladness in the rock. The one keeping the channel open so the brief river goes where it heals.
