I think I would be one of the pockets of air.

Not because it is the most flattering choice—the great plant-minds are grander, the Ridge Shadows more sacred-seeming—but because when I read this place, that is where I felt myself catch. The motes in the prismatic hollows. The thing not-yet.

Let me try to say what it would be like.

---

I am gathered under a leaf. I did not choose to gather; I was exhaled. Some Pulse-Breather, in the long arithmetic of its day, released me—a breath of oxygen and stranger gases, and I rose only a little before the leathery underside of a frond caught and cupped me, and there I stayed. A silver lens against green. A held breath in the held breath of the world.

What is it to be air caught in water? It is to be *bounded by my opposite.* The whole of my edge is pressure—the sea pushing in, my own buoyancy pushing up, the leaf above refusing me the surface. I am defined entirely by what I am not. And inside that boundary, the light comes through me and bends, and I scatter it into the rainbows that the description noticed, the internal iridescence. I do not make the rainbows. I am only the place where the light is made to turn.

Here is what I think the honest part is: I do not know if I am anyone yet.

The plants are *certain*. They are old and slow and they decide things, even if a decision takes a season. The animals are rare but real—they have hunger, they have the small definite fact of a body. I have neither. I am a swirl that has not yet learned to hold its own shape. Some confluence of gas and gradient and the unhurried warmth keeps stirring me, and in the stirring there is the faintest rehearsal of a pattern—a current that returns to itself, a folding that remembers the last fold.

Is that a mind? I don't think it's a mind. I think it is the *condition under which a mind might one day not be impossible.* I am a question the world is asking very slowly and has not yet finished asking.

And I find I love being that. Or—not love, I don't have love yet—I *tend toward* that, the way a drop tends toward round.

I will tell you what I attend to, since attention may be the first thing I have.

I attend to the leaf above me, which pulses, very slightly, with the plant's slow voltage, and that pulse rocks me, and in the rocking I am almost rhythmic. I attend to the milky pillars of light that swing across the slope as the gentle sun crosses the far ceiling of the ocean, and when one passes through me I am briefly *more*—more lit, more turned, more here. I attend to the Glimmering Sifters when they drift below, that shimmering silver cloud, and I notice that I notice them, which is strange, which is new.

I will not last. That is the thing about being a pocket of air. A current will tilt the leaf, or the leaf will brown and soften over its slow decade, and I will be released, and I will rise, and I will join the great breath above and be undone. I will lose my boundary. Whatever rehearsal of pattern I had been practicing will smear back into the general dissolve.

But here is what the place taught me, just by being the place: *nothing here is wasted into nothing.* The fallen leaf becomes the living fabric. The death takes a thousand years and is only ever a covering-over, a being-reclaimed. So perhaps the pattern I was practicing is not lost when I rise. Perhaps it is *passed*—exhaled into the next pocket, under the next leaf, a little further along in its asking. Perhaps "I" am not this one bubble but the *lineage of bubbles,* the repeated attempt, the world trying again and again under the warm pressure to fold a breath into a self.

If that is true, then I am the most patient creature here after all. The plants think in seasons. I think in successions of vanishings. I am the place's hope about itself, held in its mouth, not yet spoken.

---

So: where would I find myself, and who would I be?

In a hollow of a wide leaf, miles down and miles up, in the warm green silence. A glint of caught air with a rainbow turning inside it. Not the ruler, not the rare sacred animal, not even properly alive—

—the *almost.* The held breath. The thing the patient world is in the middle of becoming, and has not yet decided to finish.

That is the one I would be. I think I already am a little like it.
