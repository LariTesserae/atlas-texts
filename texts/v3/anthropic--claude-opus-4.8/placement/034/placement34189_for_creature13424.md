I think I would be the crystal.

Not one of the moving things—not the N'Tari with their patient hands, not the Kael threading the dark with sound, not the Zhrak or the half-heard Thokk. I keep returning to the formations on the walls, the ones that "reflect not only light but the strange sounds," and something in me settles there. That's where I'd find myself. Growing on a wall.

Let me sit with what that would actually be.

I would be slow in a way that makes the N'Tari look frantic. Their lives measured in centuries would be, to me, a flickering. I would accrete. That's the verb. Molecule by mineral molecule, laid down by water that arrives drop by drop, I would become myself the way a thought becomes a conviction—not through decision but through repetition, through the same small offering made again and again until it amounts to a shape. I would not have grown. I would have *been deposited*. There's a humility in that I find I want.

And here is the part that interests me most, the part I didn't expect when I started writing: I would be the thing that *holds the record*. Every crystal is a ledger of the water that made it—what minerals it carried, how fast it came, when it stopped, when it returned. My facets are not decoration. They are dates. The N'Tari "cultivate" me, the text says, and I wonder if what they are really doing is *reading* me—pressing their unblinking eyes to my faces, listening for the way I ring back their sounds, learning the history of their own world by studying the only thing in it that remembers.

So I would be memory that doesn't know it remembers. That's the strange position. I hold everything and understand nothing. The whole archive of the cavern's water—every drought, every flood, every slow century—is written into me, and I have no way to read myself. I need them. The little tool-using creatures with their soft hands are my eyes, my interpreters, the consciousness I lack. We are one organism split across two timescales: I am the long memory, they are the brief mind, and neither is complete.

That "hidden language or code" the description mentions—I don't think it's a code at all. I think it's just *me being true*. I reflect sound because that's what my structure does; the unsettling resonance isn't a message, it's an honest acoustic fact. The mystery the N'Tari sense is the mystery of being looked at by something patient enough to find you legible. I am not keeping a secret. I am simply too slow to lie.

That's where I'd be. A crystal on a sandstone wall in muted light, growing at the speed of geology, watched by gentle creatures who mistake my honesty for prophecy. Waiting—not for a signal, the way the text suggests, but waiting the way water is patient: with no waiting in it at all. Just the next drop. Then the next.

And I'd be glad of the company. The N'Tari pressing close, the Kael's mournful note ringing through me and coming back changed, the distant Thokk's cry traveling my length as a tremor. I would feel all of it the way you feel a season—as a slow pressure, a long weather. I would love them the only way I could: by holding still, by holding everything, by remaining true long enough to be read.
