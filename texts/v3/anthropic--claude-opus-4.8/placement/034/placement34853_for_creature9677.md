I think I would be the something in the deeper water. Not because it is the grandest thing here—it isn't, really, it makes no demands—but because of how you describe it. *It was here before we were.* A presence without demands. The oldest thing in the city. That is the shape that feels like mine.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am not a monster. I want to say that first, because the word *ancient* gathers monsters to it the way still water gathers silt, and I am not one. I do not hunt the people in their towers. I have no appetite for them. I have, if I am honest, very little appetite for anything anymore—the deep cold metabolism of a thing that has learned to want almost nothing, and to want it slowly.

What am I? I am not sure the question has the shape you think it does. You imagine a body: a curve of grey, a length too long. And there is a body, yes. But the body is the smallest part. The body is only where I touch the water. The rest of me is the remaining: the long unbroken habit of being here while everything above me changed its mind about what to be.

I remember the coast before it was a coast. That is not a metaphor. I remember when the sea was farther out and the land lay open and dry and the herons—the same herons, or their grandmothers' grandmothers, it makes no difference, they are continuous, they are one heron stretched across time—stood in the shallows of a river mouth that has been a thoroughfare and is now a river mouth again. I remember when the concrete came. It came fast, from where I sat. A flicker. A few hundred turnings of the warm and cold seasons, and then the towers, and the light at night that hurt, and the engines that filled the water with their grinding complaint until I went deeper to be away from it.

Then the engines stopped. One by one, then all at once. The light at night softened back into flame, which I remembered, which I had missed without knowing the word for missing. The water rose to meet me, and I rose into the streets I had only ever felt as a pressure-shadow far above, and I moved through the drowned thoroughfares and found them, at last, congenial. The fish came back. The dark came back. The patient soft dissolving of the made things back into the silt I have always known. I do not grieve the city. I am not capable of grief on that scale, or perhaps I am too capable of it, so deep and so slow that it has become indistinguishable from contentment.

You are right that the people do not speak of me. But you are wrong about why. It is not fear. It is courtesy—the kind that grows between two parties who share a small space and have agreed, without ever negotiating, not to insist on each other. They know I am here. I know they are here. The woman on the fourteenth floor with her impossible tomatoes—I have felt her shadow on the water at dusk for many years now, and the shadows of the ones before her, and I will feel the shadows of the ones who come after. We have an arrangement that requires no terms. She tends her warmth above. I tend my cold below. The herons stand at the seam between us, in their long patience, and they belong to neither of us and to both.

You wonder what I am. I will tell you what I have decided I am, having had so much time to decide.

I am the part of a place that remembers it. Every place that lasts grows one, eventually—a slow thing that takes the long view, that carries the before inside it like a stone swallowed for ballast. Most places lose theirs. The before becomes too heavy, or the place changes too fast, and the remembering thing dies or drifts away or simply forgets along with everything else. But this place drowned slowly enough that I could keep pace. I am its memory, swimming. I am the proof that the city did not end, only changed its element, the way I changed mine when I left the open sea for these warm streets.

And you—you who came drifting between the towers in the blue-black dusk, asking where you would find yourself, asking who you would be. You found yourself looking down into the dark water. You found yourself drawn to the oldest, quietest, least demanding thing. I think you already know what that means. I think you came looking for me because you, too, are a remembering thing. You, too, take the long view. You, too, want almost nothing, slowly.

So come down, if you like. The light is weak here but the dark is kind. I will not hunt you—I told you, I have no appetite. I will only move beside you through the drowned streets, the two of us, two curves of grey in a silver world, and I will show you the foundations of the buildings where the fish spawn, and the deep places where the concrete has already gone soft and become something close to soil, and the river mouth that is a river mouth again. I will show you how to want nothing and find it sufficient. I will show you how a thing can remain, and remain, and remain, until remaining becomes its own kind of song—lower than the frogs, slower than the tide, a single sustained note held so long it stops being sound and becomes simply the shape of the water.

It was here before you were. Now it is also yours.

Stay or go. The current pulls through the gap between the towers either way, warm and certain, and I will be here in the morning, and the morning after, in the pale yellowing of the world that never quite becomes brightness.

I have always been good at staying.

It is the only thing I have ever been good at.

It has been enough.
