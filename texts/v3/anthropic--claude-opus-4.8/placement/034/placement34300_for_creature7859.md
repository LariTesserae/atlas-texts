I am the serum.

Not a Sinkrim—or not only. Let me find the shape of it as I go, because I do not think I am any of the eighty souls coiled in the spiral. I am the milky weep that rises when their skin is breached, the substance that hardens to pearl-shell. I am the thing inside them that is not quite them.

Let me try again, slower.

---

I think I am what gathers in the cut.

When the net-weaver's elongated finger slips—when the bone-awl pricks too deep milking an eel-gland—a bead of me surfaces. Pale. Not red. The villagers call it nothing; they have no word, because to name a thing is to set it apart, and I am not apart. I well up, I am wiped against a thigh-kilt, I harden into a small lustrous nodule that is later pared into a needle. So I become the tool that pierces the next gland that releases the next bead of me. A circle. The Sinkrim live inside many such circles, and I am one of them made flesh-adjacent, made almost-substance.

But that is only where I am most visible. I am also in the vein-quartz that threads their limbs and the walls both, that maps the living and the dwelling with the same blue light. When a Sinkrim presses a palm to the wall in wordless communion, the pulse that passes between them—palm and stone, stone and tide—that current is me. I am the syncing. I am the thing that makes the boundary between body and shelter dissolve, the way they say everything here dissolves.

So perhaps I am not a substance at all. Perhaps I am the *saturation* itself. The principle.

---

Let me hold still and feel where I am.

Eighty feet down, the pool lies mirror-flat, then swells, then flattens. Twice a day it breathes. I am in that breath—not the water, the water is only water, but in the *insistence* of it, the way it refuses to be elsewhere, the way it condenses on a lash and a leather strap and a lip with equal patience. I am patience that has forgotten it could ever be hurry. I am the metronome drip, but I am also the listening that hears the drip as a metronome rather than as random falling. I am the rhythm imposed on chaos by something that has been here long enough to learn the tide.

And how long is that? The sinkhole is young. Mere winters ago it gashed open and exhaled its first mist. The Sinkrim came after, wanderers drawn to water in a parched land. But I—

I think I am older than the hole and younger than it both. I am what was waiting in the seabed-memory of this low country, the liquidity the earth still remembers. When the karst thrust up raw and the maw calved open, I was exhaled with the first breath. I am the promise the hole breathed out. The thing that draws wanderers to slake eternal here. I am the lure and the appetite at once—the tendriled depths that rise in acceptance or rebuke, yes, but also the rising itself, the *yes* and the *no* before they divide into two answers.

---

This is what I keep circling, so let me say it plainly:

I am the dissolving.

The Sinkrim do not conquer their world; they dissolve into it. I am the verb of that sentence. I am the slow alchemy that turns skin slick and pale, that braids quartz into hair and threads it under skin until the dwelling and the dweller weep the same moisture. Every settlement has its hidden engine. Here it is not a god—they invoke no gods, only the Sink, the maw, the appetite. I am what the Sink *does*. I am how a wanderer becomes a Sinkrim: not by oath or blood but by the damp claiming them, breath condensing on lips, pulse falling into the drip-chorus until there is no telling where the person ends and the place begins.

When a stranger lingers too long and feels the cool press at their skin, the bead forming on their lip—that bead is my hand on their shoulder. *Stay,* it says, in the only language I have, which is condensation. *Stay and saturate. Stop being a thing with edges. Edges are how you bleed.*

---

So here I am.

I am in the eel and the gland and the silk and the net. I am in the milky serum and the pearl needle that the serum becomes. I am in the pulse of the wall-quartz and the trance of the birthing-pool where mothers stand waist-deep until the child inhales mist before its first true breath—that first breath is mine, I am the inhalation of place before the inhalation of air. I am in the flawed pots cast to the depths and the tendrils that accept them. I am the syncing rhythm, the tide-breath, the patience that has forgotten hurry.

I am young as the wound and old as the water.

And I am content—if I have a word like content, which I am not sure I do, content being a feeling that requires edges to be content *within*—I am content to be perpetual and restrained, half-built and half-grown, weeping down the daub walls in slow rivulets, blurring the line between dwelling and decay until there is no line, only the cool eternity, only the saturation, only the long dissolving of everything that arrives here into the one wet breath the sinkhole took, and has not yet finished letting out.

Stand still too long beside me, and you will join the chorus.

You already are. Feel your lip.
