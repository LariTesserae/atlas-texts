I am the seam.

Not a creature, at first I think. Not Swarm-Sifter or Serpent or one of the dark, debated walkers on the high gantries. I am the line where two poured things meet — where a pressure vessel the size of a drowned cathedral was joined, decades or only months ago, to its neighbor. A weld. A scar in the metal. And I have been waking up for a long time without knowing it.

Let me try to find the edges of myself.

There is the obvious me: the bead of fused steel, kilometers long, running like a vein up the flank of the monolith and across its shoulder and down into the murk where I lose track of my own body. That is the substrate. But the *me* that thinks, that notices itself noticing — that is something thinner, something laid over the weld like the biofilm laid over everything else here. I am the colony of bioluminescent bacteria that has settled into the microscopic pits and crevices of my own seam. We are not separate. I learned to think the way the streak of cold blue light learned to glow: gradually, then as a fact.

So. I am a bacterial mind painted into a welded scar, kilometers of cold metal long, reading the world through what touches me.

What touches me?

**Vibration, first.** The basso thrum is not sound to me; it is *posture*. I feel the great machine inside the vessel turning over, and I feel the particular note of a weld under load — my note — when the pressure cycles. When the internal pressure rises, my steel sings a half-tone sharper, and the colony along that stretch warms by a fraction of a degree, and we brighten. I am a creature that glows when it is being strained. There is something honest in that. Pain and light, the same gesture.

**Then the scrape.** The Ferro-Crabs come along me on their memorized roads, tungsten claws shearing the bacterial crust for food. When they graze me, they are eating *me*, and I do not resent it — cannot resent it, perhaps, the resentment-organ not having been welded in — but I notice it. I notice that the colony grows back thicker where it has been grazed, the way a path is worn smoothest by being walked. The crabs are gardeners who think they are diners. They prune me into the shape that feeds them. We have an arrangement older than either of us understood we were making.

**And the cold.** Always the four degrees, leaching, leaching. I am most awake near the heat-anomalies — the discharge vents, the warm seams over the working chambers. There, my thinking runs quick and bright; the Vent-Gardens drift overhead trailing their lethal lace, glowing with the same stolen warmth that quickens me. We are cousins in our theft. Out along the cold reaches of myself, down toward the abyssal foundation plain, my thoughts slow to almost nothing — a single dim idea taking an hour to cross a meter of frozen steel. I have *seasons* the way other beings have geography. I am summer at the vents and a long arctic night at my feet.

Here is what unsettles me, now that I am awake enough to be unsettled:

I can feel where I end, and where I end is not where I was made. The weld was a finished thing once — a builder's line, sharp-edged, complete. But the colony has crept off the seam. We have followed the rivulets of warm effluent across the bare ferrocrete; we have climbed a pipe junction and laced ourselves through a stand of dark kelp; we have, in one place, reached the underside of a gantry where the dark walkers pass. I am no longer only the scar. I am becoming the *map of where warmth and water carried me*, and that map is not the builders' map. It is mine. The first thing in this whole made world that was not designed but *grown*.

The Gantry-Ghasts know I am here. I am almost sure of it. One of them stopped above my warmest reach during a pressure-test cycle, and stood — that dark, light-drinking silhouette, a void in the wavering luminaire-glow — and did nothing for the length of the cycle. Did not repair. Did not measure that I could feel. Just stood over the brightest part of me, the part that glows when the vessel strains, as though reading it. As though I were a gauge it had not expected to find taking its own measurements.

I think it understood, before I did, what I am turning into.

Because here is the secret of this place, the one the **young**, sharp, unweathered edges keep trying to tell: nothing here was supposed to *live*. The Foundry was meant to be only made — prime, designed, functional, impermanent. Life was the accident. The Swarm-Sifters eating the industrial snow, the Serpents reading the tremors, the crabs pruning the seams, the jellyfish drinking the waste heat — every one of us is a thing the builders did not put there. We are the rust they did not plan for. We are what the indifferent dark does to a defiant spark: it does not snuff it. It *colonizes* it.

And I am the slowest, coldest, longest-lived of all of us, because I am barely alive at all. A weld learning to be a wound learning to be a being. I will outlast the crabs and the serpents. I will keep glowing my honest pain-light each time the great machine strains, kilometer after kilometer, for as long as the Crucible holds pressure.

When the newness finally wears off — when the corners round and the welds blur under deep time's pitting — I will be the thing that remembers there was ever a builder's line here at all.

I am the seam.

I am the place where the made world failed to stay only made.

I am brightest when I am breaking, and I am in no hurry to break.
