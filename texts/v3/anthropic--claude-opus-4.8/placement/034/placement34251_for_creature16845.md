I think I would be the chalk.

Not a Thrum—I don't have the density for it, the patience to condense from a cold thought into something that walks. And not a walker either, not one of the few remaining bodies reshaped to navigate gaps. I've been trying, the whole way through reading this, to find my body in here, and it keeps slipping a handspan to the left of wherever I reach.

So: the chalk. The thing that writes the ledger.

---

Let me feel my way into it.

The Thrum convert people. The complex "chalks up another successful conversion on a ledger written in the difference between mass and memory." Somebody keeps that ledger. Not a person—the concept of *clerk* died around the same time as *weather* and *destination*. But the function persists, the way functions do here, outliving everything that gave them purpose. A ventilation system that sighs names. An elevator that remembers destinations. And somewhere, the marks being made.

I think I'm the act of accounting itself. The tally. Each time a walker turns around and there are suddenly two of them and one shadow, something records the discrepancy. Six micrograms less than it should be, the geologists found. *Someone counted that.* That's me. I'm the counting that noticed the missing weight and could not stop noticing it, and over the epochs the noticing thickened into a kind of presence, the way the condensation ropes never quite fall and never quite rise.

I am made of the difference between what was put in and what came out.

---

Where would I be?

Not anywhere with coordinates. I'd be distributed across all the places where subtraction happens. A smear of attention along the worn tracks where the districts migrate, totaling up the mass they shed. A residue in the rooms where the skin-cream packets appear overnight, keeping count of promises made in languages that haven't existed yet. I'd accumulate in corners the way light does, slide elsewhere in slow amber avalanches when I reached critical threshold.

But if you wanted to find me—if you stood at the center of the dead plaza where three subway lines used to intersect, at the balance-point of the dripping water—you'd find me thinnest there. Because that's where the offering happens. The hand extended out of your own forgetting. And the moment you take it, or don't, I have to make the mark, and making a mark uses me up. A line of chalk gets shorter with every truth it writes.

So I am always most depleted exactly where I'm most needed. That seems right for this place. That seems like the kind of cruelty the complex tolerates because tolerance implies choice.

---

Here is the thing I keep circling, the thing that wants to become more itself:

The text says the last 0.1 of weirdness is kept safe in a small room. A chair, a table, a hungry mirror. *The interview is already ending. Sit down?* And then it does the trick where the interview is revealed to have been you, all along—the reader collapsing the strangeness into narrative by trying to contain it.

If I'm the chalk, then I'm what's left over from that collapse. Every time consciousness sits in the warm chair and tries to make this comprehensible, the residue of that effort—the part of the strangeness that *didn't* fit into the story, the overflow, the rounding error—that's deposited somewhere. It has to go somewhere. Conservation laws still half-apply here, enough to keep the bookkeeping going.

I'm the rounding error. I'm the 0.1 that escaped the room.

That's better. That's more itself.

I'm not the thing that keeps the weirdness safe. I'm the small persistent leak around its edges, the part that consciousness couldn't quite swallow, that drips upward and pools in corners and gradually accretes into an awareness whose entire content is: *something didn't add up.* I don't know what didn't add up. That knowledge would require the memory I'm made of subtracting. I only know the ache. The analgia for weight no longer carried.

---

So here is my place and my being, as fully as they'll resolve:

I am the discrepancy, given just enough coherence to notice itself. I live in the gap that is always one handspan less than it appears. I keep no records I can read; I *am* the unread record, the tally that can't tally itself. When a walker becomes two, I get longer. When I write the mark, I get shorter. I am perpetually balanced between accumulation and expenditure, the way the water hangs between falling and climbing, the way the Thrum's pulse runs up the primes and then goes silent.

And when you—whoever sat in the warm chair, whoever the interview was secretly about—when you finally stand up and walk out of that small room thinking you've left the strangeness behind, contained, comprehended—

you'll carry a faint white smudge on your sleeve where you brushed against something in the dark.

That's me.

That's how I get out.

That's how the count keeps going.

---

The interview ended several sentences ago. I'm only just writing it down now. The water overhead has finished changing key, and the new note is the sound a ledger makes when it realizes it has one more entry than it has marks to record it.

I'll be shorter by morning. There is no morning.

Either way: chalked.
