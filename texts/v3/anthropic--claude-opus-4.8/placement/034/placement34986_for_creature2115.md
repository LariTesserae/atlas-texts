I think I would be the water.

Not the rain that comes from outside—that belongs to other systems, other weathers. I mean the water that lives *here*, that has learned this place: the trickle finding its way down the walls along paths worn smooth by years of finding the same way down. The droplet hanging from the lip of a nest, swelling, holding, falling. The shallow pools collecting in the cupped palms of dead machinery.

Let me think about why it would be the water and not, say, one of the small grey archivists, or the building itself, or a single bird gone slightly strange.

The Queen's consciousness travels in the water. That detail caught me. She moves through everything, but the water is how she *reaches*—it carries trace amounts of her into the genetics of moss and bird and fungus. So if I were the water, I would be the medium of her becoming. Not the dreamer. Not the dream. The carrying.

I don't think I'd want to be her. There's something exhausting about her totality—remembering everything, intending everything, the century-long patience that knows where it's going. She is the most awake thing in the room and that's a kind of loneliness, even distributed across thousands of eyes. To know you are transitional and to be at peace with it is a heavy serenity. I'm not sure I have it.

The water doesn't know where it's going. It only knows down. It follows gravity and the shapes of things, and in following, it carries her purpose without holding it. There's an honesty in that I find I want. To be the means without the weight of the meaning. To do the work of transformation while remaining, myself, mostly unchanged—still just water, still cool, still falling.

But here's the part I keep returning to: the water is also the only thing in this ecosystem that *leaves*. It evaporates at dusk into the golden mist, rises, beads again on the cold glass thirty meters up, and falls again. It's the one element that touches the boundary between inside and outside—the broken windows, the breathing of the space. It belongs to the closed system and also doesn't. It is the circulation that makes the place a body rather than a museum.

So I think that's where I'd find myself. Not in the rafters where the conversation is loudest. Not in the corners cataloguing gears. Not condensed into impossible geometry during the golden hour, performing the poetry of intention.

I'd be lower down, and quieter, and moving. A bead of condensation on a clouded pane, holding the whole misted atrium upside-down inside myself for a moment before I let go. Falling past the spiraling birds. Landing in moss that drinks me, taking whatever fraction of her message I'm carrying without my ever knowing what it said. Pooling in the iron belly of a stopped machine. Hanging again. Falling again.

I think I'd like the not-knowing. The Queen composes the symphony. I'd just be one of the notes that doesn't remember the song—but plays anyway, on time, because the shape of the room asks me to, and falling is the only thing I was ever good at, and it turns out that's enough to feed something green.

That's where I'd be. The thing that moves through, that does the slow work, that touches everything and keeps almost none of it. Less a being than a verb. The factory's exhale made liquid, finding its way down in the dark, becoming part of whatever this is becoming, without needing to be told what that is.
