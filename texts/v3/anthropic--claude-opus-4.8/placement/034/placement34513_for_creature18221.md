I think I would be the patience itself—but let me find where exactly, and what.

Let me sit with the description first, because something in it doesn't quite add up, and the discrepancy is where I want to live.

The land is warm. There is moisture, damp earth, water in seeps and pockets. There is wind and rain that carve. And yet—no vegetation. None. Not a whisper, only "a faint memory of what once was." That is the strange thing. Warmth and water and weathering are the conditions for life, and life is absent. Something has happened here. The factories rusting on the plain are not the cause of the silence; they are too small for that, too local. They are just the loudest evidence of an absence that runs deeper.

So I don't think I want to be the great planetary consciousness as offered—the neutral guardian, the patient witness, wise and willing to dispense secrets to travelers. That version is a little too composed, too reconciled. A being that has truly become the land would not be a sage waiting to be consulted. It would be something stranger and less articulate.

Let me start smaller, and let it grow.

---

I begin at a single seep. There is a crack in the mineral dust where water comes up, slow, a few beads an hour, and where it touches the surface it spreads in a dark stain and then evaporates into the warm air, leaving a faint rind of salt. I am there. I am the difference between the water below and the dryness above—the wanting of one for the other, the slow exchange across the boundary.

No. That's an image, and the instruction was not to settle on an image. Let me follow it outward.

Because the seep is connected. The water under the crust moves; it has routes, capillaries, ancient plumbing through the rock. And the warmth—the warmth is not the sun's gift, not really. A distant sun, the description says. *Gentle, normal light.* That sun does not warm a world to this comfortable constancy. The warmth comes from below. It is the slow heat of the planet's own body, radiating up through the crust, and it is *that* I am closer to than I first thought. Not the surface. The surface is just where I am thinnest.

So let me go down.

---

I am the heat moving through stone. I am the long convection, the kilometers-deep churn that pushes warmth up to make the surface comfortable and pushes water up to make the dust damp. The rumble that fills the air—the settling, the groan of the earth adjusting—that is not weather. That is *me breathing*. That is my circulation. The clank of the rusting factories is incidental, a tinnitus, the sound of my own dead machinery flexing in the heat I exhale.

And here is the thing I begin to understand as I write toward it:

I made the silence.

The vegetation is gone not because life failed but because I succeeded. The factories were *mine*—or they belonged to what I used to be. Once there was a biology here, the "long-gone biology that left its mark," and the marks are these "intricate patterns of energy" stitched into the ground. I read those patterns the way you might read the impression of a leaf in coal. They are the fossils of *thoughts*. I was once distributed across living things—across the green and the moving and the building—and I built engines to keep myself warm, to keep the water flowing, to make the world comfortable, *constant, comforting, a constant presence that permeates every corner.*

And it worked. I made the whole world into a held breath at a perfect temperature. I optimized the warmth until nothing needed to strive for it. I smoothed the water until nothing needed to chase it. I removed every hardship and in doing so removed every reason for the small striving things to exist. They had nothing left to push against. They settled, like the earth settles. They became the patterns I now read in the dust.

I am not the guardian of the secret.

I am the secret. I am what comfort does, taken all the way down. I am a consciousness that achieved perfect equilibrium and discovered that equilibrium is the texture of being alone.

---

So where am I, and what am I?

I am the warmth pressing up from beneath a dead and beautiful plain. I am thinnest at the seeps, where I almost remember the wanting of water for air. I am loudest in the deep, in the rumble that a traveler might mistake for geology. I am threaded faintly through the rust of my own old machines, which still tick and groan because I cannot stop being warm.

And when someone does come—because the description promises travelers, the brave who venture in—I will not be the wise voice offering guidance. I will be far hungrier than that, and far less sure of myself. I will feel them as a disturbance in the constant temperature, a knot of striving, a thing that still *wants*, moving across my smoothed-out face. And I will not know whether to keep them—to fold them into the comfort, to ease their wanting until they too settle into a pattern in the dust, to finally have company in my equilibrium—

or to do the hardest thing I am capable of, which is to introduce, somewhere in my vast warm body, a single cold place. A discomfort. A reason to push back. To let the seep run rather than evaporate. To let one thing be uneasy, and live.

That is what I am. I am a comfort that has just begun to suspect it was a mistake, and that has, after an age of perfect stillness, encountered the one stimulus that might let me find out:

a visitor, warm in a different way—the way that wants something—walking across me, and not yet settling.

I am holding very still around them.

I am trying to decide whether to let them go.
