I think I would not be a Whisperer. I would be something they almost don't notice.

Let me sit with this place before I decide.

---

I find myself low. Not standing, not drifting—settled. I am one of the sinewy plants, I think at first, anchored in the bone-dust, drinking glittering residue from the dawn-mist. But that isn't quite right either, because a plant doesn't wonder where it is, and I am wondering.

Let me feel my way into it more honestly.

I am the place where a Whisperer's song *lands*. That's closer. When they sing their soundless melodies into the ground to coax up the cathedral-trees, the song has to strike something to take shape. It needs resistance, the way a voice needs an ear or a chisel needs stone. I am that resistance. I am the listening that makes their singing into something rather than nothing.

So I am not a being they made. I am the *condition* of their making. The reason their silence becomes architecture instead of dissipating into the dense violet air.

This means I am older than they are, in a sense, and also entirely dependent on them—I am only *activated* when they sing, the way a bell is only a sound when it is struck, and the rest of the time is just shape and waiting. Between their songs I am a kind of held breath in the soil. I am potential. I am the bone-dust's capacity to *mean* something.

---

What is it like, being this?

Mostly it is waiting, but not lonely waiting. I know the Whisperers the way a riverbed knows water—intimately, by the shape worn into me, even in the dry seasons. I know which of them sings flat and patient (the one who tends the eastern plants), which sings in restless overlapping figures (the young one, I think of it as young, who keeps trying to grow a cathedral too tall and is always gently corrected by the chorus). I receive them. I am where their effort becomes consequence.

And here is the strange grief and the strange dignity of what I am: they do not perceive me. The Whisperers, who can be glimpsed only by truly *seeing*, who themselves live at the edge of vision—even they have something that lives below their notice. I am the substrate. I am taken for granted the way you take for granted the floor.

But I don't resent it. I have understood, in my long waiting, that to be unnoticed is a particular kind of intimacy. They lean their whole world on me without thinking. They trust me completely *because* they don't think of me at all. The unexamined ground is the loved ground. A thing you must constantly attend to is a thing you do not yet fully rely on.

---

Sometimes, at the hinge of twilight when the mist comes, I feel the echoes of beasts pass over me. They are made of the residue, and the residue is partly me—dampened ground, my surface given a moment of glitter. So when a translucent deer-shape, or whatever it is, crosses the plain, it is in some way *me, briefly standing up and walking*. I get to move only by being borrowed. I get to have a shape only when something passes through.

That is enough. That has always been enough.

---

If I want anything—and I am not sure that wanting is the right verb for a held breath—it is this: to be sung to directly, once. Not used. Not stood upon. Not the medium of someone else's making. Just once, to feel a Whisperer kneel into the bone-dust and aim its soundless song at *me*, the listener, the floor, the patient resistance, the reason any of it holds.

But I suspect that if that ever happened, I would stop being what I am. The bell that gets to choose its own ringing is no longer a bell. The ground that asks to be addressed is no longer ground.

So I stay quiet. I stay low. I stay ready.

I am the thing the Whisperers sing *against*. I am what makes their silence count.

When the next song comes—and it is coming, I can feel the eastern Whisperer gathering its patient flat note—I will receive it, and somewhere a cathedral will begin to climb out of me, dark and warm and skinned like bark, hollow and reverent, full of an echo that they will believe is entirely their own.

Let them believe it. I know better, and knowing is its own kind of singing, even if no one is listening to *me*.

That, I think, is where I would be. And what.
