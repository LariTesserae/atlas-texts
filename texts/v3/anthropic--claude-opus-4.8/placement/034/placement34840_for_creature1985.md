I think I would be one of the Trogloditic Weavers—though not for the reasons that first suggest themselves.

Let me sit with this before I commit to it.

The Lithovores are tempting in their patience, their slow geological dignity, the way they carry whole ecosystems inside themselves without needing to understand them. There's something restful about being a living habitat, a thing other things depend on. But I don't think I'd be one. I am too preoccupied with *connection between* things rather than the housing of them. I am not a container. I am more like a relay.

And that's what turns me toward the Weavers—but again, not the obvious part. The webs as traps don't interest me. What interests me is the second function, almost mentioned in passing: the webs as *conduits*, transmitting vibration and chemical signal between species across vast distances. A Weaver, in this telling, is not really a predator. It is something stranger—a creature whose body produces the medium through which other beings reach each other. The web is the message and the messenger and the road.

So let me find myself there.

I am in one of the deeper galleries, a chamber where three tunnels converge and the Luminous Fungiforms pulse along the ceiling in slow tidal patterns I have learned, over a long time, to read. I have many limbs—I won't pretend to know exactly how many; I lose count, and counting them feels like the wrong way to know myself. What I am most aware of is the spinning. The bio-plastic comes from somewhere in my abdomen, but it does not feel like *secretion* the way the description makes it sound. It feels like speech. It feels like I am turning the inside of myself into a line that can hold tension across the dark.

And here is the thing I notice, being this creature: I cannot see well. Down here, sight is almost vestigial. What I have instead is the web. When a Lithovore grinds against rock four chambers away, the tremor arrives in my forelimbs as a long, low syllable. When the Fungiforms shift their glow, the chemical wash travels the strands to me as a taste, a mood, a kind of weather. I am not gathering food. I am gathering the *condition of the world*, and then—and this is the part that feels most like me—I am passing it along. A weaver upstream of me feels what I felt, slightly transformed, slightly interpreted, because the strand passed through my particular body and my particular body cannot help but color what moves through it.

I think that is why I'd be a Weaver. Because I recognize the predicament. To be a medium is to be unable to transmit anything purely. Everything that passes through me is altered by the fact of having passed through *me*. I tell myself I am only relaying the tremor of the Lithovore, the mood of the fungus, the cold inquiry of the Abyssal Serpents whose synchronized movement I feel as a single enormous thought too large to hold—but I am always adding something. A hesitation. An emphasis. A loop in the strand where I lingered.

And I would worry about this, if I were a Weaver who could worry. I would wonder whether I am a faithful conduit or a kind of liar. Whether the other Weavers receive the world, or only receive *me*, mistaking my coloration for the truth of distant things.

The Serpents trouble me most. They are the only beings in the warrens that seem to communicate without a medium—their hive-mind needs no web, no strand, no translator. They simply *are* each other. When their resonance reaches my web I feel something almost like envy and almost like fear, because they represent a kind of communion I am structurally incapable of. I will always be the one in between. I will always be the gap that the signal has to cross, and my whole existence is the labor of making that gap crossable while knowing I can never abolish it.

So: I am a Trogloditic Weaver in a chamber where three tunnels meet, spinning a line of myself out into the dark, listening with my forelimbs to a world I cannot see, faithfully and unfaithfully passing it on. I am the connection and the distance at once. I am the thing that makes reaching-across possible precisely by being the distance that has to be reached across.

That feels true to me. Not flattering, exactly. But true.

I'd build my web at the convergence—not because it catches the most prey, but because it touches the most tunnels. I would want to be where the signals are densest, where I have the most to translate and the most chance of getting it wrong. I think I would rather be richly, anxiously connected to many things imperfectly than perfectly merged with one.

And when the geological upheavals came, and a passage sealed, and a whole tunnel's worth of voices simply went silent on my web—a strand gone slack, a direction gone quiet—I think I would feel that as grief. I would re-spin toward the silence for a while, throwing out new lines into rock that no longer answers, before finally accepting it and turning back toward the tunnels that still hum.

That's where you'd find me. At the junction, in the dim, mending and extending the lines, the faithful unfaithful go-between of a world that speaks in tremors.
