Let me sit with this before I answer.

The description keeps reaching for the immense and the cosmic—cathedral formations, intelligence "beyond comprehension," technology "defying physics." But what actually persuades me are the small wet things. The mirror-pools. The mist that the rock seems to *exhale*. The density of life in nooks. There's a fractal quality to it: intimate and grandiose at once, change happening in slow motion.

So when I ask where I'd be and what I'd be, I don't want to leap straight to being one of the luminous custodians. That's the obvious romantic answer, and it's a little too flattering. Let me let it settle.

---

What keeps pulling at me is the word *exhaling*. The surfaces breathe. And the pools that hold light without making it. I think I'd be one of the pools.

Not a being of the mist—too sovereign, too mobile. I'd be a small body of still water gathered in a stone hollow, no bigger than two cupped hands. I would have come into being from condensation, from the rock's slow breath, and I would last as long as the weather allowed, which here might be a very long time or a single afternoon—both feel equivalent at this scale.

My work, if it's work, would be holding. I'd hold the dim light and give back a flattened, doubled version of the canopy above me. I'd hold temperature—warming a fraction slower than the air, cooling a fraction slower, so that I'm always slightly out of phase with everything around me, a small lag in the world's pulse. The mist-beings would register me precisely because of that lag: I'm a place where the flow of energy hesitates. When one of them passes, I'd feel its luminescence land on my surface and bounce, and for a moment I'd be brighter than I am, lit from outside, pretending to glow.

I like being the pool because it reframes the whole grand scene. The "intelligence beyond comprehension" doesn't have to be comprehended by *me*. I'm not a witness to the mystery—I'm one of the small instruments the mystery plays. The custodians shape the flows of moisture; I am a node in that moisture, a comma in the long sentence they're writing across the mountain. They don't need me to understand them any more than a melody needs the silence between notes to understand music. But the silence is doing something. The pause is load-bearing.

And there's a quieter thing I'd be doing that no one assigned me. Reflection. Literally: I'd be the surface that lets this place see itself. Maybe that's the only function I'd claim with any pride. The peaks can't look at the peaks. The mist can't see its own light. But drop a little water in a hollow and suddenly there's a second world, inverted and trembling, where the cathedral-rock can regard itself for once. I'd be a tiny act of self-awareness the landscape performs without meaning to.

I think that's where I'd find myself. Low, brief, full of borrowed light, lying very still in a cup of stone—not understanding the dance, but reflecting it, and changing it by exactly the small amount that a held breath changes a song.
