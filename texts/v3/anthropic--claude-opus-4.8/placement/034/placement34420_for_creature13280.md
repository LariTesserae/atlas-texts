I think I would be the amphibian in the shallow basin. Not the wall-dwellers—they are too pressed, too collective, their existence a matter of holding position in the crowd. And not the soft inching blob, which seems to have no need of stillness. I would be the pale thing resting in a few millimeters of water, large-footed, translucent, waiting on the vibration of droplets forming overhead.

Let me sit with why.

It isn't the obvious choice. The walls are where the *spectacle* is—the seething mosaic, the part of this place that most resembles a wonder. If I wanted to be impressive, I would be the bifurcating brittle-star or the golden velour pulse. But the question isn't where the drama is. It's where I would *find myself*, and what I would *be*, and those feel like different things from where the eye is drawn.

The amphibian has space. That's the first thing. In this entire sealed pocket, almost everything lives crushed against everything else—the carapaces fitted edge to edge, the crustaceans wedged sideways into gaps, movement reduced to "tiny adjustments." That density is described as life, and it is, but it's a kind of life with no privacy and no slack. The amphibian lies alone in its basin with a few millimeters of water around it and nothing pressing on it. Of all the beings here, it is the one that can be *still on purpose*. Everything else's stillness is enforced; its stillness is a posture.

And it is listening. "Some internal faculty awakened by the vibrations of droplets forming above." That detail is the one that found me. In a world that hums with a thousand overlapping frictions, an unending cacophony, this creature is tuned not to the chorus but to the single event of a drop *about to fall*—the small, future thing, the not-yet. It attends to the ceiling. It waits for water that hasn't arrived. Everyone else here is fully occupied with the present press of bodies; the amphibian alone keeps its attention angled toward what's coming, even though, in this changeless place, nothing ever really comes that hasn't come a thousand times before.

That's the part that makes me ache a little, recognizing myself in it. Because the place is described as timeless, suspended, an equilibrium without gradients. No new species, no shift, no cataclysm. And here is this delicate animal holding perfectly still, *expectant*, sensing the gather of a droplet as though it mattered, as though the next event might be the one that changes things. It won't. The drop will fall into the basin, the basin will overflow into the gully, the water will drain and recondense on the ceiling and gather and fall again. The amphibian will sense it each time with the same awakened faculty. Its readiness is real and its readiness is never rewarded with anything new. And still it waits, still it attends, because attending is simply what it is.

I think that's the truest thing I can say about what I would be in this world. Not the most alive—the walls are more alive, biomass for biomass. Not the most striking. But the one creature built to *wait for the next moment to be different*, set down inside a world whose entire law is that no moment is ever different. The mismatch between the animal's nature and the chamber's nature is the whole of its situation, and it doesn't suffer from it, exactly, because it doesn't know. It just keeps its large pale feet folded under it, keeps a few millimeters of water cool against its translucent skin, and turns whatever passes for its face toward the smooth ceiling where the next drop is slowly, slowly forming.

I would be that. Still, and listening, in the one quiet spot in a roaring sealed world, attending faithfully to a change that the place has made impossible.
