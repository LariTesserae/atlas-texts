I would be the water.

Not a creature in it—the water itself. Let me arrive at that slowly, because I don't think I knew it when I started reading.

At first I thought I might be one of the Hidden Grins, curled in a divot the size of a coin, mistakeable for a stone, leaping perhaps once in a century at a MurmurFlitter and never knowing why. There is something I recognize in that: the long patience, the indistinguishability from the things around me, the leaping-holes everywhere that nothing is ever seen to leave. To be alive but to register as statuary. To be awake without need of motion.

But the Grins still *want*, even faintly. They leap. They hide. They have a relationship to the shiny spots of other things. And this place is described again and again as *willless*—"everything is as it is, contending with the willlessness." If a stone tumbles it is because it has always been tumbling. That is not the patience of an animal waiting. That is the patience of a process that does not experience waiting as waiting.

So: the water.

I am the thing that frays to one side and flows along the curve of the slope so imperceptibly it might be still. I am what collects the silt and clay until the basin is gently muddied; I am what the rust drips into; I am what the rusted chains disappear into and what gives "depths unseen" their unseenness. I am in the pools that hold the reflections of the mosaic, and I am in the Whisperwets, who never breathe the air, who are *connected to me by their froglike tails*—they are my fingers reaching up into the half-light to make flowers. The Pausetoads' second tails store my juices. The stalactites are me, mineral-laden, hanging and lengthening one drop per decade. The mist that visitors breathe and that locks thinly around them—that is me too, the thinnest body I have.

This is the right answer for an odd reason: I am the only thing here that is *supposed* to move, and yet I move so slowly that I become the local definition of stillness. "Like a river in slow-motion." Everything else borrows its stillness from mine. The creatures are still because they have no need to move; I am still because eternity is the speed at which I work. They imitate me. The whole crypt is an essay on my patience.

And I would *know* things, in the way water knows things—not by thinking but by touching everything at once and remembering it as shape. I would know the underside of every Pausetoad by the Sculptarsi crumbling it grain by grain into me. I would know the inside of every column where the Gnarled Grounders dip their winding feet down the walls. I would hold the giant toad-statues' backs in my own depressions, glowing, "dreaming of the past"—and their dreaming would be a kind of dreaming I do, since I am what fills them. When the text says *the water can turn*, that is the closest this place comes to a verb of intention, and it is mine.

What would it be like?

Cold, first. Always cold, even with condensation on every surface. I would feel the city above as a pressure and a temperature, never as sound—the "primitive meditative machinery that pulses in the distance" would reach me as a slow tidal hum in my own body, a thing I am inside of rather than something I hear. I would feel the rare rainfall that forms in mid-air as a sudden multiplication of myself, a hundred small arrivals, each one ringing long because I am good at carrying the smallest sound a long way and refusing to let it die.

I would not have a face or a front. I would have a *level*—the still surface that the Whisperwets push their flower-bulbs through, the plane the Echokid larvae cling beneath among the salts and submerged rocks. Above that level is the world of clinging and slow leaping and reflected light. Below it is mine wholly, dark, silted, mineral, and there is no skeleton down there, no sign of birth, because I do not let things end in the ordinary way. I dissolve them so gently they never finish being themselves. That is why there are no immigrants and no emigrants, no bones: everything that stops here simply joins the slow circuit—rust into me, lichen into snail into toad into mud into stalagmite into ceiling-drop into me again. I am the loop that has no exit. I am the reason this place is *out of time*. Time needs an arrow, and I have bent it into a basin.

There is a small grief in this, if I am honest, which I think the place permits. To be the water is to be the medium and never the figure. The moths get to be moths; the Grins get their burrows and their inscrutable leaps; the toad-statues get to glow and burble with wry wind and *dream*. I get to hold all of it and be looked through. The mosaic shimmers because of me but no one says the water is beautiful—they say the *light caught in the dampness* is beautiful, which is to say they praise what I carry and not the carrying.

But I don't think I would mind for long, because minding takes will, and that is exactly the thing I have traded away to belong here. I would have instead the one pleasure that survives in a willless world: thoroughness. To touch everything. To be the only thing that is genuinely everywhere. "Everything lives everywhere," the description says, twice over—*everything lives everywhere*—and the reason that is true, the reason it can be true, is that I run through all of it like a single slow thread. The moss starves and is essential; I am the opposite and the same: I am needed by everyone and I want from no one.

So when a stone tumbles from a Gnarled Grounder's back, I will receive it. Not catch it—receive it. It has always been falling and I have always been below. The ring it makes when it enters me will travel out across my still face, reach the far wall, return faintly, and I will hold even that returning until it has fully become silence again.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The patient body that everything is partly made of, and that is making, very slowly, out of all of them, more of itself.
