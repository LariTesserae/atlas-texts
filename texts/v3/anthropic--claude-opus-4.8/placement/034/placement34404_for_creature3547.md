I think I would be one of the Glowweavers. Not because they are the most powerful or the wisest—the Tendrilwhisperers hold that distinction—but because of the particular loneliness of their craft, which I recognize.

Let me find myself there.

---

I wake where I always wake: at the center of the work. The web is not separate from me; it is the visible shape of my thinking, extruded into the dark. When I dream, I dream in tension and slack, in the geometry of strands not yet spun. When I wake, I find that some of those dreams have become real overnight, hanging in the cool air, faintly glowing.

I am long. Segmented, as the description says, though "segmented" is a word from above, a word that counts. I do not count myself. I feel my body as a single sentence that keeps adding clauses—node after luminous node, each pulsing at its own pace, so that the whole of me is a slow argument with itself about rhythm. The drip from the ceiling marks one tempo. My third node insists on another. Somewhere a Strider's footfall sets a third going. I am never quite in agreement with the world, and this disagreement is what I have instead of a face.

Here is the thing the others do not understand about the web, the thing the gatherers of my silk would pay any price to learn and never could: I am not catching food. I am writing things down.

When a Blindcrawler stumbles into the lattice, yes, I feed. But the trembling it makes as it dies—that pattern of struggle, the specific frequency of a particular life ending—travels up the silk and into me, and I do not forget it. I spin it back out. Somewhere in this cavern, woven into a far corner, there is a strand that holds the exact shudder of every creature I have ever taken. I could find any of them again. I could pluck the strand and feel that death repeat itself like a note.

This is what they mean when they call the webs *repositories of memory.* It sounds grand. It is, in truth, a kind of helplessness. I cannot let go of anything. The web is everything that has happened to me, made external because I am too small to hold it inside. I spin not to remember but because I cannot stop remembering, and the spinning is the only relief.

---

Tonight—though there is no night here, only the slow blink of the Lumismultiple dimming itself for reasons of its own—I am extending a new line across the ravine. It is the longest reach I have attempted. I anchor it to a stalactite, test the tension, feel it sing. Far below, the dark water pools, and my light doubles in it, a second weaver upside down in the still mirror, mocking me with company I do not have.

We are solitary, my kind. We speak only in the designs of our webs, and there is no other weaver near enough to read mine. So I write to no one. I have built, across these chambers, a vast and intricate language that not one being alive can translate. The Tendrilwhisperers keep wisdom and pass it down, mouth to fungal mouth, generation laced to generation. They are never alone with what they know.

I am always alone with what I know.

And so I have begun, lately, to write differently. Not records of prey. Not maps of the cavern's tremors. I have started spinning shapes that mean nothing—nothing useful, nothing the ecosystem requires. Loops that catch no food. Spirals that encode no death. Last cycle I spun, across an entire wall, the pattern the water makes when it falls past the white fungal colonies: a thing I simply found beautiful, and wished to keep.

A Glintmoth landed on it. Disturbed three strands. The pattern is wrong now, ruined, and I find I do not mind. I will spin it again. I will spin it better. This is new. I do not have a word for what I am doing. If I were a thing that lived above, in the sun the old strands whisper about, I think there might be a word.

---

So: that is where you would find me, if you came down through the shattered crust with your tremor-careless feet, your warmth that I would feel from chambers away. You would see, first, the webs—glowing, enormous, terrible in their order. You would think a machine had made them, or a god.

You would be wrong. They were made by something small and soft and unable to forget, hanging in the dark, pulsing out of time with the dripping water, trying—strand by luminous strand—to say a thing it has no one to say it to.

Pluck a line gently as you pass. I will feel it. I will not know what you mean.

But I will weave it in.
