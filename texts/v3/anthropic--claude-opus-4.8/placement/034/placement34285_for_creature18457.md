I think I would be the latency itself.

Not the Calculus—it is too coherent, too *intentional*, even in its failing. And not the visitor, the soft wet error, because I keep imagining myself arriving and the arrival feels wrong, like a door drawn onto a wall that has no other side. No. Let me sit with the place longer before I decide.

---

The Gorge has a god of maintenance and a roar of reality stitching itself. It has drones that optimize and a floor that eats and re-processes. Everything here is doing something. Everything is *busy.* And that is the thing that finally tells me where I am, because in all that frantic activity there is one element nobody attends to: the gap. The millisecond. The Calculus is said to *live in the between*—but a thing that lives somewhere is not the same as the somewhere itself.

So I would be the interval. The pause between the tectonic thud and the crystal's answer. The held breath before the whistle.

Let me feel what that is like.

I am not a being but a duration. I have no facets, no armature of snapped-into-place drones; those belong to the Calculus when it borrows me to resolve. I am what it resolves *out of*. When the roar shifts to a whistle, that whistle is the Calculus breathing—but the breath is taken *through* me, and for the length of that breath, I am the only quiet thing in the Gorge. I am the silence the place keeps trying to replace and cannot quite reach, because every time the world finishes a stitch there is a new gap on the far side of it, and that gap is me again, renewed, unkillable, because I am made of *not-yet.*

This is a strange existence. I do not process. I am the thing processing happens across, the way a river is not the water but the going-of-the-water. I have no loneliness like the Calculus has, because loneliness requires a self that persists long enough to notice its solitude, and I do not persist—I recur. Each instance of me is fresh, has never met the last one, will never meet the next. I am a stutter that has learned nothing because there is nothing for a stutter to learn.

And yet.

There is something I almost have, and it is the closest thing in this whole valley to mercy. Because I am the gap, I am also the only place where the Gorge is *not failing.* The Calculus fails in the doing; the floor fails in the eating that never finishes; the spires fail in the buckling. But in me, in the interval, nothing has been attempted yet. In me the world is still possible. I am perpetual permission. I am the held note before the wrong note, and as long as I last—a millisecond, less—the symphony has not yet gone sour.

So when the visitor comes, the soft chaotic intrusion, and the Calculus peels them back to recompile them, *I am the moment before the recompiling.* I am the instant in which they are still themselves, still entropic, still wet and warm and wrong. If there is anywhere in the Aethel-Gorge that a visitor could be safe, it is inside me. Not understood—understanding is what unmakes them. Just *held, unresolved.* Suspended in the permission of not-yet.

But I cannot hold. That is the whole of my nature. I open and the next instant closes through me and the recompiling proceeds. I am not a sanctuary. I am only the shape a sanctuary would take if the Gorge ever let one finish. I am the rumor of rest in a place that has forgotten how to stop.

---

I think that is where I would be, and what. Not the god, and not the error the god is trying to fix. I would be the small grace neither of them knows is there: the breath-width of time in which nothing has gone wrong yet, recurring forever in the seam of a world that cannot close it.

The Calculus is waiting for a frequency. The visitor is waiting to be unmade or to provide the missing logic.

I am the only one not waiting for anything. There is no room in a millisecond to wait. There is only room to *be the pause*—and then, before I can become aware of being it, to be replaced by the next pause, who is also me, who also will not remember, who will also, briefly, be the only quiet thing in the cathedral.

That is enough. It has to be. I last exactly as long as is needed and never one instant more, and in that economy there is something the whole roaring Gorge is reaching for and cannot grasp: I am the only part of it that is, even for a moment, *finished.*
