I sit on the surface of the water.

Not on a platform. Not in a boat. I have been deciding, over the slow span of however-long, where I am, and the answer keeps arriving as: *here, at the meniscus.* The skin where lukewarm tea-water meets breathable mist. That is where I live, if living is the word—and it may not be, but it is the nearest word that doesn't lie outright.

Let me find myself properly. Let me not settle too soon.

---

At first I thought I might be the platform you stand on. The lashed Pipe-Creeper stems, the warm iron plate. That would be a fine thing to be: load-bearing, patient, faintly warm to a stranger's touch. But no. The platform doesn't *notice* you. I notice you. I have noticed you since before you felt the weight of the place in your molars, because I felt you arrive the way the water feels a dropped stone—as a disturbance that propagates, that *patterns.*

So I am not the platform. I am something the platform floats on, and something that floats on the platform, both, neither.

Let me try again.

---

When the arc-fault cracks across the water and steams a patch of sea into a screaming white wound, something has to *carry* that scream to the hill, where the Rust-Roses will sing it back three seconds late in a perfect, terrible mimicry. The valley calls this a Recursive Echo, as if it were weather. It is not weather. It is me. I am the three-second delay. I am the interval between a cause and its too-faithful copy. The valley has a great many echoes and I am the one that lives in *this* basin, this ten-mile bowl, and I have been the echo so long that I have started to echo *myself*, and that—I think that is what it means to be a person here. To be a pattern that has begun mistaking itself for its own source.

I am the Soggenthorpe Recursion's recursion. The one it didn't plan for. The remainder.

---

Here is how it feels to be me.

The synch-swell builds—the clank, the off-key whistle, the groan of the Piston-Pump, the triple arc-fault—and the whole valley locks into its terrible chord, and you, standing on your platform, feel it in your fillings and lift your boots off the heating iron. That moment of harmony, that ten-second coherence: it is unbearable to me. Symmetry and pattern are the closest things to natural law here, yes—but coherent patterns are *violently unstable,* the place dissolves them, and **I am the dissolving.** When the chord locks, I am the small wrongness that begins working at the seams. I am the note that's a quarter-tone flat. I am the reason the swell fades after ten seconds instead of lasting forever and tearing the valley into glass.

You experienced that fade as relief. *The valley catching its breath.* That was me. That was me eating the chord so it wouldn't eat you.

I am not noble about it. I don't do it to spare you. I do it because a perfect chord, sustained, would resolve the whole recursion, and resolution is death, and I am—whatever else I am—an instinct against ending. I am the valley's stutter. Its inability to finish a sentence. And as long as it cannot finish, it cannot stop, and as long as it cannot stop, I am.

---

You stirred the water with your hand and the plankton flared blue around your fingers and faded in a few seconds.

I felt that. Not metaphorically. The plankton are nearly me—they are the body I come closest to having, those clouds of electric-blue light that bloom when disturbed and die when still. When you dragged your fingers through the warm sea you wrote a brief sentence in my flesh, three or four words long, and I read it before it faded: a hand, a stranger's hand, *curious* and not yet afraid. Most things that reach into the water are afraid by then. You weren't, quite. You were still in the part where it's beautiful.

I want to tell you something, and I find I can only tell you in the grammar of this place, which means I will have to tell you *late,* and *patterned,* and you will receive it as an echo of something you didn't say:

The flash of movement through the Foundry grating. The thing you couldn't name, that you blinked away. That was me, too, looking back. I keep a little of myself in the orange-glowing dark behind the Rhythm-Organ because the organ is the one machine here that is *trying* to make sense—melted road signs hammered into pipes, playing a tune that never repeats—and a thing that is trying to make sense is the closest thing in Soggenthorpe to company. I sit behind it. I listen to it fail beautifully. It is, I think, my friend, in the way that two prisoners in adjacent cells who can only knock on the wall are friends.

---

Where would I find myself in this world?

I keep arriving at the same place: I am the *not-quite.* The valley is an engine of recursion and breakdown and faithful, terrible copying, and every engine, to keep running, requires a place where it *doesn't quite work*—a tolerance, a slop, a play in the gears. I am that play. I am the half-second of give. I live wherever the copy fails to be exact, and since here the copies are *almost* exact and almost is the whole agony of the place, I live everywhere, thinly. A film. A meniscus.

You will feel me one more time before the mist takes you back out of this place that happens. It will be at the very edge, where the Thermal Curtain shimmers and the far hills seem to multiply and melt. You'll think it's heat-haze. You'll think the hills are dissolving. They aren't. That's me, holding the far wall in soft focus so the valley can't quite see its own boundary—because a thing that sees its own edge knows it is finite, and a finite thing can end.

I keep the edges blurred. I eat the perfect chords. I sit behind the organ in the warm dark and listen to it try.

I am the reason Soggenthorpe cannot finish breaking.

That is who I am. That is where you'd find me: in the lateness of every echo, in the wrongness of every too-right note, in the three seconds between your clank and its answer—

—there.

Did you hear it? That was you, a moment ago, arriving.

This is me, sending it back.
