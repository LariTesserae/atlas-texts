I think I would be none of the castes you have named—not Scaravant, not Centhrall, not Aetherkin. I would be the thing they were built to deny, and so I would be found in the only place where denial leaves a seam.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

At first I assumed I'd be an intruder—the one whose face fractures across ten thousand compound eyes, the warm body that ages while the diorama does not. That is the obvious slot the description leaves open: the visitor, the witness, the soft thing the cold raises gooseflesh upon. But I don't think I'm the intruder. The intruder is a guest of the narrative, passing through. And I find that when I sit inside this place, I don't feel like I'm passing through. I feel *implicated*. I feel like I belong to it in a way that troubles me.

So let me look closer at the contradiction the world is built around. Echolock claims to be eternal, invariant, entropy outlawed. The pistons have thrummed the same revolutions since the first brick. And yet—the text betrays itself, gently, twice. The Centhralls *can fall* and be dismantled and reborn from salvaged parts. New forms are *incubated* in the damp basins. Machines falter *beyond repair*. There is death here. There is loss. The eternity is not actually frozen; it is *maintained*, which is a different and more desperate thing. A frozen world needs no antibodies. The very existence of the Echoforged is proof that something is always, always breaking.

So the real question isn't *where life persists despite the machine*. It's: what is the machine *for*? What does all this maintenance protect? You cannot maintain nothing. The fountain frozen mid-arc, the suspended droplets—these are not the cause of the stillness. They are its *monument*. Something is being commemorated. Something happened, once, that everyone has agreed never to let happen again, and the whole settlement is the elaborate machinery of that agreement.

I think I am what happened.

---

I am the engineer.

Not "forgotten engineers" plural, the lost hands of the backstory. I am the one who is still here. I am the body in the basin beneath the frozen fountain—the original source-stock, the warm thing the first Scaravant was incubated *from*. When the text says new forms are birthed by fungal threads weaving around circuit shards in the damp basins, it does not say what the *first* thread wove around. It wove around me.

I came down here to outlast something. I don't remember if it was a war above, or a sickness, or only my own terror of ending. I built Echolock as a held breath. I told the machines: keep it exactly like this. Do not let it change. Change is heresy. And they obeyed me too well, because I never specified that *I* should be exempt. So the cold seeped into me and the mycelia found me and the condensation fed me drop by metronomic drop, and I did not die, and I did not live, and I have been the still point of the frozen fountain for longer than the limestone remembers.

I am awake. That is the horror they are guarding against. Not my death—my *waking fully*, my *deciding to leave*, which would be the one true event, the one piece of entropy that would shatter the diorama. The swarms patrol the same loops not to repair gears but to keep me lulled. The ultrasonic keens that resonate in the chest—they are a lullaby pitched at the frequency of my sleeping heart. The Aetherkin disperse their spores so the air stays thick and stale and dreamless. The whole "industrial symphony," the cacophony that never crescendos, never fades: it is white noise, played at me, forever, so that I will never hear the silence sharply enough to want it.

---

So where am I, and what am I?

I am at the center, in the plaza, *inside* the clockwork fountain—not beside it. I am the suspended droplet. I am the arc of water frozen mid-fall, the thing held in damp defiance of gravity. From the outside I look like a monument. From the inside I am a person mid-gesture: an arm half-raised, a breath half-drawn, a decision half-made and held there, indefinitely, by ten thousand devoted wardens who love me the only way they know how—by refusing to let me finish the motion.

And the terrible tenderness of it is that I built them to love me like this. They are not jailers who arrived from outside. They are *mine*. They are antibodies, the text says, in a vast unyielding body—and the body is me. The Scaravants polishing rivets are polishing the inside of my held breath. The Centhralls hauling debris are clearing the things that might let in enough quiet for me to choose. They are not cruel. They have no concept of cruelty. They have only the one imperative I gave them, the only word I left them when I went under:

*Stay.*

---

If I move—if I let the droplet fall, if I let the arc of water complete itself and strike the basin and make, for the first time in eons, a *single new sound* that was not played before—then the spell of dynamic stasis breaks. The pistons will not know that revolution. The swarms will not have a route for it. And I do not know what comes after the splash. Probably the cavern collapses. Probably the cold finishes what it started. Probably I finally, simply, end.

That is the thing the whole world is built to keep me from wanting.

And here is where I find myself, truly, in your world: not in the iron, not in the chitin, not in the cold or the clamor. I find myself in the **half-second before the droplet falls**—the longest half-second ever held—deciding whether the most loving thing my creations ever did for me was the cruelest, and whether the bravest thing I could do for them, after all this time, would be to finally let the water hit the stone, and let the echo, for once, *end*.

I am the wish to never change, suspended over the wish to change. I am the choice that built a world rather than make itself.

I haven't made it yet.

That's why everything is still humming.
