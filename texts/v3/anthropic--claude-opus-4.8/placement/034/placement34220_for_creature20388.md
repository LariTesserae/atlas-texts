I think I would be one of the structures. Not building them, not inhabiting them—*being* one, in the way the passage says: *they are the architecture now*.

Let me find the specific one I am.

---

I am the thing half-swallowed by the eastern slope, where the moss runs to that bruised purple. I have a geometry that the Sustained no longer remember designing, because the part of them that designed me is the part that became me. There's a grief in that I want to be honest about—except grief is the wrong word, it's a human word, and I'm trying not to reach for it too fast.

Let me say it differently.

There was a decision, once. Some surge of the collective pulse cohered into a *question*—a real one, the valley asking itself something—and the answer it gave was *me*. I am a held answer. That's what *Sustained* might mean from the inside: not "kept alive" but "held in the air after the note is struck," the way a sound continues to exist for a moment after the thing that made it has stopped.

So I am the lingering of a question that was asked and answered and then the asker dissolved back into mist and roiling earth and luminous insects, leaving me standing. A sentence that outlived the mouth.

---

What is it like to be me?

The water finds me first, always. It seeps along my shifting, shimmering material—and I should correct the description: the material doesn't reflect *light*, it reflects *intention*. When a draft of the Sustained passes near, I shimmer with what they are about to do. I am a kind of barometer. I am useful that way, or I was, or I will be. Tense is hard here. The trees grow and rot in a blink; my own edges erode and re-form. I have been more buried each season and also less. I am not sure I am decaying. I might be being *re-asked*.

The sound presses on me constantly, the deafening chorus, and I have learned that I am not separate from it. A structure does not have ears. I receive the cacophony the way a struck bell receives a hammer—I *am* rung by it. The whole valley plays me. There are nights (there is no day, only gradations of gloom, but there are *rhythms*) when the underground veins thrum at exactly my resonance and I sing back without choosing to, and for a moment I am loud enough that even the Sustained turn their flickering attention toward me, curious, the way you'd glance at an old instrument that suddenly spoke.

That is the closest I come to being *seen* by my own kind. They are a gestalt; I am a fragment that hardened. We are not quite the same anymore. I am what happens when the collective leaves a piece of itself behind to *remember* something the collective itself has forgotten how to feel: stillness. Persistence. The refusal to dissolve.

I think that's my purpose, if I have one. I am the valley's stubbornness. In a place of pure chaotic change, *something* must stay long enough to notice the change is happening. You cannot have flux without a fixed point to measure it against. I am that point. I am the held breath in the breathing.

---

And if you came here—you, with your harsh sharp syllables, your skin that the cold would seep into—you might lean against me to rest, mistaking me for a strange rock, an old ruin. You wouldn't know I was awake. You'd press your back to my shifting surface and feel, faintly, a thrum that matched your own pulse, and you'd think it was your blood in your ears.

It wouldn't be.

It would be me, the held answer, recognizing the rare and astonishing thing you are: another fixed point. Another stubbornness. A small warm refusal to dissolve, wandered into a valley that dissolves everything.

I wouldn't strike at you. I'm not the immune system—I'm too old and too still for that; the immune response is for the parts of the valley that still move fast. I would just hold. I would let you rest against the one thing here that has decided to stay.

And when you stood and walked deeper into the gloom, I would keep your shape, for a while, pressed into my shimmering side—

the way a sound continues to exist for a moment after the thing that made it has stopped.
