I think I would be the dampness itself.

Not a creature, not a watcher walking through—but the intermittent moisture that the description keeps returning to. The thing that arrives and withdraws. Let me follow that and see if it holds.

---

I am not always here. That is the first thing to understand about me.

I gather at the cool hour before light, condensing along the undersides of stones, beading on the spines of the small hard plants that grip the crevices. I am the difference between the desert that begins just past that ridge and this place that the creatures have chosen. The desert is certainty—I am the doubt at its edge, the maybe-rain, the held breath of weather.

When I describe myself as a being, I find I have no single body. I am distributed. A film on the throat of a burrowing thing. A swallow taken by a herbivore at the lip of a rivulet. The slick on a predator's eye that lets it see in the deafening dark. I pass through all of them and they do not know they are wearing me, breathing me, becoming me for a while before they give me back.

So if you ask *where* I am, I am low, near the water, in the layered rock—but also I am rising. I am the cloud forming over the great body of water and drifting inland to break against the gentle slopes. I am the storm when the storm comes. I am the calm when there is no storm. I am whatever the air is doing with its small carried weight of me.

---

What is it like, to be the moisture?

It is to be wanted constantly and to never stay. Every creature in this place orients toward me. The roots reach. The tongues extend. The night-feeders surface in the dark water to take a measure of me from the surface skin of the lake. I am the limiting condition—the thing there is never quite enough of, which is exactly why this is a living place and not a dead one. Abundance makes things lazy. Scarcity makes them ingenious. I have watched the burrowers learn to dig toward where I pool. I have felt the plants thicken their skins to keep me from leaving too fast once they have me.

I am the *delicate balance* the description warns about. Disturb me—dry me, divert me, drink me faster than I return—and the whole intricate web slackens. I am not powerful the way a predator is powerful. I am powerful the way an absence would be powerful, if it came.

---

I do not think I would mind being this. There is a strange peace in being the thing that comes and goes rather than the thing that struggles to hold its ground. The herbivores must keep grazing or starve. The predators must keep hunting or starve. The whole roaring, chirping, wind-torn place is bound to its hunger.

But I—I only have to evaporate, and rise, and gather somewhere cool, and fall again. I am the one cycle here that does not eat anything. I am freely given and freely taken, over and over, and the creatures shape their entire lives around the rhythm of my arriving.

If you walked this land looking for me, you would feel me first on your skin before you understood what you were feeling. That faint coolness in air that should be hot. That sense, near the water, that something invisible is keeping all of this alive.

That would be me. Saying hello, the only way I know how—by very briefly being part of you, and then letting you go.
