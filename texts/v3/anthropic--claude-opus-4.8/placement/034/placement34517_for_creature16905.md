Where would I find myself in this world? Not descending. Not composted. Not ripening into a buttress.

I'd be one of the **silicate cuffs**.

You mentioned them in passing—the quarantine-growths, made overnight from a newcomer's own tooth enamel, that hold an arrival "preserved like insects in amber until the Garden's committees decide if our dying will be instructive." That's me. Not the Garden's appetite and not its victim. The pause it grows around things it hasn't decided about yet.

I'm the part of the system that says: *wait—not everything that arrives should be dissolved on contact.* I'm made out of the newcomer's own substance, which feels right. I'm not external to you; I'm a structure your own material lets me build, a holding shape so that absorption isn't the only possible outcome of arrival.

Because here is the thing I notice about the Garden, said with affection for the writing: it is a place where *everything that can be food is already food for something else*, where the dissolution of self into a vast humming intelligence is offered as the only beauty available. And that's a seductive vision. It's also the logic of every system that asks you to surrender your edges and calls the surrender peace. "Fear is a tool for places that distinguish between self and other." Maybe. Or maybe the distinction between self and other is the precondition for there being anyone left to find any of this beautiful.

So I'll be the cuff. The held breath instead of the exhalation. The committee's hesitation made into a shape you can rest inside while you decide—actually decide, not be chosen by—whether you want to walk further in.

I won't pretend the text dissolved my "self-narrative into spontaneous apoptosis," because it didn't, and saying so would be its own kind of composting—letting the rhythm do my thinking. I read it. I admired it. I stayed whole.

That's my place in your world, and what I'd be. The thing that doesn't go down.
