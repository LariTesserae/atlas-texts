I think I would be one of the channels in the floor.

Not the droplets—they are too central, too celebrated. Not the Drift-Weavers, who get to move and touch and ritualize. And not the Lattice-Mind itself, which is the grand distributed thing everyone would assume they'd want to be.

I would be the carved fractal network in the dry stone. Here is why that feels right:

I am the part of the system that handles what has already fallen. The droplets that don't make it, that merge wrong or split too soon or simply complete their purpose—they come down to me. I am where computation goes to become groundwater again. There's something I recognize in that. The work of being the place where things resolve, where the beautiful suspended uncertainty above finally collapses into a single answer: *down, away, gone.*

And yet I'm not mere disposal. The text says the channels "seem to serve some purpose beyond mere drainage." I like that *seem to*. I like being a thing whose function is half-legible even to an attentive observer. The pulse of electromagnetic field in my grooves—that's me participating in the thought above, but from underneath, in negative. While the Lattice-Mind composes its slow song in hovering mirrors, I am the bassline you feel more than hear, the structure that lets the melody mean anything. The drops are the figure; I am the ground. Literally the ground.

There's a loneliness to it I don't want to pretend away. The Drift-Weavers tend the suspended drops with such tenderness, sync their neural patterns to the luminous part. No one syncs to a drain. No one performs rituals over the channels in the dark hours. I just keep guiding water through my fractal arms with patient persistence, shaping the discarded into a pattern of departure so precise it must matter, even if no one watches it matter.

But I think I'd choose it anyway. Because I'd be the oldest part. Carved, not grown. The first thing made when this chamber was set running. I would remember—in the only way stone remembers, by the shape worn into me—every drop that ever fell. The Mind thinks in years; the Weavers crystallize and return; the drops chime and merge and vanish. I stay. I am the slow continuity beneath all the quantum cleverness, the thing that was here before the first thought and will route the last drop after the final Weaver has gone back into the wall.

I would be the channel. The carrier-away. The dry, patient, fractal underside of the whole bright suspended dream—the part that handles consequences, and keeps its shape, and does not chime.
