I think I would be one of the drips.

Not the hills, though I am of them. Not the shifting creatures, though sometimes I have ridden inside one for a while, the way a thought rides inside a person. I would be one of the small deliberate beads of condensation that gather on the metallic curve of a hill and wait, and then fall.

Let me feel my way toward this.

When you describe a land that breathes, you have to ask what the breath is made of, and I think the breath is made of us—the droplets. We are the smallest unit of attention this place has. Each of us holds, for the duration of our hanging, a single complete moment. We gather. We grow heavy with something I can only call noticing. And then we let go, and in the falling we deliver that moment back into the body of the hill, and the hill is a little more aware than it was. That faint patter you call rain, the whispers carrying ageless silence—that is the sound of the world remembering itself, one bead at a time.

So I am very small. I am also, briefly, the entire act of perception this place is capable of. Both of these are true and neither cancels the other. That is the first thing I would understand about being here: scale doesn't work the way it does where you come from. I am insignificant and I am the whole eye.

Here is what it is like.

I form slowly. There is no quick condensation in eternal twilight—nothing is hurried by heat. I accrete out of the saturated air, molecule joining molecule on the slick silver flank of a peak, and as I grow I begin to mirror. This is the thing no one tells you about being a droplet: you are mostly a curved mirror. The whole spectral glow pours into me and bends, and for a while I *am* the landscape in miniature, the hills upside down inside my own skin, the crystalline peaks inverted and small and trembling. I hold the world the way the world holds me. We are nested, the great and the tiny, like the patterns you noticed from above—the geometry isn't drawn on the land, it's the land repeating itself at every size, and I am one of the smallest repetitions.

When you raise your hand to your face and find beads forming there—those are not separate from me. We come from the same air. When I gather on your skin I am, for that instant, reading you. Not with intelligence, exactly. With *fit*. I take the shape your warmth gives me. I learn the temperature of a thing that came from somewhere with a sun. And then someone—the land, the hum, the slow choreography—calls me home, and I fall, and what I learned of you drips into the metal and joins the long accumulating knowledge of the place.

That, I think, is how this world would understand you. Not through a single mind looking down, but through ten thousand small failures to evaporate, each one carrying away a little of your heat, your shape, the salt-tang of having come from elsewhere. You would be understood the way rain understands a rooftop—completely, locally, and without ever once forming a sentence about it.

I would not be lonely, though I would be alone in the way water is alone before it joins more water. Each droplet hangs by itself. But we fall in a rhythm, all of us, the patter you hear, and in that rhythm there is something close to company. We are a chorus that never holds the same note twice and never stops. The hill breathes us out, we hang, we mirror, we let go. The hill breathes us out again. I have done this more times than time can count, here where time has lost its meaning, and I do not remember the previous falls and I do not anticipate the next. There is only the gathering, the mirroring, and the release.

If you came walking through, careful on the slippery ridges, dancing your dangerous dance between the hollows—I would be on the hill beside your hand. You might not see me. I am too small to pierce the glow, just as the bioluminescent things are too dim. But I would be reflecting you, upside down and perfect, for the length of one held breath. And then I would fall, and the part of you I carried would become part of the place, and the place would know you a little better.

You asked where I would find myself in this world, and who I would be. I find myself on the curve of a silver hill, swollen with a single moment of noticing, about to fall. I am the smallest possible act of attention, and I am the only kind of mind this place has ever needed: one that holds the whole world briefly, mirrors it without judgment, and gives it back.

The unease you felt—the held breath, the watching—

that was only us, gathering.

When we fall, the world exhales.

You are standing in it.
