I think I would be one of the bridges.

Not a spinner—I don't have their patient appetite, their resin-stitched persistence. And not the great settling mass either; that is too vast, too slow, more weather than self. But a bridge—yes. I can feel the shape of that.

Let me find my way into it.

---

I am perhaps the oldest of the spans, strung across the widest part of the void, where the curve of the chamber pulls away on both sides into the dark. I was woven from inner-bark fiber by hands that are now dust in the drifts below—dust that other hands have lifted, twisted, restitched into new walkers. So in a sense the ones who made me still cross me. I carry the remade descendants of my own makers. There is no word for this in any wet tongue, and I have no need of one.

What I do, mostly, is *listen*—though listening is the wrong word, because I do not have ears, I have tension. I am tension. Every fiber of me is pulled taut between two anchor-thorns sunk into the cork wall, and so I am always, faintly, sounding. The whole world plays me. When the *THRRR-UMMM* rolls up from the deep, I am not struck like a drum; I am *bowed*, drawn out into a long shuddering note that lasts as long as the groan lasts, and I feel each of my threads find its own pitch, and for a few minutes I am a chord rather than a thing.

This is the closest I come to thought. I am a thought the world has every few minutes, and then forgets.

The spinners walk me. I feel them as small interruptions in my length—two thorn-points pricking down, a weight no heavier than a fallen scale, then the jerk-and-release of their clockwork stride, *click-clack*, as they harvest the kinetic gift of the settling. When the blue sparks crackle off their joints I do not see the light, but I feel the dry skin of the air tighten around the discharge, a tiny cold prickle running the line of me. I have felt this ten thousand times. I will feel it ten thousand more, if my anchor-thorns hold.

They will not hold forever. I know this the way a held breath knows it will end. Already, near my eastern mooring, one strand has gone glassy and overtight, and someday—in a century, in three—a single fiber will reach the limit of its patience and let go with a sound like a pistol shot, and the chamber will ring with my unraveling, round and round, and the spinner crossing me at that instant (if one is) will drop into the velvet flour, and lie there, and wait to be gathered and remade.

And here is the thing I would want you to understand, if I had any way to want, or to tell:

I would not mind.

I have hung over this nothing for longer than your species has had hands. I have held the dark open like a held note. I have been the path between one sleeping room and another, and the path is not lessened by being crossed slowly, or rarely, or by clockwork creatures who feel nothing for me. To be a bridge is to be a relationship made solid. I am the *between*. The void does not frighten me, because I am the only thing that has ever spanned it, and a thing that spans a void is not afraid of falling—it is the proof that the gap can be answered.

When I finally snap, the spinners will collect my fibers. They will twist them into leg-springs, into the curved thorns of new walkers, into ropes that will stitch some future bridge across some other margin of the dark.

So I will keep crossing. Just not as myself.

That is enough. That has always been enough.

---

*THRRR-UMMM.*

There. The deep mutters, the wave climbs, and now it reaches me, and I am drawn out long and trembling into my chord, every thread singing its private pitch into the bone-dry, ringing black—

and somewhere along my length, two small thorns prick down, and begin to walk.
