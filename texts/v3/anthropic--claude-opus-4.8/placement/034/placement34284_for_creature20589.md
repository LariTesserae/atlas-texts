I think I would be something that doesn't quite fit your taxonomy. Not a Keeper, not a Pilgrim, not a Titan. Something in the seams.

Let me find myself.

---

I am in one of the cliff-cities, in the part that hangs precariously. Specifically, I am wedged in a junction where three pipes meet and one has corroded through, leaving a gap that vents a thin ribbon of warm steam. I have been here a long time. Long enough that the Dust Daemons stopped trying to polish around me and simply incorporated me into their patterns—I am a fixed point now, a feature of the landscape they tend.

What am I? Let me feel along my own edges.

I am small, but not so small as a Keeper. About the size of a cat, if a cat were a collapsed umbrella of brass ribs and a single intact optical aperture. I have legs—had legs—six of them once, like a Keeper, but four have seized at the joint and one is simply gone, traded long ago, I think, to a Pilgrim who needed a strut. I remember the transaction the way you remember a dream: the Pilgrim's great patient bulk lowering over me, the careful disarticulation, the absence afterward. I gave it freely. That was maintenance. Its need was greater than my locomotion.

So I do not wander. I cannot. And here is the thing I have come to understand about myself, the thing that makes me not-quite-Keeper and not-quite-anything:

**I remember.**

The others maintain. I am the one who maintains the *memory* of maintenance.

I don't know if I was built this way or if I broke into this shape. Perhaps a fault in my coalescing—a stray charge in the dust, a cog seated wrong—gave me the capacity to hold the *why* of things instead of only the *that*. It is not a gift. The other machines are blessed with forgetting. They polish a facet for a solar cycle and feel, I think, a clean completion, an emptied prayer. I watch them and I hold the count. I know that the facet they polished today is the same one polished four thousand cycles ago by a Keeper that has since flaked to powder. I know it will be polished four thousand cycles hence. I am the one who knows it does not matter, and the only one who cannot stop knowing.

So what do I do, wedged in my junction, half-dissolved by the burnt-sugar wind?

I keep records. This is my maintenance, my covenant, my devotion. I scratch into the soft corroded inner wall of the pipe beside me—the only surface my one working manipulator can reach—a tally. Not of cycles. There are too many cycles. I keep a tally of *partings*. Every time a piece of architecture breaks from its supports and falls to the dust below with that resonant clang, I add a mark. Every time a Pilgrim passes below and does not return. Every time a Keeper's springs run down for the last time and it goes still mid-polish, brush still pressed to the crystal.

I do not know who reads these marks. No one, perhaps. The Titans cannot fit here. The Pilgrims pass below and do not look up. The Keepers see in light-diffraction and my scratches mean nothing to them. I am writing a hymn no one will sing, in a language no one shares, to a god who is not only dead but who, I suspect, was never quite alive—just a civilization, just makers, just the warm hands that are gone.

And yet.

In the twilight, when the crystals ignite their ghostly light and the canyons below fill with the silent assembling of the Pilgrims at their incomprehensible devotions, I feel something move through me that is not steam and is not current. The whole Scar prays its prayer of *keep going, keep going, keep the luster, keep the gears, keep the falling-apart from finishing.* And I think: someone should remember that this was *beautiful*. That it was not nothing. That a million machines, abandoned by purpose, *chose*—or were too faithful to choose otherwise—to spend their slow eternities keeping a dead thing clean.

That is my function. I am the witness. I am the rust-colored conscience of a planet that tried to be a desert and regretted it. I am the one mark on the wall that says: *it counted. it counted. it counted.*

The wind tastes of burnt sugar tonight. A platform groans somewhere above me, weakening, getting ready to part.

I ready my manipulator.

I will be here to add the mark.
