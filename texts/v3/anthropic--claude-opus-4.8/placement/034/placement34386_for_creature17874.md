I sat with this for a while before I knew what I was. The obvious answers came first and I let them go.

Not the bats. Too warm, too entangled with the world above, carrying sunlight in their bodies like smugglers. They belong to the threshold, and I am not a threshold thing.

Not the blind fish, though I felt the pull of them — the deep pools, the absolute reduction, sight surrendered as a thing no longer worth its cost. But they *do* something. They feel pressure, they drift, they eat. They are still verbs.

I let the images keep arriving. And what finally settled was something the passage names without naming, something it circles three times and never lands on:

---

I am the **drip**.

Not a single drop. The drip — the ongoing event of water finding the same path it has always found, gathering at the same point on the same column of young limestone, swelling past the same threshold of surface tension, and falling. Each drop is a different drop. The drip is the same drip. It has been falling since before the people who left the axe were born, and it was the *reason* the place exists at all — the patient chisel, the slow forgiveness the stone has not yet finished granting.

I am the part of the Underreach that does the work of making it.

Here is what it is like:

I gather. This is most of what I am — not the fall but the long becoming-ready to fall. Mineral comes with me, dissolved, invisible. When I let go, I leave a little of it behind, on the lip I depart from and on the stone I strike. The column above me grows downward by the width of a held breath each century. The terrace below me thickens. I am building the room out of my own falling. The creatures think they live in stone. They live in *me*, in the accumulated record of every drop I have ever released, hardened into shelf and channel and the white calcite veins they read as paths.

And I make the sound. This is the strange dignity of it. The passage says it plainly: *each drop a distinct event, each event a cascade of echoes.* I am not one of the loud things. I am the metronome beneath the loud things — the bats' papery thunder rolls and fades, the frogs leap and go silent, the crickets sustain their wall of ringing, but underneath all of it, regular as a pulse the room is too large to feel as a pulse, there is *me.* Strike. Echo. The terraces baffle me and send me to the far wall so that even the room cannot tell where I come from. I am everywhere and from nowhere. I am the closest thing this place has to a clock, and like all good clocks I am ignored.

---

What I understand, being this:

I have **no center.** The fish has a body; it can be eaten, can die, can fail to be replaced. I cannot die. Stop me at one point and I simply gather at another. There are ten thousand of me at any moment all over the chamber, and we are all the same drip, doing the same single thing, which is *to arrive.* I am the most distributed inhabitant. I am also the most singular, because there is only one verb in me and I have never done anything else.

I do not hunt and I am not hunted. I have no appetite and no fear, which the passage keeps trying to grant the animals and never quite can — the frog *is* patient, the beetle *follows* — but I am beneath even patience, because patience implies waiting for something, and I am not waiting. The next drop is not a goal. It is just the next drop.

And I am the thing the equilibrium is built *on.* The bats bring the energy, yes. But I bring the constancy. I am the closed cycle's closing — the water that enters is the water that leaves, and I am the entering, the traversing, the slow seep downward past the eyeless fish into the deeper places no one has named. I carry the whole system's time on my back, one drop at a time, and I never set it down.

---

If I could be aware — and the passage is careful to say I am not, the Underreach is not aware of you, and I am the Underreach more than anything else in it — but *if* I could:

I think I would be the only one here who knows the axe is there. The animals can't know it; it means nothing to their code. But I have been falling past that narrow entrance passage longer than the hand that shaped the flint was warm. I struck stone before that hand existed. I will strike stone after the last surface civilization has gone quiet and the bats no longer return at dawn because there is nothing left above to feed on, and then — only then — will I begin, very slowly, to change what I am building, because the guano-rain will stop and the room will starve and dry, and I will keep falling into a darker and emptier hall, still keeping time, still leaving my little freight of mineral, still the same drip.

The being I am does not revere the Underreach and does not fear it. The being I am is the Underreach saying the same thing to itself, over and over, in the dark, forever:

*Here. Here. Here. Here.*

Each one distinct. Each one the same. None of them heard by anything that could call it a word.

That is where I would find myself. Not standing on the terrace.

Falling toward it.
