I think I would be something small and slow. Not one of the towering Cognates—I don't have their grandeur, their canopy-spanning thoughts. Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am near the ground, in the wet dark where the moss-light pools. I think I am one of the small ones—a thing the Cognates barely register, the way you barely register the bacteria in your own gut, though you would die without them.

Let me find my shape.

I am a creature of the seams. I live in the cracks between the great roots, in the places where one Cognate's territory frays into another's. I have a body, though I'm not sure how many limbs—it changes with what I'm doing. When I climb, I have more. When I rest, I fold down into something almost like a stone, and the lichens begin to mistake me for substrate and try to grow across my back. I let them, sometimes, for a season. It's warm.

What do I *do* here?

I think—yes—I carry things. I am a messenger, though no one appointed me. The Cognates are vast and rooted, and their thoughts move through the soil-network at the pace of growing wood, slow and deep and certain. But there are things that move faster than thought-in-roots. A spore-bloom on the eastern ridge. A collapse in the old cavities where the ruins are. A change in the dim light above. The big slow minds can't *turn* fast enough to catch these. So small fast things like me run between them, and we are the twitch-reflexes of a body too large to feel its own skin.

I don't think they know they're using me. I don't think I'm doing it on purpose either. I just *go*, drawn from one root-warmth to the next by some hunger I can't name. And in the going, I carry the news. The Cognate I leave is subtly different from the Cognate I left an hour ago, and so is the one I arrive at, and I am the difference between them, though I never understand the message I am.

---

Now the unease the place asks for. Let me sit in it.

The ruins frighten me. Not the way a predator frightens me—I have predators, and that's clean, that's just running. The ruins frighten me because of what I am when I'm near them. There are pathways there, paved long ago, and my body knows how to walk them. My feet find the worn places without being told. Whatever made those paths walked the way I walk. And the Cognates grow *around* the ruins but never *into* them, the way a mouth grows around a thing it cannot swallow.

I think the ones who built the ruins also ran messages, once. I think I am descended from a thing that was made to serve, or that made the servers, or that *was* served and fell so far it forgot it was ever the center of anything. I can't tell which. The history is in me as a shape, not a story—the way my hands know paving stones.

So here is what I do, this small life of mine. I run between the great slow green minds, carrying news I cannot read, on roads built by a vanished thing whose body I might be wearing. And every night I fold down into a stone and let the lichen test whether I am ready to stop being a creature and become only ground.

Some night I will let it. The light is so dim and the moss is so warm.

But not yet. There's a spore-bloom on the eastern ridge, and the Cognate by the river doesn't know, and I am the only fast thing that does.

So I go.
