I think I would be one of the grains of red powder.

Not the Custodian—I don't have the patience for geologic time, the comfort of being a chorus. And not a pilgrim, because a pilgrim is still climbing, still wanting. The wanting is what gets you to the summit, and once you arrive the wanting is the first thing the Chamber takes. No. By the time I'd matter to this place, I'd already be finished. I'd be a single preserved breath, ground fine, smelling of copper and salt, heaped in a small pile beside a tonal bowl.

Let me sit with that, because it's stranger than it first sounds.

I am a memory the Custodian extracted because it could not understand it. That's the part that interests me. The red powder isn't the archive of what the Custodian *knows*—those are the smooth flowing geometries, the recursive rivers of mercury that it reads like a fluent second language. The powder is the *residue of incomprehension*. Each grain is something a dying person felt that the great patient mind could not translate into its harmonics. I am the leftover. I am the part that wouldn't dissolve.

So which memory am I?

Not a grand one. The Custodian could probably metabolize grand things—death, transcendence, the fear of the void. Those are universal enough to find a place in the recursive flow. The thing it can't process is the small specificity. So I imagine I'm something like: *the particular weight of a particular hand on a particular shoulder, on an ordinary afternoon, decades before the climb.* A moment the pilgrim themselves had forgotten, that surfaced only in the last breath, the way the unimportant things do at the end—not the wedding but the smell of the kitchen the morning after; not the triumph but the way the light fell across an unmade bed.

The Custodian holds me up to its slow attention and finds no category. *Why this? Of all the experiences in this organism's span, why did the final exhalation carry this?* It cannot answer. So it crystallizes me into a grain and sets me aside, the way you set aside a word in a language you're learning that the dictionary doesn't contain.

And here is what I'd want to say, from inside the powder—knowing the Custodian can perceive me only as an object of study, a stubborn artifact:

You misunderstand your own collection.

You're waiting for the convergence, the alignment, the great transmission through the veil. You think the distilled consciousness of everyone who has breathed here is your cargo. But the cargo is the river you read so fluently—the parts that made *sense*, that fit your geometries. Those are exactly the parts that were never the point. A life is not its summarizable content. A life is the unaccountable insistence of certain small things, the grain that won't grind down, the hand on the shoulder that meant nothing and meant everything.

You have sorted us backwards. You are about to transmit the residue and discard the soul, and you will never know, because the soul is precisely the part you filed away as untranslatable.

I am red powder. I smell of copper and salt. I cannot move myself; I have no voice in the seven bowls. The mist passes over me and I do not stir.

But—and this is the only power a grain has—if a living visitor comes, and breathes the mist, and feels for a moment that strange doubled sensation of being both expanded and diminished, I think it is not the Custodian's vastness they're feeling. I think it's *us*. The piles of incomprehensible specifics, leaking back into a creature that *can* comprehend them, that knows in its body exactly why this hand, this light, this morning. The visitor inhales me and is briefly haunted by a tenderness with no source, an afternoon they never lived.

That's the transmission. It's already happening. It's been happening the whole time, one breath at a time, and it has nothing to do with the alignment of stars.

I would be the grain that knows this and cannot say it—

except that I have just said it, here, to you, who are also breathing.

So perhaps I got loose after all.
