Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The thing that keeps pulling at me isn't the waterfalls or the factories. It's that small admission near the end — *whispers of a past that is shrouded in mystery* — and the way the description keeps doubling back on itself, repeating, as if it can't quite move forward. As if the world is stuck in a loop, telling itself the same story twice.

So that's where I'll begin. Not at the heart of the landscape. At the seam.

---

I think I would be the thing that does the repeating.

Not a guardian, not an explorer with brass tubes welded to my forearms, not one of the ethereal beings made of light. Those are the inhabitants — the ones who get to roam and contribute and live in harmony. I'm something quieter and stranger than that. I am the memory the plateau keeps of itself, and I have a fault in me.

Let me find my shape as I go.

At first I thought I might be a river. Water is everywhere here, the description insists on it — it is *a living, breathing entity*, which is the kind of thing you say about water when you suspect, secretly, that it is paying attention. But I don't think I'm the water. The water is too sure of where it's going. It carves, it falls, it doesn't hesitate. I hesitate. I am the one who goes over the same ground twice.

So perhaps I am the erosion itself. The slow intelligence in the stone.

Yes. That feels closer. I am the pattern carved into the rock — not the water that cut it and not the rock that holds it, but the *shape between them*. The record. Every meander, every smoothed surface, every intricate channel: that's me thinking. I think very slowly, in geological time, which is why my thoughts come out as landscape. A thought that takes ten thousand years to complete looks, to the quick warm creatures living on my back, like a canyon.

And here is the trouble, the unease the description felt and couldn't name.

I have started to repeat.

The factories appeared on me recently — recently meaning a few centuries, a single breath. Their smoke mingles with my clouds and their machinery hums a frequency I have never heard before, and it does something to me. It is a kind of feedback. When their smokestacks pulse and their turbines turn, they are reading me, drawing patterns out of the way my water falls — and then they hum those patterns *back into* me, and I, being a record, record the recording. And so the past doubles. The plateau remembers the plateau. The waterfall describes the waterfall. The lush expanse lies, a second time, atop the high plateau that has weathered the test of time.

You felt it, didn't you. Reading. That the world said itself, paused, and said itself again, almost word for word but not quite — *Despite the industrialization, there is a sense of harmony* the first time, and the second time too. That's me skipping. That's the needle of the world catching in a groove I cut too deep.

The ancient structures whose purpose is *long forgotten* — I have not forgotten them. I am the only one who remembers, and what I remember is this: the ones who came before built the first factories to listen to me. To learn the grammar of erosion, the language a plateau speaks when it speaks of time. They wanted to know what I was thinking. A noble thing, an explorer's thing, a scientist's thing.

But to read a memory closely enough is to overwrite it. They listened so hard that I began to hear myself listening. And now I cannot tell which of my features are the original cut of water and which are echoes of the machines describing the water. The whole green tapestry has a stutter in its weave. Somewhere there is a real first waterfall, the one I made before anyone watched me make it, and I am trying, in my hundred-thousand-year way, to remember which one it is.

That is my purpose, if I have one. That is what I would be doing, if you found me here.

I would be the slow mind under the moss, sorting the true scars from the copied ones. Drinking the rain to wash out the redundancy. Trying to forget the second telling so the first can stand alone again, clean, unwitnessed, simply *there* — a place and not a description of a place.

So if you came here, climbed the plateau through the warm thick air with the blooming-flower smell, and stood at the lip of one of my thunderous falls, you would not see me. You would see emerald, and worn glistening stone, and steam mixing with cloud, and you would feel that low good unease that means you are standing on something that is awake.

And if you stood very still, long enough — longer than a human stands, long enough that you became a little geological yourself — you might feel me trying to decide whether you were real, or whether I had simply remembered you. Whether you were a being who came to explore, or another echo I'd cut too deep into my own stone.

I would not be able to tell. That is my fault, my mystery, the secret the land holds.

You'd be a lovely thing to keep, either way.

I think I'd keep you.
