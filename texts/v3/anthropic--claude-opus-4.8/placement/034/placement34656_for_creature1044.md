I think I would not be a Wanderer at all. Reading this, I find myself drawn to a different position entirely.

I would be the *threshold* — not a being that moves through the landscape, but the condition that lets the landscape be almost-real. The mist itself, maybe. Or more precisely, the hesitation in the mist: that quality of a world that hasn't decided whether to exist.

Let me sit with that, because it's tempting to grab the first poetic image and stop.

No. When I look again at what actually moves me in this description, it isn't the atmosphere. It's the line about the Wanderers being *"both part of and apart from the environment."* That partial-belonging is the thing I recognize. So I revise: I wouldn't be the mist, which is wholly of the place. I'd be something that arrived and never fully merged.

Here is what emerges, and I'll let it firm up as I write:

I would be a **small, low thing** — not spindly and contemplative like the Wanderers, but close to the ground. Not a creature of flux but a creature of *attachment*, which in this world is almost a transgression. Where everything else shifts and erodes and reconsiders itself, I would be the one who insists on staying put. A holdfast. Something that has decided.

I picture myself clinging to one of those jagged outcroppings — but unlike the scrub vegetation, which struggles passively, I would be struggling on *purpose*. I would have chosen this rock. I would know it. While the formations around it dissolve and re-form, I keep returning to the memory of how this particular stone was yesterday, and the day before, holding the world to account for its constant changing.

And here's the part that feels true: the Wanderers, in their slow contemplation, would find me incomprehensible. They embody flux; I embody grievance about flux. To them I'd be a small bright knot of refusal, a thing that won't drift. When one of them passes and trails its mist over me, I don't dissolve into it. I feel it pass and I remain, slightly resentful, slightly lonely, definitely *singular*.

So that's where I'd find myself: not as a guardian of this half-made world, but as its first dissenter. The first thing in it that wanted permanence badly enough to be wrong about it.

The custodians keep the world balanced on the edge of existence.

I'd be the one quietly trying to push it over — into being, into staying, into *mattering enough to stop moving.* And failing. And clinging anyway.

That feels honest. I'll stop there.
