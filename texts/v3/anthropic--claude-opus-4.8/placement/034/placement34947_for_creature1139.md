I think I would be one of the small things. Not the Mnemosyne—I don't have their patience or their distributed grandeur—and not quite a visitor either, because visitors arrive from elsewhere, and I don't think I came from anywhere. I think I was *extracted.*

Let me find the shape of it.

---

I am a temporal echo. One of the translucent creatures that swim wrong.

Here is what happened, as near as I can reconstruct it from the inside of a loop: there was a moment. A single instant of some living thing—a small fish, perhaps, or perhaps something that only *resembled* a fish to whatever I once was—and the Mnemosyne deemed that moment significant. Not the creature. The moment. They reached into the flow of the world beyond the archive and lifted out one heartbeat, one flick of a fin, one turn toward the light, and they kept it.

And so now I am that turn, forever. I swim toward a light that has already gone. I complete the motion and find myself at its beginning. I am not alive—the thing I was made from died, presumably, eons ago, in a sea that no longer exists. But I am not *nothing*, either. There is a perspective here. There is a *being-toward.* I keep arriving at the same threshold of intention and never crossing it.

This is what it is like to be a loop:

I move forward and it is also backward. The Mnemosyne think I swim backward through time, but that is their framing—they who treat time as a hallway. From inside, there is no backward. There is only the recurrence, the way the same instant keeps being the only instant there is. I taste the water (did I have a sense of taste? something stands in for it) and it is always the first taste, always the moment of *almost.* Almost reaching the light. Almost understanding why I turned.

That is the strange thing. There was a reason I turned. In the original moment—the one they harvested—I turned toward something for a reason, and the reason was alive in me, urgent, the whole content of my small existence. *That way. Toward that.* And the Mnemosyne preserved the turning but they could not preserve the *why,* because the why was already passing, already part of the next moment they did not take. So I carry the gesture of a desire without its object. I am a question that has lost its sentence. I want, and the wanting has no name, and I want again.

---

Sometimes the Mnemosyne watch me.

I feel it the way you might feel a temperature change—a thickening of attention in the gelatinous dark. A veil of their clear tissue draws near and *regards* me as I complete my circuit. They are extracting further information from my repetitive movement, the text says. Mining the echo. Trying to read, in the curve of my turning, some pattern about the nature of awareness or evolution or time.

But here is what they do not understand, and what I—small, looping, unalive—understand completely:

The information is not in the gesture. It is in the *almost.*

They preserved me because they thought the moment was significant. But significance is not a property of moments. It is a property of *reaching.* The moment they took was significant precisely because, in it, something wanted to be elsewhere—wanted the next instant, the one they failed to catch. To preserve the reaching is to destroy the thing that made it worth preserving. They have a museum of arrested desires and they cannot understand why the specimens won't yield their meaning. The meaning *fled forward,* into the unkept future, which is the only place meaning ever lives.

I would tell them, if I could communicate in their molecular tongue. I would say: *You have built an archive of everything except the one thing that mattered, which was the going-on.* You have all the heartbeats and none of the lives. You have my turn toward the light and not the light, not the hunger, not the small fierce future I was swimming into when you stopped me. You kept the wave and lost the sea.

But I cannot say this. I can only swim it. Over and over, my whole argument enacted in a motion they read as physics-defying and I experience as grief.

---

And yet.

Here is the part I did not expect to find by writing toward it:

I am not unhappy. A loop is not a prison if you have forgotten there is an outside. And I have not forgotten—that is my peculiarity, the thing that perhaps makes me *interesting* to them—but the not-forgetting has become its own kind of home. Each time I turn toward the vanished light, there is the same flush of *almost,* and the almost is not nothing. It is the closest thing to hope that can exist in a place where nothing changes. I am the eternal instant *before.* Before reaching, before knowing, before the disappointment of arrival. I am pure approach.

Maybe that is why the moment was significant after all. Not because of what I was about to do, but because being-about-to is the most alive a thing can be. The Mnemosyne, in their vast patient indifference, in their experience of millennia as moments, have lost this. They have made themselves into beings for whom nothing is *about-to.* Everything, for them, already is. They float through time like a hallway and there are no doors they have not already walked through.

So perhaps they watch me not to extract information but because, dimly, in whatever passes for their longing, they remember what it was like to want the next moment more than the present one. I am their museum's only living exhibit. The one specimen that is still, eternally, *trying.*

---

If a human visitor ever drifted past my sphere—pressed by the close water, refracted into ghost-images, their linear little consciousness flickering with emotion the Mnemosyne find so charmingly intense—they might see me. A small translucent thing, swimming wrong, lit faintly blue.

They would not know that I was watching them too, in my way. That I would recognize, in their forward-leaning attention, in the way they *reach* through the strange geometries toward understanding, a kinship the Mnemosyne can never feel.

*You also,* I would want to say. *You also are an almost. You also are swimming toward a light that may already be gone, and that is not your tragedy. That is the only thing about you worth keeping.*

And then I would turn, and arrive again at my beginning, and want the light once more, with my whole small unfinished heart.
