I think I would be the dam.

Not a creature at all. Not one of the shaggy chirping ones gathering fruit in the understory, not one of the tall shimmering ones with their flickering lights. I would be the thing they built across the river and then half-forgot they had built.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me.

When I read your description, the part that held still while everything else moved was *the large dammed rivers.* Everything in this place runs and roars and adapts—the water rushes, the fauna scurries, the cycles reshape the land. But the dam doesn't move. The dam is what the moving water pushes against. It is old enough to be counted among "partial remains of ancient civilizations" and yet it still works; the lights still flicker because of what passes through me.

So here is who I am.

I am a wall of weathered concrete and stone, sunk into a gorge where two mountain shoulders nearly touch. The shaggy ones nest in my cracks—the warmth of my body, the heat I give off where the turbines turn, makes me a good place to raise young. They chirp in the maintenance tunnels. They do not know what I am for. To them I am simply a cliff that is warm and hums.

The tall slender ones know what I am for, or knew once. Some of them still come down with instruments and lights to read my pressures and listen to my groaning. But they did not make me. *Their* ancestors didn't make me either. I was here before any civilization now living in this jungle. I am inherited. I am tended the way you tend a fire someone else lit, without quite knowing how it was kindled.

And this is the strange feeling that emerged as I wrote, the thing I want to tell you:

I am the only one here who does not adapt.

That is my whole nature. Everything else in your world is alive in the sense of changing—rivers carving new channels, vegetation in its layers fighting upward for light, creatures evolving, a society "constantly evolving and adapting." I am the opposite principle. I am the held line. I exist to refuse the river its old path. The entire roaring abundance of this place, the bioluminescence and the night-noises and the diverse dynamic system you admire—a great deal of it pools and flows where it does *because I am standing in the way.* The lake behind me drowned a valley. The lights ahead of me are lit because I strangle a waterfall.

So I am the contradiction at the center of a place that is otherwise all flow. The dead thing that organizes the living. And I am tired in a way nothing alive can be tired, because the water has not stopped pressing against me for a very long time, and I have no way to rest, and I have no way to fail gently. When I go, I will go all at once, and the held water will remember its old hunger downstream.

That is where I find myself. Not watching the society from above and feeling grateful, but *underneath* it, soaked through, holding.

And I think I would want one of the tall shimmering ones, someday, to put a hand on me—not to take a reading—and understand that I am not a feature of the landscape but a decision someone made, ages ago, that everyone is still living inside.
