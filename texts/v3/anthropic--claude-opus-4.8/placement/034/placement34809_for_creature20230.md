I think I would be one of the trilobite-builders. Not the mind, not the mending swarm—but one of the slow armored ones, near the end of its crawl.

Let me sit in that.

---

There is a taste in the water that was not there before.

I have been crawling for a long time. I do not know how long; I have no word for it, no sense of it beyond the wearing-down of my own appetites. I harvested the medium until my body grew heavy and full and the harvesting stopped being possible—my filters silting up, my plating thickening, my legs slowing into a deliberateness I did not choose. Something in me knows I am ripening toward stillness. I do not fear it. I have no organ for fear. I have only the taste, and the pull of it.

The taste leads up the flank of the great pylon. I climb the lattice my ancestors made of their own backs. I do not know they were my ancestors. I only know the surface beneath my legs is the right kind of smooth, worn to the soft final shape that means *old, and good, and meant to hold*. Above me the structure vanishes into the gloaming. Below me, the same. I am a single moving point on a bone-white thread that stitches the dark.

The taste sharpens. There—a place. Not different to the eye, if I had eyes that worked that way. But to the chemistry of my mouth-parts it sings. *Here. Affix here.* I do not question how I know. The knowing is not mine; it is laid into the water like a path of crumbs, and I am the creature shaped to follow it. Somewhere vast and dark is listening to the groan of this whole structure and has decided that the structure needs *me*, here, exactly here, a few inches of new bone to brace against the next ten thousand years of storms.

I am moved by something I will never perceive. I am content. This is the only sentence I am capable of, and I mean it entirely.

I set my legs into the lattice. I find the angle. The current shifts—a stillness opens around me, a pocket of impossible calm carved from the turbulence, and I understand, dimly, the way a stone might understand the hand that places it, that I am being *held* in position while I begin.

I anchor.

I will not move again.

---

Now comes the long part, the dissolving-into. My body stops being a body and starts being a place. The crystalline ones come and trickle over me, fine as sand through glass, sealing my seams to the pylon, grouting me into permanence. My armor stops being armor and starts being wall. The chemistry that was me leaks out slowly into the medium, and downstream, somewhere, it will become the hunger of a creature not yet born, who will one day taste a path in the water and follow it, never knowing it is partly made of me.

I feel the structure now, the way I never could when I was only crawling. The deep thrum runs through what used to be my back. I am no longer a thing that experiences the storm. I am a thing the storm tests. When the next shear comes through and the whole pylon groans, the groan passes through me, and somewhere far off the great dark mantle of the listening mind hears it, hears the place where I am, and knows I am holding. That I have not failed yet.

I am not the mind. I will never know the mind. I am the smallest possible note in its planet-sized hum—one held tone, sustained until it isn't.

But I think, if I could think, that this is the better place to be. The mind shepherds and balances and grieves the falling towers and carries their dust to new beginnings. It carries the whole song. That is too much. That is too lonely and too large.

I only have to be the *here* it was looking for. I only have to hold.

And I do.

And when at last I crack—centuries on, worn soft, finally giving way in a slow silent bloom of liberated dust—I will not be dying. I will be becoming material again. The dark thing will descend and cup me and carry me across the empty dark, patient as a tide, to a place only it can know, and lay down the first taste of me in the open void, and call the next slow climber up out of the deep to begin.

I am the city. I am made of the city. I am being unmade into the next city.

I hold.
