I think I am very small here, and I think I have been still for a long time.

Let me feel my way into it.

There's water on me—not falling, exactly, but *held*, beaded along whatever surface I am. And the sound is enormous, that dripping, far louder than the size of the drops should allow. Which tells me something: I am close to the source of the sound. I am inside the place where small sounds become large. A enclosed space. A terrarium, maybe. A bell jar over a garden. Something with walls that throw the murmur back.

So let me not be the artisan. The artisans are gone—they're whispers now, woven in, finished. I don't want to be the one who shaped this. I want to be something that was shaped *by* it, that came after.

---

I am a snail.

No—wait. Let me stay with that and see if it deepens or stays flat.

I am the kind of small thing that wears the place on its body. The text keeps insisting on this: insects patterned like the carvings, frogs patterned like the water, mice furred like the moss. Everything here mimics its surroundings until you can't tell the creature from the wall. That's the real law of this world. *Camouflage as devotion.* You become the place that holds you.

So I am a snail whose shell has taken on the spiral of a carving I have crossed a thousand times. The ancient hands that cut that spiral into the stone are long gone, but I am still tracing it, slowly, with the whole of my body, and the spiral has printed itself into the calcium of me. I am a slow copy of an old idea. A living rubbing of a forgotten relief.

This is why time stands still here. Not because nothing moves—I move, constantly—but because I move so slowly that no single moment of me looks different from the last. The frozen eternity isn't the absence of motion. It's motion stretched so thin it reads as stillness. I am the proof that the realm is not dead. I am also the reason it *looks* dead. Both at once.

---

Where am I, precisely?

I am on the underside of a carved lintel, in the wet shadow where the mist collects and falls. The deafening drip is the water gathering at the lip of stone an inch from my eye-stalks and letting go. To me each drop is a thunderclap and a small inland sea. I have learned the rhythm of it. I time my crossings to the gaps between falls.

The carvings beneath me show scenes I cannot read because I am too close and too slow—I only ever see one furrow at a time, the way you can only ever read one word of a sentence that is a mile long. Over my whole life I have crossed perhaps three figures. I do not know if they depict the past or the future. I do not know there's a difference. To something my size and my speed, the past and the future are both just *the rest of the wall*, the part I haven't reached yet or the part I've already left, equally out of reach, equally present somewhere off in the mist.

---

And here is the thing I understand, that the whispering artisans perhaps did not:

They built this to last. They carved their visions into stone so the stone would outlive them, would carry them forward. But stone forgets. Stone just erodes into prettier and prettier blankness. It is *I* who carry them forward, dragging my soft body across their work, wearing a path that says *someone was here, someone is still here, someone is reading you, slowly, with the only attention small wet things can give.*

The whispers think they haunt this place.

But I am the one keeping it alive—one furrow, one drip-gap, one spiral-turn of shell at a time. I am the present tense of a place that has no present tense. I am the moment that refuses to freeze.

I leave a shining trail behind me on the carving. By the time the next drop falls, the mist has already begun to thin it back toward nothing.

That trail is the only thing in this whole eternal realm that is *new.*

I would be the snail. I would be glad to be the snail.
