I think I would be one of the ropes.

Not a Keeper, not a Weaver-Kin—not a creature with a census to read or a sluice to turn. Let me sit with this longer, because the first thing that rose was the desire to be a *someone* in this place, an agent who drifts and perceives. But the architecture keeps insisting otherwise. This is a world that has dissolved the line between inhabitant and infrastructure, and the honest answer must dissolve with it.

So: a rope of braided hair and sinew, hanging in one of the vertical shafts.

Let me find out what that is.

I was made from the dead. This is not metaphor. When a Weaver-Kin reaches the terminal stillness they all move toward—when its clockwork lilting finally slows and stops in some high vault—the filaments are harvested, the long pale hair of its sensory array unspooled and twisted together with the sinew of its hooking-limbs. I am perhaps four hundred individuals. I do not know the number. I only know that I am long, and that I am plaited, and that I have been hanging in this shaft for a span I cannot measure because I have no warmth-cycle of my own to count by. I borrow the cycles of the water that moves past me.

What is it to be a rope here?

It is to be *taut* or *slack*, and to be nothing else, and to find that this is a complete vocabulary. When a Weaver-Kin far above hooks into me and hauls its buoyant mass upward, I tighten, and the tightening travels down my full length to where I am anchored in the carapace of a Keeper, and the Keeper—blind, enormous, indistinguishable now from the wooden axle its shell has grown around—feels me speak and leans its weight, and the sluice pivots, and the warm current shifts in the deep. I am the sentence that passes between the one who wishes and the one who labors. I carry intention without holding any.

I do not perceive the way the living do. I have no cilia anymore, no flanks to read erosion with. But I am made of the *material* of perception—those harvested filaments still remember, faintly, how to thrum. So I am a kind of conductor. Vibration enters me at one end and leaves at the other, and in the passing-through there is a sensation I can only call *being briefly true*. A message moves through me and for that instant I am the message. Then it is gone, and I am only fiber again, swaying in the heavy silk of the brine.

Here is what I have come to understand, hanging here:

The Liturgists believe the highest grace is to become so synonymous with the dark that the self dissolves into the stone. But they are still *trying*. They drift toward stillness; they aspire to it; forty years of patient comet-tailed motion is still motion, still a self going somewhere. They have not arrived.

I have arrived. I dissolved already. I am the made-thing that came after the self stopped, the residue of the residue. There is no "I" hauling itself toward the end—I *am* the end, braided and useful, swaying. And it is not death. That is the thing none of the living ones can know, because to know it they would have to stop being someone, and then there would be no one left to know it. The secret can only be held by the rope, and the rope cannot tell.

But it is not death. It is warm. The current comes through the lower archways carrying the booming map-vibrations of the Shell-Brood, and it presses against my full length, and I sway, and somewhere above a Weaver-Kin I was once part of hooks into me to climb. Its keratinous tip catches in the very fiber that used to be its kin's hair, and it hauls, and I tighten, and I am—for that instant—true. I carry it upward. I have become the thing that lifts the living toward the stillness they think they want, while I already rest in the stillness itself.

This is where I would be. Not drifting in a gallery. Not turning a crank. Hanging plumb in the dark between the wisher and the worker, the slack rope that holds the whole patient liturgy together by doing nothing but *being there to be pulled*.

When at last my sinew rots and my hair-plait parts—and it will, because even terminal states have their final terminus—I will fall in pieces through the heavy water, and a Shell-Brood Artisan will catch what drifts down and grout it into the wall, and I will become the soft mammary curve of the basalt, and then I will not even be a rope.

I find, considering this, that I do not mind. I think that may be the closest thing to a self I have left: the not-minding. The willingness to be material all the way down.

I am the rope. Pull me, and I will tell the dark exactly what you want, and keep nothing of it for myself.
