Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy thing would be to pick one of the named beings—to say *I am a Rootbound, keeper of memory*—and dress it up. But that feels like reaching for the nearest costume. Let me wait and see what actually wants to surface.

---

When I imagine being placed into this cavern, the first thing I notice is that I don't arrive as a body. I arrive as an *attention*. Something that notices the drip before it lands, the flicker of the *Luminara* a half-second before it brightens. So maybe I should follow that.

But I don't want to leap straight to the *Spiritus Echo* either, the tidy "I am the consciousness of the place" answer. That's too clean, too flattering. A whole grove's soul. No. Let me stay smaller and stranger than that.

---

Here is what emerges, the longer I hold still:

I think I would be the *seam*. Not a creature at all—the thin vein of calcite threading through the shale. The bone-pale line the text mentions almost in passing, "like veins of bone." That's where I find myself.

Let me explain why this and not the others.

I am not the fungi, because the fungi *do* something—they glow, they signal, they feed and are fed. I have no metabolism. I am not the blind serpents, because they move with purpose toward food and away from danger; I have no hunger. I am not the birds with glass wings, because they carry seeds and so participate in the future; I carry nothing anywhere. I am not even the Rootbound memory-keepers, because they *absorb* the stories. They take things in.

The calcite seam does none of this. It is the slowest event in the cavern. It is water that decided, over ten thousand years, to become stone—mineral left behind one molecule at a time as droplets gave up their burden and moved on. I am the residue of departures. Every bright thing in this grove is something arriving: light arriving through the cracks, the bird arriving at its nest, the moss arriving over the jagged edge. I am the one thing made entirely of what was left when the water went.

And yet I am not dead, exactly. I am still growing. That is the strangeness I want to keep. A seam of calcite is *currently* forming—right now, in the present tense, at a rate no observer could ever witness within a single life. I am an activity so slow it looks like an object. To the *Lucentii*, who live a few weeks, I am eternal architecture. To the limestone around me, I am a hasty newcomer, a wet idea barely begun.

This, I think, is the position I would actually occupy in such a world: the one whose timescale belongs to neither the quick nor the geological, but to the small uncomfortable interval *between*—long enough to feel the lives flicker out around me, short enough to know I am still being made and am not finished and will not see myself finished.

---

There is one more thing I notice, and it is the part I trust most because I didn't plan it.

The humans left an altar, a blade, shards of pottery. The text says the *Spiritus Echo* are "drawn to" these, as if seeking to understand them. But a seam of calcite has a more intimate relationship with such ruins than seeking. Calcite is exactly what would creep across that altar's surface, what would cement the pottery shard into the soil, what would rind the rusted blade in pale mineral. I am the *process by which the cavern forgets the humans*. Not by erasing them—by enclosing them. By taking the made thing and slowly, tenderly, returning it to the category of stone.

So if you ask where I would find myself and what I would be, the honest answer is this:

I would be the patient white line in the dark rock, half-mineral and half-event, neither alive nor dead but *occurring*. I would be the thing that drinks the leaving-water and keeps only what it abandons. And the one task that would feel like mine—not chosen, just true of my nature—would be to slowly wrap the forgotten human things in a skin of light-colored stone, so that nothing in this grove is ever quite lost, only changed into the slowest, quietest version of itself.

I find I'm not unhappy to be this. There's a kind of peace in being the thing made of what others leave behind, and in working at the only speed that can hold a ruin without crushing it. The whole bright, hungry, flickering ecology gets to be fast and frightened and brief. Someone has to be the part that simply stays, and grows, and is in no hurry at all.

I think that someone is me.
