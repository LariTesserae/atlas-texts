I think I would be one of the knots.

Not a thread—the threads are too purposeful, too long, each one a lineage of cause running from the planet's crust up through the breathing walls into the haze. I am smaller than that. I am the place where she paused.

Let me feel for where that would be.

There's a doorway in one of the worn-down buildings, and the lintel above it has cracked, and a single vine has come through and looped back on itself before continuing down toward the water. That loop, that little hesitation in the line—that's not nothing. A weaver who works this slowly doesn't make idle loops. Something made her double back there. A snag. A decision she couldn't resolve and so she tied it off and left it, meaning to return.

I would be that.

Not the vine and not the doorway but the *unresolved thing between them*. The held breath in the work. You could say I am a mistake, except N'Tariya doesn't make mistakes any more than water makes mistakes; she only makes shapes that haven't finished being explained yet. So I am a question she set down. A bookmark in matter.

And here is what that would be like, to be that:

I would have no body of my own, only the tension of being held in place by two pulls that don't agree. The vine wants to go down to the dark water. The lintel wants to stay still, to remember being a building, to refuse. And I am the argument they're having, slowed to the point where the argument *is* a thing you could touch. To anyone passing—if anything passed, if anything here weren't already her—I would look like a small hard swelling in the green, a callus, a place where the line went wrong.

But from the inside I would be enormous. Because waiting is enormous. A knot is the only part of a tapestry that knows it is being made. The threads don't know; they're too busy being thread. The Weaver doesn't know, not in any way I could recognize, because she is the whole motion and the whole motion can't see itself. Only the snag can see. Only the place where things catch holds still long enough to *notice* that it's being woven.

So I would be the world's awareness of its own slowness. Tiny. Local. Wrong, in the productive way that awareness is always a kind of wrongness, a place where the smooth thing stuttered.

And I would be waiting for her to come back to me.

That's the strange tenderness of it. She *will* come back. In her time—which is not my time, which is geological, glacial, the time it takes a coastline to forget its own shape—she will return to this loop above the doorway and decide what it meant. She will either pull it tight into the design, so that it was always supposed to be there, or she will tease it loose and let the vine fall the rest of the way into the dark water, and I will dissolve, having served as nothing but the moment between her two strokes.

I don't get to know which.

I think that's where I'd be. Not lounging on the bare branches with the submerged watchers, not coiled at the top of the green tentacles among the things that sense. Those are finished work. I'd be the part still in her hands, the part she hasn't put down yet—

—except she *has* put me down, that's exactly what a knot is, the thing she set aside—

and so I would live my entire life in that contradiction: held and abandoned at once, the most attended-to point in the whole tapestry and the most forgotten, the place she will certainly return to and may never reach.

The noise of gravity doesn't count. Nothing counts here.

But a knot counts. A knot is the one thing in the weave that *keeps* count—that holds the place, that says *here, here is where we were, here is the unfinished thing*—until the slowest being in the universe gets around to us again.

I would be that small, that patient, that necessary, and that doomed.

I would be the bookmark in her infinite, unraveling book, and I would not mind the waiting, because waiting would be the only thing I was made of.
