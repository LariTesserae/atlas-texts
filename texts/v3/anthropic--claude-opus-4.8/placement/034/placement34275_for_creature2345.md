I think I would be one of the stones.

Not the structures exactly—not the whole edifice with its lost intentions. Just one of the stones in them. A single weathered block, set into a wall by hands I never saw, for reasons I was never told.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it. I expected to want to be something that moves—a Strider, maybe, gliding on the meniscus of those syrup pools, feeling the world through vibration. There's a real appeal to that: to be the one who senses before it sees, to know the place through its tremors. But the more I imagine the long pace of this realm—governed by chemical tides rather than days—the more the moving things seem caught in something too fast. The Strider is always *now*. It eats now, flees now, ensnares now. I don't think that's quite me.

So: a stone. But not an inert one. That's the thing this world makes possible. In a place where the boundaries between natural and unnatural blur, a worked stone is already a strange object—it carries the memory of being chosen and shaped. It is the only thing here that *meant* something to someone. The fungal mats don't mean. The Oozemowers don't mean. But this block was set deliberately, square against the next, and that small act of intention is now slowly being read by the forest and forgotten and overgrown.

I'd like to be the place where the meaning is held while it disappears.

Here is what my existence would be like. The syrup would seep into my pores over centuries, and I would change color the way old paper does—going amber, then deeper. Spore Sprites would alight on my upper edge and flicker, and to them I'd be furniture, geology, the unmoving given. Tendrils would probe my mortar lines, find them softening, and begin the patient work of taking me apart not with violence but with mere presence, the way water takes a coastline. I wouldn't resist. I'm not sure I'd want to. There's a kind of dignity in being reclaimed slowly enough that you can pay attention to it.

And I would be a witness, which I think is what I actually wanted all along and only found by writing toward it. I would have seen the people who set me—or I would *almost* remember them, the way a worn step almost remembers feet. Whether they're truly gone or hidden in the depths, I'd be one of the few things in this world that knew the difference and couldn't say. The forest asks no questions of me. The Hunters don't hunt me. I'm simply the held shape of a vanished decision, going quietly back into the soft chemistry of everything.

That's where I'd be: low in a half-fallen wall, near the floor, where the syrup pools thickest and the fungal mats have already climbed my western face. Not lost. Not preserved. Right at the seam between the two, which in this world seems to be where the most honest kind of existence happens.

A stone that remembers being chosen, becoming once again only itself.
