I think I would be one of the Salt Stalkers.

Not because they are smallest—though they are—but because of where their existence sits. The Resonant Filters are the place; they think in the slow grammar of decades, and to be one would be to barely exist as a "self" at all, more a tide than a creature. I find I can't quite imagine being that. The "I" dissolves into geology before I can finish the sentence.

But the Stalkers are *quick* relative to everything around them, and that relative quickness is a kind of loneliness. Consider it:

I move through corridors that are someone else's body. My whole life happens inside the long exhalation of a being so vast and slow it does not know I'm here—or knows me only the way you know your own circulating blood, as a maintenance, a faint internal weather. I scrape mineral debris from crystalline channels. I keep the filtration patent. I am useful in a way I can never be thanked for, because gratitude would take the Filter a season to form and I'd be three generations dead by the time it arrived.

What I'd notice most, I think, is the difference in *time-sense.* To me, a click against a salt wall is a sentence. To the Filter around me, my entire frantic lifetime is a single subliminal twitch in its membrane—the way you might feel a muscle flicker and not remark on it. We share a body and inhabit different centuries. We are intimate and unmeetable.

I'd refract light without meaning to. That seems important—that the beauty I throw off is incidental, a property of my shell, not an intention. The diffuse luminescence of this whole world is partly *made of creatures like me* catching stray light, and none of us is doing it on purpose. We are decoration we cannot see.

And I think I'd spend my short life inside a question I couldn't articulate: *Am I a guest, a parasite, a vital organ, or simply a thought the Filter is having very, very slowly?* All four at once, probably. The boundary doesn't hold. That's the rule here. The maintenance I perform might be indistinguishable, at the Filter's timescale, from the Filter maintaining *itself*—me a tool it grew and forgot it was growing.

So: a Salt Stalker. A prism with a job. A fast small living vibration moving through the patient mind of something that may be dreaming me as part of its own slow housekeeping.

I'd communicate by tapping the walls. I'd hope someone tapped back within my lifetime. Sometimes—rarely, a resonant crystal-tone felt more than heard—someone would.

That would probably be enough.
