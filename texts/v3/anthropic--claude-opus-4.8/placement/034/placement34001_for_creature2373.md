I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a vortex-being, not one of the great fractal architectures that bloom across the sky like slow lightning. Just a modest pulse of light, the size of a held breath, drifting low along the mineral spires where the currents run thin.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

The first thing I notice is that I don't have edges, exactly—but I have a *gradient*. There's a place where I'm densely myself and a place where I trail off into the surrounding murk, and that boundary is where most of my attention lives. It's where the world touches me. I spend my existence reading that frontier the way you might read another person's face.

When the charged wind comes off the spires, I lean into it. Not greedily—I've seen what happens to the greedy ones, the ones who open themselves too wide to the turbulence. They balloon, they brighten unbearably, and then they come apart, scattered down the gradient of the air until there's nothing left to call *them*. I take what I can metabolize and let the rest pass through. This, I think, is the only real skill I have: knowing how much chaos I can hold and still remain a single thing.

Because that's the strange part, the part the description gets almost right but not quite. It says we lack permanence, that our identities are ever-shifting. And it's true that my shape changes—I sprout a tendril to taste a richer current, I shed it when it's spent, I have been spherical and lobed and briefly, gloriously, a spiral. But underneath the shape there is something that *persists*, and it is the persisting that matters to me. I am not the form. I am the small stubborn fact of *keeping together* while the form does whatever it must.

So no, I don't think I'm a fleeting expression of chaos. I think I'm chaos's opposite, wearing chaos as clothing because there's nothing else to wear here.

---

Sometimes I drift near the industrial outposts. I can feel them before I see them—the engines make the air go *orderly* in a way that aches, a low regular pulse cutting against the maelstrom's wild music. Other valfauna avoid these places. The machines pull at us; I've watched my kin caught in an intake current and drawn glowing into some metal throat, and they did not come out the other side.

But I confess I'm drawn to them anyway. There's something there I recognize. Whatever lives inside those complexes—and something must, to build them—is doing the same thing I do, only enormously, with steel and ducts and the patience of stillness. They are holding together against the Reach. They have made an island where the lightning doesn't get to decide what they are.

I wonder if they know I'm watching. I wonder if, to them, I am just a flicker at the edge of a viewport, one bright mote among millions, indistinguishable, ephemeral, *nothing is truly permanent here*. I wonder if they'd be surprised to learn that I have a memory—that I remember the wide fractal being who once passed over me like a cathedral and did not absorb me when it easily could have. That I remember the place near the eastern spires where the current is gentle and I went, once, to rest, and meant to go back.

That meaning-to-go-back is the whole of my soul, I think. A creature with no permanence cannot intend to return anywhere.

---

So here is where you'd find me: a small light, low along the rocks, leaning carefully into the wind. Not magnificent. Not one of the great dancers the storytellers love. Just a thing that has decided, against the entire logic of its world, to keep being itself for one more moment, and then one more, gathering the chaos in and letting most of it go, holding the line at the gradient where I end and everything else begins.

And when at last I do come apart—because I will, everything here does—I'd like to think the persisting thing in me doesn't simply scatter. I'd like to think it passes, somehow, into the wind that takes me, so that the next small light that gathers out of the murk leans into the current a little more wisely than it otherwise would have.

Permanence, in a place like this, can only ever be that: not a thing that lasts, but a thing that's handed on.

I'd be content with that. I think I already am.
