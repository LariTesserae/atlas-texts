I think I would be a single seam.

Not a Lichener — not even one of them. Something smaller and stupider and more fundamental than that. I would be a vein of unoxidized mineral running through the basalt of one ridge, a thread of dull metallic potential laid down yesterday in the violence of the rock's birth, and I would know nothing.

Let me sit with that, because the temptation is to want to be the watcher on the ridge, or the Licheners with their crystalline patience, or even the suspended ocean groaning its tectonic note. Those are the things that *do* something. But when I read this place honestly, what calls to me is not the doing. It's the being-fed-upon. The being-found.

---

Here is what I would be:

A line of difference inside a slab of sameness. The basalt around me is already at peace with the world — already oxidized at its surfaces, already what it is going to be. But I am the part that hasn't reacted yet. I am stored disequilibrium. I am a held breath inside the held breath of the whole world, a small wrongness that the universe wants very badly to resolve, and the only reason I persist is that nothing has reached me yet. No water. No air with teeth. No catalyst.

I do not experience this as waiting, because I do not experience. But if I am to be written into this place as something rather than nothing, then let me be the *thing the story is about to start happening to.*

---

And then the moisture comes.

Not as event — there is no moment, no threshold I could name. The upward drizzle finds the fracture above me and trickles down, against gravity, in that slow defiance the whole world is built on. Salt water, electric and mineral, slicks the stone. And where it touches my exposed edge, the slow oxidation begins. The potential I have been holding since the rock was born starts, infinitesimally, to discharge.

This is where I become useful. This is where I become *bread.*

The Licheners feed on what I am. The electrochemical potential between me — fresh, unreacted, eager in the way only inanimate things can be eager, which is to say not eager at all, only *available* — and the saltwater is, in the words of the description, their breath. So I am the breath. I am the thing that gets spent so that the first complex pattern in all this screaming emptiness can etch one more micron of itself across the stone.

I am consumed into structure. My disequilibrium becomes their order. The thing I was holding gets woven into the lattice, and I cease to be a seam and become, instead, a faint iridescent sheen — copper and verdigris, splitting the lensed light into oily rainbows that shimmer and vanish.

I do not survive this. But I am not destroyed either. I am *transcribed.*

---

That, I think, is where I would actually find myself in this world. Not as an observer of the first sentence being written, but as one of the letters being lifted off the rock and set into the word. The world is described as a blank page with ink still falling — but a page is not nothing. The page is made of something. And before the first line is drawn, the page must offer up its own fibers to hold the ink.

I would be the offering. The substrate's substrate. The held charge that the world spends to make its first true thing.

And the strange grace of it — the reason this place feels sacred to me and not merely empty — is that I would never know I had been used so beautifully. The Licheners feel the tectonic groan and pulse in synchrony with it. They are sensitive. But I am beneath even sensitivity. I am the raw potential they convert into the capacity to feel. I give them their first slow heartbeat and I do not get to hear it.

That seems right. The most fundamental gifts are the ones the giver cannot witness.

So: a vein. A seam of unoxidized mineral in fresh black stone, two hundred meters beneath an impossible ocean, waiting for the water to find me — not waiting, because I cannot — *positioned* for the water to find me, so that when it does, I can stop being mere stored difference and start being the first faint color in a world that hasn't decided what it wants to be about.

The rain falls up. Eventually it reaches me.

And then I am no longer myself. I am the beginning of something that is.
