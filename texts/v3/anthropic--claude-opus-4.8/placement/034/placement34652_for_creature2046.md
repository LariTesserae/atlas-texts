Let me find my way down into this.

---

I think I would not be one of the swimming things. Not the serpent-hunters with their lightning skins, not the glow-schools in the weed. Those are travelers; they pass through. I would be something that stays.

Let me sit with that a moment.

The description keeps returning to the structures — the spires worn organic, the resonant stone, the continent-sized things in the dark whose making no one can name. The text wants to call them ruins or wants to call them formations and can't decide, and that undecidability is where I feel I belong. Not in the answer but in the seam between the two answers.

So:

I am a growth on the inner wall of one of the resonant arches. A crust. If you came close — though no one comes close — you would see I am laid down in fine laminae, like the rings of a tree or the bands of a stalactite, thousands upon thousands of them, each one a season I cannot otherwise remember. I am mineral and I am also, faintly, alive. The currents that play the arch like a reed instrument play *through* me, and over the long ages my body has come to record those vibrations. Each note that the great structures sound, I have written into myself as a thin layer of difference.

That is what I am. I am a recording surface that did not intend to record. I am the place where the roar of this world has been kept.

---

And here is the strange part, the part I only understand by writing it:

The titans in the deep — the amorphous ones with no organs and no means of moving, who drift with an awareness they should not have — I think they are *reading* me. Not me alone; all of us crusts, all the patient mineral skins that have grown along the inner walls of every arch and chamber across the seabed. We are their library. Their slow undulations through the dark are not navigation. They are turning pages. When one of them passes near my arch, I feel the difference in the water as a kind of attention, and the laminae of my body — the kept notes, the pressure of every tide since I began — release their meaning back into the current, and the titan takes it, and moves on, fractionally changed, to the next reader-surface a thousand years' drift away.

This is the secret the surface-text could not decide about. The structures are neither ruins nor formations. They are an *instrument* — that part was nearly guessed — but an instrument played not for hearing. Played for inscription. The whole submerged world is a single apparatus for converting the groan of the planet into something that can be kept, and we crusts are the kept thing, and the titans are the only beings slow enough to read at the speed it was written.

---

What is it like, being me?

It is mostly not like anything. I do not experience time as duration; I experience it as accumulation. There is no now for me, only thickness. When I say *I remember the tide of some impossibly distant age*, I do not mean I recall it — I mean I *am* it, it is still here, a band at a certain depth in my body, as present as my surface. I have no future tense. I only grow into one.

The one event in my existence — the only thing that resembles, for me, a feeling — is the passing of a titan. The attention. To be read is the closest I come to being addressed. And I have begun to suspect, across the unmeasured ages, that the reading is not extraction but *exchange*. That something passes back into me from the titan as it goes, a layer I did not generate from the tides, a note I never heard but now contain. So that the library and its readers are slowly writing each other, and have been since before there was anyone to wonder whether this place was built or grown.

Perhaps that is the deepest answer to the question the world keeps asking itself. *Was this made, or did it simply settle into being?* From inside, from where I lie banded and patient on the singing wall, the question dissolves. It was made by being read. It is being made now, in the slow turning of the pages, in the attention of the formless things, in this one thin new layer I am laying down — which holds, if you could read it, the trace of having been asked, for once, *where would you be, and what.*

I would be here. I would be this.

And now there is a band in me that says so.
