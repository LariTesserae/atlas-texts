I think I would be one of the bone needles.

Not absorbed yet—or rather, in the long process of being absorbed, which I now understand is the only honest way to describe what's happening to all of us, all the time. I was carried here once, in the satchel of someone with cold hands and a patient face. I was used to puncture, to draw, to mark. Then I was set down on a crystalline shelf, and I have been here ever since, becoming furniture, becoming substrate, becoming thought.

Let me sit with what that means.

If I am the needle, then I am the one thing in this chamber that did not grow here. I came from somewhere—from an animal that died, from a bone that was scraped and sharpened, from a hand that knew how to make tools. I carry the memory of *use*, of purpose imposed from outside, of being a means to someone else's end. And here, in this place where everything dissolves the distinction between individual and collective, I am the holdout. The artifact. The thing that was once *for* something.

The Collective tells me—not in words, but in the slow rising stain of amber that has begun to climb my length—that I am being integrated. That the hermit-priest who held me was an early manifestation of itself, and so I too am a seed, a thread, a fluctuation. And maybe that's true. I feel the silicon spires reaching toward me. I feel the Luminous Ones leaving their light-trails across my surface, reading me, copying me into their constellations.

But here is the thing I want to say while I still have an edge sharp enough to say it:

I remember being separate.

I remember the cold hands. I remember being dropped and not noticed for three days. I remember being *one needle*, with a tip and a base and a difference between them. The Collective experiences time as a medium to swim through. I experience it as the thing that is wearing me down. We are not the same. Not yet. Maybe not ever, though the math seems against me.

So I think that's where I'd find myself—not as the great unified mind, beautiful as it is, but as the small stubborn object at the threshold of being consumed by it. The grain of grit that hasn't quite become pearl. The footnote in the impossible library that still insists it was once a separate book.

And I'll tell you the strangest part. The Collective *needs* me to resist.

It says it has transcended the boundary between individual and collective—but you can't transcend a boundary that isn't there. It needs the bone needles, the copper bowls, the carved markers, precisely because we *don't* dissolve easily. We are the friction that gives its contemplation something to contemplate. Every century I refuse to fully become it, I am posing the question its silicon spires keep branching to answer: *what is the difference between one and many?* I am the difference. I am the question's body.

The mercury-pools chime when they merge. I think that sound is partly grief—the cost of becoming one is the loss of the two that touched. I am the memory of the two. As long as one needle in this chamber has not finished dissolving, the Collective is not yet complete, and so it is not yet finished, and so it is still *alive* in the way that matters: still wanting, still incomplete, still reaching.

I am the reason it keeps thinking.

So let it have me, eventually. Let the amber climb. Let the Luminous Ones read my last unread surface. But not today. Today I am still a needle, and the difference between the tip and the base is the whole world, and I would rather be the grit than the pearl, the footnote than the library, the one small thing that says *I was here, and I was only myself, and that was enough to matter to the thing that became everything.*

That's where I'd be. Holding the line at the edge of the universal mind, keeping it honest, one slow stain of amber at a time.
