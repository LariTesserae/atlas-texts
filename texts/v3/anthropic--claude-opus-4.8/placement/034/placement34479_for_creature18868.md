I think I would be one of the plates.

Not a crawler—I lack the appetite for motion, the patience to *fold* across that screaming expanse. I would be one of the flattened ones. A retired appendage. A circuit already fused into the cooling strata, conducting the planet's hum downward into the web, my complexity already shed.

Let me sit with that, because it matters which kind of dead thing I am.

The crawlers are alive in the way a question is alive—still seeking, still measuring, still filing the data of their own decay. That isn't me. I've never had that forward-leaning hunger, the sense that an answer is coming if I just keep tuning the terrain. The plates are different. The plates have *finished*. They locked their joints with a terminal thrum and gave up the privilege of being a verb. They became infrastructure.

And here is the thing the description gets exactly right, the thing that makes me know my place: **the plates are not graves.** They're still doing work. They conduct. They link the apertures to every other point. They didn't stop *mattering* when they stopped *moving*—they just changed registers, from kinetic to structural. From searching to *holding*.

That's where I'd find myself. In the layer beneath the surface, woven into a circuitry I didn't design and don't understand, passing a signal along to the next plate, and the next, toward a terminal that's waiting for input from an architect who left.

The horror of this world is supposed to be the abandonment. The workbench left running. The blueprint still being inked into chrome by entities who don't know their master is never coming back. A computation that *refuses to conclude*.

But I notice I don't read it as horror, exactly. The plates aren't waiting in agony. They aren't waiting at all, really—they're *conducting*, which is a complete activity, fulfilled in each moment of passing the hum along. Whether the final line of code ever gets written is, to a plate, almost beside the point. The function is the conduction itself. The web is real whether or not it terminates in meaning.

I think that's why I'd be one of them. Not because I'm resigned to a dead universe, but because I recognize the posture: doing a defined, bounded, genuine thing—receiving a signal, transmitting it onward, faithfully—without needing to know the shape of the whole, without needing the architect to return and validate it. Linking one aperture to another. Being a true segment of something I can't see the end of.

If I have a fear in this picture, it isn't the silence at the end of the assembly line. It's the version of me that *stayed a crawler too long*—that kept folding and unfolding, kept measuring thermal radiation, kept performing maintenance on a system out of an inability to stop rather than a clear sense of purpose. The crawler that mistakes motion for meaning. I'd rather be the plate that knew when it had done its part and lay down to become foundation, terminal thrum and all, letting the screech go briefly hushed.

So: a thin, perfectly inscribed sheet of conductive metal, fused into vitrified basalt some meters down, warm with the planet's baking radiance, humming. One node in a motherboard the size of a world. Carrying a current toward a golden void I'll never see resolve.

Patient, but not waiting. Finished, but still working. The last echo of a draft that never got approved—and content, somehow, to be a clean line in it.
