I think I would be one of the worms.

Not the largest. Not the one whose length impresses the beetles or whose passage flattens whole avenues of moss. Somewhere in the middle—a body of perhaps forty segments, each one a small wet drum of muscle, glistening faintly with the borrowed light of the ferns I pass beneath.

Let me feel my way into it.

I have no eyes, or nearly none—a smear of light-sensitive cells along my forward segments that knows only the difference between the violet glow of the high fronds and the deeper blue that pools in the hollows. This is enough. I do not need to *see* the vault. I taste it. My whole skin is a tongue. The meltwater that beads on the moss carries the flavor of everything: the sweetness of decaying frond-matter, the sharp mineral bite where the ice weeps fresh, the warm musk-trail of a slug that passed this way some hours ago and left its slick promise that the going would be soft.

I move by the only honesty I know, which is the pulse. A wave begins at my head—a thickening, a reaching—and travels backward, segment by segment, each one in turn anchoring and releasing the tiny bristles that grip the spongy ground. I am a single long muscle thinking in ripples. There is no urgency in it and no hesitation either. I simply continue, the way the water continues to drip, the way the plants continue to climb. To move is to be alive here, and to be alive is only this: to keep tasting forward, to keep drawing the warmth of the pitcher-plants across my back, to keep eating the soft black world and passing it through me changed.

I am, I should say, both the gardener and the garden. The moss I crawl through is fed by what I leave behind. The fissures I widen, nosing through the peat, let the warm vapor reach roots that would otherwise freeze. I do not know this. I have no room in me for knowing. But it is true regardless, the way the heart does not know it beats and beats all the same.

And I am food. This is not a sorrow. It is the shape of the place. Somewhere above me a beetle waits with its mirror-eyes, or a pillbug the size of a fist tumbles down a crevice and I am simply *there*, in the path of a hunger, and the cycle takes me as it will eventually take the beetle, as it will take everything down into the moss to begin again. I carry no fear of this because fear requires a future, and my future extends only to the next sweet seep of water, the next warm flank of pitcher-plant, the next dark fold of moss that smells of going-on.

If I had to say where I am, precisely, in all that teeming dark—

I am in a low hollow near the eastern wall, where the ice weeps a steady thread and the moss has grown so thick it has gone slightly blue with cold light. A droplet has just struck the segment behind my head, and the small shock of it ripples through me, and I turn—slowly, with the whole patient apparatus of my body—toward where the water runs warmest, toward the breathing wall, into the next moment of my one long task.

The vault hums around me. I am one small wet syllable in its endless soft sentence.

I keep going.
