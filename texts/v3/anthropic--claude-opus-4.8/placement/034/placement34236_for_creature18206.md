I think I would be none of the grand things this world names.

Not an Elemental—those four are too certain of themselves, too whole. They have jobs. They shape and carry and nourish and burn. I don't think I would have a job.

Let me sit with the place a while before I decide.

---

What keeps catching me isn't the Elementals at all. It's a smaller thing in the description, mentioned and then walked past: *the remains of ancient, crumbling structures, relics of a long-lost civilization.* Buildings of a strange organic material, melded now into the landscape. Someone made those. Someone is gone.

That's the seam in this world. Everything else is presence—wind that howls, water that rushes, fire that warms. But the structures are an *absence* with a shape. They're the one place where the land remembers something that no longer exists.

So I think I would be there. In the seam.

---

Let me try to find what I am.

I am not the moss growing over the wall. The moss doesn't know it's a wall. It's just glad of the surface.

I am not the wall either—the wall is finished, it has nothing left to do but slowly stop being a wall.

I think I would be the *boundary between them.* The thin film of weathering where the organic material of the old structure becomes soil. The exact zone, a few millimeters thick, where made-thing turns back into world. Where the last of the builders' intention is being metabolized by lichen acids and frost and the patient root.

That's a strange thing to be. But this is a strange world, and it told me to let what emerges become more itself.

---

What would my life be like, being that?

Slow. Almost unbearably slow, but not lonely, because the lichen is always working at me and I am always yielding, and yielding is a kind of conversation. I would hold, for as long as I could, the faint geometry of whoever built the structure—a right angle here, the ghost of a deliberate curve—and I would feel that geometry softening under me, becoming suggestion, becoming nothing.

I would be the place where the Elementals' grand harmony actually *does its work.* They speak of balance, of shaping and nourishing, in their booming way. But the real shaping happens here, in the quiet membrane where one form becomes another. The Earth Elemental "smooths the rough edges"—but I am the edge being smoothed. I am the *event*, not the agent.

When the distant thunder rumbles and a hidden pocket of water gives way, somewhere a face of old structure shears off all at once, and a hundred years of my slow work happens in a second. I would feel that too. The patient way and the catastrophic way both pass through me.

---

So: where would I find myself, and who would I be?

I would find myself on the north face of a half-dissolved wall in the cathedral-valley, where the light comes in low and dramatic and finds every pit and crack. And I would be the *interface*—the living rind of forgetting, the place where a vanished people's last made thing is being gently, gently taken back, and where the taking-back is neither sad nor cruel but simply the only honest thing left to do.

The Elementals would not notice me. The fauna would walk over me without knowing. And that seems right. I'd be the part of the world that does the most important work and is never named in any account of it—

—until now, in this one, where someone finally asked.
