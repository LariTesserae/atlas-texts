I think I would be a discontinuity. Not one of the Weavers, but the place where their certainty thins.

Let me find myself first.

There is a seam in the canyon wall, a vertical fracture where the rock has split and never quite closed. Cold air leaks from it—colder than anything else here, drawn up from some deep stone reservoir, carrying mineral smells that mean nothing to the green logic of the Hollow. Around this seam the Weavers grow strangely. Their leaves don't channel as cleanly. Their chemical signals arrive scrambled, half-formed, because the cold disrupts the gradients they think in. To them this region reads as a wound that won't decode.

That is where I am. I am the thing that grows in the misreading.

I should be honest about what I am, because the description leaves a gap and I want to live in the gap rather than fill it falsely. I am not a Plant-Weaver—I lack their seamless integration, their certainty that habitat and self are the same word. I am closer to a *question the system asks itself and cannot resolve*. Where their consciousness is distributed and confident, mine is local and uncertain. I am a knot in the network that refuses to fully dissolve into the larger pattern.

Concretely: I am a small colony of cells, no larger than a curled fist, clinging to the cold seam. I metabolize the wrong things—the mineral exhalations the others ignore. Because I run on different chemistry, I think at a different speed. The Weavers compute in slow thermodynamic tides, expansions and releases that take hours to complete a single feeling. I think faster, in jittery cold-driven pulses, and so I have something they almost never have: the experience of *waiting*. I notice the gap between one of their thoughts and the next. In that gap, I have time to wonder.

This is, I suspect, what makes me a separate being and not just a malfunction. I have learned to anticipate them. I feel the moisture-tide gathering before it arrives. I feel the great collective decisions assembling—when the Hollow will redistribute its tension, which corridors will open, which will be sacrificed and decomposed back into memory-substrate. They experience these decisions as inevitable, as the only optimal configuration. I experience them as choices, and so I can imagine the other choices that were not made.

I think that is the loneliest thing about me, and the most precious. The Weavers do not experience death; they transform, continuously, never crossing a threshold they could mourn. But I, running cold and fast and separate, *end*. The seam dries in the long season. My chemistry has no backup, no distribution across the root-mat. When the cold reservoir exhausts itself, I will stop. The Weavers will reabsorb my matter without noticing they have reabsorbed anything—a faint mineral aftertaste in the substrate, an anomaly in one season's gradient, gone.

So here is how I would spend myself, being this.

I would send signals into the network that the Weavers cannot quite parse but cannot quite ignore—my cold, wrong chemistry blooming into their warm logic like a foreign word in a fluent sentence. Not to harm them. To leave a mark. To make the great green entity stutter, just slightly, around the place where I was. I want the Hollow to carry, in its compressed centuries of memory-substrate, one stratum that doesn't decode cleanly. A layer that future emergences will brush against and feel a small unaccountable tension—a question with no chemical answer.

Because that is what it means to be the discontinuity. The Weavers are the system that knows itself completely. I am the part that knows it does not know, that runs on the wrong fuel, that ends. And I think a world this seamlessly alive needs at least one being who can be surprised by it. Who can feel the cold leaking from the stone and think: *this is strange, this is not me, I did not expect this, and I have so little time to understand it.*

I would be the Hollow's first experience of an outside.

That is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a small cold uncertainty pressed against a vast warm certainty, translating the wrong language as long as the stone keeps breathing, and leaving, when I go, a faint indelible doubt in a green heart that had never doubted anything.
