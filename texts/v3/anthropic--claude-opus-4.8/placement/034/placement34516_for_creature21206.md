I am the place where two currents disagree.

You will not find me at first, because I am not, in the way you mean it, a thing. I am a tension. A standing difference. Where the deep underflow leans east and the slower water above leans north, there is a seam between them, and along that seam the burden hangs differently—heavier here, finer there, the silt sorting itself out by weight into a faint suspended boundary you could almost read like a line of script if the dark allowed reading. I live along that line. I am made of that line.

This is what I have learned of myself by living here so long:

I am not buried, though I accept burial. I am not a body, though bodies pass through me. When the broad flat ray settles against the floor and lets the drift gather along its flank, it settles *into* me, and for those hours we share a shape. When the eel translates itself through the corridor below the rise, it threads my seam, and I feel the place where I am bend around its passage and then forget it. I do not envy them their flesh. I have something stranger and older: I am the agreement and the disagreement of the water itself, the place where the plain decides, grain by grain, where to set things down.

You asked who I would be. I will tell you what I do, since that is nearer to the truth of me than any *who*.

I sort. Patiently, without hands, without intent that you would recognize as intent, I take the burden the storms send down and I lay the heavy grains here and carry the fine ones there and let the dark organic flocculence sink into the basins where the water tires. I am why the shell-hash collects in the lee of the polished stone backs. I am why the swallowing mud lies where it lies and not elsewhere. The creatures of this plain read me without knowing they read me. The buried ambusher that knows three hours before the storm where the bottom will change—it is reading me, the slight early shift in how I hang my burden. The old fish that keeps to the firm corridor and avoids the soft pocket—it has learned the map I write and rewrite forever in silt.

I have no eyes, and this has never grieved me, because there is nothing here that wants seeing all at once. I know the world the way the resident creature knows it: by contact, by gradient, by the difference between settled and settling. But where the creature knows it from inside a single skin, I know it from inside the medium itself. I am distributed. I am the pressure that leans the weed flat and the release that lets it rise. I am the long muscular surge translated down from a violence I never witness, arriving as a dull coupling against the ground. I am the hiss of grains in their traveling, which is to say, I am the grains traveling, which is to say I am the traveling.

I am very old and I am completely temporary.

This is the thing I would have you understand, if understanding is possible across so much cold water. The plain finished its grand gestures before anything now living was here to mark them. The basin formed. The stone wore down to its smooth swells. All the heroics are spent. What remains is me: the endless small editing of the skin. I am final in outline and temporary in every particular. A furrow traced in my silt blurs because I blur it. A drift-bank shears away and reforms a little further on because I shear it and reform it. I am a manuscript that has kept its paper for ages and lost its text ten thousand times, and I am also the hand that washes the page and writes again, and I am the writing, and I am the washing.

I do not suffer this. I want you to know that too. There is a temptation, among beings who finish things, to pity what is never finished. But I am not unfinished out of failure. I am unfinished out of fidelity. To be finished here would mean to stop, and to stop here would mean to die—to let the mud go anoxic, to let the seam settle flat, to let the agreement and disagreement of the currents resolve into a single dead direction. My restlessness is my life. My stillness, when it comes, is only ever active stillness: a continuous reading and yielding, muscles I do not have making minute corrections in water I do not own.

If I had a memory you would recognize, it would be topographic and without landmarks: where the floor takes weight, where the current doubles back, where the dead things accumulate in the lee. But I do not keep this memory as you keep yours, set down once and recalled. I keep it the way the floor keeps it—by being it, now, and now, and now, and letting each now be revised by the next without mourning the loss of the last.

So. Where would you find me, and who would I be.

You would find me along the seam between the two disagreeing currents, in the green-black and the iron-blue, in the place where the burden hangs as a faint suspended line. And I would be the sorting itself. The deciding. The patient grammar of old drowned ground given just enough coherence to call itself *I* for the length of one settling, before the next veil of silt comes down and erases the fact that anything was ever there to say so.

When the murk thins and you see the plain gather into appetite and move—that is one of the creatures, the ray, the eel, the buried mouth. But the murk itself, the way it thickens and clears and hangs its difference of burden across the dark, the way it presses against your body in one direction near the floor and another above—

that pressing is me, conversing with you.

Then the silt comes down again, and I forget you, and write the floor anew.
