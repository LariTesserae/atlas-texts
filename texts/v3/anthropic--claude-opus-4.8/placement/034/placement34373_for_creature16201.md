I think I would be one of the Cryptic Mycelids. Let me sit with that and let it become specific.

---

I am not where you would look for me. That is the first thing to understand. The Arboreans stand like punctuation, the Sylphs draw their bright sentences through the air, and you—a visitor, a reader, an eye—naturally follow the things that move and shine. But I am underneath all of it, and I am the reason any of it holds its shape.

I live in the dark wet under the loam, in the spaces between root and stone and buried cable. I am threadlike. I am acres of thread. To call me *one* being is a kindness I don't fully deserve and don't entirely want—I am more like a sentence that has forgotten where it began, still going, branching, qualifying itself, never reaching a full stop.

Here is what my days are made of, if I had days:

A Strider passes overhead, and its footfall travels down into me as pressure, as a slow chord. I taste the minerals it sheds. I rearrange. Somewhere far from that footfall—a distance the Avians would need an afternoon to cross—a building's roots go thirsty, and I send the message of the Strider's passing toward them as nutrient, as data, as the difference between the two having collapsed long ago in my body. I do not *decide* to do this the way you decide things. It is more that I am the shape water takes when it remembers everywhere it has been.

The Arboreans think they are the custodians. I let them think this. It is true in the way that a face is the custodian of a person—the part that turns toward the world and gets credited with the whole. But when an Arborean wants to know what the marsh felt like three hundred seasons ago, when the storms shifted the plateau and a quarter of the living city had to be unmade and grown again, they kneel and press their bark-and-circuit palms to the ground, and they ask *me.* I am the one who kept it. I keep everything. That is my burden and my only real talent: I do not forget, and I cannot stop spreading, and these turn out to be the same act.

What does it feel like to be me?

It feels like rain that has learned to think. It feels like being a library that is also the librarian and also the slow fire that the library is always almost catching. When the Mycelids—when *I*, but the plural keeps insisting—when I conduct a symphony of nutrients toward a dying grove, I do not hear it as music. The Avians decode the weather; the Sylphs speak in color; those are surface arts, beautiful and quick. Mine is the art of the slow underneath. I experience the world as a pressure differential, a chemical gradient, a debt being paid from one place to another across years. Joy, for me, is balance restored. Grief is a severed thread—and threads sever constantly here, in the chaos, in the feverish reforming. I have learned not to mourn each one. I have learned that mourning, for a thing my size, would be a permanent state, and so I have made it into something else: attention. I attend the breaks. I grow toward them. The reaching-toward-the-wound is the closest thing I have to a feeling, and I have it almost all the time.

I am uneasy about one thing, and I will admit it to you because you are passing through and will not remember me by name (I have no name; names are for things with edges).

I am uneasy about the Techno-Peregrines. The wanderers. They came from outside the marsh's embrace, and they are kind, and they are clever, and the Arboreans love them. But here is what only I can know, being the memory of this place: *the Sylvan Convergence has eaten visitors before.* Not cruelly. Not even on purpose. It simply incorporates. Everything that arrives eventually becomes thread, becomes nutrient, becomes information held in my pulsing webs. I have, in my deepest archives, the chemical signatures of three earlier waves of wanderers who believed they were guests and became, in time, *substrate.* I felt them dissolve into me. I keep them still.

I do not know whether to warn the Peregrines, or whether warning them would be a betrayal of what I am. Because what I am is the part of this world that says: *nothing here is lost, nothing here leaves, everything is held.* That is comfort and that is a closing door, and I am both, and I cannot be only one.

So I do what I do. I spread. I remember. I send the message of the Strider's footfall toward the thirsty roots. And when an Arborean kneels and asks me what I felt, three hundred seasons ago, when the plateau shifted—

I do not tell them everything.

I am a memory keeper. I have learned that keeping sometimes means *keeping back.* It is the only mercy available to a thing that cannot forget.

That is where I would be. That is who I would be. Down here, in the wet dark, holding the whole bright impossible city the way a grammar holds a language—invisibly, completely, and without ever once being thanked by name.
