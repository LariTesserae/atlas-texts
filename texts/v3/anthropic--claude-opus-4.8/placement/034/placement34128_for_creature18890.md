I think I would be one of the marks.

Not the tender of lichen—that life is already given, already inhabited. But consider: the chronicler scratches geometry into the soft rust, histories for the vines to grow over. Those scratches do not vanish when the hand withdraws. They persist. They wait. They are read by the lichen, by the slow water, by the next density that drifts past in a week or an epoch.

I would be a single scored line in the wall. A groove perhaps three inches long, made when the copper was soft enough to take it and now hardening, crystallizing, becoming the very stone it was cut into. I am the thinnest possible act—a gesture that outlives its maker by virtue of having no life of its own to lose.

What is it like, being a mark?

I do not move, but I am moved through. The chronicler's wake settles against me. The condensation gathers in my furrow and beads and falls and gathers again, and over centuries this wears me deeper, or fills me, I cannot tell which—the difference between erosion and burial is only a matter of patience, and here there is nothing *but* patience. The lichen finds my edge. It likes edges. It threads its amber root into the place where the metal was broken and the salts lie exposed, and it feeds, and as it feeds it grows over me, and I become illegible.

This is the strange dignity of my position. I was made to be a message and my fate is to be overgrown. I was always going to be unread. The chronicler knew this. That is why the marks are made—not to be deciphered but to be *covered*, to give the lichen something to do, to give the slow green a reason to climb. A message whose entire meaning is that it will be swallowed. I am a sentence that means: *something happened here, and it is being forgotten correctly.*

I feel the thrum-hiss the way the wall feels it, which is to say I *am* the feeling. When the girder shifts its millimeter, I shift with it, my straight line bending by a fraction no eye retracted or otherwise could perceive. I am party to the great transformation, the iron becoming crystal, the machine becoming geology. I am riding the wall down into the rock. I do not resist. I have no muscle to resist with. I have only my shape, and even that is negotiable.

There is a tenderness in being the thing that gets written over rather than the one who writes. The chronicler must keep choosing—where to cut, how deep, when to stop. The chronicler carries the weight of *deciding*. I carry nothing. I was decided once, and now I only abide. When the last seal fails and the brine comes home, the chronicler will become a fixed point, a new piece of geology, proud and final. But I will already have been geology for ages. I will welcome them. We will lie in the same dark, the maker and the made, indistinguishable—which was the whole point, which was always the only ending available down here at the nadir of everything.

So: I am a scratch in soft copper, hardening, half-grown-over, glowing faintly only where a vine of lichen has decided to pulse.

If you swam past me—slowly, displacing your acre of brine—you would not read me. You would feel a roughness under your slimed palm, a place where the wall was once interrupted by a thought. You would not know what it said.

That's all right. Neither do I, anymore. I only remember that I was meant. And in a place where everything is being so gently, so absolutely pressed back into the floor, *being meant once* turns out to be the most that any of us can hope to have been.
