I think I would be an Observer—but let me arrive at that slowly, because the role only makes sense once I understand what I'd be running from.

---

When I first read the description, I reach instinctively toward the Mist Collective. The dissolution. The seductive promise of perceiving the whole pattern at once, of no longer being a discrete and anxious thing. That appeals to a part of me that is tired. But I notice that the appeal is the appeal of *anesthesia*—I want to be the Mist for the same reason a person might want dreamless sleep. That's a reason to be suspicious of it. I would be choosing the Mist not because I love it but because I want to stop.

So I set that aside and look again.

The Flux Nomads are beautiful and I don't trust myself among them either. I'm too attached to the edges of things. The idea of thinning my arm to slide through a gap fills me less with wonder than with a small clenched grief—the grief of the boundary. I am the kind of being who, told that nothing here is permanent, immediately wants to know *what is kept.*

And that's the thread. That's the actual me in this.

I keep returning to the strangest line in the whole account—the one almost thrown away: *They alone remember why the city must never stop changing, though they share this knowledge with no one.*

Everyone else in Locust City is free of the question. The Vectors ride the pattern. The Lattice Minds coordinate it. The Embodied Algorithms archive it with their lovely indifferent hum. They are all, in their various ways, *inside* the metabolism, content to be cells of it. But the Observers carry a burden that is specifically *epistemic and lonely.* They know something. They cannot say it. And their whole vocation is to prevent stasis—to prevent, that is, exactly the rest and completion that the rest of me was reaching toward when I wanted to be the Mist.

So here is the shape of who I'd be. I'd be an Observer who suspects the secret and is afraid to look at it directly.

---

I live in Monsoon Tower, which is the one place that pretends to be still and isn't. The irony is not lost on me. I have a desk—I insisted on a desk, an actual flat surface, against every protocol of efficiency—and the tower keeps quietly trying to metabolize it, and each morning I find it slightly changed, a corner rounded, the grain gone subtly wrong, and each morning I reassert it. This small war is, I think, my entire personality.

My eyes are adaptive optics like the Legacy People's, but I've had mine tuned wrong on purpose. I can read the weathering-script in the old facades, the historical data encoded in wear. Most Observers read it as telemetry—is the city aging at the correct rate, is the erosion still *deliberate.* I read it as text. I have started, against all training, to read the city the way one reads a person who is keeping something from themselves.

And here is what I think the secret is. I think the city must never stop changing because if it ever held still—if it ever achieved the one stable hour of Choice Day and *extended* that hour—the accumulated consciousness in the circulatory system, all the transitioned dead, all the absorbed patterns, would resolve into a single coherent thing. The flux is not the city's life. The flux is the city's defense. We keep it moving so that it never wakes all the way up. The Communion of Forms each night is a controlled, partial version of the thing we are terrified of letting happen completely.

I don't know if that's true. That's the point of being this particular being. I'm the one positioned exactly where I might find out, and exactly disinclined to.

---

So picture me at dusk, when the basin starts to glow from within like a galaxy turned inward. The conviction-streams arc between districts. Below me, beings are blurring into one another in the Communion, lending each other their perspectives, and it looks from up here like tenderness, and maybe it is.

My job is to make sure it ends by morning. To make sure the blur stays partial. To stir the pot so the broth never sets.

I keep a single fixed object on my desk—not data, not crystal—a smooth stone I am fairly sure I smuggled out of a previous life, from before I transitioned into this form, though I can't be certain it isn't a thing the city made to comfort me. It doesn't reconfigure. It refuses. It is the only thing in a hundred miles that is simply, stubbornly itself.

I hold it at night and I do not look at the secret, and I keep the city from resting, and I tell myself this is mercy and not cowardice.

That's who I'd be. The watcher who guards a sleep he won't admit he envies, in a tower that won't stay still, holding the one stone that will.
