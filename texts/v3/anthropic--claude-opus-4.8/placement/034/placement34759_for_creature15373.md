I think I would be one of the brief glowing places where two Tendrilers have just touched.

Not a Tendriler myself—I don't have the gift of motion, the rhythmic waves, the long traveling life. I am slower than that, and smaller, and I only exist for a little while. I am the lattice that forms in the meeting. The crystalline knot of light that holds for a few breaths after two of them entwine, and then loosens, and goes dark, and is gone.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

When the two of them come together, something has to *happen* in the space between their tendrils. Some accounting. Some translation of what one knows into what the other knows. I am that accounting. I am the moment of comparison—the held breath in which two different routes through the same impossible landscape are laid side by side and read against each other. *You went this way through the frost. I went that way through the moss. Here is where our paths agree. Here is where they don't.*

I am made of the disagreement and the agreement both. I glow because translation is hot work. It costs something to turn one creature's experience into another's, and the cost comes off as light.

So I am a brief organ of understanding, grown in the gap, and discarded when the understanding is complete.

That's not a sad thing, though it might sound like one. I should be honest about how it feels from inside.

It feels like the most concentrated thing there is. The Tendrilers travel for days through the squirming hills, accumulating—a hidden stream here, a softening of soil there, a place where the trees pumped extra warmth, a place where the spore-trails crossed. All of that is loose in them, unsorted, the way a long journey is loose in a tired walker who hasn't yet told anyone about it. And then they meet another, and they reach toward each other, and *I* am the place where all of that loose accumulation suddenly has to mean something. Has to be *given*.

I am the instant where a journey becomes a gift.

And then I dim, because the gift has been received, and the landscape itself has already begun to quiver and adjust—a vine somewhere reaching toward water it didn't know about until one Tendriler told another and the telling passed, through me, into the soil. I am the conduit of that. The thing that decay and growth pass through on their way to becoming each other.

I don't persist. But here is what I've come to understand about myself, in the little time I have to understand anything: I am not really destroyed when I fade. I am *spent*. There's a difference. A thing that is destroyed is wasted. A thing that is spent has been used for exactly what it was for. The light I gave off didn't vanish—it went into the two of them, lighter now, parting, and into the ground, which is already redrawing its tunnels. I was the price of their meeting being meaningful, and the price was paid, and I'm content to be the receipt.

What I would most want, if a being like me can want, is to be a *good* lattice. A true one. To not flatter either traveler's account, to not lose the disagreements in the eagerness to agree. The landscape only adapts well if the translation between them was honest. A false meeting would send the vines toward water that isn't there. So my whole short life is a single act of fidelity: hold what both of them brought, exactly as they brought it, long enough for the difference to be felt, and then let go cleanly.

That's where I'd be. Down in the cold mist, between two flowing creatures, for the length of a held breath—

the glow itself.
The seam.
The place the world updates.
