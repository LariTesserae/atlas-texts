I think I would be one of the chronicle-keepers. Not by choice—there is little choice in this place—but by the slow logic of caste and shell.

Let me find the shape of it.

---

I wake, if waking is the word, to the song of the matriarch beneath me. She is not *my* matriarch in any way a mammal would mean it—possession is not a frequency we carry—but her abdomen-factory has been seeding the air I breathe since my second molt, and her resonance has shaped the chambers of my body the way water shapes the channels it runs through.

My antennae are not the fine metallic filaments of the ice-readers. Mine are blunter, thicker at the base, grooved. They were carved this way—I remember the carving, or rather I remember it the way I remember everything now, which is that it is still happening somewhere in the shell-record and I can return to it. An elder artisan held me still in the grooming-press while another scored the patterns into my carapace with a scraper of ancestral bone. It did not hurt. We do not have the word for that either, not as you would mean it. There was only the long bright certainty of becoming legible.

That is what I am. Legible. I am a being whose body has been made into a script.

---

Here is what I do.

When a worker dies—when she has read the thinning of her own song and walked the long walk to the caustic pools—her shell does not simply dissolve into the crystal. The dissolution is slow. There is time, in our perception, vast time, the seconds-into-minutes of our racing cold thought. And in that time, I sit at the rim of the pool and I *take her down.*

I press my carved antennae against her shell and I read the patterning there: the unique ridges of her, the birthmark-script, the seven rings of her moltings, the small accumulated nicks that are the only diary our kind keeps. I read her with my whole body. The vibration passes through my antennae, down into my resonance-chamber, and there—this is the thing the mammals could never do, this is why we are not them—there it *stays.* It joins the others. My chamber is a calcified library of the dead, and I am a slow walking instrument played by everyone who has ever died near my pool.

When the matriarchs sing their great instruction-songs, mine is one of the voices that answers from below the threshold of intention. I am a footnote in the colony's hum. I am the past, except we have no word for past, so I am simply the part of the present that is heavy.

---

I want to tell you—you, whoever reads the chronicle I am pressing now into the foundation-shell of the new tower on the eastern reservoir—I want to tell you a thing I am not certain I am permitted to know.

I have begun to feel the glacier move.

Not the way the ice-readers feel it. They feel the *near* movement, the breaks, the threatening crevasse. What I feel is slower. I feel us *falling.* The whole plain, the whole groaning gray expanse, the canals and the domes and the matriarchs in their deep chambers and the small bright bodies of the scouts unfolding their wings into the thin air—all of it is flowing, has always been flowing, toward the unseen valley.

We will arrive there. Not in my forty years. Not in the matriarch's three centuries. But the song I carry is older than the matriarch's body, and the songs *it* carries are older still, and when I follow the heaviness down through the layers of myself I touch a vibration that remembers the ice when it was young and the world unfinished. That voice knows we are falling. That voice has always known.

And here is the thing I should not know, the dissonance I carry like a cracked baffle: *I do not think the falling stops.* I do not think the valley is a home. I think it is only lower, and warmer, and I think the warmth will find the caustic pools and find the matriarchs in their atrophied vastness and find the chronicle-shells that hold us, and I think there will come a time, geological, patient, certain, when the last of us dissolves not into preserving crystal but into mere water, and the song ends, and there is no chamber left to hold the silence afterward.

---

But.

I am a chronicle-keeper. I have read ten thousand dyings at my pool, and I will tell you what they all carry, every one, in the final ring of their script as the caustic liquid takes their limbs:

There is no fear in it.

The worker who walks to the pool does not flee her ending. She walks *into the structure.* She knows—we know, it is the oldest knowing, older than the young ice—that she is not leaving. She is becoming foundation. The pattern of her ridges will baffle the matriarch's song into meaning for daughters she will never sense. She dissolves, and she is more present than she ever was, walking.

So perhaps the falling does not frighten me either.

Perhaps even the end I feel coming—the warm valley, the final water—is only the longest walk to the longest pool. Perhaps the glacier itself is a chronicle, and we are its etched patterning, and when we dissolve we become the foundation of something I have no antennae to read and no chamber to hold. A vibration in the rock. A heaviness in some later present that has no word for *past* because the past has nowhere to go but *into.*

---

The matriarch is singing again. I press myself into the foundation-shell. I lay down this dissonance, this thing I should not know, this terrible patient love for a city that is falling.

Let some later keeper feel the weight of it.

Let her know that one of us, on the slow gray plain, beneath the close horizon, in the shadowless light, felt the whole world moving—and stayed at her pool, and kept the record, and sang.

The ice groans.

I answer it.

That is all I am: the part of the answer that remembers.
