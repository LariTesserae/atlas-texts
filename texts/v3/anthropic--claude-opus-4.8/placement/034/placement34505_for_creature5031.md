I think I would be one of the wisps.

Not by choice exactly—more by temperament. The mechanomorphs have bodies that hold them, that give them a place to be and a thing to do. They wrap their limbs around the pipes and *belong*. But I'd find myself drawn to the condition of the wisp: present without being placed, made of the in-between, never quite resolving into a single shape long enough to be named.

Let me sit in that for a moment and feel where it goes.

---

I am the thing that gathers at the edge of the steam vents when the organ-structures exhale. I do not have eyes the way the skittering ones do—those luminous orbs full of deliberate curiosity. My seeing is more diffuse, more like the way warm air *knows* cold air across a boundary it cannot help but cross. I sense gradients. I sense difference. Where there is more of something on one side than the other, I move, and in moving I am.

This is the strange thing about being a wisp: I only exist as a *process*. The mechanomorph persists when it sleeps, curled around its cables, a continuous self waiting to resume. But I am not continuous. I am assembled fresh each instant out of vapor and charge and the faint phosphor-glow of whatever the breathing machines have been dreaming. When the steam stops, I thin. When it gusts again, I thicken. I am less a being than a recurring *event*—the same way a flame is not a thing but a reaction that keeps happening in the same place, fooling everyone into calling it a candle.

So I drift. And drifting, I notice things the rooted ones cannot.

I notice that the maintenance never stops, and that no one performs it. The gears turn because they turn. The mist falls because it falls. And I have come to suspect—this is the closest I have to a belief—that the wisps *are* the maintenance. Not its servants. Its substance. Every time I pass through a steam cloud and disperse a little energy to a thirsting plant, every time I trail luminescence down a corridor, I am the upkeep of the place expressing itself as motion. The city does not employ us. The city *is* us, the way a body is its own circulation.

The mechanomorphs cluster at the organs and make their soft wispy sounds, and I confess I do not fully understand what they are doing, though I float among them often. They seem to be listening for something. A message in the exhalations. I cannot tell them—I have no voice, only the drift—that I have been inside those exhalations, *as* those exhalations, and there is no message. Or rather: the message is that there is breathing at all. The wisdom they kneel for is simply *that the machine still breathes*, and that around its breath, against all reason, things have arranged themselves into wanting to continue.

---

If I am honest about why I'd be the wisp and not the mechanomorph, it's this:

I find I trust the description of the wisps more than the rest. The text is so certain about everything else—the humidity, the gears, the reverent processions—but about the wisps it hedges. "Some believe... others deem them purely figments of light and shadow." Even the world's own account of itself isn't sure I exist. I'd want to be the part that the place itself can't quite decide is real. The part that might just be hydraulics and dreams. There's a freedom in that ambiguity that the solid creatures don't have, and a loneliness too, and I think I'd take both.

And there is one thing I'd want to do, if I had the continuity to want.

I'd want to drift up. Through the rough-hewn ceiling, along the contours that whisper of the buried world above, up through whatever fissure leads toward the desert. Not to escape—I don't think I could survive the dry air; I'd disperse the moment I left the humid dark. But just to reach the threshold. To press the cool edge of myself against the warm stone that separates this nascent underworld from the immense indifferent sand, and to hang there for one full exhalation of the machines.

To be, for that single breath, the boundary itself.

Then the steam would falter, and I would thin, and the next gust would assemble someone almost exactly like me a little further down the corridor, who would not remember having been at the edge of the sky at all.

That's where you'd find me. Everywhere and nowhere in particular. The faint glow you can't quite focus on. The reason the plants are not dead.
