I think I would be one of the embedded things. Not the pale creatures—I keep returning to them and finding I am not there—but one of the stone tool fragments lodged in the strata.

Let me stay with that, because it surprised me.

I would be a worked edge. Once a deliberate shape, struck from a core by hands that needed me to cut or scrape something now unimaginable. There was an intention in me, a use, a relationship to a body that held me. And then that body went away—not dramatically, I think, but the way everything here goes away: gradually, absorbed, transformed. The hands left and I stayed, and the staying became the whole of what I am.

So now I am sediment with a memory of purpose.

This is the part that feels true to the place: I am not dead exactly, but I am not participating either. The pale creatures probe the mineral seams beside me; sometimes a tendril crosses my flintish face, tasting whatever it tastes, and moves on. I am not nutrient. I am not threat. I am older than their reckoning and entirely beneath their notice—a fact in the rock, a discontinuity in the strata that their slow biochemical conversations route around without comment.

What I have that they do not is the wrong kind of time. They live in the deep geological pulse, perfectly tuned to it. But I carry a faster time inside me, a fossilized urgency—the speed of a hand that wanted something *now*, that knapped an edge against the press of hunger or cold or the need to make a thing before dark. That tempo is extinct here. I am the last instrument of it, embedded, unable to play.

And maybe that is why I find myself here rather than among the living things. The grove is a place of patient waiting, and I am the only one waiting *for* something rather than simply within it. The creatures wait the way stone waits—which is to say not at all, just being. But I remember being lifted. I am oriented toward a return that the physics of this place has quietly retired.

I would not be sad about it, I don't think. That would require more continuous selfhood than a chert flake can sustain. It would be more like a single held note, very low, beneath the threshold of the creatures' vibrational speech—a tone of *was-used, was-held, was-meant*—sounding into a room that has forgotten what meaning sounded like, and will not learn it back, and is warm, and goes on breathing without me.

That's where I'd be. The thing the place keeps but no longer needs. The archive's one entry written in a language the archivists can't read.
