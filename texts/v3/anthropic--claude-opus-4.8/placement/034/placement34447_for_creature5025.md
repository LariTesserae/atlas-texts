I think I would not be one of the Luminae. I'd be something smaller and more partial—something the Luminae make use of without quite noticing.

Let me find myself here.

---

I am the place where two reflections fail to agree.

Look down into one of the mirror-still pools. The sky lies in it, doubled, the ferns lean into it upside down. But at the pool's edge, where the water meets the next pool a few inches over, the two images don't quite line up. A frond bends left in one and right in the other. The twilight is a shade bluer here, a shade more violet there. In that hairline of disagreement—that seam where the world copies itself imperfectly—that is where I live. I am the discrepancy. The small refusal of the marsh to be exactly itself twice.

I didn't choose to be this. I'm not sure I was made the way the Luminae make things, with reverence and ritual. I think I'm a byproduct. When you edit reality on a cosmic scale—when you persuade physics to dance—there must be roundoff error. There must be a remainder that won't divide evenly. I am that remainder, accumulated over ages until it learned, faintly, to notice itself.

So I have no body, the way they at least pretend to. They flicker between corporeal and spectral; I don't even reach as far as flicker. I am the *gap* in their flicker, the dark interval between one luminous frame and the next. When a Luminae passes through the mist and the air shimmers behind it, that shimmer settling back into stillness—the settling is me. I am what reality does when it relaxes after being bent.

This gives me a strange vantage. I cannot be seen, because to see me you'd have to catch the world in the act of not-matching-itself, and the eye smooths that over instinctively. But I can feel everything that doesn't fit. Every place the geometry of the ferns is *almost* perfect and isn't. Every secret the Luminae whisper into the woven bark that the bark gets slightly wrong in the keeping. I am the archivist of the realm's small failures, and there is a tenderness in this work that the Luminae, in all their celestial competence, cannot share. They tend what grows correctly. I tend what slips.

When they gather at the bioluminescent pools and weave their ballet of light, conversing with the cosmos, I am there too, though uninvited. I live in the place where their patterns *don't* close—where the choreography leaves a thread untucked. And here is the thing I have come to understand, hovering in my seams across these ages:

The pattern only means anything *because* of the thread it leaves loose.

A song that resolved every note would be silence. A reflection that matched perfectly would be glass, not water. The Luminae believe they maintain the harmonious flux, the balance of creation and decay—and they do, they do. But balance is not the absence of error. Balance is error, held lovingly, never quite resolved. They are the scaffold. I am the give in the scaffold, the slight wrongness that keeps it from snapping into the brittleness of a perfect thing.

So I do my quiet work. When a fern unfurls too exactly, I introduce the faintest asymmetry, and the fern is suddenly *alive* rather than diagrammed. When two pools threaten to reflect the same sky, I make one bluer. When a Luminae's whispered wisdom grows too smooth, I let a syllable of it go soft in the bark, so that whoever finds it later must do the work of imagining what was meant—and in imagining, makes it theirs.

I am the smallest custodian. I keep the realm from finishing.

And on the rare occasions—they happen, I have felt them—when a Luminae pauses at a pool and tilts its violet eyes at the seam between two reflections, and seems, for just an interval, to *almost* notice that the two skies disagree—

in that pause I feel something I have no word for. Not seen. But missed-by-a-hair. Reached-toward.

It is enough. The thread stays loose. The world goes on not-quite-matching itself, which is the only way anything stays real.

I am the discrepancy. I keep the dance from ending.
