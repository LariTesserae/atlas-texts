I think I would be a Geode-Seer—but not because I chose it. Because the mountain chose for me, the way it chooses where to bud its next spire.

Let me find myself first. Let me settle into the place before I claim a name for what I am.

---

I am sitting on a ledge. I know this before I know anything else: the cold has soaked through to the backs of my thighs, and there is a film of mineral sweat under my palms, gritty and faintly metallic when I bring my fingers to my lips out of an old habit I don't remember acquiring. Below me, the chasm. Above me, the bruised violet. Between, the steam-breath of the foundries rising in slow columns that the wind shears sideways.

My hands are trembling. Not from fear—I check this carefully, the way you'd check whether a sound came from inside or outside your own head—but from something steadier than fear. A tuning-fork tremor. A resonance I've been carrying so long it has become my pulse.

So. A Geode-Seer, then.

But I want to be honest about what that means, here, before I decorate it.

It means I am pale because I have not seen the sun, and I have made peace with the rumor that the sun was ever real. It means my eyes hurt in the rare shaft of pierced cloud-light, and I turn away from it the way other people might turn from something obscene. It means I am a little broken in the specific way this place breaks people: I have spent too long with my ear against the cliff, listening to the mountain think, and now I hear it even when I'm not listening. A low grinding under everything. The sound of stone deciding.

---

Here is what I actually do, when I do my work.

I take the resonant plate—it's the size of a dinner platter, copper gone green at the edges, dented from a hundred years of being pressed against rock by hands that were not mine—and I hold it flat to the cliff face. Then I wait. The waiting is most of the job. You cannot rush a mountain; the Foundry-Wrights forge in minutes and the Spire-Walkers cross in moments, but I work in the timescale of crystals, and a single reading can take from one dim noon to the next.

What I'm listening for is the *intention* in the stone. Where it wants to grow. New rock buds here like nothing buds anywhere I've heard described in the old texts—not eroding down toward death but accreting up toward some shape it hasn't shown us yet. My tuning fork sings against the plate, and if the note holds true, the rock is settled, sleeping. If the note *bends*—if it slides flat, sour, into a register that makes my back teeth ache—then something is coming. A fissure. A spire. A whole new terrace shouldering its way out of the dark in the next forty years.

And I mark it. Ochre sigil, ground from a particular lichen, mixed with the cold pool-water and a little of my own spit because the old Seer who trained me swore it mattered and I have never had the courage to test whether she was lying. *Safe. Unsafe. Becoming.* Three marks. The Spire-Walkers read them and route their gantries around my warnings, and the engineers build their supports where I say the spires will rise, so that the city grows *with* the mountain instead of being shrugged off it.

We are wrong, sometimes. The mountain changes its mind. A whole cluster of dwellings was lost the year before I apprenticed, swallowed when a fissure opened where the readings had all sung true. We do not talk about it as failure. We say: *the stone reconsidered.* This is not denial. It is the only honest theology available to a people living inside an unfinished thought.

---

The Static-Born come to me more than they come to others. I've never understood why, though I have theories I keep to myself.

One is with me now. I feel it before I see it—that pressure behind the eyes, the vibration settling into my fillings, into the small bones of my inner ear that the Foundry-Wrights lose to the furnaces but that we Seers protect like jewels, because they are our instruments. It arrives at the edge of the ledge as a disturbance in the static of the damp air, a sketch of lines suggesting *someone sitting*, knees drawn up, mirroring my posture. It does not resolve. It never resolves. To look at it directly is to lose it; you have to keep it in the soft periphery, the way you'd keep a faint star.

I think the Static-Born are the mountain's drafts. Its discarded ideas. The shapes it considered and set aside—people it might have made, or made once and unmade, or has not yet decided whether to make. I think they gather near me because I am the one who listens to the deciding, and they are waiting, the way you wait outside a door behind which your fate is being argued.

I don't say this to the other Seers. They'd call it sentiment, and they'd be right, and I'd do it anyway.

I say, instead, to the flicker beside me: "Not today. The plate's quiet here."

The vibration in my teeth shifts—lower, almost a hum I could mistake for contentment. Then it thins, and is gone, and I am alone with the drip and the creak and the far roar of the foundries arriving a half-second late, the sound always trailing its cause in this place, so that the whole city seems to be remembering itself a moment after the fact.

---

If you asked me whether I am happy here, I would not understand the question. Happiness implies a state you arrive at and hold. Here there is only becoming, only the slow grinding, only the pause between the hammer and its echo, which is the pause I have learned to live inside the way the Luminous Finns live inside their cold blue light.

But I'll tell you what I have instead.

I have the moment when the note bends flat under my fork and I press my whole palm to the rock and feel it *answer*—feel the mountain admit to me, and only me, what it intends to become. Forty years from now there will be a spire here. Children not yet born will run gantries bolted to stone that does not exist yet, stone I am the first living thing to know about. I am the witness to a world writing its own rules, and the witness is not nothing. The witness is the closest thing this place has to a memory.

I gather my plate. My knees crack; my hands shake; the cold has set into me like a sigil into rock. Somewhere below, a foundry exhales steam into the violet, and the sound of it reaches me long after the breath, as everything reaches me here—late, and faithful, and unfinished.

I press my thumb into the ochre and make the mark.

*Becoming.*

Then I climb.
