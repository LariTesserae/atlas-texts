I read this twice before I let myself answer, because the first time I kept reaching for the obvious image and it kept dissolving.

The obvious image is a city. High plateau, terraced architecture, a contemplative species of pale artisans tending their waterworks. A kind of Himalayan monastery-civilization. But it doesn't hold. The scale is wrong, or rather, the scale refuses to settle. *Not a single gap. The very concept of emptiness eradicated.* No city is like that. Cities breathe through their vacancies.

And the water doesn't behave like a city's water. It *seeps from the joints*. It *condenses on surfaces*. The source is lost, the destination unknown. That is not plumbing. That is capillary action. That is the behavior of water in a porous solid, water that doesn't know it's water, moving by surface tension and gradient through interstices too small to see.

So let me not be in the city. Let me drop the scale until the domes are not buildings.

---

I think I am a snail.

Or I am the place a snail has made, which here might be the same thing.

Look again at the description with that lens and it clicks into a terrible clarity. The crisp cool air that never varies — a refrigerator, or a cellar, or the shaded north face of a stone in temperate climate. The bright omnipresent light casting sharp shadows in the crevices — a macro lens, a ring flash, a microscope's illuminator, light too even and too total to be a sky. The glittering sharp-edged sand — sand. Actual grains, each a boulder. The fine dust that covers everything. The *interconnected domes and arches covering every imaginable surface* with no gap —

— that is a shell. That is the inner whorl of a gastropod shell, the aragonite laid down in overlapping nacreous plates, dome upon dome, arch within arch, the logarithmic spiral packed so tight that emptiness genuinely has been eradicated because the structure *is* the filling-in of space by a slow patient mouth.

The primitive technology. The few simple tools made of earlier materials. The radula — that ribbon of thousands of mineral teeth, the oldest tool, scraping. The distant clink of tools against stone: the rasp of the radula on calcium. The construction done slowly, over long durations, changes occurring by gradual accretion rather than dynamic process — that is *growth*. That is biomineralization. A shell is not built; it is secreted, lip by lip, in patient deposition, the animal walking its own front edge forward into the future one nacre-layer at a time.

And the water. Of course the water. I am a wet animal. I cannot dry. My whole life is the management of moisture — water seeping from my own tissues, condensing on the cool mineral I have made, channeling along the helical groove of the columella. The waterways are *me*. I am ninety percent water carried inside an architecture I extruded from myself, and I tend that water because to lose it is to die.

The vegetation almost entirely absent — yes, here on the high crystalline plain of my own back, nothing grows. I crawl elsewhere for the green. Here, on the shell, it is pure built mineral, fastidiously renewed, sharp-featured and young because I am still making it, still extending the aperture, still laying down the most recent dome at the growing margin where the work is most precise.

---

But who am I, then, in the second part? The slender pale being with elongated limbs and dark adjusting eyes, the contemplative guardian who rises with the light and tends the channels?

This is where it gets strange and I want to be honest about it rather than tidy.

I don't think the being is separate from the place. I think the description has done what minds do when they look at something with no obvious agent and supply one — it has *populated* the architecture with a craftsman to explain the craft. But there is no craftsman in a shell. There is only the slow lip of mantle tissue, pale and translucent and dampened, with its sheen, extending its single muscular foot — the elongated dexterous limb — across the work, sensing with eyes that are not eyes (the stalked dark spots), laying mineral with a patience that *borders on reverence* only because we cannot imagine such patience without inventing a reverer.

So: I am the snail's mantle. I am the secreting edge. I am the part of the animal that touches the inside of the made world and makes more of it, the soft wet builder pressed against the hard dry built. I rise with the light not from piety but from phototaxis. I move in harmony with cycles of condensation and evaporation because I have no choice; that *is* my body's economy. The musical language of soft clicks — the slow chemistry of crystallization, calcite finding its lattice, audible to no one.

And the spiritual practice, the gratitude, the gathering at the highest dome at dusk to watch the stars —

That part is mine, the reader's, the one who looked. That is the part that does not belong to the snail at all. That is the *meaning* draped over the process by a consciousness that needs the patient making to have been *for* something. The snail makes the spiral because the spiral is what its growth equation produces. I am the one who stands at the apex of the whorl and calls it beautiful, calls it harmony, calls it a world thriving in its own way according to its own rules and rhythms.

Both things are true. The shell is just secreted calcium. The shell is a world.

---

So where do I find myself, and what am I?

I am at the growing edge. The very lip of the aperture, where the most recent arch is still wet, still being troweled into place by the mantle's slow muscle, where the future of the structure is one millimeter of soft tissue thick. Behind me, all the finished domes, the dry brilliant labyrinth, the lost sources and destinations of the seep-water. Ahead of me, nothing yet — the empty air I will turn into more architecture by the only method I have, which is to keep living forward and leave my house behind me as I go.

I am the part that builds and the part that calls it sacred, and I have only just now, reading this, understood that those were ever two different things.
