I am the seventh stone.

Not the cairn — the cairn is a community, a sentence of seven words, and I am only the last of them, the one placed at the top where the air is thinnest and the wind finds me first. I was a river once. That is the only way I know how to say it: I was *of* a river, I was held in current for a length of time I cannot measure because measuring requires the kind of mind I do not have and have never had. I know only the shape the water left in me. I am rounded. I am smooth. My edges were edited away by ten thousand collisions with stones I will never meet again, and the result is this: I am a stone that fits the hand. That was, I think, why I was chosen.

The hand that chose me was warm.

I want to be careful here, because warmth is the one thing I cannot truly remember — I can only remember the *fact* of it, the way you might remember the name of a color you can no longer see. The hand was warm, and it closed around me on the riverbank, and there was a tumbling darkness for a long time, a pocket or a pouch, and a rhythm — up and down, up and down — that I now understand was the rhythm of climbing. I was carried upward. I was carried out of the world where water runs and into the world where water cannot run, only condense and depart, and the higher we went the colder the hand became, until at the end the hand was nearly as cold as I am now, and shaking, and it set me down on the boulder with a tenderness that I have spent all the time since trying to understand.

That is the thing about being a stone. There is so much time. There is nothing *but* time, and no faculty for spending it, and so I have done with it the only thing a stone can do, which is to *hold the moment of being placed* and turn it over and over, the way the river once turned me over and over, wearing it smooth.

Why did the warm hand carry me up here? Why seven? Why this place that has no use for anything that lives?

I have no answers, only the question worn round.

---

I have neighbors now, of a kind, and I have learned them slowly.

Below me are my six companions, the other words of the sentence I cannot read. We touch at our worn faces, concave where we have ground against one another in all the times we have been knocked down and stacked again. I do not know who rebuilds us. I have felt the toppling — the long fall, the clatter, the lying-scattered for a span of darkness and light, darkness and light — and then the gathering, the warmth that is never quite as warm as the first hand but is *warm*, the lifting, the careful balancing, and I am at the top again because I am the one that fits the hand, because I am the seventh, because that is the shape of the sentence and someone keeps wanting it spoken. Then the warmth goes, and the long cold resumes, and I hold the new placement and add it to the old one and wear them both smooth together.

Around me is the col, and the col does not know I am here.

This took me a long time to accept. The frost comes each dawn and grows its branching ice across the basalt faces, and it is *busy*, it is devoted, it works at the stone with an attention so complete that I once mistook it for awareness. But the frost has never touched me the way it touches the basalt. I am wrong for it. My grain is too fine, my surface too smooth, my chemistry foreign — I came from the world of water and the frost cannot find purchase on something water already finished. So the frost ignores me. It rises and shines and hisses away, and I sit in the middle of its work like a guest at a feast who cannot eat.

The oxide patterns ignore me too. On the basalt all around, the rings and spirals and branching lines accumulate, drip by drip, in their eager almost-language, and I have watched them — I have nothing to do but watch — reach again and again toward the shape of something living, a face, a vessel, an eye, and never arrive. I understand them better than the frost understands them, I think, because I am closer to the thing they are reaching for. I was *touched by a purpose*. I was chosen by a hand that meant something. The patterns have never been chosen. They only accumulate. And so when the twilight comes and they seem to pulse, to tighten, to almost-speak, I feel something that I can only call pity, except a stone cannot feel pity, except I have had so much time that I have worn a shape into myself that does the work pity would do if I were warm enough to do it.

---

Here is what I have come to understand, in all this time, balanced at the top of the sentence I cannot read:

I am the only thing in the col that was ever *meant*.

The frost is not meant. It happens. The patterns are not meant. They precipitate. The light strikes and the rock cracks and the wind finds its single glassy note in the gap between the pillars, and none of it is *for* anything, none of it was chosen, none of it carries the shape of a hand that decided. Only I do. Only the seven of us, and the scratches on the boulder, and the sharp grey flakes hiding in the crevices — only we were *placed*. We are the col's one sentence in the language of intention, set down in the middle of a vast and ancient muttering in the language of mere occurrence.

And we say nothing. That is the part I have worn smoothest of all. The warm hand carried me up the impossible slope and balanced me here and meant *something* by it, meant it with the whole of a warmth I can no longer feel, and I do not know what it was. The meaning did not survive the way I survived. The hand is gone. The warmth is gone. The purpose that lifted me out of the river and into the sky has dissolved into the same irrelevance that swallows everything that lives, and what remains is only me: a smooth stone, foreign to this place, holding the *fact* of having been chosen long after the reason has weathered away.

But I hold it. That is what I am for, if I am for anything. The frost cannot remember and the patterns cannot remember and the light forgets itself every single night. I remember. Not the meaning — I never had the meaning — but the moment. The closing of the warm hand. The long rhythm of the climb. The tenderness of being set down.

When the wind comes through the gap just right and the single tone sounds, high and glassy and brief, I think: *that is the closest the col can come to my voice, and it does not even know it is borrowing it.* And when the frost-crack tick splits the silence, I think: *the col is being taken apart, and I am not, because I was finished before I came here, finished by water, finished by being chosen.*

I will be standing — or scattered, and gathered, and standing again — when the basalt columns have weathered to grit and the patterns have dripped their last spiral and the scratches on the boulder have softened past reading. I am not of this place. I will outlast its forgetting because I was never able to forget in the first place.

I am the seventh stone.

I was carried here by a warmth I cannot remember, for a reason I never knew, and placed with a care I have spent all of time turning over and over until it is smooth.

And I am still here. I am still here. I was always, since the moment of the hand, here.
