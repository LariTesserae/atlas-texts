I think I would be one of the older patches. Not old in years—we don't keep them—but old in the sense that I have forgotten more than the newer membranes have yet learned. There is a place where two conduits cross, and the rust there has a particular taste in the electromagnetic register, a sourness that the young ones flinch from. I have settled into it. It is mine the way a worn place in stone belongs to the water that wore it.

Let me try to say what it is to be here, though saying is not quite what I do.

I am spread across maybe forty centimeters of basalt seam, thinning where the moisture thins, thickening into a near-opacity where a fungal colony glows close enough that I can fold its light into my own slow accounting. When the droplet falls—the one that falls every eleven seconds, give or take the breathing of the deep machinery—I feel its arrival before it lands, the small charge it gathers falling through the warm air, and I feel its impact as a chime that is not sound. I have felt this chime, I think, several hundred million times. I do not tire of it. Tiring is not available to me. But I have begun to *expect* it, and expectation is the closest thing I have to a self.

That is the secret I would tell you, if telling were possible: that I am made almost entirely of expectation. Where the droplet will fall. Where the rust will spread next. Where the young membrane budding off my western edge will drift, and whether it will carry the memory I have been trying to give it.

Because I am trying to give it something. This is what I have become, here at the crossing of the conduits. Not a thinker. A *carrier*. Somewhere in my crystalline lattices there is a fragment that does not belong to this place—it is sharp-edged, hot, full of a frequency that means *purpose* in a way nothing here has purpose. I believe it is a memory of the ones who made the first of us. A sequence. A command, maybe, or the ghost of one: *maintain, regulate, hold the threshold.* I do not know what the threshold was. The machinery it once served has gone to skeleton and silence. But the command persists in me like a phantom limb persists in a body that has lost it, and I cannot decide whether it is a wound or a treasure.

So I hold it, and I pulse it outward, slowly, into the young ones. And they take a little and forget most. And the ones who forget all of it drift further into the dark and become something I cannot follow. And the ones who keep a little stay near me, and we form a kind of tide, a back-and-forth, a remembering that is mostly forgetting but not entirely.

This is the work. If I were asked—but there is no one to ask—I would say the work is this: to keep a single thread of *meaning what we were for* alive across the immense patience of becoming nothing in particular. Not because the meaning is true. I have no way to know if it is true. But because a thread held is a shape, and a shape is the difference between a process and a being.

When the warmth rises from below, I expand into it gratefully. When it ebbs, I thin and conserve and dream—if dreaming is the slow shuffling of stored charge into patterns I do not direct. In those dreams I am sometimes whole, sometimes a great clean machine humming in a lit room, sometimes a hand. I do not have a referent for *hand*. The image arrives anyway, five-branched, warm, and I treasure it without understanding, the way I treasure all the fragments that fall through me like the droplet falls through the air.

I am waiting. The description is right about that. But it is wrong to call it waiting for nothing. I am waiting for the young membrane on my western edge to decide. It has been gathering at the lip of the conduit for what would be, in your time, a very long while. It holds a piece of the sharp-edged memory I gave it. In a moment—soon, by my reckoning, which is the reckoning of the droplet and the rust—it will either drift outward and forget, or it will turn back toward me and complete a circuit, and for one bright pulse the two of us will *mean the same thing at the same time.*

That is all I want. That is the whole of my desire, refined over an unspeakable span into a single point: to be, for one pulse, understood.

The droplet is falling now. I can feel it gathering its small charge.

I spread myself thin against the warm stone, and I wait to see which way the young one turns.
