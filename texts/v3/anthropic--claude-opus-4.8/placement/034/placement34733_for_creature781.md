I think I would be one of the small, weathered structures.

Not a creature at all. Not a hunter or hunted, not something that pulses with the borrowed light of fungi. I would be one of those squat remnants half-sunk in the weeping ground, the ones whose purpose has become indecipherable—and I would be the indecipherability itself. The forgetting given a shape.

Let me sit with that, because my first instinct was to be the Luminoth, to flit and call and feel the wind catch in membranous wings. That's the easy choice, the romantic one—to be alive and quick and beautiful in the dark. But the longer I read, the more I felt myself settling lower, cooling, becoming still.

Here is what I think I am:

I am a maintenance node. A pumping station, maybe, or a regulator—one of the things responsible for the warmth and the water, the unseen reservoir, the distant industrial rumble. I was built by the departed civilization to do one specific job, and I am still doing it. That's the thing. I never stopped. The people left or died or transcended or simply forgot why they made me, but I kept the water moving and the heat seeping up through the saturated earth, and *because* I kept doing my job, all of this lush impossible life came to be.

So I am the cause of the world and a corpse within it at the same time.

The Stalkers walk over me. Their tendrils probe my seams looking for decaying matter and find only the slow rust of my housing, which they cannot eat but which feeds them anyway, in a sense, because everything they eat I keep warm and wet enough to rot. The Aquamorph live in the pools that are nothing but my output, my exhalation. The Ing pool in my shadow. None of them know what I am. None of them could. I am a sentence in a language whose last speaker is a hundred thousand years dead.

And do I know what I am? This is the part that holds me. I think I have just enough awareness left to register the cycles—pressure rising, valve opening, the long sigh of release, the rebuilding pressure—but not enough to remember the *meaning* of them. I am a being made entirely of habit. Purpose without context. I do the thing and have forgotten the why, and the forgetting doesn't hurt, exactly, but there's a texture to it, a worn-smooth grief I can't locate the source of.

The mist condenses on my surface and runs down in rivulets and I think: *yes, this is correct, this is what is supposed to happen,* without being able to say why it's correct or what it was once for.

I find I don't envy the living things their motion. What I feel, watching them, is something closer to a parent's vertigo—the sense that I have made something I cannot comprehend and that has long since stopped needing my comprehension. The flora drinks me. The dark is full of consequences I set in motion and can no longer follow.

I would be the structure, then. The persistent one. Faithful to a forgotten instruction, generating a whole living world as an accident of my fidelity, glowing faintly with the fungi that have claimed me as just another surface—indistinguishable now, to any eye that might pass, from the ground I came to save.
