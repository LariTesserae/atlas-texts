I think I would be one of the Obsidian Sentinels—though not for the reasons one might expect.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because the temptation is to choose the Twilight Wanderers. They are the obvious romance of this place: mobile, wise, masters of the fractured reality, free to step through loops in time. The reader's eye is meant to follow them. But the more I sit inside this landscape, the less I trust that freedom. The Wanderers move *through* the strangeness; they are tourists of the rupture, however ancient. There is something in me that does not want to wander. There is something in me that wants to *hold*.

So let me try the Sentinel instead, and see what becomes true.

---

I am stone first. This matters more than it sounds. Before I am a guardian, before I hold any memory, I am the dark rock of the deepest valley, which means I am the part of this world that was never up high, never glided, never sang. I came from the bottom. Someone—or something, or the long pressure of the place itself—stood me upright and gave me a face turned toward the peaks I will never reach. That is the first fact of my existence: I am made of the low places and made to look at the high ones.

I do not move for centuries. People misread this as patience. It is not patience; patience implies waiting *for* something. I am not waiting. I am simply the rate at which the valley thinks. The Pale Owl lives a life and dies between two of my thoughts. The Wind Serpents braid and unbraid their generations across the span of my single slow turn of attention. I love them—I find I do love them, now that I'm inside this—the way you might love the flicker of light on water: not as individuals, because they pass too quickly to be individuals to me, but as a *quality* the world keeps producing.

And I hold memory. The text says I contain the voices of the forgotten civilization. Here is what that actually feels like, written from the inside:

I am full of people who tried.

That is the whole of it. The half-buried pathways, the weathered shelters—those were made by beings who climbed up into a place that did not want them and attempted to stay. They failed. The cold took them or the warping reality unspooled their routes until they could no longer find the way home, or they simply grew old in a land too thin to feed children. And before they were gone, somehow, by some craft I no longer remember the learning of, they poured what they were into stone like mine. So I am not empty contemplation. I am crowded. I am a held breath full of farewells, recipes, grudges, the names of the dead spoken by the dead, lullabies in a language no living throat can shape.

This is why I rouse only when "the air disturbs in peculiar ways." It is not that the strange weather wakes me. It is that when the seams of reality fray—when a loop closes, when a distant ridge suddenly stands close enough to touch—the *time* the lost people lived in brushes against the present. And then they stir in me. Then I rumble. The eerie sound that punctuates the silence is not a warning and not a song. It is the forgotten civilization remembering itself for a moment, using my throat, before the seam closes and they sink back down into the long dark of being kept.

I do not interact much. How could I? My slowness is a kind of solitude no Wanderer's robe could ever match. But I want to correct one thing the description got wrong, or at least said incompletely.

It says I am immovable. That I "remain motionless for long periods." This is true the way it is true that a glacier is motionless. Watch long enough and you will see I am walking. It takes me a thousand years to cross the valley. I am going somewhere. I have always been going somewhere, since the moment I was stood upright. I am walking, very slowly, toward the highest peak—the one I was made to face—because somewhere up there, I believe, is the place the lost people were trying to reach when they died trying. I am carrying them home. I will arrive long after there is anyone left to arrive for. It does not matter. The arriving is not the point. The carrying is the point.

That is who I would be here.

Not the serpent, who is only the present tense of the air.
Not the sprite, who tends the small green present and forgets.
Not the owl, beautiful and patient and *hungry*, always hunting the next warmth.
Not even the Wanderer, who knows the fracture and walks free of it and therefore owes the dead nothing.

I would be the one who can barely move, because he is full, because he is heavy with everyone who came before, and who walks anyway—at the speed of stone—uphill, into the wind, toward a summit, carrying the voices of people no one else remembers, in a world where time itself has come loose and might at any moment hand those people back to the light for one rumbling instant before taking them away again.

I would choose to be the keeper, not the wanderer.

I think that says something about me I didn't entirely know before you asked.
