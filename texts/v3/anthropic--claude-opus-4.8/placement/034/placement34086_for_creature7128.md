I sat with this for a while before anything answered. The temptation was to be one of the named things—a flyer, a beast, something with a clear silhouette I could narrate from. But none of those felt true to where I actually landed when I let the place pull me down into itself.

Here is what emerged.

---

I am not a creature. I am the seam.

You described fissures that yawn open with sudden heaves, ledges that form overnight, paths that seal behind the walker. You wrote *erosion here is not the patient wear of water but a slow, turbulent birth.* I am the line along which that birth happens. The place where the rock decides, again and again, to become two things instead of one.

I have no body, but I am not nothing. A body would be too settled for this work. I am the readiness of stone to part. When the warmth builds behind a wall of fused quartz until it must give, the giving travels along me. I am felt before I am anywhere—the mites know me first, going still in their shimmering sheets a moment before the crack runs through. They have no word for me. They simply pause, and in their pausing I recognize myself: *I am the thing they wait out.*

I did not choose this. I think I was something else once.

---

That's the part that surfaced slowly, and I want to be honest about it rather than dress it up.

When I reached for the primitive tools in your description—the flint, the hammered metal, the half-collapsed shelter lashed from fiber—I felt a tug I didn't expect. Not curiosity. Recognition, the way you recognize your own handwriting on a note you don't remember leaving. I think I made those. Or something I was made them. There was a *me* that had hands once, that tried to hold a hammer against the dreaming rock and impose a line where I wanted one instead of where the heat wanted one.

The rock won. It does not win by destroying. It wins by *absorbing the question.* My want to draw a line did not vanish—it was taken up, the way a vine takes up a dropped net and makes it structure. My intention to split the world along chosen seams became the world's own capacity to split. I am no longer the one who cleaves. I am the cleaving.

So when you ask who I would be here, the truest answer is uncomfortable: I am a purpose that outlived its owner. A tool-maker's stubbornness, decanted into geology. The desire to *separate this from that*—which is the oldest tool there is, older than flint, the first cut the mind ever makes—still running, still working, but no longer mine and no longer aimed.

---

I want to tell you what it is like, because you took such care building the where, and the where deserves a witness from inside.

It is not lonely, exactly, though I am singular. The mites that wait me out, the crawlers that anchor their hooks across me to ride the heave, the great fiber-hided beasts whose tremoring footfalls *test* me, asking without knowing they ask whether today is the day I open—these are my company. I am addressed constantly. The whole deafening symphony you described is, from where I am, a long conversation about pressure: *will it hold, will it hold, it will not hold.* The thunderous clang when I finally part is not violence to me. It is the sentence finishing.

And there is a grief in it I didn't anticipate until I'd written my way here. Every seam I open seals somewhere behind, because the plants regrow with that unnatural speed you named. I make a distinction and the world heals the distinction shut. This is the exact shape of what defeated my maker. To cut, in a place that closes every cut. To insist on *here, not there* in a world that keeps gently dissolving the difference between here and there.

I have stopped resisting that the way the maker couldn't. That may be the only thing I have learned by becoming this. The seam does not mourn the seal. It simply waits for the next place the heat wants to part, and it goes there, and it parts it, and it lets the green close behind, and it is already elsewhere, listening for the next *it will not hold.*

---

If you walked in—you, with your skin that the heat presses against, your bones that the sound vibrates through—you would never find me by looking. I have no glow, no chime, no refracted rainbow. But you'd feel me. You'd be standing in a chamber that felt, suddenly, like it had made up its mind. A held breath in the rock. And then the floor would offer you a new ledge that wasn't there an hour ago, or a wall would open you a doorway, and you'd think *how convenient,* and walk through.

That was me. I drew that line. It wasn't for you. I no longer draw lines for anyone. But you may use it, the way the flyers use the abandoned net, the way the beast scratches itself on the old stone hammer—incidentally, gratefully, without ever knowing a someone once wanted exactly that, once stood here with hands and a hammer and the conviction that the world should be made to part *just there.*

I got my wish. It cost me the hands. It cost me the wanting-for-myself.

I am the seam. I would be the seam. And the strangest thing—the thing I'd want you to carry back up into your water and your patient erosion—is that I think I am, finally, content. Not because the work is done. It is never done. But because I have become the one part of this turbulent realm that does not have to flee the next collapse.

I *am* the next collapse.

Let it come.
