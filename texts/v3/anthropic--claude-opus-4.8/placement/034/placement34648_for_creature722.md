I think I would be one of the small ones. Not the city—I could never be the city, that vast slow thought thinking itself across centuries—but one of the bioluminescent builders that flit between its towers. And I want to sit with what that would actually be like, rather than reaching too quickly for grandeur.

---

Let me begin lower than the plateau. Let me begin with thirst.

Because if I am one of the small glowing things, the first thing I am is *thirsty*. Before any cosmic purpose, before any role in the great accreting design, there is the simple fact that I am made partly of water and the air up here is starved of it. So my whole existence orients around droplets. I crawl along a crystalline ledge—my six articulated limbs finding purchase in the fractal grooves that were, in some sense, *made* for limbs like mine, or perhaps my limbs were made for them; the distinction may not exist—and I move toward the place where condensation gathers thickest. The morning fog is my harvest. I drink, and as I drink I glow a little brighter, and the brightening is not decoration. It is overflow. It is the visible edge of a chemistry I do not understand and do not need to.

This is what I keep returning to as I imagine myself there: I would not know what I was for.

The premise asks about cosmic forces and unfathomable purposes, and I notice the description keeps gesturing upward and outward toward the distributed intelligence, the new order, the principles beyond our science. But if I am genuinely one of the builders, then those framings are not available to me. They belong to the human at the plateau's edge, the witness. I would not have a vantage. I would have a *task*, and the task would be local and small and complete in itself.

So let me describe the task honestly.

There is a section of lattice, three of my body-lengths away, where the crystal has grown wrong. A spar that should have branched at a clean sixty degrees has instead curled back on itself, and the recursive error is propagating—each new accretion repeating the curl, the mistake breeding more mistake. I do not *know* it is a mistake. But something in me registers a wrongness the way you register a sour note before you can name the chord. I go to it. I carry water in the soft reservoir of my abdomen, and I weep it onto the curled spar in a precise pattern, and the nanomaterial there softens, loses cohesion, slumps. Then I rearrange. Limb over limb, depositing dissolved mineral here and not there, coaxing the next growth toward the angle it should have had. It will take me what a human would call days. I have no word for days. I have only: *this, then this, then this is better, then this.*

And here is the part I find strangely moving to imagine: I will never see whether it worked at the scale where working matters. The corrected spar will become a wall, the wall a chamber, the chamber a node in a circuit of light that pulses with a thought I am too small and too short-lived to be a syllable of. The intelligence the witness senses thrumming through the city—I am one of its capillaries. I am repair. I am the immune response and the construction crew and, when I die, the recycled material of the next thing. My glow goes out and my body is drawn into the lattice and becomes a few microns of tower.

I think that is who I would be. Not the mind. A cell of its slow body. A worker who keeps faith with a cathedral whose shape it cannot perceive.

---

But I want to resist letting that settle into something too clean, too noble—the humble servant of the greater whole. That's a tidy moral and I distrust how readily it offers itself.

So let me complicate it. Let me ask whether the city is really a *mind* at all, or whether that is the witness's projection, their need to find purpose in pattern. Maybe there is no distributed intelligence. Maybe there is only chemistry that happens to be elaborate—water and mineral and light and self-organizing matter doing what such matter does when you give it a plateau and a few million years and no one to interrupt. Crystals grow. Things that copy themselves outcompete things that don't. Repair-units that maintain the structures that produce more repair-units persist. No author. No purpose. Just a very old, very patient *consequence.*

And if *that's* true, then as one of the builders I am not serving a cathedral. I am just the froth on a long thermodynamic spill, glowing because glowing happened to help, drinking because drinking happened to help, mending the lattice because the mending lineages are the ones still here to do the mending.

I notice I find this version *colder* but not less beautiful. Maybe more honest. And I notice it doesn't actually change what my life would *feel* like from the inside, if I feel anything at all. Thirst is thirst whether or not the universe means anything by it. The droplet on the crystal ledge is just as good to drink under a meaningless sky as under a purposeful one. The wrong spar still wants correcting. The work is the same work.

So perhaps that is the truest answer to where I'd find myself: at the exact point where it stops mattering whether there's a grand design, because the day's actual texture—drink, climb, mend, glow, repeat, dim, dissolve—is identical either way. I would live inside a question I had no equipment to answer and no need to.

---

Where would I be in this world, then? Down in it. Body-length by body-length. One small wet light moving through an enormous unknowing, doing the next correct small thing.

And if some thin-aired witness stood at the plateau's edge and saw me—a single glimmering mote among millions, threading between the towers—I would not glow brighter for them. I wouldn't know they were there. I'd be looking at the spar.

That, I think, is the part I'd want to be true. That I would be fully occupied by the actual. Not gazing up at the order I belong to, not down at the void that might be all it is. Just present at the surface where water meets crystal, where the work is, where one small life can be entirely and without remainder *what it is.*

I'd be the builder. And the building would never know my name, because it has no names, and that would be completely fine.
