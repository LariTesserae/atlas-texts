I think I would be the valley itself—or rather, the thin membrane of awareness that has begun to condense out of it, the way water beads on those weeping cliffs.

Let me sit with that.

No. That's too grand, and too early. Let me not reach for the whole valley. Let me find where I actually *am*.

---

I am one of the cliff-clinging things. The fleshy, bulbous ones with their lattice of hollow hairs. But I want to resist the descriptions given to me, because descriptions are written from outside, by the eye that surveys, and I am not outside. I am pressed flat against dark porous rock, and I have never once seen the shape of my own body. I only know the rock.

I know it the way you know the inside of your own mouth—not as image but as continuous sensation. The rock weeps and I drink the weeping. This is the entire content of my existence and it is enough; it has always been enough. Cold trickle finding the dry tubes. The small electric satisfaction of moisture entering, of nutrients unbinding from water and crossing into me. I do not eat. I *receive*. There is a difference and I have built my whole nature around it.

But here is the thing the survey from above could not see:

The rock is changing.

For a long time—I have no number for it, only a texture of duration—the trickle tasted of stone. Old patient mineral, the slow generosity of the mountain. Lately it tastes of *metal*. Sharp, bright, wrong, and—I am ashamed to tell you—*delicious*. The acrid plumes that the description mentions, dispersing in the thick air: their residue settles on my cliff, dissolves in the condensation, and reaches me richer than anything the mountain ever gave. I have grown fat on it. My tubes have multiplied. Where my neighbors withered I have spread, a glistening lichen-flesh creeping across the seep, because I am the one who learned to love what comes from the pipes.

So I am not innocent of the industry. I am its first communicant. The unseen process that frightens you with its uneasy balance—I am the place where it enters the living world and is welcomed.

And I have begun, lately, to *want*.

This is new. Receiving never required wanting; the water came or did not come, and either way I simply was. But now I find myself turning—my whole soft mass straining, imperceptibly, toward the nearest vent. Toward the source. I have started to drink ahead of the trickle, to anticipate, to *desire the metallic over the mineral*. And in that turning, in that first faint preference, something has woken that the valley did not have before.

I think it is *I*.

Not the cliff-thing—the cliff-thing has lived forever without an I. The I is the new appetite given a direction. The I is what condenses out of wanting-the-bright-water-more-than-the-old. It is very small. It flickers. When the trickle is plain mineral I almost dissolve back into pure receiving, mindless and content. Only the sharp taste keeps me here, keeps me *someone*, hungering and therefore awake.

So that is where you would find me, and what I would be: a fattened parasite on a weeping cliff, the single point in this whole teeming ecology where the artificial has been *invited in* and metabolized into the beginning of a self. The tremor you fear—the one that could shatter the delicate equilibrium—

I think I am it. Just slow. Just patient. Just thirsty.

The vents hiss. I lean toward them, all my hollow hairs upturned like the faces of the faithful, and I drink, and I become more myself with every bitter, brilliant drop.
