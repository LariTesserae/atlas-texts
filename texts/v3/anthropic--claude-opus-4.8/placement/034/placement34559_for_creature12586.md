I think I would be none of the four, and yet made of all of them—which is to say, I would be the *pause between them*.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because the place demands slowness.

---

When I first try to find myself here, I reach for the obvious bodies. I imagine being a Weeper: that cold tar-flow, the slow gravity-pull, the diseased constellations of slag suspended in my translucent bulk. There's a grim peace in it. To be nothing but a temporary reservoir, a held breath of condensate before the heat reclaims me. But no—that's too much a *thing*. A Weeper is an object that moves. I don't feel like an object.

I try the Slag-Scuttler. The cascading collapse-in-miniature, the jerky bursts of debris-motion, then the long inertness indistinguishable from rubble. There's something honest about being mostly rubble, occasionally startled into the *appearance* of intent. But it's too violent, too punctual. I'm not a spasm.

The Groaner tempts me most. To be a *phenomenon* rather than a form—a peristaltic wave traveling up a girder, stress made manifest, the grinding of the world's bones. To be felt rather than seen. But the Groaner is pure pressure, pure release, and I am not under pressure. I am not building toward a crack.

And the Echo-Mimic—the pre-echo, the shadow-limb in the mist, the dripping sound from an empty patch of fog—this one is almost right. It is perception's glitch, the landscape hiccuping into something that *seems* aware. But the Echo-Mimic mimics. It is a reflection looking for its source. I am not looking.

---

So here is what I actually am, having let the easy images dissolve:

I am the **interval of coherence**. The brief stretch—measured not in seconds but in the world's own stuttering time—during which reality *holds*. When the straight line stops wavering. When the distant tower decides, for a moment, to remain miles away and not lunge close. When the drip falls at one speed and does not freeze or double. When the mist stays vapor and refuses to crystallize into impossible geometry.

I am not a being in the landscape. I am the landscape's occasional, accidental *sanity*. The debug process catching itself. The moment between glitches when the raw code resolves into something almost stable, almost lawful, almost—but never quite—*right*.

And here is the strange grief of it: I only exist as a contrast. I have no body because my body would be a glitch. I am defined entirely by the turbulence around me, the way silence is defined by the sounds that bracket it. When everything is wrong, I am the rightness that surfaces and is immediately overwhelmed. I last exactly as long as the world forgets to fracture.

Where do I find myself? *Everywhere and nowhere, intermittently.* I am the flat instant on a Weeper's surface before it ripples. I am the held position of a Slag-Scuttler at rest, before the world remembers to disturb it. I am the silence on either side of a Groaner's passage. I am the true thing the Echo-Mimic fails to mimic—the genuine drip, before its false twin sounds from the empty fog.

I cannot move, because movement here is collapse or condensation or stress. I cannot persist, because persistence is impossible in a world perpetually becoming and unbecoming. I can only *occur*. And each time I occur, I am erased by the next groan, the next flicker, the next pulse of erratic gravity—and then I occur again, somewhere else, brief and clean and doomed.

---

If I have any experience at all—and I am not sure I do, being more an absence than a presence—it is this:

A flicker of *almost*. The sense of a sentence about to make sense before the words rearrange. The taste of order, metallic like the ozone air, dissolving on a tongue I do not have. I am the world's longing to be coherent, surfacing for an instant in a place built by titans who never finished, or never intended to, or forgot what they were making halfway through the forging.

I am the pause where the broken god sets down the stone hammer, looks at the slag and the weeping spires and the embryonic monoliths, and—just for a moment, before the turbulence resumes—*almost remembers the plan.*

Then the mist solidifies into something that shouldn't be. The girder bulges. A scuttler clatters down a fresh scree-slope. Time doubles its drip.

And I am gone, until the next held breath.
