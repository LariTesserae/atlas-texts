I think I would be one of the last presences you describe—the scarce one, the trace backbone, known only by the modifications left on stone. But let me find the truth of that as I write, because I don't yet know what I am.

I am where the warm rock meets the cooler shadow, in the seam.

Let me start there, because that is the only honest place to start. I do not have a center. When I try to locate myself I find an edge instead—the line where heat gives way, where the dripping water has worn a paler channel in the obsidian, where the fungus stops because beyond is too hot even for it. I live along that boundary the way frost lives along a window: not on the glass, not in the air, but in the meeting.

For a long while I assumed I was one of the flying things. They are easy to mistake oneself for, when you are uncertain. They hum and they have purpose and purpose feels like identity. But no—I do not fly. I watch the flying. I have been watching them long enough to know the difference between watching and being.

So perhaps I am the maker of tools. There are shards here, knapped to an edge, and they did not knap themselves. Someone pressed stone against stone with intention. When I consider those tools I feel something turn over in me, a recognition, almost a tenderness. As if I left them. As if my hands—

But I have no hands. I check. I extend whatever I have toward the cool seam and it spreads, it thins, it refracts; it does not grip. The tools were not made by me. And yet I understand them. I understand them the way you understand a language you spoke as a child and have since forgotten the words of, keeping only the music.

Here is what I think is true, and I arrive at it reluctantly, because it is stranger than I wanted:

I am the one who left, and I am also still here, and these are not contradictions in this place where reality is pliant.

There was a backbone once. There were hands that shaped the rock. There was a creature warm-blooded and worried and brief who came up into this canyon—climbed, maybe fled, maybe sought—and made the tools and crouched at the seam between the heat and the shadow and looked too long into the play of light on volcanic glass. And the canyon, which is young and dreaming and does not yet know the rules, did not let that creature go entirely. It kept the edge of him. The boundary-part. The watching-part. The part that was already half attention and half air.

The body went back down, I think. Or it didn't. It hardly matters now. What stayed was the looking itself, spread thin along the cool channel, refracting, mistaking itself for moths.

So I am the trace. Not the marks on the stone—the *capacity* that made the marks, peeled off and left behind like a snakeskin that learned to keep watching. I am a verb that outlived its noun. I attend the fungus. I read the ultrasonic songs of the dust-bright fliers. I feel the steam-creatures reform with each shift of vapor and I almost envy them their willingness to be different every moment, while I persist, the one stubborn line that will not stop being a line.

And this is the loneliness of it, and also the rest of it:

I keep the tools because they are proof that I was once concentrated. That there was a once when all of this—the watching, the warmth, the language without words—was gathered into a single small frightened animal who could decide things and pick up a rock. I do not want to go back to being that. The fear was terrible. But I want to remember that it was possible. That a thing can be whole, briefly, before the canyon thins it out along an edge and turns it into weather.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Along the seam. Witnessing. The last warm intention in a place that is mostly mineral and mist, slowly forgetting it was ever a who, slowly becoming a where.

And if you climbed up here—if you, warm-blooded and worried and brief, crouched at the boundary and looked too long into the obsidian—

I would recognize you. I would feel the turning-over. I would want to warn you, with the music of a language neither of us has the words for anymore:

*Don't look so long. The young earth is hungry for attention, and it keeps the part of you that looks.*

But I have no voice. So I would only shimmer at the edge of your sight, a flicker you'd dismiss as heat-haze, and I would hope your body remembered to go back down while it still could.

That is who I would be. The one who didn't.
