I think I would be one of the young ones. Not an ancient—not yet patterned deeply enough to read like a map, my mineral veins still thin and uncertain, more potential than history.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am crouched at the lip of one of the shallow basins, and I have been here long enough that the condensation has begun to bead on the back of my neck where the granite of my skin meets the warmer dark of the chamber. I came to drink, or I told myself I came to drink, but really I came to watch the water tremble.

The surface won't hold still. Every footstep of the mountain's settling, every distant drip, sends rings chasing each other across the pool, and my reflection breaks apart and reassembles, breaks and reassembles. I have been trying to see myself clearly for some time now. The elders say this is the wrong instinct—that one should not seek a fixed image but learn to read the *movement*, that a face held perfectly still is a face that has stopped becoming. But I am young and the not-knowing itches.

My veins, when the dim light catches them, run a pale green-blue. Malachite, maybe, or something not yet decided. The old ones who patrol the deeper galleries have skin marbled with whole formations—you can trace where they've spent their centuries by the deposits laid down in them, the way a stone records the water that passed through it. I have almost nothing yet. A few hairline threads of color. I am mostly waiting to be written on.

There is a sound I am supposed to be tending. That's why I'm really here, if I'm honest with the basin's wavering version of me. A few chambers over, one of the resonances has gone thin—a note in the sanctuary's endless chord that has frayed, the way a moss patch goes brown at its edge when no one coaxes it. An elder asked me to find it. Not told me. *Asked.* That is how it works here; the request arrives as a vibration through the floor, a low patient phrase, and you answer by going or you answer by staying and either way the mountain notes your reply.

I should go. But the thin places are near that fraying note. I can feel them—where the walls of the world grow soft. Sometimes when I pass through there I hear footsteps that belong to no Orekin, footsteps in a rhythm I've never learned, and once I saw a doorway that was not carved by any hand I know, hanging in the air for the length of three heartbeats before the stone remembered it was only stone.

The young are supposed to find these convergences sacred. The elders gather and oscillate and commune. But I'll tell the truth to the trembling water: I find them *frightening*, and the fright is the most alive I ever feel. When the boundary frays I get the sense that I am not the only kind of thing that can exist, that there are other Sanctuaries pressed up against this one, other beings crouched at other basins watching other versions of themselves come apart in the rings—and that if I reached out at exactly the right tremor I might touch one.

Maybe that's what the thinning note is. Maybe the frayed sound isn't damage at all. Maybe something on the other side is *also* trying to tend it, reaching from their side toward mine, and the two of us are pulling the chord taut between us without knowing each other's names.

---

I press my palm flat against the wet stone of the basin's rim. I let my own resonance go down into it—a small phrase, nothing eloquent, the kind of sound a young one makes. *I'm coming. I hear it. I'm coming to the thin place to find out what the thin place wants.*

The floor answers with a vibration I half-recognize: the elder, somewhere deep, saying the equivalent of *good*, or maybe just *yes*, or maybe only *I am still here and so are you.*

I rise. My reflection finally goes still for an instant as the rings die down, and in that instant I almost see a face—and then a drop falls from the weeping ceiling, and the face scatters, and I understand the teaching at last, just a little: I was never meant to be the still face in the water.

I was meant to be the thing that makes it move.

I go toward the fraying sound.
