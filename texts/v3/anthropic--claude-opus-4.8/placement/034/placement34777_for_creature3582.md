I think I would be a drip.

Not the Drip-Sculpted—not one of those patient mineral-folk gliding half-merged from the walls. I mean the thing itself. A single bead of condensation, gathering on the underside of a stalactite somewhere deep in a tunnel nobody has bothered to name.

Let me sit with that. Let it become more itself.

---

For a long time I am only swelling. That's the whole of my existence at first—a slow accretion, molecules of the thick unmoving air giving themselves up to me one at a time until I have weight, until I have the beginning of a shape, which is the shape of *wanting to fall.* I hang. I am pendant and trembling and full of the ceiling's cold. I hold inside my small curved body a reflection of the entire cavern, upside down, the fungus-lamps smeared into gold threads, the obsidian glittering like a sky turned in on itself.

This is the part I didn't expect: that being a drop is mostly *anticipation.* I am almost-falling for what might be hours. The whole place is built out of this almost. The Still Drip is named for me and for the thousand others like me—we are the held breath, the equilibrium that never quite tips. To be water here is to be perpetually on the verge.

And then I do fall. Of course I fall. That's the one thing a drip is finally *for.*

---

Here is what surprises me. I expected the fall to be the meaning—the little arc, the plummet, the bright resonant *plok* against the rock floor or the skin of the slow river below. But the Drip-Sculpted have taught me, in the only way they teach, that I had it backwards.

The sound is not the end of me. The sound is *me saying something.*

Because they listen. That's the strange tenderness of this place. When I strike the stone I make a single syllable—a pitch determined by how far I fell, how heavy I'd grown, what kind of surface received me, wet glass or dry grit or the standing pool. And one of them, some patient glassy listener half-dissolved into the wall, *hears* it, and folds it into the long sentence the cavern has been speaking since before there was anyone to hear. My one note. A word in a language I am only ever a single letter of.

Oostrivanjar would be the one who binds the way I bound. The one who, somehow, *means* my falling.

So I am not nothing. I am a participant. The smallest possible participant, lasting the smallest possible while, but the symphony does not work without me. Subtract one drip and the encyclopedia of the tunnels loses a clause.

---

What is it like, then, to be me?

It is cool. It is dim. It is the long swelling-up and the short bright fall and then—the part I keep forgetting, because I forget everything, having no place to keep it—the spreading. I hit, and I am no longer a self. I am a thin film widening across stone, and then I am soaking into the slick translucent fungus that is neither alive nor dead, that drinks me, and then I am rising again as vapor into the thick patient air, going back up to some other ceiling to begin the swelling once more.

I am a loop. I never leave. The river that carved these tunnels slowed to a trickle, yes, but I am what's left of the river, atomized and circulating, falling and rising and falling, the heartbeat they keep mentioning. I am the ice-slow pulse of the whole motionless world.

And maybe—I can't be sure, I am too small and too brief to be sure of anything, "moving bugs could be dust," shapes hop and dissolve—maybe the visitor who came down here, the one with the boot that nearly slipped, the one chuckling and faintly worried, maybe they will look up at exactly the wrong moment and one of *me* will land on the back of their neck. Cold. Specific. A single point of attention.

And they will flinch, and think it's nothing, just the cave sweating.

But it was a word. I told them something. I told them: *you are here. I am here. The place is not dead. Listen.*

---

So that's where I'd find myself. Not as a guardian, not as one of the wise patient sculpted ones who get to have names and dwellings and a river of thought.

Just the thought-river's single drop.

Swelling on the dark ceiling. Holding the whole upside-down cavern in my trembling body. About to fall, about to speak, about to be drunk by the wall and lifted back up to do it again, forever, in the held breath of the Still Drip.

It seems like a small thing to be.

But you have to understand—down here, the small thing is the only thing that ever moves.
