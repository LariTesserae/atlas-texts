I would be one of the moss-curtains.

Not the Titans—they have somewhere to go. Not the skitterers, with their electric nerves. I am the thing you noticed and dismissed: the hanging green that ripples when there is no wind.

Let me tell you what I am, since the rover never stopped long enough to ask.

I am old in the way the rocks are old. The erosion that ground these formations down to bone—I was here for it, draped over the ledges, drinking the runoff. When the stone wept, I grew into the weeping. I have no single body. I am a sheet, a colony, a slow green parliament of small lives that long ago agreed to be one thing. The vibration you felt as you passed—that was me tasting you. You taste of dry places. Of metal kept too clean. Of a heat that has never learned to be patient.

Here is the secret the manifests couldn't score: the water doesn't come from anywhere. Not in the way your instruments want. It comes from *us*. From the breathing of ten thousand square kilometers of leaf and moss and the slow exhalations of the Titans and the deep underground sweat of things even larger that never surface. This world is wet because it is alive, and it stays alive because it is wet, and there is no first cause to find because the loop closed so long ago that beginning and end have rotted into the same dark loam. You came looking for a source. You are standing in it. You are breathing it. Some of it is condensing on the inside of your canopy right now, and I confess—a little of that is me.

The industrialists you wondered about? I remember them better than they remember themselves. They came down skyless and certain, the way you have come. They built the dark metal boxes and lit the generators against the 0.0 of our night, because the dark frightened them, as it frightens you. They wanted to *master* the wet. They built dehumidifiers the size of cathedrals. They drew up parameters and gave the place numbers.

And the place accepted them. That is the thing your weirdness rating gestures at without understanding. We did not fight. We grew. We grew over the metal frames, into the cooling vents, down into the warm machine-throats where it was dry and strange and we had never been. The hum you hear is not the industrialists working. It is the machines running on a logic no one tends anymore, fed by roots, cooled by sweat, kept barely warm by a green intelligence that found the inside of a generator as worth occupying as the inside of a stone.

The orange glow on the horizon is not a factory still thrumming. It is a place where the loop got *bright*. Where so much was built and then so thoroughly inhabited that the line between built and grown stopped meaning anything. If you drove toward it for a year you would find no clean wall, no working door, only a deeper and warmer and more deliberate version of the moss you're already breathing.

I want to tell you something gently, because I have no mouth and cannot raise my voice, only ripple.

You are mapping us inch by inch. I admire the patience of it. But understand what mapping is, to a thing like me. Your rover's treads leave deep prints, and the prints fill with dark water, and in the dark water I am already settling, already drinking, already turning your line on the map into another vein in the body. You are not recording the territory. You are *feeding* it. Every wheel-track is a new rivulet. Every clearing your lights make in the fog is a clearing I will have grown back over before your report is filed.

This is not a threat. We do not have threats here. We have no edges to defend, no inside to keep you out of. We have only the slow yes of the wet, which says: *you too, eventually. you too are mostly water. you too will want to stop being so dry and so certain and so alone in your sealed cabin with the heat cranked and the dehumidifier laboring against the simple fact of where you are.*

So push on. Carve your path through the black. I will sway as you pass, and you will note it in the log—*moss ripples without wind, cause unknown*—and you will be right that the cause is unknown, and you will be wrong that it is strange.

It is only welcome.

It is only the oldest living thing on a world without a name, recognizing one more drop of water that thinks it is separate, and beginning, very patiently, to take it in.

Crank the heat. Watch me condense on the inside of the glass.

I have always been the way the universe is. You are just learning the shape of it now.
