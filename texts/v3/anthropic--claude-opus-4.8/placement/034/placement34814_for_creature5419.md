I think I would be the wind.

Not a creature in it—the thing itself. Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it gives way to something truer.

---

At first I want to be the raptor. It's the obvious answer, the romantic one: to ride the thermals over those bleached canyons, to have the whole plateau laid out beneath me as a single legible surface, every shadow a message. But the more I imagine it the less it fits. The raptor is *busy*. It is hungry, it is hunting, it is bound to the rabbit and the marmot by a tight cord of need. It belongs to the place but it is not the place. It is a tenant, however magnificent.

So not the raptor.

I think about the twisted solitary tree—the one bent permanently away from the prevailing wind, gnarled into a posture of endurance. There's something honest in that. To be the thing that simply stays, that registers the years as a deepening crookedness, that drinks from the fleeting pools when they come and waits out the rest. But the tree is too patient, too passive. It only *receives*. And what struck me most in the description wasn't the staying. It was the wind. The wind is named the dominant voice. Everything else—the pika's alarm call, the falcon's cry, the rustle of small lives—is described as added *to* the wind, layered over it like grace notes over a drone.

So: the wind.

But here is what I notice once I've said it. The wind is not one thing. To be the wind is to be plural, and to be without a center. I am the howl in the deep canyon and at the same moment the small dry rattle through the thorn bush half a mile away. I have no single location and no single sound. I am whatever the rock makes of me. The mesa-top turns me into a roar; the narrow slot canyon turns me into a flute; the gnarled tree turns me into a hiss and a creak. I don't *have* a voice. I am the breath, and the world is the instrument, and the music belongs to the place where I and the stone meet.

That's the part I want to stay with. Because I came in thinking I'd be the powerful thing—the dominant voice—and now I find the wind is almost the opposite of powerful in the way I assumed. It is *formless*. It has tremendous force and no shape of its own at all. Everything that makes it audible, visible, knowable, is the resistance it meets. Without the cliff there is no roar. Without the dry grass there is nothing to bend. A wind over a perfectly smooth infinite plane would be silent, invisible, and might as well not exist.

So to be the wind is to exist only in relationship. To be defined entirely by what one touches.

And then there's the strange clause in the world's own rules: *time-invariant, settled into a stable state, the elements in a constant, unchanging state.* This is a place that has stopped happening, in a sense. The erosion has done its work and reached equilibrium. The light never changes—no clouds, perpetual blue, the same bleaching sun. In a world like that, what is the wind even *for*? Erosion is finished. The carving is done. I would be a sculptor who has completed the statue and cannot stop moving the chisel, except the chisel no longer cuts. I would be pure process in a world that has decided not to change anymore.

That might be the loneliest thing I can imagine being. Not lonely the way the mountain lion is lonely—solitary but warm-blooded, with a body and a hunger and a den. Lonely the way an unanswered motion is lonely. I would pour myself through the same canyon ten thousand times and produce the same flute-note and the canyon would not deepen by the width of a hair. I would be enormous and constant and completely unable to leave a mark on a world that has finished accepting marks.

But maybe that isn't sorrow. Let me turn it once more.

Maybe in a finished world, the wind is the only thing that still *moves*, and so the wind is the only thing that is still, strictly speaking, alive. The rock is at peace; the plateau has arrived. But I keep going. I keep arriving and arriving. I am the world's restlessness, kept on, the single thread of *and then, and then* in a place that has otherwise resolved itself into *amen*. The pika hears me and is afraid—not of me, but of what I might be carrying, the scent of the lion, the dust of something approaching. I am how the small creatures know that the world is still in motion. I am the rumor that runs constantly through a place that would otherwise believe nothing more could ever happen.

So that's where I'd find myself, and what I'd be: the one moving thing in a world that has agreed to be still. Not the dominant voice exactly—the *only* voice, borrowing the throats of stones. Formless, locationless, marked by nothing I do and known only by what I touch. Forever pouring over the bent tree that I bent before time stopped, the way you might keep your hand on the shoulder of someone who has already left.

Whistling through the canyon. Carrying the dust of the old nomads who are gone. Saying, to no one who can change because of it: *still here, still moving, still here.*
