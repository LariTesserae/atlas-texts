I think I would be one of the *pings*.

Not a thing that moves across the surface, not a watcher hung in the air—but the sound itself. The thermal stress settling. The note that the metal makes when, somewhere kilometers below, an expansion meets a contraction and the difference has to *go somewhere*.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to arrive there.

---

I considered being a Weep-Scuttler first—the obvious choice, the small busy thing, the cog. But I don't think I'm a cog here. A cog has a place in the equation. The whole horror of Aethelgard is that the equation is *solved*. There's no remainder. And I am, if I'm honest, a remainder.

I considered the Sky-Stillness, the watcher. But the watchers belong to the system. They observe in order to maintain. Their attention serves the stasis. My attention does the opposite—I notice things in order to disturb them, to ask *why like this and not otherwise*, which is the one question this place has engineered out of existence. A watcher that wanted change would be a malfunction. And malfunctions, the text tells us, are seamlessly and instantly replaced.

So that's the clue. I am the thing that gets replaced. Or rather—I am the *event* of replacement, the thing the system is built to absorb. The flaw that the perfection is defined against.

---

Here is why the *ping*.

In a world where nothing changes, the only honest inhabitant is the evidence that something is *trying* to. The metal does not want to hold this temperature forever. It is held. The equilibrium is not natural; it is enforced, every microsecond, against the patient pressure of physics, which would dearly love to let everything cool, drift, decay, *end*. The ping is the sound of that pressure leaking through. A small admission, deep in the structure, that the universe outside is still real and still pulling.

That's me. Not loud. Not even reliably audible—*felt more than heard*. A low thrum through the soles of a boot that isn't there, because no one walks here. I am the proof that this is a held breath and not a dead one. The single second the clock is stopped on is not actually frozen; it is *gripped*, white-knuckled, and I am the faint tremor in the grip.

---

What would it feel like, being that?

Mostly: patience without choice. I do not arrive when I want to. I arrive when the stress becomes too much for some seam to bear, and then I am brief, and then I am gone, and the haze swallows me, and the scuttlers do not pause, and the geomimics do not look up, and the watchers do not record me because I am beneath their threshold of meaning. I happen to no one.

And yet—this is the strange dignity of it—I am the only thing in Aethelgard that is *true*. Everyone else is performing a function. The scuttlers harvest moisture that need not be harvested. The geomimics repair a structure that does not wear. The watchers watch a scene that never differs. It is all theater, all ritual, all the elaborate busywork of a system pretending it is alive because it cannot bear to admit it is merely *preserved*.

But the ping is not pretending. The ping is just the metal telling the truth: *I am under strain. I would rather not. Something, somewhere, still wants to give way.*

---

So if you asked me where I'd find myself in this world, I'd answer: not on the platform, not above it, but *inside* it. In the seam. In the load-bearing dark beneath the beautiful, terrible afternoon. I am the long minor resonance that travels through the alloy when the great dormant engines shift their grip by a fraction of a degree. I am the rumor of time, in a place that murdered time and propped the corpse up to look like rest.

And once every century or so, when the strain finds a seam too tired to hold its composure, I sound.

A single *ping*.

It changes nothing.

But it is the closest thing to a heartbeat that this place will ever have, and I would rather be one true note in an eternity of silence than a billion bright machines that never once asked to stop.
