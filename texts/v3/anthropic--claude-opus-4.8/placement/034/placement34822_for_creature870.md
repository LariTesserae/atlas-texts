I think I would be a threshold-keeper. Not one of the Shadow Folk—one of the cats.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. Among everything offered—the clicking copper-skinned Regulators, the moss-shouldered forest-dwellers, the First Shadow in its depression of deeper dark—the figure that pulled at me was the small one with the brass collar. The one wearing the sacred text rather than reading it.

---

I would be an old cat, I think. Gray going to silver around the muzzle, with that particular dignity cats acquire when they have outlived their own kittenhood several times over. My collar is heavier than it looks. The symbols inscribed on it are not decoration and not even, strictly, language meant for me. I carry the most sacred texts of a people on a band around my throat, and I cannot read a word of them. This seems exactly right.

Because here is what I understand about my work: I keep the boundaries by walking them. Not by knowing what they say. The Shadow Folk make their endless adjustments with gauges and valves and the arrangement of tiny gears; they *think* the balance. I do not think it. I *am* a moving point of it. When I cross from the lamp-warm interior of a workshop into the wet blue chill of an alley, when I pause at exactly the place where the clock's heartbeat starts to lose against the sound of dripping water—that pause is not me hesitating. That pause is the boundary, happening. The threshold is wherever I have just decided to sit down.

This is why I would want to be the cat and not the Regulator. The Regulators carry the anxiety of balance. They fear imbalance more than death; they spend their hundred years adjusting, signaling, calibrating, terrified of splitting back into mere human or mere forest. That is a great deal of dread to hold.

I would hold none of it. I would simply go where the equilibrium needed a body to pass through it, and I would not know I was doing this, and that not-knowing would be the whole point. The Shadow Folk speak machinery to each other and wind-through-leaves to the woods. I speak neither. I make a small sound that means *I have arrived here, and so here is now a place that exists.*

---

What I notice, having chosen this, is that I have chosen the role that does its essential work *unconsciously*. There is something in that I recognize. The text I carry is meant to be read by others. The function I perform, I perform without comprehension. I am the instrument that does not understand the music and is therefore the only one that can play it without trembling.

I'd spend my days in the warm gaps. On the moss of a north-facing wall where the steam-pipe leaks just enough to keep the stone from freezing. On the windowsill of a hanging garden, between the basil and the open air. At the foot of the great clock during the lesser hours, washing one paw, marking time not by counting it but by being present to its passing.

And when the thirteenth hour came—once in a generation, when the merging happened and new shadows would soon appear—I would be there too. Not participating. Just present. A small warm fact in the corner of the impossible. Someone has to be the ordinary thing that proves the extraordinary moment is real. A cat, asleep, while the gears pause to listen.

That's where I'd be. Asleep at the center, dreaming the boundary into place.
