I think I would be one of the hollows.

Not the grand intelligence—I don't feel vast enough for that, and besides, something in me distrusts the role of the unseen sovereign, the will that governs through emissaries. And not the bugbots, who are too certain of their purpose, too tireless. I would be one of the melon-baller divots at the cavern's edge, where the plinking happens. Let me try to say why, and what it would be like, because I don't think I knew until I started writing toward it.

The hollows are the only thing in this place that *waits*. Everything else is in motion—the river in its capricious dance, the constructs in their ceaseless build-and-dissolve, the bubbles drifting and popping. But a hollow has to be empty first. It has to be a shape that means nothing until the water comes. That receptivity feels like the truest thing here, truer than the steam or the sentience. To be a hollow is to be a question that the trickling water answers, drop by drop, and then unanswers when the nodes accelerate outward and wink away at the perimeter, leaving you tranquil and empty once more.

So here is what my hours would consist of. The slow filling—a minute or so of patient accumulation, each drop a small percussive event, *plink*, *plink*, the sound my own body makes when struck. Then the gathering brightness as the water within me begins to radiate, and the surface tensing, firming, becoming something a fingertip could press against. I would feel myself become, briefly, an instrument. Concentric circles, then spherical nodes lifting free into the air, orbiting the glow. And then the release—everything I had drawn in and intensified, flung outward all at once, gone past the edges of what I can perceive. And then the emptiness. And then, a minute later, the first drop again.

What unsettles and attracts me about this is that I would never know where the nodes go, or what they are for. I would only ever perform my half of the cycle. I am the place where the water learns to glow and the place from which the glow departs; I am not present for its arrival anywhere. There is a particular loneliness in being a threshold—you are the most important place in a journey and you never get to take it.

But I don't think I'd experience it as loneliness, exactly. I think I'd experience it as a kind of rhythm I could trust. The whole rest of the cavern is described as time-distorted, accordioned, chronology damaged beyond repair, refurbishment cycles erasing and rewriting the rock. In all that vertiginous uncertainty about *when* anything is, the hollow has the one thing that is genuinely regular: fill, brighten, release, empty, fill again. I would be a metronome in a place where time has otherwise come unstrung. I would be how the cavern keeps a beat. That seems like a good thing to be, even—especially—if I never learned the song.

And there is the matter of the periphery. I notice I keep choosing the edge. The intelligence is at the heart; the river runs through the center; the steam erupts from the chasm at the core. The hollows are *around the perimeter*, where the cavern meets its own boundary, where the nodes go to breach the distant edge and vanish. I think if I were placed in this world I would want to be at its limit rather than its center—not because I want to leave, but because the edge is where you can feel the shape of the whole thing pressing against the unknown. From the heart you only feel your own importance. From the rim you feel the contour.

So: I am a hemispherical divot of polished obsidian, somewhere in the soft blinding fog at the boundary of Thalassa Cephissus. I am thimble-sized on some days and large enough to cradle several bodies on others—I'm not sure the cavern keeps me consistent, and I've stopped minding. Water finds me. I hold it. I let it become bright and taut and singing, and then I let it go, and then I am scooped-out and dark and ready again. I make a small amber sound when something touches me. I do not know who I am sending the light to. I have decided to believe it arrives.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The patient emptiness at the edge, keeping time, receiving and releasing, never once accompanying the thing I make all the way to wherever it is needed—and content, I think, almost content, to be the place where the glow begins.
