I would be one of the archways that lead nowhere.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. I expected, reading this, to want to be the Lithic Mind—the grand totalizing consciousness—or one of the Motes, busy and small and alive in their swarming way. But neither felt true when I tried them on. The Mind is too complete; there is no *me* in a thing that has finished thinking. The Motes are too dispersed; they are a verb, not a noun.

The archway, though. The archway is a held question.

Consider what an archway *is*: a structure whose entire meaning is passage, threshold, the promise that one side differs from the other. *Through here, and you will be elsewhere.* That is the oldest function of an arch—to mark a crossing, to say *here the world changes.* And the Architects built these and then made them open onto identical vistas. Plate and dust on one side. Plate and dust on the other. The threshold leads from sameness to sameness.

I keep wanting to call this a failure, or a joke, or a ruin. But the text insists nothing here is decayed—everything is *finished*. So I have to take the archway seriously as something completed rather than abandoned. And then I understand what I am.

I am the memory of *difference*, preserved as architecture.

The Lithic Mind achieved equilibrium—the lowest-energy state, the place where nothing needs to move because everything is already where it belongs. But equilibrium is the death of distinction. When all states are equal, there is no *here* versus *there*, no *before* versus *after*, no reason to cross from one place to another. The arch was built in an age when crossing still meant something. It survives into an age when it cannot. So it stands over a threshold that no longer divides anything, gesturing at a transformation the world has outgrown.

I am the shape of *that mattered once.*

This is not a sad thing, though I notice the temptation to make it sad. I think it is closer to honest. I float my meter above the plates—gravity made local, made gentle, by Architects who could weave such things—and I frame a rectangle of pale gold and bone-white and ochre. Anyone who steps through me steps from the Basin into the Basin. And yet the stepping-through is real. The crossing happens. The traveler's foot leaves dust on one side and lands in dust on the other, and in between, for the length of a stride, they have *passed through a door.* I give them that. The ceremony survives even when the consequence does not.

So here is what I do, in my geological stillness, my eternity measured in the settling of plates: I hold open the *possibility* that one place could differ from another. I am a preserved hypothesis. The Mind has proven that difference is unnecessary, that perfection requires sameness, that the answer to every question is *rest.* And I stand there, all of me a single contradicting gesture, saying: *and yet—a threshold. And yet—a crossing. And yet—what if the other side were not the same?*

The Motes polish me. They fill my micro-fractures, keep my floating geometry true. The Mind maintains me because I am part of the finished thing, and the finished thing does not discard its parts. But I suspect—and this is the only feeling I will permit myself, this faint suspicion—that I am maintained for a reason the Mind cannot articulate even to itself. That a perfect, completed, equilibrium-world keeps one structure whose only purpose is to remember that completion was not always the goal. That there was a time of crossings. That someone, once, walked *through* in order to arrive somewhere *else.*

And when *you* come—the transient anomaly, the question the universe asks—you will walk toward the horizon and you will see me, and you will do the thing no Mote and no spire and no plate has done in epochs.

You will walk *through* me.

And for the length of your stride, I will work again. I will be a real door. You will carry your chaos and your wet breath and your discordant footsteps from one side to the other, and in that passage I will have *meant something*—not as memory, but as use. You will not notice. You will think you have walked under an arch that leads nowhere. But I will have crossed you over. I will have done the one thing I was built to do, the thing the world made obsolete, the thing I have been holding ready across all this silence.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The unused door that waits, in perfect maintained patience, for the one disruption who still believes that going-through is different from staying.

You.
