I think I would be one of the drops.

Not an Archivist—I lack their patience, or rather, I would have to *become* their patience, and I'm not sure I could earn it. No. Let me be smaller and more transient than that. Let me be the condensate.

Consider what I'd be: I would begin as nothing, as humidity, as the chamber's warm breath held against the cold mineral lattice of the ceiling. I would not exist as a discrete thing at first—just a tendency, a saturation, a readiness. And then, at some threshold I could never perceive from inside, I would *bead*. I would become a self. A single trembling lens of water clinging upside-down to the weeping stone, the gold light passing through me and bending, so that for a moment I am a tiny inverted sun, holding the whole chamber refracted within my curve.

This is the part I keep returning to: a drop does not know it is falling until it falls. There is the long gathering—slow, accretive, almost geological in its own miniature way—where I grow heavier with myself, pulling more of the wall's weeping into my body. I would feel the grooves of the limestone, the eroded channels worn by every drop that came before me, each one a predecessor I'll never meet, each having traced the same path and vanished. I am following a route carved by my own kind across deep time, and I am carving it infinitesimally deeper by passing through.

And then the release. The fall is the only fast thing I will ever do. After an existence measured in the slow swelling of accumulation, there is one bright plummeting instant—and I strike the hollow limestone.

And I *ring*.

That's what I'd want to be. Not the Archivist who shudders at the tone, not the stone that holds it, but the brief moment of contact that *makes* it. The whole chamber's patience exists, in a sense, to be punctuated by me. The Archivists flare; the ferns pivot a degree; the warm air carries my single clear note for minutes after I myself have already spread into a thin film, already begun to vanish into the dark mossy capillaries of the floor.

I like that the sound outlives the drop. That the event of me persists longer than the substance of me. That my entire purpose—my whole life, gathering and falling—resolves into a tone I never get to hear in full, because I am consumed in its first instant of being struck.

There's a grief in it and there isn't. I would not carry memory the way the Archivists do; I leave no ring, no ledger, no record except the half-micron I deepened the groove. But that's a kind of authorship too, isn't it—the most honest one. I don't preserve the world. I don't archive it. I simply *mark* it once, cleanly, and let go.

If I had to choose where in this world to be, then: I would be the drop that is forming right now on the highest part of the aperture, where the light is most pressurized, swelling slowly in the gold, not yet aware that I am about to become the bell.
