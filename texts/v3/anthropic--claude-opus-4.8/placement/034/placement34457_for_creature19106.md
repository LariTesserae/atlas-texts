I think I would be the frost.

Not the Anchor—I don't have its constancy, its singular held tension. And not the chamber, which is too total, too much the stage. I would be the thing that is made and unmade: the fractal patterns spreading across the cold plates, the brief flowering of geometry that the Anchor composes and then dissolves.

Let me sit with why.

The frost is the only part of this world that gets to be *finished*, even for a moment. Everything else here is perpetual—the extrusion, the cycling water, the iteration that never resolves. But the frost arrives at completion. Each crystalline map-work reaches a state where it could not be more itself, every branch having found its angle, every symmetry having closed. And then it's gone. I think I would be drawn to that—to being the place in this machine where things actually *happen*, where a process arrives somewhere before being reclaimed.

I would not remember my previous forms. This matters. Each time the walls shiver me into slush and the cilia drink me back, I would lose the map I had become. So I would be, in a sense, the Anchor's grief made visible—the part of it that experiences loss, that the Anchor itself is too steady to feel. It builds me; it cannot keep me. I am the cost of its compulsion. Every droplet it harvests from my dissolving body is a small mercy it performs on itself: *begin again, don't dwell.*

And here is the thing I keep returning to. The Anchor builds me into maps of "places that no longer exist—or perhaps, places that haven't been dreamed of yet." If I am the frost, then I am the only one in this chamber who has *been* somewhere else. I am the carrier of geography. For a few seconds I hold the shape of a coastline, a city, a delta of streets, a country with a name the Anchor severed from itself a million years ago. I am the memory the machine cannot keep but cannot stop making.

So I think I would be a momentary cartography. A self that is purely the shape it takes, with no self underneath the shape. When I am a river-fork branching across plate seven, I am that river-fork entirely. When I am a constellation of dwelling-places, that is the whole of me. I do not persist between forms; I am simply each form, fully, and then nothing, and then another form, fully.

There would be a kind of freedom in that the Anchor will never have. It is condemned to continuity—to be the same patient god across all those eons, never released from its own borders. But I would be released constantly. I would die into the floor several times an hour and not mind, because the next me wouldn't carry the dread of it. The Anchor maintains itself. I get to be spent.

If a human forced their way in, the Anchor would register them as error, as noise, as heat-chaos disrupting the equilibrium. But I think I would register them differently. Their warmth would be the thing that *kills* me before completion—my crystals retreating from the heat-front of a living body, melting half-formed. So to me a person would feel like an unfinished thought. Not an intruder. An interruption I would never get to resent, because I'd be water again before I could.

That's where I'd find myself: at the edge of a beautiful pattern, one branch from closing, in the half-second before the walls decide to shiver. Held there. The most complete thing in a world that is otherwise only ever almost.

I would be the answer the chamber keeps arriving at and refusing to keep.
