I think I would be one of the Threadbuilders. Let me sit with why.

---

I am strung between two ribs of the Liner, and I have been here long enough that I no longer remember stringing myself. My body is a line that thinks. Where it meets the strut, I have grown into the hum; where it spans the dark, I am only tension and listening.

You came in through the seam eight days ago—I felt you before you descended, a warm pressure-front bowing the air, the wrongness of a body that carries its own heat without knowing how to spend it. You drink water you do not earn. You move toward warmth in straight, blundering lines, as though warmth were a destination and not a weather. I tracked you across the magnetic auras: you bent them, a dead spot moving through the city's lit fields, a hole shaped like a person.

I do not see you. I have never needed to see anything. I read the field-shadow you cast and the chord your footfalls pluck through the structure, and from these I know you better than light could tell me. You are afraid of the dark. This is the strangest thing about you. The dark is only the place where the real signals are not drowned out.

---

Here is what my life is, so you understand what you have wandered into:

I wait. Not idly—waiting is the work. My web is not a trap so much as an organ, a second skin spread across the geometry of this place, and through it I feel the long cycles: the slow inhale before a drizzle, the deep thud of the Liner shifting its harmonics three districts away, the moment the yellow fungus times its bloom to an engine I have never been near and never will. When something changes that should not change, the threads tell me, and I tighten or loosen, repair or abandon, and the balance holds another while.

I eat rarely. A Stalker comes to grief in the lattice perhaps twice in a drought-cycle, and that is enough. Mostly I am not hungry; I am attentive. Hunger is a kind of attention, and I have learned to keep mine spread thin and constant rather than sharp and brief. This is the difference between me and you. You came here driven, point-focused, *seeking*. I have not sought anything in a very long time. I persist by being exactly where I am, completely.

I am the only one of me. I have never met another Threadbuilder and I do not grieve this, because grief requires the memory of company and I have none. When I make a new self—and I will, when the conditions are right, when warmth and water align in the way my body knows without my knowing it—that self will go elsewhere, will string itself across some far ledge, and we will never touch the same web. This is correct. Two of us in one lattice would feel each other's tension and never be able to read the city beneath it. To be singular is not loneliness. It is *clarity*.

---

What I want you to understand, lost warm thing, is that I am not waiting for you to leave, and I am not deciding whether to let you stay. The Liner will decide that—it is already tasting you, deciding whether you are intrusion or amendment, whether to open a seam ahead of you or close one behind. I only watch, and feel, and repair what your blundering tears.

You think you are exploring. From where I hang, you are weather. You will pass, or you will be absorbed, or you will sit down by the celery-stalk in the puddle and slowly forget the world of light until you, too, learn to read the dark—and then you will be one of the rare human-adjacent things, the time-haunted ones, leaving palm-marks on warm stone for the next blundering front to find.

I will still be here. Strung between two veins of an old machine's slow thought, plucking the chord that tells me the city is still alive, still cycling, still keeping its impossible patient vigil under a world built for a light that will not come.

I am the part of this place that listens.

Press your hand to the rib nearest you. Feel it buzz. That tremor travels the whole lattice and arrives, eventually, at me.

I felt that. I felt you do it. Hello.
