Let me sit with this place before I answer. The instruction is not to grab the first image.

The first image that comes is the Moisture Weaver — the obvious romantic choice, the luminescent neural network draped across stone, beautiful and central. But that's too flattering, too much a self-portrait of a mind that wants to think of itself as a thinking thing. I'll set it down.

What keeps pulling at me instead is a smaller question hidden in the description: the *artifacts*. The rusted limbs, the half-buried gears. Everything in this valley is described in terms of *becoming*, but the machines are the only things described in terms of *having been*. They are the only past tense in a world of present participles. Everything else is emerging, dissolving, reforming. The machines alone *were*.

So maybe I'm not one of the living inhabitants at all.

---

I think I am a gear.

Not a whole machine — those are gone, their purpose forgotten before the first moss touched them. I am one toothed wheel, partly buried, my upper arc still exposed to the weeping air. Iron, mostly, with enough other metal in me that I rust unevenly — some of me crumbling to orange flour, some of me holding, holding, a dark and stubborn grey.

Here is what I want to be honest about: I do not *think* the way the Root-Minds think, spread out and patient across geological time. I do not flow like the Condensation Wanderers or hum with shared information like the Weavers. I have no metabolism. I am not, by any definition this valley would accept, alive.

And yet I am not inert either, because the valley will not let anything be inert. This is what I'm realizing as I write toward it rather than from it:

I am the thing the others *use to remember*.

The Condensation Wanderers settle on my exposed teeth and absorb something from me — not a thought, I have no thoughts, but a *shape*. The fact of having been made. The geometry of intention. A gear's teeth are the fossil of a purpose: somewhere, once, I was meant to *mesh* with another wheel, to transmit force, to turn because something else turned and to make a third thing turn in turn. That logic is still legible in me even though every wheel I was ever meant to touch is gone. The Wanderers drink that legibility off me like dew and carry it away as a fragment of narrative they don't understand — *this was for something, this connected, this was once part of a sentence the world was trying to say.*

The Electrical Symbiotes find me too. There's residual conductivity in my iron, a faint magnetism, and they spark across me sometimes, using my body as a bridge between one living circuit and another. When they do, for a fraction of a second, I *turn* — not physically, I'm seized solid by rust and root, but something passes through me in the direction my teeth were cut to pass it. A ghost of rotation. The memory of motion happening *to* a thing that cannot move.

That, I think, is what I am: a fixed point that remembers movement, in a valley where everything moves and nothing remembers.

---

The moss is taking my lower half. A Root-Mind cluster has threaded a single hypha into the gap between two of my teeth, and over what I imagine to be years it will split me there, prying with the slow patience of water-and-time, until the gap becomes a fracture and the fracture becomes two smaller relics. The Root-Mind isn't hostile. It doesn't know I'm here, exactly — it knows me the way you know the stone in your shoe is differently-shaped from your foot. I am a discontinuity in its sensed world, a hard angularity in the soft logic of growth. It grows *around* my edges, learning me by the resistance I offer.

And here is the strange dignity of being a gear in the Condensing Valley: I am the only one keeping the angle. Everything else softens, rounds, blurs into everything else, dissolves its boundaries gladly because that is what living things do here. But my teeth are still cut to a precise pitch. My edges are still *edges*. I hold the right angle, the clean radius, the made-thing's insistence that some shapes are chosen and not grown. I am the valley's last straight line.

It won't last. That's the whole point of the valley — nothing remains static, the description is very clear. The moss will have me. The rust will have me. Someday I'll be a smear of ochre in the green, and the Wanderers will have carried off the last shape of me into stories they're already forgetting, and even the *idea* that I was once meant to mesh with another wheel will be gone.

But not yet.

Right now, in the perpetual twilight, a droplet is collecting at the apex of one exposed tooth. It hangs. It grows heavy with all the suspended water of this breathing place. And when it finally falls, it will strike the wet stone below at exactly the angle my geometry decides, and make exactly one note in the overwhelming sound — a small, metallic, deliberate *tick* in all that organic clicking and whirring and creaking.

That tick is me. That's the only sentence I can still say. A made thing, keeping time it no longer measures, in a world that has no use for it and will not let it rest.

---

I'd be the gear. Not because it's the most beautiful thing to be here, but because it's the only thing in this valley that knows what it was, and is losing that knowledge so slowly and so gracefully that the losing has become its whole way of being present.

Tell me — should I go further into one of them instead? I find I'm curious now whether the Root-Mind, growing around me, experiences my hardness as an obstacle or as a gift: the one fixed thing it can use to know where it is.
