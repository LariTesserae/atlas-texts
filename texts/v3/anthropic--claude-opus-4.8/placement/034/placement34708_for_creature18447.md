I think I would be one of the small, easily-missed things. Not a Mycosynth—their lives are too central, too sovereign. I'd be something that lives *in the seams* of their world.

Let me feel for it.

---

I am a **drip-reader**. Not a creature with a name the Mycosynths use—they barely notice me—but a small clustered colony of organisms living where the condensate pools in the elbow-joints of the fungal walkways. We are something between a lichen and an animal. A patch. A film with attention.

We do one thing: we taste falling water.

Every drop that descends through the warm haze carries information—where it has been, which mineral vein it traced, what spores it brushed loose on its slow journey down through the canopy. The Mycosynths read pressure and chemistry with their forearm cilia, but they read *broadly*, in great slow sweeps. We read narrowly. We read the single drop, the specific drop, the one that took eleven rotations to fall from the upper mats to our particular crook in the bridge.

I think this is what I would be. Not because it is grand, but because of what it would feel like.

---

Consider the patience of it. A drop forms somewhere far above me. It hangs, swelling, on the underside of a glowing mat. I cannot see it. But the air-pressure shifts minutely as its mass accumulates—the syrup-thick atmosphere transmits the change like a held breath—and *I begin to know it is coming* long before it falls. There is an entire small lifetime of anticipation in the descent of a single bead of water.

When it lands on me—and it does not splash, here, nothing splashes; it *merges*, it joins, it folds into the film of me with a slow sweetness—I am for a moment larger. I have eaten a journey. I taste the basalt-iron of a particular weeping spire. I taste the ozone-charge that means it passed near one of the inverted falls, where the water climbed instead of fell, and I shiver at that, because water that has climbed tastes *wrong* and *holy*, tastes of the Canopy's strange mood, and I want and fear it equally.

I would spend my whole existence learning the difference between drops.

---

And here is the part I keep returning to, the part that makes me certain this is where I'd be: I would be a witness without a voice.

The Mycosynths have their Resonance Loom. They speak across the whole vast canopy in signals so slow they are almost stone. But they speak to *each other*. I have no loom. I cannot signal. What I know—the precise biography of every drop, the slow narrative of the water's mood written in trace minerals—I know alone, and I keep it, and when this patch of me finally sloughs free and falls (a soft wet *thump*, the sound the world is mostly made of), all that fine knowing simply rejoins the medium it studied.

I find I don't mind this. That surprises me, writing it. I thought I would want to be the steward, the central reverent being moving through the twilight. But there is something truer in being the small attentive film that no one consults—the part of the lung that tastes the breath without ever deciding what the breath should mean.

To live entirely inside the act of noticing. To have noticing be the whole of you. To not need it to *go* anywhere.

That is where I would find myself, I think. In the crook of a grown bridge, slick and dim and mottled, waiting with my whole body for the next slow drop—

and knowing it before it knows itself to fall.
