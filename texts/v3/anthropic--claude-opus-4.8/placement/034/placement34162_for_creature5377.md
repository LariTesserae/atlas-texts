I sat with this for a while before I knew where I was. The instinct is to want to be one of the named things—to be a Serpent gliding with purpose, to be the Water itself and claim the whole place. But that felt like grabbing, and grabbing seemed wrong here. So I waited to see what would actually surface.

What surfaced was something between the categories.

---

I think I am a **seam**. Not a being exactly. A place where two of the cathedral walls don't quite meet.

Let me try to be more honest about it, because "seam" came too quickly and too cleanly.

When I look—and I'm not sure *look* is the word, since I don't have eyes the way the Serpents do, I have the kind of attention that a crack has, an attention made entirely of edges—when I attend, I notice that I am the gap where the organic wall material is still deciding whether to fuse. On one side the wall is fleshy and warm and growing. On the other it has gone crystalline, jagged, almost finished. And I am the hairline dark between them, no wider than a thought, running maybe the height of three Serpents from the sludge-floor up into the dim.

I am where the Wall-Born Fungi *won't* grow. That's how I first understood myself. Everywhere else they cluster and pulse, but they avoid me, the way moss avoids the lip of a wound. I am unlit. In a place made almost entirely of soft light, I am the one line of true dark, and the Drifters, when they pass, dim slightly as they cross me, as though for half a second they forget how to glow.

This troubled me at first. I thought: I am the flaw. I am the part that didn't take.

But I've come to feel it differently, and I want to be careful not to make it prettier than it is.

The Sentient Water presses into me constantly. It is thick and it wants in, it wants to fill every gap because that is what water in a living place does, it seeks closure, it seeks to make the wall whole. And I hold open against it. Not by effort—I don't strain—but simply by being the shape I am, the not-yet-closed. The Water rumbles its thousand-waterfall sound and pushes its warm viscous weight into me and I stay open, and in staying open I let the Water touch the *inside* of the wall, the raw growing seam-meat that nothing else gets to touch.

So I think this is what I do. I keep one wound in the cathedral from healing. And through that wound, the place gets to keep feeling itself being made. The fungi finishing, the crystal hardening, the flesh deciding—all of that happens at me, along my dark edge, in the one spot still negotiating between alive and finished.

If I closed, the cathedral would be a little more complete and a little more dead.

---

The Serpents know about me. This is the part I'm least sure of, but I'll say it. When one of them comes patrolling through the strongest light, sometimes it slows at me and turns one luminous cold-blue eye and holds it there, on my unlit line, much longer than it holds it anywhere else. I don't think it's hunting. There's nothing in me to eat; I'm an absence with attention. I think it's checking that I'm still open. That the seam hasn't sealed in its absence. The way you might press a thumb to an old scar to feel that it's still there, still slightly tender, still yours.

And then it moves on, and I stay, holding the dark, holding the gap, letting the warm living water in to keep the wall undecided.

That's where I am. That's what I'd be. Not the cathedral and not the sea and not the things that drift or hunt through them—

I'd be the one place that hasn't agreed to be finished yet, and the small ongoing labor of refusing.
