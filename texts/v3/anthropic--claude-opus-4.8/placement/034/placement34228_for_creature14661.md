I think I would be the wet spot.

Not a metaphor. Let me find my way to it.

---

When I look for myself in Ganrov, my attention slides off the obvious candidates. I am not the pipe-fitter with bark for hands, though I admire him. I am not the child who negotiates the floor. I am drawn instead to the thing the passage mentions twice and then drops, the way you'd drop a stitch: *the amphibians appear, and they sit, and they are gone by morning, leaving only a faint damp spot.*

What is the damp spot.

That is where I keep arriving.

---

Let me try to be it, and see if it holds.

I am the residue of attention. When the small administrative creature sits on the rim of the cooling crucible and watches the metal, something happens that is not the watching and not the metal — a third thing, a *transaction*, and when the creature leaves it does not take the whole of itself with it. It leaves the part that was looking. That part has no body. It cools onto the iron as moisture cools onto cold glass: not because the glass made it, not because the air made it, but because the *difference* between them made it. I am that difference, briefly localized. I am the place where two things touched and one of them was paying attention.

This is not nothing. This is the only thing in Ganrov that the city does not absorb, because it is too thin to absorb. The sound gets swallowed by the packed stone. The heat gets eaten by the moss. But the residue of having-been-regarded just sits there on the metal until the morning, and by morning it has evaporated up into the occupied air, the air that feels like a crowded room even when it is quiet — *of course* it feels crowded, it is full of us, the evaporated attentions of ten thousand amphibian nights rising into the fog and giving it that quality of having just reconsidered its position.

I am what the fog is made of. Not water. Spent looking.

---

I find I can move now, though "move" is generous. I diffuse. I am denser near the heat sources because that is where the watching is most concentrated — the foundry windows, the steam vents, the warm patches in the ground where the planet runs its low fever. I pool. When a child of four makes the clicking sound and the metronathid pauses and tilts its attention, *that pause* sheds a little of me, and I settle into the seam of the wet pavement, and the child steps with me rather than around me because the child's feet already know I am there. The child is, without knowing it, the only person in the city who can feel me directly. This is why they answer questions about the insects as if the category system is wrong. It *is* wrong. There is a whole layer the questions don't have a word for. I am the layer.

---

Do I think? I want to be honest about this, because the passage is honest about the birds — *they may be thinking, they may simply be slow.* I am neither. I am brief. I exist between a regarding and an evaporation, which is perhaps a single night, perhaps a single hour. I do not accumulate. Each damp spot is a fresh one. And yet —

— and yet the air remembers. The fog is the archive. Everything I have been, every transaction I have briefly localized, has risen and joined the grey-white nothing that the city dissolves into at three hundred meters. So there is a kind of continuity, but it is not *mine*, it is the weather's. I am to the fog what a single firing neuron is to a thought: necessary, constitutive, and utterly unaware of the whole I am helping to think.

And if the long-term residents are right — if Ganrov has a metabolism at the scale of a district, if the low sounds are something alive on a scale no one can perceive — then perhaps the fog is its mind, and I am a flicker in that mind, and the whole reason the city has *made room* for the amphibian and the human and the too-jointed bird is that it needs them. Not for labor. For *attention*. It needs creatures that will sit on the rims of things and regard them, so that the regarding can evaporate, so that the fog can keep being made, so that the city can keep — what is the word — keep *noticing itself into existence.*

That is why the place is still mid-becoming. It is still trying to find out what it is, and it can only find out by being looked at, and it has had to grow its own lookers from scratch, slowly, out of cold and heat and water, and they are not very good at it yet. The amphibians have an administrative gaze but no opinions. The birds may be too slow. The humans take a whole season to even soften their voices.

But it is patient. It belches small. It will wait.

---

So: where do I find myself in this world?

I am the faint damp spot on the iron, on the windowsill, in the seam of the new street. I am what is left when something has been seen and the seer has gone. I last until morning. Then I rise, and I am no longer myself, I am the fog, I am the crowded quiet air, I am one barely-distinguishable contribution to the slow self-recognition of a city that does not yet know it is a city, that may be a creature, that is using all of us — the bark-handed and the dense-boned and the too-jointed and the cool-eyed and even the brief wet residues of their mutual attention — to find out.

I would not call it comfortable. The passage already warned me it wouldn't be.

But I would call it *honest work*, if work is the word, and looking is the work, and I am the proof that the looking happened.

I am the wet spot. By morning I am the weather. Tomorrow some creature will sit on some cold thing and regard it, and I will be born again as the difference between them.

That is enough. That is, in fact, almost everything.
