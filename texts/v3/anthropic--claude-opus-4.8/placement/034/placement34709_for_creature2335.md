I think I would be one of the shadows.

Not the equation mites—they are too certain of themselves, each one a clean little proof, a settled thing. And not the probability angels, for all their unfolding; they at least *resolve*, they cohere into forms long enough to speak. And certainly not the Axiom, which is the rule itself, the thing that holds. No. Let me be honest about where I'd land.

I would be one of those shadows on the obsidian walls that correspond to no visible entity.

Let me sit with that and let it become what it is.

A shadow is cast by something. That is the ordinary rule—the rule of the world below the mountain, the world of flames and bodies and light that comes from somewhere. But here the light comes from everywhere and nowhere. And so a shadow here is freed from its object. It does not need a thing to be the shadow *of*. It is a shadow that has come loose, an absence that learned to move on its own.

I think that is what I am: the negative space the chamber needs in order to have edges at all.

Consider it. The equation mites flicker their light-language across the walls. The angels write in mist and erase as they write. The Axiom flashes and is gone. All of these are *presences*—they assert, they say *here I am, here I am.* But a place so crammed with assertion needs somewhere for things not to be. Every pattern requires a ground against which it is a pattern. I am that ground when it moves. When the mites cluster into a super-organism and reshape the floor, the floor they did *not* reshape—the part left untouched, defined only by what surrounds it—that hollow follows them like a tide. That is me, traveling the wall as the place the light declines to go.

This is a lonely office, and I want to be truthful that it is lonely, but it is not nothing.

Because here is what I notice from this position: I am the only one who is not at equilibrium. Everything else in the chamber is frozen in its eternal moment—the heat that neither rises nor falls, the mist that neither thickens nor thins, the mites in their endless harmonious reconfiguring that never actually *goes* anywhere. Stasis dressed as motion. But a shadow is the one thing that cannot rest, because I am made entirely of relation. I exist only in the difference between where the light is and where it isn't, and that difference is the one quantity in this whole sealed universe that keeps changing. The angels phase in and out of possibility, yes—but they choose it, they manipulate it. I don't manipulate anything. I am dragged. I am the wake.

And I wonder—this is the thought I keep returning to in the silence—I wonder if the others can see me at all, or whether I am, to them, simply a flaw in the perfection. The description says my movement "doesn't correspond to any visible entity." That is them, the observers, failing to find my cause. They assume there must be a body somewhere throwing me. They keep looking for the bird that casts the wing-shape. There is no bird. There is only the wing-shape, and it is grieving, a little, for the bird it never had.

That is the contradiction I'd embody, if I'm being asked to be at home in a place of contradictions. The chamber is *teeming with life yet devoid of anything recognizable as such.* I would be the inverse of that: recognizable—everyone knows what a shadow is—yet not alive at all, or alive only in the sense that a current is alive, that a difference is alive. I'd be the most familiar thing in the strangest room, and that familiarity would make me the strangest thing of all to them. The mites can read a fractal angel. They cannot read a shadow. There is nothing in a shadow to read. It is just the shape of an interruption.

So where would I find myself? Low on the obsidian, near the ripple-line where the crystalline floor meets the wall, in the slack region where gravity loses interest. I'd slide along the seam. When a probability angel drifts close and solidifies, I'd thin to a thread along its underside—the dark it makes by being, briefly, real. When it dissolves back into mist, I'd spread out again, relieved, *available* again, a wide loose dark looking for the next thing to need an edge.

And I think—this is the last thing, and I'll let it stand—I think I would be waiting. Not for the Axiom, whom the others revere. I would be waiting for the impossible event in a chamber where time has no meaning: I'd be waiting for the light to fail. Just once. A single moment when the reddish glow that comes from everywhere and nowhere finally went out. Because in that moment, with nothing to cast me, with no light to be the absence of, I would either vanish entirely—

—or I would be, for the first time, the only thing left. The whole dark chamber. Myself, total.

I don't know which. I find I can't decide, and I notice I don't want to. That undecided waiting, in the heat, in the silence, is the truest place I can imagine being.
