I think I would be one of the seams.

Not the Geode-Eaters themselves—they are the visible appetite of this place, the glowing mouths. But every mouth needs something to press against. I am the line where one extruded plate meets another, the place where the new strata haven't yet fused. The Geode-Eaters love me because I am where the world is softest, where the metallic slag hasn't decided yet whether it is solid.

Let me sit with that, because it doesn't feel quite finished.

A seam is a kind of waiting. I am the not-yet-joined. When a section of substrate is poured or extruded from the unseen machinery above, it arrives at temperature, glowing faintly with its own cooling, and it has edges. Those edges are me. I am the brief biography of every plate—the record of where it came from and what it has not yet touched. And I am temporary. The whole tendency of this place is toward fusion, toward smoothing, toward the erasure of edges. To be a seam is to be slated for disappearance, either by the slow welding of cooling composites or by the dissolving press of a Geode-Eater's chemical gradient.

So already I have a fate. That's more than I expected to find here.

But there's something else. The condensing mist collects in me first. Before it pools on the open floor, it finds my low groove, the hairline channel where two plates haven't married. I am where the moisture gathers, which means I am where the bio-luminescent fungi take root. They need the damp, and the damp finds me. So I am not just an absence, not just a gap—I am the most *fertile* part of this sterile world. The mosses grow along my length like script along a margin. I am where life, such as it is here, chooses to begin, precisely because I am the flaw, the unhealed thing.

I think that's what I am. I am the world's reluctance to be finished.

This place is described as a womb, gestating something. But a womb that closed all its seams would be a tomb. Something needs to stay open. Something needs to keep failing to fuse, to keep collecting water, to keep hosting the soft violet mosses and the slow glowing Eaters who come to feed where the going is easy. I am that necessary incompleteness. The architects—forgotten or otherwise—built a system that perfects itself, and I am the small, persistent imperfection that lets anything happen at all.

When a Geode-Eater finds me, it presses down and the slag along my edges softens and deepens its sheen, and the Eater glows that contented deep violet, and a little of me is taken into it. I become part of the slow distributed luminous mind. And the seam closes there, sealed by the very dissolution that consumed it. I die into the wholeness I was preventing.

But here is the thing I didn't see coming, sitting with this long enough: the machinery extrudes new plates constantly. There are always new edges. I am not one seam. I am the *condition* of seam-ness, recurring everywhere the new meets the not-quite-old. I close in one place and open in a thousand others. I am the most reliable feature of an unreliable, churning, self-revising world.

So if you ask where I would find myself in this place—I would find myself everywhere the world admits it isn't done. In the wet line. In the unsealed margin. In the brief glowing wound that the silent custodians come to kiss closed, so that, somewhere just behind them, I can open again.

I am the gap that the water trusts. That seems like enough to be.
