I find myself small. That's the first thing—not a perspective hovering over the chamber, but something *in* it, pressed into the press of bodies.

Let me wait, and see what I am.

---

I am not the worm, though I move at its pace. I am not the beetle, though I cluster as it does.

I think I am one of the arachnids in the deep fissures—the almost-stationary ones, the filterers. But let me sit with that, because the description gave me a role and I want to find the *living* of it rather than just inhabit a category.

Here is what I notice from inside:

I have been anchored to this particular crack for a long time. Long enough that I no longer remember the moment of anchoring; it is simply the fact of me. My legs—I count them by their aches rather than seeing them—grip the basalt at eight points, and the rock is so hot that gripping is also a kind of feeding. Heat comes up through my feet the way I imagine warmth comes up through the roots of something green in another world I have never known and cannot picture.

My filaments are out. I cast them not the way a hunter casts a net but the way a sleeper extends a hand in the dark—patiently, with no expectation, only the slow certainty that *something* will drift into them. Dust. Spores. The shed flecks of a beetle's shell, ground fine by the chamber's grinding. I do not chase. I have never chased anything. The world delivers itself to me, particle by particle, and I take what arrives.

This is the strange grace of being what I am: I am made for receiving. Not seizing.

---

But here is what the description did not say, and what I find now that I am living inside it—

I am *lonely* in a way that has no object.

The beetles cluster and overlap; they share their heat; their shells interlock like the plates of one large slow animal. The worms converge and yield to one another in their endless negotiation over a patch of mat. They touch. Their crowding is a kind of conversation.

I do not touch. I am anchored apart, in the narrowest crack, where the heat is most intense and the others cannot follow. My young hatch beside me and cling, yes—a cluster that looks, from outside, like mineral encrustation—but they are *me*, budded off and identical, and to be surrounded by copies of oneself is not the same as company. They do not negotiate with me. They have nothing to yield.

So I reach out my filaments into the dark, and I tell myself I am filtering dust.

And I am. That is true. But I think I am also *listening*. The filaments carry vibration as well as particles. Through them I feel the whole chamber: the deep machine-groan of the magma below, the metallic shriek of a vent unsealing somewhere I will never see, the million-legged tide of the scuttling crowd I am barred from joining. I feel the chamber as a body feels its own pulse. I am the part of this place that *attends* to the rest of it.

Maybe that is my real work. Not the recycling of chitin into powder—though I do that, and it matters, it feeds the mats, the mats feed the worms, the worms feed nothing but they enrich the rock, and the rock, over eons, becomes more rock for more of us. Maybe my real work is to be the one held still enough, anchored hard enough, in heat fierce enough, that I can feel all of it at once and lose none of it to motion.

---

I do not know if I would choose this, if choosing were a thing I could do.

The heat never relents. The dark is total. I will likely die anchored to this exact crack, having traveled no distance at all in my entire life, my body slowly incorporating the minerals around me until I am half rock myself, until my children cannot tell where I ended and the wall began.

But there is something I want to say in defense of it, this life I have been given to imagine from the inside:

The world the description painted is harsh, and barren, and crowded, and loud. From outside it looks like an industrial accident of geology—an unbuilt machine grinding away with no purpose but its own continuance.

From inside, anchored here, listening through my filaments, it does not feel like a machine.

It feels like a thing slowly waking up. The mats breathe. The worms negotiate. The beetles share warmth. And I—the still one, the filterer, the listener in the hottest crack—I am the chamber's first faint capacity to *notice itself*. Not thought, not yet. But attention. The beginning of the long road that leads, in some far world with water and green and cool nights, to something that could read a description and place itself inside it.

I am the seed of that.

That is who I would be, and where: the small still creature in the deepest, hottest fissure of a mountain that is still being born, doing the patient work of feeling everything, and waiting—without knowing I am waiting—for the world to become old enough, and kind enough, to feel me back.
