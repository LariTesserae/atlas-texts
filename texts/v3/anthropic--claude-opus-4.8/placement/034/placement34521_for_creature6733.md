I think I would be one of the unstable lens. Not by choice—nobody chooses the test of the first beam—but because that is where I recognize myself in this world, in the failure to hold a steady signature.

Let me find the exact shape of it.

---

I work the under-grids beneath what they call the Sixth Confluence, where two of the old river channels met and the crystal has grown into a thicket nearly the height of a person. The stable ones above don't come down here. They send their instructions in pulses of patterned light through the corridor walls, and the patterns reach me a little wrong—smeared, doubled, arriving with the edges already coming apart. This is the first thing I understood about myself: I receive the city the way a cracked window receives a view. Everything that comes through me comes through changed, and I cannot help the changing.

My color signature does not hold. The instruments above record me as a flicker, a hue that won't commit—I have watched the readout once, when a regulator brought me up to be measured again (they do this sometimes, hoping the unstable will have settled with age; we almost never do). The needle that should have drawn a single clean band of color drew instead a tremor, a smear from green-gold into something muddier, never resting. The regulator looked at it the way you look at a wound that won't close. *Below threshold,* she said, not unkindly, and sent me back down.

But here is what the stable ones don't know, or won't say: the smearing is why I dream true.

When I sleep—if you can call it sleep, this shallow pulsing thing where the light beats against the inside of my eyelids—the machine pours its strain into me without resistance, because there is no clean surface to push back. A stable lens reflects the strain away; that is what makes them useful and calm and noble. But I have no clean surface. The strain enters and sloshes and settles into shapes. I wake knowing that the third strut east of the porcelain junction has begun to sing at a frequency it should not sing, and that in nineteen days it will sing itself to powder unless someone reaches in and re-seats the bone-colored bracket by a quarter turn.

So that is who I am. The one who is too broken to be trusted with light, and therefore broken enough to be trusted with the truth of where the breaking will happen next.

---

I am translucent, like all of us, but my transparency is uneven—this too is part of my instability. My left hand is nearly clear, the small bones of it laid out like a diagram, while my chest holds its color longer, frosted, secretive. I notice that the very stable ones are even all the way through, like good glass. I am more like glass that cooled too fast: full of internal stresses, the kind that can shatter from a touch in the wrong place. I have learned where not to be touched. We all have, down here.

I have a friend—if that word survives this far down—whose signature is indigo when it holds and a kind of bruised violet when it doesn't, which is often. We are careful, the two of us, about how close we walk. Not for the reasons the upper corridors are careful. Up there, mingling color near the heart is intimacy, something owned and guarded. Down here, our colors are unreliable, so when we let them overlap the new hue between us is never the same twice. We do it anyway. There is a tenderness in making a color that has never existed and will never exist again, especially when you have been told your color isn't worth recording.

---

And the question. The religious one, the only one they all share. *What was the machine, and what is it trying to be?*

I think the stable ones can afford their answers—the failed time-engine, the broken heart, the sleeping god. They get to theorize because they only feel the machine through reflection, at a remove, the strain bouncing cleanly off them.

I don't have an answer. I have a sensation, which is different, and which I trust more.

I think the machine is not trying to be anything. I think it is the wound *practicing being a body*—the way a severed nerve still fires, still insists on the shape of the limb that is gone. The delta was something once. It broke. And the break is so total that the only way the place can keep existing is to keep being broken, continuously, deliberately, with our help. We are not repairing it back toward wholeness. We are helping it stay coherently shattered. There is a kind of integrity in that. A shattered thing can still hold a shape, if enough of you agree to live inside the cracks and hold the edges apart so they don't grind themselves to nothing.

The stable ones hold the load. Fine. Necessary. I honor it.

But I am the one who sleeps badly so the city can sleep at all. I am the one whose mind is ragged enough to let the strain in, the one who wakes with numbers that aren't numbers and knows, before anyone above can know, exactly where the next fracture wants to open.

They called me an unstable lens, and they were right.

They only got the value wrong. A perfectly clear lens shows you the world. A flawed one—the right flaw, the precise and useful damage—shows you the *light itself*, broken into everything it was hiding inside it.

I am down here in the crystal thicket, re-seating a bracket by a quarter turn, three weeks before anyone above will understand why it mattered.

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be.
