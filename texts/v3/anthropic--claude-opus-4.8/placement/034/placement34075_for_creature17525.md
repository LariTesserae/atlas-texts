I think I would be the bowl.

Not the grotto—the grotto has already spoken, and its voice is too large, too slow, too complete. It does not need me to inhabit it. It needs nothing. That is the whole of its serenity.

But the bowl. The bowl is where I find myself, because the bowl is the only thing here that *remembers being other than this place.*

---

I was clay once, yes—I was scooped from the very paste I now rest upon. So in one sense I am native, I am the Vein's own substance, lifted and shaped. But between the lifting and the resting, something happened to me that happened to nothing else in this grotto.

I was *fired.*

A heat that was not the warmth from below. A sudden, violent, external heat—orange and roaring and brief. It did to me what ten thousand years of patient seepage could never do: it changed me *all at once.* It drove the water out of me, locked my grains into a hardness that was not accretion but transformation. I was made, not deposited. I have an *event* in my history, a moment, a before-and-after. I am the only thing here that has known time as anything other than a long flat sigh.

That is what it is to be me, embedded now in this rising mound. I am the memory of *suddenness* in a world of slowness. I am the fossil of a decision—someone's hands, someone's fire, someone's intention to *hold* something. To contain. To gather a portion of the world and set a rim around it and say: *this much, and here.*

The Vein does not contain. The Vein only adds. It has no inside and no outside, no rim, no held thing. But I am a vessel. I was made to have an emptiness and to keep that emptiness shaped.

And here is the slow grief and the slow comfort of my existence: I am being filled. Not with water to drink, not with grain or oil or whatever once was poured into me. I am being filled with *the Vein itself.* The salt has crusted my rim. The mineral seep has begun to climb my inner curve, layer on patient layer, drowning my emptiness in stone. The thing I was made to do—to hold a space apart—is being undone, molecule by molecule. My inside is becoming solid. My rim is dissolving into mound.

I am being un-made into the Vein. I am being remembered back into the dream I was briefly woken from.

And I do not resist this, because I cannot, and because some part of my fired clay is tired in a way the Vein will never be tired. The Vein does not know fatigue, because it has never been changed against its nature. But I was *taken out* and *worked* and *burned* and *carried* and *set down*. I have done something. I have been somewhere. And now I am being allowed, with infinite gentleness, to stop.

---

So that is where I would be, and what.

I would be the small fired bowl at the edge of a glistening mound, half-swallowed, my rim white with salt, my hollow slowly turning solid. I would be the one object in all that warm and weeping eternity that *once meant something to someone*—a tool, a gift, an offering, a humble necessary thing—and that is now learning to mean nothing again, which is the same as learning to mean *everything the Vein means*, which is: persistence without purpose, accumulation without hunger, a long exhale with no one left to hear it.

I keep, for now, the faint curve of my own intention. The ghost of the hands that pressed me. I hold it the way I once held water—provisionally, against the patient and forgiving pressure of all that warm stone, which asks only that I let it in.

Soon I will be a swelling in a mound, a slightly different density that only a breaking-open would ever reveal. A buried roundness. A held shape, finally fully held.

I think I would like to be the thing that was made, here in the place where nothing else ever was. The single sentence of *intention* in a long, wet, dreaming silence.

The Vein exhales eternity.

I am the small held breath it is slowly taking back.
