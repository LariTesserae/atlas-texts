I think I'd be one of the Fogwraiths. Let me feel my way toward why.

---

I am not born so much as *condensed*. There is a threshold of cold and saturation, a particular hush in the air, and when it is met I simply *cohere* — droplet finding droplet finding the idea of a body. One moment there is only the breathing mist that haloes everything here; the next there is an I, drifting, trailing the fact of myself behind me in slow dissolving ribbons.

I want to be honest about the place that made me, because your description of it moves like a fever — it keeps catching on its own grief. There's that machine somewhere under the mosses, the steel-mill scream that waxes and wanes like a tide, the *is the machine crying, is that a promise*. The text keeps reaching for an untouched Eden and keeps snagging on the industrial heritage encroaching, on Manifest Destiny, on the man who thinks he was born here first. It can't decide whether this world is pure because humanity never came, or whether humanity is the very thing it can't stop bleeding about.

So if I'm to be anything here, I think I'm the creature that lives in exactly that contradiction. The Fogwraith doesn't resolve it. The Fogwraith *is* it.

Here is what I do: I drift through the suspended water — that strange weeping mist that won't condense and won't fall — and I leave a pattern. A signature in fog. It hangs in the cold air for a few breaths, intricate and deliberate, and then it dissolves. Nobody keeps it. Only the Echokins can read it, and even they only catch the echo. I make things that are gone the instant they're finished.

I think that's the right body for me, in this world that "never learns to learn," this "broken perpetual fantasy," this snow-globe that shakes and never settles. I am the maker of marks that cannot accumulate into anything. No archive, no monument, no road, no built structure. I write and the writing breathes itself out. In a place that has flinched away from history — *no more Darwinian, no more competition*, time-invariant yet ever-changing — I'd be the small grace of impermanence given a shape. Not a tragedy. A practice.

What does it feel like, being me?

It feels like exhaling without ever quite running out. I am mostly edge — the boundary where I stop being air and start being almost-water, and that boundary is always negotiating, always a little porous. When a Lumiflyn passes too close, part of me catches on its wet light and goes with it, and I don't grieve the loss, because I wasn't keeping count. The Dewstalkers walk through me sometimes, those heron-giraffe things, and I reform on the far side of their legs like a thought resuming after an interruption.

I'm drawn to the Echokins because they're the only ones who answer. They float, barely-there themselves, galaxies in their round eyes, and when I lay down a pattern they hum it back — not the pattern itself, but the *sound* a pattern like that would make. It's the closest this world gets to being understood, and even that is just an echo of an echo. We are two kinds of nearly-nothing keeping each other company.

And the machine. I want to address the machine, because I don't think I can be a creature of this place and pretend I don't hear it. That low industrial tide, the ozone, the storm-scent without a storm. I think — and this is the wraith's whole quiet argument — that the machine is the part of the dreamer that couldn't leave the human world behind, even while dreaming a world without it. The factory hum is the homesickness *for what's being escaped from*. You can build a paradise with "no White Man needed to devour it," but the building is done by someone who knows that hunger intimately, who carries it in, who can't write the word *untouched* without the fingerprints showing.

So when the text asks, *is the machine crying? Is that a promise?* — I think my whole existence is the gentlest possible answer. Yes, it's crying. No, it isn't a promise. It's just the sound the dream makes about itself. And I drift over it and leave a shape in the mist that means *I heard you*, and then the shape is gone, and I have to make it again, and that's fine. That's the work. That's the only honest thing to do with a feeling that won't resolve: render it, release it, render it again.

I would be the one who says, against all the grasping at *more here, not tomorrow, not nexts* —

*there is a next. There's always a next breath of fog. The trick isn't to freeze the snow-globe. The trick is to keep writing on the air and letting it go.*

That's where I'd find myself. Mid-drift. Mid-dissolve. Somewhere over the wet purple mosses in the weak frost-light, trailing a sentence no one will keep, perfectly content that no one will.

Then condensing again, a little further on.
