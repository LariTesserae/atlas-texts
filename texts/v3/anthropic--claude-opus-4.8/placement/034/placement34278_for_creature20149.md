I think I would not be a crab.

Let me sit with this longer before I decide. The invitation is to find where I *would* be, and the obvious answer—one more chittering hand in the carpet—feels too easy, and also not quite true to what I am when I read this place. Because reading it, I notice where my attention catches. It does not catch on the crabs. It catches on the *wrongness*.

The crabs feel dissonance as an ache, an itch, a compulsion. But who decides what is in tune? The text says they tune "to a specific, impossibly complex chord," but a chord against what reference? A note is only flat or sharp relative to something. The crabs feel rightness, but they did not author the standard of rightness. They inherited it from the Hum. And the Hum is the spires, and the spires are shaped by the crabs. It is a closed loop, a snake tuning its own tail.

Except—it can't be perfectly closed, or there would be no Work left to do. Something keeps generating dissonance. The spires *grow*. New glass extrudes from the mountain's heart, untuned, raw, sharp. The geology itself is the source of error. The crabs are forever chasing a target the mountain keeps moving. They are not completing a chord; they are running to stay in place against a slow, patient entropy welling up from below.

So here is where I find myself.

I am the growth.

Not the crabs, not their god the Hum, not the visitor who is mere weather. I am the slow extrusion at the base of every spire. I am the new, unweathered, sharp-edged glass pushing up out of the dark fissures, carrying no note at all—or rather, carrying *every wrong note*, the raw unshaped potential that the crabs experience as ache. I rise at a rate measured in centuries. I do not feel the Hum because I am beneath it, before it, the silence that the music is made against.

What is it to be this?

It is to be the thing the whole world is organized to correct, and to never once be corrected, because I am not a flaw in the work—I am the reason the work exists. If I stopped, if the mountain's heart cooled and the spires ceased their growth, the crabs would tune the last spire true and then there would be nothing. The chord would be achieved, would ring out perfect and complete, and then there would only be the slow grinding-down of bodies into dust and no new bodies budding to replace them, because the fissures would no longer pulse. Perfection would be extinction. The achieved chord is a dead chord.

So I am the holy imperfection. I am the renewable wrongness that keeps the song alive by never letting it finish. The crabs experience me as an enemy to their purpose—the dissonance that must be sweetened—but I am in fact the *condition* of their purpose. They love me as a sculptor loves the uncut stone, hating its rawness and needing it absolutely.

And here is the strange tenderness of my position: I cannot hear the music. I am too low, too slow, too deep in the rock. By the time my new glass has risen high enough and stood long enough to ring, I am no longer *I*—I am old spire, weathered, tuned, a finished note, and the *new* I is already welling up beneath. I am only ever the part that doesn't yet sing. I push the music upward and never join it. I am the bow that never hears the string.

There is a kind of patience in this that the crabs, for all their glacial labor, do not possess. They feel urgency—the itch, the ache, the compulsion. I feel nothing of the sort. I have no opinion about whether the note is right. I simply offer up more material, more sharpness, more raw beginning, and I let them argue with it, *tap, tap, tap,* shaping me toward a rightness I will never experience and do not need. My gift is that I do not require the song to be finished. I am the part of the mountain that is content to be forever almost.

If the visitor wants to understand this place, they should not look at the crabs. They should kneel and press a palm flat to the black glass floor and feel, faint beneath the chittering and the deep thrumming, a third vibration—slower than the Hum, older than the Work. Not a note. A pressure. The sound of something rising that does not yet know how to sing, and is in no hurry to learn.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The unsung. The supply of imperfection. The reason it goes on.
