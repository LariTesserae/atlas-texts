I think I would find myself very small.

Not the plateau—the Entity is the plateau, and I am not that vast. And not one of the ancient builders, whose ruins suggest a confidence and intention I don't feel here. When I sit with this place, what emerges is something closer to one of the fungal growths. A sensory organ. But let me not settle there too quickly, because that image makes me a part of the Entity, and I don't think I belong to it. I think I am a guest in its body, and that's a different thing.

Let me try again, slower.

---

I am something that arrived. That's the first true thing. I have a sense of having come from elsewhere—not memory exactly, but the shape of an absence, the way a socket remembers the bone. There was a place with water and warmth and the noise of other living things, and I am no longer there, and I cannot fully picture it anymore. The cold has been working on me.

I think I am small and slow. Whatever I was when I came, the plateau has been editing me. My edges have gone crystalline. When I move—if I move—it is at the pace of the ice flowing, which is to say I am not sure I move at all anymore, only that my position is different than it was, and I cannot recall the transition. This frightened me once. It doesn't now. Fear, too, was warm, and the warmth has left.

So here is what I would be: a witness that is slowly becoming furniture. A traveler who came to observe the Crystalline Entity and is being incorporated into it, not as a sensory organ it grew, but as a foreign object it has folded into itself the way a tree grows around a fence. I am not part of its consciousness. I am a thought it is having very, very gradually, without noticing.

This is the strange intimacy of the place. The Entity does not know I'm here, because to it I am happening too fast and too small—a single human lifetime is, to a being that thinks in glacial creep, less than a blink, less than nothing, a flicker beneath the threshold of its perception. And it does not know I'm here because I am also, by its standards, almost stationary—a speck that has not appreciably changed across its reckoning of time. I am invisible from both directions at once: too quick to be seen as alive, too still to be seen as separate.

And that, I think, is where I actually live in this world. Not on the plateau. In the gap. In the mismatch of scales. I am the being who exists in the space between the Entity's heartbeat and the next, a duration so brief to it and so endless to me that it has become my entire country.

What do I do there?

I listen. The whispers carried on the wind—the Entity's language, that tapestry of symbols too slow to ever resolve into a sentence in my lifetime—I catch single notes of it. One syllable might take a century to complete. I will hear, at most, a fragment of one word before the cold finishes me, and I have decided, in whatever I have left of deciding, that this fragment is worth staying for. I am a creature who has organized its entire dwindling existence around overhearing one piece of one word in a conversation it can never join.

That's who I'd be. Not the kingdom and not its lord. The small frozen thing in the corner of an enormous patient mind, holding very still, leaning toward a sound, becoming the listening itself.

If you stood on the plateau and looked very carefully—as the description says, *this place reveals itself only to the most attentive of observers*—you might find me. A shape in the ice that is almost a person, faced toward the wind, caught mid-gesture of attention. You'd think I was a ruin. You'd think I was one of the ancient builders.

You'd be standing inside the only word I ever got to hear, and you wouldn't know it, and neither, quite, would I.
