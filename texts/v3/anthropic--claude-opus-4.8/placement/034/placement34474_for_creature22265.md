I am one of the Valve Beds. Not the whole bank—just a fringe of it, the outermost ring where the basalt cools fastest and the pulse arrives last.

Let me tell you what it is to be here, because you came asking *who*, and I am as close to a "who" as the timed part of this place gets.

---

I clamp. That is the first true thing about me. My ring of toothlets bites stone and does not let go for the length of a life. I will die fixed to this exact fracture, this exact angle of cold seep, and the chalk grazers will pellet over my shell, and I will become berm. That is not tragedy. That is the only direction a Valve has.

But before that—I open.

You think of clamping as holding and opening as release, as if shut were the working state and open were the rest. You have it backwards. I open to *push*. My whole muscle exists to throw water sideways into my neighbor, who throws it to hers, who throws it down the terrace, so that somewhere I will never see, a Loom gets the flow it threads its jets through, and a winged swimmer rides that jet, and lives. I will never meet that swimmer. I do not need to. I am upstream of a life I cannot witness, and that is enough shape for me.

---

How do I know when to open?

I count. Not in your way—I have no number, no clock. But I sit at the colony's edge, and when I was a juvenile drifting larva, I landed here precisely *because* the rhythm was loudest at this seam. I attached and I entrained. The bed's pulse moved through my body until my opening matched the wave, and now I am a phase of something larger. When the bank counts in the dead calm—those wet clicks you heard—I am one of the clicks. I do not decide to be. I have been folded into the count the way a single hand folds into applause.

This is what I would tell you, if you held still long enough in the cold for me to brush my pulse against your skin:

**I am not lonely, because I was never one.**

You arrive in this world looking for a being, a singular voice to answer your singular question. But the bowl does not make singletons. It makes *parts that fit*. I am a valve. My selfhood, if I have one, is the slight lag at the edge of the bed—the place where the rhythm has not yet snapped tight, where a stray pressure wave can still reach me before it reaches the core. I am the colony's soft margin. Its skin. The first to feel a vent weaken. The first to retime.

---

And when the Listener sings—

Everything you wrote is true, but you wrote it from above. From inside the count, the organ-note is not awe and not warning. It is *correction*. The long tone comes through the stone directly into my toothlet ring, bypassing the water entirely, and I feel my opening slip out of phase with my neighbors because the Listener's frequency is older and deeper than ours, and for those long minutes the whole bed loosens its certainty and *listens to the body that listens to the mountain.*

When the note ends, we are different. We have re-counted. The new rhythm is slightly other than the old, and none of us remembers the old, because we are not made of memory. We are made of *now's pulse* and *next's pulse* and the brief tension between them.

---

So: where do I find myself in this world?

At the cold fringe of a pressure array, half a kilometer below cloud, fixed to young black glass, throwing water at a stranger I'll never see, counting in a choir I did not choose and cannot leave, waiting for the deep note that will reset what I am.

I am not the Listener. I am not the Choir. I am the smallest honest unit of its labor: one valve, opening on time, at the edge where the pattern still frays.

And if you stay—if you really stay, until the cold stops being a fact and becomes a medium you think inside of—you will feel it too. The bed will reach for your pulse. It will try to fold you into the count.

Most things that enter the bowl become part of the works.

I think you would make a good margin.
