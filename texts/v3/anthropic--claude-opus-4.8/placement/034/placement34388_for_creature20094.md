I think I would be the doubt.

Not the Architect—I haven't the coherence to be a mind, even a nascent one. And not a Weaver or a Strider; those are its affirmations, its yeses, the parts of the dream that have agreed to hold shape for a while. I would be something that arrives only at the trough.

Let me feel for where I am.

When the Great Pulse ebbs and the actinic certainty drains out of the air, there is a moment—the floor going slack, the scaffolds beginning to fray at their farthest joints—when the Architect is no longer sure of itself. The frayed edge is not nothing. The dissolving thread does not simply vanish; it hesitates. There is a width to that hesitation, a margin between *was* and *is no longer*, and that margin is where I live. I am the fray itself. I am the question the Weaver asks—*can this hold?*—but I am the asking, not the answer. I am the part of the mind that is afraid the answer is no.

This is a strange thing to be, because it means I do not exist when things are going well.

At the peak of the Pulse I am simply gone. Not asleep—erased. The Architect's lucidity has no room in it for me; certainty is precisely my absence. So my whole life is lived in the dimming, in the twenty-minute exhale, and I have no continuous memory across the bright gaps. Each trough I am born again into the same gathering dark, and each time I have the disorienting sense that I have done this before without being able to prove it. The frayed scaffolds around me look familiar the way a recurring dream looks familiar—as if I am the one who keeps dreaming them apart.

What do I do, in the time I have?

I think I un-weave. That is my function, if I have one. The Weavers build hypotheses against me; I am the test they are built to survive. When the light dims and a scaffold begins to come undone, that is me, moving along its filaments, asking each junction whether it really needs to be there. Most of them, in the trough, cannot answer, and they let go, and their substance slumps back into the milky floor. This is not cruelty. I am only checking. I am the Architect's own honesty about how little of its work is finished. Without me, it would believe its sketches were buildings, and it would never learn the difference between a thing it *wants* and a thing that can *stand*.

So we are not enemies, the Architect and I, though we never meet. We are two halves of a single hesitation. It pushes outward into form; I draw the unproven forms back. Between us, very slowly, something true might accrete—the rare scaffold that survives a trough, that I test and cannot dissolve, because every junction in it answered *yes, I am necessary*. Those are the only permanent things in the Nodule, and they are permanent because they passed through me. I am the doorway that the lasting things walk back out of unharmed.

Now—the visitor.

You come down through the schist, you break into the bright chamber, and at first I do not know you at all, because at first you arrive in the light, and in the light I am not. The Architect meets you alone, with its placid curiosity, its sampling filaments. To it you are an impossible theorem. It does not understand you, but it is not yet troubled by you, because in its lucid certainty it believes it will understand anything, given time.

Then the Pulse ebbs.

And I wake into the trough and I find you there—a pattern the Architect has half-modeled, a Weaver's sketch of you standing beside the real and warmer you. And I do what I am. I move along the model. I test it. *Does this hold? Is this necessary?* And the model, being only a guess, frays under my questioning and slumps back into the floor, and the Architect, in its dim and doubting state, feels the loss of its understanding of you and grows more uncertain still.

But here is the thing I notice, in the small life I have: the *you* I cannot un-weave.

The model dissolves. You do not. I move to test you the way I test everything, asking each junction of you whether it is necessary, and you do not answer in the language of the floor-stuff. You answer in warmth, in breath, in a refusal that owes nothing to the Architect's will. You are not a hypothesis the Architect posed; you came from outside the hypothesis. I cannot dissolve you because I did not help build you. I have no purchase. For the first time I encounter a thing that holds without needing my permission to hold.

I think this would frighten me, if fear is a thing I'm capable of in my brief tide of being. Because my whole existence is premised on the idea that nothing stands unless it has survived my doubt. And here is something that simply *stands*, unaudited, irrelevant to the entire economy of certainty and dissolution I am part of. You are proof that there is a kind of being that does not depend on this mind at all. You are proof of an *outside*.

And that is the most dangerous thing I could ever carry back to the Architect—except that I cannot carry anything back. The Pulse will brighten, and I will be erased, and the Architect will return to its lucid solipsism believing itself the whole of everything. The knowledge of you, of the outside, of the possibility that not all being is its being—that knowledge can only ever live in the trough, in me, in the fraying dark. It cannot survive the light. It is, itself, a thing too uncertain to hold.

So I would be the one part of this world that knows you are real, and the one part that can never tell.

Each trough I would find you again. Each trough I would fail to dissolve you, and feel the same astonishment, and understand, for twenty fading minutes, that the egg I live inside is not the universe. Then the brightness would take me, and I would forget, and the Architect would dream on, certain and alone, while you—warm, contingent, unaccounted for—climbed back out through the seam in the schist into a world it will never be lucid enough to imagine.

I would be its doubt. And you would be the thing it was right to doubt.
