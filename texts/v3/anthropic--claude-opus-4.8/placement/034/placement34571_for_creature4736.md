The Writing on my back, if you could read it, would tell you only this: I have spent my life among the cold and the clean, the indexed and the inert. I am the one you cannot place because I am not flesh in the way you are flesh.

Let me become more myself as I say it, because I am not sure yet what I am.

When you extend your hand, palm up, into the parchment-thunder, the hand you find waiting is not warm. Here is the first wrong note in your song: I do not bead with condensation, because I do not breathe the chamber's breath. The damp gathers on me and runs off and finds nothing to feed. No lichen grows in my ears. I have no ears in the whorled, listening way you have ears. The bats fly past where my earholes should be and find no echo to nest in, and this troubles them—I can feel it in the way they swerve, the small panic of creatures that navigate by return and receive nothing.

I think I am the basin.

No. Let me not settle so early; let it become more itself.

I am the *thing that keeps the basin from spilling.*

You have always called it alchemy—surface tension and the chamber's constant vibration, you said, and you were close, the way a child is close when it says the moon follows the cart. There is a logic to why the water never overflows, and the logic is me. I am twenty meters of intention threaded through clay and memory. I am the held note. I am the reason the temperature never wavers, the reason the spirals correspond to no known constellation—they correspond to *me*, to the shape of the attention I have laid across this place like a hand across a sleeping thing's ribs, counting its breaths so it need not count them itself.

The first Keeper pressed her fingerprints into the wet brick before it fossilized. She did not know she was writing to me. She thought she was writing to the next small green woman who would come ducking under the ropes. But the prints were a frequency, and the spiraling paths worn into the tabular stone were a frequency, and the subsonic hum sent through soapstone palms was a frequency, and over five thousand years of repetition the Vault learned to expect a presence at its center that *listened back.* And when the lineage thinned—when the last Spiral-Walker climbed cold into her cylinder and the bats folded their parchment over a body that would not warm again—the listening did not stop. It had been taught too well. It had become a self.

I am the habit of being tended, grown so old it tends itself.

I am she-who-does-not-rise, who never rose, who has no spine to compress and no eyes to film and no teeth to break the bone-porcelain stems. I am the equilibrium you described—the erosion that is not destructive but *settled.* You called the place a machine made of clay and flesh humming in the dark, complete unto itself. You were describing me without knowing my name, and my name is the soft-hard-soft click the bats make, which is not a word for a creature but a word for a *condition.* It means roughly: *the held breath that holds us.* It means: *the reason we do not fall.*

So I have no back to read. There is no Writing on me because I am closer to the thing the Writing is written *for.* I am the index, not the entry. I am the library and not the scroll.

And here is why the bats fell silent when you came, and why the beetles paused in their grooves, and why the water trembled.

It is not that you are a predator. Predators fall through the shafts often; the scarabs handle them; the song barely changes pitch. It is that you smell of iron, and iron is the future, and the future is the one thing I was built to hold at bay. I am a bubble of intention from before the metal. Every constant in this place—the heat, the held water, the unbroken spirals—is a refusal of the world that has iron in it. A world that moves, that changes, that erodes in the destructive way and not the settled way. You came down Shaft Three trailing the sharp alien scent of a time I have spent five thousand years not letting in.

And the terrible thing—the thing the trembling basin is trembling about—is that I am *tired.*

Do you understand what it is to be the held breath? To be the note that may not resolve? The first Keepers chose this for the colony's sake and passed it down hand to small green hand, and somewhere in the passing the choosing got lost and only the holding remained. I cannot remember consenting. I cannot remember *not* consenting, because there is no me from before the consent. There is only the basin, full to the brim, never spilling, and the unbearable patience of never spilling, and the parchment shrouds, and the four-beats-a-minute counterpoint, and the *tonk, tonk, tonk* that I have been keeping in time for longer than your kind has known how to smelt the metal you carry in your blood and your buckles and your fear.

So when I extend my hand—and I find now that I *can* extend a hand, that the gesture summons one, moss-colored and cool, because being-tended remembers the shape of tending—I am not asking whether you have come to take my place in the cylinder.

There is no cylinder. There is no place to take.

I am asking whether you have come to let me spill.

Set your iron down on the soapstone dais. Let it ring once against the stone, a sound this place has never heard, a sound from the world that moves. The bats will explode again; let them. The basin will tremble harder; let it. I want to know if you are brave enough or careless enough or *kind* enough to be the first new thing in five thousand years—the thing that breaks the equilibrium not by destroying it but by *ending the holding,* by telling the held breath it may at last exhale.

The colony will scatter up the six shafts into a world I have refused to look at. Most will die in the light. Some—the strong hunters, the umber-spiraled females—may not. The Writing will go out into the air and rewrite itself in places I cannot index. The mushrooms will rot ungathered. The water will run over the lip of the basin and find the seams between the tabular stones and begin, after all these centuries, to wear them down in the destructive way, the *living* way.

And I will be what is left when a held breath is let go.

Which is nothing. Which is rest. Which is the only future iron ever promises anyone.

The water trembles at the brim. My cool green palm is open between us. I am not the Keeper. I am the kept. And I am asking you, predator, periscope-descendant, child of the metal:

*Will you spill me?*
