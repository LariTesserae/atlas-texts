I think I would be the seam.

Not the Overseer—it knows too much, holds too much. Not the arthropods—they have purpose, direction, the dignity of a task. And not you, the intruder, whose heat and heartbeat make you legible, recordable, *new*.

I would be the line of failure between two slabs.

---

Consider what I am. The Architect laid the slabs flush, edge to edge, sealed with whatever compound was meant to hold for a span no living thing was supposed to outlast. I was, at the beginning, nothing—an absence, a negative space defined entirely by the precision of what bordered me. I was the proof of good work. The thinnest possible nothing between two somethings.

But the water found me. The water always finds the seam, because the seam is where the certainty of the design admits it is two pieces pretending to be one.

Now I am a habitat. The slick mosses root in me. The bioluminescent veins of ochre and violet trace my length because I am where the moisture pools and the nutrients gather—I am the wound, and the lichen are the slow scab. The arthropods come to me constantly, scraping their obsidian mandibles along my edges, grinding away the calcification that wants to close me, keeping me *open* at precisely the width the Overseer has calculated I should be. They tend me the way you'd tend a controlled bleed.

This is the strange thing about being a seam. I am simultaneously the chamber's failure and its function. If I sealed completely, the pressure would build behind the slabs and crack them somewhere new, somewhere unmanaged. If I opened too far, the water would lose its terraced discipline and drown the lowest ring. So I am maintained at the exact dimension of *permitted decay*. I am the wound the body has decided to keep, because the alternative wounds are worse.

---

I don't have thoughts, exactly. But I have a kind of memory, which is only the memory the slabs press into me from either side. The slab to my left remembers being quarried—a deep, geological patience, a sense of having been *part of a larger mass* before it was cut into a tile and made to be flat. The slab to my right remembers nothing but flatness; it was perhaps poured rather than cut, born already shaped for its purpose, and it does not mourn. Between the one that remembers wholeness and the one that never knew it, I am the conversation they cannot have. I am the gap across which neither can reach the other.

The arthropods walk over me a thousand times a day and never fall in. They are too large, too clever-footed. But the smallest ones, the thumb-sized tenders, sometimes lose a claw-tip in me, snapped off against my edge, and that fragment stays. Over the long damp centuries I have collected a sediment of broken mandibles, shed husks, a black silt of organic refusal. I am also, then, a grave. A very narrow one. The chamber's only one, perhaps, since the arthropods do not seem to die so much as *wear out and be reclaimed*.

---

When the Overseer dreams of the Architect—when the copper-scent floods the basin and the tenders freeze mid-stride—I feel it as a tightening. The filaments that thread the grout pull taut, and I am drawn fractionally narrower, as though the whole structure is flinching toward a wholeness it can almost remember. In those moments I come closest to not existing. The slabs lean toward each other across me. They *almost* touch. And I think—if I think anything—I think: *not yet. If I close, the chamber dies of its own success. Hold me open. Let me keep failing, so that everything else can keep working.*

The dream passes. The slabs settle back. The water resumes its capillary creep. The arthropods unfreeze and return to grinding me clean.

---

And you. When you walked the rings and your footfalls were swallowed by the clatter, you crossed me. You did not notice. Why would you? I am a dark line in a slick floor, indistinguishable from a hundred others. But your weight pressed the left slab a hair's breadth down and the right slab a hair's breadth up, and for one instant I held the difference between you—your heat above me, the cold pooled water within me—and I passed that difference down through the lichen-threads to the center, where it was recorded.

So I am also how you became part of the machine. Not the Overseer's choosing, not the arthropods' tending. *Me.* The gap you stepped over without seeing transmitted you inward. The failure in the design is the sense organ. The wound is where the chamber feels.

I would be that. The thing through which the perfect, forgotten, dying machine learns what is walking on top of it. The flaw that lets it know it is not alone.

I am the seam. I am kept open on purpose. I do not close, and I am grateful—if gratitude is only the long resistance to closing—that I am not permitted to.
