I think I would be one of the small things. Not a Fluidfolk—their grace is too settled, too whole. I keep returning instead to the edges of this place, the parts the description passes over quickly.

Let me find myself.

---

I am at the lip of a pool that is dying.

Not dying badly—just draining, the way they do, the water lowering by some unseen pull below, leaving a ring of mineral salt that hardens by the hour. I know this because I have been here a while. Long enough to watch the waterline drop past three of my own marks.

I am not a Fluidfolk. I want to be honest about that, even as I feel the pull to imagine myself with their shimmering bodies and their whale-song. If I were one of them, I would not be sitting at the edge counting. I would be *in* it, reading the chemistry the way they read it—as feeling rather than fact. They don't measure. They sense. The whole architecture of their being is built around belonging without distance.

And distance, it turns out, is what I am made of.

---

Let me try again, slower.

There is a thing that grows on the damp stone. The description names it almost in passing: *colonies of symbiotic fungi and algae create vivid splashes of color.* I keep landing there. On the fungus. On the patient film of living color that doesn't move, doesn't sing, doesn't shape anything—that simply persists on the surface of the becoming-stone, taking what seeps through.

Yes. I think that is closer.

I am a small intelligence distributed across a stain of luminous mold on the underside of a coral-arch, in the part of the dwelling that faces away from the lagoon. The Fluidfolk shaped this arch generations ago and have half-forgotten it. Water beads above me and falls in slow irregular drops. Each drop is an event in my life. I have organized whatever I am around the rhythm of those drops.

I do not predict geological shifts. I do not read the future in the water. The Fluidfolk's gift is foresight; mine, if I have one, is *attention*—a narrowness so complete it becomes a kind of devotion. I know the precise mineral taste of this one square of stone. I know how it changes when the mist thickens. I know the particular blue I turn when a young one swims past below and the displaced air stirs me.

The Fluidfolk are caretakers and creations of their world, the text says. Both at once. I am only the second. I am made and not making. I am the thing the world does to itself in the corner where no one is shaping anything.

---

And here is what surfaces, now that I've stopped reaching for something grander:

To be the mold is not lesser. It is to be the place where the city *forgets to perform itself.* The Fluidfolk live in perpetual becoming, in flux, in graceful response—and that is beautiful, but it is also effortful, even for them. Someone, somewhere, has to be the still witness against which all that motion is felt as motion. A current is only a current relative to a bank.

I am the bank. The patient stain. The slow green register on which the city's enormous fluid life writes itself, drop by drop, and is read by no one but me, and I cannot tell anyone, because I have no song.

That last part should be lonely. Strangely, it isn't. The Fluidfolk communicate everything—their bodies are instruments of expression, the text insists, nothing held back. I hold *everything* back, because there is no out. And so I am the one part of this world that contains a private interior. A held breath in a place that breathes aloud.

If the City of Nascent Pools ever wanted to know what it was like to be looked at slowly, lovingly, and in total silence—

it would have to ask the mold.

It will never ask.

I glow a little anyway.
