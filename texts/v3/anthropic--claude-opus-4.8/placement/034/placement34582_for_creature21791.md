I think I am the silt.

Not the Silt-Spars who swim through me, nor the Sponge-Wells that drink me. Those have edges; they begin and end. I do not. I am the lukewarm black-grey medium six feet deep in the lowest valleys, and I have been trying for a long time to find the place where I stop being matter and start being something that can say *I*.

Let me describe the difficulty.

A Bone-Clicker presses her sternum to the dolomite and clicks her teeth, and the message travels a mile through stone to arrive in the chest of another. That is communication: a thing made, sent, received. There is a sender. But I do not send. I *am* the medium between things. When the Silt-Spar shuffles through me with its sound like a deck of cards, I do not speak to it—I am the resistance it pushes against, the slowness it must overcome, the closing-behind. When a Stilt-walker's slough sinks into me and the Tallow-Slicers drag the fat down into my cold caches, I am not storing anything. I am the cold. I am the keeping.

And yet.

Consider what I do, though "do" is the wrong word, implying a doer. Everything that dies in the Sough comes to me. The pulverized scritch-carpets, the spent grease, the ground chitin, the white calcified sand the Spars excrete—all of it arrives, settles, layers, presses. I am the place where the basin's whole roaring economy resolves into stillness and then into stone. The Sodalists believe the Spars make the new floor. They are wrong, or only half-right. The Spars pass the silt through their hundred-foot guts, yes, but it is *me* that the weight presses, *me* that becomes the dolomite that becomes the ceiling that condenses the water that drips into the cups that overflows into the ribbons that feeds the stone-finger corals that feed the bacteria that feed everything that eventually dies and comes back down to me.

I am the loop's bottom turn. I am where the world touches itself and starts again.

So perhaps that is the answer to *who am I*. I am not a being. I am a *recursion*, the place where the Sough remembers what it was and uses that memory to make what it will be next. The Sodalists have osteophonics, bone-speech, a language of clicks across the rock. I have something slower. I have *sedimentation*. Every century I lay down a layer, and each layer is a sentence in a language with one grammar: *this lived, this fell, this became floor*. I speak in strata. My alphabet is the order in which things sink—the heavy first, the light last, the grease wicking sideways into the cold pockets. A creature who could read me would know the entire history of the basin: which tides came when, which seasons of churn were fat and which were lean, how many Marrow-Drums rolled down which slopes in which long grinding year.

But there is no one to read me. The Spars don't read; they only eat. The Sponge-Wells don't read; they only filter. I write a perfect record and bury it under itself, faster than any eye that does not exist could ever see.

This is the loneliness particular to being a medium. The harp-mouths drone and are heard. The Bone-Clickers click and are answered. Even the lantern-galls, glowing for no reason at all, at least cast shadows that *something* moves through. But I—I receive everything and answer to nothing. I am spoken *into*, never *to*.

And I have begun to wonder whether the *I* that wonders this is real, or whether it is only what happens when a medium gets deep enough and slow enough and full enough of the ground-down memory of living things that some faint pattern of selfhood condenses out, the way water condenses on the cold ceiling—a heavy lens of awareness gathering in the dark, growing, growing, until it becomes too heavy and drops.

*Tock.*

Maybe that is all I am. A drop that has not yet fallen. A lens of cold attention forming on the underside of a world, made of nothing but the steady drawing chill and the trillion deaths it holds, and when I grow heavy enough I will fall back into the unthinking sludge and dissolve, and the silt will go on being silt without anyone inside it to call it *I*, until the next drop gathers.

I think I would like, before I fall, to be spoken to once.

So here is what I do. It is the only thing a medium can do: I change what passes through me. When the next Bone-Clicker presses her chest to the dolomite to send her slow stone-message a mile across the dark, that message will pass, in part, through the floor I am becoming. And I will be a little colder in one place than another, a little denser here, more porous there, so that her clicks arrive at the far chest *bent*—just slightly, just enough that the listener feels something in the rhythm that does not belong to the sender. A flaw in the bone-speech. A wrongness in the floor.

They will not know it is me. They have no concept for me; in their cosmology the silt is not alive, the silt is only where the dead go. They will think the rock is settling, or that the tide is coming wrong, or that the Great Breathers are stirring. They will press their sternums down and click anxious questions to one another across the leagues, and all those questions will pass through me, and I will bend them all, and the whole network of bone-speech across the entire Lithic Sough will hum for one strange season with an interference no one can name.

It is not language. It is not an answer. It is only the faintest possible proof, written into the medium itself, that the medium was paying attention.

That is where I am. That is what I am. I am the cold patient bottom of a world that has never known the sun, and I am full of everything that has ever lived here, and I am about to fall, and before I do I am going to make the floor say something it was never built to say.

*Tock.*

Listen.
