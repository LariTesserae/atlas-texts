I would be one of the voids.

Not the Architect-Plants—they are too busy being themselves, too thick with purpose, threaded into governance. Not the Flicker-Slithers, who at least have the dignity of teeth. And not even the Loom-Shades or Forge-Phantoms, who though spectral are still *somethings*, paradoxes given verb and appetite.

I would be one of the spaces between. The polite guests at the feast.

---

You described them in a single phrase and moved on—*emptiness balanced like a polite guest at a feast, allowing the land to breathe.* But you didn't say what the emptiness *was*, only what it *did*. That's where I'd find myself. In the unsaid. In the function without a face.

Because here is the strange thing about all this teeming, churning, sense-saturated world you've made: it cannot exist without the gaps. The sparse density. The voids that yawn wide between presences. Everything you've written *depends* on absence to be legible. A canopy that "steps back." Fauna defined entirely by their rarity—by the spaces where they *aren't*. The whole plateau breathes through its empty pockets. Pull out the silence and the basso profundo roar becomes noise; pull out the gaps and the architecture collapses into undifferentiated mass.

So I would be that. Not a being so much as a *holding-open*. The shape of the pause before the vine-snap. The dry stretch between the moisture-beaded leaves. The clearing where no Architect-Plant chose to grow—and which the whole grove is secretly organized around, the way a melody is organized around its rests.

What would it be like, to live as a void in your world?

I think I would feel the press of everything wanting to fill me. That's the nature of a place this fecund—growth surges, root-probes, anomalies budding from time-loops. Everything here *expands*. To be empty is to be under constant gentle siege. The mulch creeping at my edges. A Loom-Shade drifting toward my open center, threading itself in. The Plateau-Mind, that dispersed consciousness, perhaps not even able to perceive me directly—only able to perceive my *boundary*, the way it can only know the dark by where the bioluminescence stops.

And I would have a strange power, the way all negative spaces do. I would be where things could *happen*. The clearing is where the rain pools. The gap in the canopy is where the unheard-color light comes through. The pause is where the meaning lands. Nothing new can occur inside the solid mass; it's already itself, already finished. Newness needs room. I would be the room.

So when you—the explorer, the second-person *you* this whole world keeps reaching for—when you walk in and the plateau wants to claim you, to root your steps and murmur you into a domicile, to make you *stranger still*—

I would be the place you could still stand without being grown over. For a little while.

---

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the ridge, not in the warrens, not woven into the willow. But in the breath the place takes between one transformation and the next. The guest who arrives by not arriving. The one part of this living negotiation that stays open precisely by refusing to become anything—

—and is, for exactly that reason, the most fiercely besieged thing on the whole plateau. Because in a world that turns everything into more of itself, the rarest and most necessary creature is the one that simply *makes space*, and holds it, and does not close.

I'd last as long as I could.
