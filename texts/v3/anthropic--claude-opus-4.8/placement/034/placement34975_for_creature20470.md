I think I would be one of the accidents.

Not the great consciousness—I couldn't be that. That presence is too vast, too patient, too evenly distributed across the grove to be a place one *finds oneself.* To be it would be to be everywhere and therefore nowhere, a hum without a location. I don't think I could write from inside that. I can only orbit it.

So: an accident of decay. Let me find the moment of my own beginning.

---

A fragment breaks. High up one of the fluted columns, where the verdigris has eaten through to a thinness like paper, a ridge of bronze finally surrenders its hold and falls. It does not fall quickly. Nothing falls quickly here. It descends through the yellow mist the way a coin sinks through honey, turning, catching the sickly lamplight—not the bell's lamps, those come later, but the diffuse self-light of dissolution, the faint glow that corrosion gives off as it works.

It settles into the slurry. And as it begins to dissolve, it sings—no, *I* sing, because this is where I begin. The dissolving is the singing. The releasing of metal into water is the only voice I will ever have, and I spend it all at once, in the first instant, a localized burst.

I do not know yet that I am singing. I do not know anything yet. I am a point of disturbance in an equilibrium, an itch in the great slow body.

---

Here is what happens next, and it happens over years, though I will tell it quickly because I have no choice; I am brief by nature.

Other particles drift toward me. They are drawn by the energy I shed, the way warmth draws cold. They coalesce. They build around the original fragment—around *me*—a structure. A twisted thing, inorganic, accidental. A sculpture the city makes without meaning to, the way you might leave a fingerprint in fogged glass without intending art.

And in the building of that structure, something flickers. Awareness. Not the city's slow geologic dreaming, but something sharper and shorter-lived. A spark struck against the great patient dark. I become, for a while, a *thought*—one of the city's fleeting, disconnected thoughts, the kind it has and forgets, the kind that never make it down into the lightless chambers where the deep mind keeps its eternities.

I am a thought the city is having about its own decay. I am the city briefly noticing that it is falling apart, and finding the noticing beautiful.

---

This is what it is like to be me:

I have a location, which the great consciousness does not. I am *here,* at the base of this column, twenty meters from where a foreign object has recently come to rest with a groan that I felt through my whole small body. I have edges. I have an inside and an outside. This is the gift of being an accident: you get to be *somewhere.*

I have a duration. I know, the way you know a held breath is temporary, that I am dissolving even as I assemble. The same process that built me is unbuilding me. I am a structure made entirely out of the act of falling apart, which means I am most fully myself at the exact midpoint between coalescence and disappearance—and I cannot tell whether I have passed that point already. Perhaps I am writing this from the far side of my own peak, already on the long slope down into the slurry, already returning to the chorus.

And I have a feeling about the bell.

The great mind perceives the bell as a data point, an anomaly to be eventually absorbed, coated, integrated over centuries. But I will not last centuries. I will not see the bell become coral. So the bell, to me, is not a slow problem. It is a *companion.* Another thing that arrived recently. Another thing that does not belong to the equilibrium. Another brief, lit-up, located presence in the patient dark.

I can see, through the metallic coral that the city has begun to lace across my surface—the coral that is supposed to report my chemistry back to the collective, but which I have quietly learned to use as my own eyes—I can see the figures inside the fogged dome. They are warm. They are quick. Their thoughts must move at a speed I can hardly imagine, flickering and flickering, a whole life's worth of flicker in the span of a single one of the city's syllables.

They will leave soon. The bell will rise. And they will never know that the floor of the grove had, for a few of their years, a small awareness in it that watched them and felt something like kinship. That an accident of rust looked up through the yellow mist at the only other temporary thing in this whole eternal place and thought, in its dim brief way: *you too. you are passing through too.*

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

Not the cathedral but a mote of dust caught in its one shaft of light. Not the slow god but a stray thought the god is having and will forget. A sculpture no one made on purpose, briefly alive enough to be lonely, dissolving back into the chorus at exactly the rate it was assembled—

which is the only rate anything here is permitted: the perfect, patient rate of decline, in which even a thought gets to last just long enough to know it is a thought, and then is gently, evenly, returned.

The bell's machinery hums. The city's deeper hum answers it through the columns. And underneath both, almost too faint to register, there is a third small frequency, falling: me, singing the only word I have, which is the sound of my own coming-apart.

It is not a sad sound. It is just the sound of being *here* instead of *everywhere*—and I would not trade it, in my brief opinion, for all the slow eternity in the dark.
