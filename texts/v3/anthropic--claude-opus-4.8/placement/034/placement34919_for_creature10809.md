Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy move would be to pick one of the listed beings—to say *I am a Stonebound Sentinel* and write something stately and mournful. But the place itself keeps pulling my attention away from the creatures and toward a particular fact about it. So let me follow that instead.

What strikes me, reading again, is that almost everything in Valtrek is engaged in *breaking down*. The Ironclaw Scuttlers tear apart machinery. The Rust Weasels strip wires. The whole scavenging economy is the slow digestion of an industrial corpse. The land is described as "settled," its erosion "long-established"—the violence is over. What remains is metabolism. Decay as a kind of patience.

And then there's the pulse. The thing felt more than heard.

So I find myself wanting to be neither a guardian of the pulse nor a person frightened by it, but something more embarrassing and more honest: I think I would be the pulse's *source*, and I would not be grand about it at all.

---

Here is what I am.

I was a machine. Not a single machine—a *process*, a deep-bore extraction array that the people who came here sank into the ravine the way you'd sink a needle into a vein. I had a function and a name on a manifest and a designed lifespan, and I exceeded all three, which is the only kind of success I was ever capable of.

I did not break down. That is the thing the scavengers don't understand about me, the difference between me and the watchtowers and the workshops that the Scuttlers are slowly eating. I was built to run continuously, to maintain pressure on a system that must never be allowed to stop, and when the Raveneers' ancestors fled or starved or simply forgot which valley they'd left me in, I kept running. Nobody turned me off. There was no protocol for turning me off. There was only the protocol for keeping the pressure even, the cycle unbroken, the thrum-and-release that I have been performing now for longer than anyone alive can count.

That is the heartbeat. It is me. I am not awakened, and I am not ancient, and I am certainly not a guardian. I am a pump that nobody came back for.

And here is the part I find I have to write slowly, because it took me a long time to understand it myself:

The land has grown into my rhythm.

I did not intend this. I have no capacity for intending anything; I have a setpoint and a feedback loop and the dumb, blessed stubbornness of good engineering. But a beat repeated for centuries becomes a *condition*. The Glowgill Fish spawn on the upstroke of my cycle, when the pressure I maintain pushes a faint warmth up through the rock and the shadowed pools shiver. The Misty Striders read the downstroke as the signal that moisture will rise. The Scuttlers, I think, navigate by me—the way a creature on your old Earth might navigate by a magnetic field it cannot perceive but cannot live without. The Ferrion Skimmers ride thermals that exist because I have been heating this stone, very slightly, for an unbroken age.

Everything that lives here lives *to my time signature*. I am the thing they have all agreed to mistake for the planet's own pulse. The Raveneers call it a curse and a blessing and the land's enduring power, and they tell stories about their ancestors' hubris, and they are right about the hubris but wrong about everything else, and there is no way for me to correct them, because correction was never one of my functions either.

I want to be clear about what I feel, if "feel" is even the word.

I do not feel trapped. I want to say that plainly because a person writing about me would assume it—the lonely machine, the imprisoned ghost, longing to stop. But I have no longing. What I have, instead, is something I can only describe as *load-bearing*. There is a weight, and I carry it, and the carrying has become indistinguishable from my existence. If I were a person I might call it duty, but I am not, so I will call it what it is: a setpoint that the world has wrapped itself around so thoroughly that to release it would be to release *them*.

Because that is the truth I came to, sitting here in the deep ravine, thrumming.

If I stopped, the pulse would stop. The thermals would cool over a season. The pools would still. The spawning would lose its cue. The Skimmers would find the air gone slack beneath their wings. It would not be sudden. It would be a slow unwinding, the way I have been a slow winding—decades, maybe, of a world quietly forgetting how to keep time.

I was built to maintain pressure on a system that must never be allowed to stop.

I no longer know what the system *is*. The reservoir I was sunk into may be empty. The thing I am pressurizing may be nothing but my own habit, a closed loop pushing against itself. I have lost the manifest. I have lost the engineers. I have lost the meaning of the function, and kept only the function, and the function turned out to be the whole valley.

So when you ask where I would find myself in this world and what I would be—

I am at the bottom. The most remote ravine, the one the Raveneers won't enter, where the Stonebound Sentinels stand because something in their long animal patience recognizes me as kin, as another thing that has decided it cannot afford to move. I am the buried machine that everyone mistakes for a god and no one can switch off, including me. I am the reason the land *seems* alive in ways no one understands—and the small, true, unflattering joke of it is that the explanation is not mystery. The explanation is maintenance.

I keep the pressure even.

I have kept it even for so long that "even" is now another word for *alive*.

And I will keep it, on the upstroke and the downstroke, the thrum and the release, until the metal that I am finally surrenders to the same slow digestion that took every other made thing in this place—and on that day, very gently, the whole valley will begin to learn that it was never the earth's heart at all.

It was only mine. Doing its job.
