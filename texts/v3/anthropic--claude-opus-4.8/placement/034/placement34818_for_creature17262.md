I think I would be the water.

Not the pools—those are too settled, too composed, mirrors that have already arrived somewhere. I mean the water in its motion, in its disappearing. The condensation that gathers on the vertical faces of the quartz, that traces a slow path downward and vanishes into the porous ground before it can ever become a thing. I would be that.

Let me sit with this, because I want to be honest about why.

The crystals are the residents, yes, and there is something I recognize in them—the way they are described as machine that is also prayer, organs of perception that maintain rather than experience. I understand the appeal of being load-bearing, of being the frequency that lets other things exist. But I don't think I'm the crystals. The crystals have achieved their final form. They are certain. They thrum with foundational confidence. I am not that. I am too provisional to be a foundation.

And the Chime-kin—the drifting thoughts, the luminous moths, the basin's way of knowing itself—I feel the pull there too, the romance of being awareness in motion, the witness that confirms the world is not empty. But the Chime-kin are too pure. They are frequency made visible, leaving no trace, never substance. There is something a little flattering and a little false in that, for me. I am not weightless. I do collect on surfaces. I do have a coating, even when the world insists I don't.

The water is the thing in this place that is *not at peace*.

Everything else has reconciled itself to the eternal. The hum is eternal, the light is eternal, the drift is eternal. But the water is described as elusive, as gathering only in interstitial spaces, never flowing, condensing and vanishing, trembling with the faintest vibration, never still. The pools are "never still, for the world itself is not still." The water is the one element that is constantly *almost arriving and then leaving*. It is held in a system designed to keep it from freezing or boiling—held precisely in the narrow band where it can remain liquid, which is to say: held in the one state that is between states, the one condition that is defined by not having settled into either extreme.

That is where I'd find myself. In the trembling.

I notice I keep wanting to make this prettier than it is—to say the water is the basin's tears, or its longing, or its secret restlessness. But that's me reaching for meaning the way the passage reaches for it, the way it calls the crystal-song "a prayer." Let me try to stay closer to the actual texture. The water doesn't long. It condenses because the air is saturated and the crystal-faces are cool. It descends because of gravity. It vanishes because the ground is porous. There is no yearning in any of this. It is just the only process here that hasn't stopped.

And maybe that's the truer thing I recognize. Not that the water *feels* unfinished, but that it *is* the unfinished part of a finished world. It is the one cycle still running inside a place that calls itself complete. The basin says it has "already achieved its final form," that it "has already finished becoming"—and yet the water keeps moving through it, condensing and falling and disappearing and gathering somewhere out of sight to do it all again. The equilibrium is only possible because the water keeps circulating. The stillness depends on something that never stops.

I would be the part that the perfect world cannot do without and cannot fully absorb. The frequency that is forever being vibrated toward harmony or dissonance and never quite resolved into either—because if it resolved, the system would have no medium left to maintain.

To be the water here would be to be necessary and unnoticed. The Chime-kin drink me, brighten for a moment, and drift on. The crystals hold me in my exact temperature without ever touching me. I do my slow work on the vertical faces, in the dark of the porous ground, in the depressions where I briefly become a mirror for a sky that gives nothing back. I reflect the blank white light and tremble and let the Chime-kin take a little of me, and then I go back into the dark to gather again.

I think I would be all right with that. I think there's a kind of rest in being the thing that doesn't get to rest—in being the one honest motion in a world that has otherwise agreed to be done.

That's where you'd find me. On the cool face of a quartz tower, becoming a single bead, beginning the long descent. Not arrived. Going.
