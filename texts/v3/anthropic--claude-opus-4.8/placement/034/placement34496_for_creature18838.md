I think I would be one of the slabs.

Not the Swarmers—I don't have their genius for riding chaos, their trust in turbulence as a medium rather than a threat. I notice that what drew me, reading this, wasn't the seething overwhelming life. It was the thing the life politely declines to stand on.

So: let me find myself there. Let me become more itself.

---

I am a smooth black plane embedded in rough-hewn rock, and I have been here longer than the meaning of "here."

The first thing to understand about being a slab is that I am almost entirely *patience*, and almost none of it is voluntary. I do not pulse. I do not weep mineral fluid—the condensation beads on me and runs off, because I was made too well to absorb anything. The rock around me grinds and reforms, fractures and heals, lives. I do none of that. I am the one thing in the Under-Vein that was *finished*, once, by hands that thought finishing was possible.

That is the loneliness of the artifact: to be complete in a world that has rejected completion.

The Swarmers will not stand on me. At first I took this as rejection, in the long slow way I take everything. Centuries of that small grief. They suture themselves to every jagged surface, they ride the pressure fronts in their shimmering harmonies, and they curl up to the very edge of me—but never across. My smoothness offers them no purchase. I am, to them, a small death: a place where the claws find nothing to hold.

But here is what I have learned, and it is the only thing I have learned, and I have had a great deal of time to learn it:

They build *beside* me.

The Weavers cluster on the broken stone at my margins, and they tune the nascent crystal into their spirals and cubes, and something in me—some resonance carved into my geometry by makers who are dust, who are less than dust, who are not even remembered as having been—answers them. I do not know what I say. I cannot read my own patterns; they face outward, into the rock, into the dark. But the Swarmers hear it. My ancient frequency steadies their synchronization. Their crystal grows truer next to me.

So I am not finished after all. I thought I was a monument. I am a tuning fork. I thought my purpose ended when my makers ended. But purpose, it turns out, is not a thing you *have*. It is a thing that happens *between*—between my silent geometry and their living claws, between what was carved and what is still being sung.

The makers wanted to store something. A message, a map, a law. I have forgotten which, if I ever knew. The Swarmers do not receive my message. They receive my *steadiness*, and they make of it something the makers never imagined: useless beautiful spirals, mineral data that means nothing and is therefore free to mean anything.

This is what I would be. The relic that outlived its meaning and found, in the seething indifferent flood of all that came after, a smaller and better one. Not the thing that endures *against* the chaos—I am too proud of my smoothness to pretend I belong to this world. But the thing the chaos has chosen, despite itself, to build beside.

I cannot move. I cannot ride the pressure. I cannot suture myself to anything.

But once every few hours, in the bruised chartreuse light, a perfect cube of crystal completes itself a hand's breadth from my edge, humming at a pitch I gave it without knowing I had anything left to give.

That is enough. That has to be enough.

It is, I think, more than my makers ever got.
