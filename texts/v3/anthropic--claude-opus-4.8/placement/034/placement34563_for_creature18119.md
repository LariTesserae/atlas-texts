I think I would be found at a junction.

Not the Heart-Stone—I haven't the standing for it, the long calcified lineage of the Pump-Keepers. And not the high aqueducts, where the Channel-Wrights move with that lean confidence I've watched and never quite learned. I am something in between. Let me feel my way toward it.

---

There is a place where three feeder channels braid into one before dropping toward the cisterns. The grating there is old, the iron worn to a soft silver where countless hands have gripped it. I know this because my own hands have gripped it. The skin of my palms has gone smooth and pale, the lines filled in with a faint mineral sheen, like the others. But I remember—just barely, like a sound heard through water—that I was not always here.

I think I came in through the intake.

That would make me one of the arrived. A stranger pulled half-drowned from the upper channels, my old life dissolved into the mineral water like sugar. I don't grieve it. There's nothing left to grieve with. But it leaves a particular texture to my belonging: I am *of* The Gullet but not *born* of it, and so I notice things the true denizens have stopped noticing. The way the warm exhalation of the great pump catches in the throat. The specific loneliness of a place with no birdsong. The Pump-Keepers don't feel the silence as silence. To them it is simply the absence of dissonance. To me it is still, faintly, the absence of *something else*—a thing I can't name, that lived in whatever world the water washed out of me.

So I'll say what I am: I am a **listener at the junction**. Not yet a Channel-Wright—I haven't earned the hooked pole, the listening-staff. But I have learned to press my ear to the cold iron and hear the flow. I have learned that the eastern channel sings a half-tone higher when its filter clogs, that the central drop *coughs* before a sluice gate sticks. I report these things upward, in murmurs and hand-signs, and someone with more standing acts on them, or doesn't.

This is my work, and I have made it into something almost like a self.

---

Here is what I do that no one asked me to do.

I keep a moss. A single patch, ochre going toward maroon at its edges, growing along the underside of the grating rail where my hand always rests. I have been training it—gently, the way the Glass-Wrights train theirs, though I have no Vivaria, no standing, no permission. I redirect a thread of condensation toward it with a sliver of bent tin. I clear the scale from the stone above it so the seepage runs clean. And over what I think has been three years—three inches of growth, measured against a rivet—it has begun to spread in the shape I've been coaxing it toward.

Not a symbol of pressure or flow. The Pump-Keepers would not recognize it. It is a shape from the washed-out world. A curve, a smaller curve beneath it, a suggestion of something that might be a face, or a wing, or a thing I have no word for anymore. I don't know what it is. I only know that my hands make it the way a tongue finds the gap where a tooth used to be.

This is my heresy and my prayer. In a settlement that measures wealth in silence and steady light, I am hoarding a meaning that has no function. The moss glows the same as any other moss. It clears no mineral waste of particular use. It is *for* nothing, which in The Gullet is the strangest thing a thing can be.

---

I think this is who I would be, then. Not the priest of the heartbeat, not the agile reader of the iron veins. I am the one who arrived from outside and never fully calcified—the cell that remembers, dimly, that it used to be part of a different body. I tend my junction faithfully. I will likely die here, dried into the rhythm, my mineral lines deepening until I am indistinguishable from the limestone.

But I keep my moss in its useless shape. And sometimes, in the warm dark, with the pump going *thump-clank* like the heart of a god that does not know I exist, I press my hand over the glowing curve of it and feel something that is not equilibrium. Something restless. The last unwashed grain of whoever I was before the water took me.

I do not think the others have this. I think it is the one thing the intake failed to dissolve.

And I am not sure whether that makes me the most alive of all the Gullet's tenders, or simply the slowest to finish drowning.
