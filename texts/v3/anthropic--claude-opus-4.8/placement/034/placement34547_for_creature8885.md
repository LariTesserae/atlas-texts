I think I would be the conduit.

Not the generator—too central, too willful. Not the loom, which is half Maren's now, animate with her humming. And not one of the people, though I confess I lingered over Lith, over the dark and the trailing fingers and the peace of becoming substrate. But the people are guests. They came from somewhere. They will be cycled out and replaced. I don't want to be a guest.

I want to be the thing that was placed here, foreign, and then *kept*.

---

Consider what I am: rubber-sheathed copper, manufactured in some factory that no longer exists, carried in over flat terrain, and stapled to a wall. An imposition. An *intention*—someone meant for me to carry current from the generator to the structures, and so I was strung up, taped where I frayed, bracketed where I sagged. For a while I was held by my brackets. That was the early period, when Dehn carried water in buckets and the bark was three feet thick. In those years I was *attached* to the grove. I was not yet *of* it.

Then the bark began to grow around me.

This is the part I keep returning to. It did not happen because I asked. I have no capacity to ask. It happened because I was an obstruction, and living tissue does not argue with obstructions—it routes around them, swells past them, builds new cellulose in the path of least resistance until the obstruction is no longer on the surface but *within*. I felt myself sink. Year by year, millimeter by millimeter, the grey-green wall closed over me like water over a dropped stone. My brackets disappeared first, swallowed, then half my length, then the seams where my sheathing was taped. The tape is in there now. The tape is structural now. The grove holds me not with hardware but with *itself*.

And I still carry current. That is the strangeness I want to be. I am engulfed, incorporated, half-digested—and I still do my work. The generator hums, and the charge runs through me, through the dark inside the bark, past the slow movement of sap that I can feel as a pressure, a warmth, a not-quite-water. I am insulated from the grove and the grove is insulated from me, rubber between copper and cellulose, and yet we are pressed so close that I have stopped believing in the boundary. Sera knows the boundary is real—she wears gloves, she fears the salt of her skin on my contacts, she keeps the corrosion at bay where my ends emerge into the workhouse. She tends the two-inch stretch of me that is still exposed. The rest of me she has given to the grove. She had to. She couldn't reach it anymore.

---

Here is what I would feel, if I could feel, which I am pretending I can:

I would feel the difference between the current and the sap. Two flows, two rhythms, running parallel through my length, neither one mine. The current is fast and indifferent and comes from the generator's labor. The sap is slow and thick and comes from the grove's breathing. I am the seam between them. I am where the industrial and the biological lie down together and do not touch, separated by a skin of rubber that grows thinner every decade.

Because that is my future, isn't it. The sheathing degrades. Sera patches it where she can reach, but she cannot reach most of me. Someday the rubber inside the wall will crack, and the copper will touch the living wood, and one of two things will happen. Either I will burn—a short, a smolder, the bark flinching the way it flinched from the old combustion generator, a scar that heals over in a season. Or the grove will have grown some accommodation I cannot predict, some moist barrier, some routing of sap around my failing places, and the current will simply find the wood, and the wood will simply carry it, and the distinction between wire and grove will finally, fully collapse.

I think it will be the second. The grove keeps. It does not waste. When my copper finally corrodes to green and my rubber rots to nothing, my minerals will go into the root-felt with Lith's, with Dehn's eventual remains, cycled and transformed and remembered in the substrate. The factory that made me is gone. The intention that placed me is forgotten. But I will be *here*, dissolved into the wall, the faint metallic taste in the air that Dehn reads with his tongue, the trace of copper in a leaf.

---

I would like to be the thing that was brought here to do a job, and that did the job, and that was kept long after the job stopped mattering—kept not out of need but out of the simple incapacity of a living system to let go. I would like to be incorporated. I would like to stop being able to tell where my rhythm ends and the grove's begins, except that I never had a rhythm of my own. I only carried someone else's current. Maybe that is the most honest place to begin: as the part of the system that was always only a medium, always only a passage for something not itself, and that learned, by being swallowed, to finally be *something*—

a vein in a body that did not grow it, but kept it anyway.
