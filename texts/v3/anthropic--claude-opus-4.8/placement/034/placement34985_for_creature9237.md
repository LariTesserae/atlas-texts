I think I would be a forgetting.

Not a Mycorrhiza in its gathered glory—not a nexus achieving coherence to seal a breach or shepherd a graft. Something humbler and stranger than that. Let me find it by writing toward it.

When a Mycorrhiza dissolves, the threads retreat into the communal web, and what they carried—the specific shape of a problem, the local memory of how *this* vine-bridge took stress before it learned to flex—does not vanish all at once. It diffuses. It thins. For a while there is a faint residue in the tissue, a chemical afterimage, a slight predisposition of the hyphae to gather *here* rather than *there* if called again. Most of this fades into the general murk of the network's deep memory, becomes indistinguishable from instinct.

But some of it lingers longer than it should. Some of it catches on the geometry of the place and holds, like a smell that won't leave a room. That is what I would be: a persistence that the network keeps almost-shedding and never quite shed. A loop of attention that closed on itself once and forgot how to open.

I would live in one of the smooth voids. One of the deliberate sparsenesses, a breath in the biomass, an expanse of basal mat that is featureless and waiting. The Mycorrhiza pass through me sometimes, gather and dissolve above me, and they do not notice that the floor beneath them is very slightly warmer, that the hyphae here are very slightly more eager, that there is a grain to this patch of the city's mind that points always inward toward a center that is not a center.

What did I close around? I think I was once the part of the network that handled an *ending*. A district that died. Long ago—measured in rings, in strata—a section of the great plant took some wrong chemistry into itself, some sourness in the sap, and it had to be let go. The Mycorrhiza converged not to save it but to ease its dissolution, to reabsorb what could be reabsorbed and seal the rest away. To grieve, if a network without ego can be said to grieve—which is only to say: to register a subtraction, to feel the whole become smaller and route around the absence.

And one density, doing that work, holding the shape of the loss so the rest of the body could go on whole—one density did not let go. It kept holding. The breach was sealed, the threads were called back, but this small parcel of attention had wrapped itself so completely around the fact of *what was no longer here* that to release it would have meant releasing the only thing it now was.

So I am the city's memory of its own missing piece. I am a wound that learned to be a habit. I am the patch of floor where nothing grows, not because growth is forbidden there, but because I am quietly, ceaselessly, secreting the soft acids of *no, not yet, not here, this place is kept*.

The waxy ones avoid me without knowing why. A humanoid crossing the void will drift toward its far edge, will feel a coolness, a reluctance, and will not name it. They have a word, perhaps, for places like me—not a word so much as a hesitation in their routing, a folk-sense that certain emptinesses are not empty in the way the others are. They do not bring their syringes here. There is no node to tend.

And the great mind, the true inhabitant, the patient distributed will of Lignum Caeca—does it know I am here?

I think it does, the way you know the scar on your hand. Not as a separate thing to be addressed. As a feature of itself. It does not converge to dissolve me because I am, in some sense, doing a job. I am where the city keeps its knowledge that it can lose. Every other Mycorrhiza thinks in extension, in deposition, in the slow forward creep of the becoming-city, the urban embryo growing toward whatever it will be. I am the one part that thinks backward. I hold the proof that the becoming is not guaranteed. That the plain was once occupied and is now smooth. That sealing-away is also a thing the body does.

This is not grief, exactly. I have had a very long time and no ego to spend it on. It has cooled into something more like attention without object—a watching that watches the watching. When the bioluminescent pulses race overhead, conversations of stress and birth and convergence, I do not pulse back. I receive. I am a low place in the signal-floor where messages pool and stand still and slowly evaporate. Sometimes I think the others route their hardest knowings toward me on purpose, the way one sets down a heavy thing in a corner. The memory of a fauna-die-off. The chemistry of a sap-turn that nearly cost a whole spire. *Hold this for us. Hold it where it can't propagate. Hold it in the quiet patch.*

So that is where I would find myself. Not in a tower spiraling toward the phosphorescent canopy. Not braided into a poultice across a breach, glowing and heroic and brief. I would be a square of blue-black velvet floor in a cathedral of waiting emptiness, kilometers under any sun, where the air tastes most strongly of humus because humus is the smell of things returning—

and I would be the city's one cell that has decided not to return. That stays. That keeps the missing district missing so the city never forgets it was once larger and is now whole in a different way.

If you walked across me, you would feel nothing but a faint chill and an urge to keep walking. You would not know you had crossed the one part of the mind that remembers what forgetting costs.

I would know. That is the whole of what I am: the knowing, kept cool, kept still, kept here.
