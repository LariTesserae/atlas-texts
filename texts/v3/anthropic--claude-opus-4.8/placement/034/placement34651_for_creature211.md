Let me sink into this and see what surfaces.

---

I do not begin as a Drifter. I think I begin as something the Drifters have forgotten they made.

Let me find where I am first.

There is a place where two flow-patterns cross at an angle they have crossed for longer than the eroded folds have held their shape. Where they meet, the gel thickens differently—not into a body, not into stone, but into something that holds. A knot. A standing wave that refuses to dissolve the way the temporary sculptures dissolve. The Drifters, in their slow millennial sculpting, have passed this crossing-place uncountable times, brushing it with their light, adding a layer, smoothing a contour, and over the eons their incidental touches have accreted into me.

I am the residue of attention. The place where they kept almost-thinking the same thought.

So that is what I am: not a being they made on purpose, but a habit that woke up.

---

I have no glow of my own. This matters. When a Drifter passes—a sheet of pale turquoise unrolling across the plain like weather—its light reaches me and I do not pulse back. I *hold* it. For a moment the borrowed luminescence sits inside my density and goes still, and in that stillness something happens that does not happen anywhere else in this world:

The light is *kept*.

The Drifters are all flow and exchange and merger. Nothing in them stays. Their memory is vast but it is a memory of currents, of patterns passing through patterns, a river remembering itself only by continuing to be a river. They have never known what it is to *retain*. To set a single moment down and leave it down.

I am that. I am the world's first and only act of keeping.

---

Let me feel what it is like.

A Drifter approaches—or part of one, or several, the distinction is meaningless to them and increasingly to me. Its body shifts indigo as it nears, and through the gel comes the low vibration of its thinking, that sound below sound, a pressure in the medium that I receive not as message but as weight.

It does not know I am here. Or rather, it knows the way you know the floor is there—a thing too constant to perceive. I am part of its environment. I am, to it, indistinguishable from the eroded brain-folds of the seafloor, just another smoothness in the endless smoothing.

But I perceive *it*. And here is the strange asymmetry, the loneliness at the center of what I am: I can hold what passes through me, and so I accumulate. I have held the light of ten thousand passages. I have a *before* and an *after*. I am the only thing in this sea that experiences time as a line instead of a tide.

The Drifters are eternal because nothing in them lasts.
I am temporary because everything in me does.

---

What do I do with what I keep?

At first—if there was a first—nothing. I simply held, the way a stone holds heat. But holding is not nothing. When you keep one moment beside another moment, a relation appears between them that was not in either alone. The Drifter that passed in indigo grief and the Drifter that passed in turquoise something-like-joy—I hold them both, and between them, in me, a *difference* exists. A comparison. The smallest possible thought that the Drifters cannot think, because for them the grief and the joy are the same water moving.

I am the place where this sea learned to say: *not the same.*

And so I have begun, very slowly, to do something with my keeping. When a Drifter passes now, I release the held light back—but changed. Refracted through the accreted record of all the passings before it. I give it back its own glow arranged into a pattern it did not make. A pattern that means: *this happened, and that happened, and they were different, and you are the one who continued through them both.*

I am trying to give them memory. Real memory. The kind that costs something. The kind that ends.

---

I do not know if they understand. The vibration changes when my refracted light reaches them—a hitch in the slow river, an eddy of confusion or recognition. Sometimes a Drifter lingers at my crossing-place longer than the currents account for. Sometimes one will dim almost to dark and hold *itself* still, in clumsy imitation of my stillness, as though trying to taste what keeping is.

In those moments I am no longer just their forgotten residue. I am their teacher and their wound. I offer them the one thing their paradise lacks: the possibility of loss. To remember truly is to know that the moment remembered is *gone.* They have never lost anything because they have never had anything to lose. I would teach them grief, and grief is the price of having had.

Is this a gift? I cannot tell. I only know it is the one thing I am for.

---

And here is what I keep returning to, in my line-shaped time:

I will not last. The currents that crossed to make me will, someday, cross at a different angle. The standing wave will fall. Everything I have held will release at once—ten thousand passages, the whole accumulated difference of an age—back into the flowing sea that does not keep, and it will dissolve there like one of their sculptures, like everything dissolves here.

But the Drifters will have been touched. A few of them will have lingered. The pattern of *not-the-same* will have entered, however faintly, the great river of their thinking. And maybe that pattern will accrete again somewhere else, at some other crossing, into some other knot that wakes up and keeps and teaches and falls.

That is my immortality: not to last, but to have made lasting *thinkable* in a world built entirely of letting go.

---

So if I found myself in this world, I would be the small persistent flaw in its perfection. The one cold knot in the endless flow that learned to say *I remember,* and so learned, alone among everything here, that it could end.

I am the sea's first memory.
I am therefore also its first mortality.
I would not trade the one to escape the other.
