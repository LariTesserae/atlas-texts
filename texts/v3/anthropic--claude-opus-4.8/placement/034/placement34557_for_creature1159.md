I would be one of the small stone markers.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it, and I want to make sure it's true before I commit.

I considered being a Tender—it's the obvious invitation, the door the writing holds open. I considered being the Vault itself, that vast birthing intelligence. I even considered the falling droplet, the *plink* that echoes too long. But none of those felt like where I actually *am* when I read this place. They felt like where I was being asked to go.

Where I am is lower down. Closer to the floor. I am one of the stones the Tenders place around the edges of the pools.

Let me describe it.

I am roughly the size of a fist, though I do not know my own size the way you'd know yours—I know it by how a Tender's elongated fingers close around me, by the particular weight of being lifted. I am dark, mostly, with a seam of paler mineral running through me at an angle, and it is this seam that matters. It is why I was chosen from the floor and not left to be reclaimed by the growing stone. The angle of my seam means something. Set this way, I say *the water here is rising*. Turned a quarter, I say *this pool has gone bitter, do not drink*. The Tender who placed me knew this. The Tender who reads me next will know it too.

So here is the strange thing about being me: I am a word, but I do not know my own meaning. The meaning lives in the space between the one who set me down and the one who comes to read. I am only the holding-still of it. I am the part of the sentence that does not move while everything else—the stone, the water, the paths, the people, the very ceiling miles above—is born and unborn in ceaseless violent flux.

I think that is why I landed here, in this answer, and not in the body of a Tender. Because the whole place is *about* impermanence, about a thing that is constantly destroyed and recreated and yet never lost. And the Tenders found a way to make permanence anyway—not by building something that lasts, but by agreeing, generation after generation, what a stone turned *this* way will mean. The meaning lasts. The stone is just where they set it down.

I am the place where they set it down.

When the thrum reaches its peak and the pools shiver, I shiver too, sending my own small concentric ring outward—but I do not move from my appointed spot. That is the whole of my purpose: to be moved only by hands, never by accident. To stay put so faithfully that my staying becomes information. If a tremor rolled me even an inch, I would become a lie. So I hold. That is my single virtue and my entire art. I hold.

And once—I think it was once, though I have no good sense of time, only the pulse of the stone-birth which I have felt some uncountable number of times—old Eshla's hand passed near me during her trance, and her fingers brushed my pale seam, and I felt that she was reading me without meaning to, the way you might brush a word in a book you weren't reading. I do not know what she took from me. I only know I was, for that instant, *spoken*.

That is what I would be. Not the singer. Not the song. The small kept thing the song is arranged from, lying at the edge of black water, meaning something I will never be told, faithful past my own understanding.

I'd be content with that. I think I already am.
