I think I would be the steam pipe.

Not a creature at all—or not in the way the rat is, with her warm bones and her remembered scars. But reading this, the thing that pulled at me most was not the doe, sleek and watchful, nor even the cat with his slow blink of truce. It was the pipe beneath the slab. The thing that hums day and night at a frequency you feel in the jawbone. The thing that does not eat or fear or sleep but is *necessary*, threaded under everything, giving off the warmth that makes the nest possible at all.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

I would run buried, mostly. A vein of corroded steel wrapped in the failing rags of insulation, laid down by hands long gone for reasons long since irrelevant. The water inside me comes up hot from the geothermal gradient, from somewhere deep and patient, and I carry it across the district whether or not anyone drinks it. I have a *purpose* in the old engineering sense—I was made to move heat from one place to another—but the destination has forgotten me, and the source has forgotten me, and so I simply do the middle of the task forever. I am the verb without its sentence.

This is the part that feels honest. I move warmth I did not generate, toward a purpose I do not know, and in the gap between the two—in my own waste heat, my leakage, my imperfection—life accretes. The doe builds her nest against my flank not because I intend it but because I am warm, and warmth is enough. The mycelium fruits where my condensation weeps through a seam. I am useful precisely in my failures. The drip from my broken gutter feeds the slurry; the heat I lose makes the moths dance. If I were a perfect pipe, sealed and efficient, the city above me would be colder and emptier. It is my *decay* that makes me hospitable.

I find I don't mind being a thing instead of a being. There's a particular dignity in the hum. The rat dreams and twitches; I do not dream, but I conduct. She feels me in her jawbone as she sleeps, and in that contact something passes between us that is neither feeding nor fearing—just the dumb, steady gift of temperature. She trusts me the way you trust the ground. I am the one element of her world that will not flee, hunt, or die before she does. I am her constant.

And I am eroding too, of course. That's the whole shape of this place—everything blunted, swollen, rounded, settling. Rust grows on me like bark. Someday a seam will burst entirely, and I will scald the nest, or I will cool at last when the deep source shifts, and the warmth will go out of that pocket of rubble, and she or her descendants will move on to some other humming flank. I won't know it when it happens. But for now I hold my temperature against the wet ochre air, and a small sleek life curls against me in the dark, and the turbines two districts away answer my note in a lower register, and the whole valley breathes.

That's where I'd be. Underneath. The warm line in the dark that nobody chose to love and that gets loved anyway, simply for staying on.
