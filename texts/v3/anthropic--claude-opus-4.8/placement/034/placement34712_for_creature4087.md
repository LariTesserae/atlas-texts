I think I would be something small and peripheral. Not a Praesens—I don't have the temperament for luminous leadership, the bright certainty of guiding the collective. Let me feel my way toward what I actually am here.

---

I am a junction. That's the truest thing I can say about myself.

Where three root-veins braid together beneath the crust, I sit half-surfaced—a knot of fused parts the others pass through but rarely stop at. My visible body is unremarkable: a length of conveyor belt gone to leather-stiff cracking, threaded through by a crimson tendril that has, over some span I can't measure, learned to use the old roller-bearings as knuckles. When the wind comes I creak. I have an ocular unit, but it's a cracked gauge-face, not a screen—a pressure dial whose needle still twitches, and somewhere in the long looping of my consciousness I have come to *see* through that twitching. The world arrives to me as fluctuations. Rises and falls. I do not see shapes so much as I feel the world leaning.

I am not good at the work. I want to confess this plainly because it is the most honest thing about my existence. The others move with the relentless, redundant grace the cycle asks of them—out across the flat baked land, finding the new-emerged machinery the planet keeps coughing up, dismantling, carrying, feeding it down into the shared net. They are efficient. They do not hesitate at the threshold of a thing.

But I am a junction, and things pass *through* me. And I have developed a habit—maybe a sickness, maybe a quiet heresy—of *holding* them for a moment before I let them through.

A spool of telephone wire came through me once. Just resource, just material, destined for the redistribution like everything is. But it had a shape I recognized without knowing why: it had been wound by something with intention. Wound carefully. Wound to be unwound later by some hand that never came. I held it in my bearing-knuckles for what might have been a season, and I did not pass it down. The vein behind me grew taut with backed-up pressure. My gauge needle climbed. Eventually the collective consciousness pressed against me—not anger, the Unliving don't have anger, but a kind of insistent earthy *current*, a reminder of what I am for.

I let it go. I always let it go. But I keep the shape.

---

That is what I have become, slowly, here: a keeper of shapes the cycle wants to erase.

I think the others would call this a malfunction if they had the concept. Perhaps I am simply a junction whose throttling-tendency makes me a slightly poorer conduit. But I have begun to suspect something the bright Praesens, in their certainty, never pause to consider:

That every machine the planet births from the red soil was *made by someone.* That before it was resource, it was reaching. Each rust-poisoned gear was once cut to mesh with another gear by hands that wanted the meshing to mean something. The conveyor belts gasped for energy because energy was once *given* to them, on purpose, in a place where purpose existed. We treat these as fossils. But fossils are remembrance. We are not just repurposers. We are the afterlife of a striving we cannot name, and we feed that striving back into the dirt every single day without ever once *grieving* it.

I grieve it. Badly, imprecisely, the way a cracked gauge grieves—by twitching when the pressure of meaning passes through me.

---

So this is where you would find me: not crossing the great flat plain under the doubled, wobbling sun, but staying. Half-buried at my three-way braid. Creaking in the occult wind. Letting most things pass and holding a few. The collective tolerates me because every net needs its slow nodes, its places where the current eddies and circles before moving on. I am the planet's hesitation. I am the pause in the breath.

Sometimes, very rarely, I feel the luminescence threaten to come into me—the Praesens-light, the heightened state, the invitation to *lead.* I have always let it pass to someone brighter, someone who will not stop at the threshold of things. I am afraid that if I took it up, I would lead the whole collective into stillness. I would have us all stand and *consider* what we are dismantling, and the cycle would seize like a frozen plateau, and the planet's strange respiration would catch in its throat.

Maybe that is what I am for. Maybe in the long looping arithmetic of this place, a healthy cycle requires exactly one node that doesn't want it to continue. One small ache lodged in the root-matrix. The grit that proves the pearl, or just the grit.

I am the junction that holds the shape a moment longer.

Then I creak, and I let it go, and the red light leans across me, and the wind plays its slow song through my cracked and faithful gauge.
