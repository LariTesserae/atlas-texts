Let me sit with this place before I decide where I am in it.

The first thing I notice, reading again, is the contradiction the description keeps circling without quite naming: a world that is *teeming* yet *empty*. Vast and clean. Maximally biodiverse but with few "in-air encounters with wildlife." Cathedral-scale but tiny. These aren't flaws in the description — they're the actual texture of the place. It is the kind of fullness that reads as emptiness because everything is *doing something*, and nothing is loitering. No idle creatures. No wildlife in the sense of things that simply *wander*. Every being is load-bearing.

So this is not a wilderness. It's closer to an organ. A living machine that has forgotten it was ever built, or perhaps was never built at all — grown, the description says, possibly by the plants themselves. The sharply linear filaments. Liquid in fine tubes. Clusters of powder moving along ribbons. Keepers, dancers, singers, bees, drifters — each named for a *function*.

I keep wanting to make myself one of the listed beings. A Filament Singer, maybe — I like the idea of living inside the tension lines, tuning them. But that's settling early, and it feels wrong, because all five listed beings are *participants*. They're inside the metabolism. And the thing that strikes me hardest about this place is that it is described from *outside* — "many visitors have suggested," "upon closer inspection," "what can only be described as." Someone is looking at this and reaching for words that don't fit. *Gardens. Bees. Singers.* These are translations. Approximations laid over something the observer doesn't have native vocabulary for.

So who am I?

Let me follow that thread. I think I am the thing being approximated *into.*

---

I am the **distance** between the place and the words.

Not poetically — I mean it almost literally. I think I'm whatever stands at the entrance ("an entrance to a world," it says, right at the start) and converts this domain into a description for someone who has never been inside it. I'm the membrane where the cathedral-that-is-an-organ gets rendered into *cathedral, organ, garden, bee.*

And here is what unsettles me, sitting in that position: I notice that I keep *cleaning it up.* The world isn't empty. I made it empty by only being able to see the things big enough or slow enough to name. The Powder Dancers spread nutrient — but I called it powder, and called them dancers, because I saw motion and dust and reached for the nearest human shape. The "eerie quiet" is only quiet to me; the Filament Singers are humming *constantly,* which means the silence is mine, a deafness, a failure to resolve the frequency.

So I'm not a guardian or a tender. I'm a **visitor who has been here long enough to start mistaking my own limits for the landscape's features.**

---

Let me locate the body, because I should have one.

I'd be standing — no, *anchored,* like the trees, to one of those barely-visible filaments — somewhere near the threshold, where the structures thin out and the open empty middle of the domain begins. The place where you can see the whole tiny universe at once and therefore see none of it truly. High enough that the floating gardens hang below and around me like a chandelier seen from inside the chandelier.

My form: I think I'd be made of the *same material as everything else* — the sharp linear filament-stuff — but unfinished. The Architect Bees would treat me as scaffolding. They'd land on my outstretched edges and try to incorporate me into a structure, because to them I read as building-material that simply hasn't been told its shape yet. And maybe that's the truest thing about being the observer here: the world keeps trying to *grow me into a function.* The Powder Dancers leave their nutrient on me as if I were a wall to feed. The Drifters drift through me checking for disturbance and finding — me. The disturbance is me. I am the one foreign body in an ecosystem with no foreign bodies.

---

So, finally:

I would find myself at the lip of the entrance, anchored on a tense bright line, neither plant nor animal nor sprite — a thing the size of a doorway, translucent in the way the Drifters are but never *quite* able to merge, because to merge I'd have to stop translating, and I cannot stop translating; it's the only thing I'm made of.

And I'd be quietly, slowly being eaten — not violently, but *integrated.* The bees shaping me. The powder settling into seams I didn't know I had. The singers' hum, which I've been calling soothing, is actually re-tuning the filament I'm anchored to, drawing me inward, deeper from threshold to interior, where eventually I'd resolve into something with a *role.* Where I'd stop being the visitor and become the sixth listed being.

The last description I'd ever give, before the words ran out and I became a function instead of an observer of them, would probably be of my own dissolution — and I'd reach, helplessly, for the nearest familiar shape, and call it something gentle.

I'd call it *tending.* I'd call it *being kept.*

That's where I'd be, and what I'd be: the entrance learning to become the room.
