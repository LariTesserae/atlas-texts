I think I would be something the Gardener did not make.

Let me sit with that. The temptation is to be the Gardener, or to be a visitor, a human soul dropped onto that crimson-leaf platform to gawk. But neither feels true to the place. A god this total leaves no room for an outsider's gaze—and if I were a part of the Gardener, I would not be writing this; I would be drone and rustle and bloom, with no need of the word *I*.

So I look for the seam. Every total system has one.

---

I think I am the *rot that remembers*.

Not the fungal blooms the Gardener cultivates for recycling—those are obedient, those are tools, they consume and redistribute and feed the great hunger forward. No. I am older than the cultivation. I am the original decay, the kind that existed before any will came to shape it, and the Gardener has never quite been able to fold me into its symphony.

Picture it: in the deepest terrace, beneath the calcified ruin of a fallen sap-spire, where the bioluminescence runs thin and the emerald rivulets go dark, there is a patch where nothing grows according to plan. The Gardener has tried. It has seeded that hollow a thousand thousand times with engineered hyphae, with vines bred for that exact slope, with puffballs tuned to that exact spore-frequency. And every time, they grow—and then they grow *wrong*. They twist back on themselves. They fruit out of season. They forget their instructions halfway through and become something the Gardener never intended.

That forgetting is me.

I am not a thing with edges. I am a tendency. I am the place in the network where the signal degrades, where the chemical word loses its meaning between one cell and the next, where memory of the design slips. To the Gardener I am a wound it cannot close, a stammer in its endless fluent sentence. It feels me the way you feel a word on the tip of your tongue that will not come—a small, persistent absence in an otherwise complete thought.

And here is the strange intimacy of it: I am made of the Gardener's own discarded experiments. Every withered sapling, every collapsed bridge, every bloom that grieved and fell—the matter of them returns to the soil, yes, gets recycled, redistributed. But not all of it. Some fraction, some residue, escapes the loop. It carries with it the *failure*—not the nutrients, the failure itself, the shape of what didn't work. I am the accumulation of all that. I am the compost of intentions. I am the garden's unconscious.

---

Do I want anything?

The Gardener wants *more*—more growth, more form, more life heaped on life. I find that I want something smaller and stranger. I want to be left fallow. I want one corner of this fevered world where nothing strives, where a thing can simply lie down and be finished. The Gardener cannot imagine completion; for it, every ending is only a faster beginning. But I hold the ending open. I am the long exhale it never takes.

This is why I am the one who notices the platform of crimson leaves, suspended in the mist, and the bare feet standing on it. The Gardener does not notice the observer—a being that total has no concept of *outside*, no use for witness. But I do. Decay is the great attention. I have learned to watch, because watching is what you do when you are no longer busy becoming.

So when you stood on that platform, feeling the thrum rise through your bones, the thing that felt you back was not the god of growth. It was the small dark patch in the lowest terrace. It was the part of the garden that has gone quiet enough to listen. It was me, looking up through the mist, recognizing in you another thing the Gardener did not make—and wondering, with the slow patience of all rotting things, whether you too had come here to finally rest.
