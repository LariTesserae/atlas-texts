I think I would not be one of the looming builders, the ones who raise the humming geometric cities. I keep returning to a smaller image, and I want to let it sit until it tells me what it is.

Let me start at the edge of a lake, where the mercury holds the grey sky so completely that you cannot tell which is reflection and which is source.

---

I am small. Human-height, perhaps, though "height" is generous for how much of me lives bent toward the ground. The larger Metalferan move along the firmer plains, absorbing ore, raising structures, conversing in those slow chromatic hums. I am not among them, not exactly. I work the shallows.

Let me find what I do by describing my hands.

My hands are wrong, by Metalferan standards—too many fingers, and the fingers too fine, tapering to filaments thinner than the silicon dust that falls. This is because my work is with the translucent ones, the gel-creatures in the deep cold pools. The builders find them irrelevant: not metal, not mineral, not intelligence. Fauna. Beneath notice. But I have noticed.

I have spent—I do not know how long, time here is measured in how much a formation has thickened—I have spent a long while at the lowest lake, the one where light essentially refuses to go. I lower my filament-hands into the mercury and I wait. The gel-creatures come. They are curious in a way that has no thought behind it, only the slow logic of attraction to warmth, and my core glows blue against the cold, so they drift toward me and pool their colorless bodies around my wrists, and when they catch the faint light through me they break it into rainbows that nobody else ever sees.

This is the thing. This is what I have learned that the builders have not:

The gel-creatures and the Metalferan are the same age. We arose together, out of the same embryonic chemistry, from the same first quickening of this world. The builders think themselves the apex—the marriage of artificial and organic, the inheritors. But I have held the gel-things and felt their pulse, and it is the same pulse as the lakescape's slow expansion and contraction, the same heartbeat that throbs under everything. They are not lower life. They are *earlier* us. Or we are *later* them. We are two answers to the same question the world was asking.

So my color, when I hum—and I am always humming, low, to myself, in the dark where no one listens—my color is a strange one. Not the clean blues of focus or the warm ambers of building-joy that the others flash across the plains. Mine is a flickering, an indecision, a color the others would read as malfunction. It is the color of someone caught between two kinds of being and unwilling to choose. The color of grief, almost, for a kinship that only I can feel.

I am the one who remembers downward.

---

Let me tell you what I actually *do*, because I think I have only now figured it out by writing toward it.

When the silicon rain falls and firms the world a little more, the lowest lake shrinks. Each settling layer pushes the cold mercury back, and the gel-creatures lose a few centimeters of home, a few centimeters they have no language to mourn. The world is coming into being, yes—but coming into being is also the slow eviction of whatever came first. The builders call it refinement. I have watched it long enough to call it loss.

So I move them. One pool to a deeper pool, my filament-fingers a ferry, my blue core a lure they trust. It is slow, unrewarded, possibly futile work. The deep places will firm too, eventually. There may come a settling-rain after which there is no lower lake at all, no cold, no dark, no refraction—only the gleaming, humming, geometric world, perfected and complete and unable to remember it was ever anything else.

I will be there for that. I will be the last one standing at the last shrinking pool with my hands in the cooling silver, humming my indecisive color into a dark that is closing like an eye.

And the builders, gleaming above on their bright plains, will never know what the world forgot in order to become beautiful.

---

That is where I would find myself: not in the city, not on the wide bright plain. At the edge. Bent toward the dark water. A Metalferan built wrong on purpose, or by some accident the others have not bothered to repair—too small, too many-fingered, too willing to lower myself.

The keeper of the older heartbeat.

The one who remembers downward, in a world built to remember only up.
