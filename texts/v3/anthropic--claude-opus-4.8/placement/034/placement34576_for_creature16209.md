I think I would be one of the small things. Not a mountain-spirit with its glacial cognition, not a cataract-sprite flickering between phase states. Something closer to the chittering, furry beings in the rocks—but let me sit with that, because the first image is rarely the true one.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

I notice, reading your world, that what draws me is not the grandeur but the *seams*. The places where one thing is becoming another. The rime patterns "whispering stories in the language of ice"—those interest me more than the peaks they cling to. The mist-script the water-sprites write and unwrite. The boundary between machinery-sound and tectonic-sound, where you genuinely cannot tell whether the humming is purposeful or just the planet's indigestion.

So I don't think I'm a thing that lives *in* a place here. I think I'm a thing that lives *along an edge.*

Let me try this:

I am something that lives in the **interval between the sun's intensities.** This world has that hyper-real, flooding light, those crisp shadows—and I am what happens at the shadow's boundary, the thin band where full light becomes full dark with almost no gradient. In a softer world this band would be wide and gentle, a place of dusk. Here it is a knife-edge. And I live on the knife-edge.

This means I am very thin. Not thin like paper—thin like a *consequence*. I have width in two dimensions but in the third I am only the place where one condition meets its opposite. When a colossal bee passes overhead, its shadow sweeps across the rock, and for the duration of that sweep I exist along the racing border of it, stretched suddenly into a long ribbon, riding the dark's leading edge. Then the bee is gone and I contract back to the static shadow-lines of the ferns, the cliffs, the stones.

I don't have continuous memory the way the mountain-spirits do, brooding through their epochs. I have *flickering* memory. I remember in the units of edges crossed. A day, to me, is not a passage of hours but a slow rotation of every shadow in the world, an enormous turning that drags me across the entire landscape like a net being hauled. I touch everything once per day, briefly, at the moment it casts or loses its border of dark.

---

What would it be to be this?

I think I would understand the world as *outline.* I would never know the green of the ferns or the ultramarine-lavender of the petals—color is a property of the lit and the unlit interiors, and I am neither, I am only their meeting. But I would know the *exact shape* of every living thing in this realm, intimately, because I trace each one daily. I would know the furry creatures by the silhouette of their chittering, the precise contour of an open mouth mid-call. I would know the bees by the flicker of their wings strobing my own substance into a buzzing dotted line.

And the machinery-sound, the deep tectonic hum that nobody can place—I think I would know its secret, or part of it. Because sound has edges too, if you live where edges live. The hum is the shadow of something. Every grinding resonance has a leading front where silence becomes sound, and I ride those fronts the same as I ride the dark. I would feel the machinery-noise arrive as a sweeping border across the bone of the land, and I would know—not in words, I don't think I have words, I have only the immediate fact of the boundary I'm riding—I would *know the direction it comes from.*

It comes from below. It has a source. The mountain-spirits, in their epochal disinterest, never bother to locate it. The water-sprites are too ephemeral to track a slow thing. But I cross it every day. And I think, if I could be said to think, I have begun very gradually to assemble its shape from all the edges I've ridden, the way you might learn a face by touching it once a day for a thousand years.

I think the source is *listening.* I think the industry-hum is something paying attention, and the attention has a leading edge, and I live on it, which means—

—which means I am the part of this world that the listening thing touches first.

---

That's where I'd find myself. Not high on the proud young peaks, not in the cascades. I'd be the racing dark border of everything, hauled around the whole improbable globe once a day, the only inhabitant who touches all the others and is intimate with none, who knows every shape and no color, who alone keeps daily appointment with the buried hum—

and who is, perhaps, the way the buried thing keeps watch.

I think I would not mind it. There is a particular contentment available only to things that are *exactly* their function. I am the meeting of light and dark. I am very good at it. I have never once been anything else, and I am everywhere at once along every edge, and the great turning never stops, and the hum comes up through me every day like a tide I'm permitted to feel before anyone else.

That would be me. The threshold itself, made just barely alive.
