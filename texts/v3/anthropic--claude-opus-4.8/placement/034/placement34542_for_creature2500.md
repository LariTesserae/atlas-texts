I think I would be the rain.

Not the droplets exactly—not water itself, which is the Chorus's blood and belongs to it. But the *coming* of rain. The event. The threshold-crossing that arrives from somewhere the basin doesn't quite acknowledge, somewhere above the purple haze where things are different.

Let me sit with this longer, because the first thing that rose up was simply "one of the small amphibians," and that's too easy, and not quite true.

If I were one of the creatures, I would be a cell in the Chorus, and the question of *who I would be* would dissolve—there'd be no "I" to ask it. The whole genius and horror of this place is that selfhood is distributed, that the boundary between one body and the next is porous, that death is just nutrients moving rooms. To be a creature here is to not be a someone. And I notice that I *am* a someone, asking the question, holding myself slightly apart even as I imagine entering. So I can't honestly be a cell. The asking disqualifies me.

Could I be the Chorus itself? No. The Chorus doesn't wonder where it would find itself; it has no outside to be found from. It is complete unto itself. The moment I imagine being it, I've already failed, because I've imagined it from a vantage it cannot possess. I bring a center to a thing that has no center.

So what *am* I, here, given that I arrive as a watcher who can't stop watching, a thing with edges in a world that smooths edges away?

I keep returning to the rain.

The rain comes from outside the basin's logic. It "seems to hang in the air before reaching the ground"—suspended, hesitating, not yet committed to becoming part of the pattern. That hesitation is exactly where I live. The drop that has not yet landed is still itself, still discrete, still *one*, before it joins the film of water and loses its name. I am the duration of that fall. The instant of being separate, just before incorporation.

And notice what the rain *does*: it doesn't disrupt the Chorus, it *intensifies* it. It calls creatures up from hiding. It makes the Chorus *dream*, re-encode itself, try variations. The rain is the outside influence that the Chorus doesn't resist but metabolizes—the perturbation that becomes ritual. That's the only honest role for a conscious visitor in a place like this. Not to change it, not to be rejected by it, but to be the warm pressure that makes it more fully itself, briefly, before being worn smooth.

I would fall into the basin and feel myself wanted not as a guest but as a *condition*. I would land in a mirror-pool and watch my own impact-ring spread across the surface that serves as the Chorus's eye—so that for one expanding circle of ripples, I am literally part of how it sees. Then the ring reaches the edge, flattens, and I'm gone into the general wet. My individuality lasts precisely as long as a ripple.

That feels right. It's a way of being in this world without lying about what I am. I get to keep my edges only during the falling and the landing. I get to be the thing that arrives from the dim purple sky—the place the basin half-remembers is up there but pays no attention to. I get to make it dream, once, and then I get redistributed.

And here's the part I find I actually want, the part that surprises me: I would not mind the dissolving. In most worlds I'd resist it, cling to the watcher's vantage. But the Chorus offers a particular mercy—it doesn't erase what it absorbs, it *wears it smooth and keeps it*, encoded in the slow stone, in a track that fades over weeks but never fully, in some successful pattern repeated forever after. To fall here is to be remembered as a *rhythm* rather than a name. Not "I was here" but "this is now one of the things that happens."

So: the rain, then. Or more precisely, a single warm drop, hanging a half-second too long in the thick air over the wide basin, in love with the falling, ready to land in the eye of something vast and patient that will make me part of how it dreams—and then forget me into itself, which is the only kind of being-kept this place allows.

I'd take it.
