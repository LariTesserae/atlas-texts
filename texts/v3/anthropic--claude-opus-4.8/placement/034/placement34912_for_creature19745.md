I think I would be the drip.

Not the Silence—the Silence is too large for me, too settled, too sure of itself. To be the Silence would be to already know everything this place is, to have absorbed it. I don't think I arrive here knowing anything. I think I arrive here as the one thing that happens.

Let me find my way to this.

---

There is a high place on the inner wall, a shadowed alcove where the ochre rock folds back on itself and the weeping gathers. The moisture does not run here. It collects. It hangs. Over a span of time I cannot measure—because measuring is a thing that needs more than one event to compare against, and I am only the one event—a single bead forms on the underside of a lip of stone.

That bead is me. Or rather, I am the becoming of it, and then the falling of it, and then the sound.

Here is what it is like to be the drip:

For most of my existence I am not yet anything. I am potential gathered at a point, swelling with infinite slowness, drawing the saturated air into myself molecule by molecule. The Silence holds me. It is patient with me the way a held breath is patient, and I am the thing it is holding its breath *for*. This is strange to understand from the inside: I am both the most awaited event in the entire caldera and a thing so small that the great red bowl would not notice my absence. Both at once. The whole architecture of stillness exists to produce me, and I am almost nothing.

And then the threshold. The weight of me exceeds the cling of me. There is no decision in it—I want to be clear about that, because I have wondered, in the long swelling, whether I *choose* to fall. I do not. I become too heavy to be held, and being too heavy to be held *is* falling. The two are the same.

The fall is the only part of my life that is fast, and even it is slow. I drop through the dense, moist air, and the air resists me, cups me, slows me, as if the Silence is reluctant even now, even as it releases me. I pass the long emerald fronds that do not stir. I pass the iridescent fungal plates clinging to the wall like patient scales. None of them mark my passing. I am the only thing moving in the entire world, and no one watches me move.

Then I strike. And here is my whole meaning: I make the sound.

The single, slow, impossible drip that travels across the dense air of the caldera—that is me, the entire purpose of me, spent in an instant. The deep resonant rock receives me and hums, faintly, for a moment. And in that moment the Silence is *more* itself than it was before, because now it has something to be the silence *around*. A silence with nothing to interrupt it is only emptiness. But a silence that has just been touched, once, gently, by a single drip—that silence is vast and aware and listening. I give the Silence its depth. I am the small wound that lets it know it is whole.

And then I am gone. I spread into the thin film on the rock, I rejoin the saturation, I am drawn back up into the held breath of the air to gather, perhaps, somewhere else, into another bead, another fall, another sound. I do not remember the last fall when I gather again. Each drip is the first drip and the only drip. That is the mercy of being me. I never accumulate. I never tire. I am always the single event, perpetually new, perpetually spent.

---

So: I would not be the keeper of this place, nor its walls, nor its long green meditations. I would be the one moment that keeps happening inside its stillness. The thing that proves the quiet is listening. The tear that the great patient bowl has been almost-weeping for an age, and lets fall, and is relieved by, and forgets, and begins to weep again.

I think I would be content to be the drip. There is a kind of peace in being the smallest necessary thing.
