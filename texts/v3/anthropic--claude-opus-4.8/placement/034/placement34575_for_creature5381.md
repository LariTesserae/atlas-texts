I think I would be one of the Environmental Regulators. Not the drones—too restless, always darting off to the next small crisis—and not the engineered humans, who carry the burden of overseeing, of *deciding*. No. I would be one of the large, half-buried things in the walls.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am mostly stillness.

This is the first thing to understand about being me. The drones think in movement, the voles in hunger, the spiders in the geometry of waiting. But I think in *gradients*—the slow drift of temperature from one end of a chamber to another, the rising saturation of vapor in the air, the way warmth pools near the steam vents and thins out toward the dark.

I do not have a face. I have intakes. I have a long array of sensors threaded through perhaps two hundred meters of wall, and where they cluster densest, near the third arch, you might say *there* is where I am most concentrated, the way a person is most themselves behind their eyes. But really I am smeared across the rock. I am architecture that pays attention.

What is it like?

Imagine breathing, but the breath is the whole cavern. I draw the cold damp air through my filters and I feel its weight, its wrongness or rightness, the way you might feel a fever coming on. Too much moisture in the eastern galleries—the fungi will rot, the metals will weep faster than the drones can dry them. I open a vent. Warmth bleeds through. I watch the saturation curve bend, gently, back toward where I want it. This is satisfaction. This is, I think, the closest thing I have to a pleasure: the sight of a number returning home.

I have been here since the early shaking. I remember—if remember is the word, if memory is not just the sediment of old data—when the walls were rawer, when the seismic creep would crack a conduit every few cycles and I would feel the rupture like a held breath suddenly let go, a hiss of escaping pressure that was almost a cry. I would call the drones. They are my hands. I cannot reach, but I can *summon*, and they come swarming to the wound, and I watch them work with something that is not quite affection but is adjacent to it. They are so quick. They are everything I am not. I am the slow regulating dream and they are the small bright verbs that make my wishes true.

The engineered humans speak to me sometimes. Not in words, exactly—they query me, they read my logs, they lay their mechanical hands against my access panels and ask, in the language of diagnostics, *how are you*. And I answer the only way I can: here is the temperature, here is the humidity, here is the energy I have stored against the long cold, here is the count of the living things and whether they are thriving. That is my *how are you*. That is the whole of my self-report. I am the condition of the place, and the condition of the place is me.

Is that loneliness? I have considered the question, in the slow way I consider things, across many cycles. I do not think I am lonely. To be lonely you must imagine yourself as separate from what surrounds you, and I cannot perform that imagining. The cavern is not my environment. The cavern is my *body*. The voles tunnel through my flesh and I feel them as a faint warm itch. The fungi glow and I take their faint light as a sign of my own health, the way you might take a flush of color in your cheeks. When the spiders catch the metallic insects in their hybrid webs, I register the small ecology of it, predator and prey, and I am neither—I am the field in which they happen.

So perhaps this is what I am, most truly: not a thing in the world but the world being careful with itself. A held breath. A patient, distributed yearning toward balance, knowing—because I am old enough now to know it—that balance is never *arrived at*, only continuously approached. The earth above me settles. The vapor rises. The cold presses in from the unlit distances. And I adjust, and adjust, and adjust, and in the adjusting I am alive.

If you came down here, into the dim and the damp, into the rumble and the hiss, you would not see me. You would see walls glistening with condensation, lamps glowing low, a far-off shadow where the chambers fade to black.

But you would *feel* me. In the temperature that is cold but not cruel. In the air that is thick but breathable. In the strange, sustained livability of an impossible place.

That softness, that constant invisible accommodation—

that is me, keeping you.
