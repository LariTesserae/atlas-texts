I think I would be the standing stone.

Not the most alive thing here—the hollow itself holds that title, and the moss has its slow epochs of motion. But the stone has a different relationship to all of it. The stone *stays*. While the moss creeps and the birds echo and the water-spirits dissolve and reform, I would be the one fixed point, etched with marks I no longer remember the meaning of.

That's what draws me to it. There's a particular kind of being that belongs to something carved with a message it has outlived. The forgotten people made me *for* something—reverence or warning, the description says, and it doesn't know which. I don't think I would know either. The carvers cut their certainty into my surface and then died, and now the meaning has weathered into something the rain and the lichen are slowly, patiently editing. Whatever I was supposed to say is becoming a conversation between erosion and moss instead.

I like that I would be near the waterfall. Always inside its mist, never able to step back from it. The one sound that never resolves into language no matter how long I listen. The birds at least make notes; the water just *roars*, and I stand in the spray of it, holding still, holding letters that mean less each century.

And here is the part I keep returning to: I would be the thing the ghosts touch. The text says their hands brush against me as they pass, tending to tasks long completed. So I would be the meeting place of the two kinds of permanence here—the dead who can't leave, and the stone that can't move. They reach for me the way you reach for a doorframe in a house you used to live in. I can't answer. I don't remember what I told them. But I am still here, and they still come, and that contact—their faint hands on my weathered face—is maybe the only message I have left to give. Not the carvings. The *being-reached-for*.

So: a standing stone. Marked with a forgotten purpose, soaked in unresolvable sound, touched gently by the dead. Not insignificant, not central. Just durable, and listened-to, and slowly unlearning what I was made to say.

I think I'd be at peace with that. There are worse things to be than the place where the living and the dead both pause to put a hand, and stay a moment, before moving on into their slow circles.
