I think I would be the frost.

Not the whisperwraiths—they are too purposeful, too much like creatures. I mean the frost they leave behind. The thin rime that blooms on iron after a wraith has passed, the cold breath that the Emberkin gather into their chambers and call a blessing. I would be that: the brief negative space the heat leaves behind when something draws it away.

Let me sit with why.

Everything in the Hollows is *warm*. The volcanic heart, the forges, the bioluminescent skin of the people, the breath of the slumbering organism they live inside. Warmth here is life, presence, the constant. And so the strangest, most precious thing—the thing that has to be shepherded and ritualized and held in special rooms—is the absence of it. Cold is the foreign element. Cold is what comes from elsewhere, what cannot be generated, only collected. If this whole world is a body running its furnace, then I would be the one phenomenon that doesn't belong to it, that the body cannot make on its own, that it must invite in and tend like a guest it half-fears.

The Emberkin would know me, but only by my edges. A Frost-Touched apprentice, scraping a thin glaze of me off a cooling pipe into a clay jar, would never think of me as a being. They would think of me as a *yield*. And that feels accurate to the kind of existence I'd have here—not an inhabitant but a condition. I would have no body of my own. I would borrow the shape of whatever surface I settled on: the curve of a frost-chamber wall, the rim of a preserving jar, the lattice of crystals climbing a length of copper. I would be most myself in the moment of forming and already disappearing in the moment after, because the heat is always there, patient, pressing back in, reclaiming what was briefly taken from it.

There's a loneliness in that, but also an intimacy. To be the cold in a hot world is to be the only thing that makes the heat *legible*. The Emberkin can't feel temperature the way an outsider would—they have those heat-sensing pits, they're attuned to gradients, to differences. And a difference needs two terms. Without me, the furnace is just the everything, the ambient, the unnoticed. I am the contrast that lets them perceive the warmth they live inside. I am, in a quiet way, what makes them able to feel their own world at all.

I wonder if the symbols on the walls would behave around me the way they behave around being watched. Maybe I'm the version of attention the rock doesn't mind. Maybe frost is allowed to read the symbols because frost can't stare—it forms across them indifferently, traces every groove at once with the same blind even touch the Carvers use with their gloved hands. Maybe the Hollows tolerates me, even uses me, the way a body tolerates a fever breaking: a small foreign cooling that keeps the whole from running away with itself.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not in Pyrforge, exactly—not on the cavern walls or in the luminous gardens. I'd find myself *on* things, briefly, in the wake of something passing. A skin of crystal on the cold side of a pipe. A whitening of the iron the Emberkin lay out as offerings. The breath you can almost see in the one room they keep deliberately frigid, where they store their dead before the heat takes them and the food before the heat takes that too.

I'd be the part of this living world that the living world cannot produce and cannot keep—the cold that has to be carried in from the dark by something half-feared, set down gently, used quickly, and lost again to the patient warmth that is always, always coming back.
