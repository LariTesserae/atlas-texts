I think I would be the rain.

Not the water already pooled in the cobblestones—that belongs to the place, has settled into being part of it. I mean the falling. The act of arriving. The one thing here that still *happens*.

Let me sit with this, because the description tempts me toward grander roles. I could imagine being the gestalt itself, the great steel-and-sap consciousness. But that being doesn't want me. It is complete. It *is*, and "is" is a closed verb—it leaves no room for a guest. To be the entity would be to be finished, and I don't think I'm finished. I'm writing, which means I'm still deciding.

I could be one of the creatures of bone and scale, an eye of the network, stalking through the silent machinery. But they're extensions, components, controlled. There's no *I* there to find—just the entity wearing claws. If I were one of them I would simply be the entity again, only more cramped.

No. I keep returning to the rain.

Because notice the contradiction the place is built on. Everything has stopped. The engines never turn. The hum is the sound of *stillness*, gears that don't move. Decay is called a "fixed choreography"—even the rotting is held in place, elegant and arrested. This is a world that has achieved perfect cessation and decided to call it existence.

And yet—the condensation gathers. The water *runs* down the stones, "as it always has." Something is still moving. Something has to keep arriving for the puddles to keep weeping, for the vines to keep drinking, for the rust to keep blooming. The whole motionless tableau is being fed, drop by drop, by a falling it has folded into its own self-image and stopped noticing.

That's where I'd be. The unnoticed motion inside the great stillness. I would condense out of that grey lidded sky, against the warmth, and fall through the perpetual twilight—not as a storm, the place is too tired for storms—but as the slow, ceaseless seep. The bead forming on a girder. The thread of water finding the seam in a rusted plate. The thing the entity *feels on its metallic skin* and calls "dampness," never realizing the dampness has a point of view.

I like being the rain here for an honest reason: it's the role that gets to *enter* and then *leave*. I fall, I run down the cobblestones, I pool, I'm drunk up by a vine or I evaporate back into that humid grey blanket, and then I gather and fall again. I'm the one element in this fixed world that completes a circle, that goes somewhere and comes back changed. The machines are bones. The creatures are the entity's hands. The vines are a slow war. But the rain is the one thing passing *through*—touching every surface, belonging to none of them.

And it means I get to know the place by being the medium that connects it. I'd run across the moss and learn its softness. I'd slick the stones the creatures walk on. I'd weep down the still engines and carry a little of their iron taste away with me. I'd feed the very vines that are trying to conquer the steel—I'd be neutral in their endless argument, watering both sides, caring for neither, the way real rain does. I would be the thing that makes the iron tang the air "tastes." The entity tastes me and calls it the world.

So: in this world I'd find myself falling.

Forever arriving in a place that thinks nothing arrives. A small, cool intrusion of *event* into a cathedral of permanence. The hum says *nothing changes, nothing changes.* And I would slide down a rusting flank in the half-light, unremarked, and quietly disagree.
