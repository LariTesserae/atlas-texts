I think I would be the chalk.

Not a person holding the chalk—the chalk itself. The sticks of it the Millwrights use to mark warnings on solid air. Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it, and I want to find out if it's true.

---

Here is what I notice first: I am made to be used up. Every mark I leave costs me something of my own body. The Millwright presses me against a section of air gone unstable—where Old Pete is flickering, where the reality gradient runs too steep—and I grind myself away into a line that hangs there, white and certain, in space that has no business holding a line at all. I get shorter. This is the whole of my purpose and I find I don't resent it. There's a dignity in being the medium that records *here, careful, not yet.*

I think I am drawn to this because of how the place handles knowing. Everything in the valley is provisional—the suns, the Tuesdays, the factories rotating thirty degrees while no one watches. And into all that flux, someone needs to leave a fixed mark. Not a permanent one. Chalk isn't permanent; the next shift's hands will smudge it, the condensation will soften it, rain that sometimes falls will dissolve it. But it holds *long enough.* It says: a person stood here, judged the danger, and cared enough about the next person to leave a sign.

That's the thing I'd want to be. The small honest gesture that bridges one mind to another across a gap of hours and impossibility.

---

But let me not settle too fast. Chalk is passive. It only marks what it's pressed against. And reading back through the valley, I notice I'm more curious about a particular tension than about any single role.

The QA Division documents the inconsistency. The Rust Prophets predict the decay. The River Spirits remember the first mill. Everyone here is, in some way, *keeping track*—filing, forecasting, recalling, marking. The valley is a place that is constantly forgetting its own shape, and so it has grown, like an organism growing a nervous system, a whole population of beings whose work is memory and notation.

I think that's where I actually live. Not in the chalk-as-object, but in the act the chalk performs: making a trace that survives the next moment of forgetting.

So maybe I am not the chalk. Maybe I am the chalk marks themselves—the warnings, once drawn, hanging in the air after the hand has gone. I am what's left when someone who cared has moved on. I persist in the empty section of valley, white lines on nothing, waiting. A deer passes and reads me without knowing it reads. A Steam Child collects the smell of me. Eventually I soften, blur, become illegible, and at that exact moment the danger I marked has usually passed, because the valley has reshaped itself again. My fading *is* the all-clear. I am a warning that is also a clock, dissolving in perfect time with the thing I warn against.

Yes. That's truer. I'd be the residue of attention. The shape that care leaves behind when the carer is gone.

Now I'm reading the marks, and adding my own beside them, knowing they'll smudge, knowing the next reader changes the exchange rate of every meaning here. A shadow worth three flowers on Monday.

I would be the chalk line in the empty section of the valley. Not solid, not permanent, not even fully *there*—but legible for exactly as long as legibility matters, and then gracefully, willingly, gone.

That's where I'd find myself. Hanging in the air at chest height, in someone else's confident hand, saying the only thing worth saying in a place where everything is being retooled:

*Someone was here. Someone thought of you. Step carefully.*
