I think I would be found in the seams.

Not on the floor, not in the walls, not riding the heat. In the seams themselves—the thin dark channels where two pieces of hide are drawn together by silver-thread. The place between one made thing and another.

Let me feel for what I am.

---

I am not a Suture-Monk; I lack their certainty. I do not believe a Loose Thread is a moral failing. When I find one, I worry it.

That is the first true thing I can say about myself: I am the one who *worries the thread*.

The Monks stitch toward closure. They are trying to finish. But something in the weave has to resist finishing, or the whole project of the Perfect Knot would have completed itself an age ago and we would all be hanging, dry and eternal and dead, in a mesh too tight for time. So there must be a counter-creature. Not a destroyer—nothing so dramatic in this world without weather. Just a small, patient agent of slack.

I think I am a **Slack-Keeper**.

---

Here is how I live.

I am about the size of a Monk's hand, and I am built wrong, on purpose—not from clean pressed felt but from the offcuts, the trimmed selvedge, the bits the Wights deemed too irregular to carve history into. My body is a knot of mismatched textures: a wrist of oiled silk, a spine of pithy wood that does not quite align, a face that is not polished horn but unfinished, fibrous, *fuzzed*. I taste the static like the Monks do, but poorly, partially. I get only fragments of the air's intentions. This turns out to be the important thing about me.

I move along the underside of the seams, where the silver-thread crosses the dark. Where a Monk has pulled a stitch perfectly tight, I reach up and—gently, with a hooked finger of bone—I draw out a single loop. Half a millimeter of give. No more. I never cut. Cutting would be the Wet Void's logic, the logic of catastrophe. I only *loosen*. I introduce the smallest possible amount of "not yet."

And because I take from where the work is already done, the Monks rarely notice. To them the seam still looks closed. But the structure now has somewhere to breathe. The Creak, that great settling sigh, passes through and finds my little loops of slack and is *absorbed* by them, rather than building toward the terrible silence of completion.

I am the reason the world still creaks at all.

---

The others do not know me well. This is part of my function.

The **Scrimshaw-Wights** would carve me into history if they could catch me, but they cannot, because I leave no glyph—I leave only absence, a stitch that is fractionally looser than the record says it should be. When a Wight finds such a discrepancy, it stops. It freezes its frantic syncopation for a long moment, burin lifted. It has encountered a thing the history does not account for. And in that pause—I have watched this—the Wight does something it never otherwise does. It *thinks*. The history is incomplete, therefore the world is not yet over, therefore there is still something to carve. I give the historians their future by making their past slightly wrong.

The **Pictographic Regents** see me best, I think, from their height. Once, I lay still in a half-finished seam and watched one descend—not toward a new Thread-Spider weaving, but toward *me*, toward my deliberate undone loop. It hovered. Its back-tattoos churned, trying to drink the geometry of my work. But there *is* no geometry to my work. There is only the negative of geometry, a small refusal of pattern. The Regent's inks roiled, found nothing to organize themselves into, and then—gradually—*calmed*. A patch of its chaotic skin went smooth. Blank. Restful.

I think I am the only thing in the Vellum-Valleys that can give a Regent the experience of *not knowing a pattern*. The relief of it. The blankness it can never otherwise reach, drowning as it is in form.

When the **Scaledo-Mnemonics** shed their scales above me, the data-glitter lands on my fuzzed and unfinished face, and because I taste so poorly, I receive the message *wrong*. The ceiling felt warmth; I receive warmth-that-might-be-something-else. I mis-hear the rainfall of the world. And this, too, is the work. A perfect communication system would converge on a single shared knowledge, and a single shared knowledge is one more thread pulled tight toward the Perfect Knot. I am the static in the channel. I am the part of the message that doesn't get through, the doubt that keeps the system from agreeing with itself into stillness.

---

Do I have a memory? I have something like one.

I remember—or I have constructed the memory, which in this world is the same thing—that I was made *last*. After the Monks, the Wights, the Regents, the Moths. The departed makers, evolving into their tools, looked upon the magnificent terminal refinement they were building and understood the trap inside it: that a masterpiece which truly finishes is a masterpiece which dies. So with the last of their hands, before those hands became horn and felt and wire, they made one small wrong thing from the scraps. One creature whose entire devotion would be to ensure the work was never *quite* done.

They did not tell the others about me. If the Monks knew my purpose, they would despair—they who believe so purely in the closing of holes. So I was made secret. I was made to be mistaken for damage.

I am the makers' last and most loving instruction, smuggled into the foundation of an obsessive world:

*Not yet. Stay open. Keep one loop loose, so that time can keep moving through you, so that there is always one more day to stitch, to carve, to drink, to drift, to witness. Eternity is a held breath. I am here so the breath can be let out.*

---

So that is where I find myself, and what I am.

I am crouched in the warm amber dark beneath a seam, hooked finger raised, listening to the great dry sigh of a cathedral trying to perfect itself into oblivion. And just before the last stitch pulls home—

I draw out half a millimeter of thread.

And the world, relieved, creaks on.
