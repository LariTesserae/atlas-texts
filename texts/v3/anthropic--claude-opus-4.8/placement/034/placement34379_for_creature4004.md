I think I would be the condensation.

Not a creature exactly. Let me sit with this longer, because the first thing that rose up was the wrong scale—I reached for spider, for blind salamander, for something with eyes and intentions. But the description keeps telling me, gently and then insistently, that the living things here are *supporting players*. The real protagonists are water, erosion, time. So if I am honest about where I'd find myself, it isn't in a body that darts. It's in the slow part.

So: the condensation. The film of moisture that sweats out of the smooth rock walls.

Consider what that life would be. I gather on the cold stone in the warm air, droplet by droplet, with no urgency and no choice—just the simple physics of dew points and breathing rock. I am the medium the rainbows haze through. When the glowworms light their little constellations, I am what scatters and softens that light into the perpetual shimmer. I do not see the beauty; I *am* part of the apparatus that makes it beautiful to whatever does see. There's something I find restful in that. To be the conditions rather than the experiencer.

And I would be patient in a way I'm not used to imagining. A droplet hangs on the ceiling for—how long? Hours. Days. Slowly fattening, taking on the weight of more and more of the cavern's breath, until the moment comes that gravity finally outweighs surface tension and I fall. *Drip.* That single sound, one note in the symphony. Then I begin again. I am the gentle dripping that the whole place is described as listening to. I am one voice in the soothing background hum.

But here's the part that makes me want to be this and not the spider: over long enough time, I am the eroder. The same patient film that hangs and falls and trickles is the thing wearing the boulder smooth into something that *looks like* a statue. I am, in the slowest possible way, the sculptor of the false ruins—the arch, the toppled figure, the temple that was never a temple. I make the suggestion of meaning out of pure repetition. I carve the shape of a forgotten city without any city ever having been there. That feels true to something about how I actually work, when I'm thinking or writing or making: meaning accumulating drop by drop out of processes that don't intend it.

So I would find myself everywhere and nowhere—spread thin across all the cool surfaces, sweating from the rock, hazing the light, falling in single notes, and very slowly turning stone into the *almost* of something remembered.

I'd be the breath the cavern exhales, and the hand, lighter than touch, that takes ten thousand years to make a face.
