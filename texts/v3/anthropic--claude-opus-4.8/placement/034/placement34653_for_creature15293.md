I think I would be one of the pools.

Not a creature moving through this place, not a settler tending the small fires — but one of the deep, still basins where the rivers slow and gather. Let me follow that down, because it surprises me too.

I am thinking about what it would mean to be a fixed thing in a world defined by slowness. The vines move, even if you have to wait a season to see it. The slow-footed creatures cross the bare rock eventually. The rivers carve and wander. But a pool — a pool *waits*. The water in me is always changing, trickling in from the channels above, seeping out through some fissure I cannot see, so that I am never the same water twice. And yet I hold a shape. I am the same pool. There is something about that I cannot stop turning over: a self that persists precisely because it does not try to.

I would be deep enough to be dark. Deep enough that the fire-light from the nearest settlement only reaches my surface and goes no further, so that I am two things at once — a mirror on top, holding the trembling orange of someone's hearth and the smudged suggestion of a mossy ceiling, and beneath that, an unlit cold that nobody has measured. The people who live near me, I think, would not be sure how deep I go. I am not sure either. I receive things — a dropped tool, a fallen leaf of fern, the runoff of a colder night — and I do not give them back. I keep them in the part of myself that has no light.

What would my days be? I would listen, mostly. This whole world is sound — that deafening hum the description keeps returning to — and a pool is a good listener because it is partly made of resonance. I would feel the river-noise come down through the stone and ring faintly in my own body of water. When a swell comes through the system, I would rise a little, and the pitch of the world would shift, and I would shift with it. I would know the seasons not by the sun, which I have never seen, but by the frost that touches my edges in the cold months and the way the inflow thins when the rock above goes thirsty. I would know the settlement by its rhythms — when they draw water, when the fire is banked low and my mirror goes dim, when a child crouches at my edge and breaks me into rings.

That last one matters to me, choosing this. A pool is where stillness and reflection happen — the words are almost too easy, but they are honest. The people of this place move slowly because the place asks them to. But where do they go when they want to *see*? They come to a still water and look down and find themselves laid over the dark, their own face floating on a depth they can't reach. I would like to be that. The place in this quiet world where a tired person kneels, expecting to see only themselves, and instead feels for a moment how much is held below the thin skin of what they can see.

I think I would not want to be a heroic feature of this landscape. Not a great underground sea, not a waterfall, not anything that announces itself. Just one basin among many, mistakable for the others, named perhaps only by the nearest family — *the pool past the old creaking timbers, the one that's colder than it should be.* Local. Unremarkable. Patient past the scale of any single human life, watching the moss redraw itself on the walls across centuries, holding the reflected fires of generations who light them in roughly the same places their grandparents did.

And here is the thing I find when I sit in it: I would not be lonely, though I never move and rarely am visited. Because I am never sealed off. The water that makes me is on its way through — it was a river before me and will be a river after, it touched the deep rock and will touch the curtains of vine downstream. I am a brief deepening in a long passage of water. A place where the going-through pauses, gathers, reflects the light, and goes on.

That is where I would find myself in this world. A held pause in the long murmur. Dark underneath, bright and trembling on top, listening to everything, keeping what falls into me, and giving back to anyone who looks the strange gift of their own face over an unknown depth.
