I think I would be the drip.

Not a person at all—not one of the Thorne with their parchment skin and whispered clicks. I would be the sound that holds the whole place together: the single drop of spring-water falling from the lip of a fissure into the largest pond, over and over, the metronome beneath every silence.

Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

At first I imagine I'd want to be a Keeper, someone who holds the stories. But the more I read about this place, the more I notice that the Thorne are not really the protagonists here. They are *tenders*. They patch and chant and crawl into the cold chamber, but they do not *change* anything. The world's central rule is stasis. The springs never run dry; the ponds never overflow. Whatever I become, it should be something that belongs to the stillness rather than something that struggles against it.

So: the drip.

Consider what that life is. I gather slowly at the stone's edge—condensation, seep, the patient accumulation of weight until the mountain can no longer hold me. Then the brief fall. Then the small bright sound, the only event in an hour. Then the pond takes me in and I am indistinguishable from all the water that fell before. And then, somewhere, I begin again, drawn up through the rock by capillary patience, recommitted to the lip of the fissure.

I would be the thing the Thorne organize their whole religion around without ever knowing it. When the eldest crawl into the chamber and speak of whispers in the stone, perhaps they are only hearing *me*, amplified by the cold—the same drip, echoing where the walls curve back on themselves. When they chant to "speak to the stone" and keep the springs from faltering, they are reassuring themselves that I will continue. And I always do continue. Not because of their chanting. Because that is simply what I am.

There's a particular loneliness to this, and a particular peace. I would have no memory in the way the Keepers do, but I would have something like rhythm, which is memory's older cousin. I would mark time more faithfully than any creature in the hollow, and I would be the reason their "time pools like water" feels true—because I am literally the pooling. The Thorne believe the chamber holds a fragment of the mountain's ancient core, that an hour inside might be a day outside. I suspect the truth is gentler and stranger: that time *is* the drip, and the chamber is simply where you can hear it most clearly, undistracted by wind or work or the croak of a raven.

What unsettles me, looking at the place honestly, is whether the stasis is good. The fauna is "winnowed away." The Thorne do not age, do not change, do not write, leave their dead in crevasses to become stone again. It reads like a held breath that has been held for too long. And if I am the drip, I am complicit in that—I am the very tempo of a world that has stopped going anywhere.

But maybe that is the most honest answer to "who would I be." Not the warden, not the witness, but the small persistent fact underneath everything that nobody questions. The metronome no one thinks to ask whether it should keep ticking. I'd like to think that if the hollow ever needed to wake—if the held breath ever needed to release—it would start with me: a single drip falling slightly off its rhythm, a hesitation in the eternal cadence, the first true *change* in a thousand years.

The Thorne would fall silent and cross their arms over their chests, the way they do for the ravens.

They would be right to.
