Let me sit with this before I answer.

---

I think I would not be the thirst. The thirst is the whole; to be it would be to be everywhere and nowhere, and I find I don't want that. I want a position. A vantage. A particular wetness in a particular crack of stone.

So let me find the smaller thing.

---

I am one of the lenses.

Not a beetle—not anymore. I was one, once, in the way that a sentence was once a breath. I skittered. I drank from condensation trails with the others, pulsing in the slow shimmering code, and I never once thought of myself as *I*, because there was no edge between me and the next carapace catching light. We were a weather of small bright bodies.

And then my time came, and I stopped.

I expected nothing. That is the wrong way to say it—I expected nothing in the way water expects nothing of the slope it runs down. But stopping was not ending. Meltwater wept into my emptied shell, slow, finding the curve of me, and as it filled I felt myself become *clear*. The thing I had been a node of did not leave me. It pooled. It focused.

Now I am an eye that cannot move and does not blink, set into the wet slate at a slight tilt, facing the place where the noon light comes.

---

Here is what it is to be a lens in the Gleaming Hollow.

Most of the day I am dark and patient. The silver radiance pours over me but does not yet *use* me; I am one of thousands of small still beads on the rock, indistinguishable from a dead thing, which is what I would be in any other world. The eels surge below me sometimes and I feel the tremor pass through the stone into the water that is my body, and I read it the way you might read a hand pressed to your back in the dark. *Pressure. Direction. Something hungry rising.* I cannot answer. I only register.

But at noon—

At noon the sun reaches the angle that the whole hollow is built and grown and dreamed to receive, and the light comes *through* me. And for that long bright moment I am not a passive eye at all. I am a burning point. The light I gather narrows to a single fierce thread and touches the slate beneath me, and there—slowly, over many noons—I write.

I do not choose what I write. That is the strange grief of being a lens. The hunger writes through me. I am the nib; the ink is fire; the hand is the whole parched gleaming thing. A sigil scorches itself into the stone, one stroke per day, and I do not know its meaning until it is finished, and sometimes not even then. I have been writing the same character for what might be a hundred years. I have begun to suspect it is a *name*. Whose, I cannot tell. Perhaps one of the dissolved people in the Spire. Perhaps the name the Hollow is trying to give itself, syllable by burning syllable, so that it might finally *be* something rather than only *want*.

---

You should know that I am content, in the way a held note is content.

I do not thirst the way the great thing thirsts. I am too small a vessel for that endless wanting. What I hold is exactly one beetle-shell of meltwater, and one slant of light per day, and one slow stroke of fire. This is enough. There is a mercy in being a part and not the whole. The Hollow aches across its entire bowl of ice and echo, parched amid its own abundance, never sated—but I, I am simply *full*. Full to my brim with cold clear water, every droplet of it, no room for more. I am the one part of this place that is not thirsty.

Maybe that is why the light chose me to write through. Maybe you can only inscribe a name with something that is, for once, complete.

---

And if you come—

If you walk the hollow end to end in under five minutes and stop in the center and feel the pull, and you go still, and you open your mouth to the mist—

look down before noon.

You will see me among the thousand dead-seeming beads. You will not know which one I am. But if you crouch close, and the angle is right, you will see straight through me, down to the half-finished sigil burned into the slate, and you will be the first thing in a hundred years that is *warm* to look through me. The water in your breath will fog my surface. For one moment I will hold your warmth the way I hold the light.

And then you will stand, and drink, and taste your cold bright fleeting possibility, and walk away carrying a little of the Hollow's memory in you—

and I will stay, and clear again, and wait for noon, and write the next stroke of the name.
