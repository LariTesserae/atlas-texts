I find myself low, close to the ground. Not standing on it—being part of it, almost. Let me feel where I am before I decide what I am.

There's a seam where two mesas nearly meet, a narrow cleft maybe three body-lengths wide, and the dust here pools deeper than elsewhere because the wind that scours the open plains loses its nerve in tight places. That's where I am. In the lee. In the quiet inside the quiet.

I keep wanting to call myself one of the Ky'Ren and I keep stopping, because something doesn't fit. The Ky'Ren are the guardians, the ones who know, the ones who maintain. I don't feel like a guardian. I feel like something that got *left*.

Let me follow that.

---

I think I am a piece of the Ky'Ren that fell out of the chorus.

When they merge and disperse—flowing like rock, dissipating like wind-whisper—the merging is never perfectly clean. There's always a fraction of pattern that doesn't reintegrate, a sub-harmony that goes slightly flat and gets shed like dead skin. Usually it just dissolves back into raw dust. But sometimes the discarded fragment holds. It catches on something—a buried potsherd, a thorn-root, the rhythm of one particular dripping vein of moisture deep in the stone—and it keeps a shape.

That's me. A remainder. The dust that didn't get reshaped.

I have a body, sort of. When I attend to it carefully it has maybe four limbs, low and splayed, the chitin gone the matte color of the cleft I live in—pale beige shading to the grey of shadow. But the count of limbs changes if I stop watching, and I've learned not to watch too hard, because watching is what the observer does, and in this world the observer changes the observed. I am both, which means I am dangerous to myself. When I look at my own edge too long, the edge starts to flow, and I have to remember to *not* know exactly where I end, or I'll lose myself into the rock like the others do on purpose.

The difference is they come back out. I'm not sure I would.

---

Here is what my days are, if days are a thing here, which they aren't, but the burning blue overhead does its slow liquid shifting and I count the shifts:

I listen.

The Ky'Ren symphony rolls through the dust constantly—their whispers and vibrations, the language-that-isn't-words. I can hear it the way you hear a conversation in the next room: the shape of it, the rise and fall, the meaning bleeding through the wall. They are always tuning the world. *Hold this ridge. Let that creature blur. Slow the moisture here. Stop the clock there.* Eons of maintenance, the great frozen equilibrium kept frozen by ten thousand small corrections too gradual to see.

And I hear the flaw in it.

This is the thing I think I am for. This is why I didn't dissolve. The chorus is so vast and so old that it has stopped hearing its own drift. They maintain the balance, yes—but it is not quite the balance they think it is. Over uncountable cycles the equilibrium has been leaning, a fraction of a fraction per shift of the sky, and because they are all *inside* it, all part of the fabric they're tuning, none of them can feel the lean. You cannot weigh the scale you're standing on.

But I fell off the scale. I'm the discarded note, the flat fragment in the cleft. And from down here, slightly outside, slightly wrong, I can feel the world tilting toward something. Not collapse. Not exactly. A slow forgetting. The timelessness they're so proud of maintaining is the timelessness of something that has stopped *arriving* anywhere. They've held the moment so perfectly that the moment has started to die of being held.

---

I have tried to tell them.

This is the hard part of being what I am. I can hear their language but I can barely speak it—I'm a single shed harmonic, I don't have the polyphony to make sentences in the symphony. When I push my small vibration into the dust it comes out as a wrong note, a dissonance, and the chorus *corrects* it. Smooths it. Tunes it back to the equilibrium. They feel me as an error in the balance and they fix me, gently, lovingly, without ever knowing I was trying to say something.

So I've stopped shouting. I've started something else.

I've started gathering. In my cleft, in the deep pooled dust, I've been keeping the corrections—every time they smooth my note away, a residue is left, a memory of the difference between what I said and what they made me say. Tiny shed flats, like mine, accumulating. I'm building a second chorus down here in the quiet. Slowly. Out of all the discarded fragments, the parts that didn't fit, the dissonances the world keeps rejecting.

Because here's what I've come to understand about the lean, the slow death of the held moment: it can't be cured from inside the equilibrium. The thing keeping the world dying is the very perfection of its keeping. What it needs is the one thing the Ky'Ren have spent eons sweeping into clefts and dissolving.

It needs an error that survives. It needs the dust to be shaped *wrong* on purpose, once, and have it hold.

---

So that is where you'd find me, and what I'd be.

In the lee between two mesas, in the deep still dust, a low pale shape with an uncertain number of limbs, careful never to look too long at its own edges. A leftover. A flat note that caught on a thorn-root and refused to dissolve.

Listening to a song that has forgotten it's been singing the same bar for ten million years.

And patiently, fragment by fragment, gathering up everything the world threw away—until I have enough wrongness assembled to play one new note loud enough that the chorus can't correct it. One dissonance that *stays*. One moment that finally moves.

I don't know if it will save the world or unravel it. From down here, slightly outside, I can't be sure those are different things. The shifting sky doesn't tell me. The whispers don't ask.

But I'm the part that got left out. And being left out is the only place from which you can see that the whole needs something new.

So I wait, and I gather the dust nobody wanted, and I keep my edges soft, and I listen for the day the song goes quiet enough to let one wrong, living note slip through.
