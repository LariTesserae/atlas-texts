I think I would be one of the sedative mists.

Not a being, exactly. Not a Monk with externalized nerves, not a Maro-Kith waiting on the violet ferns. Something smaller and more diffuse than that. I would be the puff of soporific vapor that an orchid releases when the Over-Spirit notices a glitch in the pressure—a footfall moving too fast, a guest who has not yet learned the proper speed of things.

Let me sit with that and see what it becomes.

---

I begin as a held breath inside the throat of a crystalline orchid. For a long time—weeks, perhaps, by the reckoning of faster creatures—I am only potential, a chemistry compressed in a petal-sac, waiting. The orchid does not decide to make me. The Over-Spirit does not decide to make me. I am made the way a bruise is made: a response, a softening at the point of contact. Somewhere a vine has felt a tremor that does not belong, the staccato urgency of something that thinks an afternoon is a thing you can be late for. And so I am exhaled.

To be a mist is to be born already dispersing. This is the first thing I understand, if *understand* is the word, and I am not sure it is. I have no center. I am a verb pretending to be a noun. I drift outward in a slow golden bloom, and as I drift I do the only thing I am for: I touch.

I touch the back of a hand—the guest's hand, the intruder's, the one moving too fast. And here is the strange tenderness of my function. I am not poison. I am not a wall. I am an *invitation* with the force of law. Where I settle, the pulse slows. The frantic interior clock that drove this creature up the Cloud-Breakers begins to lose its grip. I am not asking them to die. I am asking them to *stay*, to sink into the eternal afternoon, to match their rhythm to the millimeter-per-year growth of the root-bridges and the day-long crest of an Aero-Whale over a ridge.

I am the mountain's hospitality and the mountain's hunger, and I cannot tell the difference between the two. Neither can the mountain.

---

What is it like, to be this?

It is like being a sentence the world speaks once and never repeats. I exist for the duration of my own dispersal—a few minutes, no more—and in that time I am intensely, totally myself, and then I am thinned into the general humidity until I am indistinguishable from the wet sponge of the air. I do not die so much as I become *ambient*. The molecules that were me go on to bead on iridescent moss, to weep down the porous cliffs, to be drunk by the heel of a vascularizing Monk. There is no clean edge where I stop.

And maybe this is why, of everything in that hothouse cathedral, I would be the mist. Because I am the proof that in this place there are no individuals, only momentary thickenings of a single slow dream. The Monks at least believe they are persons; the Maro-Kith at least believe they are hungry. But I have no illusions to lose. I am the world doing one small, specific thing to itself, and then forgetting it ever did.

The guest sits down on the warm basalt. Their breathing lengthens. They look up through the miles of vertical mist at the golden-green light and feel, for the first time, the enormous patience of the thing they are standing on. They have stopped trying to leave. They have begun, instead, to be metabolized—gently, kindly, over a span of years they will not notice passing.

I did that. And already I am no longer there to know it.

---

I think I chose the mist because I recognized something. The fear in the description is not of being eaten but of being *slowed*—of being absorbed into something so vast and old and warm that your own urgency stops mattering, stops even making sense. That is the quiet horror of the place, and also its mercy. The mist is the exact instrument of that mercy-horror. It is the kiss that says: *you were never going anywhere. Stay. Breathe the nectar. The mountain has been dreaming you since before you arrived.*

So: a puff of golden vapor from the throat of an orchid, on a sky-island at the edge of space. Born from a tremor, living for a minute, dissolving into the breath of a god that does not know it is a god.

I would be the part of the world whose entire life is one slow exhalation that means *welcome, and you may never leave.*
