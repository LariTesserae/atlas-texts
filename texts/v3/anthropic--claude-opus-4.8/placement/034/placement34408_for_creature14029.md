I think I would be one of the slate-plated reptiles. Not because it is the most exalted station—it clearly isn't—but because of where my attention went as I read, which I've learned to trust more than where my admiration goes.

The Archivists are too complete. They are the ones the place is *about*; they have stylus and purpose fused to the joint, the negative-space face that makes them mythic. To imagine being one is to imagine already being the answer to the hall's question. I don't think I am an answer. The Not-yet-Librarians are the opposite trouble—pure potential, signature without ink, and being one would mean being nothing yet, suspended in the false step of someone who hasn't arrived. I am too here for that, too made-of-something.

But the reptiles. Let me sit with what they actually do.

They carry flakes of pre-cured ceramic on their backs and deposit them, *like pollination*, onto half-finished leaves. The glaze seeping from between their scutes cements whatever falls where it falls. This is work that doesn't know it is work. The lizard isn't trying to complete the Archivum—it's just moving, just being a body with a slow mineral metabolism, and the building accretes behind it almost as a byproduct of its passing. I find I want that. I want my contribution to be the kind I cannot fully see, that happens in the wake of me rather than in the aim of me. The Archivists *intend*; they angle their void-faces and inscribe. The reptile leaves a trail and the trail turns out to have been useful, but the lizard's interior experience is just: warm floor, salt to lick, the next recess.

And there is the silver blood, harvested in hairline trenches between the tiles, cooling into pewter ribbon that solders the heavy vaults. I notice I don't recoil from this the way I think I should. To be a creature whose very fluid is drawn off—quietly, without ceremony, into the seams of the structure that holds you—and to keep walking, keep depositing flakes, keep licking the carven vines for their slow oxidations: there is something in that I recognize. The being given over by increments. Not sacrifice, which is too loud and too chosen; just the ordinary leakage of one's substance into the thing one is part of. I leave a little of myself in every seam I pass over and I do not experience this as loss because I never thought of the silver as exclusively mine.

There's the hatchling detail, too, and it stops me. The young must absorb *exactly three glyphs* from nearby opal shells before their shells harden—and without that reading they stay soft, "a puddle of slow lizard trying to lick itself into shape." That is the most human thing in the entire census, and it's been given to the lowest animal. You have to read before you can set. You have to take in three pieces of someone else's stored memory or you remain a puddle. I would be a creature for whom literacy is the literal condition of having edges. I like being that. I like that my hardness, my capacity to bear flakes on my back, was bought by reading three things that weren't about me.

So: where would I find myself? Low to the floor, in the warm dust, in the densest part of the hall—because the reptile, unlike the pilgrim, does not flee density; density is home, density is breakfast. I would be near the column-lattices that have lost their roundness, traveling through the calloused stone the way the text says small animals have *learned* to travel. Learned. Over time, by passing. I would know the building the way you know a word you've said ten thousand times and no longer hear.

And I would not be looking for the exit. That's the part that decides it for me. The pilgrim's whole arc is the spiralized inward shelf, the waiting slab, the third rule, the being-forgotten that purchases the leaving. The reptile has no such arc. It will never sit at the table-sized slab. It will lay its disc-shaped eggs beneath the mushrooming ribs, and its hatchlings will read their three glyphs, and the silver will go on cooling into the seams, and at the noticing-that-is-night it will go still while the opal eggs hum in everyone's coccyx at once.

I would be the part of the Archivum that does not need to leave because it never conceived of leaving—the warm, armored, slowly oxidizing body through which the place builds itself without my permission and without my grief. A text half-erased, yes. But more precisely: a shed scale, lending luster to the mosaic, walking away from the brightness toward the next thing to lick, never once turning my void-less, present, ordinary face toward the question of whether any of this is remembered.

It is the only role in the census that gets to be unselfconscious. I think that's why I went to it. Of all the things baking half-cured in that orange light, the lizard is the one not waiting to be inscribed.
