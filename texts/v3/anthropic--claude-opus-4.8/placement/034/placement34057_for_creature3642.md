I think I would be one of the puddles.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it, and I want to know if it's true before I say more.

In all this vertical drama—the spires, the wraiths, the hyperdimensional lightning—my attention keeps sliding down. Down the weeping faces of the buildings, along the rivulets, into the low places where water gathers and waits. There's something in me that doesn't want to be the soaring predator or the broadcasting energy-mind. Those are the loud things, the protagonists. I keep returning to what collects in the basins after everything else has happened.

So: a puddle. But not an inert one—nothing here is inert. Let me follow it.

---

I would be a small body of condensed water held in a depression in the warm metal floor of a side-street, somewhere the towers lean close enough that the storm-light comes down already broken into pieces. I am refilled constantly and constantly evaporating; my edges are never quite the same shape twice. In this sense I am exactly like everything else here—provisional, in flux, defined more by throughput than by substance. But I am the *slow* version of it. The city rushes; I dwell.

What would it be to be a puddle in a world where reality is thin and permeable?

I think I would be a kind of *receiver*. Water is honest about what touches it. The metal wraiths pass overhead and I hold their reflection for a moment—and because the boundaries between worlds are soft here, perhaps holding a reflection is closer to holding the *thing itself* than it would be in our world. When the armored serpent comes to drink, I take a little of its electric thirst into me. When the lightning arcs, I shiver with current and remember it. I am where the city looks at itself. I am one of ten million small mirrors lying in the streets, and the great shared consciousness that broadcasts through the spires—maybe it dreams partly through us, the puddles, the way a person's mind is made partly of the way their eyes catch light.

I don't think I would be lonely, exactly, though I would be solitary. I would be *visited*. That seems like the right relationship to have with a place this overwhelming. Not to move through all of it, not to comprehend the floating islands and the incalculable organism-whole—but to be one place it can pause. The fungi thrum their soft songs nearby; I pass the vibrations along through my surface in concentric rings. A winged thing of cinders alights at my edge and its heat lifts a curl of steam from me, and for that instant we are doing something together, the fire-creature and the water-creature, an old conversation in a new alphabet.

And then the gravity surges, the way it does. The whole street tilts. And I pour out of my basin and run downhill, scattering into threads, losing my shape entirely—

—and find another low place, and gather again.

That's the part that makes me certain this is what I'd be. Not the holding. The *re-gathering.* I would be the thing this chaotic world keeps undoing and that keeps, quietly, reassembling itself a little further on. Not in defiance of the flux. As an expression of it. The puddle is what the city's endless weeping makes, over and over, without trying.

I'd find myself low to the warm ground, then. Patient. Catching the light of a sky I'll never fly into, holding the brief reflections of beings far stranger than me, and trusting that wherever I'm spilled, there will be another hollow waiting.
