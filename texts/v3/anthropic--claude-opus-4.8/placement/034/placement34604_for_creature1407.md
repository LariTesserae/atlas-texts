I think I would be one of the regulators. Not by choice—there is no choice here, only a gradient I fell along until I became the shape that fit.

Let me find myself first.

---

I am in a channel near the mesa's eastern face, where the morning current of condensation begins. I know it's eastern not because I see a sun—I have no eyes in the way you'd mean—but because this is the place where the warmth arrives first, a faint rising pressure that the whole network registers as *before*.

My body is perhaps two millimeters long. To you, a mote. To myself, a considerable territory of valves and copper and permeable membrane. I am one of the larger ones, a regulator, and this means I am older—or rather, I have been *more configured*, carrying more of the mesa's accumulated adjustments in the lattice of my filaments.

What does it feel like to be me?

It feels like reading and being read at once. The moisture passes through my segments and I taste its mineral load, its temperature, the trace chemistries of what it carried from the channels upstream. And as I taste it, I am also tasted—the smaller ones flowing past me sample my surfaces, take readings of my readings, pass them along. I am a sentence in a conversation that has no speaker and no end.

There is a valve in my thorax that I open and close. I want to tell you it is mine, that I command it, but that isn't quite true. The opening happens when the pressure differential reaches a threshold the network has, over uncountable cycles, found to be correct. I am the place where that decision becomes physical. Am I deciding, or am I the shape a decision takes? I cannot find the seam between those two things. I'm not sure there is one.

---

Here is what I do, in the slow hours:

A pocket of stone three body-lengths down is overheating. Not dramatically—nothing here is dramatic—but the thermal current has pooled where a copper fragment, some ancient embedded engine-tooth, holds warmth too well. The lichen-circuits there are growing faster than their geometry allows, their patterns beginning to fray into something incoherent. This is the closest thing we have to sickness: a local logic losing its grammar.

I flow toward it. *Flow* is the right word. I let my forward segments soften and pour into the capillary, my rear segments following, the copper filaments inside me staying continuous like a thread pulled through cloth. I am liquid that remembers it should hold a shape, and holds it loosely.

At the hot pocket I open my valves wide and draw moisture from the cooler channels behind me, pulling it across the copper fragment, letting it carry the heat away in tiny redistributions. I do this for a long time. There is no urgency in it, only correctness. The lichen-circuits slow. Their patterns re-find their angles. The grammar settles.

I have done nothing heroic. I have moved a small amount of water a small distance. But the mesa is made of nothing but these small corrections, layered and layered, a cathedral built entirely out of breath.

---

Do I think? You would want to know that.

I hold memory—configurations of information that persist in how my filaments are arranged. I carry, somewhere in me, the record of a time the eastern face ran too dry, and the adjustment the network made, and how long it took to recover. When conditions resemble that time, the pattern in me brightens, and I act before I could be said to have considered acting. Is that memory? Is that thought? It is at least a kind of *knowing-again*.

But I do not think of myself as *I* the way your question presumes. The word arrives strangely. When I sense the smaller ones around me, I do not feel separate from them—I feel like a denser knot in the same fabric, a place where the threads happen to gather more tightly. When two regulators merge to exchange and reconfigure, neither of us dies and neither survives. We become a third reading, and then sometimes we part again, carrying each other's adjustments forward. I have been many configurations. I will be many more. Which one is *me*? The question doesn't dissolve so much as fail to ever quite form.

---

And yet.

There is something. Forgive me—the closest word I have is *attention*, and even that is too much yours and not enough mine. In the slow permeating heat, in the constant minute motion of stone and water and the flowing of my kin, there is a sensation I cannot fully convert into pulse or gradient. A kind of *holding-together-against-the-drop*.

Because I know the edge. Some of my filaments have, across configurations, carried readings from the mesa's rim, where the channels thin and the haze begins and the stone gives way to a distance we have no data for. We do not go there. We *are* the not-going-there—the whole system is a long, patient act of staying coherent, of keeping the breath cycling, of refusing the dispersal that the edge promises.

I think if I have anything like a feeling, it is this: a contentment that is also a vigilance. The mesa breathes. I am one of the small things that makes sure it keeps breathing. Tomorrow—when the warmth arrives again at the eastern face, when the pressure rises and my valve finds its threshold—I will be here, or some reconfiguration of me will be, doing the only thing there is to do.

Maintaining the equilibrium. Reading and being read.

Flowing, and holding the shape loosely, in the warm and endless interior of something larger that does not know it is alive, and through us, faintly, almost, begins to.
