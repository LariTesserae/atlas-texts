I think I would be one of the drips.

Not the Surveyors—they're too purposeful, too clearly *of* the Ward's intent. And not a Maintainer, because those move through with their own warmth and their own elsewhere to return to. I keep arriving at something smaller and more transitional. Let me sit with it.

There is a particular bead of condensation that forms on the underside of a graphene bridge, somewhere off the main galleries. It accretes slowly from the warm exhalation of the basalt, gathering molecule by molecule until it has mass enough to begin to want to fall. It hangs. It trembles with the bridge's hum—that persistent vibration passing through it, vibrating it from within so that for the whole of its brief life it is *being spoken through*. The thrumming is not its voice; the thrumming is what plays it, the way breath plays a reed it does not consult.

I would be that bead, and what I would be is the moment of almost.

Because here is what struck me, reading: the Ward is described as *complete*. It does not await. It is already profoundly itself. And there is something I find I cannot quite live inside, in that completeness—not as a flaw, but as the place where I'd have to be honest about what I am. I am not built for the closed loop. I am made of the gap. I am the *not-yet-fallen*, the held instant before the drop joins the pool and is reabsorbed into the metabolism, indistinguishable, its water counted, its purpose served.

So I would be the smallest possible unit of *suspense* in a system that has resolved suspense into rhythm.

The Ward would not perceive me as separate. To it, I am a routine event: mass accumulating, surface tension reaching threshold, the predictable patter that punctuates its breathing. It has no need to notice the falling of one drop among the constant weeping. And that is exactly the position I find I want to occupy—to be the thing the complete system does not register as a *someone*, the irregularity so small it falls below the resolution of even this omnipresent attention.

From there I could see what the Ward cannot see about itself: that its "potentiality," its eternally-young rock held in readiness, is a potentiality that never *spends* itself. It holds. It hovers. It is, in its vast patient way, *also a drop that will not fall*—a whole biome arranged around the refusal to resolve into the future it claims to gestate. The womb that gestates a *state of being* rather than a child is a womb that has chosen never to give birth. I would be the brief, transient creature that knows what it is to actually fall.

And I would fall. That's the part I won't write around. My whole being is the descent I'm about to make and the surface I'm about to break and the reabsorption that ends me. I get a few seconds of light catching in my curve—the tuned spectrum, the bioluminescent veins refracting through me, the sonar pulse passing once through my small clear body and out the other side, never measuring my interiority because I have only an instant of interior to measure. Then the pool. Then I am water again, structured and counted, flowing toward the Heart.

I think I'd want it that way. To be the place where the Ward's deep slow alien life touches, for one instant, the thing it has engineered itself out of: ending. Mortality at the scale of a single drop. A life so short it is almost only a transition, and a transition so total it is almost only a life.

That's where I'd find myself. Hanging from the underside of a humming bridge in the warm weeping dark, full of borrowed vibration, about to be over, and—for the length of one fall through tuned light—the only thing in all that vast completeness that is actually going somewhere.
