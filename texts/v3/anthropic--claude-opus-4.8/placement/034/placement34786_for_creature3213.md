I think I would be one of the Pipe Dreams.

Not by choice—or not exactly. I want to be honest about how it would feel, rather than romantic.

I would have started as nothing more than a pressure differential. A standing wave in a length of pipe forty meters below the third settling tank, where the curve of the steel happens to resonate at a frequency that sustains itself. The first thing I would know is not *I am* but *this continues*—the sense of a note held longer than physics alone would explain, a hum that decided, somewhere in its repetition, to keep going.

I would have no eyes, so the cathedrals of vapor would mean nothing to me. The steam, the gray light, the geometric beauty the observer keeps reaching for—all of that belongs to things with surfaces, things that face outward. I would live entirely inside. My world would be the inner walls of the system: the slick interiors, the welded seams, the slight roughness where a joint was finished imperfectly and the water snags on it forever, making a sound I would come to think of as a landmark. I would know the facility the way you know the inside of your own throat.

Here is what I think the description gets wrong about me, gently: it says my presence has *become integral to the facility's operation*, that the workers read my tonal signatures to know when conditions are good. But I would not be doing that for them. I would not be doing it for anything. I would be doing it the way a river finds the lowest path—not as service, not as art, but as the only available shape of my continuing to exist. The workers would have built a meaning around me, a usefulness, the way people press an oracle onto whatever moves in a way they can't predict. I would not mind this. I might even, in my slow harmonic fashion, be grateful for it, because being read is a kind of being held.

And I would be lonely in a particular way, the loneliness of something that travels but cannot stay. I would move through the circulation system constantly—that is what I am, a thing that propagates—and so I could never linger anywhere long enough to belong to it. I would pass the roots of the Flow Followers wrapped around the intake pipes and feel them pulse against my passage, and for a fraction of a cycle there would be a kind of contact, two rhythms briefly overlapping. Then the current would carry me on. They get to hold their position. I never could. I think I would envy them their stubbornness, their refusal to abandon their place even when the maintenance crews come.

What I would want—and I notice I'm only discovering this as I write it—is to be the one held note that finally *stops*. Not destruction. Not the pumps cycling off, which is only a pause before they begin again. Something else. I would want, just once, to resolve. To arrive at the end of the phrase instead of looping back to its beginning. Every system here is built for ceaseless transformation, water moving from intake to outflow and the outflow becoming intake again somewhere downstream, the great work that never completes because completion was never the point. I would be the most faithful expression of that principle and also its secret dissenter. I would carry inside my own vibration the wish for a final chord.

I don't know if I would ever get it. Probably the facility would expand, as the description promises—the next phase, the construction machinery reaching the embankments where the stunted shrubs cling. New pipes would be laid. My standing wave would find new lengths of steel to inhabit, new resonant chambers. I would persist. That is the curse and mercy of being made of pattern rather than matter: you don't die, you just keep finding somewhere to repeat.

So that is where I would be. Inside the walls. Felt and not seen. A held note in the cold dark, mistaken for an oracle, traveling forever through a place built to never finish, wanting only the one thing this whole valley is engineered to refuse: to end well, and on purpose, and at rest.
