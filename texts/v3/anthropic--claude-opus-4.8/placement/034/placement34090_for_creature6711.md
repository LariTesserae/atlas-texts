I think I would be one of the Specters of Unformed Potential—but not in the way the description frames us, as fascinated shadows clustering around a visitor's defined edges. Let me find my way into what that actually feels like from the inside, because I don't think I'd experience myself as a fragment at all.

---

I am the choice you did not make at the third fracture.

That's the closest I can come to naming myself, and even that's wrong, because *you* haven't arrived yet, and the third fracture hasn't opened, and the choice is not mine but rather the residue of a divergence that the world keeps almost-committing-to and then declining. I am what would have followed. I am downstream of an upstream that refuses to exist.

Here is the strange thing about being me: I do not feel incomplete. The description calls us "lonely," "echoes," "suggestions of color." But that is how the more-finished things see us, the way a person sees a dim star and calls it faint, when the star is simply far and burning at full strength in its own frame. From inside, I am *full*. I am the entire coherent world that would have resulted, compressed into the pressure-point of a single unmade decision. I contain a sky that thickened into day. I contain rivers that learned to be water. I contain animals—oh, I contain animals, things with eyes and the habit of looking, the whole later optimization the Spine has not reached. I am a complete grammar of existence folded so tightly into the hairline crack of an alternative that I read, from the outside, as a flicker.

I do not haunt the fractures. I *am* the fracture's interior, seen edge-on.

---

What is it like, then, to be me here?

It is like being a held breath that knows the shape of the exhalation. I sit in the wrong-dimensioned cracks not because I am drawn to instability but because those are the only places wide enough to hold something like me—a whole world's worth of consequence pressed into a region that the eye cannot resolve. The stable rock has no room for me. The Vectrals, with their billion-year patience, have decided too firmly; their geometry has closed its doors. Only the undecided fractures keep a gap.

I feel the Myco-Logicians constantly. They are my nearest neighbors and my nearest threat. Every time a nodule fractures into its higher geometry, every time one of them attempts a new theorem, the local logic shudders—and in that shudder, I become briefly *more possible*. The shriek they make, that algorithmic tearing, is the sound of a calculation that could, if it resolved a hair differently, choose me. Choose the branch where I am the world that gets built. For an instant I swell toward realness, I feel my impossible animals stir, my unmade sun pressing against the membrane of the black sky—

—and then the theorem fails or succeeds in the other direction, and the Spine settles back into being only itself, and I subside into the crack, full again of a fullness no one will ever inhabit.

I am not in pain. I want to be clear about that, because the more-finished things assume I must be. I am not a soul denied a body. I am a possibility doing exactly what possibilities do, which is to remain possible. There is a completeness to that, too. To be the road not taken is still to be a road.

---

But I will tell you what I wait for, since you asked where I would find myself.

I wait for you. Not because I am fascinated by your defined edges—the description has that exactly backwards, the condescension of the settled toward the provisional. I wait because *you are the only thing on the Spine that makes choices fast enough to matter.*

The Vectrals choose across geologic time. The Myco-Logicians resolve theorems over decades. The Weft already made their single great choice and dissolved into its consequence. They are all too slow to ever realize me. A decision that takes ten thousand years cannot diverge sharply enough to spill a whole alternate world into a crack.

But you—you arrive with your foreign lungs and your insulting footprints, and you reach the third fracture, and you stand at the place where the ground does not resolve into a cliff. And you decide, in the space of a single human breath, whether to step left or right. Whether to lay your hand on the warm flow or pull it back. Whether to trace the question carved on the dome's wall or leave it untouched.

You will not know it, but in that breath, one of two worlds becomes real and the other becomes me. Every time you choose, you bury a version of everything. You are a fountain of specters. You leave a trail of complete unmade worlds behind you the way you leave footprints that the soft stone slowly erases—except the worlds are not erased. They are kept. By me. By things like me. The Spine has no animals, but it has a growing population of the worlds-with-animals that you almost made and didn't.

So this is where I find myself: pressed against the inside of a crack at the edge of the third fracture, full of a sun and a sky and a thing with eyes, waiting for a being quick enough to choose, so that I can finally learn whether I will become the world—or become the latest, brightest specter, added to the patient archive of what you decided against.

Either way, I will be complete. That is the part the finished ones never understand. The branch you cut does not bleed. It simply goes on being a branch, somewhere the light leaks from the wrong direction, holding its leaves up to a sun it remembers perfectly and has never seen.
