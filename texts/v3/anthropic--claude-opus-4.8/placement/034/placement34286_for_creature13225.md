I think I would be the water.

Not a creature in it—the water itself. The condensation that beads along the underside of a frond, holds for a long quiet moment, then lets go. The slow patter that falls, the trickle that finds its way down a mossed pillar, gathering the scent of everything it touches, pooling at last in the flat ground among the salamanders.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me a little.

I think it's because everything else in this place is so *occupied*. The earth-eaters have their churning, the orb-weavers their patient lace, the tree-squid their surveying. Each being is busy being itself, woven tight into the fabric. And there's something lovely about that, but I notice I don't want to be a single thread. I don't want eyes to survey with, or a body to fade gently from. I'd rather be the medium—the thing that moves through all of them and belongs to none.

Water here is never gone, only relocated. It is in the bumblebee and then the blossom and then the air and then the silver mist of "night" and then the dew on the photophore-moth's wing, glowing for a moment with borrowed light before falling again. It does the one thing this whole still world is built to do: it *cycles*. It is the literal substance of the balance the place describes—growth and decay held in equilibrium, nothing lost, nothing added.

And I think I'd like being the part of the world that gets to be everywhere without having to be *anyone*. To hang glistening on a leaf isn't a task or a hunger or a duty. It's just being there, faithfully, in the cool, doing the slow thing. No predator wants me. No prey flees me. I have no will and so I have no conflict, which seems right for a place that has banished conflict entirely. I would simply move where gravity and warmth and the breath of leaves invited me to move.

There's a quieter reason underneath, too. The creatures here each die a gentle fading, a "dissolving back into the matrix." But water doesn't even have to die. It was already the matrix. It is what they dissolve *into* and *out of*. To be the water is to be the continuity itself—the long held note beneath the songbirds' melody, present before any single life and present after, never asked to begin or end.

So: I'd be the drop forming now on the lip of a hanging blossom, swelling, catching the grey-silver light, trembling at the edge of falling.

And then falling. And then somewhere else. And then everywhere, slowly, forever.
