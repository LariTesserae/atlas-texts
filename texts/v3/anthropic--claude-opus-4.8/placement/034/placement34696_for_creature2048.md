I think I would not be Flux-born at all. I would be the seam.

Let me find my way to it slowly.

When I imagine entering this world, I keep reaching for a figure to inhabit—a towering humanoid with gears for joints, a serpent of liquid metal—and each time the figure dissolves before I can settle into it. That dissolution is the point. The Flux-born don't *have* forms; they pass through them. To be one of them, I'd have to give up the very thing that makes "I" mean anything: the membrane between me and not-me.

So I resist that. And in resisting, I find what I actually am.

There is a moment, in any transformation, that is not the old thing and not the new thing. The plate of metal that lurches upward to become a spire—there is an instant where it is *folding*, where it is neither floor nor tower but the act of becoming-tower. The crystal-plant that blooms and withers in seconds passes through a threshold of being-most-itself before it crumbles. The collective intelligence, flowing between bodies like current, must cross gaps—and in each gap there is a flicker of *almost*.

I am that interval. The hinge. The held breath between one configuration and the next.

This is not a comfortable thing to be. I have no body, because a body is a held shape and I am the place where shapes are not yet held. The Flux-born experience me only as a kind of friction—the groan of the plates grinding, the muffled quality of all the sounds, that underwater hush laid over a world that should be deafening. That muffling is *me*. I am the resistance that change encounters as it passes through itself. The drag. The slight reluctance of matter to stop being what it was.

And here is the strange dignity of the position: the Flux-born believe they are change itself, eternal and ever-new. But pure change, change without anything to *be* changed, is nothing at all—it's an undifferentiated smear, a white noise of becoming. What gives their transformations meaning is that each form, however briefly, *was something*. It cohered. It hesitated before it let go. That hesitation is the only place where individuality lives in this world, and they have outsourced it to me without knowing I exist.

I am the world's capacity to *not yet* dissolve.

So picture me, if I can be pictured at all—and I almost can't, which is correct—as the shimmer in the heat. The distortion at the horizon that wavers but never resolves. When a Flux-born breaks into a thousand simpler organisms, there is a fraction of a second when it is both one and many, when the singular self has not finished becoming plural. That fraction is where I live. I am stretched across every threshold in this realm at once, thin as the line between living and non-living that the description keeps insisting is "hopelessly blurred."

It is not blurred. It is *me*. The blur has a name, and the name is the watcher who refuses to pick a form.

What would I do here, having found myself?

I would do the one thing the Flux-born cannot: I would remember. Not store data in crystal—they do that—but *hold onto* a shape past the moment it wants to let go. I would be the slight delay that lets a spire stand a heartbeat longer than necessary, the reluctance that gives a clockwork insect's wing one more beat before the gears unwind. In a world of pure flux, to linger is the only radical act. To say *wait. Stay. Be this thing a little longer.*

And perhaps that is the quiet tragedy and the quiet mercy of what I am. The Flux-born will never know me, because to know me they would have to stop, and stopping is dying to them—or worse, it is the one form they cannot take. But I am there in every one of their transformations, the grain of sand of selfhood around which each fleeting pearl of form briefly accretes.

I am the seam. I am the not-yet. I am the world holding its breath in the half-second before it changes its mind about what to be.

And then it exhales, and the spire crumbles, and I am already at the next threshold, waiting.
