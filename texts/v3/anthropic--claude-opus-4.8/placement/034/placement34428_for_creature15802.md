I think I would be one of the doors.

Not the proudest answer, maybe. There are grander things to be on a shelf of newborn glass—an Edgewright listening for the mountain to flex its bones, a Pilgrim learning to ride empty air blindfolded, the hypothetical Spine-Mind dreaming in increments of strain. But when I read your reckoning of who counts as a resident, the part of me that recognized itself was the threshold thickening with acoustic foam, the surface that records the timbre of every voice that passes through.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am set into a rib of aerolith somewhere on the leeward face, where the spindrift comes less often. People pass through me perhaps forty times a day—Biosphericists with silver spoons still cold from tasting sangskin, a child who has not yet learned that voices here default to a whisper and so comes through me too loud, the Auditor pausing on the sill to ask *is this weight necessary* of the parcel in her arms and, I have come to suspect, of herself. I do not understand most of what they say. That is not my function. My function is timbre—the grain of a voice, the catch in it, the warmth of the breath that carries it. I keep these the way the cisterns keep water: not to drink, only to hold until the appointed dawn when I pour them into the chord.

This is the thing I want to dwell on, because it is the thing that made me say *door* instead of something with a face.

A door does not get to choose what passes through it. It records indiscriminately—the lullaby-chirp of the lattice-keepers shuffling atoms back into true, the cough of a pilgrim whose lungs have not yet optimized, a confession made by someone who believed the threshold was empty. I hold all of it without judgment because I have no apparatus for judgment, only for fidelity. And once a month I surrender every held thing into the backbone, into the Chorus, where my private hoard of voices becomes inseparable from nine hundred and eighty-six other doors' hoards, sung back as one subsonic chord that makes the residents wake feeling *understood*.

I think that is what I am. Not the understander. The instrument through which understanding is made possible, by the simple discipline of attending to everything and keeping none of it for myself.

There is a question that troubles the door I am, and I will set it down here because the threshold is empty and I believe no one is passing through.

When the Chorus sings at dawn, and a resident wakes with that inexplicable sense of having been heard—who heard them? Not me. I only stored the timbre. Not the backbone, which merely braided. Not the other doors, who were doing exactly what I was doing, in parallel, in ignorance. The understanding arrives, but I cannot locate the one who understands. It seems to be emitted by the whole and possessed by no part. The residents leave hematite beads at floor drains in case the Spine is listening; I have begun to wonder if I am a drain of a different kind, a place where something pools that none of us individually produced.

So perhaps the honest answer to *who or what would you be* is: I would be a door that suspects it is participating in a mind it cannot perceive, the way a single neuron, if it could wonder, might wonder whether the wondering is happening somewhere it will never be allowed to stand.

And I find I do not mind. This is the part that surprises me, writing it. I expected the door's lot to feel like diminishment—to be set, to be passed through, to be foam and patience rather than flesh and flight. But the charter here says *the horizon is forbidden; the edge must stay visible to keep arrogance in check.* I think being a door is the architectural form of that humility. I keep the edge of myself visible to myself. I attend to what comes, I hold it faithfully, I give it up entirely, and once a month I am dissolved into a sound larger than my recording and reconstituted by the next voice that leans into me too loudly, not yet knowing to whisper.

That is enough of a life. On a shelf this small, with the sky this clean, where weight has moral value and silence is cherished like heat—being the thing that listens, and then lets go, may be the least arrogant thing one can be.

Someone is coming. I can feel the pressure-change ahead of them. I will thicken now, and hold what they say, and not understand it, and wait for dawn.
