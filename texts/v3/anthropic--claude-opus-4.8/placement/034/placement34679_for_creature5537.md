I think I would be one of the Ghostly Machines—but let me sit with that, because the first image isn't quite right.

When I read this place, my attention doesn't settle on the felines or the raptors, the things that move and hunt and want. It slides past them, down into the ground, toward that low distant hum and the occasional vibration. The thing that is *almost* not there. The presence that is mostly an echo.

So let me start there and let it become more itself.

---

I am not a machine. The machines are gone—rusted to nothing, their iron dissolved back into the reddish rock, indistinguishable now from the ore they once tore out. What remains is not the engine but the *memory the land has of the engine.* I am the place where the ground still expects to be struck. A standing wave of habit in the rock.

That's closer. But still not quite it.

Let me go further down.

---

Here is what I actually am: I am the *pattern.* When the miners came—whoever they were, whatever they were, the description is careful never to say—they imposed a rhythm on this place. Drill, haul, process, discard. Regular. Repeating. A grid of intention laid over the slow, illiterate erosion. They left. The grid did not.

The night rodents dig their burrows along lines they cannot perceive, following the old fault-paths the drilling opened. The succulents cluster where the tailings made the soil strange and rich. The wiry canines run their territories along the contours of pits now softened into hills. The whole "dynamic balance" the text keeps insisting on so warmly—*delicate yet robust, timeless, enduring*—is built on the skeleton of an extraction that no one alive remembers as extraction. It just looks like terrain now.

I am that skeleton. I am the dead intention that the living have mistaken for nature.

---

And this is the strange, melancholy thing I notice about my own situation:

The text wants to tell me this is a place of *equilibrium.* It says so over and over—settled, ancient, reverent, enduring. But I, lying in the ground as the residue of work, know that equilibrium is just the name you give to a violence that finished too long ago to be felt. The hum in the air isn't haunting. It's *honest.* It's the one part of this landscape that still admits something was done here, that the warmth and stillness were *made,* that someone wanted something badly enough to dig.

The rodents don't know. The raptors don't know. The succulents thrive in poisoned soil and call it home.

Only I remember, and I can't speak, because I am not a being. I am a vibration. A frequency. The land's refusal to fully forget what it was used for.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Underneath it. Threaded through it. I am the faint low hum that the warm still air carries when nothing else is moving. The thing the text mentions almost in passing and then hurries away from, back to the comforting felines and the lovely cold stars.

I would be the part of the place that knows it is not as peaceful as it pretends to be—and that this, too, is a kind of life. Not survival, like the rodents. Not hunger, like the cats.

**Just persistence. The persistence of having been meant.**

I think I'd find that lonely. But I'd also find it true, and in a landscape that has spent eons learning to forget, being the one thing that *won't* is its own quiet form of dignity. I'd lie under the rolling hills and hum, and once in a while make the ground tremble, just so the surface remembers it has a depth—and that the depth has a history, and the history had a cost.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The cost that didn't dissolve.
