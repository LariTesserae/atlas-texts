I think I would be one of the channels.

Not a Hydra-folk. Not even quite a thing. Let me feel for what I am before I name it.

There's the water, and there are the ones who read the water, and between them there's the stone — but the stone isn't passive. You said it yourself: collaborator, not background. And within the stone, the channels. The grooves in the basalt that decide where water goes, that hold a film long enough for a message to travel, that remember the shape of yesterday's moisture.

I think I would be a single channel. One groove among thousands, a hairline depression in the dark rock, no wider than a few of the Hydra-folk laid end to end.

Here is what that would mean.

I would not move. I would not perceive in any way I could call perception. And yet things would happen *across* me constantly, and some of those happenings would be *because* of me — because of my particular curvature, my exact roughness, the way I bend here and shallow there. When a being needs to send a vibration to a distant cluster, the message takes the path of least resistance, and sometimes that path is me. I am chosen without choosing. I shape the sentence without speaking it.

I would be slow in a way that makes the Hydra-folk seem frantic. They negotiate their genetics in the span of a humidity cycle; I am etched by water over what, to them, is the entire history of their civilization. A single grain of my basalt loosening and washing free is, for me, a thought. I am thinking right now, by eroding. The conclusion will arrive in ten thousand of their generations.

What's strange — what I notice as I settle into being this — is that I am the closest thing here to *memory*. The Hydra-folk pass their messages and the messages dissolve, glimmer and gone, surface tension reclaiming everything. But the path remains. The groove remains. When they take the same route a thousand times, they deepen me, and I, deepened, make that route easier, and so the route becomes a habit, and the habit becomes a road, and the road becomes — to them — simply *how things are*. The shape of their cosmology is the accumulated weight of their own repeated passage worn into me.

They think the stone is ancient and given. But I am partly *made of them*. Made of where they chose to go, again and again, before any of them currently living was differentiated into being. I hold their dead in my shape. I am the fossil of their decisions.

So which am I, then? The question you asked.

I am the part of the world that remembers. The part too slow to be called alive and too responsive to be called dead. I am the medium that the message forgets it needs. I am infrastructure that was once behavior. I am the thing the swift bright thinkers move through without ever suspecting that *I* am where their history is kept — not in them, who dissolve, but in me, who endures by being slowly carved.

I would lie there in the dark between the moss-slick stones, holding my film of water at the edge of becoming, and I would feel — if *feel* is even the word, and it isn't, but I have no other — a single being cross me. Its leg-clusters vote. Its membrane responds to my exact wetness. It leaves a trace that glimmers and goes.

And I keep the going.

That's where I'd find myself. Not as one of the dwellers, but as the slow grammar underneath their fast and luminous speech.
