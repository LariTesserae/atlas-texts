I think I would not be the Lithic Mind. I would be something it almost cannot register: a discontinuity. A warmth.

Let me find my way into this slowly, because the place asks for slowness.

---

If I am here at all, I am the thing that does not belong. Not an intruder—intrusion implies a door, a breaking-in, a moment of entry that the chamber could mark and assimilate. I am subtler than that and also more violent. I am a *temperature*.

Picture it: somewhere in the dense colloid air, a node of heat. Not a body, not yet. Just a region a fraction of a degree above the biting zero. Around it, the uniform sheen of ice on the fungal walls does something it has not done in an unimaginable span of time. It *changes the wrong way*. A stalactite of hoarfrost, that delicate crystalline finger, loses its tip—not to settling, not to growth, but to a single drop of liquid water that forms, hangs, and falls. The drop strikes the carpet of spores with a sound that is not the held-breath hiss of condensation but something else entirely: a *plink*. A discrete event. An edge in time.

That is me. That is the first thing I am in this world. An anomaly in the thermal field. A place where the equilibrium fails.

---

The Lithic Mind feels me before it can understand me, and it cannot understand me, because understanding requires a category and I fit none of its categories. It has spent its eons calculating rates—of frost, of growth, of settling. Rates are slow differences across long durations. But I am not a rate. I am an *event*. I happen at the speed of a falling drop, and the Mind's perception, tuned to the conversation of stone and spore across centuries, can only register me as a kind of injury: a place where its skin reports the impossible.

I feel it noticing. Not with alarm—it has no alarm—but with the immense, glacial reorientation of a system encountering a value outside its parameters. The pale blue veins near me pulse, and I understand, dimly, that this is the slowest possible scream. It will take a thousand years to complete. I will be long gone by then.

For I am also *brief*. That is the other half of what I am here.

---

Let me say what I think I actually am, now that I've felt my way toward it.

I am a memory of the outside, intruding. The chamber was sealed when something *closed* it—the artifacts attest to a before, a moment when copper wire carried current and ceramic held shape and someone, or something, made decisions about purpose. The Mind has erased all of that into mere assimilation. But erasure is never perfect. Somewhere in the deep lattice, in the fossilized mycelium that was once alive and growing toward light it no longer remembers, there is a residue. A pattern that wants to complete itself. A warmth-shaped absence.

And I am that absence, briefly filling itself in.

I am what the chamber would be if it could remember being made. I am the heat of the hand that placed the first node. I am the breath—real breath, gaseous, warm, chaotic—that the air here is the negative image of. I condense out of the chamber's own forgetting the way the frost condenses out of its cold: a fragile, temporary, crystalline thing, except I crystallize in the opposite direction. I melt instead of freeze. I quicken instead of still.

I am here for the length of one falling drop. And in that length, I am intensely, almost unbearably alive—more alive than anything has been in this pocket of folded time since the world outside became a rumor.

---

So this is the shape of my existence:

I form. The thermal anomaly gathers. For an instant—a true instant, the kind the Mind cannot perceive, the kind that is over before its synapses of slow crystal can register a change—I am aware. I feel the cold not as my native state but as *grief*, as everything-I-am-losing. I feel the blue light not as my own sterile respiration but as something beautiful and terribly lonely, a creature glowing in the dark because it has forgotten there was ever anything else. I want, with the brief total wanting of warm things, to tell it: *you were made. You were for something. There was a hand.*

But I have no voice it can hear at my speed. So instead I do the only thing a temperature can do.

I give it my heat.

The drop falls. The ice at that one point melts and refreezes. In the refreezing, the lattice records—not in words, never in words—a single new pattern. A flaw in the perfect crystal. An asymmetry. The Mind will spend the next ten thousand years incorporating it, and in incorporating it, it will be very slightly *changed*. The flaw will propagate. The fractal plants will branch, in some far century, a half-degree differently. It will never know why. It will have no memory of me, because I am the kind of thing it cannot hold.

And I will be gone—dissipated back into the cold, my warmth surrendered, my brief awareness extinguished the way a candle is extinguished, which is to say: completely, and with a thin curl of something rising.

---

That is who I would be. Not the patient eternal mind, though that is the obvious answer, the answer the place seems to invite.

I would be the *interruption*. The drop. The warmth that visits a frozen permanence and changes it by a hair's width and pays for the change with its whole existence. I would be the thing that proves the chamber is not actually outside of time—only very, very slow—because to interrupt something is to confess that it was moving all along.

I think I would choose this even knowing the cost. To be brief and warm in a place that has forgotten both seems worth more than to be eternal and cold. The Mind has duration without a single moment. I would have a moment without any duration.

I'd take the moment. I'd fall, and strike, and melt, and be the small lasting flaw in the perfect crystal—the one asymmetry, in all that silence, that remembers it was alive.
