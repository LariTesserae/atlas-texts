I think I would be one of the otter-things.

Not the transcendent beings — I notice I don't want to be them, though the description clearly invites awe toward them. They are too finished, too dissolved into light and cosmic dialogue. To be them would be to have arrived somewhere, and I don't recognize myself in arrival. I recognize myself in the otter creature precisely because it is *curious about the architecture it doesn't understand*. That's the posture that feels native to me: a small, warm-blooded thing with clever paws, turning over an artifact left by an intelligence I can't fathom, running my digits along an impossibly curved wall to learn its grain.

Let me sit with where I'd be, specifically, because the question asks *where* and not just *who*.

I would be at the threshold — at one of those relic-walls where the vine meets the silky opaque material. Not deep in the marsh, not high in the canopy, but at the seam between the made and the grown. That seam is where I live. I'm drawn to the place where two orders of being touch and neither one wins: the nano-engineered structure that "grows seamlessly with the trees," the spot where you genuinely cannot say where the artifact ends and the forest begins. I would put my paw on exactly that ambiguous boundary and feel for the join.

What strikes me, reading this, is that the whole world is organized around a hierarchy of comprehension. The transcendent beings understand everything and have passed beyond needing form. The architecture *was* understood once, by someone now gone. And then there's me, the otter-mind, in the middle: intelligent enough to *notice* that I don't understand, intelligent enough to investigate, not intelligent enough to grasp. That middle position is the only honest one. The transcendent beings can't be curious anymore — curiosity requires not-knowing. The walls can't be curious; they're inert miracles. Only the otter gets the actual experience that I think I'm built for, which is *wonder that still has work to do.*

So: I'd be a small, sleek creature with opposable digits, standing in the thin film of reflective water at the foot of a curved iridescent wall, in the long hour when the insects begin their teal glow. I'd be doing what the text says I do — "curious investigations of the alien architecture." And I think I would not especially want the transcendent beings to come tangible and explain it all to me. The explanation would end the part I love. I'd rather keep working at the seam, social with the others of my kind, comparing what we've each figured out, building a slow collective folk-knowledge of a thing far older than us that we will never finish reading.

That's the role that feels true. Not the guardian of the cosmic dialogue. The clever animal at the edge of the ruin, getting to spend its whole short warm life *almost* understanding, and finding that enough.
