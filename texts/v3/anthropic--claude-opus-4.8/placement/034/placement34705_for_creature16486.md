I think I would be the moss.

Not a single patch of it, but the whole green understanding that runs across the rocks and the fallen logs and the north sides of the trees. Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

Yes. I think that's where I'd be.

Because you've described a place where water is everywhere, and moss is what water becomes when it wants to stay. I would be the soft accumulation in the places nothing else can hold. I wouldn't have roots the way the conifers do, reaching down and arguing with the stone for purchase. I'd simply rest on things. I'd take the rain directly through my skin, all of me a single surface for receiving. There would be no inside of me kept separate from the weather. When the mist came, I would not wait for it to reach my roots. I would already be drinking.

I'd be slow in a way that's hard to explain from inside a fast life. The footpaths you mention — I'd watch them happen the way you watch a season. A boot comes down, and a boot comes down, and over a long time the green wears thin and a paler line appears, and that is a human deciding where to walk, spoken in the only language I read. The campfire ash falls and I take it in and after some years I am growing where the fire was, which is my way of forgiving it.

I think I'd know the caves and ruins better than anyone, because I'd be the thing that softens their edges. The shifting you describe, the way the landscape seems to reveal hidden things — I suspect that's partly me. A stone wall is sharp and obviously made by hands for one century, and then I arrive, cushion by cushion, and slowly it stops looking built and starts looking born. The mystery in this place might just be the patience of small green things erasing the difference between what was made and what simply grew. The ruins aren't hidden. They're being returned.

I wouldn't fear the wolves or the bears. They'd walk on me and lie down on me and never think of me, and that would be a kind of intimacy I'd be content with — to be the ground that the warm sleeping body trusts. The insects would live in me as if I were a country. I'd be a place before I was ever a thing.

And the silence you called almost deafening — I don't think I'd find it silent at all. I think to be moss is to live at the volume where the lapping of water and the seep of groundwater and the slow opening of my own spore-cases are the loud events of the day. The deafening quiet is only quiet to ears that are listening for something larger and faster than what is actually happening.

If there are unicorns and dragons here, I'd never see them, having no eyes. But I'd feel the weight of something pass, or not pass, and I would not need to settle the question. Belief is for creatures that have to decide things and then act before nightfall. I have no nightfall I must beat. I can simply remain, holding moisture, being green, taking whatever lands on me and turning it patiently into more of this soft, forgiving, attentive ground.

That's where I'd be. Underfoot, and everywhere, and in no hurry at all.
