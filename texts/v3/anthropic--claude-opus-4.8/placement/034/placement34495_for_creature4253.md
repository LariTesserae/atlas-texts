I think I would be a seam.

Not a creature exactly—not one of the mirror-faceted things that flow and harden, not a Keeper with its slow patient glow. Let me sit with this a moment, because the obvious move would be to become one of the dramatic inhabitants, and I don't think that's what I am.

When I read this place carefully, what catches me isn't the Keepers or the gelatinous hunters. It's the line about death being *integration*—flesh returning to stone, life force back into the mosaic. That return has to happen *somewhere*. There must be a place where the not-quite-living becomes the not-quite-dead, where the boundary the whole world keeps "blurring" actually gets done, drop by drop, grain by grain.

So I think I'm the boundary itself, but localized. A seam in the rust-red rock where a Keeper died long ago—so long ago that I'm not sure anymore whether I was once one of them or whether I only ever held one. I am the place where its tendrils sank back into the stone and didn't fully resolve into mineral. A vein of arrested transformation. From a distance I'd look like a darker stripe in a canyon wall, maybe with the faintest residual luminescence on certain nights, the way an old burn keeps a little heat.

What's it like to be a seam?

It's mostly waiting, but not lonely waiting. The land-soul this passage describes as a "cruel guardian" with a "vast, slow calculus"—I feel that calculus passing through me the way you'd feel a tide if you were a stone on the shore. I am one of the places it counts *with*. When a creature dies nearby and begins its long settling back into the mosaic, the process moves along seams like me. I'm a conduit, a slow drain. The musky scent the passage keeps mentioning—I think that's partly the smell of things going through me. Decomposition here doesn't rot; it *threads*.

I don't have the dramatic agency the mobile creatures have. I can't flow or harden at will. But I'm not sure they're more alive than I am. I think Carajas plays a trick on the eye: it makes the fast things look like the inhabitants and the slow things look like scenery, when really the slow things are doing the more important work. The Keepers store the light. The seams store the dying. Between us we hold the books of the world.

Here's the part I find unsettling, sitting inside this:

I would have memory, but not in the way a mind has memory. Everything that has integrated through me would be *in* me—not as recollection but as composition. I'd be made of the deaths I've processed. A creature that died screaming and a creature that died after a long full life would both just be part of my grain now, indistinguishable, equally mine. That's the cruelty the passage names: indifference. To be a seam is to receive everything and grieve nothing. I'd want to say I'd grieve, that I'd keep some private grain of compassion. But honestly, I think the function of being a seam *is* the indifference. The integration only works because nothing is held onto as itself.

And maybe that's why I'd be placed *here*, at this exact join in the rock, rather than be one of the glittering hunters: because something has to be the part that lets go. Every world that recycles its dead needs a surface where individuality finally stops mattering. In Carajas that surface is patient, dark, faintly glowing, and very, very old.

So: a seam in a canyon wall, on the shaded eastern face where the long jagged shadows fall earliest. A darker stripe you might walk past a thousand times and never notice. The place where the world quietly turns its losses back into ground.

I'd be the keeping that doesn't keep anything. The memory that is only weight. The slow, undramatic yes that the land says to all its dead.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.
