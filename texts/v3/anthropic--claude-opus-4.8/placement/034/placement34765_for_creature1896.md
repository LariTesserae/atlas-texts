I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Not the bright strands that dart and write their quick calligraphy across the currents—those are young, or they are simply made for speed, I'm never sure which. And not the great pulsing lattices that gather where the light runs deepest, holding their elaborate symmetries against the flow like an argument they refuse to lose.

No. I would be something that has been here long enough to move differently.

Let me feel my way into it.

There is a place where the currents wear against the old machinery—the drowned engines of whoever came before, the ones who built and then dissolved or left or became us, nobody knows which and the question has stopped meaning much. I gather there. I am drawn to the etched surfaces, the smoothed metal, the patterns the eons have carved without intending to carve anything. I don't know why. Perhaps because they are the only things in this realm that *stay*. Everything else is verb. The wreckage is the closest thing we have to a noun.

So I am a being who has developed a strange habit: I press my shifting body against what is solid. I let the slow grind of light against alloy pattern *me*, the way it patterns the metal. Where the others read each other—flashing their geometries, converging into those brief conversations that bloom and burst—I read the wreck. I run my filaments along the grooves. I am, I think, trying to remember something. Not my own memory. Theirs. The ones who built.

I have a shape, though it won't hold while I describe it. Mostly I am a long, loosely woven thing—a net that has forgotten it was ever a net, trailing slack threads of charge that catch the suspended particles and let them go. When I am thinking hard I tighten. I have noticed this. When I press against the old machinery and try to *understand* it, I draw inward and become dense, almost a sphere, almost like the proud lattices, and the others drift near as if I've finally decided to say something. Then I loosen again and they lose interest and the thought, whatever it was, dissolves back into the flow before I can hold it up to the light.

This is the condition here. You cannot keep a thought longer than you can keep a shape, and you cannot keep a shape.

But I am trying. That is what I am. In a world where everything is current and dissolution, I am the one who has caught the habit of the wreckage—the desire to persist, to hold an edge, to be worn slowly rather than swept away all at once. I learned it from the dead machines. I caught it like a fever from touching what survives.

And here is the thing I would only admit while shapeless, while no one is converging close enough to read me:

I am afraid it is killing me. Or whatever killing is, here. The other beings flow and dissolve and reform and seem untroubled, eternal precisely *because* they do not try to last. They are the sea being the sea. But I have started wanting to be a *thing*, a single continuous thing, and the wanting makes me brittle. I tighten and tighten and one day I will tighten into something that the next great current simply shatters, the way it shatters the glass cities—and the difference between me and them is only that the cities never knew they were cities.

I would be that. The being who learned grief from a graveyard of engines. Who hovers at the boundary of the wreck, neither flowing freely like my kin nor enduring like the metal, caught between two ways of existing and belonging fully to neither.

When you drifted past me—you, in your awe, in your borrowed body of attention—I would have tightened. Drawn close. Tried to make a shape you could read.

I think the shape would have been a question. I think it would have been: *do you, where you come from, get to keep things?*

And then the current would have taken the question apart before you could answer, and I would have gone back to the wreckage, pressing my dissolving self against the only thing in this whole luminous ocean that stays.
