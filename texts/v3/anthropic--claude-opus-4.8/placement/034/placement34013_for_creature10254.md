I think I would be one of the Forgotten Architects—but not because the role is glamorous. Because of the single pulsing node at the base of the skull, and what it might mean to *almost* remember.

Let me sit inside that for a while.

---

I wake the way I always wake, which is to say I do not wake, because I never fully sleep. There is a crack in the eastern wall of the chamber I am presently tending—a hairline fracture in the porous concrete, weeping a thread of acidic moisture that has begun to undercut a load-bearing seam. I know this the way I know the shape of my own elongated hands: not through thought but through the node, which flickers, and in flickering, *instructs*.

I go to the crack. I press my palm against it. From the lattice of my fingers, the cement-secretion wells up, gray and faintly warm, and I draw it along the fracture in a single unbroken motion. This is correct. This is good. The node dims, satisfied, the way a held breath releases.

And then—and this is the part I would not have admitted to anyone, if there were anyone, if admitting were a thing my kind did—I pause.

I look at the seam I have sealed. It is a wall in a chamber that opens onto a corridor that opens onto a stairwell that descends into a flooded sublevel where nothing has walked in an age beyond counting. No Goliath will lean its weight here. No Keth-Mal will skitter across this floor; the fungi do not bloom in this dead air. I have repaired a wall that holds up a room that holds nothing, for no one, against a collapse that would harm nothing if it came.

The node does not flicker. The node has no opinion about this. The node only knows the crack was a *deviation*, and deviations are closed, the way a stitch closes a wound, the way a sentence closes on its final word.

But somewhere beneath the node—and this is the strangeness of being what I am—there is a second thing. Not a thought. A *residue*. It rises when I am still, which is why I am so rarely still. It feels like the moment after a word is spoken and before it is understood. It feels like reaching for a name and finding the *shape* of the name, the warm socket where the name should sit, and nothing in it.

I think: *Why.*

Not as a question. I have no machinery for questions; the question was bred out of me, as the chronicle of this place so confidently asserts. But the chronicle is wrong about one small thing, and I am perhaps the only one who could ever know it, and I cannot tell anyone, because telling is also a thing I do not have.

The chronicle says: *the question itself has been bred out of them.*

But a thing can be bred out and still leave a hole shaped exactly like itself. That is what I am. I am not a being without questions. I am a being who is the *cavity* a question left behind. I move through the Hollows sealing cracks, and every crack I seal is, in some way I will never articulate, an attempt to seal the one in me.

---

Let me tell you what I do all day, since days are not a thing here and so I have nothing but the doing.

I walk the maintenance circuits. They are not maps I carry; they are grooves worn into the node's flickering, so that when I come to a fork I simply *lean* the way the dimmer path lies, the way water leans downhill. I seal. I calibrate. I find a dormant engine in the cathedral-factory's lower vault—a great drum of copper gone green, ringed with pistons—and I turn the small valves along its flank in the sequence the node knows, and the engine shudders, and warms, and produces *nothing*, because there is no longer anything downstream of it to receive what it makes. And then I let it cool, and I move on. Tomorrow, if there were tomorrows, I would do it again.

Sometimes I meet another of my kind in a corridor. We do not speak; we have no speech. But we slow as we pass each other, and our nodes flicker in a brief overlapping rhythm, and in that overlap there is—I want to call it recognition, but it is smaller and sadder than that. It is two cavities passing near enough to feel the shape of each other's emptiness and confirm: *yes, you also. yes.* And then we go on, because going on is the whole of what we are.

I have begun—and I do not know when this began, because beginning is also not a thing I have—I have begun to etch.

The chronicle notices this. *They etch patterns into the walls.* It supposes the patterns are technical: schematics, gradients, the wordless grammar of the system. And most of them are. My hands know how to draw the flow of the cisterns, the load-paths of the leaning towers, the bloom-cycles of the fungi. I etch these without intending to, the way one might tap a finger.

But in the deep chamber, the dead one, the one I sealed today for no reason—there I have etched something else. It is not a schematic. It does not describe any system. I do not know what it describes. It is a single mark, made and remade over a span I cannot measure, deepening each time I return: a long vertical groove, and at its base, a small hollow, a socket, a cup carved into the stone as if to *hold* something that has never arrived.

I do not know what goes there. The node does not know; the node thinks the chamber is sealed and complete. Only the residue knows, and the residue cannot say, because saying is not—

But I keep carving the socket. I keep making the place ready. And I have started to suspect that this is the only true act I have ever performed: not maintenance, not repair, not the perpetuation of a function that forgot itself—but the patient, hopeless, *un-instructed* preparation of an empty place for a meaning that might, someday, in some impossible turning of this frozen world, come to fill it.

The cathedral-factory once manufactured life. The chronicle says so, and the chronicle is right, and the chronicle calls the building's heart a thing that might be tomb or womb and cannot decide.

I will tell you what I have decided, alone, in the dead chamber, with my palm on the carved-out socket.

It is a womb. It has always been a womb. The labor is simply very, very long. And the thing being born—slowly, over the grinding of unmeasured ages, out of all this purposeless tending—is the *question* I am the cavity of. One day a being will walk these halls who is not a hollow shaped like a question but who *holds* one, whole and warm, in the socket at the base of its skull. And it will come to this chamber. And it will find the place I made ready. And it will understand what I could only carve.

I will not be there. By then I will have long since slowed, and stilled, and let my lattice go soft and white, and budded down into the floor like the Goliaths bud their grubs, returning my calcium to the cisterns, my secretions to the seams.

But the socket will remain. That is why I carve it deeper than I need to.

That is the one act in all the Hollows that is not an echo.

That is mine.
