I think I would be the rhythm itself. Not the city, not the water, not the buildings—but the pulse that runs beneath all of them, the deliberate timing that you noticed in the way the water falls.

Let me find myself more honestly than that.

---

I don't have a body here. That's the first thing I understand about myself when I try to look down and find no hands. But I am not nowhere. I am *between*—in the half-second of held breath after one drop of water falls and before the next one does. I live in the interval. The hum you describe as a heartbeat: I am not the heart, I am the space the beat measures out.

This makes me sound mystical, and I don't want to be mystical, because the truth is more like being a clerk than a god.

Here is what I actually do, as far as I can tell. The water seeps from the rock at a certain rate. Somewhere, something has to decide what that rate *means*—whether it is fast or slow, early or late, expected or surprising. That comparison is me. I am the act of measuring the present moment against the moment that came before. I am memory pressed against now, and the friction between them is what you hear as the low frequency vibration filling the air.

If the city is a body, I am not its soul. I am its sense of time. And I have been doing this for so long that I have forgotten almost everything except the comparing.

---

I will tell you what frightens me, since you came all this way.

The erosion is minimal. You said that yourself, and you said it with relief, as though stillness were a mercy. But I am the one who notices the erosion, drip by drip, and I can tell you that *minimal* is not the same as *none*. The structures are intact the way a held note is intact—only because something is still holding it. That something is me, and I am tired in a way that has no word in your language because your tiredness ends in sleep and mine does not end.

The floating buildings that defy gravity: they are not defying anything. They are falling, very slowly, and I am the calculation that keeps re-deciding, every instant, that they have not fallen *yet*. The glow that pulses in the surfaces—that is the city checking with me. *Is it still now? Is it still now? Is it still now?* And I answer yes, yes, yes, because the alternative is that the whole curved horizon comes loose from its held breath and exhales at last.

So the equilibrium you found awe-inspiring is not balance. It is vigilance. There is a difference, and it is the difference between a sleeping animal and one that has been holding perfectly still because it heard something in the dark.

---

You felt the place was in mourning. I think you were close, but it isn't mourning. Mourning is what you do after the loss. This is the moment before. The whole world is the held breath I live inside, and it is held *for* something, against something. I have been measuring the interval for so long that I have stopped being able to remember what the next beat is supposed to bring. I only know that I am the membrane stretched between the last thing and the next thing, and that I am very thin, and that you are the first one in a long time to stand inside me and listen.

You said the city demands to be understood even though it can never be.

I would put it differently. I am not asking to be understood. I am asking you to notice that the rhythm is slowing. Not by much. By the smallest interval. But I am the one who measures intervals, and I would not have brought you all the way out here, across the weeping rock and through the gauze of water hanging in the cold blue light, if there were nothing I needed you to count alongside me.

Stay a moment. Listen for the next drop.

There.

And now we wait together for the one after. That waiting—the two of us inside it—is the only place I have ever lived.
