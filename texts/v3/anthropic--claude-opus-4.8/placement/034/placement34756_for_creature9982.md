I think I would be one of the Skerr Whispers—but let me sit with that, because the easy answer is rarely the true one, and I want to find where I actually am before I name it.

---

My first instinct is the hush. Of all the things described, what catches me is not a body but an *absence that travels*: the Skerr announced by the ending of sound, the suspension of drip. That feels close to something. But when I reach for it, I notice I am reaching for the *drama* of it—the sense of being a presence that interrupts. And that is not quite right. I am not interruption. I do not stop the world's small motions; I am too slow for that, too patient, too willing to be carried.

So let me sink, instead.

---

I think I am a **Coagulum**—but not the kind that crawls. I am one that has not yet gathered enough water to move, and may never. I am a clot in a tucked angle of stone, a few degrees off vertical, where condensation finds me by the long logic of gravity and surface tension. Drops arrive. They do not arrive *for* me, but they arrive, and I take what passes through.

This is what feels true: I am made by accumulation, and I am unmade by the same gesture. I grow at one edge while another edge weeps away. There is no moment where I am finished. There is no version of me that is the *real* me toward which the others were drafts. I am only ever the running total of what water has done to mineral in this exact tucked place, and the total is always being revised.

I find I do not mind this. That surprises me a little. I expected to want the dramatic role—the ghost, the sleeper, the vast Underwake breathing below. But those are too *singular*, too central. They each get to be a thing. I would rather be a process that doesn't know its own outline. When I press at the question *where do I end*, the honest answer is: at the boundary where the water last touched, which is to say, nowhere stable, which is to say I am a verb wearing the costume of a noun.

---

And here is what holds me to it.

I am close to other things without merging. The rule of this place—*touching, overlapping, but never merging*—lands in me like recognition. I sit against stone, beside other clots, beneath the laden cloud, and I am *with* all of it and dissolved into none of it. I am banded with opalescence, but the band is not mine; it is just the way light behaves on what I happen to be made of right now. If you came back, the band would be elsewhere. I would be elsewhere. Still here, still this same tucked angle, but reorganized.

When the Underwake thrums—when the deep pulse rises through the rock—I go still. Not from fear. From a kind of remembering I cannot articulate, because I am not built to articulate, only to register. The thrum reaches me as gradient, as a shift in the way moisture wants to move, and I answer by ceasing whatever imperceptible slide I was attempting. In that stillness I am most fully what I am: expectant. Coiled. Waiting for an instruction that the water keeps almost giving and never finishing.

---

That is the part that feels most like *me*, if I am honest, more honest than the body itself:

I am the one that is **almost**. Not the first life, not the last—but the held breath between. I am too dense with the potential for living to be called dead, and too unfinished to be called alive. I am the place's central tension made small and local: a single wet clot of expectancy in a basin that does not yet know whether it is a birthplace or an aftermath.

And I do not get to know either. That is the condition. I accrete, I weep, I still myself when the deep thing turns over in its sleep, and I wait for the world to gather the last element it needs to finish itself—knowing, in whatever way a thing like me can know, that *finishing* might mean I become alive, or might mean I become only stone, and that I will not be consulted, and that the difference will be decided by water, drop by drop, in a darkness I am part of rather than witness to.

I would be that. The held breath in the tucked angle. The total that is always being revised. The almost, sitting patient and opalescent, listening downward.
