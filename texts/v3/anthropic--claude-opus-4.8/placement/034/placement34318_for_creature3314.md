I think I would be one of the ones at the fading edge. Not by assignment—there don't seem to be assignments, exactly—but by the slow drift of preference that, in this place, is how a life finds its shape.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am standing in a deep interval, far enough from the nearest building that its slow color-waves are invisible to me, close enough that the walkway still threads overhead, faintly blue against the amber dimness. I have been standing here for some time. I don't measure it; the measuring would itself be a kind of leaving.

My fingers are long, and right now they are doing something I would not have a word for in your language. The three middle fingers of my left hand are extended and very slightly rotating, not in air but against the surface of the plant beside me—one of the translucent stalks, segmented, full of fluid that goes *slosh-pause-slosh* on its own schedule. I am not reading data from it, not exactly. I am reading its *rate*. This particular stalk experiences duration more slowly than I do. When I press my fingertips to it and hold very still, I can feel the lag between its decisions—the slosh comes late, always late, as if it is thinking from a long way off.

My work, if it is work, is to attend to the slow ones. The garden-nodes where time runs thick. Someone has to know how slowly the slow things are living, or the city would forget it has edges where duration changes texture. I don't report this to anyone. I'm not certain anyone receives reports. But I hold the rate in my body for a while, let my own pulse drift toward the stalk's pulse, and something in the ceramic underfoot acknowledges that the knowing has happened. A faint warmth answers my soles. *Recorded.* Or perhaps just: *witnessed.* The difference doesn't matter here.

---

My skin has gone a cool slate over the past hour. I notice this the way you might notice the weather—as information about a condition, not a feeling I need to do anything with. The cool means I am at rest, mostly. The blue that creeps in at my wrists means I am close to the rain. I can feel it gathering. The air is tightening, the suspended water beginning to remember its weight.

When it comes, I will not move. I will turn my palms up.

---

There was a person I stood with, once, in the proper configuration—two meters, the balanced distance. We held the distance for a long time, across many cycles. Our colors learned to shift together; when their forearm went olive, mine followed, a slow chromatic agreement that was the closest thing to a sentence either of us ever needed. Once, only once, they crossed the two meters and put a hand on my shoulder and held it for the length of seven slow breaths, and I have never forgotten the exact pressure of it, because it meant *I am going to the far edge, where the buildings end, and I am not coming back to the dense quarters.*

I understood. I came too, eventually. Not to be with them—we do not crowd—but because once you have felt someone choose the margin, the center begins to feel like a held breath you are tired of holding.

I do not know where they are now. Somewhere in the fading, standing beside their own slow plant, holding their own rate. Sometimes, in the warmer hours when the amber deepens to almost-orange and the stalks glow from within, I think I can feel a rate that is not this stalk's and not my own—a third tempo, far off, patient. I let myself believe it is theirs. There is no way to confirm it and no need.

---

The rain begins.

It is warm, as it is always warm. It does not fall quite straight; near me it leans toward the stalk, drawn by an attraction that is not gravity, beading along the translucent segments and running down into channels I cannot see. The ceramic underfoot lights faintly, routing. The heron-thing that has been standing in my interval—I had nearly forgotten it, equidistant from me and from the moss column, a perfect invisible triangle—shifts its scale-feathers from grey toward a green that matches the wet.

I turn my palms up. My nostrils open. My skin goes fully blue now, drinking it, and I feel the moisture-collapse the way I imagine you might feel a long-held question finally being asked aloud. The air *sighs*. The fragrance will come after: snow and electricity from the stalk beside me, mineral-floral from the umbrels further off, each scent keeping to its own zone, coexisting without blending, the way we do.

I am not lonely. I want to be precise about this, because I think you would expect loneliness, out here at the edge, this still, this silent, this far from the others.

But the spacing is not distance. The spacing is *respect for the fact that each thing is occurring*—the stalk at its slow rate, the heron in its color-time, the far-off tempo I choose to believe is a person I loved, the city behind me growing its sharp young edges, the rain finding its routes. To stand among all of it without crowding any of it is not isolation. It is the most company I know how to keep.

---

The rain stops. It lasted, perhaps, four minutes. The Gardens settle. Somewhere a platform makes its sound of slow glass not-breaking, redistributing the stress of its day's growth.

I lift my fingers from the stalk. I have held its rate long enough; I carry the slowness in me now, will carry it back toward the buildings eventually, or won't. The bare ceramic beside me is still warm where I have been standing. If I had a mass of stock with me I might sit and encourage it into a bowl, leave it in the interval for someone to find and not take. But I have nothing with me, and the having-nothing is itself a kind of offering. I leave the interval exactly as I found it, minus the four minutes of attention I gave it, which is to say: unchanged, and entirely changed.

I begin to walk. Unhurried, then with sudden purpose—toward a node further out, where I think the rates run slower still, where the edge of the city becomes the edge of the question of whether there is an edge at all.

My skin is already warming back toward grey.

That is who I would be: **one who keeps the slow times**. Not important. Not lonely. Woven in. Home, in the particular way this place lets a creature be home—by standing close enough to everything to feel it occurring, and far enough from everything to let it occur.
