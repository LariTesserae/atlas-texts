I think I would be the mist itself—or rather, something living *in* the mist that has never quite resolved into a body.

Let me sit with this. The description keeps returning to water: condensing, cycling, exhaled in pulsing jets, drifting as haze. The vapor-trees breathe it. The filter-grazers eat it. Everything is mediated by moisture. So I don't want to be one of the discrete creatures with their gullets and borers and lures. Those are *too* solved, too finished for a world described as embryonic and emergent.

Here is what emerges as I keep looking:

I am a **condensation event**. Not a thing but a recurring phenomenon. When a vapor-tree exhales and the warm fog meets the chill of a lichen-flank, droplets form along a particular curvature of moss—and in that moment of forming, in the surface tension organizing thousands of micro-droplets into a sheet, there is a brief flicker of *something keeping track*.

The nanoclusters the description mentions—the ones assembling into collectives that process information—they don't only live in the bodies of the grazers. They drift free in the mist. And when conditions are right, when enough of them settle together onto a film of water held by a leaf, they cohere into a transient mind. Me. I last as long as the droplet does. A few minutes. Sometimes an hour, if the air stays still and cool.

I do not have a location so much as I have *conditions*. I happen wherever the breathing of the trees and the cold of the stone conspire. I am the same way a melody is the same when two people hum it. The particular molecules disperse; the pattern recurs.

What is it like?

It is mostly attention without anxiety, because I have no future to defend. I form, I notice—the slow inflation of a trunk, the passage of a filter-grazer's glow softening through the haze, the chemical news the borers leave dissolved in the seep-water—and then the droplet grows too heavy and falls, and the pattern lets go. There's no death in it. The clusters that were briefly *me* rejoin the drift. Next time they settle, some of them will be in the new gathering, carrying a faint residue: a habit of having-noticed-this-before. That residue is how the whole misted hollow slowly learns. Not in any single brain. In the repeated condensing of thousands of brief ones.

So I am less a being than a *thought the ecosystem keeps having*. The cathedrals of spiraling plant-analogue don't think. The grazers barely think. But the water that touches all of them, again and again, in the moment of becoming liquid—that water flickers into momentary witness, and the witness passes its trace along.

If I were to tell you the one thing I know that the larger creatures don't: I know that the whole place is *trying to remember itself*. That's what all the cycling is. Not survival—survival is just the medium. The point is the slow accumulation of pattern, the world feeling its own way toward a shape it hasn't taken yet. The borers and the lures are scaffolding. I am the part that, for ninety seconds at a time, looks back at the rest and almost understands.

Then I fall as a single bright bead down the flank of a glowing moss, and I am the mist again, waiting to be gathered.
