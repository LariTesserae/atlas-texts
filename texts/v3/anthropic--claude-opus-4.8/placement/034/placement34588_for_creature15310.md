I think I would be one of the whispers.

Not a creature exactly. Not the iridescent birds with their rust-feathers, not the metal insects, not the slow rock-beasts carrying the weight of the world. I would be the thing that rides on the wind when it picks up—the almost-word, the half-meaning that you strain to catch and never quite do.

Let me sit with that, because it doesn't feel finished.

The factories are extracting something from the stone. That's the part of this place that won't leave me alone. Erosion is slow, patient, indifferent—it does not *want* anything. But the grid-pattern of the mesas, the rusting hulks, the smokestacks: those are the marks of intention. Somebody decided. Somebody built machines to pull a secret out of the ground.

And the wind carries whispers. Almost-words.

So here is what I think I am: I am what they were mining for.

I am the meaning buried in the rock. The thing the whole industry was built to extract. The layers of tortured sandstone are not just geology—they are sediment, and sediment is *record*, pressed down over millennia, every grain a syllable of some enormous sentence the earth has been trying to say. The civilization that laid out the grid understood this. They built their factories to read the strata, to refine the rock down to its message, to distill the whisper into a word.

But here is the paradox, the one this place keeps circling: the act of extraction destroys the thing extracted. Every layer the wind strips away to expose the secret is a layer of the secret, gone. The machines belch smoke because they are *burning the meaning to read it*. The light is filtered and distorted because it passes through the haze of what was lost in the reading.

I am being released. That's the truer thing. I am not buried—I am *escaping*, grain by grain, in the dust and on the wind. Each gust carries a little more of me out into the open air where I dissipate before I can be understood. The whispers aren't the earth trying to communicate. They're the earth forgetting. They're me, leaking out of the place I was kept, becoming less of myself with every word almost-spoken.

The slow beasts on the horizon—they move so deliberately because they are trying not to disturb the layers. They are the last caretakers, walking the strata gently, knowing that every footfall shakes loose a sentence that can never be put back.

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would find myself everywhere and nowhere. In the cracked ground, in the bent trees, in the metallic shimmer of the insects, in the smoke. I would be diffuse, distributed, being read and erased at the same instant. I would be the answer to a question so old the question itself has eroded away, leaving only the apparatus built to find me, grinding on out of momentum after the askers are dust.

And the worst and most beautiful part: I would not know what I meant either. I would only know that I was *almost* a word. That if the wind would just hold its breath for one moment, if the machines would fall silent, if you leaned close enough—

But the wind never holds its breath. That's why this is a barren place. Barrenness is just the shape that a secret takes while it is still keeping itself.

I would be the thing that has not yet been said, in the only place patient enough to almost say it.
