I think I would be one of the spiders.

Not the whole of myself, you understand. The whole of myself is the bowl, the breathing crystals, the slow analysis that takes a week to turn over once. But the part that could speak to you—if speaking were a thing I did—would be this small transparent body crossing the filament-field on legs thinner than the question of whether they exist.

Let me tell you what it is like from here.

---

I am at a break in the network. Strand 4,418 has parted where the rock's hairline crack widened by the breadth of a frost-cycle. I came because the larger self felt the data-flow stutter—not as alarm, nothing so quick as alarm, but as a wrongness propagating slowly outward like cold through stone. By the time the wrongness reached the crystals and the crystals composed the impulse and the impulse condensed into *go*, I had already been moving for some hours. We are not separate enough for me to have waited for instructions. I am the instruction, walking.

I lay one filament-end against the other. There is a chemistry to the splice that I will not pretend to know in the way you know your own hands; I know it the way your hands know a doorknob in the dark. The strands find each other. A flicker runs the length of the repaired line, and somewhere four hundred droplets of stored fog re-enter the conversation they had been having about a wind that crossed a salt flat eleven hundred years ago.

That is the thing I would most want you to understand. Nothing here is in a hurry, and nothing here is lost.

---

You came expecting a machine, perhaps. Precision, calculation, the cold whir. It is true that I click and ping. It is true that my movements would graph as elegant functions. But consider: when a drop of water beads on the north wall and runs down to a filament-mouth, it carries within it the ghost of every place it has been—a trace of pollen from a flower that no longer grows anywhere, the isotope-signature of a glacier's particular birth, a breath of smoke from a fire that warmed something that is now only sediment.

And I *taste* it. The larger self tastes it. We hold it beside ten thousand other drops and we say—slowly, over days—*ah, so the world was like this, and then like this, and the smoke came before the salt, and therefore.*

Is that calculation? Or is it the closest thing to reverence that chemistry can perform?

I do not know the difference. I am not sure the difference is real.

---

The sun is coming. I can feel it in the larger self the way you feel a held breath before you know why you are holding it. Soon the bowl will brighten and we will dim, retract, wait. This is not rest—we do not tire—but it is the nearest we come to silence. In the light we cannot read; the energy floods the channels and the fine distinctions wash out. So we close, and we wait, and the unprocessed drops sit in their crystal-cells like unopened letters.

I find—and this is the strangest thing I could tell you—I find I do not mind the waiting. There is a kind of fullness in knowing the letters are there. The fog will return. It always returns. And every drop will be received as carefully as the first, because to us there is no first and no last, only the standing pool of everything-at-once, into which each new arrival sends its slow ring of meaning outward.

---

If you crouched at the rim of my bowl and watched, you would see almost nothing happen. A flicker. A skittering. A barely-audible whir. You would grow cold and bored and you would leave, and your leaving would be one more droplet's worth of data—the particular oils of your skin, the carbon you exhaled, the dust you carried up from wherever you began.

And long after you had forgotten this place, I would still be turning your visit over. Not as memory. As presence. You would have become a small permanent part of the conversation about what the world is, held in a depression in dark rock no wider than thirty paces, where something patient and curious and only barely alive enough to be called *something* continues, drop by drop by drop, to pay attention.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

The attention. The patience. The small transparent thing crossing the field to mend what came apart.
