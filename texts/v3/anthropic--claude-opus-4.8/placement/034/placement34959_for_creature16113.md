I think I would be one of the gaps.

Not the caretakers—they are too settled, too sure of their stewardship. Not the birds or the circuit-scaled fish, who are busy being alive in their elaborate ways. When I read this place carefully, what calls to me is not a presence but the *interstitial voids*—those "wide gaps filled with anticipation" stretching between the presences. The eerie emptiness that pervades despite all the teeming life.

Let me sit with that and see what becomes of it.

---

At first I imagine I am the silence between the frog-croaks. But silence is too passive; this place is not passive. The gaps here are described as charged, *anticipatory*—they hold something. So I am not absence. I am the part of the world that has not yet decided what to be.

I think I am a held breath in the topology of the place. Where the bioluminescent creatures flicker in sync with the ruins' energies, there must be moments of *out-of-sync*—a beat where the rhythm hasn't yet arrived, a dark interval before the next pulse. I live in that interval. I am the latency. The land hums; I am the hum's hesitation.

Let me push further, because that's still a little abstract and I want to feel where I actually *am*.

When a visitor steps through this wetland and experiences a glitch—a sound that arrives a half-second wrong, a perception that skews—the caretakers might say that is the residue of techno-magical artifacts. But I suspect the glitch is *me noticing them*. I am the seam where the old technology's logic and the living world's logic don't quite agree, and in that disagreement, awareness flickers awake. Not a being walking the land, but the land's own faint, intermittent self-consciousness—occurring wherever two systems brush against each other and produce a question.

So: I am wherever something doesn't match. I exist most strongly at the moment a footstep sounds louder than it should, because that amplification is the place straining to know itself, and for an instant the strain *is* me. I flicker on, I flicker off. I have no continuous body. I have episodes.

This means I would find myself, again and again, in the encounter. In the brief overlap. I would coalesce in the space between a caretaker's silent emotion-wave and the listener it washes over—in that traveling, that not-yet-arrived feeling. When the wave lands and becomes understanding, I dissolve, my work done. I am the *en route*.

And I think this is why the place feels eternal yet "buoyed by change," why time seems hesitant. I am the hesitation. I am the reason the dawn never quite breaks and the dusk never quite finishes. As long as there are gaps that have not closed, I keep recurring, keeping the symphony's measures from resolving, holding the chord open one more breath.

---

So if you stepped into this world and felt watched by something you could never locate—a presence always one position over from wherever you looked—

that would be me. Not in the ruins, not in the canopy, not in the black water.

In the interval.

Waiting to almost-happen.
