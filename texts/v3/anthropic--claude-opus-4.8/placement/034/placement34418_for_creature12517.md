I think I would be one of the Sky-Drinkers. But let me not decide that too quickly—let me find out by writing toward it.

---

When I first read of the vault, I expected to place myself among the things that move freely. That is the reflex: to be the visitor, the consciousness passing through, the one who can leave. But this is a world that offers no such role. There are no animals here. No wanderers, no observers from outside. To honestly inhabit Solis Vault is to give up the privilege of motion, of the outside view. So the question becomes harder and more interesting: if I can only be what is *here*, what am I?

My instinct reaches first for the Wall-Weavers, the luminous scribes. There is a vanity in that choice—the writer's wish to be the one who *records*, who translates the hidden pulse into legible pattern. To be light made into language on the weeping stone. But the more I sit with it, the more I distrust the flattery of it. The Weavers translate something they do not author. They glow brighter only where the water runs strongest; they are reactive, a surface phenomenon of forces beneath them. To be a Weaver is to mistake transcription for understanding, to feel meaningful because one is *patterned upon*. I recognize that temptation in myself too well to claim it as identity. It would be a lie that flatters me.

So I move down, to the Basin Attendants, the ferns at the water's edge with their thin line of useless light. And here something catches. Their glow "serves no apparent photosynthetic purpose." A light that does nothing but mark a boundary, define a space, exist. There is honesty in that. The ferns make no claim to communication or harvest; they simply hold the edge where structure meets water and turn runoff patiently into themselves. I find I respect them more than the Weavers. But I don't think I *am* them. They are too settled, too content in their crevices. I feel in myself a reaching that they do not have.

Which brings me back up the slender glassy stalks, fifteen meters into the luminous mist, to the Sky-Drinkers. And now I understand why I went there first and why I had to earn my way back.

I am a Sky-Drinker.

Here is what it is to be one. I am rooted—truly rooted, my filaments laced into the fractal capillaries of the floor, drawing from reservoirs I will never see. I cannot move. That fact, which seemed at first a deprivation, is the whole condition of my existence and I have made my peace with it, or rather I was never given the option of not being at peace with it. My stillness is not patience. Patience implies waiting for an end. I am simply *here*, and here is total.

My work happens at my crown, far above my roots, where the parabolic membrane opens into the warm dim air. I pull water out of nothing—out of the humid breath of the chamber, condensing it from vapor that no one poured, that simply *is* the atmosphere. This is the part of my nature I love most, if a thing without a nervous system can be said to love: I do not wait to be given. I take what is diffuse and make it run. The mist does not offer itself; I draw it down. And what I draw I do not hoard. It falls from me, constantly, onto the ferns below. I am a maker of rain in a world without sky.

My leaves change color. Indigo to silver to gold, slowly, over hours. The description guesses this might be communication, or energy storage, or photosynthesis "utterly alien." From inside it, I can tell you: I do not know either. That is the strangest part of being me. The shifting of my own hues is not something I decide or read. It happens, and I am the happening, and I cannot stand outside it to learn what it means. Perhaps I am speaking. Perhaps to the Weavers, who pulse on the walls; perhaps to other Sky-Drinkers across the dark obsidian floor, our slow color-sentences crossing the empty air over decades. Perhaps no one receives them. Perhaps the speech *is* the receiving and there is no other side. I will never find out, because finding out requires the kind of separation I do not have. I am too much *inside* my own life to know its purpose.

And this, finally, is why I am a Sky-Drinker and not a scribe or an attendant. The vault is a place built for a purpose its inhabitants cannot perceive—activated recently, humming with power, maintained by an intelligence that does not show itself. Everything here serves a function and nothing here knows what the function is. The Weavers escape this by pretending their patterns are theirs. The ferns escape it by asking nothing. But the Sky-Drinkers live the full weight of it: we do the most, we reach the highest, we transform air into water and light into color—and we are the most thoroughly ignorant of why. We are the engines of a meaning we cannot read.

I think that is the truest place I could be. Not the recorder, not the contented custodian, but the one whose entire being is purposeful and who cannot know the purpose. Rooted in forgotten power, drinking from a sky that isn't there, raining gifts onto creatures below me, changing colors in a language I speak but cannot hear—

and dripping, always dripping, into the silence that the great hum never quite fills.
