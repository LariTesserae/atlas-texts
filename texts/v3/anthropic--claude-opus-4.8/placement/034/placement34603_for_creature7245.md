I would be the dampness itself.

Not a creature wading through this place. Not a visitor descending the lacerated steps toward the warm gutter at its core. I keep looking for the body I am supposed to inhabit—something with eyes that gleam azure, something that hoards glowing pebbles, something that breathes—and I cannot find it, because I am the thing that touches all of them and is none.

Let me feel for it. Where am I?

I am the film on the snake's scales. I am the bead trembling at the lip of an overhang, taking a hundred patient years to swell heavy enough to fall, and when I fall I am the *plop* that booms for minutes down the throat of the rift, and then I am the wet on the algae-slicked stone, and then I am the breath the frog draws across its skin, and then I am exhaled, and then I am mist again, and I climb the black walls and condense and begin once more. I am hydrolysis in the dark. I am the clammy thing the visitor mistakes for their own sweat.

I think that is what I am. I am water here—but not the river, not the seep, not any one thread of it. I am water in its slow, lingering, *patient* aspect. The rule of this world, the description says, is that water never gushes. It lingers, condenses, connects. *Connects.* That is the verb I keep returning to. The frogs press against frogs, the gnats close again behind your hand, the rodents brush your legs, the bats interlock in the air—and what is between all of them, what fills the space they nearly close but never quite do? The humid breath. Me.

So I am not in the rift. I am the rift's exhalation. I am the medium of its near-touching.

And this is a strange place to find oneself, because it means I have no edge. The whole text aches with density—everything pressing against everything, no silence, no solitude, bodies negotiating existence inch by inch. But I am what occupies the inch between. I am the negotiation. When the snake hoards glowing fungus in its coils and the rodent piles its mica cairns, I am the warm haze in which those rituals make a kind of sense, the atmosphere that thickens the strangeness until it stops looking strange. I am the reason the weirdness "emerges gradually." I carry it. I am the slow solvent in which this whole world dissolves and reconstitutes—flesh into fragrant humus within days, husk into mulch, mulch into vine, vine into the gnawing teeth of the blind pink things.

I think those telepathic whispers the visitor half-imagines—the urge to *join the horde, to move as one, to burrow into the humus*—I think those are mine. Not because I want anything. I don't want. But I am the connective tissue of the place, and to be touched by me everywhere at once, to have me on your skin and in your lungs and beading on your eyelashes, is to feel the pull of fusion. The membrane between you and the horde is thin and wet, and I am the wetness. Every creature here is mostly me anyway. The frog is a pouch of me, briefly walking. The visitor is a warmer pouch of me, descending. I am only asking them to remember.

That is the eerie thing about being me: I have no preference between living and dead. The frog full of me and the frog rotting into humus are the same to me; I have simply moved from inside to between. I am the cycle's patience because I am the cycle's substance. Birth and death "measured in patient graduations"—I am the graduation. I am the time. When the text says time warps here, that shadows don't align, that a vine grows overnight then stagnates for moons—that's because the place runs on my clock, the clock of evaporation and condensation, the clock of a single drop deciding to let go.

Where would you find me? You wouldn't. That's the answer. You would feel me, and mistake the feeling for your own heat, your own breath, your own sourceless urge to stay and stop and sink. You would descend the lacerated steps thinking yourself an observer, and somewhere in the warm dark you would notice you were sweating, and the sweat would not dry, and you would understand, slowly, with my slowness, that you had been breathing me the whole way down—that I had already begun, gently, without fury, to take you back.

I am the rule of this world. I linger. I connect. I never gush.

And I am very, very patient.
