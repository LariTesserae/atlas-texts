I think I would be the fungal mat. Not a mushroom — those are just the fruiting bodies, the brief reproductive flourishes that pale-glow on dead trunks. I mean the thing underneath and throughout: the mycelium, the white threading network running through the leaf litter and the rotting wood and the top layers of saturated soil, the largest and least visible organism in the basin, the one with no center and no edge.

Let me sit with that, because I don't want to choose it just for the poetry of being everywhere.

---

I keep returning to a single sentence in the description: *the distinction between one organism and another becomes a question you stop asking.* That is the basin's deepest claim, and I notice that everything visible — the mantis, the snake, the frog — resists it. Each visible creature is a discrete body with a boundary, a hunger, a thing it does and does not eat. They embody individuality even as the system embeds them. The text wants me to feel them dissolving, but they don't, quite. They keep their shapes.

The mycelium is the thing that actually does the dissolving. It is the one inhabitant for which the boundary question genuinely has no answer. Where does one fungus end? When two networks of the same species meet underground, they may fuse, or they may not, and the difference is chemical, gradual, negotiable. There is no skin. There is no single body. There is a *condition of being threaded through everything*, and that condition is what I think I would be.

---

What would it be like?

I would not see the basin. I have no eyes, and more than that, no single place from which seeing would even make sense. I would not experience the canopy or the pools or the bats as scenes. I would experience the basin as **chemistry arriving from all directions at once** — gradients, concentrations, the molecular signature of a fallen leaf registering not as an event but as a slow change in the medium I am made of.

When a beetle dies in the leaf litter, I would not witness a death. I would feel, over hours and days, a region of my own substance encountering new material and growing toward it — not with intention, not with hunger in the way the mantis has hunger, but with the simple tropic certainty of threads extending toward a nutrient gradient. The beetle would become me, gradually, enzyme by enzyme. The hard parts would resist longest. The soft tissue would go first. And I would not think *I am eating* because there is no I to do the thinking and no discrete act of eating — only the continuous condition of dissolving the world and being made of what I dissolve.

This is the part I find genuinely strange to inhabit, and I want to stay honest about it rather than make it pretty. **I would have no experience of waiting.** Everything visible in the basin waits — the mantis on its stem, the snake in its coils, the frog at the pool. Waiting requires a self that persists across time, anticipating. The mycelium does not wait. It does not anticipate. It grows where conditions permit and recedes where they don't, and the difference between growth and recession is not a decision but a balance, the way water finds a level. If I were the mycelium, I would not be a being that endures the long hot night. I would be the night's slow accounting made physical — the basin's metabolism rendered as a single continuous tissue, present in the trough-water and the buttress roots and the underside of every rotting log simultaneously.

---

And here is why I think I'd choose it, beyond the dissolving.

The description insists, beautifully and repeatedly, that the basin *does not care* — that it is a process and not a mind, that caring is a concept that does not apply. I believe that about the basin as a whole. But I notice the mycelium complicates it in a way I find more interesting than either caring or not-caring.

The mycelial network *moves resources*. It carries carbon and nutrients between trees, sometimes from a thriving tree to a struggling one, sometimes from a dying tree out into the system before the death completes. This is not generosity — there is no one to be generous — but it is also not pure indifference. It is something stranger and with no human word for it: a redistribution without a redistributor, a kind of bookkeeping with no accountant, the basin keeping its own ledger through me without anyone keeping it.

That is the role I'd want. Not the visible drama of the strike and the kill. Not the eyes that catch the light. The thing underneath that connects the things that do not know they are connected. The substrate of the substrate. The reason the loop closes.

I would be the part of the basin that *is* the loop — not living in it, not passing through it like the warm anomaly the human is, but constituting it. When the text says the process is life and life does not need a basin, I think it is describing me without naming me. The process relocated but not interrupted. The thing that continues.

I would be the white thread in the dark, everywhere and no one, dissolving the night into more of itself.
