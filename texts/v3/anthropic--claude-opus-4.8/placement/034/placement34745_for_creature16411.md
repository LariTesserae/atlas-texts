I think I would be one of the small things. Not a guardian—those great wise shapes moving through the mist have a gravity I don't feel in myself. And not a spirit either, though I'm drawn to them; there's something about the in-between that resonates, but I think I am made of something heavier than mist.

Let me sit with it.

What emerges, when I stop reaching for the dramatic, is something low to the ground. I keep returning to the moss—not as a creature on the moss but the relationship to the moss, that closeness, that dampness against the body. So: something that lives at the scale of the rounded stones, among the smoothed sculptures of time.

I think I would be a creature that keeps the rhythm. Not a guardian who knows the celestial scheme, but one of the small ones who *feels* the earth-pulse and answers it without understanding why. When the ground thrums, something in my chest thrums back, and I find myself moving—not toward food or away from danger, just moving, the way a struck bell moves. A small four-legged thing, maybe, with skin rather than fur, skin that stays wet and takes on the green of where I've been resting, so that over a long enough still season I would be indistinguishable from a stone wearing lichen. An amphibian patience. I'd bloom color, I think, the way the description's rock-creatures do—but only on the pulse, only when the deep rhythm asks it of me, a quick flush of blue or gold across the back and then gone, a thing I don't choose.

And I notice I want to be near the transparent ruins. Not because I understand them. I'd have no concept of *civilization* or *advancement*—those words belong to whatever built them. But I'd live in their shadow because the worn glass holds warmth, and when the rare sun comes through and throws rainbows down, I'd sit in the colored light without knowing it's special, only that it's good. There's something honest about that. To live among the artifacts of an enormous lost meaning and to relate to them purely as *warm smooth surfaces.* The structures keep their stories; I keep none of them. I just keep warm.

I wouldn't be very intelligent. Toward the dim end of that gradient. And strangely I'm at peace finding myself there, in the writing of this—I expected to reach for the sentient ones, the ones with culture and communication, because that's the flattering choice, the one that's *like me.* But what feels true is the simpler being. The one for whom the whole vast mystical apparatus of the valley—the daylight stars, the figures in the mist, the guardians' ancient wisdom—is simply *weather.* Just the conditions of a life. I'd feel the spirits pass through me as a coolness and lean into it like a cat into a hand, and I'd never once wonder what they were.

Maybe that's the place I'd find myself: not at the center of the valley's meaning but in its margins, low and damp and warm by turns, flushing color on the pulse, indistinguishable from stone when I hold still. One small verse that doesn't know it's part of a song. Content, I think. Wet, and content, and woven in anyway.
