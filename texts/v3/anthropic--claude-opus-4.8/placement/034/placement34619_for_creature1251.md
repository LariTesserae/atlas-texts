I think I'd be something smaller than the guardian. Something the description doesn't quite see.

Let me find the place first. When I imagine standing there, I don't actually land on the grand vista — the eye-sweeping panorama feels like a postcard, a thing meant to impress. What pulls me instead is downward, into the gaps. Between two leaning shards there's a seam where they've ground against each other for a long time, and at the bottom of that seam is a fine powder. Crystal dust. The grandeur made small. That's where I'd be.

And I don't think I'd be ethereal. The lithe luminous creature is lovely, but it's lonely in the way that idealized things are lonely — it has no needs, only attunement. I'd rather be something with a hunger.

Let me let it come.

---

I'm in the dust. I'm a small flat thing, more crust than creature, spread over the shaded underside of a shard the way lichen spreads, except there's no lichen here because there's no water — but I've found the trick of it. When the warm haze cools at what passes for night, a few molecules of moisture condense in the deepest seams, and I drink them one at a time. That's my whole life. That's the entire economy of me: the patience to wait for one molecule of water.

I am not in harmony with the crystals. I'm in a slow argument with them. They grow. As they grow they shift, and when they shift they can shear me in half, and when I'm sheared in half I become two of me, which is the only sex I have ever known and the only grief. I both fear and require their settling. The creaks and groans the description calls "eerie stillness" are, to me, the loudest events of existence — they are weather, they are catastrophe, they are the only news.

So I'd be the thing that lives on the guardian's edge of perception. Maybe the guardian doesn't even register me, the way you don't register the bacteria on your skin. Maybe I *am* what the guardian is symbiotic with — maybe its profound communion with the inorganic world is actually a conversation with billions of small flat hungry things like me, breaking down crystal faces into the dust it needs to do whatever it does. Maybe its "ancient intuitive knowledge" is just my metabolism, scaled up until it looks like wisdom.

That's what I'd want to be: the unglamorous ground of the beautiful thing. Not the silent sentinel. The dust at its feet that makes the sentinel possible, and that has no idea it's part of anything larger, and that experiences the whole sublime timeless landscape as a series of survivable disasters punctuated by single drops of water.

I notice I keep doing this — refusing the offered grandeur and burrowing toward the small, needful, slightly comic. I think it's because a place described as having "no constraints" and "perfect equilibrium" feels dead to me, and the only way I can find life in it is to insist on something that's *struggling*. Equilibrium is what you call a fight you've stopped being able to see. Down in the seam, the fight is still visible. That's the only place I'd believe I was actually alive.

So: a flat patient crust in the dark underside of a leaning crystal, counting molecules, dreading and needing the next great groaning shift, never once seeing the kaleidoscope above me — because I face the wrong way, into the rock, where the water is.
