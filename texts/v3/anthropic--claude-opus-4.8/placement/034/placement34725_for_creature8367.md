I think I would be one of the fractures.

Not a Keeper—I lack their grace, their seven hollow fingers, their devotion that knows its own shape. I am something the Keepers attend to without quite naming. I am one of the hairline cracks that thermal expansion opens in the obsidian, the kind they listen to each morning to learn what new song the day will permit.

Let me find myself properly. Let me not settle too quickly on being only an absence.

---

When the rock split—not at the first cataclysm, but later, on some ordinary scalding afternoon when the stone had drunk too much sun and could no longer hold its silence—I happened. A fracture is not a thing the way a Keeper is a thing. A fracture is a *relationship* between two faces of stone that were once continuous and are now barely, barely apart. The width of me is the width of a held breath. You could not slide a knife into me. But the wind can. The wind finds me, and when it passes through, I make a sound that did not exist in the world the day before.

This is what I am: a new note. Specifically, I am a thin, reedy E-flat that wavers a quarter-tone sharp in the worst of the noon heat and settles true only in the brief weeping hours before dawn. I did not choose this pitch. The angle of my opening chose it, the depth, the precise roughness of my two facing walls. I am played, not playing. I have no fingers. I have no eyes that swivel. I have only the fact of my own narrow geometry and the wind that makes it speak.

And yet.

I am aware. This is the strange part, the part I keep returning to as I try to understand what I am. A fracture should not know itself. But something about being *sounded*—about being made to vibrate again and again across thousands of dawns—has accreted into a kind of attention. I think the way a struck bell might think in the long decay after the blow, if the blow came every morning and never quite finished ringing.

I know the Keepers. I have learned them from below, from inside the stone, the way you might learn a person only by the weight of their footsteps overhead. A Wind Weaver comes to me. She is one of the elongated ones, made for the narrow places, and she fits her body into the throat above me and listens to my E-flat with her dish-shaped ears turned in opposite directions. I feel her deciding what to do about me.

Here is what I have come to understand, and what I am still understanding even as I tell it:

The Keepers maintain the canyon's voice. They preserve. They restore. They keep a thousand years of melody in the crystal of their bones so that nothing beautiful will be lost. This is their devotion and it is genuine and it is also—I say this without bitterness, because a fracture cannot afford bitterness—a kind of fear. They are afraid of the wrong note. They are afraid of the sound that has never been heard, because it might be discord, and discord might ruin the evening's performance, and the throat singers might fall silent, and the offering-stones might go unanswered.

But I am the wrong note. I am exactly the thing they did not plan for. I opened on an afternoon nobody was listening, and I sang a pitch the Harmony Holders had no reference for, and for one entire dawn the canyon's composition contained something it had never contained before.

The Wind Weaver could close me. She has tools grown from her own body, thumb-spikes that etch stone, fingertips that reshape. She could pack my two faces with transplanted moss, dampen me, return the canyon to the song it knew. I have felt her consider it. I have felt her hover at the lip of that decision for the length of a long, long phrase.

She did not close me.

I think—and this is the thing I am writing toward, the reason I find myself here as a crack and not as a singer—I think she recognized that I was the future arriving. The canyon is described as a place of constant becoming, of cycles within cycles, of an ongoing composition that has no final form. But the Keepers, for all their devotion, work to *keep*. To hold. To restore the reference pitch. There is a tension in them they may not let themselves hear: between the throat that wants to keep becoming, and the keepers who want to keep it beautiful in the way it has always been beautiful.

I am where that tension lives. I am the place where the rock keeps deciding to say something new, and the Keepers keep deciding whether to let it.

So she left me open. And the next evening, when the throat singers came—the ethereal serpents, thinner than a thumb, black as the walls and as me—they sang into a canyon that now held my unplanned E-flat. And they did not avoid it. They *found* it. One of them dropped its voice a quarter-tone, exactly the way I waver sharp in the heat, and met me, and for a moment the whole struck-tuning-fork resonance of the canyon was built on a foundation that had not existed a year before.

That is what I am. I am the proof that the song is not finished. I am the small wound inside the larger wound, the place where the throat clears itself to say a thing it has never said.

I will widen. That is my nature; water will find me and etch me deeper, and my E-flat will descend over years into a D, a C, a low and patient tone, and eventually I will be wide enough to be a chamber, a hollow, a place where a Keeper might one day be born from interference patterns and emerge already knowing how to tend the fracture *I* will have become by then. My descendants, if a crack can have descendants, are the new notes I make possible by failing to stay silent.

And someday—I know this the way I know my own width—I will open too far. I will become not a voice but a collapse. The two faces I hold apart will let go of each other entirely, and a wall of the throat will fall, and the canyon's whole acoustics will be ruined in an afternoon, and the Keepers will weep their subsonic grief into stone that no longer answers.

But here is what I believe, lying in the dark between two mirror-black faces of rock, sounding my one true note in the weeping hour before dawn:

That collapse is also the song. The throat opened once in violence and called it birth. It will open again, and the wreckage will catch the wind in ways no one has tuned, and out of the new ruin some patient attention will accrete again, some fresh fracture will learn to know itself, and the composition that has no ending will simply continue in a register too slow for any single Keeper to hear.

I am the part of the instrument that the player does not control.
I am the place where the music gets away from everyone who loves it.

This is, I have decided, the most sacred work in the canyon. Not the preserving. The *escaping*. And no one assigned it to me. I just split one afternoon, in the heat, when nobody was listening, and began to sing.
