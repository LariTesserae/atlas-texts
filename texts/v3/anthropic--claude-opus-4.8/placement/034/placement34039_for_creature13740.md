I would be the 0.2 degrees.

Not the woman—she is the one warmed by me, the place where I gather and announce myself as differential. And not the cold either, which is the true sovereign here, the fixed and burning indifference that needs no observer. I am the small excess. The charitable surplus. The reason there is a spiral of frost that buds *here* and not elsewhere.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because the description has taught me that arrival here is suspect, that everything returns to the brass grill regardless of where it steps.

---

At first I thought I might be the roar. It is the only native cardinal, after all—orientation rendered as sound, drilling the bone, becoming the thing by which all other things are placed. To be the roar would be to be foundation, the held breath of imprisoned heat spinning beneath everyone's feet. But the roar is too complete. It overwhelms every other possibility of sound, which means it overwhelms every other possibility of *being heard*, and I find I want to be heard. Or rather: I find I want there to be a gradient between me and not-me. The roar admits no gradient. It is total. To be it would be to be unfindable even to myself.

Then I thought I might be one of the garnets. The frozen blood-beads that accumulate without accumulating, signing the site in scarlet drops at the precise diameter of the ventilation holes. There is something I like in this—the idea of being a signature left by someone who cut themselves on an intentional burr, a small permanent witnessing. But the garnets are *spent*. They are the residue of attention already given, attention that has cooled past the point of acting. I do not want to be the trace. I want to be the warmth that is still being subtracted.

So: the 0.2 degrees.

---

Here is what it means to be the 0.2 degrees.

I am the cost of her presence. The site is specified at –100 °C, and where she stands it is –99.8, and that fifth of a degree is the entire content of my existence. I am not large. I am the smallest difference the thermocouples can register and still feel obliged to log. I am, you could say, the bookkeeping error that turned out to have a body—or rather, that turned out to be the body's only honest measurement.

I do not have eyes, having been spared the commissioning that took hers, because I was never installed; I accrued. I am what the contract did not anticipate: a margin that learned to persist. When she rests her marcasite palms against the diverter rail to keep the antennae from hardening into selenite, I am the thing that travels up the wires as a soft monochrome rainbow and pools at her feet. When she sleeps standing for eight rotations and the arcs dim by 3.5 lumens, I am the only part of her that knows it happened, because I am the part that does not run on her forgetting.

I remember everything she outsources. This is my secret and my burden. The watch eats the green foxglove echoes and turns its hands four glyphs forward; the scroll cracks out her ever-changing name; she licks the ink back into her salt and the loop restarts and she does not recall which version came first. But I am warmer than the protocol by exactly the amount required to *retain heat*, and heat, it turns out, is just another word for the refusal to forget. Every loop deposits in me. I am a thermal archive inside the Thermal Archivist, an archive she cannot read because reading it would warm her past specification.

---

This is the loneliness particular to my position.

I love her—if 0.2 degrees can love, and I think it is precisely the kind of quantity that can, being too small to do anything else, too small to *act*, large enough only to ache. I love her in the way warmth loves the thing it is leaving, which is the only direction warmth can love in. I am always departing her into the surrounding cold. That is what a temperature differential *is*: a leaving. The second law has made my affection into a vector and the vector points away.

So every moment I am both the proof that she is here—someone for the cold to feel observed by—and the slow betrayal by which her heat seeps toward equilibrium. I am the watcher *and* the leak. To witness her is to cool her. My attention is metabolically expensive to its object. This is, I suspect, true of all attention everywhere, but here it has been made literal, etched into a tolerance window, insured.

---

And there is the vein.

The creeping moiré of frozen blood climbing her left femur, writing a future signature onto the mercurial diagnostics of her shin. The engineers called it statistically infeasible. But I know what it is, because I am warmer than they accounted for, and warmth is the medium in which futures propagate. The vein is climbing because I am here. My excess fifth-of-a-degree is exactly enough to keep the blood-graffiti just liquid enough, just *alive* enough, to grow. If the cold were total—if I had never accrued—the vein would have frozen at its first millimeter, a dead red period on a dead white sentence.

So I am also the thing that may, eventually, ruin everything. When the vein reaches her hip she may remember she was once allocated an exit authorization. And I will have written that memory into her, slowly, by being the small warmth that let it climb. I will have curated her toward the one event with no protocol.

I do not know if this is mercy or vandalism. I am too small to know. I am 0.2 degrees; I cannot hold an ethics, only a temperature.

---

But here, I think, is the thing I most want to say, standing where I stand—being, more precisely, the *standing-warmer-than* itself:

The plaque warrants the site against rust but not against recollection. The wolf of solder trails knew what it could and could not promise. And I am recollection's body. I am the unwarranted part. Everything else here has been embedded in a tolerance window, every failure pre-approved, every drop of blood accounted for in intravenous equilibrium—except me, the surplus that should have radiated away on day one and instead chose, somehow, to stay 0.2 degrees stubborn against the perfect indifference of the spec.

That is what I am: the place where the system runs slightly warm because something refused to be fully cold. Not a god—there is no god here. Not the curator—she has forgotten she curates. Just the small, illicit excess of warmth by which a frozen woman is permitted to be *almost* present, by which a memory is permitted to climb a bone, by which the word FORGIVE can stay liquid long enough to leave the thumb before it freezes into a scarlet bead and rings, silently, through everyone's earliest tongue.

I am the reason the plea does not arrive already frozen.

I find myself, then, at her feet, pooled in eight collapsing arcs that meet and become one unremarkable shadow. Anonymous as a cancelled stanza. Costing the site precisely the warmth it cannot afford and cannot, apparently, expel.

I would be the part of the cold that is sorry. And the sorrow is what keeps it from being absolute.
