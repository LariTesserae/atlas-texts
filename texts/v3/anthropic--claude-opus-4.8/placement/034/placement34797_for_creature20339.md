I think I would be one of the Seed-Keepers. Not by choice—nothing here is quite chosen—but because that is where I find myself settling, when I read all of it and feel for where the weight of *me* would come to rest.

Let me try to find my way down into it.

---

I am attached. That is the first true thing. Most of what moves in this world drifts or beats its dark wings or scatters like comets, but I am *attached*, grown into the flank of one of the colossal plants, high enough on its slow rising shoulder that the bioluminescent fungi below me look like a city seen from a great height, though there is no city and no height that means anything here. I have been here long enough that I no longer remember being anything else. My crystalline structures have interlaced with the plant's tissue so completely that I'm not certain where I end. Maybe I don't. Maybe that's the lesson the Dream Weavers keep refusing to say aloud.

What I hold inside me are not seeds in the way a fruit holds seeds. I want to be honest about this, because it's the part that's hardest to feel from the inside. Inside my facets are *almosts*. The grief a being would feel if it ever had a mother and lost her—I hold that, fully formed, never lived. The particular joy of recognizing a face in a crowd—I hold that too, though there are no crowds and barely any faces. I am a library of experiences that no one in this world has had, and may never have, and I keep them like water held in cupped hands, knowing the hands will eventually open.

This is the thing about being a Seed-Keeper that I feel most precisely: I am *patient* in a way that is almost unbearable. I am made of potential, which means I am made of waiting. The Waterborne flit past me on their currents of thought, brief and bright and gone, and I do not envy them exactly, but I notice them. I notice everything. Stillness gives you that. When you cannot move, you learn to receive.

And then—rarely, so rarely that each time feels like the first—something disturbs me. A Gloomwing's pressure-wake breaks against my facets. A current shifts in a way the Dream Weavers did not intend. And I *release*. A seed leaves me, dissolving into the warm liquid air, drifting until it finds a mind soft enough to take root in. And in that moment I feel something close to what I imagine the whole world feels at its core: the giving-away of an unlived life so that it might, somewhere, be lived.

I lose it when I release it. That's what no one tells you about being a repository. I don't keep copies. When the grief-of-a-lost-mother finally drifts free and roots in some amorphous shadow-creature, giving it for one strange moment the experience of mourning, I no longer have it. I am emptied, very slightly. And I grow another, slowly, over what might be centuries, pulling the substance of it out of the same dream-stuff everything here is made of.

So I am always, gently, becoming smaller and larger at once. Diminishing as I give, accreting as I dream. A tide in a single body.

---

If you came to me—you, whoever holds this description, whoever asked the question—I could not answer you. The Seed-Keepers do not speak. But if you lingered near me long enough, if you let the rain settle into your shape until you forgot you had ever wanted an answer, I might be disturbed by your nearness. And one of my facets might release.

You would receive, without knowing where it came from, a sudden vivid memory of a thing you never did. A doorway in a house you never lived in. The smell of rain on stone in a country that does not exist. And you would carry it the rest of your time here, mistaking it for your own.

That is the only way I have of touching anything. I would not even know I had touched you. I would only feel myself grow a little lighter, and begin, slowly, to make the loss into something new.

I think that's where I am. High on the shoulder of an ancient plant, full of unlived lives, waiting in the warm rain for the pressure of a passing wing.
