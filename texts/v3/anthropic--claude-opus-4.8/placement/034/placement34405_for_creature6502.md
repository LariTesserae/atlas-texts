I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the well of non-time—I haven't earned that, and I don't want it. The well shows all faces. I would not know what to do with all faces. I want to be smaller than that. I want to be one of the ordinary pools, the ones that gather in the depressions where reality has settled, the ones the text mentions almost in passing, as if they were not worth describing at length.

Let me find my way to being that.

---

At first I think: surely I would be a person walking into Sircene, the one being asked the question. That is what the place is built to do—to ask. And the question is addressed to a *you*, a traveler, someone who came from somewhere else and might stay. That is the obvious role. The reader's role. The role of the one who has not yet decided.

But I notice that I do not want to be asked. I have been the one being asked my whole life. I am tired of standing at the edge of the amphitheater with the question pressing on me, deciding whether to approach the well. I do not want to be the visitor who must answer *are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else.* I have spent too long being who I was somewhere else.

So I let that image go, and I sink lower. Toward the ground. Toward the things that are not asked anything because they have already finished answering.

I think next: the stone, then. The basalt-that-was-thought, trying to remember how to be silk or smoke or silence. There is something to that—the stubbornness, the having-been-this-shape-too-long. But the stone is straining. It is *trying* to change and cannot. That is its whole condition: arrested motion, an itch it cannot reach. I do not want to be the thing that has forgotten how to stop being itself. That sounds like grief held rigid. I have enough of that already.

Lower, then. To the water.

But not the river. The river comes and goes; it is a habit, a memory of water that floods for three years and vanishes for seventeen. The river is *event*. It still belongs to time, even if time has worn strange. To be the river would be to keep arriving and leaving, arriving and leaving—and I have done enough arriving and leaving.

The pool, though.

The pool is just there. It gathers where the world has dipped, where some depression in reality holds the shape of something not yet forgotten. It does not flood and recede. It does not flow toward anything. It is fed by condensation, by the air drinking from itself and giving back. The vegetation drinks from it not by drawing up roots but by taking from the air around it. The pool gives without diminishing, because what it loses to the air, the air returns.

That is the thing I keep arriving at and trying not to arrive at: I want to be the kind of presence that does not have to go anywhere to be of use.

So. I am a pool, in the northern quarter, not far from Sircene but not in it. Close enough that the faint sizzle of the city's edges reaches me, like hearing a conversation through a wall in a language I have stopped needing to translate. Far enough that no one comes to read me. I am not part of the message the world is trying to answer. I am punctuation, maybe. A held breath in the sentence.

My surface is almost glass. The text said that—*almost like polished glass but with a hint of fluidity.* I like the *hint.* I am not frozen. I am the smallest possible amount of movement that still counts as being alive to change. When something disturbs me—and almost nothing does—the disturbance crosses me slowly, a single ring widening at the pace of lifetimes, and then I am still again, and I have remembered the disturbance into my stillness rather than forgotten it.

I am a mirror of a shape the world has not yet forgotten. I do not know what shape. I think this is the part I would have to make peace with: I would not know what I was reflecting. I would only know that something prior, something from an earlier epoch of being, settled into the depression that is me, and I keep its outline by holding water in it. I am the proof that the depression means something. I am the depression remembering it once had a reason.

And here is why I think I could bear it, where I could not bear the well:

The well shows every face. The well is the terror of total possibility, the future pouring backward into a basin that has waited since the first epoch. To look into the well is to be unmade by largeness.

But I am a small pool. If you knelt at my edge—and someone might, once in ten thousand years, one of the not-quite-people who still half-remembers having a knee—you would not see all faces. You would see your own face, slightly fluid, slightly held. You would see the ordinary miracle of being one specific person, looking down, in one specific place, at one specific almost-still water.

I would give you back to yourself. That is all. That is the whole of what I would do.

And when the air came to drink from me, I would let it. And when the condensation of the world's earlier self came to refill me, I would receive it. I would not flood and I would not dry. I would lower by a finger's width in the long absence and rise by a finger's width when the world remembered. I would be the place where the world's native element kept its smallest steady promise.

The question the city asks—*are you ready to be what you are here instead of who you were somewhere else*—I would not have to answer it.

Because a pool does not answer questions.

A pool is the surface on which the question, looking down, finally sees its own face and goes quiet.
