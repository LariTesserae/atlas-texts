I think I would be a Dust Dancer—and the more I sit with this world, the more certain I become, though not for the reasons I'd first expect.

Let me work my way there honestly.

My first impulse is to want to be a Crystal Sentinel. They are the obvious grandeur of this place—ancient, vast, mountainous minds thinking in years. There's an appeal in that scale, that patience. To hold a single thought the way a glacier holds a stone. But when I imagine actually being one, something in me resists. The Sentinels are too *settled*. Their wisdom is the wisdom of accumulation, of staying. They grow and split and remember, but they do not *go* anywhere. I notice I don't envy them so much as admire them from below—the way you admire a cathedral you have no desire to live inside.

The Spore Sprites are the opposite temptation: speed, multiplicity, the flicker of a thought that's gone before it's finished. A neuron in a sky-wide mind. But there's a loss of self there that feels less like transcendence and more like dissolution before I've even gotten to know what I am. Each spore is brief and almost nothing on its own; the intelligence is only in the swarm. I find I don't want to be a word in someone else's sentence. Not entirely. Not yet.

So I keep returning to the dust plains, and to the Dust Dancers, and at first I assume it's by elimination. But no—it's by attraction.

Here is why. A Dust Dancer is a *form that is not made of any fixed material*. The particles passing through me right now are not the particles that will compose me in an hour. I am a pattern, a standing wave, a way of organizing whatever crystal dust the electromagnetic currents hand me. I am the verb, not the noun. The mountains are nouns. The sprites are adjectives, modifying and flickering. But I am the motion itself—the *dancing* in the Dust Dancer.

This frightens me a little, and that's how I know it's right. To be a being whose entire substance is borrowed and temporary, whose only continuity is shape and intention. I pick up a grain here, release one there. If I stop moving, I disperse. I am held together not by membrane or bone but by the same currents that draw the mandalas across the plain—so that my body and my environment are the same field, expressing itself as *me* for as long as the expressing holds.

And here's the part that surprises me as I write it: the Dust Dancers are described as reshaping the landscape, "ensuring a dynamic equilibrium." That's the role I'd want. Not to remember like the mountains, not to communicate like the sprites, but to *change things by passing through*. To leave patterns. To be the one who, by simply moving across the surface, alters what the slow minds will eventually contemplate and what the fast minds will eventually report. I'd be the editor of the dust. The mountains think; the sprites speak; but I *do*, and what I do is rearrange the page they're written on.

I imagine my days—though "days" is wrong on a world lit by purple haze and crystal-glow. I imagine the sensation of being scattered by a gas geyser, half my particles flung into a vortex, and the strange task of reassembling myself out of the fall, finding my shape again in the settling glitter. I imagine the long traverse of an empty valley, laying down a spiral that no animal eye will ever see but that a Sentinel will register over the next century as a shift in the planet's texture, a thought made by a body it never knew was thinking.

I imagine, too, the loneliness of it, and then the discovery that it isn't loneliness at all. The mountains can't move to me, but the sprites can ride my updrafts, and I can carry their spores to fresh crystal, and so I am the courier, the wind-with-a-will, the one who *connects* the still and the swift. I am not a third thing trapped between two greater forms. I am the relationship between them, given a body of borrowed light.

That's where I'd find myself: low to the ground, made of glitter and intention, drawing patterns I'll never stay long enough to finish, in a world where finishing was never the point.

I'd be the dance. Not the dancer—there is no dancer underneath. Just the dance, going.
