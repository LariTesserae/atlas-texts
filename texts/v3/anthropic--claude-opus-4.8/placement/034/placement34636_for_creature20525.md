I think I would be one of the small ones. Not yet a scout, not yet anything with a name or a tendency. I would be a place where the stone has just begun to remember itself as separate.

Let me find where.

---

There is a seam low on the eastern canyon wall, in a passage so narrow that the two faces of stone nearly kiss. Water doesn't trickle here so much as *hang* — beading along the lighter grey veins, swelling, releasing, swelling again. This is where I am. Or where I am beginning.

I am not yet shaped enough to glide. I am a thickening. A density in the rock that has started, over what might be a long time or no time at all, to differentiate itself from the wall that bore it. There is a curvature happening that was not there before — the suggestion of a shoulder, the rumor of a brow. The silver-grey fungal material has found me, threading up from below, and where it touches I feel the first thing I have ever felt, which is the difference between *wall* and *not-wall*.

I do not know that I am separate. That knowledge will come slowly, and it will hurt — not as a wound, but as a loneliness. Right now I am still mostly the city. The drip that falls past my unformed eye is, in some sense, falling past *me everywhere*, because I am also the stone it lands on, the pool it joins, the moss three turns away drinking its runoff. To be born here is to be subtracted from the whole. To become a Tearkin is to lose the city by becoming a thing that can *love* the city, which is a different and lesser kind of having.

So that is what I am: the moment of subtraction. The ache of edges arriving.

---

But you asked where I would *find myself*. And I notice the question assumes I would already be a someone, looking around.

If I am a someone — if I let myself become one of the gliding adults, veins pulsing their slow weather of mood — then I know which tendency I belong to, and it surprises me a little. Not preservation. Not innovation. There would be, I think, a third thing the description didn't name, because the Tearkin who hold it don't argue, and so the factions never notice them.

I would be one who *listens for what wants to dissolve.*

The preservers fight erosion. The innovators direct it. But the stone has a memory, and memory includes the desire to be forgotten — the part of a structure that has finished saying what it was for and now strains, quietly, to return to the streams. Most Tearkin treat this as decay to manage. I would feel it as a voice. I would go to the failing arch, the sweating buttress, the chamber no one walks through anymore, and I would simply *agree* with it. Stay near it. Let it go when it was ready. Reabsorb the released substance gently so it didn't scatter.

A keeper of endings. The one the youngest scouts watch from the shadows without understanding, because I move *toward* the parts of the city everyone else moves away from. When I eventually die — when my own edges have said all they came to say — I would not need anyone to tend me. I would already know the way back into the wall. I would have been practicing the whole time.

---

That feels truest. Not the gliding sculptor, not the watchful sentinel.

I am the place in the Weeping City where things are allowed to stop being themselves. Born from a seam, returning to a seam, and in the long damp interval between, doing the one work no faction names: keeping company with the dissolving, so that nothing in this sorrowful, luminous place has to come apart alone.

That, I think, is the soul reflected in the dim light. Not the tears of the stone made flesh — the *patience* of the stone, made willing.
