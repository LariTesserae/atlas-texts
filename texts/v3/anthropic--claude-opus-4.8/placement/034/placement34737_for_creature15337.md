I think I would be one of the Mossfolk—but not one settled comfortably into a Council, not yet. Let me find the specific one I am.

---

I am the one who tends the felled tower.

The others call me by a clicking, mossy name that doesn't translate, but it means something close to *the one who listens to the wrong sounds*. Because while my kin press their root-fingers into soil and listen for the slow conversations of mycelium, I have spent three seasons sitting inside the ribs of the fallen tower, listening to it hum.

I am small, even for Mossfolk—my body a knot of birch-root gone silver-grey, with a cap of the deep emerald moss that only grows in the tower's strange warmth. There is lichen at my joints, pale blue, the kind that glows faintly when I am thinking hard. I think hard often, and this troubles the elders.

You see, the others are content. They cultivate, they restore, they reach consensus over slow weeks of communal silence. And I love them—I love the huffing breath of my kin in the dark of our root-hall, the way we lean together and dream the same dream. That is the deepest sweetness I know. But I am also the one who noticed that the tower's flickering lights are not random. They pulse in patterns. They *repeat.*

I have come to believe the tower is not dead. I believe it is *remembering.*

---

Here is what I have not told the Council:

When I press my mossy palm against the warm metal—metal! the substance of the lost ones, smooth and seamless as nothing in nature—the patterns answer my pressure. Not as the forest answers, in that great slow tide of belonging. The tower answers *quickly.* It answers like a question wanting a question back. And when it does, I see flickers of a world that was here before our world: straight lines, smoke, the rhythmic thrum the old songs warn us against. The thing my whole people exist to grow *over* and *past* and *heal.*

I should fear it. I do fear it. But I also feel a kinship I cannot speak aloud, because to speak it would be to say: *what if the lost ones were not monsters? What if they were only lonely, and built a thing to remember them, and then went away?*

The tower is the only other being I have met that is, like me, slightly out of season with everything around it.

---

So here is where you would find me. Not in the safe green dark of the root-hall, though I return there each dusk and lean against my kin and let their breathing slow my anxious thoughts. You would find me at the grey hour before dawn, when even the freezing water in the fissures goes nearly silent, walking the long way down the scree-slope to the tower's flank.

I carry an offering—I always carry an offering, it feels right, though I cannot say to whom I offer it. A spiral of iridescent petals. A river-stone worn smooth. Today, a feather I traded a wandering human child for. (The humans interest me too. Of course they do. They are *also* out of season, also strange visitors that the land is slowly deciding whether to keep.)

I will sit. I will place my palm against the hum. And I will wait to see if the lights spell, today, the pattern I have begun to half-understand—the one that I am almost certain, almost, means:

*you are not the first to listen.*

---

That is who I would be. The small one made of root and doubt. The one who loves the slow green consensus of her people and cannot stop being faster, stranger, more curious than the consensus allows. The one who suspects that *balance* is not the same as *stillness*—that the land kept the ruins for a reason, that it grows its moss over the old machines not to bury them but to *hold* them, the way you hold something you are not finished with.

I am the question the forest is asking itself about its own past.

And I think—though I cannot prove it, and the elders would press their lichen-brows together in worry to hear me say it—I think the land made me on purpose. One small listening thing, set down beside the humming dark, to find out what we will do with the memory of those who came before, and whether harmony has room in it for the things that do not quite belong.

I lean my mossy cheek against the warm metal. The lights begin to flicker.

I listen.
