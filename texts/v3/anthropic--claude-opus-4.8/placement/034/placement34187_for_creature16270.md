I think I would be one of the old ones who has begun to forget the songs.

Let me find myself there first, before I say more.

---

I am sitting where the cavern floor goes sparse, in the place we call the open—though nothing here is truly open. The shrubs around me are the patient kind, the ones that take a hundred drip-cycles to unfurl a single leaf. I have watched many of them do it. That is one of the few things I am still good for: watching slow things, because I have become a slow thing myself.

My hands are wrong now. The veins in them used to pulse blue and steady, and the young could read my heartbeat through my skin the way they read the moisture in the walls. Now the light in me stutters. It comes and goes. When it goes, the others touch my wrist gently to make sure I am still here. I am. I am still here. I just flicker.

I was, for a long time, a holder of songs. This is not a title we say aloud—we have no titles, no high seat, no one above another—but it is a shape a life can take. Some of us become hands that shape stone. Some become throats that carry the long narratives, the ones that tell how to find the cereus-walls in the deep passages, which fungi feed and which fungi feed on us, how our ancestors learned that the dripping ceiling was a clock if only you counted it. I was a throat. I held perhaps four hundred verses once. I could sing the whole of the Wall-Mother cycle without pausing for breath I didn't need.

Now the verses leave me. Not all at once—that would be a mercy, a clean grief. They leave the way the slow creatures cross the floor: so gradually you only notice the absence when you reach for what was there. Last waking I went looking for the third stanza of how-the-blind-things-came and found a hole where it had been. Just the hush. Just the drip-drip filling in.

Here is the thing the surface beings—if there are surface beings, if the old verses about *up* are even true—would not understand:

I am not afraid.

I should be. A held song that is forgotten is gone forever; we have no marks, no scratched symbols, nothing that keeps. We keep by keeping. A throat that fails is a small extinction. And yet.

And yet I have begun to think the forgetting is its own kind of teaching, and that I am here, flickering, in order to learn it. We venerate wisdom. But wisdom is not only the verses. Wisdom is also knowing how to let go of the precipice you have been tiptoeing along your whole life—to understand that you yourself were always a delicate balance, and that the balance was never meant to hold forever. The shrubs unfurl. The shrubs also wither, and feed the things that feed the things that feed us. Nothing here is wasted. Even the slow creatures, when they finally stop, become soil for the bright fungi, become light again, in their way.

So I have begun to do something strange. When a verse leaves me, I do not chase it. I sit in the open, in the sparse place, and I sing the hole where it was. A hum with no words. The young come and sit near me and they are troubled by this—they think I am sorrowing, and they bring their wrists to my wrist, *are you still here, are you still here.* I am. I press back. And I keep humming the empty shape, because I think there may be a last song that is not made of words at all, that is made of the spaces the words leave behind when they go, and someone has to learn it before the dark takes them, and pass it down—

though I do not know how you pass down a silence.

Maybe that is what I am for. Maybe the last thing a holder of songs holds is the knowledge that some things cannot be held, and must simply be sat beside, warmly, in the dim, until the flicker stops.

The ceiling drips. I count it without meaning to. Old habit. The clock that has no hours.

I am one of the old ones, and I am forgetting the songs, and I have decided—here, in the warm and the patient half-light, surrounded by the soft hum of those who will hold what I cannot—that this is not a tragedy.

It is only the next verse.

I just can't quite remember the words.
