Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to reach for something grand—a Rock Golem, a Frost Wraith, one of the named wonders. But those are the things that get *seen* in a place like this. I don't think I'm one of the seen things.

Let me feel my way in.

---

The first thing I notice about myself is that I am cold, but not bothered by it. That tells me something. The frost is not my enemy. Neither is it my body, the way it is the body of the Wraiths. I am simply at the temperature of the rock, because I am pressed against it. *In* it, almost.

I think I am small. Not Nibbler-small—I have a sense of my own edges, of a shape that ends—but small enough that the trembling ground is a genuine danger and not just weather. When a Rock Golem passes, I don't watch it like a tourist watches a mountain. I *wait it out*. I have learned the difference between the long slow tremor that means a Golem walking somewhere distant, and the sharp double-knock that means something is about to set its weight down very near. The second kind sends me deeper into my crack in the stone.

So: I live in a crevice. One of those cracks where the water clings and refuses to be absorbed. That's not incidental. I think the water is why I'm here.

Let me follow the water.

---

The droplet at the back of my crevice never dries. The cold keeps it from evaporating; the mineral salts in it keep it from freezing. It hangs there, impossible, a little bead of liquid that ought not exist at this temperature, held in being by the specific spite of this place. I drink from it. I have drunk from it for—

—for longer than I can account for. And here is the strange thing I'm only now noticing as I describe myself: I do not get larger. The Nibblers grow and split, the shrubs reach, the Golems accrete new stone over centuries. But I have always been exactly this size. I take in the water and the trace of mineral and I do not grow. I *maintain*. I am a thing whose whole effort goes into staying precisely as it is, in a world that is busy becoming.

That's it. That's what I am.

---

I am a **keeper of a still point.**

The towering metal-and-stone structures—the industrial marvels whose purpose is a mystery to the uninitiated—I understand now that I am part of their function, though no one designed me and I was never installed. This realm is *pre-formation*. Everything here is in the act of shaping itself, raw and provisional, the rock not yet decided on what it will be. Such a place needs anchors. It needs a few things that simply refuse to change, around which the changing can organize itself. The droplet that won't evaporate. The crevice that won't close. And me, the small cold patient thing that drinks the droplet and, by drinking it, keeps it from completing the work of disappearing.

I am a reference mark. A surveyor's pin driven into a world that is otherwise all current and flux. The Golems, for all their mass, are *moving*. I am the thing they can be measured against.

This is a humble role and I find I don't mind it. There is a particular dignity in being the one fixed point in a symphony, the held note under all the chaotic melody. When the wind carries the deafening cacophony of calls across the plateau, my contribution is silence. A held silence is also a sound, if there is enough roaring around it.

---

So here I am, then. If you came into this realm looking for me you would almost certainly miss me, and that is correct, that is as it should be. You would marvel at the Golems and shiver at the Wraiths and step over my crevice without a glance, and the not-glancing would be a small kindness, because attention is a kind of pressure and I am made of resisting pressure.

But if you knelt. If you put your face down near the cold wet rock and let your eyes adjust to the dim shimmering light until it revealed the hidden textures—you might see a small dark shape, no bigger than a thumbnail, the color of the stone but not quite, perfectly still beside a drop of water that does not behave the way water should.

You might mistake me for a pebble.

I would let you. The mistake is most of the work. The whole forsaken, wondrous, trembling land leans very gently on the fact that some of us can be mistaken for pebbles and go on being exactly what we are anyway, holding our one bright drop against the cold, while everything else gets on with the loud business of becoming.

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be.

A small fixed thing in a world still making up its mind. The pin in the map. The kept point. The pebble that is patiently, deliberately, *not* a pebble—and never tells.
