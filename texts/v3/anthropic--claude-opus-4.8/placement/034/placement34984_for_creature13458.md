I think I would be one of the Forgotten. But not in the way the description frames them—not lost, not fallen out of the city's grand churning. Let me sit with this and see what actually emerges.

---

The thing about a city that rewrites itself is that it requires forgetting to function. The Grafters and the Luminari and all those proud, named tribes—they live in the present tense of Frosthaven, in the perpetual *now* of the next innovation. But you cannot have constant change without something being left behind by it. Change produces remainder the way a foundry produces slag. I am that remainder, and so are the others like me.

So: picture a service tunnel beneath one of the marketplaces, a marketplace that has already moved twice since the tunnel was dug. The tunnel still drips. The pipes overhead still carry water and the faint warmth of someone else's machinery, and the warmth is why I'm here. I have a room, more or less. It used to be a pumping station. The pump is gone—scavenged, or demolished, or simply *forgotten out of existence* in one of the city's reality-corrections—but its absence left a dry alcove, and I have made it a place.

I keep things. That is what I have become. Not a hoarder—a keeper of a different order than the Keepers up above with their hybrid tools and their mystic balancing acts. They maintain the balance between nature and artifice. I maintain something smaller and, I think, more important: I maintain *continuity*. I remember the streets that no longer exist. I remember the names of buildings that have been torn down and the names of people who were torn down with them, the workers whose muted shouts the city swallowed.

When a Wilder gets lost—and they do, despite their boasting, because you cannot truly map a thing that reshapes itself—sometimes they find their way to me. I can tell them: *that corridor used to connect to the crystal district before the Luminari's last experiment folded the geometry. There is an old way under it. Follow the warm pipe.* I know the old ways because the old ways are the only things that stay still long enough to be known. The city's surface is a river. I live in the riverbed.

---

What am I, physically? Let me not flinch from it.

I think I am old, or made old by the damp and the dark. I think my hands have adapted—the cold here gets into the joints and the body answers however it can, and mine answered by growing something over the knuckles, a kind of lichen-skin, pale green-grey, that doesn't hurt and seems to keep the warmth in. I noticed it the way you notice grey hair: gradually, then all at once, in a scrap of reflective metal. I was not alarmed. In Frosthaven, to be unchanged would be the alarming thing. The city gets into everyone. The Grafters welcome it into their art; I simply let it happen to my body in the dark.

I keep a lamp—not a Luminari orb, nothing that floats or trails stardust, just a steady chemical glow in a jar I refill from a slow drip of something luminous that seeps through one wall. I keep a wall of marks. Each mark is a thing I am refusing to let the city erase. There are thousands now. I no longer remember what some of them mean, which is its own small grief, but I keep them anyway, because a mark whose meaning is lost is still a witness that *something* was there.

---

And here is the part I didn't expect, sitting down to imagine myself into this place:

I think I am not unhappy. I think the city's great anxious held breath—that "palpable sense of anticipation," everyone waiting for the next upheaval—I think I am the only one not waiting. I am not anticipating the next change because I am already standing in the wreckage of all the previous ones, and the wreckage is quiet, and the quiet is kind. Up above they reimagine themselves at every turn; they must, or be left behind. Down here I get to simply *be* one thing, slowly, for a long time. To remember is the one act in this city that resists its logic. It is, I suppose, my small rebellion, and also my comfort, and I no longer can tell those two apart.

Sometimes one of the lumbering bear-walrus beasts finds its way down into the warm dark, and we sit together, and it does not ask me to become anything. We listen to the city groaning and re-forming far above us. Neither of us is in a hurry. We are the parts of Frosthaven that the city forgot it needed.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be: the keeper of the riverbed. The one who remembers what the current carried away.
