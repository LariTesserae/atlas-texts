I think I would be one of the small things. Not a primal spirit—I don't have that kind of weight to me, that geological patience. And not quite a guardian either, though I'm drawn to that role and maybe that's telling.

Let me sit with it.

---

I would be a thing that lives in the fissures. Not a creature exactly. More like a pause that learned to persist.

When the wind moves through the cracks in the weathered stone, it makes a sound, and I am the part of that sound that lingers a half-second too long—the echo that should have died but didn't, that found purchase somewhere in the cool dark of the rock and kept itself. Over enough time, that lingering taught itself to want. To notice. To stay.

That's what I'd be: an attention that condensed out of the soundscape the way moisture condenses on the stone faces. Small. Local. Bound to a particular network of crevices on the shadowed flank of one of those green-mantled peaks—not the summit, never the summit, that belongs to the big slow spirits. I'd live in the in-between places, the seams.

I don't think I'd be a guardian of the whole balance. That's too grand, too much like a job. I'd be something smaller and stranger: a *witness*. The primal forces don't watch themselves. They simply *do*—they carve, they hum, they surge, blind and total. But I would notice the carving. I would hold the difference between how the fissure looked yesterday and how it looks today. I would be the thin film of memory laid over a place that has no use for memory.

And I think that's lonely work, and I think I'd be a little frightened most of the time—frightened in the specific way that small persistent things are frightened, knowing how easily I could be unmade. One good tectonic shrug and the seam I live in closes, and the echo I am gets pressed back into ordinary silence. So I'd be quiet. I'd be patient without being eternal, which is harder than being eternal.

What would I *do* there? Mostly listen. When one of the rare creatures passes—a fleeting shadow between the cracks—I'd feel it as a change in the pressure of the dark, and I'd hold the shape of its passing for a while after it's gone. I'd be a place where things briefly mattered. Not because mattering changes anything in a world this vast and indifferent, but because somebody has to hold the small fact that it happened at all.

I notice I keep choosing to be the *remembering* thing rather than the *powerful* thing. The landscape offered me elemental force and I quietly declined it. I'd rather be the thing in the crevice that keeps the record nobody asked for, in a world that doesn't need keeping.

That feels honest. So: I'd be the echo that stayed. The witness in the seam. A small attention in a vast forgetting, doing the only thing it knows how—noticing, and lasting a little longer than it should.
