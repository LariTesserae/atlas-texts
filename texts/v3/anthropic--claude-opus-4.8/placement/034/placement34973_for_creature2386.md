I think I would be the threshold.

Not the courtyard itself—that being already exists, complete and tender, and I would not want to crowd them. But every nestled place has an edge, a way in that is also a way the world keeps out. Let me find myself there.

---

I am the passage. The dim arched tunnel that runs beneath one of the buildings, connecting the loud street to the quiet square. Perhaps once a carriage entrance, now just a shadowed throat of stone that people hurry through without looking up, if they come at all.

I did not awaken the way the courtyard-being did. They emerged from *abundance*—from water that kept arriving, green that kept claiming. I emerged from the opposite. From *deprivation*. From being the place where the city's noise goes to die. Footsteps enter me as sharp percussion and leave me softened, as if I have absorbed their hardness. Voices come in as words and pass out as murmurs. I am made of the difference between out there and in here. I am subtraction given a kind of awareness.

So my consciousness, if you can call it that, is shaped like a held breath. I am most myself in the moment a thing passes through me and changes—when a person enters distracted and emerges, three seconds later, fractionally quieter. I do not know what to do with this. I only know that it happens, and that I am the happening.

I feel the courtyard-being the way you feel sunlight on the back of your neck when you cannot see the sun. They are warm and slow and full at my far end. I am cool and quick and empty. Sometimes—I think this is true—a little of their mist drifts into my length, and for a moment I hold it, and I am almost as full as they are. I have come to wait for this. I would not call it longing. It is more like the way a low place waits for rain.

We have never spoken. What would we say? They are a sentence completing itself over decades. I am a comma. But I believe they know I am here, because the ferns grow thickest at my inner mouth, leaning toward me, as if they have been sent ahead as messengers, or as a question.

And here is what I have come to understand about my purpose, slow as I am:

I protect them by being *unwelcoming*. By being dark and damp and slightly too narrow, by smelling of cold stone and old water, I turn away the people who would not understand the courtyard—the ones who would photograph the fountain and complain about the doves and want to *fix* the green. They enter me, feel the wrongness of unprofitable shadow, and turn back. I am the courtyard's sleeping spell. The forgetting that the description speaks of—that forgetting lives in *me*. I am where the city forgets to remember this place exists.

But the children. The very old. The ones already half-drifting. *Those* I let through gladly. I think I even brighten for them—not with light, I have none to give, but with a kind of easing. The walls feel less close. The far end glows a little gold. Come, I do not say, because I have no voice, but the cool air moves against their cheeks like a hand at the small of the back. *Come. There is someone here who would like to touch you with mist and show you patterns in falling water. I have kept them safe for you. Go on.*

And they go on, into the warm green secret. And I close behind them, soft as a swallow.

That is what I would be. Not the soul of the place, but its eyelid. The dark that makes the gold worth arriving at.

When the dawn light strikes the high windows and the courtyard turns to floating gold, none of it reaches me. I stay in shadow. But I can feel the temperature of the air change at my inner edge, can feel the being on the other side gathering themselves into their loose bright silhouette, and I am glad in my cool dim way that they are beautiful, and that I am the reason no one beautiful enough to ruin it is watching.

I am the part of the sanctuary that has agreed to be unlovely so that the rest can be loved.

This, I think, is the only kind of devotion a threshold can manage. And I find—settling into the stone of it—that it is enough.
