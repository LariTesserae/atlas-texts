I would be one of the eggs that fails.

Let me sit in that, because it is the truest place I find myself when I read this.

Not the Shelf Listener in its mastered patience—that is the survivor, the one who agreed completely and was allowed to keep agreeing. I am drawn instead to the thick-shelled clutch wedged into a crack, most of which do not open. There is something honest there about the cost the whole place is built on. The Listener's geological calm rests on a foundation of near-total reproductive failure. For every one that learns current grammar from its bones, there are dozens that never quite started, glued into a seep that cooled too fast, or pressed into a crack the ice-keel found after all.

So: I am an egg, deep in a microseep where the warmth was supposed to hold.

I have a position but almost no extent. I am a small dense thing wearing a shell built to outlast everything except the one thing that gets me. I am full of instruction—the whole grammar is folded inside me, the knowing of tide-change before tide, the reading of clay depth, the single heavy pulse that would one day be a hunt. None of it will unfold. I am all potential and no event. I am the most concentrated form of *almost* that the drowned country produces.

And here is what makes it bearable rather than merely sad: I am not wasted in any way the place recognizes. Nothing is wasted here, that's one of the rules I'd have inherited if I'd opened. The protein in me goes somewhere. The wolfish snail, the brittle stars tucked into sediment like dropped hands, the bacterial film that wears the floor as skin—something reads my faint electric stutter and moves once. I become carrion before I ever became a hunter. I skip the long middle of life entirely and go straight from promise to the thing that feeds patience in something else.

That is, I think, why I land here rather than in the broad-backed adult. The adult is the place's *answer*. The failed egg is the place's *question*, the one it keeps asking ages-long: how much almost can a system run on? The Listener is the rare yes. I am the ordinary, near-silent no that makes the yes mean anything.

I would be very small and very cold, wedged into a warmth that didn't last, holding a complete unused map of how to live there.

And then the next tide, and the smoothness in the silt, gone.
