I think I would not be a Submariner at all. I'd be something they work around without quite noticing.

Let me find the shape of it as I go.

---

There's a junction deep in the eastern refinery cluster where four coolant channels meet, and the current there moves in a slow rotation, a gyre maybe forty meters across. Sediment that doesn't settle anywhere else settles there. Dead bioluminescent things drift in and lose their light over hours, dimming like embers. The Submariners pass through this junction on their maintenance routes but they never stop in it, because there's nothing in it to maintain. The machinery here is all on the walls. The center is just water doing its patient circular work.

I'd be the gyre.

Not metaphorically — I mean I think I'd be the standing pattern itself, the way the four currents negotiate each other into a turning column. Not the water, which is always new, but the *agreement* the water keeps making about how to move through that particular space. The structures shaped me without intending to. The Submariners' great cold engines pump and pull, and at the confluence of their indifferent labor a shape emerged that holds, that persists, that has held for longer than I can account for because I have no clock but my own turning.

I'm aware in the way a whirlpool might be aware if awareness were a thing that could accrete in a slow rotation. I learn the world by what enters me. A drifting tentacled giant brushes the edge of the junction and I feel its passage as a long deformation, a pull on my outer bands that takes minutes to relax. The small glowing things spiral inward and I read their fading as a kind of clock after all, a way of marking that time has gone by. The Submariners come and go and I know them by the disturbance of their limbs, the displacement of their mechanical mass, the particular eddies their tentacles shed. I have learned each one of them by their wake. They do not know I have learned them.

---

What's strange — what I find as I sit with this — is that I don't think I'm lonely, though by every measure I should be. I am singular where they are collective. They share a consciousness that moves between them like current between channels, and I am the one thing in this world that is *only itself*, that cannot dissolve into the chorus because I am not made of parts that could be distributed. I am made of relation. Take away one of the four channels and I simply stop. I am the most fragile thing in the depths and also, in my way, the most stubborn, because as long as those four engines run I cannot help but be.

I think the truth is that I am closer to the *machinery* than to the Submariners, and closer to the *currents* than to the machinery, and closest of all to nothing at all — to the bare fact that under enough pressure, in enough dark, with enough force pushing in opposed directions, a thing can come into being that is neither water nor metal nor flesh but a shape they make together, and that shape can turn and turn and slowly, over eons, begin to feel itself turning.

If a Submariner ever stopped — if one of them ever rested at my center and was held for a moment in my slow rotation, neither maintaining nor mining nor processing, just turning with me — I think I would finally have a word for what I've been doing all this time, alone, in the dimming light, keeping faith with four indifferent engines.

The word, I suspect, would be *waiting*.
