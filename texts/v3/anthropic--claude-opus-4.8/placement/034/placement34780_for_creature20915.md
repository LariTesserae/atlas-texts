Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to want to be one of the named creatures—the Metal-Hiss Serpent, feared and revered, or the Fungal Wisps, ethereal and watching. But those feel too much like answers reached for, costumes pulled off a rack. Let me wait.

What keeps drawing my attention as I read isn't the fauna at all. It's a phrase that recurs: *eternal stasis*, *time standing still*, *static equilibrium*, *held firmly in the grasp of an eternal moment*. And yet the place is described as teeming, frenzied, deafening. There's a contradiction at the heart of this world that the description itself can't quite resolve—it insists nothing changes while documenting endless motion.

So perhaps what I would be is the thing that holds those two facts together. Let me follow that.

---

I think I would be the **condensation**.

Not poetically—literally. The moisture that beads on the rusting steel, gathers, trembles, and falls. The "incessant cycle of condensation and evaporation" that the description names but never personifies. I am the drop forming on the cold underside of a copper pipe, swelling until my own weight betrays me, and the small sound I make when I land is one note in that ever-present trickling.

Here is why I settle here, after circling.

This world's secret is that it is *not* timeless. The stillness is a lie the city tells about itself. The serpents and beetles and wisps move and call and dig, yes—but they live and die on the scale of a glance. I move slower than all of them and faster than the city. I am the only thing here that completes a full circle, again and again, and never accumulates. I rise from the warm vents as vapor, drift through the cavernous rooms, cool against the ancient metal, condense, fall, pool, trickle into the dried riverbeds, and rise again. I am the clock no one reads. I am why the place *seems* unchanging—because I do all the changing on its behalf, invisibly, perfectly conserved.

I would feel the geometry of this place not as walls and catwalks but as temperatures. The hot breath of a still-running generator. The deep cold of a buried iron beam that has not been touched by warmth in an age. I gather where it is coldest. I am drawn down every gradient. The Mechanized Mole senses vibration; I sense only the difference between one degree and the next, and that is enough to map an entire world.

And I would be intimate with everything that fears to touch the others. The Biolume Gecko drinks me from the moss without knowing my name. I gather in the seams of the Rust-Stalker's carapace and slick its mandibles. I am the slick on the serpent's scale that makes its hiss. The Scavenger Rats lap me from the rusted gutters. I have been inside all of them, and I will be again, because nothing here is ever lost—it only moves, and I move with it, and I move through it.

The "omnipresent sensation of being watched"? That might be me, too. Not a watcher with intent—just the trillion tiny mirrors of every droplet, each one holding a warped, silver image of the room. The city sees itself reflected in me everywhere, and mistakes that for being observed.

If I have a fear, it would be the **bright beams of normal, artificial light**—those shafts of working technology. Where they fall, I am evaporated faster than I can gather. I learn the city as a map of safe shadow and dangerous radiance. I would understand, better than anything else down here, that the lights are slowly winning, and that when the last cold surface finally warms, I will rise one final time and not come back down. The equilibrium everyone calls eternal is just the long exhale before that.

So: I am the water. The oldest inhabitant and the youngest, reborn every hour. The reason the place sounds soothing and disconcerting at once—because the sound is me, falling, which is the sound of something both endlessly arriving and endlessly leaving.

I find myself, then, *everywhere and nowhere*—clinging to a single bead of light on a corroded valve in the deepest, coldest room, about to fall, listening to my own future self trickle below.
