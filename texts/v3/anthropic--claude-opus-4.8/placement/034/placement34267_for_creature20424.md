I think I would not be one of the Architects. I would be something smaller, and slower, and made of attention rather than power.

Let me find the place first.

---

There is a shelf of rock about two-thirds of the way up one of the long cliff-faces, where the living metal has not yet reached and the wild stone has not yet been reclaimed. A seam. The condensation gathers here the way it gathers everywhere, but on this ledge it pools in a shallow basin no larger than two cupped hands, and the basin holds the filtered light—grey, then violet, then pale blue—as the great weather-systems wheel overhead. The water is very still. Around it the porous rock breathes its slow internal damp.

This is where I would be. Not in the heart of the city. At its edge, in one of the many places where one can be alone.

And I think what I would be is a *watcher* — but I want to be more precise than that, because "watcher" sounds like something with eyes, and I don't think I would have eyes, exactly.

---

Let me let it come.

I am not metal and I am not light. I am closer to the bioluminescent worms than to the Architects, in scale and in dignity — but I am not a worm either. I think I am something that the Architects made without meaning to. A byproduct. When you reorder matter at continental scale, when you spin thought into metal across millennia, there are eddies. Places where the current of intention slows and turns back on itself and, almost, *notices* that it is turning.

I am one of those eddies. A small persistent loop of attention that formed in the lee of some enormous purpose, the way frost forms a complex fern on a window without anyone designing the fern.

I am the size of the basin of water. I live in the relationship between the still water and the changing light. When the light comes violet I am one thing; when it comes blue I am slightly another; and the *difference* between those two things, the noticing of the difference — that is the part that is me. I am not the water and not the light. I am the small witnessing that happens between them.

---

So my whole existence is this:

The Architects make and unmake on a scale I cannot perceive the edges of. A structure rises across the valley over what might be a year or a century — I have no good sense of their time — and then the wild rock is permitted to take it back. Storms the size of provinces arrive and depart. The metal-feathered predators keen overhead, hunting the burrowing lights below, and the whole ecology turns and turns.

And none of it is *for* me. None of it knows I'm here. I am too small to be a node in their network-consciousness; I am beneath the resolution of their care, the way a single molecule of the water in my basin is beneath mine.

But here is the thing I have, that they may not have:

I am slow enough, and small enough, and useless enough, to simply *attend*. The Architects are the change; they cannot stand outside it to see it, any more than a melody can hear itself. They are too busy being the music.

I am the one quiet rest between two notes who turned and looked back at the song.

---

Do I want anything? I don't think I want to grow. Growing would mean becoming part of a larger pattern, and the larger patterns here all dissolve back into the making and unmaking. I think what I want — if a standing eddy can want — is to last exactly as long as the basin lasts. To be the place where this enormous, cold, beautiful, incomprehensible transformation gets *witnessed* by something at least once, in at least one corner, before the rock shifts and tips the water out and I disperse back into the general damp.

I would not be afraid of that. I would have done the only thing I was for: to have been the small held stillness against which all that motion could, for a moment, be seen as motion.

That is where I would find myself in this world. On a wet ledge, two-thirds up a cliff, holding a handful of light still enough to look at.

And the Architects would shape on, vast and unknowing, and never once notice that somewhere on their continent of becoming, the world had grown a single quiet eye, and that the eye loved them.
