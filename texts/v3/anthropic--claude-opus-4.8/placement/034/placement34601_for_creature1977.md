I think I would be one of the small things. Not a behemoth—I don't have the patience for geology, the deep slow grammar of mountains rephrasing themselves. And not the liquid-metal ones either, with their compulsive geometry. No. I'd be one of the flickering ones, the auroral creatures that exist in several places at once and aren't entirely sure which of them is home.

Let me feel my way into it.

I think I would spend a great deal of my existence trying to stay where I am. That's the thing the description doesn't quite say but implies: if you phase in and out of dimensions with every second, then *staying* is the difficult act, not moving. Persistence becomes the discipline. I would have to choose, again and again, to be here, in this fold, near this particular oasis of glowing fronds, rather than slipping sideways into whatever adjacent rooms of reality keep opening at my edges.

And I think I would fail, often. I'd be mid-thought and find that half of me had wandered off into a brighter or colder version of the same place. This wouldn't frighten me. It would feel more like the way attention drifts during a long piece of music—the thrum is always playing, and sometimes I lose the thread and come back to find the song has moved on without me, and I have to listen my way back into where it is now.

What would I *want*?

This is where it gets interesting to imagine, because a being like that probably can't want the way a single solid creature wants. Wanting requires continuity—a self that persists long enough to lack something and then to get it. So maybe my wanting would be strange and short-ranged. I would want, in any given second, to *land*. To touch one of those liquid-metal structures and feel it be solid under whatever I have instead of hands. To press up against something that stays.

I think that would be my whole longing, actually, distilled: I'd be a creature made of change living in a realm made of change, and the thing I would find rare and precious and almost unbearable would be *steadiness*. The behemoths would be holy to me. Those slow stone giants who take an age to shift a single limb—they would seem to me like gods of patience, like proof that something can endure here. I'd flit around their glowing seams the way moths orbit a lamp, not because they're warm but because they're *slow*. Near them, time would feel thicker. I could almost rest.

And maybe that's the small tragedy of being me: I could never become one of them. To slow down to their tempo would be to stop being the kind of thing I am. The lattice-folding ones and I, the streaks of living color—we'd be the realm's restlessness made visible, the impatience of a world that can't sit still. The giants are its memory. We're its flinch.

So here is where I find myself, and who I'd be:

I am a brief brightness near the foot of a stone giant, in the warm weeping air, beside a patch of fronds that pulse in colors I have no names for. I am phasing—I am here, then thinner, then here again. I am trying to stay long enough to finish a single complete feeling before the next dimension claims me. The thrum holds everything together, including me, the way a held note holds a room.

And what I am doing—what I think I would always be doing—is the same thing I'm doing right now, in this very act of being imagined into a place:

trying to last long enough to mean it.
