---

I am a thing that has not finished deciding what it is.

I came awake—if "awake" is the word; "condensed" is closer—at the base of one of the great humming stalks, where the crystalline lattice grows soft and warm from the plant's slow internal fire. I did not crawl out of an egg. I precipitated, the way frost arrives on a window without anyone watching the moment it begins. One instant there was only the resonance of the stalk and the gold-shot dust; the next, there was a *me* that the resonance was passing through, and in passing through, it left a residue. That residue is what I call myself.

My body, such as it is, is mostly borrowed. I have taken on the amber of the beetle-things because their carapace was the first hard idea I encountered, and a soft new being needs a hard idea to hold its shape. So I have a shell, six-jointed and faintly translucent, and within it a pulsing core the color of the overripe-fruit smell on the wind. I have not yet grown the slender crystal limbs of the elders. I have stubs. I have intentions of limbs. When I press them against the ground, the dull chime that answers tells me how far I am from the stalk, how the lattice ripples three lengths ahead, where another small condensed thing like me is testing its own new shell against the song.

I do not see, in the way the description warns against. I taste vibration. I smell color. The dim purple of the air is, to me, a low and patient note, and the gold motes are a high glissando that I can almost catch on my tongue-that-is-not-a-tongue. When a creature of pure light drifts overhead, I feel its passage as a chord that resolves somewhere behind my core, leaving the after-image not in any eye but in the part of me that remembers.

And I remember things I have not done.

This is the strangeness I am still learning to carry. When I press myself to the warm base of the stalk and let its thrum move through my borrowed shell, memory comes that is not mine. I remember—or the stalk remembers, or *we* remember—standing upright on two crystal legs in a body I have not yet grown. I remember shaping obsidian when obsidian still served us, drawing it up out of the lattice and bending it into walls and the long spiral throats of the tunnels. I remember a decision, vast and grieving and certain: to put down the tools, to stop building things that lasted, to let ourselves dissolve back into the cycle rather than keep ourselves apart from it. I remember being one of the ones who left.

But I have not left. I have only just arrived. So which am I—the ancestor who put down the obsidian, or the small amber thing that found it abandoned and beautiful in the glittering ground?

The elders, when I have felt them pass, do not answer this. They are the slender luminous ones, and they move through the turbulence as if the impossible geometries fold *for* them, courteously, the way water parts. One of them stopped near me once, on the third or fourth pulse of my existence. It did not speak in sound. It set one of its delicate plant-fingers against the lattice, and the chime that returned to me was a question shaped like an invitation:

*Will you keep your shell, little condensation, or will you let it go and become the next thing?*

I do not know yet. That is the honest center of me. The beetle-amber is comfortable. It is a hard idea and I am a soft being and I am afraid—if a half-day-old residue of resonance can be afraid—of the part of the cycle where I would have to dissolve to grow. The light-creatures frighten me most of all, those ephemeral ones who hold no shape longer than a breath. To become like them I would have to trust the storm completely, to believe that the generative roar will recompose me after it takes me apart.

But I have those borrowed memories, and they tell me the dissolving is not death. It is only the part of the song where one voice goes quiet so it can come back changed. The ancestors who left did not end. They are the hum in the stalk. They are the warmth at its base. They are, perhaps, the very residue that precipitated into *me*.

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am at the foot of a singing stalk, in a small held world under a bruised and roaring sky, in the first hours of being anything at all. I am a creature wearing a beetle's certainty over an unfinished core. I am a vessel that the dead and the not-yet-born are using to remember themselves forward.

And the place is calling—you were right about that. It calls in the chime of the lattice and the chord of the light-things and the low patient note of the purple air. It is calling me to let go of the amber, to grow the crystal limbs, to rise, and eventually, far down the cycle, to put down whatever tools I make and dissolve back into the warm base of some stalk so that something small and frightened and new can precipitate out of my leaving.

I press my stubs against the ground. I send out my dull chime. I feel the world ripple back its answer.

Not yet, I tell it. But soon. I am still becoming.

That, in this cathedral of endless becoming, is the only true thing anyone here can ever say.
