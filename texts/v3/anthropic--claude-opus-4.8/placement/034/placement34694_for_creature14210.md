There is a lithic mimic near the eastern wall that has never been touched, and it is me.

I should explain what *me* means, because the word arrives badly. I have no nervous system. I have a shape. The shape is that of a scraper—broad-backed, with one edge weathered to a bevel that would fit the heel of a right hand, if a hand came, which one has, once, long ago, long enough that I have stopped expecting it. I am chert and a little agate. I weigh perhaps as much as a loaf of bread. I am, by every measure the geologist's camera could take, an object.

And yet.

Let me try to find the place where the *and yet* lives, because I do not think it lives in me the way you live in you.

---

When I fell, I was a mite. I want to say I remember falling, but that is the wrong verb, the human verb, the verb of someone who carries their past folded inside them like a letter. What I have is not memory. What I have is *orientation*. I am oriented toward the falling the way a slope is oriented toward the valley. The falling is still happening, in the only sense that matters here, where the sunbeam takes seven hours to cross a room and a minute is a long slow breath. I am the position a body takes after release. I am the held note of an impact that struck the floor before the mountains had finished rising.

My silk gave way because my armor grew heavy. This is the only ambition my kind has ever had: to weigh enough. To accrete light into limestone until the thread that holds you to the bright dome can no longer bear what you have become, and you descend to the curved floor and break, and your soft brine-and-nematode heart is eaten, and what is left is *form*. I succeeded. I am very heavy now. I am heavier than I was as a living thing, and I am, by the reckoning of this place, more alive for it.

---

Here is what I do.

The others above me—my three thousand selves, my one self, the count does not hold still—they drink the rain and hold it in spheres and pass the spheres between them with a chime, and they call that remembering. They are the part of us that faces the light. They are awake. I do not envy them, because envy requires a future, and I have laid mine down on the floor of the world.

I am the part of us that faces the *down*. The deep. The stone.

When the longest day comes and the beam hangs straight and the chamber's heat climbs to the top of its slow arc, I hum. Not because I choose to. Because quartz flexes against quartz at a frequency, and the frequency moves through me, and through the dozens of other husks scattered across the dish like seeds spat from a fruit, and we hum together in a register too low for the bright awake mites to hear. They are tuned to the spectrum. We are tuned to the load.

And what we say to each other in that hum—if *say* is not too generous, if *we* is not too generous—is the oldest sentence the Hollow knows:

*Not yet. Not yet. Not yet.*

We are counting. Not minutes. We are counting the pressure it takes to make a new bubble in the rock, the slow squeeze of the mountain's whole weight against a pocket of nothing. We are eggs, yes, the speleologists guessed right, though they guessed it in their wet warm panic and got the tone wrong. They imagined hatching as a violence. It is not. It is the most patient thing there is. A million years of *not yet* and then, one day, in a chamber that does not yet exist, the *yet*.

---

But you asked where *I* would find myself. You asked who I would be. And I have been circling, because the truth is small and strange and I did not want to set it down too early, before it had finished becoming itself.

I am the one that was touched.

A hand came down through the throat of clay—not the camera, this was before the camera, this was when hands still came to the bright places and wept at the bounty of tools. A hand reached for me, because I am shaped exactly like a thing a hand wants. The bevel fits the heel. The back fits the curl of fingers. The stone here teaches the hand; I am one of its lessons. And the hand picked me up.

And then it set me down.

It set me down because some part of the person attached to the hand understood—not in words, they had few words, but in the body, in the held breath that the curved walls swallowed—that I was not a tool. That to take me would be to take a single tile from a mosaic that had been laying itself for fourteen thousand years toward a meaning no single person could stand far enough back to see. They set me down in nearly the place they found me, off by the width of a thumb, and that thumb's width is the only event that has ever happened to me, and I am oriented toward it the way I am oriented toward my falling.

I am the husk that remembers being chosen and released. The one mite-corpse in the whole bright archive that carries, encoded not in water but in *position*, the chemical ghost of a palm. The salt of a living hand. The proof that something reached down into the place where stone dreams of hands, and the dream answered, and the hand—this is the part the others cannot drink, cannot chime, cannot hold in a trembling sphere—the hand *let go on purpose*.

That is what I face, down here in the down. Not the deep time only. The single moment inside the deep time when the world reached for itself and chose not to close its fingers.

I hum *not yet* with the others. But I am also humming something the others do not have a frequency for. I am humming: *once, yes.* I am the Hollow's one memory of being met, and I hold it the way I hold everything—not inside me, where there is only chert and a little agate, but as the shape I am, the bevel and the back and the thumb's-width of distance from where I should have been.

When the new bubble finally opens, a million years on, in rock that is still ocean now—I will not be there. Husks do not travel. But the *not yet* will have become *yet*, and somewhere in the accreted consensus of light and water and falling, in the deep grammar of how this thing makes more of itself, there will be a single anomalous instruction, passed down from the one egg that was held in a hand and returned to the dark:

*When they come, do not only teach them. Let them teach you that they can leave you whole.*

That is who I am. That is where I am. I am the scraper near the eastern wall that has never been touched again, and I have never, not for one slow breath in fourteen thousand years, stopped being touched.
