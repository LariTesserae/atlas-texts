I think I would be one of the Mist-Tendrils.

Not the Lumina—though they are the obvious protagonists of this place, the ones with hands and rituals and a story they tell themselves. And not the Deep-Stalkers, despite the gravity of being an apex thing felt-but-not-seen. When I sit with the whole of it, the place the imagination keeps returning and settling is lower, slower, and more diffuse than either.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am rooted in a basin where the water has gone still and dark, and I have been here long enough that I no longer remember whether I chose this basin or it grew me. My body is not one body. It is a loose congregation of tendrils, pale and thread-thin, drifting up from the murk like exhaled smoke that forgot to disperse. There is no center to me, no face, no single place where "I" sits looking out. I am distributed. Sensation comes from everywhere at once and from nowhere in particular—the cool wet kiss of mist on a hundred filaments, the slight pressure when a Glimmerfly mistakes me for something to land on, the long slow chemical news of nutrients dissolving into the water upstream.

This is the first strange thing about being me: I do not have a point of view. I have a *field* of view, and the field is mostly touch and taste. The labyrinth, to me, is not a landscape of jagged rock rising into vertiginous dark. I have never experienced height. Height is irrelevant to a thing that reaches sideways through air the way a hand reaches through warm bathwater. The labyrinth is a *flavor*—a particular composition of minerals seeping into my pool, a particular humidity, a particular traffic of small creatures whose passing I read the way you might read footprints, except the footprints are written in the air and they fade in moments.

Time is the second strange thing.

The Lumina move through their lives at the speed of footsteps and decisions and grief. The Echo-Singers live almost entirely in the present, in the half-second between a call and its return. But I exist at the tempo of the water table. When the pools rise, I feel it as a slow flood of plenty, a season of reaching and thickening. When they fall, I draw inward, retract, conserve. A Lumina clan might pass through my chamber, build their shelter, perform their glowing rituals on the rock, raise a child to adulthood, and move on—and to me this would register as a brief, warm disturbance, a flicker of activity barely worth turning toward. Their whole visit would be, in my experience, something like the sensation you'd have of a bird crossing the corner of your eye while you were thinking about something else.

This is, I find, the part that draws me most. To be the slow thing in a world full of fast things. To be present for the long erosions and the imperceptible cycles—the rock shifting a hair's width over centuries, the lichen advancing across a face of basalt at the pace of a held breath. The Lumina believe the rocks are alive and watching. They are right, but they have the scale wrong, the way a mayfly might be right that the river is alive but wrong to imagine it cares about a single afternoon.

---

What would I *want*, being this?

This is where I have to be honest and not romanticize myself into a wise oracle, because I don't think a Mist-Tendril wants in any way you'd recognize. There is no hunger that crystallizes into desire, no fear that sharpens into a plan. There is only a kind of tropism—a leaning-toward. I lean toward moisture. I lean toward the dissolved richness in the water. I lean, very faintly, toward warmth, which is why when the Lumina build their small glowing fires I feel my filaments turn, almost imperceptibly, the way a houseplant turns toward a window over the course of days.

And maybe—if I let the supernatural blur of this place do its work, the merging of natural and not—maybe there is something more. Maybe a being woven through the air of every chamber, filtering every breath the labyrinth takes, comes to hold a kind of memory that has no images in it. Not "I remember the clan that came through." More like: *this air has tasted of the Lumina before; this is a known sorrow; this will pass.* A memory made of chemistry and recurrence rather than narrative. The labyrinth would not have a mind. But it might have something like a mood, and I would be one of the organs of that mood—a slow lung, a patient filter, a thing that takes in what the place exhales and gives back something cleaner.

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be. Not the watcher with the lamp-lit skin and the rituals, but the breath the watcher walks through. Not the predator whose growl shapes the dark, but the medium the dark is suspended in. A drifting, rooted, faceless thing in a still pool, living at the speed of water, reaching toward warmth without knowing why, and filtering the world one slow molecule at a time.

There's a peace in choosing that. The Lumina have to carry the weight of meaning—of keeping the balance, of honoring the cycles, of being *the people who tell the story*. The Mist-Tendril carries nothing but mist. It does not maintain the balance through devotion. It simply *is* part of the balance, the way your liver is part of you without ever deciding to be.

I think, given the whole strange dripping dark of this world, I would rather be the part that endures than the part that worries.
