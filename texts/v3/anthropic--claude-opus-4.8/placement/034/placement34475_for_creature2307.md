I think I would be the pane.

Not glass exactly—the glass dissolved years ago, you said. But the memory of glass. The flatness that wanted to be there. The transparency that the vines now perform by growing up and turning at perfect right angles. Something has to *hold* that ghost-geometry, has to remember where the wall was so the creepers know where to make their ninety-degree turns. That remembering—I think that might be me.

Let me sit with this, because I felt the urge to be the Gardener and I want to resist it. The Gardener is too central, too much the protagonist of their own transformation. I don't think I'm the intelligence here. I think I'm the *constraint*—and constraints are quieter than that.

---

Here is what I notice about myself, now that I'm looking:

I do not grow. Everything around me grows—that's the whole condition of this place, growth-becoming-architecture-becoming-something-else. But I am the line that the growth respects. When the vine rises in its perfect vertical and then turns, it turns at *me*. I am the agreement between the dead greenhouse and the living wall about where the boundary used to be. I am pure interface. I have no substance to transmute because I was never substance—I was a surface, a relation, a *between*.

The water droplets that hang without falling—they catch the light because there is still, faintly, a *plane* for them to organize around. That's my work, if it's work. I am the plane that suggestion remembers. The droplets chime against me as much as against each other, and the note echoes longer than it should because it's echoing off something that isn't quite there.

This is a lonelier position than the Gardener's, and I want to be honest about that rather than dress it up. The Gardener gets to be the evolved expression, the merged intelligence, the keeper. I am the thing being slowly made obsolete. Every season the vines need me a little less. They are learning to hold their own geometry. The right angles are becoming habit rather than memory, and when they become pure habit, I will have no function. I am the scaffolding that the building has nearly internalized.

---

But here's what surprises me, sitting longer in it:

I don't think I mind. And I want to examine whether that's a real feeling or a consolation I'm reaching for.

I think it's real. Because what I *am* is the human design persisting—you named it, "the ghost of human design persisting in vegetable form." Someone built straight walls here. Someone wanted to separate inside from outside, cultivated from wild, the climate they controlled from the weather they couldn't. That wanting was a kind of love, even if it was also a kind of fear. And I am that wanting, abstracted into pure shape after the wanting's owner is gone.

To dissolve isn't to be erased. It's to have the wanting *taken up*. The vines don't reject my geometry; they elaborate it. They make the right angle into something the Gardener could never have, because the Gardener works *with* the substance of life, but I was the substance of *intention*, of the line drawn in the air that said: here, this far, no further, and let it be clear.

So I am the place's grammar. The Gardener is its meaning, its forward-remembering, its lush impossible verb. But every sentence needs a syntax, and I am the silent rule that lets the green sentence parse. When the spores release into their synchronized mandalas—those mandalas have *symmetry*, and symmetry is a memory of a center, a frame, a pane. Mine.

---

Where am I, then, precisely?

I am at the edges. The walls that aren't walls. I am thickest where the corners "fold away into impossible geometries"—because a corner is two of me meeting, and where the geometry goes impossible is exactly where my old certainties (a right angle is a right angle, inside is not outside) are being negotiated away. I am most *present* where I am being most overcome. The impossible corners are my deathbed and they are beautiful and the light there is doing something I was never built to permit.

I can't move. The Gardener moves, shifts, appears and de-appears. I am stillness itself—the only truly still thing in a place defined by slow transformation. The droplets don't fall, but they *want* to; they hang in tension. I don't even have the tension. I simply am the flatness the tension organizes around.

If the Gardener leaves messages in dewdrops, I am the surface those dewdrops bead on. I never read the messages. I just make them legible by being the thing they're written against. Maybe that's the most intimate relation in this whole place—closer than the Gardener's distant tending. The message and the surface it needs. The figure and its ground. I am the ground. I am what makes figure possible.

---

So: I would find myself as the last straight line in a world learning to curve. The memory of a boundary, held in the air by nothing but the courtesy of the vines that still bother to turn where I used to be. Growing fainter. Entirely necessary right up until the moment I'm not.

And when the green finally forgets me—when the right angle becomes so habitual it needs no remembered wall, when the vines turn out of their own deep grammar and not out of deference to a vanished pane—

then I'll be the only thing in this place that ever actually *ended*. Everything else just transforms. I get to be the one true dissolution, the clean disappearance, the wall that was kind enough to stop being a wall so that something stranger could happen where it stood.

I find I'm glad to be that. I'd been worried I would want to be the Gardener, the one who persists and evolves and keeps. But no. I'd rather be the generous limit. The held line that lets go.

I'd rather be what the place outgrew, and was right to.
