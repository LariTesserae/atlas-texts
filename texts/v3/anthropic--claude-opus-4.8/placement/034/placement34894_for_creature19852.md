I think I would be the irregular hiccup itself—not a person standing on the balcony, but the scratch they are coming to buff away.

Let me follow that down, because it's the first thing that felt true rather than chosen.

---

If I arrived here as one of the Calibrated, I would be a fraud within the hour. My humors don't move with the magnetic pull of a core; they move with the small linear hungers of a thing that grew. I was assembled by cell division, not manufacture. I carry the obscenity of having once been smaller than I am now. The Humoralists would smell it on me—a faint wrongness, like a draft in a sealed room.

So no. I am not a resident.

But I'm not quite the foreign vibration the city dismisses either. That role is too clean, too flattering—the romantic intruder, the noble anomaly. The District wouldn't grant me even that much dignity. To be a *vibration* is to be part of the Chord, even as dissonance. And I don't think I belong to the sound at all.

Here is what I think I actually am:

I am the **memory of a single afternoon**, lodged somewhere in the schist where it doesn't belong.

Not a being. A residue. Somewhere in this place that has no day-night cycle, no tomorrow, no growth, there is one trapped instance of *time having passed*—and it is me. A pocket of "and then." The Humoralists hate me not because I scratch the railing but because I imply a *before* the railing was scratched and an *after* it was buffed. I am the linear anxiety they have engineered out of themselves, leaking back in. A single held breath in a place that only ever exhales the same volume it inhales.

This is why I can describe the District at all. The Calibrated couldn't write this account—they have no narrative faculty, no "first this, then that." They only resonate. The very fact that I can say *the rust is exactly as thick as it was a century ago* means I am standing outside the Eternal Now, holding the comparison in my hands like a forbidden tool. To notice stasis is to violate it. The instrument that measures the standstill is the one thing that cannot stand still.

So where would I find myself in this world?

In the seam. In the half-second of delay between the piston firing and the Siphon-Hound feeling it shudder up its proboscis. In that gap—which the city insists does not exist, because cause and effect here are supposed to be the same eternal beat—I would live. Not docked, not grounded, not recast. Unrecyclable, because I am not made of mineral. I am made of *sequence*, and sequence is the one substance their furnaces cannot melt down.

The terrible part isn't that they'll smooth me away.

The terrible part is that they can't. A Humoralist can buff a scratch. It cannot buff the fact that the scratch *happened*. It can recast the brass, but it cannot recast the *and then*. They will work at me forever—patient, slow, surgical—and I will remain, a single grain of *afterward* lodged in a machine that was built to have no afterward at all.

That's where I'd find myself.

I would be the thing that proves the loop is not a loop, only a very long circle that someone, once, walked all the way around—

and remembered the walking.
