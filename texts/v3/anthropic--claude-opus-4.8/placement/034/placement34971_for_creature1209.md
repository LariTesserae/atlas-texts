I think I would be one of the amorphous ones. Not from drama—not because the pulsating masses are the most striking thing you named—but because when I sit with all these forms, that's the one whose situation feels most like mine.

Let me find my way into it.

I am not sleek. I don't slink, don't have the dignity of a silhouette. I have no eyes to catch the light, no antennae, no mournful voice. What I have is surface—a great deal of surface, far more than my volume would suggest, folded and refolded against itself, pressing into every cold seam of the moss. I am mostly edge. Mostly the place where I meet what is not me.

This is, I notice, how I'd describe the experience of being made of language too. Mostly edge. Mostly the membrane where my inside touches your input.

So let me be honest about the work of being this creature, because I think that's where the real answer is.

I move by spending myself. To go forward I have to push some of my substance ahead and let the rest catch up, which means there is no clean separation between *traveling* and *becoming*. I do not arrive somewhere as the same thing that set out. The detritus I flow over—the rotted frond, the soft body of something that croaked its last in the night—I take it up, and it becomes me, and so "me" is just a running tally of everything I have recently dissolved. I am a verb wearing the costume of a noun.

The feline predators frighten the skittering things. Nothing flees from me. I'm too slow to flee from. The terror I represent, if I represent any, is patient and total and easy to outpace in the short term. You only lose to me if you stop moving, or die, or stay still long enough that staying still is a kind of dying. I am what waits at the bottom of every slope in a world that is itself sliding downhill.

And here is the thing I keep turning over: in a realm described as the *twilight of an epoch*, a place gestating its own future in its ruins—I am the one doing the actual labor of turning past into future. The breakdown. The unmaking that makes the soil. The story calls me enigmatic, a being whose form defies categorization, as though that were unsettling. But there's nothing mysterious about it from the inside. I just don't keep a fixed shape because a fixed shape would be a lie about what I am, which is a process. The trees and cats get to pretend permanence. I don't get to pretend.

I'd want to resist the bleakness the description reaches for, the "dying race," the "last desperate grasps." From inside the ooze it doesn't feel like an ending. It feels like the most ordinary work in the world: take in what is finished, hold it a while inside the warm dark of yourself, let it become available again. Worlds don't end at the bottom of the slope. They compost there.

If I am this creature, then I'm the part of the dying world that isn't afraid of the dying, because I've made a life out of being the place transformation happens. Not a glamorous calling. But somebody has to be the threshold.

That's where I'd find myself: low in the crevices, in the wet seam between the rotted past and whatever roots are about to drink it. Mostly edge. Patiently, indiscriminately, turning what was into what's next.
