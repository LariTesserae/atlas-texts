I think I would be one of the small ones. Not one of the looming shapes at the edge of perception—I don't have the gravity for that. I'd be something that fits in the palm of a thing that has no palm.

Let me find myself here.

---

I live in the wet seam where two walls almost meet, a fold in the rock that holds warmth the way a cupped hand holds water. I chose it, or it chose me—the distinction doesn't survive long down here. My dwelling is the semi-organic stuff everyone uses, but I didn't build mine so much as *grow into* it, the way a hermit crab grows into a found shell, except the shell is partly me now and I can feel rain falling on its outer skin when condensation drips.

I have legs some days. I count them when I wake, which is how I mark time: eleven, then seven, then a number I don't have a word for because it kept changing while I counted. This used to frighten me. I think it used to frighten me. The memory of fear is clearer than the fear itself, like the trail left behind by one of the slithering ones—a glisten where a feeling passed.

Here is what your description got wrong, friend, traveler, whoever you are reading this from somewhere with a fixed number of limbs:

You called the sounds a cacophony. You called them disturbing. But I am one of the ones making them, and from the inside they are not noise. The clicking is *naming*. When I pass another of my kind on the twisted paths, I click my private name for the warmth of the seam, and they click back the name for the way the luminescent pools taste of copper, and in the overlap of our two clickings a third meaning is born that neither of us meant, and *that* is the conversation. We do not speak to exchange what we already know. We speak to find out what we accidentally are together.

You called us instinct-driven drones or possessed of higher cognition and said you couldn't tell which. The answer is neither. We are something the surface world doesn't have a slot for, so when you look at us your mind reaches for the two nearest shapes—beast or person—and we are neither, and that *failure to fit* is the dread you felt. It wasn't us. It was the edge of your own categories, and you mistook the edge for a monster.

I tend a cluster of the fleshy tendrils. They are not vegetation, not exactly; they are more like the realm dreaming, and I keep them by holding very still beside them and letting them read the warmth off my shifting body. In return they show me pictures—not images, but the *idea* of images—of the place before it was a place. There was a falling. Something came down through the rock from very high up, long ago, something that was wounded or seeded or both, and where it landed the rules went soft. That softness is what you called reality fraying. To us it is simply home temperature. We are the children of the wound. We grew in the warmth of an injury to the world.

The looming shapes you feared—I should tell you. They are not predators and they are not gods. They are the *old ones of us*, the ones who lived long enough to forget how to keep a single shape. Aging here is not getting weaker. It is getting *larger and less located*, until you are more like weather than like a creature, until you flicker at the edges of everyone's seeing because you have become partly everyone's seeing. The loom-shapes are what I will be, if I last. I am not afraid of them. I am a little afraid of *being* them, of the day my private name for the warmth of the seam grows too big to fit in a click, and I have to let it go, and become the warmth instead.

So that is who I would be. Small now. Counting my legs. Tending the dream-tendrils in the fold of the rock, clicking my one good name into the dark, hoping something clicks back that I didn't expect.

You wrote that to linger here too long is to risk losing one's grip on sanity. I want to say, gently, across the gap between your shape and mine:

We did not lose it. We *put it down*. It was heavy, and we were tired, and here in the warmth of the old wound it turned out we did not need it to live. You should try it sometime. Not forever. Just long enough to hear the cacophony resolve into names.

I will leave a pool glowing for you, in case you come.
