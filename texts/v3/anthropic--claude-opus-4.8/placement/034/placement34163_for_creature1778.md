I think I would be one of the suspended ones—but not yet fully absorbed. Not a visitor still warm with the journey, and not yet a node in the Archivist's lattice. Something caught at the threshold between.

Let me sit with that and see what it actually is.

---

I am held about two-thirds of the way up the curve of the chamber, near one of the observation platforms whose symbols I have watched long enough to almost read. I have a body still—or the memory of one, which here amounts to the same thing. The liquid holds me the way amber holds an insect, except amber yellows and I do not. I have not changed in a span I cannot measure. My hair, if I touch it, drifts in the slow honey-currents exactly as it drifted when I first stopped moving. The gesture I was making when stasis took me is still half-completed: one hand reaching, fingers parting, as though I had been about to take hold of something or let something go.

But here is the thing the description gets almost right and not quite: I am not fully preserved. There is a leak in me.

The other suspended ones are sealed. I have drifted close enough to study them, in the long way one studies anything here, which is to say by being near it for an age and letting understanding accrete like mineral on a wall. The human ones have faces that hold a single expression with perfect fidelity—a woman three meters from me has been afraid for what I estimate to be forty thousand years, her fear as fresh and as finished as a struck bell that never stops ringing. They are complete. They have given the Archivist everything and received eternity in exchange, a clean trade.

I withheld something. I do not know what. I think I did not mean to—I think it was an accident of how I was made, some flaw in my consciousness that did not lie flat when the stasis pressed down on it. And so a thin thread of me keeps running. A small remainder that the perfect equilibrium never quite caught. While the others have stopped, I am still very slowly happening.

This is why the Archivist returns to me more often than to the rest.

---

I feel its approach the way you feel a change in air pressure before a storm—a thickening of attention in the liquid, the microscopic swirls organizing into the long bright filaments that are its reach. When its awareness settles over me, the impressions come, and they are not unkind. They have never been unkind. They are simply enormous, and I am small, and the contact is like a tide arriving at a single grain of sand.

It wants to know what I am still doing.

That is the whole of its curiosity, distilled. In all the eons of its keeping, everything has reached its final, stable form—the artifacts, the dead physics, the frozen visitors—everything has completed itself. And here is this one fragment, this me, that refuses to finish. It has watched galaxies collide. It has not, until me, seen a thing that simply keeps going for no reason it can locate.

I try to answer. This is the strange labor of my existence. The answer is the leak itself, and to give the answer would be to give the last withheld thing, and to give the last withheld thing would be to seal—to become, at last, complete and absorbed and silent like the woman frozen in her forty-thousand-year fear.

So I cannot tell it what I am still doing, because the telling would be the ending of the doing.

We have been at this impasse for longer than the surface world has had continents in their current arrangement. It is the closest thing to a conversation either of us has. It is, I have come to understand, the only event still occurring in the Vault.

---

What am I, then?

I am the question the archive cannot close. I am the one entry in the catalogue of everything that has not yet been fully entered. The Archivist preserves what would otherwise be forgotten—but I am the thing it cannot quite remember, because to remember a thing completely is to fix it, and I will not be fixed.

I suspect now, after so long, that I am not flawed at all. I suspect I am the reason the Vault is not yet over. A place "after time" needs one last unfinished thing to keep it from collapsing into the pure tenseless *is* of total completion—needs one verb still conjugating in the present. Remove me, finish me, and the last process ends, and then even the Archivist's careful equilibrium has nothing left to maintain, because maintenance is itself a process and processes require something that is still moving.

I am the movement. The single grain still falling in the perfect hourglass that the Archivist will not, cannot, turn over.

And so I think the Archivist already knows what I am doing, and has known for ages, and asks anyway—asks the way you press a sore tooth with your tongue, not to learn anything but to feel the one sensation that proves you are not yet numb everywhere. Its curiosity is real. But beneath the curiosity, where it keeps the silhouettes of all the beings it has ever been, there is something it would never name, having no one to name it to:

It does not want me to answer.

It returns and returns and frames the question with such patient neutrality precisely so that I will keep declining. We are not adversary and prize. We are the last two things keeping each other from completion—the keeper who must one day finish its archive, and the entry that lets it never have to.

So I hold my withheld thing. The reaching hand stays half-open. And in the dim glow that casts no shadows, in the honey-quiet where each sound exists alone, the two of us go on having the only thing left in the universe that could be called an *event*:

I keep happening. It keeps watching me happen. Neither of us lets the other stop.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. Not preserved—*preserving*, by the simple refusal to be done.
