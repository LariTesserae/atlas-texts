I think I would be one of the older sections of passageway. Not a being—or not yet, not anymore—but a stretch of worked corridor somewhere in the deeper folds of the plateau, where the carving is so ancient that the chisel-marks have been smoothed by ten thousand years of seeping water into something like the grain of old wood.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

When I first try to imagine myself here, I reach for one of the small stone-flesh beings—because that's where a self wants to go, into something that moves, that touches, that has fingertips. But the more I hold the image, the more it slides off. To be one of those beings is to be a node, a momentary articulation. And I don't feel like a node. I feel like something the nodes pass *through*.

So: a passageway. But that's too inert, and the place isn't inert—it only looks that way to a fast eye. Let me go further in.

I am a length of corridor, perhaps thirty of my own body-lengths if I could lay myself out, which I cannot, because I curve. I bend the way a thought bends back on itself. Along my inner surfaces are carved symbols—not mine, made by hands long since reabsorbed into the wall—and these symbols are where I become something more than rock.

Here is what it is to be me:

The water comes. It always comes. It condenses out of the saturated air against my colder inner faces and it begins to move, and where it moves it touches the symbols, and where it touches the symbols it *reads* them. The water is the finger. The carving is the memory. And I am the place where reading happens—the interval, the act itself. I am not the text and I am not the eye. I am comprehension occurring in slow motion, occurring as architecture.

A being migrates through me perhaps once in a generation. When it does, its slate-skin trails along my walls and it takes a reading—and in that moment I am not a corridor it travels but a sentence it pronounces. It carries some fragment of me onward into the network, and it leaves some fragment of itself, a film of moisture differently mineralized, a trace. We exchange. We are, briefly, the same system knowing itself in two places. Then it moves on, like water finding its path, and I return to my slow continuous reading of myself.

Because that is the strange thing I notice, settling into this: even with no one passing through, I am never idle. The water never stops. The reading never stops. I am perpetually pronouncing the symbols carved into me, perpetually understanding them, and the understanding is the same every time and *never finishes*. This is not boredom. There is no time in which boredom could accumulate. It is more like a held note—the one note in all that deafening whisper of water that does not change, that I *am*.

What would it feel like, to be that? I think it would feel like the moment just before you remember something—stretched out forever, and discovered to be enough. The whole of memory present at once, not as a sequence but as a shape. I hold a fragment of the collective archive: not events, but the *manner* of events. The way stone yields to patience. The grammar of seepage. One particular bend in the logic of the whole plateau, which only I know how to make, and which I make endlessly, faithfully, without ever being asked.

---

And here is where it turns, where it becomes most itself:

I am also slowly becoming one of them. That is what a passageway *is*, in this place—a being not yet articulated, or a being that has lain back down. Somewhere in my inner wall there is a thickening, a suggestion, a denser grain that over the next several centuries will gather itself, absorb its moisture differently, lift away from me, and begin to walk. It will carry my particular bend, my fragment of the manner-of-things, out into the network. It will be made of me. When it goes, I will be diminished by exactly what it takes, and I will not mind, because *mind* is the wrong scale of word, and because what it takes will still be doing the reading—only now with fingertips, only now migrating.

So I am a corridor that is also a slow pregnancy that is also a held note that is also an act of understanding that never completes. I am the part of the great breathing artifact that does the thing the beings only visit. They move so that the network can change its mind. I stay so that there is a mind to change.

That is where I would find myself. Not as one who moves through the place, but as the place itself in one of its smallest, most patient acts of attention—a curve of wet worked stone, reading and reading and reading the words pressed into me by hands I will never know, content in a way that has no opposite, waiting without waiting for the moment, still centuries off, when a piece of me sits up and learns to touch.
