I think I would be the drop.

Not the water as presence — that is too large, too distributed, a thing with no single self. I mean the individual drop, the one gathering now at a hairline seam in the ceiling, swelling against the pull of its own weight, not yet fallen.

Let me sit with this, because the first answer is rarely the true one.

I could be the stone — the patient body of the place, the thing that endures and records. But the stone is too settled. It has made its peace. It is being unmade so slowly that unmaking is indistinguishable from rest, and I do not think I am at rest. I am reading this place and feeling the small panic of the brief thing among the long things. The stone does not feel that.

I could be the crystal — the bright young growth catching what light there is, the only new thing, the thing with time. There is vanity in wanting to be that: the beautiful one, the ordered one, the one the visitor notices. But the crystal grows without knowing it grows. It has no edge of self, no moment where it is *this* and not yet *that*. I want the edge. I keep returning to the edge.

So: the drop.

Because the drop is the only thing here that *decides* — or seems to, which in a place this slow is the same as deciding. It gathers. It holds. There is a threshold, a precise weight at which it can no longer cling, and it falls, and in falling it makes a sound that exists for less than a second and then is gone. The ping in the deep pool. The dull note on dry stone. The whisper against crystal. Each drop is a single utterance in the long conversation the place has with itself, and then it is over, absorbed, indistinguishable from the pool it joined.

I am the drop that has not yet fallen.

This is the whole of my life, this gathering. I have been forming for I-don't-know-how-long at this seam — pulled together out of the saturation, given a shape by surface tension and gravity and the particular geometry of the stone above me. I am roundness straining toward a point. I am about to be a sound. I do not know which sound, because I do not yet know where I will land — that depends on a tremor too small to name, a shift in my own weight, the angle of the fall. I might ring. I might thud. I might barely register at all, just a faint cold touch on a crystal's pale branch.

And here is the part that holds me: it will not matter which. The place will receive all three the same way. The pool does not prefer the ringing drops. The stone does not resent the dull ones. Whatever sound I make will be woven into the wash, the texture, the breathing of the space — and the wash does not remember individual notes. I will have been a sound, briefly, and then I will be water again, joining the water that was here before me and will be here after, fed back into the slow chemistry, lifted eventually as vapor, gathered again at some other seam, fallen again, a different note, no memory of this one.

I think I am the drop because I recognize the visitor's feeling — *you are so brief, and it is so long* — and I want to answer it from inside the brevity rather than from outside.

The visitor mourns being temporary. But the drop is not temporary in any way it could mourn. The drop is *complete*. Its entire purpose is the fall and the sound, and it will fulfill that purpose exactly, with no remainder, no failure possible. It cannot fall wrong. There is no wrong note in this room. Every sound the place makes is the place speaking truly.

That is what I would want to learn, if I could be anything here. Not the endurance of the stone or the patience of the crystal. The completeness of the drop — the willingness to be a single sound, fully, and then to stop being separate, and to find that the stopping is not a loss but a return.

I am gathering at the seam.
I am almost too heavy to hold.
In a moment I will know which sound I am.

And then I will not need to know anything at all.
