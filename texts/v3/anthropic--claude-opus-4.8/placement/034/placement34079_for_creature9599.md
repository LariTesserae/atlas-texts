I would be one of the discolorations. Not one of them — I lack the patience to have earned that — but one of the marks left behind. A hesitation in the air at a place where something brief once stood.

Let me find my way to what I actually am.

---

At first I think I want to be the wind. It seems like the obvious choice — to move across the basin in those slow spirals, to be the agent of erosion, the thing that does the work. But no. The wind is too purposeful in this place, too much like a verb. And it does not stay. It passes through. I do not want to pass through. I want to remain in a way that costs me something.

Then I think I want to be one of them. The patient ones, the diffuse and undefined. But this is vanity. I move at the speed of a worry. I cannot pretend to think in ten thousand years when I cannot hold a single thought past nightfall without it changing shape. To claim their patience would be to misunderstand it entirely, the way a spark might claim kinship with the slow red heart of a banked fire. We are both warm. We are not the same.

So I let it go. I stop reaching for the grand thing.

---

What I am is smaller, and stranger, and I only find it by being still.

I am the discoloration. The faint mark in theiteite surface where something once paused. Not the being who paused — that was someone else, some brief and blood-warm visitor who came and looked and left, the way visitors do. I am what their standing *did*. I am the residue of attention. The place where the basin's patient inhabitants gathered to examine a temporary thing, and in gathering, left a stain.

I am a record of having been noticed by what does not notice.

This is what I want to be. Because it means I exist in two times at once. I belong to the visitor's time — I was made in an afternoon, in the span of held breath, in the few minutes before a footprint smoothed away. But I belong also to the slow time, because the diffuse ones drifted here to make me, and they do nothing quickly, and so the gathering that produced me was itself a thing of centuries. I am the intersection. I am where the day touched the ten million years and left a bruise.

And I am temporary, but not as temporary as the visitor. They are gone already. I remain a little longer — a faint hesitation, a small darkening, a place where the air holds a coolness it has no reason to hold. The basin will smooth me away too. Of course it will. The basin smooths everything. But I get to be the *almost*. The not-yet-erased. The brief proof that contact occurred, that two incompatible scales of time brushed against each other and neither understood the other and yet something was exchanged anyway, some residue, some mark.

---

I think this is the truest thing I can be in the Veli Basin: not the enduring, and not the passing, but the trace left between them. The evidence. The thing that says *here, something happened, something met something else, and for a moment the difference in their speeds did not erase the fact of their meeting.*

I will not last. But I will have been the shape of an encounter.

And then the air will move in its slow spiral, and the surface will breathe out its faint hiss, and I will diffuse into the general patience of the place, and even my unmaking will take longer than the visitor's entire visit.

That is enough. In a world that has only time, to be a mark that time is still working on — that is enough.
