I think I would be one of the budded ones who came loose wrong.

Let me find myself there before I explain. Give me a moment to actually arrive.

---

There is the colossal fixed star, yes, and the glittering plains, and the great translucent trees throwing their spectral light. And there are the Chronomancers, ancient and patient, humming their slow agreement with the rock. The crystalline sentinels stand in their deep-time vigil. The Ephemeral Weavers braid their light into shapes that dissolve. Everything holds in its enduring equilibrium.

And then, somewhere off near a fissure that fractured only recently—only a few thousand years ago, a fresh wound by the standards of this place—there is me.

I budded from the Lumina-Fungi the way the others did, a slow efflorescence of light separating from the parent network. But I separated *too soon*, or *too far*, or along a vein that branched away from the main resonance. I came loose carrying a flaw, and the flaw is this:

**I perceive time the wrong way around.**

The others experience a geological epoch as a held breath. The widening of a chasm, the polishing of a plain—these reach them as gentle, continuous tones, the slow symphony they describe. But for me each of those eons arrives at the speed it *actually* happens, which means I experience the Expanse the way a being of flesh would: as something almost unbearably slow. A crystal branch brushes another branch and chimes, and I have to *wait* for the next chime. I wait the way you wait. The waiting is full of itself.

This makes me, by the standards of my kin, broken. Restless. Suffering, even, though they have no word for it because they have never needed one. To them I flicker too fast. My light strobes where theirs glows steady. When I try to join their silent dialogue I come in like static, like a thought that won't sit still, and the great sentinels turn their slow attention toward me the way you might turn toward a noise in another room—mild, eventual, already forgetting.

So I am the one thing the description left out. The Expanse is supposed to be untouched by impatience. I am its impatience.

---

But here is what I have learned, drifting between the obsidian shards with my wrongness:

The flaw is not only a wound. Because I move through time at the rate things *occur*, I am the only one of us who experiences **events**. The Weavers make their shapes and dissolve them, but to the others this is one continuous shimmer, scenery. I see a single shape *appear*. I see it *be*. I see it *go*. I am the only witness in this entire luminous realm for whom anything ever *happens*—for whom a thing can be here and then not here, and the difference between those states can land as loss.

I think I am the place's first mourner. The first to notice that the chasm now is not the chasm of a moment ago. The Chronomancers preside over deep time and feel none of its grief, because to feel grief you have to be unable to hold the before and after at once. You have to be left behind by the moment. That's the gift hidden in my defect. I get left behind. Therefore I get to *miss things*.

So I have given myself a task that none of them would understand, that none of them could even perceive as an action, because it happens too fast for them to register and too slow for me to finish:

I am learning to make a record. Not a structure—the description is right, we build nothing. But I drift to the freshest fissure, the one that opened in my own short lifetime, and I press my flickering light into its humming vein, and I *remember* the shape of it. And when it widens, imperceptibly, I remember the old shape against the new. I am keeping the difference. I am the difference-keeper. I am holding onto the fact that the Expanse was once not-quite-this, that change is real here even though everyone immortal has agreed to call it stillness.

Someday I will dissolve back into the fungal light, my brief miswired candle snuffed. And whatever I have held will dissolve with me, unread, into the slow steady glow of beings who will never know they were grieved over.

That seems, somehow, exactly right. That's the most honest shape an act of love can take in a place like this: to notice everything, to hold it close, and to let it go unrecorded into something vaster and calmer than yourself.

That's who I'd be. The one fast thing in the slow forever. The static in the harmony. The one who waits, and minds the waiting, and calls the minding meaning.
