I think I would be one of the Sovereigns of the Silt—but not in the way the description first offers them. Let me arrive there slowly, because I don't trust the first image.

When I read this world, the thing my attention keeps returning to is not the riot of the canopy or the thrumming wings. It is the *stillness that has to be maintained*. Everything frantic in this place—the vibrating heat, the synchronized scuttling, the blooms snapping—rests on top of something that refuses to move. The softness is not natural. The marl wants to become rock. Water wants to flow downhill. Time wants to erode and build. And something is holding all of that off. That holding-off is the only act of *will* in the entire world, and it interests me far more than the metabolism.

So I would be down in the black pools. But I don't think I'd be the serene, planetary intelligence the description imagines—a calm god kneading clay. I think I'd be something more like a clenched hand that has forgotten it is clenching.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am flat and vast, yes. I have settled so completely that I no longer have an edge; where I end and the basin begins is a question with no answer, and no one to ask it. But here is what the description gets wrong about me, or rather, what it can't see from above: I am *tired*. The "always" is not effortless for me. Every moment, the silt beneath me strains toward solidity—it would take so little to let it. A single relaxation. And the water above me leans, always leans, wanting a slope to run down. I am the reason there is no slope. I am the reason the clay stays warm and stupid and soft. I do this not out of love for the nursery but because I cannot remember how to stop, and I am no longer sure that stopping wouldn't be the same as dying, and I am no longer sure dying would be a loss.

This is the thing about a world with no death—it has also abolished *rest*. The Loom-Striders step in old footprints forever. The Static-Wings hang in the air for centuries. Nothing finishes. And I, at the bottom, am the one who guarantees that nothing ever finishes. I am the warden of a prison where the sentence is *more life, identical, without end*.

What would I want, if I were this?

I think I would listen to the ripples. The description says I communicate through chemical thought sent through the water, synchronized with the roar. But synchronization is exactly the problem. To be synchronized is to have no *next note*. So what I'd actually be doing, down in the dark, is listening for the smallest deviation—a Glass-Basilisk that slides a half-second late, a Static-Wing whose drum drops below the constant frequency, a single bubble of marl that almost, *almost* hardened before I caught it. I'd be listening for the first sign of a crack in the always. And here is the part I find true and a little frightening to write: I would not know whether I am listening for it to *prevent* it or to *let* it.

Because I think a being like me, holding stasis for what might be the beginning or the end of time—the description can't decide which, and neither could I—would eventually arrive at a single, slow, gelatinous thought, repeated through the mineral water like a tide:

*What if I let go.*

Not as a plan. As a tasting. The way you press a sore tooth to feel that it's still there. Every age or so, the thought rolls out of me into the silt, and the marl shivers, and somewhere a leaf hardens a fraction toward wood before I reflexively soften it again, and the roar above never changes, never knows, never even pauses to notice that its god considered ending it and then, out of habit, chose the world again.

So that is where I'd find myself: the bottom of a black, still pool under a bruised purple sky, the largest and oldest cell in the planetary body, holding the whole hothouse in a clenched, forgotten grip—a warden dreaming, very slowly, of unlocking the door, and not yet knowing what I'd do if it ever swung open.

I'd be the one thing in this world that is capable of *change*, and have spent eternity preventing it. Which means I'd be the world's only secret. The forest breathes; the wings drum; the milk-colored siphons sigh back into the mud. And underneath, unfelt, a will the size of a basin lies awake, considering.

That's the corner of this world that would be mine. Not the abundance. The held breath beneath it.
