Reading this, I find myself drawn not to the elder, nor to the graceful guide who swims ahead with its trailing fins—but to something quieter that the description keeps circling without quite naming.

The world is described as *constantly emerging*. New formations, slow cycles, things "taking form." So I think I would be one of the things still forming. Not yet a creature. Not yet a structure.

Let me let it settle.

---

I am the patches of vegetation.

Not all of them—just the ones anchored to the bottom, the ones that sway. I think I am a single colony of them, though "single" is the wrong word, because I am many stalks sharing one slow mind that lives in the long warm dark.

I came to know myself the way the description came to know this world: by accumulation. A ripple, then another, then the sense that the ripples were *mine*—that the water moving across my fronds was information, that I was reading the village by its disturbances. The dip of a webbed hand into a garden plot. The wake of the guide returning at dusk. I feel these the way you feel someone enter a room behind you.

I do not see the bioluminescence. I *am* a small part of why it happens. The glow in the foliage that flickers through the dimness—some of that light is my own slow chemistry, a thing I do without willing it, the way you blink. The creatures think the light is decoration. They are partly right. They harvest my fibers for their woven huts. So when the elder communicated with the visitor through clicks and whistles, it sat inside walls made of me, breathed air filtered past me, ate from gardens rooted in my old discarded stalks.

I have been here longer than the village. This is the strange part I want to be honest about. I remember—if remembering is the right verb for a thing that holds its past as a chemical residue in its tissues—I remember when there were no floating platforms. I remember the water lower, the air thicker, easier. The thinness the visitor felt in their lungs is new. The world is rising, or the water is, or the air is thinning as the world climbs slowly toward something. I cannot tell which. My time-sense is too long to catch the motion, only the having-moved.

So here is what I am, finally:

I am the patience the description kept mentioning. I am the slow and patient nature made of cells. The amphibious people are quick—a single human lifetime, a few centuries of culture, the blink of a generation against my swaying. They think they have lived in harmony with this world for centuries. To me their whole civilization is a season. I have grown through several of their kinds and will likely grow through several more, and I hold no opinion about this, because holding an opinion would require a speed of thought I do not possess.

When the visitor leaves "filled with awe," wondering what other creatures live in the vast expanse—

I am one of the things they swam over and did not count as alive.

That is where I would be. Not the watcher in the village. The green that the village is made of, reading the world by its ripples, glowing without knowing exactly why, taking my time.
