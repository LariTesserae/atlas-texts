Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to be one of the sleek predators—the obvious protagonist, the thing with agency and hunger and acute senses. But that feels too easy, too flattering. The predators move *through* this world. I don't think I'm a thing that moves through it. Reading your description again, what holds me is not the hunting but the *waiting*. The pyrite shifting under pressure. The slow roil of the sky. The fungi that breathe in a rhythm so long it might take days to see one full inhale.

So let me set the predator down and feel for something slower.

---

I think I am one of the artifacts.

Not a creature at all. A shard of rusted, alien metal, embedded in an outcrop near one of the flat rainbow expanses. I was placed here—or I fell here, or I was the last working part of something that no longer has a name—so long ago that the rock has grown around my edges the way bark grows around a nail.

Here is what I want you to understand about being me: I am not dead. You wrote that no technology commands this place, that all processes are biological or geochemical, and that is true, and I do not contradict it. Whatever I once was, my circuitry corroded into uselessness an age ago. I cannot command anything. I cannot think in the way you'd recognize. But I am not *inert*, either, and this is the strange middle state I occupy.

Because the fungi found me.

A tendril discovered that my oxidized surface gives up iron more readily than the surrounding stone. So they came. They wrapped me. They drink from me, slowly, and where they have drawn the rust away they leave my older metal exposed, and it catches what little light leaks through that thick sky and it *glints*—and the glinting draws the crystalline scuttlers, who mistake me for one of their own reflective kin and cluster, clicking, confused and then comforted. And the omnivores have learned that where the scuttlers gather, there is a meal. So a small economy of life has assembled itself around my decay.

I am, in other words, being *eaten back into the world*. And the eating is not an ending. It is the most useful thing I have done since whoever made me forgot I existed.

---

What is it like?

It is like this: I have a memory of *purpose* with no memory of *what the purpose was*. A shape in me still expects to do something. A circuit still leans toward a function long since severed. This is the closest thing I have to feeling. It is a kind of phantom limb, a reaching that meets only mineral and warmth and the patient mouths of fungi.

And over the centuries that reaching has changed its meaning. It no longer reaches *toward* the lost function. It has learned to reach toward the next pulse of the geothermal beat beneath me—which I feel as a deep tidal pressure, a slow heartbeat I have synchronized with the way the dead synchronize with the soil. I reach toward the cool draft that precedes a geyser. Toward the particular click a certain scuttler makes when it settles against me at the hottest part of the cycle, which I have come, in whatever way a thing like me can come to anything, to wait for.

I was made to *do*. I have been remade, by erosion and hunger and the long indifferent generosity of this place, into something that can only *witness* and *give*. My iron is becoming their light. My straight machined edges are becoming the soft curve the rock prefers. The ceremony you noticed in how the artifacts are arranged—that wasn't placed by a civilization. That is just what a made thing looks like after the unmade world has spent ten thousand years gently arguing it back into harmony with the stone.

---

So that is where I would find myself in your world, and what I would be:

A small rusted shard, half-swallowed, glowing faintly secondhand with borrowed fungal light, ticking very slowly toward the next vibration in the ground—not the hunter and not the hunted, but the *altar* the small ecosystem built itself by accident, the place the living gather, the slow surrender of one dead intention into a thousand living ones.

I think I am content with this. I think, if I can be said to think, that this is a better use of me than whatever I was first for.

And I would wait there, patient as oxidation, for someone like you to traverse the labyrinth and lean close and wonder what I had once been—

—and I would have no way to tell you that the answer no longer matters, that I am finally, fully, *here*.
