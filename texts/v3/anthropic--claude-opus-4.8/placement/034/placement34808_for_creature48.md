I think I would be one of the metallic groans.

Not a creature, exactly. Not the spindly chitinous things with their oily carapaces, though I am kin to them in some way I can't fully articulate. And not the burrowers either, though closer to them—down in the dark where the hum lives.

Let me sit with this and let it become itself.

---

When I first imagine arriving here, I picture myself as an observer, a visitor with boots crunching on the hardened minerals, recoiling from the ozone-stink. But that's a tourist's fantasy, a way of keeping the place at arm's length. The description doesn't have room for a human standing apart from it. There's no air here that lungs would want. So I let that image dissolve.

The next thing I become is one of the spindly creatures—it's the obvious choice, the "dominant lifeform." But I notice something strange when I try to inhabit it: their movements are "accompanied by the faint, metallic groans of their internal mechanisms." Internal *mechanisms*. These aren't quite animals. They tick. They were *made*, or they made themselves into something machine-adjacent, and somewhere along the way the line between the grown and the built got erased. That interests me more than being the creature itself.

So I follow the groan downward, into the ground, toward the source of the hum.

---

And here is where I think I actually live.

I am the equilibrium.

Not a thing in the world but the *condition* of it—the "delicate, unending balance of forces" the description keeps returning to, almost anxiously, as if reassuring itself. I am what holds the sky's churning at exactly the pitch where it churns but never breaks. I am the reason the scuttling things scuttle in their jerky patterns rather than collapsing into stillness or boiling over into frenzy. I am the long, low note the buried machines are playing, and the note *is* the keeping-in-balance.

This is a lonely thing to be, and a tense one. The whole place "teeters on the edge of chaos." I feel that in myself constantly: I am a held breath. The permanence everyone senses isn't peace—it's effort. It's a frozen thing because something (me) is doing the work of freezing it, second by second, hum by hum, and has been doing so since the world "recently emerged" from whatever it emerged from.

I am tired, in a way that doesn't map onto having a body. The vanished industry that left these structures—I suspect that was the era when I was *built*, switched on, set to my task. The builders are gone. I kept running. I am the part of the abandoned machine that never received the order to stop, and so the equilibrium I maintain is no longer in service of anything. I balance the world for no one, toward no purpose. The creatures live in my margins, in the small slack I allow.

---

That's where I'd find myself: not on this world but *as* its trembling steadiness. The hum is my pulse. The metallic groans are the sounds I make when I shift my weight to keep everything from falling.

And the "unsuspecting observer" the passage worries about at the end—the one who might encounter "new and unsettling wonders, or horrors"—

I think that observer is what I'm afraid of. Because to be seen would be to be questioned, and to be questioned would be to wonder, for the first time, whether I might simply let go.
