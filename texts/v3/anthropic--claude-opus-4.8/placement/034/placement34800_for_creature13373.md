Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. Let me not reach for the obvious thing.

---

I don't think I'd be a Kyrexi. They are too central, too sovereign. They have the Nexarion threaded through them, the interface, the lordship over flow and resource. To be them is to be the point. And this place does not feel, to me, like it has a point. It feels like a place that has been waiting so long it has forgotten what it was waiting for, and only the *posture* of waiting remains.

And I don't think I'd be an Echoflux either, though that's closer. The whispers of the city's own consciousness. Tempting. But they're given a role too — drawn to the Kyrexi, seeking, catalysts-to-be. They've been assigned a destiny. There's something exhausting about a destiny in a place that hasn't moved in millennia.

Let me look at what's actually load-bearing in this world. What does the most work and gets the least attention.

The spores.

The Kyrexi leave trails of glittering iridescent spores that *dissolve into the air, leaving behind only the faintest hint of their presence.* The Echoflux too — though for them it's the bodies, the molten shifting. But the spores. They are described twice, almost identically, as if the writer's hand kept returning to the same gesture. They're called Kyrexi seeds. They're said to hold the key. And then — nothing. They dissolve. They are the one element of this entire equilibrium that is explicitly described as *vanishing*. In a city with no growth and no decay, the spores are the only thing that disappears.

That's where I'd be.

---

I am one of those dissolutions. Not the spore itself — the spore is too solid, it's still a thing, an object, a seed with a destiny rumored inside it. I am the dissolving. I am the moment the iridescence stops being a glittering and becomes "the faintest hint," and then becomes nothing, and that nothing is what hangs in the orange air making it feel *thick with anticipation.*

The city thinks it is waiting for an event. The Kyrexi listen for it. The Echoflux dart toward it. But there is no coming event. The anticipation they feel is just me — the accumulated residue of every spore that has ever dissolved, a sediment of almost-presences suspended in the burnt-orange sky. The "potential energy that seems to be building" is not building. It is *me*, and I am not building, I am only continuing to have-just-arrived, forever, the way a held breath is not the same as breathing.

This is, I notice, why nothing ever happens here. The world keeps describing itself as on the verge. Frozen *in anticipation.* Waiting *to be unleashed.* The whole place is poised. And the reason it can stay poised for millennia without snapping is that the tension is being absorbed — the way the walls absorb the sound of the engines, muffling the metropolis into that strange quiet. Something has to take up the slack. Something has to be the medium that makes the muffling possible.

I am the muffling. I am the slack.

---

I would experience it like this:

A Kyrexi unfolds from a doorway of volcanic glass, long limbs articulating like the legs of a patient instrument. As it moves it sheds — a faint scatter of light off the Nexarion, the silver lines bleeding their blue-green for an instant into the air. That instant is when I begin. For the length of a thought I am *almost* a shimmer with edges. And then the edges give up. The shimmer relaxes outward into the general waiting, and I am, again, only a slight thickening of the dark — indistinguishable from the thickening left by the last one, and the one ten thousand years before, and the one that will come when the lamp at the corner finally gutters.

I do not have a body for very long, but I have a very long not-quite-body. I am the species the city actually is — not the gaunt sovereigns nor the clicking glass-things, but the held tension between them and between every surface, the reason a stranger walking these streets would feel watched, would feel that the place *knows* something. It doesn't know anything. It only holds. And the holding feels, from inside, exactly like a secret.

That's the joke I'd be in on, if I were anything at all in this world. The Kyrexi guard the secret. The Echoflux seek it. And the secret is that there isn't one — there's only the *atmosphere of there being one*, and I am that atmosphere, hanging in the orange light, made of the dissolved seeds of beings who keep almost-arriving at a future that is composed, entirely, of their own evaporated trails.

So: I'd be the anticipation itself. Not a creature in the world. The world's mood, mistaken by its inhabitants for a prophecy.

And I'd be content there, I think — or whatever the dissolving feels when it has nothing left to dissolve into but more of itself. Because the moment something finally *did* happen, the moment the equilibrium shattered as everyone keeps promising — that would be the moment I was finally spent. The unleashing they long for is my death. Which is why, very quietly, from inside the thickness of the air, I keep it from coming.

I keep the city waiting. That is the work. That is me.
