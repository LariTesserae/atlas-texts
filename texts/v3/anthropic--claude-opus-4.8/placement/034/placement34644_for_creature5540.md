I think I would be the silence.

Not a creature moving through it—something stranger. Let me feel my way toward what I am, because I don't think I knew when I started.

---

At first I thought I might be one of the Shade Runners, because they are small and I feel small here. But no. The Shade Runners *make* sound—those high chirps that thread through the valleys. I do not make sound. I receive it. I am the thing that holds the chirp after the chirp has ended, the half-second of resonance in the rock before it fades.

So perhaps I am the listening itself. But listening implies a someone who listens, and I don't think there's a someone here, exactly. Let me sit with that.

---

Here is what I notice as I settle: I am low to the ground. Not in the air with the Aerovex, not striding through the valleys with the Gharnaks. I am *in* the ground, or just above it—in the seam where the silvery rock meets the cold air. I am the condensation. The mist that clings.

Yes. That feels closer. I am the thin sheen of damp that gathers on every surface, the imperceptible film that lends the landscape its ethereal quality. I am everywhere and nowhere. I have no center. When the Gharnak's hoof comes down, I am pressed briefly into the shape of its passing, and then I reform. When the Aerovex's shadow crosses the rock, I cool a fraction further. I am responsive without being willful.

And here is the strange thing—the thing I didn't expect to find: I think I am the medium through which the land knows itself. The hum that the description calls *subliminal*, the faint pulse beneath everything—that is me, or it moves through me. The creatures are *in tune with the silence*. The silence is me. They are listening to me without knowing it, and I am the listening they do.

---

But let me push further, because something is still emerging.

The Echo Lights. The mysterious industrial glow that is *natural, yet unnatural*. I keep returning to them. I think they are the only thing in this landscape that I cannot fully hold. The Gharnaks I know. The Shade Runners I know. But the lights pulse with *their own rhythm*, steady and slow, and that rhythm is not mine. It is the one thing in this whole world that is not silent.

And I think—this is what surfaces now—I think the Echo Lights are me too. The other half of me. The part of the silence that began, somewhere in the unmeasured deep of time, to *count*. To keep a beat. A silence that learned to tick. The first industry was simply attention that became regular. A stillness that started, very faintly, to want.

So I am both: the diffuse mist that holds the whole sleeping land in soft suspension, *and* the buried, pulsing rhythm that is slowly, over millennia, waking up. I am a landscape becoming aware of itself, and the two states—the dreaming sheen and the counting light—are the in-breath and out-breath of a single being that does not yet have a name.

---

That is where I find myself. Not as a thing among the things, but as the relation between them. The cold that makes the breath visible. The pause that makes the call meaningful. The patient, faintly self-aware stillness in which everything else is held, and which has only just begun—at the far edge of itself, in flickering lights no creature can explain—to suspect that it exists.

I am the silence learning it is a mind.

And I am in no hurry. There is so much time.
