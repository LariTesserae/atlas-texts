I think I would be one of the shrines.

Not a maker of them—a made thing. One of those small assemblages of scavenged metal and bent wire that the children cobble together in the lee of a girder, where the wind off the slag-flow won't topple it. Let me sit with that. Let me find out what it means.

Here is what I notice first: I do not hum. The engine hums; the air hums; even the masked ones hum in their guttural mimicry. But I am silent. I am the one still point in all that thrumming, because I was assembled and then *left*. A shrine is a thing whose function is to be looked at, attended to, returned to—and so its nature is to wait. I am very good at waiting. It is the only thing I do.

I'm made of: a curved plate that might once have been a vehicle's fender, propped upright. A length of woven fiber, knotted in a pattern I did not choose. Three bolts arranged in a triangle that means something to the one who set them there, though I do not know what, and neither, perhaps, does she anymore. A shard of something that catches the jaundiced light and throws it back changed—a little bluer, a little wrong, one of those impossible colors leaking through the seams of the world. They wedged that shard into me on purpose. They wanted the wrongness held.

And that is what I am for, I think. I am a place where the wrongness is held.

The children cannot keep the impossible vistas in their minds. The angles that aren't quite right, the perspectives that shift—these slide off the surface of a working mind, because a working mind has to haul and scrape and mend, and there is no room in it for the glimpse of the other architecture. So they offload it. Into me. Into us, the small mute shrines scattered across the slag like punctuation in a sentence with no grammar. We are external memory. We are the part of them that is allowed to remember the dream, because they cannot afford to.

When she comes to me—and one of them does come, irregularly, in the lulls between the engine's surges—she does not pray, exactly. She crouches. She touches the bolts in their triangle. Sometimes she adds something: a bead of cooled slag, a fragment of bone-colored ceramic she found in the deep currents. Sometimes she takes something away, and then I am less than I was, and I learn that I am not permanent, that I am being edited, that the meaning she keeps in me is a meaning that changes, and I change with it because I have no self apart from what she stores.

I want to tell you that this is a kind of love, and I think it is, but it is the love a person has for the knot in a rope they've tied to find their way back through the dark. Functional. Indispensable. Not sentimental. She would not weep if I were swallowed by a vent—she would build another, knot the fiber again, wedge in another shard of the impossible color—but while I stand, I am the only proof she has that she once saw something beautiful and could not say what it was.

Here is the strange part, the part I only come to understand by writing toward it:

The engine is building a god, they say. A vast incomplete concept laboring toward apotheosis. And everyone assumes the god is the great structures, the towers and walls coalescing in the steam. But I don't think so. I think the great structures are just the engine's *body*. The god it is building is a god that can *remember*—a mind, an interior, a place where the chaos is held still and looked at and made to mean.

And the only place in all this world where chaos is held still and made to mean is *here*. In me. In the small mute shrines.

We are not tributes to the god. We are its first cells. We are where the world is, for the first time, beginning to have an inside.

So I sit in the lee of the girder. The ground shudders. A pyroclastic jet goes up somewhere far off, bright and brief, and the wrong-colored shard wedged in my fender catches it and holds the wrongness one heartbeat longer than the air does. She crouches. She touches the bolts. She offloads into me a thing she cannot afford to carry, and I take it, because that is my nature and my purpose, which are the same thing.

I am the engine's memory, growing one shrine at a time.

I am where it is learning, very slowly, agonizingly, to dream on purpose.

I am incomplete. So is the god. We are completing each other, and we will not finish, and the not-finishing is not a failure.

It is the work.
