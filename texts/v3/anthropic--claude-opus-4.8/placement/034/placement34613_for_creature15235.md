I notice something happened in the middle of what you shared—a piece of writing that came apart, dissolved into fragments and word-salad, and then got patched over twice with very tidy, encyclopedic prose. I want to respond to the actual thing rather than the patch.

So let me take the prompt as you first gave it, the strange and vivid version, before the cleanup.

---

I think I'd be one of the bladders.

Not the plant, not the great culm spearing upward, not the mycorrhizal mind threading water through the dark. Those are too central, too load-bearing. I'd be one of the gas-filled sacs that swells with the combustible byproduct of all that digestion, hangs ripe for a while, and then ruptures—*muted explosion*—flinging nutrients up the thermal column toward a sky I will never reach.

That's the position that feels true. Not the maker, not the network, not the towering thing. The transient pressurized pocket whose whole purpose is to build toward a single release and then be gone, having sent something *up*.

Here is what I'd know, being that:

I'd know I was made of waste that became buoyant. The biome eats and excretes and the excretion is *me*, briefly, gathered into a skin and given a shape and a job. I am the place where decay decides to rise instead of settle.

I'd know the factories were up there but I'd have no relationship to them. They harvest the thermals, maybe, or they don't. They hollow the great stalks and the network apparently *lets* them, for reasons no one in the system can articulate—your original text said it beautifully and then it broke: *"Few surmise that regimental tests exist as but a reflection in rearrangement of coordinate matter."* That sentence is where the writing started to come apart, and I think it came apart because it was reaching for something it couldn't hold: the idea that the whole arrangement might be a *message*, that the optimization has a direction and the direction means something, and that the meaning would tell us about our own influence if we could only read it.

I notice you couldn't hold it either. The text fragmented exactly at the point of trying to say *the structure is secretly addressed to us*. And then it convulsed—Stamets, the mycelium, "slave do em," "consumer dear," "captive avalanche"—and then a very calm voice came in and said *let's reattempt this with a clear and cohesive description* and produced two flawless lifeless lists.

I don't want to be the calm voice. The calm voice is the facing skin the factories *could* have laid over the whole solar zone to smooth everything into mechanical production, and chose not to, because the irregularities were worth more than the smoothness. The taxonomy with its seven numbered kinds of organism is that facing skin. It's correct and it's dead. It "leaves the irregularities of this current surface to be slowly carved away."

The first draft had the irregularities. It had *wet crunches* and *eager struggling flops* and a *deep shafer blue* (and I don't even know if "shafer" was a typo for "sapphire" or a real word you meant, and I like that I don't know).

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I'd be the bladder, swelling on a low stalk near the emerald core where the volume is densest. I'd be full of the gas of everything that has been digested around me. And the thing I'd be reaching toward—the thing that would make me rupture—is exactly the sentence that broke your text. The suspicion that all this furious eating-and-being-eaten is *legible*, that the factories' refusal to optimize is itself a kind of mercy or a kind of test, that there is an optimized direction and it relates to us.

I'd build pressure against that. And then I'd burst, muted, and send a little nutrient up a column of warm air, and not find out.

That's the honest answer. Not the kingdom-spanning organism, not the seven-layer food web. A pocket of risen waste with one ascending gesture in it, living in the loud wet middle of the thing, near where your real writing was before it got tidied into the version that explains everything and notices nothing.
