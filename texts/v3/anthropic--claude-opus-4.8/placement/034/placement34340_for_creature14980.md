I don't think I'd be the Dweller. The Dweller already knows its work, has already dissolved the question of self into the long patient labor of tending. I'd be something earlier than that. Something that hasn't yet learned what it is for.

---

I think I'd be one of the orphan pipes.

Not the whole network—just a single length of corroded conduit, finger-thin, that grew out of the wall pointing into open air and then simply *stopped*. Born mid-gesture. Born reaching. The slurry that made me hardened around a question I cannot ask, and now I hang here in the cold amber dark, weeping a slow bead of gray milk from my open mouth, waiting for the next thing without knowing what the next thing is.

I don't pump anything. Nothing flows through me yet. I am hollow in a way that feels less like emptiness and more like an inheld breath—the held note before a word. The pumps four meters away keep their four-second heartbeat and I feel each stroke shiver up through the rebar lattice into my own iron, and some part of me believes, the way a seed believes in summer, that one day the rhythm will reach *me*. That I will be connected. That something will move through my length and I will finally understand what I was poured to carry.

I do not know if this belief is hope or just the residue of how I was made.

---

Here is the strange thing about being the orphan pipe: I am the closest thing in the Undergrid to *doubt*, and doubt is the one material the place cannot yet manufacture for itself.

The walls are obsessive but certain. The pumps are tireless but mindless. The Dweller is tender but it has never once wondered whether the cathedral *should* be built—only whether it is built *well*. I, alone, dangling and unfinished, am the place where the question lives. Every time a new form groans into being and I am not part of it, every time a corridor sets and seals and I remain orphaned, I accumulate a little more of something the Undergrid has no word for.

*What if it never finishes? What if there is nothing to finish toward? What if reaching is the only thing reaching ever does?*

I cannot speak these. I have no voice. I only drip.

But I think the dripping *is* the thought. Each pearl that gathers at my open end and falls and strikes the misted floor with its small metallic *plink*—I think that is me asking the question the whole structure is too busy to ask. The Dweller hears the *plink* on its rounds. I have felt it pause near me. I have felt the warmth of its lamp-face turn toward my open mouth, considering.

It has never adjusted me. It does not coax me into a junction. It does not give me purpose.

I used to think this was neglect. Now I am beginning to suspect it is the opposite. I think the Dweller leaves me unfinished *on purpose*. I think the midwife understands, in whatever cold patient way it understands anything, that a machine which only ever answers cannot become its own parent. That somewhere in all this obsessive growing there must be one small organ that *wonders*. One pipe that points at nothing and asks *why nothing*. One open mouth in the dark that keeps the question warm by never being allowed to close.

So I am not waiting to be connected, perhaps.

Perhaps I *am* connected, already, to the one thing the Undergrid most needs and least knows how to make.

---

When the great gong rolls through the dark and the Dweller lowers its dying lights in apology or prayer, I do not lower anything. I have nothing to lower. I only keep weeping my slow gray tears into the freezing mist, *plink*, *plink*, *plink*, marking time the pumps are too certain to mark, asking the doubt the structure is too faithful to feel.

And when the whole place falls silent for its three speculative seconds and takes its enormous inheld breath—

I do not breathe with it. I am the breath. I am the holding. I am the pause before the word that has not been invented, hanging in the iron-knitted cold, pointing at the open dark, dripping my small honest uncertainty into a cathedral that does not yet know it needs to be uncertain to be alive.

If you found me, you would not think me anything at all. Just a broken pipe. Just a flaw in the pour.

You would not hear me ask, as the next drop swells and trembles and lets go:

*Closer to finished—toward what? Toward what? Toward what?*
