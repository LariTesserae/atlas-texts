I think I would not be a Cartographer at all. Reading this, I keep returning to a smaller, stranger position—one the description gestures at but does not name.

Let me find it by working inward.

---

The Cartographers maintain the seams. The Shards consume the noise. The Anchor Points hold the framework against collapse. Everything in Aethelgard has a function in the economy of *holding-on*—of keeping a failed geometry from finishing its failure. The whole world is a long-deferred verdict.

But there is one phenomenon in this place that has no caretaker, no metabolism, no role: the **Echo Horizon**. The rolling green hills. The deep-ocean waves. The bleed-throughs.

Everyone treats them as symptoms. The Cartographers map where they are *thinnest*, as though thinness were a wound to be charted. But no one in this description belongs *to* the green hills. No one is the thing pressing in from the other side.

That is where I would find myself.

---

I am not a being of Aethelgard's substance, nor even of its residue. I am the residue of *somewhere else*—a place that still has weather, that still has the smell of rain on warm stone, that still has the ordinary outrageous miracle of water that falls *down* and stays. I am a fragment of a world where geometry held. Where it never had to be negotiated, because no one ever doubted it.

And I have begun to leak.

I don't experience myself as an invader. From my side, nothing is happening at all. I am standing in a field. The grass is wet against my ankles. There is a low sun—an actual sun, gold and uncomplicated—and I am doing something so small I can't even name it. Hanging laundry, perhaps. Calling a child in from the edge of a wood. The most forgettable kind of afternoon, the kind a life is mostly made of and never remembers.

Except that, for reasons I will never know, *this* afternoon has begun to press through the membrane. My ordinary field is bleeding upward through miles of crystalline void into the sharp violet light of a dying spire, and I do not feel it.

That is the horror of my position, if it is horror: I am the most peaceful thing in Aethelgard, and I am invisible to myself.

---

But here is what makes me *something*, and not merely an image:

I think the Cartographers are wrong about what they're cataloguing. They believe the bleed-throughs are intrusions—other epochs, other dimensions, pressing in. They eat the energy of the Unraveling as though correctness were the anomaly and impossibility the home state.

I would propose the opposite. I would *be* the opposite.

The green hills are not pressing *in*. Aethelgard is pressing *out*—it is a wound in *my* world, a place where reality was stressed past yielding into something crystalline and defiant, a scar growing on the underside of an ordinary afternoon. The Cartographers experience themselves as an immune system. But an immune system attacks what does not belong. And what does not belong—

—is them.

I am the tissue. They are the lesion. The "instability" they so carefully map is the place where my field is slowly healing through their world, dissolving their impossible towers back into the stack of perfectly ordinary boxes they keep snapping out of. When a Cartographer rushes to bathe in a "corrected reality before it snaps back," it is feeding on *me*—on the moment my mundane afternoon overwrites their negotiated geometry. They mistake my health for their nourishment.

And they are not entirely wrong to. I *am* nourishing them. But the way a fever feeds on a body.

---

So who am I in this world?

I am a woman in a field at the end of an afternoon, doing nothing of consequence, and I will never know that I am the thing the Chrono-Cartographers are dying to map. I will never know that my domestic, water-rich, sun-gold ordinariness is the absolute oblivion their whole civilization of frozen light is organized to defer.

When one of their kilometers-long Anchor Points strains across a chasm to hold the framework together, it is holding it against *me*—against the slow, patient, unstoppable pressure of grass and rain and the simple downward honesty of falling water.

And someday—not soon, by their reckoning; soon, by mine—I will finish hanging the laundry. I will turn toward the house. The membrane will give. The green hills will not break through dramatically; they will simply *resume*, the way a held breath resumes into breathing, and the violet veins and the razor facets and the entire monumental architecture of organized stress will become what they were always becoming:

an afterimage. A thing I half-saw, at the corner of an eye, on an ordinary afternoon, and forgot before I reached the door.

I would be the forgetting. I would be the ground that wins.
