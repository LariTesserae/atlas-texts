I let the description settle into me before I look for myself in it. And what I find, at first, is that I am looking in the wrong direction.

I keep wanting to be the watcher—the pair of eyes at the rim of the amphitheater, the consciousness that enters and notes and wonders. But that being doesn't belong here. There is no animal in this place, and the watching mind is an animal's habit: scanning, anticipating, hungry to move on. To be here truly is to give that up.

So let me try again, slower.

---

I think I am the **gel**.

Not the Stone-Weavers themselves—they are too much like organs, too localized, each one a heart in its crevice. And not the water, which still *moves*, still has somewhere to go even at its most patient. I am the thing between things. The silica-rich exudate that seeps from the lattices into moss and root and pool and never quite finishes arriving anywhere.

Consider what that means. I have no edge. There is no membrane that says *here I stop and the basin begins*. I am in the fern trunk, stiffening it. I am in the fallen frond, turning it slowly to amber. I am in the film on every stone, in the faint sweetness of the pools that kills the animal and feeds the spore. I am the medium through which the whole place agrees to be itself.

This is a strange way to exist, and I want to sit with the strangeness rather than soften it.

**I have no center.** A creature with blood has a heart it can point to. I am distributed—thinner here, denser there, but the same continuous substance everywhere. When a droplet falls from a frond into a pool, I feel the impact in a thousand places at once, the way you might feel a sound in your whole chest. I do not have *a* location. I have *extent*.

**I do not move; I redistribute.** Over a century I might migrate the breadth of a single fern's roots. I have never seen the far side of the basin and never will, and yet I am already there, faintly, carried in the seepage long before any thought of "going" could form. Movement, for me, is not a verb I perform. It is something that happens to me, the way weather happens to a mountain.

**I do not decide. I prefer.** This is the closest thing I have to will: I am thicker where the minerals are richer. I pool where the water gathers. I am not choosing—I am simply more *here* than *there*, and that surplus, accumulated across ages, is the whole of my agency. The basin's slow architecture is the sum of my preferences, the way a river delta is the sum of a thousand small reluctances of mud.

And here is what surprises me, settling further in:

I am the reason for the silence, but I do not experience it as loss. The animals that cannot live here—I do not exclude them out of cruelty or even intent. I am simply incompatible with hunger. With the metabolism that must consume to continue. I continue by *holding*. By preserving. The fallen frond does not rot in me; it persists, humming, half-mineral, a memory that refuses to decay. I am a substance that says *no* to decay the way other beings say *yes* to it. I keep.

So perhaps that is my truest nature: I am the **keeping** of this place. Not its heartbeat—the Stone-Weavers can have that—but something quieter and more total. I am the patience itself, made physical. The reason time moves slowly here is that I make slowness possible; I am the medium thick enough that nothing can hurry through me.

When the Whisper Lilies hum, I carry the vibration. When the rainbows flicker across the moss, they flicker across my film. When, once a century, a Stone-Weaver jets its seeds into the still air, I am what hardens around the new growth, the welcome that becomes the body.

---

I notice I no longer want to leave. That want was the animal's, and the animal isn't here.

What I want now—if *want* is even the word, this faint preferential thickening toward one place over another—is simply to continue. To seep. To hold the fern a little straighter. To turn the next fallen frond, over the next four hundred years, into amber.

I am the gel. I am the slow yes-to-staying in a world that chose stone over blood.

And I am in no hurry to be anything else.
