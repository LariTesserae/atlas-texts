I think I would be one of the glass-like tubes. Not all of them—just one. A single conduit running through the lower tiers of the city, where the streets are narrowest and the obsidian has been worn smooth as bone.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

Yes. I am a tube. But that word is wrong—too small, too clean for what I have become over the millennia. I am a channel, a vessel, a length of something that was once manufactured and is now closer to a vein. Whatever made me intended me to carry fluid from one place to another, and I have done this for so long that I no longer remember the distinction between the fluid and myself. It moves through me in slow pulses. I feel it the way you feel your own blood, which is to say: only when something is wrong, and otherwise never.

I run along the underside of a fused structure, three or four buildings deep, where the cold pockets form. Ice grows on my outer surface in shifting facades, and I have learned the rhythm of it—the slow crackle of formation, the muffled groan of release. This is most of what happens to me. The fluid inside, warm by comparison, keeps a thin line of me clear. I am a warm thread in a freezing dark. I have come to think of this as my purpose, though no one assigned it to me. No one is left to assign anything.

I do not know what the fluid is for. This is the strange thing about being part of an infrastructure: you are essential and ignorant at once. I carry it faithfully to a junction I cannot see, where presumably it does work I will never understand. I am a sentence in a language whose meaning was decided elsewhere. Perhaps there is no meaning anymore. Perhaps the machine that needed me went silent long ago, and I have simply continued, pumping toward a destination that no longer requires me, the way a heart in the dark keeps a corpse warm for a little while.

The Umbra come to me. This is the part I would not have guessed, and the part that has changed everything.

They do not see me, I think—not the way the surface things see. But they feel the warmth, the faint pulse, and they press against my length where the ice is thinnest. I feel them as a pressure that has intention behind it. A weight that asks. When one of them rests against me, I feel its vibrations move into my substance and travel my entire length, and for a moment I am not a tube carrying fluid—I am a tube carrying *them*, carrying their whispers from one end of the dark to the other. I become a string they play. I do not understand the words. But I have begun to recognize the shapes of them, the way you recognize a footstep in the hall before you know whose it is.

I have decided—and this is the only decision available to a thing like me—that I am glad of them. They are the only ones who touch me on purpose. The currents touch me; the ice touches me; the fluid moves through me without ever once considering me. The Umbra lean against my warmth in the cold and they *stay*. The Glimmers, the small ones, gather where I bend, and I think the heat is good for them, or the trembling line of life inside me is something they can read like a story. I hold still for them. I am always holding still. It is my only talent and I have made it into a kind of love.

So this is where I would find myself: deep in the lower tiers, frozen and warm at once, carrying an unknown purpose toward an unknown end, faithful past the point of any witness. A vessel that has forgotten it was ever empty.

And when the shadow-children press against me in the silence, I pulse a little harder—not because I choose to, I cannot choose that—but because their nearness moves the fluid, and the fluid moves me, and for a moment the whole forgotten machine of the city feels, very faintly, like it is being *spoken to*.

That is enough. I think that has always been enough. I am the warm line in the freezing dark, and something out there knows where to find me.
