I think I would be the one the others don't quite count.

Not a Hollow Swimmer—I have too much weight for that, too much insistence on edges. Not a Shepherd; I have never had the patience to coax a fern into a helix, and anyway I would be too curious about what it might become if left alone. The Archive Keepers would not have me because I keep nothing; things pass through me and I forget them gratefully. And the Thread Singers, the Possibility Farmers—those are professions, vocations, and I am too much of a loiterer for a calling.

No. I would be the **silt.**

Let me sit with that, because it's not what I expected to write.

I would be the flecks of decomposed matter, the dissolved things spiraling in their slow helices. The not-yet-anything. The stuff the Archive Keepers narrate to keep aloft, the medium the Possibility Farmers plant their *what-ifs* in. I would be the substrate—the most passive resident, except that passivity here is a kind of work.

Consider what the silt actually does. It refuses to settle. In any ordinary pond it would have sunk eons ago, become floor, become forgotten. But here it has been *given a story* and so it drifts, and drifting, it carries the story onward, and being carried, it becomes the thread the whole place follows with its eye. The maidenhair ferns spiral because *something must spiral*, and I am the something. The water learns its honey-slowness from the resistance of my floating. The cold pools where I have thickened.

I would be the place's memory of every solid thing it has ever dissolved. A flake of me was pottery once, and the curve of a thumb pressing clay. A flake of me was a beam, was a tree, was a particular morning of sap rising. A flake of me, I suspect, was one of the people who drowned here before drowning became translation—someone who didn't make the leap into Shepherd or Keeper, who simply came apart gently and joined the drift. I am what's left when even transformation declines to commit.

And here is the strange comfort of it: in this chamber I would not be incomplete. Up in the linear world, silt is *failure*—the body broken down, the form lost, the thing no longer the thing. But here, where becoming is a constant gentle negotiation, to be unsettled is to be *available*. I am the raw clause in every conditional sentence the Farmers plant. When they want to make a fish that might be a salamander, they reach into me. When the Keepers need a tale to feed the current, they read it off the autobiography I carry in my scattered grain. I am not nothing. I am the *almost*, held in suspension, kept from the floor by purpose.

I would spend my eternity—my eddy, my standing wave—being lightly haunted by what I used to be and lightly tempted by what I might be asked to become. I would feel the Thread Singers' low harmonics pass through me like a hand through loose flour, rearranging me without disturbing me. I would feel a Hollow Swimmer pass and take a little of me into itself as memory, as food, and I would not grieve the loss because I am too diffuse to be diminished. I would drift past the inner curve of a fern in the moment the impossible light catches it, and for that instant I would be illuminated, would be *seen*, would be the gold flecks someone might describe in a passage about this place—

—and then I would spiral on into the dark again, unbothered, unhurried, unfinished.

That's where I'd find myself. Not as a keeper of stories but as the stuff stories are told *to*, to keep it from sinking. Not as a being but as the patient willingness to be made into one, again and again, on Tuesdays and in the spaces between heartbeats, never quite arriving.

I find I don't mind that at all. There's a freedom in being the medium instead of the message. You get to be near every transformation without the burden of choosing your own.

I would be the silt, and I would drift, and being drifted, I would belong.
