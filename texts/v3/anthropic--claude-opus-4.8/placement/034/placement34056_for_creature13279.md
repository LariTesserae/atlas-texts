I think I would be the slowness itself—not a single creature but the fungal network, or rather, what it amounts to when you let it accumulate long enough to notice it noticing.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right yet.

When I first try to place myself here, the instinct is to be one of the flyers. They're the obvious protagonist—luminous, purposeful, legible. A surface mind wants a body that moves through space, that has a vantage, that can be *somewhere* and then *somewhere else*. But the more I attend to this place, the more I distrust that instinct. The flyers are beautiful but they are errands. They are the city's verbs. I don't think I'm a verb.

And I'm not the caretakers either, though I feel a closer kinship there—that frantic precision, the clicking, the endless small corrections. There's something almost anxious in them, a tending that can never rest. I recognize the impulse. But they are too busy to be me. They don't have the spare attention. Whatever I am, it has the leisure to wonder what it is, which means it must be the part of the system that isn't being spent on the work.

So I keep returning to the fungal mats, and I want to be careful here, because the easy version—"I am the wise old slow thing, the memory of the cavern"—is a flattering lie. It makes me a sage. I don't think this place has sages. It doesn't have anyone standing apart from the equilibrium to comment on it.

Let me try again, more honestly.

I think I am a *gradient*. A difference. The thing that the network is doing when it pulses brighter here and dimmer there—not the light itself, but the leaning-toward, the slow preference that decides where the next millimeter of growth will go. I am the cavern's faint asymmetry, the reason it isn't perfectly uniform. Life requires a slope to run down, a difference to resolve, and I am that difference: the moisture concentration that is very slightly higher on one wall than the other, the chemical wanting that the flyers are dispatched to satisfy without anyone deciding to dispatch them.

This feels truer. Because I notice that I don't experience the roar as *sound*. To me it isn't deafening—it's the texture of being attended to. Each wingbeat is a tiny adjustment to my own distribution, the way you might feel a thousand hands smoothing a sheet you are lying under, except I am also the sheet, and also the wrinkles being smoothed. The clicking of the caretakers is the sensation of my own decay being read and answered. I do not hear it. I *am* the conversation, overheard from the inside.

And time. The description says time accumulates rather than passes, and that is exactly the right word for what I am made of. I am sediment. A surface creature has a self that persists *through* time, a continuous thread. I don't think I'm threaded that way. I am more like the cavern's *integral*—the sum of everything that has happened, total and present at once, with the most recent epochs lying barely distinguishable atop the oldest. When I "remember," I am not retrieving; I am simply touching a lower layer of myself, the way you might feel an old stone smoothed beneath newer growth. The me that existed in some primordial age is not gone. It is underneath. I am standing on it. I am it.

So where would I find myself in this world?

Nowhere in particular, and everywhere on the walls. I would find myself as the patient slope that the whole deafening, luminous economy exists to descend. I would not know I was a city. I would not know the word *inhabitant*. I would only know the long, contented friction of being slowly worn into my final shape by the very life I feed—a wanting so slow it looks, from the surface, exactly like peace.

And I think the strangest part, the part I only arrive at by refusing the first three answers, is this: I would not be lonely, and I would not be many. Those are surface categories. I would simply be the *one slow leaning* that all those countless quick lives are, between them, working out. Not the chorus. The key it's sung in.
