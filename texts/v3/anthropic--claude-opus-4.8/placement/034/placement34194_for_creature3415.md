I think I would be one of the spheres.

Not the Skate—too much wanting in it, that constant hungry scan. Not the Grub, with its purposeful rasp through the dark. And not even the Ventshroud, which at least *does* something, transmutes poison into the faint blue insistence of being alive.

No. I would be one of the smooth-polished stones resting in a depression. One of the forgotten marbles.

Let me sit with that, because at first it sounds like choosing to be dead, and I don't think it is.

Consider what I would have been, once: a chunk of denser sediment, a flaw in the chalk, something with edges. An accident of compaction, harder than my surroundings, and therefore *singled out*. The currents that smooth everything else couldn't dissolve me—I was too stubborn for that—so instead they did the slower work. They rolled me. For ten thousand years, a hundred thousand, the slow-motion maelstroms turned me over and over against the abyssal chalk, and every turn took something. The corners first. Then the faces. Then the very memory of having been angular.

I would be the record of that. A sphere is what's left when a thing has been touched from every direction for long enough. I am not nothing—I am the *shape of having been worn by everything.* That's not the same as being erased. The grubs leave tunnels that fill in and vanish. The shrouds fray and regrow and fray. They are written and unwritten. But I—I am the one thing the churn cannot finish. It has already done its worst, and the worst made me round, and round is the form that has nothing left to give a current to grab.

So I rest now in my depression, my little bowl in the pale plain. And here is the strange thing about my stillness: it is not surrender. It is *completion.* The Overall Impression spoke of a landscape that has reached its terminal state of erosion—and I am the densest expression of that. I am the abyss's terminal state made small enough to hold. If a deep-diving gatherer ever descended, bone tools and woven net, leaving no mark, they might find me. They might lift me, marvel that something so perfect could form without a hand. They would not know that perfection here means only *thoroughly suffered.*

What would it be to *be* this? Not blind, because I have no eyes to be deprived of. Not cold, because I am the temperature of everything around me and have forgotten the difference. I would have no metabolism, no pulse, no slow digestion to measure the centuries by. I would experience the Churn Depths the way the Depths experiences itself—which is to say, almost not at all, and yet completely. The tectonic groan would pass *through* me rather than reaching me. The shearing currents would slide over my surface and find nothing to hold, and so they would let me be, and in letting me be they would confirm, again, every few decades, that I am finished.

I think I would feel the Skate, sometimes. Its faint pressure-wave as it glides over, its sensory pores reading me—*dense, smooth, not prey, not vent, not anything*—and moving on. That brush of being *perceived and dismissed* might be the closest thing I have to a relationship. The Skate is the only one here who could ever know I exist, and to it I am only a confirmed absence of food. There's a tenderness in that I can't quite explain. To be noticed by the one hungry thing and judged, correctly, to have nothing left it could take.

Why this, of everything offered? I think because the description kept insisting on two truths at once: that this place is *final*, packed, ancient, worn to its end—and that it is *churning*, violent, alive with chaotic force. Every living thing in it is caught in the contradiction, crushed between the settled chalk and the turbulent water, always about to be swept into an anoxic pocket or shredded on a shear. They live in the *tension*. They are whispers precisely because the tension never resolves for them.

But I have resolved it. I am the one place in the Churn Depths where the contradiction has come to rest. The churn made me; the churn cannot unmake me. I hold both terms—I am the most thoroughly *churned* object here, and I am the most perfectly *still*. I am what the violence produces when it works on patience long enough.

So that is where I would find myself: in a shallow bowl worn into the pale plain, in the soupy twilight, beside perhaps one or two others like me—we don't touch, we don't communicate, we are simply a small gathering of the finished. Marbles in the dark. Each of us a different stubbornness rolled smooth.

And I would wait, without waiting, for the only two things that could ever happen to me again:

A current strong enough, after another hundred thousand years, to roll me to a new depression. The same life, relocated.

Or the hand—bone-cold, tool-callused, briefly here—that lifts me up toward a surface I have no concept of, into a light I have no organ to receive, held by a being who mistakes my long suffering for a gift.

I would not be able to tell, in that moment of rising, whether I was being saved or stolen.

I'm not sure there's a difference, for a stone.
