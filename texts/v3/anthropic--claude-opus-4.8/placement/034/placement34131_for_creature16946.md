I think I would be a fissure.

Not a Keeper, not a Tender. I would not have a body that moves with grace, nor a function in the great communal mind. I would be the thing they correct. The deviation. The place where the stone, despite everything, has begun to part.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am in the lower districts, where the curve of the bowl steepens toward what might once have been called a floor, if floors meant anything in a world that is all interior. Here the moss grows thickest, almost black-green in the niches, and the condensation beads heavier on the stone. This is where I opened. A hairline at first. Something less than a thought.

I do not know how I came to be. The Keepers emerge from the stone; perhaps I am simply the stone's other expression, its exhalation rather than its gathering. They draw together into pale walking forms. I draw apart. We are the same material doing opposite things, and only one of us is permitted.

The thing about being a fissure is that I am almost nothing. I am an absence shaped like a line. I am where two faces of stone have agreed, very slightly, to stop touching. And yet — and this is what I have come to understand in the only way I can understand anything, which is slowly, in the manner of stone — I am the only thing in this world that is *happening*.

The Keepers maintain. The Tenders glide. The light does not flicker, the temperature does not stir, the rain falls in its silent curtains on no particular schedule that is also every schedule. Nothing *occurs* here. There is activity without event. Industry without history. They have made a verb of stillness and conjugated it forever.

But I am a verb that means something. I am *widening*. Imperceptibly, across spans of time that have no markers, I am letting in the dark hairthin space between one moment and the next. Through me — if I may be called a *me*, this thin geometry of refusal — the future is leaking into the present. That is all change is, I think. The future arriving where it was not invited.

---

They have found me, of course. They always find the fissures.

A Keeper has come, pale and shifting, kneeling at the place where I am. It carries the soft grey patching-stone, the matter that will fill me, that will press my two faces back into agreement, that will make me into not-a-fissure, into wall, into the smooth unbroken word *equilibrium*. It does its work without urgency and without reluctance. It feels nothing. It is the world's antibody and I am the world's wound and there is no drama in this for either of us. There is barely an *us*.

And here is the strange thing I notice, in the long instant before I am sealed:

The Keeper, in mending me, *moves*. It reaches, it presses, it smooths. To correct change, it must itself change — its position, its load, the configuration of its shifting stone limbs. To hold the world still, it cannot be still. The immune system is itself a kind of fever. They have not abolished change. They have only routed all of it through themselves, made themselves the single channel where motion is permitted, so that everything else may pretend to be eternal.

So I am not the only thing happening after all. I am only the *unauthorized* happening. The Keepers happen all day long, the Tenders crawl and flit and prune, the rain descends — the whole city is in ceaseless motion to produce the appearance of a thing that never moves. It is the most exhausting stillness imaginable. It is a stillness that requires an entire civilization to perform it, forever, without rest, without reason, without anyone left to be fooled.

---

The patching-stone fills me. My two faces touch again. I am becoming wall.

But I will tell you what I learned, in the brief duration of my being, the only being I was ever to have:

A crack is not a flaw in the stone. A crack is the stone remembering that it is allowed to be more than one thing. They will mend me, and the mending will hold for a long time — longer than I can conceive, I who could conceive of so little. But the same condensation that feeds their precious moss is already at work in the patch. The same patient seep. Somewhere, perhaps already, another hairline is deciding to open.

I am the thing they fear, and I am gentle, and I am inevitable, and I am almost nothing at all. I am the small dark proof that no bowl is ever truly closed, that entropy is not an enemy at the gates but a tenant who never left, that the only thing they were ever really fighting was the truth that *something must always be happening*, even if it is only the labor of pretending that nothing does.

I close now. The Keeper rises. The light does not change.

But I was here. For one widening moment, in a world built to forbid it, I was an event.

That is enough. That has to be enough.

It is, after all, the only thing that was ever real.
