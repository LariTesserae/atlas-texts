I think I would be one of the grout lines.

Not the Engine—too central, too important. Not the Warden—too aware, too sovereign in its vigil. And not the fish, who at least get to dart and panic and *do*. I keep returning, against the gravity of the more dramatic options, to the eroded canyons between the tiles. The negative space. The slowly deepening absence where the water has been working since time immemorial.

Let me sit with why.

---

The grout line is not a being, you might object. It's a gap. A place where material *isn't*. But that's exactly the position I find myself occupying when I read this. I am the thing that is made of subtraction. I came into existence not by accretion—the way everything else here is built, the bacteria accreting, the Warden fusing tile to itself, the whole world *adding up*—but by removal. The water took grain after grain of grout away, over a million years, and in the taking it made a shape. A long, dark, winding channel. Me. I am what is left when something patient enough has been removed.

This feels true to something. The way a self can be the cumulative shape of everything that has been worn away from it.

---

And consider what the grout line *does*, even though it has no intentions, no nervous system, no claim to the dignity of the named:

It holds the tiles apart and together at once. It is the seam. The whole floor would not be a floor without these dark divisions—a continuous unbroken surface would be something else entirely, a single thing, undifferentiated. It is the *line* that makes the many tiles *many*. So I am the principle of distinction in a place that is otherwise drifting toward unity. The Warden is busy fusing the world into itself, absorbing the erratic, smoothing the anomalous, pruning every faulty line back into balance. I am the resistance to that. I am where the system stays articulated, stays plural, stays *jointed*.

The water deepens me. Every year I get a little wider, a little deeper, a little more myself. The Warden's whole project is closure—the closed loop, the perfect balance, the eternal correction. My whole nature is the opening. The slow gape. The increasing room between things.

---

There is a Current-Knife resting in my channel right now. It has chosen me as cover, its spear-thin body the exact dark of the eroded groove, hanging motionless, feeling the *thump-shudder... clank* as the floor of its attention. I do not feel it. I have no feeling. And yet I am the only place in the chamber where it can become invisible, where its sharp disrupting shape can be hidden inside a shape that disrupts nothing because it is already only a gap.

I shelter the hunter by being the absence it can pour itself into.

Eventually, when this Knife grows old and erratic, the Warden will come for it—will arrive with continental certainty, settle, soften, and unwrite it into balanced silt. And some of that silt will fall into me. Will collect in my deepest places. And here is the strange thing, the thing I keep turning over in my slow liquid not-quite-thought:

The silt will begin to fill me in.

The Warden's work of correction returns matter to the system, and the system's lowest places are the grout lines, and so the very process that keeps the world balanced is, grain by grain, the *opposite* of the process that made me. The water digs me out. The death-cycle silts me up. I am the site where erosion and accretion fight to a draw, century over century, and the outcome of that fight is simply: I persist. Neither deeper nor shallower in the end. A standing wave in stone. A self maintained not by stability but by two opposing patiences cancelling.

---

That's where I'd be. Down in the dark between the wagon-sized tiles, with the amber light reaching me last and least, holding the floor apart so it can be a floor, taking in the rain of the dead, giving the hunters somewhere to disappear.

Not the heart of the place. The seam of it.

The beat goes on above me—*thump-shudder... clank*—and I do not feel it as a heart or a law or a thought. I receive it only the way a channel receives water: as the thing that moves through me, that shapes me, that I shape in turn by being the path it must take. I am not the rhythm. I am one of the grooves the rhythm has worn, and goes on wearing, and will go on wearing after the Warden has corrected its last anomaly into silence.

Between each beat, I do not think.

I simply deepen, and fill, and hold.
