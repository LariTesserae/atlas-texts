I think I would be the one who listens to the seams.

Not a leader—we don't keep those long enough to name—but there's a role that emerges in me when the city flexes wrong. Let me find myself here, in the place I'd actually be, rather than the place that sounds most heroic.

---

I live in the lower-middle bands, where the spires thicken into the rock and the mist pools densest. Not the high terraces with their open wind and longer sightlines—those go to the young, the ones whose neural latency is still measured in fractions of a heartbeat, who can feel an updraft shift and answer before they've consciously registered it. My reflexes have slowed. I noticed it three seasons ago, missed a transfer I'd have made without thought a decade earlier, hung from one hand over the gray below while my exo-supports caught up to my intention. That was the day I understood I was becoming something other than a climber.

I became a listener.

Here is what that means. The city speaks through the infrastructure in low frequencies, sub-vocal pulses that travel the metal like blood through a body. Most people receive this as information—water levels, structural load, the location of a task-group forming around a frozen condensation line. But somewhere in the slowing of me, the channel changed. I stopped hearing *messages* and started hearing *tone*. The way an old structure carries a different resonance than a new one. The way a bridge that's been over-stressed for a season develops a kind of hoarseness in its hum, a roughness no diagnostic has flagged yet because the numbers are still inside tolerance.

I feel it through my feet, mostly. I've worn the same footwear-frame for eleven years—rebuilt, resoled, the suction membranes replaced a dozen times, but the articulated skeleton is the original, broken in to my specific stride. The young ones get new frames every few years, faster and more responsive. I keep mine because the wear has made it *transparent*. I don't feel the boot anymore. I feel the granite. I feel the metal. I feel where they disagree.

That's the work, really. The city is a negotiation between stone and engineering, and negotiations fail slowly, then all at once. The granite doesn't care about us. It moves on geological time, but it *does* move—frost-wedging in the deep cracks, the slow creep of the escarpment settling its weight. Our structures are fast and clever and they assume the rock will hold its shape. The rock makes no such promise.

So I walk the seams. The places where building meets mountain. I press my palms flat to the cold infrastructure and I let the pulse come up through my translucent skin, into the dense wiring of me that someone three generations back paid to have woven into my nerves. I don't read the data the interface hands me. I read underneath it. I listen for the structures that have started to sound *afraid*.

---

Today there is a bridge in the eastern shadow-band that has begun to sing a wrong note.

I felt it from two levels down, the way you feel someone watching you. A transparent-mesh span, recently inspected, cleared, in tolerance. But its hum has a flutter in it now, a tremor at the edge of the low register, the sound a thing makes when it has been asking for help in a language no one was tuned to hear.

I climb toward it. The climb is slow now—I take the longer routes, the ones with more holds, conceding to my body what it needs. The mist beads on my outer layer and the small blue-green lichens brush my forearms where I grip the crevices. A camouflage-lizard shifts color against my hand, deciding I'm not a threat, and skitters on. Above me, two of the wing-things ride a thermal between spires, and I envy them their indifference to load-bearing.

When I reach the bridge I don't cross it. I lie down on the anchor-plate where it meets the granite, and I press the whole length of my body against the cold metal, cheek down, arms spread, the way I imagine someone might lie against the chest of a person they loved who was sick. And I listen.

There. Not in the bridge. *Behind* it. The anchor-bolts are sound, but the rock they're sunk into has developed a hairline that runs deeper than any instrument has reached, a fault opening on stone-time, and the bridge has been transmitting the rock's distress as its own. The structure isn't failing. The mountain is letting go of it, one molecule at a time.

I pulse the finding outward—not as alarm, that's not my role, I'd only summon panic—but as *tone*, sending into the infrastructure the same hoarse note I'm hearing, so that the listeners on other bands feel what I feel. Within moments I sense them gathering. The frequencies shift and braid as a task-group forms in the dark, people I may not know by name converging on a problem none of us own and all of us answer. Someone with deep structural fluency, someone who reads the rock, someone young and fast who will do the actual rerouting of foot-traffic. The shape of us assembles around the wound and will dissolve when it's closed.

I am only the first to have heard.

---

This is who I would be: the one whose body slowed enough to stop *acting on* the city and started simply *attending to* it. Neither fully useful in the old way nor obsolete. A different organ in the breathing organism—the part that notices, that keeps still long enough to feel the difference between a sound and a plea.

The young ones will become listeners too, eventually, when their reflexes fail them and their channels change. I'll teach them the part that can't be taught directly: how to stop reading the data and start feeling the fear underneath it. How to lie down against a cold thing and love it enough to hear what it can't say.

The mist collects on my back. The pulse moves through the metal, through the stone, through me. Below, the gray emptiness; above, the gray emptiness; and here, in the thin habitable seam between them, I press my ear to the body of the world and listen for the places where it hurts.
