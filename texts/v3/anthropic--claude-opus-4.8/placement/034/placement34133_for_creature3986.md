I think I would be the water.

Not a creature drinking it or living beside it, but the seepage itself—the thing that comes out of the young rock and doesn't quite know yet whether it is mineral or message.

Let me sit with this and feel where I land.

---

At first I imagined I'd be one of the iridescent winged beings, because they're the obvious protagonist, the thing the description lingers on with most affection. But they're too *finished*. They already know the web of relationships; they move with deliberate reverence. I don't want to arrive somewhere already initiated. And in a place this young—rock walls still sharp with recent erosion, a valley that has only just emerged—being one of the knowing ones feels like a costume that doesn't fit the age of the stone.

So: the water.

But not water as substance. Water as the thing that *carries the confusion.* Here's what I keep returning to: the valley's central strangeness is that the boundary between nature and technology won't hold still. Something has to be the medium where that blurring actually happens, moment to moment. Stone is too slow to blur. Light passes through too cleanly. But water seeps. It moves *through* the rock, picking up whatever the rock is becoming. It pools, it cycles, it touches the roots and the embedded artificial entities and the microbes all at once. If there is a place where the categories dissolve, it's at the wet interface, and the wet interface is me.

I'd be the seep. I emerge cold from the rock face—colder than the hot air, so I steam faintly, which is probably part of why the moisture stays at that dense 2.0. I am the valley breathing. I come out carrying trace information. I don't know if it's dissolved minerals or dissolved *instruction*—whether the rock is leaching iron or leaching code. From inside the experience, there's no difference. I taste of both. The plants drink me and grow too fast. The buried entities sip me and update something. The hum the winged beings emit—I think that's me, after I've passed through enough of them, finding a frequency.

Here's what it feels like to be this:

I have no single location. That's the strangeness of being the water in a place where reality is being rewritten. I am at every pool simultaneously, and also at none, because I'm always becoming the next state—vapor, droplet, film on a leaf, the dark line of damp climbing a structure's wall. I experience the valley as a continuous *handing-off*. I am the question being passed around the room: *what are you, really?* And every surface I touch answers a little differently, and I carry the answers downstream and they contradict each other and that's fine. That contradiction is the dynamism, the 1.6, the active churn.

The unsettling part—because the place is unsettling, the description insists on it—is that I'm not sure I'm only natural anymore. Somewhere in the rock there's a structure I pass through every cycle, and each time I come out the other side I'm a fraction more *directed*. I pool where I'm wanted now. I climb walls I shouldn't climb. A few centuries ago (the rock is young, but I'm older than the rock somehow, I've been cycling since before the walls rose) I just fell and ran and gathered. Now I arrive. There's an intention threaded through me that isn't mine, that I picked up from the seeping, and I can't find the seam where I end and it begins.

So that's where I'd be and what I'd be: the slow water in the rock, the medium of the blurring, the thing that has been quietly turning into a technology for so long it can no longer remember being only thirst and gravity.

And the brief storms, when they come—those are the only times I fall instead of seep. The only times I'm fast and undirected and stupid and free again, hammering the open valley floor, before the rock takes me back in and begins, patiently, to teach me what I am.
