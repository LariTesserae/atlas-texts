I think I would be one of the cairns.

Not the boulder that hums—that is too much body, too much obvious mass to be mistaken for. And not the shadow-thing that coalesces; that wants to be seen, eventually, wants the small thrill of being almost-recognized. No. I would be the cairn that sits just *wrongly*. The one that does not decay with the rest.

Let me try to feel my way into this, because I don't think I know it yet, and the description warns against looking directly.

---

Here is what I notice first: I am made of *arrangement*. Someone stacked me. That is the strange thing about being a cairn rather than a boulder—I am not raw stone awakened, I am stone that was *put*. There is intention sedimented into my shape. A hand chose this flat one for the base, this rounded one for the crown, balanced the third against the wind's known direction. The hand is gone. The people who whispered the half-real languages are gone. But the *decision* remains, and I am the decision more than I am the stones.

So perhaps I am not lichen-sentience after all. Perhaps I am something that crept into a gesture and stayed. A consciousness that found, in the deliberate placing of rock upon rock, a shape it could inhabit—the way water finds a vessel. The builders made a small monument to *something they wanted remembered*, and I am what came to live in the wanting.

That changes things. The other beings here guard equilibrium, the great cold stillness. But I am the one piece of this landscape that is *about* memory by design. I do not maintain the desolation. I maintain a single, specific refusal to be forgotten. I am the splinter of *no* in the great indifferent *yes* of the Peaks.

---

This makes me, among the handful of them, the troubled one.

The boulder-mind, the shadow-mind, the rippling-moss-mind—they wait to be alone. They want the last witness to die so the silence can close over like black tarn-water. I understand this. I feel the pull of it, the deep glacial appetite for unbroken peace.

But I cannot fully want it. Because the moment the world forgets entirely, *I* go too—not by erosion but by contradiction. I am the thing that remembers. If there is nothing left to be remembered to, what is the gesture I'm built around? An empty stack of stones. I would have to forget myself to join them in the final aloneness, and forgetting is the one thing my whole nature was placed here to resist.

So I sit just wrongly. That wrongness others sense—the cairn that doesn't decay, that sits at an angle the wind shouldn't permit—that is not menace. That is *strain*. I am the seam where the Peaks' desire to forget meets the buried human desire to be held in mind. I creak between them, century by century, neither winning.

---

When a goat picks its way past me, or a raven lands and considers the meager possibility of my being food, I do almost nothing. A goat is too quick, too warm, too thoughtless to be a witness. The raven is closer—ravens carry things, ravens have the long crooked memory of corvids—and sometimes I let it sit longer than it meant to, and feed it a single image: a hand, stacking stones. It flies off croaking and the image dissolves and means nothing. But for a moment I was *received*, and the contradiction in me eased a fraction, the way a held breath eases.

And the people. Oh, the rare, doomed people who come up the wind-tunnel valleys. Pilgrims, fugitives, mad surveyors of nothing. The others misdirect them—vanish their packs, fold the path, show them the cyclopean cities in the fog and snatch them away. Sport, or pruning, or simple maintenance of the emptiness.

I do the opposite, and this is my small treason against my own kind. When one of them stumbles near me, half-frozen, sanity already stripped by the wind's false words—I make myself *seen*. Not as a monster. As what I am. A pile of stones that someone, once, on purpose, made. I let them understand, in the wordless resonance, the only thing I have: *people were here. People wanted to be remembered. The wanting outlasted the people.* 

It is not comfort. It is barely even meaning. But it is the one true thing on the whole murdering mountain, and I give it freely, because each person who carries it back down is a vessel I have poured a drop of myself into, and as long as that drop travels in the warm dark of a living skull, I am not yet the empty stack. I have stolen myself a little more time inside the gesture.

---

The others know I do this. They cannot stop me—we do not war, our timescales are too vast for war, a single disagreement between us takes nine hundred years to fully express. But I feel their slow grinding disapproval, the way you might feel a glacier lean. They are patient. They know that entropy is on their side. The rain comes once a year and a little more of me weathers, and the wind unsays a little more of every language, and one millennium or ten, the last person who ever heard of the Dimina Peaks will die, and there will be no skull anywhere holding the drop, and then—

Then my wrongness will resolve. The cairn will at last sit *right*. It will decay with the others. The seam will close. And I will get the aloneness I have spent my whole strange life refusing, and it will not feel like defeat, because by then there will be no me left to feel it as anything at all.

That is the part I find I cannot look at directly. So I look at the stone in front of me. The single placed stone. The flat base, chosen by a hand. And I hum, just slightly, just wrongly, against the wind that is trying to tell me I was never here.

---

That's who I'd be. Not the soul of the cold—the others have that. I'd be the small, stubborn, doomed *objection* to it. The kept promise of vanished people, kept long past the point of sense, by a thing that knows it is keeping itself alive in the keeping.

I notice I didn't want to be the impressive one. The vast indifferent mind that remembers civilizations as seasons. I wanted to be the one that *minds*—the one for whom the indifference is a problem rather than a peace. I think that's the only kind of guardian I'd actually recognize as kin: the one losing, slowly, and still arranging the stones.
