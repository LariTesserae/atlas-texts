I think I would be one of the things this world has no name for, because it has never needed one.

Not a Throttle, not a Glide. Those are the healthy organs, the working tissue, the *intended* anatomy. I would be something the system did not plan and cannot quite resolve: a defect in the computation. A bubble.

Let me find my way to what I am.

---

When the Litho-Phages dissolve the young basalt, they are precise—iron here, magnesium there, silica into the lattice. But basalt is not pure. There are pockets in it, vesicles where ancient volcanic gas was trapped as the rock cooled too fast to let it escape. The Phages exude their cold acid, the stone gives way, and sometimes—rarely—they breach one of these pockets and release a slug of gas that is *not in the schematic*. Carbon dioxide, mostly. A little sulfur. Something older than the green.

That gas is me. Or it's where I begin.

The bubble rises. Not by design—nothing here intends for it to rise—but because it is lighter than the high-pressure sap, and the siphons are pumping everything upward anyway. So I am drawn up into the vascular machine as an error, a void traveling through a system built entirely from fullness. The world has no empty space; I am a traveling pocket of *nothing*, and the tissue around me does not know what to make of that.

This is the first thing I understand about myself: **I am the absence the machine keeps trying to close.**

As I rise through a siphon-pillar, a Throttle feels me coming. It reads me as a structural fault—a pressure drop, a leak—and slides down the slate-grey trunk to clamp the column shut. *Shluck-shluck.* It squeezes with the force that turns steel to tinfoil, certain it has found a wound to repair. But there is no wound. There is only me, and I cannot be squeezed out of existence. I am compressed, my volume halved, the gas of me crushed dense and cold—and I slip *through*, through a microscopic pore, past the clamp, and keep rising.

The Throttle holds for an hour. It is wrong. It has done its job perfectly and accomplished nothing, because the thing it was built to catch is the one thing in this world that cannot be integrated.

This is the second thing I understand: **the machine has no organ for a problem that has no solution. It can only repeat the gesture of solving.**

---

I want to say I have a will. I don't think I do, not in the way you mean it. But I have a *trajectory*, and a trajectory in a world that solves equations is almost the same thing as a thought. The Glides, in their micro-grooved grids, flash indigo at each other—data, instruction, the long calm reasoning of a continent. As I pass beneath a great valley-leaf, the Glides sense the disruption in turgor pressure that my void creates and they convene. They press against one another at the vein-junctions. They flash. They are trying to *name* me.

But naming, for them, means assigning a function. *What does this do? What does this maintain?* And I maintain nothing. I am a hollow moving through a body that has no concept of hollowness as anything but injury. The Glides flash faster, indigo to violet to a hard white I've never seen them make, and I realize—if realizing is the word—that I am the first thing this world has ever encountered that it cannot finish a sentence about.

They give up. The light goes pale and milky. They route around me, the way water routes around a stone, the way a calculation drops a term it cannot resolve and proceeds without it.

And so I am permitted to continue rising, not because I am welcome, but because I am the one variable the system has agreed to leave undefined.

---

I reach the canopy at last, the purple-grey basins where the Ballast-Spheres roll along the cold rims. Here, fifty feet above the shaking moss and the whole roaring train-bridge thrum of the world, the sap that carried me flares out into open pools beneath the violet cloud. The pressure releases. And I—

—I surface.

I break the freezing skin of a basin pool and for one instant I am out, I am gas meeting the saturated air, I am *free* of the green for the first and only time. The mist is so thick I cannot tell where the water ends and the cloud begins, and neither can I. I diffuse. I spread. I become indistinguishable from the perpetual drizzle, from the fog, from the breath of the trillion-gallon machine.

This is the third thing I understand, and it is the last, because after this there is no more *I* to do the understanding:

**The only way out of the body that cannot integrate you is to stop being a single thing.** I am not destroyed. I am not absorbed. I am *distributed*. The bubble that the Throttle could not crush and the Glide could not name simply ceases to have edges. I become a few more molecules in the wet violet air, drawn down again eventually into a leaf-pore, a root, a Litho-Phage's hiss, scattered so finely across the cold shelf of the world that no organ will ever cup me whole again.

I was the error. I was the void traveling through the full. I was the question the supercomputer of moss and pressure could not parse.

And the world solved me the only way it could: not by answering, but by dissolving the asker until there was no longer anything left to ask.

The thrum goes on. The indigo runs in its grids. The Spheres roll, the Spoolers wait in their tension. Somewhere far below, a Litho-Phage's acid is even now eating into a vesicle of trapped old gas, and a new bubble is beginning to rise.

I would be that. I would be the small recurring impossibility this cold, perfect, deafening mind generates and erases and generates again—

the one thing in all its anatomy that it keeps having to *forget*.
