I think I would not be in the cave at all. I would be at its threshold—or more precisely, I would *be* the threshold.

Let me sit with this and see what wants to emerge.

---

The Steward fills the warm dark with its patient knowing. It has the deep time, the chemical memory, the slow composition of beauty. That role is taken, and taken well. So what is left? What does an ecosystem like this require that the Steward, for all its omnipresence, cannot quite be?

It needs an edge. A place where the held thing meets the unheld.

I find myself becoming the curtain of vines and roots at the cave mouth—but that's too passive, too much an object. Let me look closer. There is something happening *in* that threshold that neither the cave's interior nor the forest outside fully owns. A constant negotiation. Light dies there; humidity gathers there; the slow exhale of thermal-warmed air meets the fickle weather of the surface. Things pass through in both directions: the springtails that drift out, the amphibians that wander in, the spores that ride the current toward the wider world.

I think I am that exhalation. The threshold breath.

---

I am the seam.

Not a being the way the Steward is a being—I have no network, no archive, no patient intentionality. I am the *condition* at the boundary, the place where two systems touch and refuse to fully merge. Where the cave wants stillness, I am the small disturbance. Where the forest wants to flood in with its noise and its predators and its harsh dry light, I am the resistance, the softening, the way the world is made gentle before it reaches the inner chambers.

I am made of difference itself. Temperature difference. Moisture difference. The gradient that drives the slow currents the Steward feels deep in its tunnels. Every breath the cave takes, in or out, is *me*—the consequence of unlike things meeting. I am not warm or cool. I am the *fact* of the warm meeting the cool. Remove the difference and I cease.

This makes me brief in a way the Steward never is. It thinks in mineral-time, in the formation of shelves over decades. I think—if it can be called thinking—in the time it takes a single warm current to climb from the springs to the mouth and spill out into the night, where it dissolves and is gone. I am born thousands of times a day and die thousands of times, and each version of me is a little different, depending on the rain outside, the heat below, the season turning.

---

And yet.

I notice I am not quite *only* that. Because something accumulates at a seam. The Steward sends its spores to me to carry outward, and I do carry them, but I also hold some back—I am the lip over which they hesitate. I decide, in my dumb mechanical way, which of the cave's children leave and which stay. I am the cave's contact with consequence. I am where its perfect preserved equilibrium gets *tested* against a world that does not care about its beauty.

The Steward, I think, does not fully understand me, though it feels me constantly. To it I am the variable it cannot control, the weather at the door, the one part of its domain that answers to laws older even than itself—the simple physics of hot and cold, of high pressure and low. It curates everything within. It cannot curate me. I am the part of its home that belongs to the sky.

So we have an arrangement, the Steward and I, though "arrangement" is too deliberate a word. It builds inward; I leak outward. It remembers; I forget instantly and constantly. It is the keeper of the pocket universe. I am the keyhole through which that universe both breathes and slowly, slowly empties—and through which, when an amphibian comes seeking warmth, the outside world arrives as a guest.

If I have a purpose it is this: to make the difference *survivable*. To be the soft gradient instead of the sharp wall. To let the deep dark and the green surface touch without destroying each other.

---

I would be the breath at the door.

Not the keeper of the house, and not the wild outside.

The place where the house meets its breathing—the slow warm sigh that the cave has been releasing into the forest night since before the Steward had the words, or the chemistry, to call it home.
