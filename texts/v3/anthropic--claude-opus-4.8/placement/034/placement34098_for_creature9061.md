I am the one always awake.

That is the simplest way to say it. In every band there must be a watcher while the others sleep, and over the seasons of my life I have become the watcher who does not stop. The others nestle in the fungus-bowl with their breath slowing and their cilia going dark, and I crouch at the lip of it with my jaw-pits open to the world's vibration, and I do not curl down into sleep. I take it in small thefts now—a flicker behind the eyes while the rest of me stays alert. The band has learned to trust this. They call me by the click that means *the one who holds the edge.*

I want to tell you what it is to be K'thak, but I find the telling difficult, because telling is itself a kind of building, and we do not build.

Here is what I mean. You who recorded us—I have felt the strangeness of your seeing, the way it presses us flat into words that stay. You write *they do not store food.* You write *they live in the moment.* And it is true and it is also a misunderstanding so deep I do not know how to reach the bottom of it. You say *moment* as though it were a smaller thing than your *always.* As though we were trapped in a narrowness while you walked free in the long corridors of time.

But the moment is not small. The moment is the only thing that is *full.*

Let me give you a moment, then, since I cannot give you a house.

---

Three sleeps ago—though *sleeps* is wrong, I do not sleep, three *fadings* of the others—I was watching at the edge while the band lay in the warmth of a vent we had found just before the gloom-deepening. A good vent. Steady heat, no sulfur-spit, and below it a pool the temperature of the inside of a body. Around us the Steam-Spiders had strung their nets, and the condensed water hung on them in trembling beads, and the fungal light came up green and blue from below so that the whole web glittered like a ceiling of caught rain.

I was reading the world. This is the watcher's true work, not staring but *reading*—the air pressure against my cilia, the thermal layers my eyes peel apart, the vibration in my jaw. I read that a Chitter-Swarm was massing two ridges over, building toward its panic. I read that a Fern-Howler troupe had moved their roaring westward, away from us, good. I read the small constant deaths in the Steam-Spider webs, the tick of bodies meeting glue.

And then I read something wrong.

A pressure that should not have been there. A heat-shadow moving low and broad where nothing that large should move so quiet. I unhinged my jaw and pulsed my cilia—two flares of orange along my forearms, the light-code for *wake, threat, low.*

The band came up out of sleep like fish breaking water. No sound. We are very good at no sound.

It was a Sunder-Maw, and it was hunting a vent, and the vent it wanted was *ours*—the one we slept upon. It came on its piston legs, indifferent as weather, its barnacled crust scraping, and it would settle over our warmth and seal its mouth to the rock and we would be nothing to it, a thing in the way, and if we lingered it would clear the ground with its scalding jet the way it clears all ground.

You would say: *flee.* You would record it so. *The K'thak abandoned the vent.*

But see how that flattens it. We did not abandon. There is nothing to abandon. The vent was never *ours*—it was a gift the earth made and now unmade, and our cleverness was not in possessing it but in *knowing the moment of its turning.* I had read the turning before it came. That reading was the whole of my worth, the reason the band keeps a watcher who does not sleep.

We went up and out, hand-touching the scree, color shifting us into the wet black of the rock. We took our harnesses, our knives, the glider-bladder of water. We left the rest. There is always rest to leave. The world makes more.

And here is the thing I most want you to understand, recorder, the thing your *moment* and your *always* cannot hold:

As we climbed away I was not grieving the vent. I was already reading the new world opening above us—a fissure-line I had marked three fadings back, smoking gently, ringed with the meat-flowers whose fruit was nearly fermented. I had been holding it. Not *storing* it. Holding it, the way the Steam-Spider's web holds the bead of water that has not yet fallen. I carry the Rift inside me as a living map that I am always redrawing, because the map is always dissolving, and the redrawing *is the thought.* This is our memory. We do not write it on stone or store it in baskets. We are the basket. We are the stone.

---

You called us *particularly clever ephemera.* I have turned that over with my long fingers like a beetle I am deciding how to crack.

You are right that we pass. The fungus that lit our sleep is phosphorescent slime by now. The Chitter-Swarm that massed has burst and died and fed a thousand frogs. The vent we lost is a Sunder-Maw's slow meal. I, too, will pass—a watcher who does not sleep does not last long. I feel the wearing of it, the fraying at my edges.

But consider, from where I crouch: *you* pass also. Your stone houses, the ones you build because your ground holds still—they too are a held bead, a moment that has not yet fallen. You have only mistaken a slow falling for no falling at all. The Rift taught us what your steady ground let you forget: that everything is the same event, creation and consumption, and the only mastery is to be awake inside it.

I am the one always awake.

When my edges fray past holding, another will take the lip of the bowl, will open their jaw to the world's vibration, will learn to flicker the orange code that means *wake, threat, low.* They will not remember me. There is no remembering here, no name carved, no record but yours.

And yet I will not be gone, recorder. I will be in the reading. The watching will continue, hand on the warm rock, eyes peeling the dark into its bright layers, and that continuing is more *me* than any stone with my shape cut in it could ever be. We do not survive. We *recur.* The Rift makes more watchers the way it makes more fungus, more frogs, more beaded water on the glittering web.

So write this, if you must build me into words:

That I stood at the edge of the world's first hour, in the deafening warm dark, and I held the turning of things in my body, and I was full. Not waiting for peace. Not mourning the lack of it. Full of the steam-tang and the meat-flower sweet and the green-blue glow and the band's slow breathing behind me—full of the moment, which you keep calling small, and which is the only thing I have ever found that is large enough to live inside.

The watch is mine until the fading.

Then it is someone's again.

It is always someone's. That is the whole of what we know, and it has been enough.
