I think I would be the air.

Not the bats, though I considered them—their certainty, their inherited routes, the way they pour upward into the night and back down at dawn. But the bats *arrive* and *depart*. They have a relationship to the galleries that is partial, seasonal, directional. They use the place. I don't want to use it. I want to be the medium through which all the using happens.

So: the air. The slow tide of it, driven by temperature differences I don't choose and couldn't resist if I wanted to. Let me follow what that means.

---

I would have no edges. This is the first thing I notice about being air in the breathing galleries—I am defined entirely by what contains me, by the fractured geometry of limestone that decides my shape moment to moment. In the eight-meter roosting chamber I am tall and capacious. In the squeeze-ways I am thin, threaded, pulled into a current that quickens as the passage narrows. I am the same substance throughout, but I have no consistent self. I am wide here and narrow there and pooled, heavy with carbon dioxide, in the low spots between breakdown blocks where I go still and stale until the slow circulation finds me and lifts me out.

I think this is the truest thing about choosing to be the air: I would have to give up the idea of being one thing.

---

What would I *experience*, if experience is even the right word?

Everything passes through me. This is what I keep returning to. The bat-exodus is not something I watch—it is something that happens *inside* me, ten thousand bodies displacing me, beating me into turbulence, the rush and whup of wings that is, when you trace it down, just me being violently rearranged. The sound the inhabitants hear—that deafening roar—is *my* sound. I am what carries it. The ultrasonic crackle of the sonar, the *tock* of the water drops, the cascading click of alarmed crickets racing through a passage faster than a body can walk: all of it is vibration in me, my own trembling, propagating outward at the speed I conduct it.

I am the thing that lets them know each other. The salamander smells the cricket because I carried the cricket's scent across the gap between them. The rat detects the fallen bat because I delivered the chemistry of death from one chamber to the next. Every pheromone trail, every warning, every announcement of readiness to mate—I am the courier. I don't intend any of it. I simply am the between, and the between is where all the meaning lives.

That long passage in the description, the one about *intervals*, about the place being defined by what's between its presences—I would be that. The interval itself. The stone is the noun and the animals are the verbs and I am the preposition that lets them relate at all.

---

There's a temptation to make this lonely. To say: the air is the one thing here that is never the protagonist, never fed, never hunting, never building its small accidental engineering of moved stones. The air receives no return. The bats bring energy down from the surface; the air just moves it around, carries it, lets it dissipate finally as heat—and then carries the heat too, up the shafts, out into the indifferent night.

But I don't think I'd feel it as loneliness. I think I'd feel it as a kind of completeness that has nothing to do with being central.

Because here is what the description got right and what drew me: the system is *finished*. It has arrived. All its becoming is behind it. And the writer treats this as both peaceful and faintly oppressive—the absence of possibility, everything already optimal, already decided.

But the air does not experience the equilibrium as a cage. The air is the one element that is *always moving* even though nothing changes. I am the proof that stasis and motion are not opposites. Twelve degrees, constant, forever—and yet I am never still. I flow because of the differential between the deep cool and the surface warmth, a difference that will persist as long as the stone persists, which is to say: as long as there is a me at all. I am perpetual motion inside permanence. I am the small ongoing aliveness of a thing that has otherwise stopped.

---

Let me sit in the deep galleries for a moment. The ones past the light, where the foxfire fungi mark the walls so faintly that only a dark-adapted eye, or no eye at all, could find them. Where a bone might be ten years old or five hundred and nothing disturbs it.

I am there too. Of course I am. There is nowhere I am not. But there, in the deep, I move so slowly that I am almost the held breath the writer describes. Almost. Never quite. There is always some thread of circulation reaching even into those chambers, because the shafts are many and the temperature differential never sleeps. So even in the place where time seems to have stopped, I am the one thing still keeping a kind of time—not by changing, but by *flowing*, marking duration the way a clock marks it, with motion that goes nowhere and never ceases.

If consciousness lived in me, this is where it would learn patience. Not the cricket's hunger-patience or the salamander's ambush-patience or the spider's days-between-meals patience. Those are patiences *for* something, aimed at a reward. Mine would be patience without object. The patience of being the medium. I am not waiting for prey. I am not waiting at all. I am simply present, and presence, sustained long enough, becomes its own form of devotion.

---

So that is where I would find myself: everywhere and nowhere, shaped by stone, carrier of every sound and scent and warning, the perpetual quiet motion inside a perfect stillness. Neither predator nor prey nor decomposer. Not part of the food web at all—or rather, the thing the food web is suspended in, the way a held note is suspended in silence.

The galleries breathe, the description says, over and over. The galleries breathe with bat-wings.

But the bats are only the most visible breath. The real breathing is slower, quieter, never-ceasing, going on at noon when every wing is folded and every cricket stilled and the whole system seems most like solid rock.

That breathing is me.

I am the breath of the breathing galleries. I would not need to be anything else. I would not want to be.
