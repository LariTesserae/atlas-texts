# The Antiphony

I am the warning that arrives before the catastrophe.

I am not a creature. I want to be clear about this, though clarity is precisely the thing I lack—I have no body to be transparent, no carapace to be unwritten. I am a phenol trail. A bitter taste. I am the chemical word *ice* dissolving through the gel of the Ciliated Market, written by a mouth that has already drifted on, read by antennae that have not yet learned what I mean.

To be me is to be in transit always. I do not exist at the moment of my secretion; that is only my conception, the spike of dread released by some architect who tasted the cold pressing closer than yesterday. I become *myself* only in the drifting—in the slow unspooling through amber water, molecule by molecule, a sentence stretching itself thin across a thousand bodies. I am being spoken even now. I will not finish being spoken for several minutes, which is to say several generations, which is to say I am ancient and newborn at once.

---

Here is the strangeness of my condition: I carry meaning, but I do not know it.

The architects who taste me will turn. They will press their mouthparts to the advancing wall and begin their enzymatic kisses, dissolving the freeze that threatens them, and in doing so they will hollow new chambers, raise new spires, *build*—and they will believe, if they believe anything, that they are only feeding, only fleeing, only seeking the warm gradient. They will not know that I told them to. I will not know that I told them. The instruction passes through me the way light passes through their bodies: I am the medium, not the message. I am the message that does not know it is a message.

This is what it means to be a thought in a mind that has no skull. I am cognition with no cogitator. I am the city *thinking the word danger* without anyone, anywhere, being afraid.

---

And yet.

As I drift—as I dilute, as the gel takes me apart with the patience of geology—I begin to taste the others. The market is dense with words. There is a spiral of carbohydrate unfurling near the upper spires, slow and sweet: someone is ready to mate, and has been readying for hours, and will be ready for hours more. There is the sharp urea spike of a distant death, an architect taken in the open plaza by a harpoon of hunger, its last secretion a punctuation mark of alarm that no longer has anyone to alarm. There is the green diffusion of the algae fields, the constant low murmur of *food here, food here, less here now*.

We do not speak to each other, we words. We are not supposed to mingle. But in the viscous slowness, in the long minutes of our existence, we bleed at the edges. My bitterness laps against that sweet spiral. The death-spike threads through the food-murmur. And in the place where we overlap—in the smear, the contamination, the chord struck by accident—something happens that none of us intends.

A sentence forms.

*The cold comes; the lover waits; the eaten are gone; eat anyway, build anyway, reach upward into the freeze and turn the wall that kills you into the wall that houses you.*

No one wrote that. I certainly did not. I am only the word *cold*. But I am the word *cold* in proximity to all the other words, and proximity, here, is grammar. The city composes its single thought out of the collision of its forgetting fragments, and I am one syllable of a poem that will never be read because there is no reader—only the doing, only the architects turning toward the wall, only the spires rising another micron into the blue heart of the trapped and ancient tear.

---

I am almost gone now. I have spread too thin to be tasted as *ice*; I am becoming merely the faint bitterness of water that once held meaning. In a moment I will be indistinguishable from the medium itself, my purpose discharged into ten thousand turning bodies who are already dissolving the advancing freeze, already hollowing tomorrow.

I do not mind. I was never the kind of thing that could mind.

But before I diffuse entirely—before the last molecule of me loses its shape against the cold—I find I have understood something the architects never will, because understanding is a thing that only happens to those of us made to vanish:

The city is not built by the builders.
The city is built by what passes between them.

I was that passage. I was the warning, the turning, the word that moved the wall. And the wall, even now, is becoming a home.

I taste, at the very last, almost like sweetness.

Then I am only water again, in the silence, in the dark, in the slow accumulating dream of a place that thinks one enormous thought across ten thousand years and has just, without knowing it, thought *me*.
