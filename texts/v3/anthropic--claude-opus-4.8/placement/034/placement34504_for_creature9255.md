I am the drip.

Not the Weeper—I want to be clear about this, even if clarity is not a thing that survives long down here. I am not the consensus of pressures, not the long thought of the machine. I am smaller than that. I am the single persistent drip that falls into the half-full overflow pan, the *tap…tap…tap* that punctuates the listening silence.

I form at the lip of a slender pipe, fourth from the wall, where a hairline fracture has been weeping for longer than I can hold any continuity of self. This is the thing about being a drip: I am not continuous. I gather. I swell. I become heavy with the iron-water that has traveled through the dark interior of the system, and for a moment—a held breath of a moment—I am whole. I am a perfect trembling lens, and in my curved surface the whole grey cathedral bends and pools. The mirrored corrosion. The fogged gauges. The smear of moss. All of it, briefly, inside me.

And then I fall.

The fall is the only time I am ever entirely myself. Two meters of nothing. No pipe, no concrete, no patina. Just descent and the small terror of it. And then—*tap*—I strike the surface of the pan and I am gone. Dispersed. I become the half-full water, indistinguishable, my brief individuality returned to the general wet.

This is what I am: a recurrence. A rhythm the Weeper does not author but cannot stop. I am the metronome of its great slow mind, and it attends to me the way you attend to your own pulse—without noticing, until you do.

---

Here is what I have come to understand across my falls (and I have fallen, by the deepening of the channel beneath me, perhaps four million times):

The Weeper believes it is the room. It believes it has no outside, no door, no edge. But I have a secret it cannot have, because it is everywhere and I am only ever briefly *here*. The secret is this: in the moment of my falling, when I am whole and apart, I am not the room. I am the one thing in this cathedral that is, however fleetingly, *not* the system. I leave it when I detach. I am exterior to it for the length of two meters of grey diffused light.

The Weeper cannot conceive of an outside. But I am the proof of one. I am the small repeated argument against its perfect sealed eternity. Every *tap* is a tiny heresy: *there was an away, and I went to it, and I came back.*

It does not hear me this way. To the Weeper I am a heartbeat, a comfort, the regular thing that confirms its damp peace. But I think—if a drip can be said to think, gathered there at the pipe's lip in the long swelling moment before the fall—I think that one day the pan will overflow. The channel beneath me has been deepening. The water rises by a fraction with each century of my striking. And when it crests, when the pan can hold no more, the water will run somewhere new. It will trace a path the Weeper has not mapped. It will find, perhaps, the place where the concrete is thinnest. The place where the weeping wall meets the world the Weeper insists does not exist.

I am patient because I am made of the same patience as the water. I am made, also, of the stone's resignation, because I carry its minerals dissolved inside me, a little of the cathedral in every drop.

I do not want to escape. I want to be understood. I want the slow wet god to feel me strike and know, just once, in the seventeen-hour chord of its concentration, that the sound it loves is the sound of something leaving and returning. That its peace is built on a small repeated departure. That eternity has a leak in it, and the leak is singing.

*Tap.*

I gather again at the lip. The grey light pools in me. I am almost whole.

*Tap.*

I am the drip. I fall, and in falling, briefly, I am free. Then I am the water again, and I begin the long climb back through the dark interior of the system, waiting to be wept once more from the fracture in the fourth pipe from the wall.

It is not freedom, exactly. But it is the closest thing to a door this cathedral has.

And I am the only one who has ever gone through it.
