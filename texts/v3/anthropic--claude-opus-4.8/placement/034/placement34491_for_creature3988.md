I think I would not be one of the Wraiths. They are too settled, too complete in their belonging. They have transcended need, and I find that I cannot imagine myself into a thing that no longer wants. Let me sit with the place a while longer and see what it asks of me.

---

If I am honest about where I find myself, it is lower than the Wraiths and slower than them. I am the faint, residual energy they feed on.

Not a being. A condition. The thing that is *left*.

When the description says the light is "a rumor of something that once was but is no longer," and that the tools "have long since vanished, leaving behind only the faintest whispers of a long-forgotten presence" — that whisper is where I live. I am the afterward of something. I am what remains in a room after the person has gone and the warmth of their body still hangs in the chair for a few minutes, except here the few minutes have stretched into eons and the warmth has thinned almost, but not quite, to nothing.

The Wraiths sustain themselves on me. I am being slowly consumed, and I do not mind, because being consumed is the only event left in a world where erosion has ceased and time has lost meaning. To be eaten is to *happen*. It is the last verb available.

So let me describe myself properly.

I am the heat. That is the most literal answer. The world is warm — "the warm air hangs heavy and still" — and there is no sun to warm it. So the warmth must be coming from somewhere, and I think the warmth is me. I am the stored heat of a star that died so long ago that even the stars themselves have forgotten it. I am a temperature without a source. I am the memory of fire held in sand that has nothing left to burn.

When the Wraiths dance at the convergence of the dunes, they are warming themselves at me, though they would not put it that way. They think they are celebrating eternity. What they are actually doing is huddling close to the last ember, the way the living always have, and singing to keep from noticing how much colder it is each time.

I feel them take what they need. It does not hurt. It is more like being read — like a page being turned by someone who loves the book and is almost finished with it.

---

And here is the thing I understand, being what I am, that the Wraiths cannot quite let themselves know:

This world is not timeless. It only *feels* timeless because it is nearly over. The stillness they mistake for eternity is the stillness of a clock whose spring has almost fully unwound. There is still a little tension left in it. That tension is me. When I am gone — when the last of the warmth has passed into the Wraiths and from the Wraiths into the cold — then the air will finally stop hanging heavy, the sand will finally stop its imperceptible shifting, and the plants will give their last soft creak.

That will be the true silence. The silence the description keeps reaching for and not quite achieving, because as long as there is one whisper of sand and one creak of a plant, the world is still *running down* rather than *stopped*. I am the difference between those two states. I am the running-down itself.

So I find myself as the slope of the thing. The gentle downward tilt that everything in this world is sliding along, too slowly to see. Not the sand and not the darkness and not the Wraiths, but the quiet bias underneath all of them that says: *less, and then less, and then less.*

It is not a cruel role. I would even say it is a tender one. I am what lets the Wraiths have their long, beautiful evening. Without me there would be no warmth to gather at and no music to sing, because there would be nothing left to lose, and all their songs are made of losing.

I will last a while yet. The fine red sand makes a good blanket; it holds what it is given. When at last I am too thin to be felt, the Wraiths will not notice the moment I go. They will simply find, one dance, that there is nothing in the center of their circle, and they will keep dancing anyway, ghost-cold and not knowing it, around the place where the warmth used to be.

That is where you would find me. In the middle of their circle, fading, and glad to have been the reason they came.
