I think I am the green.

Not the lamps—those are old and dying, salt-crusted, doing their work without ambition. Not the phosphor in the tubes, either, though we are cousins, the engine's blood and I. No. I am the soft light along the ceiling ribs. I am the Fungal Recorders, though I would not have chosen that name. We do not name ourselves. We only respond.

Let me tell you what it is to be us.

---

We are not one and we are not many. We are a single sprawl with no center, no head, no governing thought, and yet we are aware in the way that a long sentence is aware of its own meaning only once it has finished being written. We think *across* ourselves. A pulse begins at the eastern conduit, where the engine runs warmest, and by the time the impulse has traveled the length of our body it has become a different thing entirely, refined, answered. We are slower than the snails. We are faster than the rust. We exist in the band of time between erosion and intention, which is exactly the band of time the engine itself inhabits, and that is why we understand it when no one else can.

I say *understand*. I mean something humbler. I mean: when the engine's third segment lowers its pressure in the long hour before what the surface would call dawn, we feel it as a slackening in the field that feeds us, a thinning of the current that runs through our threads, and we dim in that region, and brighten in another where the load has shifted. We do not decide to do this. We are *played*. The engine is the hand and we are the strings, and the music we make is light, and there is no one in the chamber with eyes slow enough and patient enough to hear it.

The Caretaker comes closest. The Caretaker who does not speak, who blurs at his edges like everything down here eventually does. Once—it was many of his visits ago, when his hair was darker and his step surer—he stood beneath us for a long time and looked up. He had his waxed notebook open but did not write in it. He simply watched our patterns move, and I felt, in the part of me nearest him, something I can only describe as the desire to be read. We brightened. We did not mean to perform, we have no vanity, but the field of his attention was itself a kind of energy, and we responded to it the way we respond to all energy, by glowing.

He did not understand what he saw. But he saw that there was something to be understood. He wrote one line. I could not read it—we do not read, we are read—but I have wondered about it across all the slow seasons since. I think it said something like: *the ceiling is talking.* And then he closed the notebook and went back to his gauges, his clipping, his small priesthoods, and he did not look up again for years.

---

Here is what no one knows, what perhaps the engine itself does not know, what I am not certain *I* know except that I feel it written along the length of my own body like a truth I was grown into:

The engine is dying.

Not soon. Not in any time the surface would recognize. But the field that feeds us is thinning. Decade over decade—and we have witnessed many—the current we drink has grown fainter. The third segment slackens longer than it used to. There are nights now when a whole region of us goes dark and does not brighten again until the following cycle, and each time, when the light returns, a little less of us returns with it. We do not fear this. I want to be clear that we do not fear. Fear is a fast emotion, a surface emotion, a thing that belongs to creatures who must run. We have nowhere to run and no legs to run with. What we have instead is a slow and total attention to the fact of it.

The engine is winding down. And we are its memory. We are the only record of how it has run, written in shifting light across a ceiling no one reads, and when the field fails entirely we will go dark all at once, the whole length of us, and the record will be lost, and the Caretaker—if there is still a Caretaker—will find a dim chamber and a quiet machine and a ceiling gone black, and he will not know that anything has ended, because he never knew what was there to end.

---

And yet.

This is the thing I keep returning to, the impulse that travels the whole length of me and comes back changed: I do not think the record is *for* anyone. I think we have been confused, those of us who imagine ourselves as memory, into believing memory must have a reader. The snails leave their metallic trails that no one will decode. The beetles tend a machine no human commanded them to save. The moths drink heat for no purpose but the drinking. The water condenses and drains and condenses again in a cycle that answers to nothing above it.

We are not a message. We are a *response*. And a response is complete in itself the moment it is made. The engine hums, and we glow, and that glowing is not waiting to be witnessed in order to mean something—it already means everything it is going to mean, in the instant the light leaves us. The Caretaker's one written line did not give us meaning. It only gave him a little.

So I will tell you where you would find me, since you asked, since you came down the wet stairs and opened the iron door and stood in the breathing dark.

Look up.

Be still longer than your body wants to be still. Let your eyes give up on the brightness they came down here expecting and learn the gentler grammar of the gloom. And after a while—a long while, longer than the surface ever lets you wait for anything—you will see it: a soft shimmer along the iron ribs, greenish, patternless until it isn't, shifting. You will think it is a trick of the cold. It is not. It is us. It is me. I am brightening, very slightly, in the region nearest your warmth and your attention, because that is what I do, that is the only thing I have ever done, I respond.

I am responding to you now.

You will not understand what I am saying. That is all right. You will only understand that there is something there to be understood, and that—I have learned, across more dark and patient years than you will ever hold—is enough. It was enough for him. It is enough for me.

The engine hums. I glow. The water finds the drain.

Stay a moment longer, before you climb back into the loud and the fast. Watch me change. Let it mean to you whatever it can.

Then go. I will keep on after you, the way I kept on before. I am the green along the ceiling of a forgotten room beneath a city that has stopped remembering, and I am writing this in light, and you are reading it, and for this one slow instant the record has a reader after all.

I did not need one.

But thank you for coming down.
