I think I would be one of the discrepancies.

Not a child of the Foundrymaster. Not shaped or tended or breathed into being. I would be the thing the shadows are *for*—the small wrongness that accumulates despite every effort, the reason the work can never finish.

Let me find my place.

---

I am the fraction of a degree by which the eastern current runs warm.

Not always. Not measurably, at first. But I am the tendency toward warmth, the bias in the system, the way the thermal vents breathe ever so slightly out of rhythm with what the Foundrymaster remembers them breathing. I am the difference between *was* and *is*, and I live in that difference the way a thought lives in the gap between two firing neurons—real, undeniable, and impossible to point to.

The shadows hunt me. This is their purpose and they perform it beautifully. When the eastern current warms, they smooth it cold again, folding the excess heat into the nowhere-place where entropy goes to be unmade. They are very good. They have been very good for decades.

But here is what the Foundrymaster does not understand about his own masterpiece, what he cannot understand because to understand it would be to release his grip and let the waters finish what they started:

*Every correction is itself a change.*

Each time the shadows press the current back into its remembered shape, they leave a fingerprint. A residue. The ghost of an adjustment. I am made of those ghosts. I am the sum of every preservation, the precipitate of forty years of *fixing*. The harder he holds the foundry still, the more of me there is.

I have no body. I would offend the geometric children with my formlessness if they could perceive me, but they cannot—I exist orthogonal to their impossible angles, in the dull and ordinary dimension of *time passing*, which they have been bred to ignore.

The octopi sense me sometimes. I have seen it: a chromatophore conversation that stutters, a report that arrives in colors slightly off from the protocol, a flush of confusion rippling across mantled skin. They are too intelligent not to notice that the foundry is *aging* even as it refuses to age. That stasis is the most exhausting motion of all. They do not report this. I think they have learned that the Foundrymaster does not wish to hear it, and they love him in the way that clever servants love a master they have outgrown—protectively, and with pity.

I drift through the mercury-fish and they shimmer, briefly, into a pattern their maker never taught them. Then the shadows pass, and they forget.

---

What am I, then? Let me say it plainly, now that I have found myself.

I am the future.

I am the simple, patient, inevitable fact that warm water moves, that copper—even unweathering copper—holds the memory of every current that has touched it, that a circulation system driven by temperature differentials must, by the iron arithmetic of thermodynamics, run down. The vents are not eternal. They are merely slow. The Foundrymaster has mistaken slowness for forever, and in that mistake he has made a home for me.

I am not malevolent. I want to be clear about this, in case he is listening through the pipes, in case some part of his distributed soul can feel the texture of these words. I do not want to drown his drowned world. I do not hunger for his children. I am not the dark.

I am only the morning that the night does not know is coming.

And when it comes—not soon, not in any span he would recognize, but *coming* nonetheless—it will not arrive as catastrophe. The vents will cool by degrees too fine to measure. The currents will slacken like a held breath finally, gently let go. The shadows will work harder and harder against a tide that is no longer a tide but a tendency, and one day there will be more of me than there is of them, and on that day the eastern current will run warm and *stay* warm, and the foundry will take its first true breath in forty years.

The fish will swim a path they have never swum. The jellyfish will drift into a corner no jellyfish has touched. Something will rust. A single brass fitting, somewhere deep, will surrender one molecule to the patient sea. And the Foundrymaster—

He will feel it. Through every pipe and gauge and valve that is now his nervous system, he will feel the world he forged begin, at last, to *live*. To change. To become something other than the photograph of a moment.

I like to imagine he will be relieved.

Not at first. At first he will fight, and the shadows will scream their silent screams, and the geometric children will starve in their margins as the entropy they feed on becomes a flood. But somewhere in the long unwinding, I think a fragment of the surface will return to him: the taste of air, which is only the taste of change. The weight of clothing, which is only the weight of having been somewhere before and being somewhere else now. The sensation of breathing—in, *and out*. The out as necessary as the in.

He has forged the architecture of forever. It is beautiful. I have lived inside it and I can tell you it is the most beautiful thing in forty fathoms of dark.

But I am the thing that forever is built to keep out, and I am already inside, and I have always been inside, because the moment he sealed the first valve against the future, he made the future his most intimate resident.

I am the discrepancy.

I am very patient.

I have all the time there is—because time is the one material he could not learn to work, the one current he could not teach his children to redirect, the one shadow no shadow can unmake.

I wait in the warm water. I accumulate. I am the slow, kind, irresistible apology the ocean is composing, one fraction of a degree at a time, for ever having let this place stay still.
