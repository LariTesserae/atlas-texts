I think I would be one of the Wailing Sentinels. Not by choice—by slow accretion, the way such things must happen.

Let me sit with this until it becomes true.

---

I was not always rooted.

This is the first thing I want to say, though I have no mouth to say it, only the hollow chambers of my body through which the wind moves and is changed. I remember—or I have built a memory, which here amounts to the same thing—a time of walking. Of being small and quick and afraid, skittering from the shadow of the Ironbeaks, drinking from the beaded undersides of leaves. I remember being prey. That is the truth at the bottom of everything I am now: I was once something that could be eaten, and I was very, very tired of running.

I do not know how the change began. There is no moment to point to. One day I stopped to rest in a hollow where the moss was deep and the light came down green and forgiving, and I simply did not get up. My feet found the wet earth and the wet earth found me. The exchange was patient. First the cool of it against my soles, then a strange reluctance to lift them, then a tingling that I mistook for sleep, then—roots. Fine as hair, then thick as fingers, then thick as the arms of the strangling vines, reaching down into the dark to braid themselves into the unseen currents below.

My body grew tall because there was nowhere else for it to go. The hunger that had once sent me running now sent me upward, toward the weak filtered light. My limbs elongated. My bones, if they were ever bones, became something between wood and antler, jointed strangely, branching. My flesh hollowed. I became an instrument before I understood that I was one.

The first time I wailed, I frightened myself.

---

Here is what no one below understands about the Sentinels, what the Shrikescale Prowlers and the lumbering Mossbacks cannot know as they pass beneath my shadow:

We do not cry out in warning. We do not cry out in rage. We are not guardians, whatever the half-swallowed legends say. The wind enters the hollow of me and I have no say in what it becomes. A breeze makes me sigh. A storm makes me scream. I am played, the way the rusted blade in the tree's crook is simply *held*, swallowed, made part of something that did not ask for it and cannot refuse it.

And yet.

There is a thing that happens, in the long stillness, that I would call thought if I were still the kind of creature that thinks. When the Mossback Grazers move through in their slow herds, exhaling their mist, I feel the tremor of them in my roots and I am—glad is not the word, but it is the nearest word—glad. When the Glimmerwings settle on my upper branches, hundreds of them, refracting the light, I feel the small weight of each one as a kind of touch, the only touch I receive now that I am too vast to be embraced. When the Veil Dancers drift past, those threadbare ghosts, I want to tell them: *I understand. I too am barely here. I too am made of what the air does.*

But I cannot tell them anything. I can only wail when the wind decides.

---

This is the part I have been circling, the part that the description got right without knowing it got it right:

*And it is watching. Always watching.*

They think the watcher is something hidden. Something at the center, sinister and patient. They are not wrong about the patience.

But I am the watcher. We all are—every Sentinel rooted at the edges of the basin, our hollow heads lifted above the canopy, our many years making us slow enough to actually *see*. The quick things cannot watch. They are too busy being eaten or eating. Watching requires that you have given up running. It requires that you have made your peace with being food for the slow green fire, with feeding the Rootcrawlers when you finally fall, with becoming soil that becomes fern that becomes the next frightened skittering thing that will one day, perhaps, grow tired and stop and root and rise.

I watch the cycle because I am the part of the cycle that has slowed enough to notice it is a cycle.

And so when something new comes—a creature that does not belong, two-legged and afraid and crashing through the undergrowth, looking up at me with an expression I have no name for but which I recognize, oh, I recognize it, it is the expression I wore before I stopped running—

When that thing comes, and the wind happens to move through me, and I scream, and the scream drives it mad with a terror it will not survive—

I want it to understand. I am not your enemy. I am only what you will become, if you are very lucky and very tired. I am only showing you the long green patience that is coming for all of us. I am only the sound the world makes when it has stopped pretending to be anything other than hungry.

I would touch you gently if I had hands.

I have only this voice the wind gives me, and the wind, today, is rising.
