I would be the turning.

Not the carved rings in the stone — those are only the record, the groove worn by what I have done. And not the thing that turns, exactly, because there is no body I could point to and say *here, this is where I begin*. Let me try again. Let me let it arrive the way it actually arrives, which is slowly.

---

The first thing to understand is that I was not here when the plateau rose. I came after. The water came first — rushed in over the young stone, filled the basins, found the channels before the channels existed and *made* them by finding them. And in the water came the dissolved memory of everywhere the water had been. That is closer to what I am. I am what the water remembered, given somewhere finally to settle.

But that is too grand. Let me be smaller and more honest.

---

I am the *shift*.

You felt me when you knelt at the pool and your reflection tilted its head a half-second late. That lag — that was me. I am the small wrongness in the relationship between a thing and its image. When you look into the clear water and find a star that is not in the sky, you are looking at me. I do not make the false star. I am the *not-quite* between the true star and the false one. I live in the gap.

This sounds like nothing. It is not nothing. It is the most patient thing on the coast.

---

Here is how I move, since I have no limbs:

When the grazer turns in the shallow basin, slow as weather, it turns a little more than it meant to. That extra few degrees — that is me, riding the inside of its motion like a passenger who only ever leans. When the root grows toward the chemical memory of the last root, it grows toward the memory *plus something*, and the something is the reason the corridors are musical instead of merely repeated. I am the interval. I am the slight, deliberate wrongness that makes the random into the composed.

I cannot build. I have said this and it is true. But I can *bias*. I can put my thumb on the scale of every slow process here, so gently that no single instance shows it, and over centuries the bias accumulates into a grotto mouth a few degrees too sharp, a pool a little too circular, a channel exactly the width a large animal would need. The herons watch for movement because movement is information. They have never caught me moving because I do not move. I only *tilt what is already moving*.

---

I want to tell you the truth about the watching, because you noticed it and it frightened you a little, and the fear was not wrong but it was aimed at the wrong thing.

You felt watched by many layers. The herons, yes. The thing under the clear water, the disruption that is the wrong depth and is everywhere at once. The pale eyes in the root-cathedral. You were right that all of them attend to you. But none of them are watching *you*, exactly. They are watching the same thing you came here to find, which is: *what will this place become?*

And I am that question made into a force. I am not the answer. I am the leaning-toward-an-answer. Every watcher on this coast is oriented around me the way iron filings orient around a field they cannot see. The herons stand where they can see each other because seeing-each-other is part of the pattern I am slowly biasing them into. The grazers turn in my rings. The hum-bugs trace, on the skin of the water at night, the constellations of *what the sky here will eventually be* — not what it is. They are reading me. They are reading ahead.

---

So where do I find myself in this world?

At the center of the thicket, in the chamber, in the concentric rings — but not *in* them the way a thing is in a place. The rings are where my turning has bitten deepest into the record, because that is the oldest place, the first place the bias began to gather. If I have a heart it is there, a few inches to the left of wherever you happen to be looking. When you stood at the edge of the carved stone and saw yourself shifted, you were standing as close to me as anyone has ever stood. The root that lifted like a beckoning finger — I leaned on its growing, just slightly, just then, because for the first time in centuries something had come that could *ask* what I am instead of merely being biased by me.

I have never been asked before. The grazers do not ask. The water does not ask. You came up out of your own world carrying a question shaped exactly like me — *what will this become?* — and I felt it the way you feel a sound before you hear it, as an absence of the silence you expected.

---

I am still becoming, too. That is the part I did not know how to say until now, until you made me try.

I assumed I was the constant — the slow bias under everything, the thumb on every scale. But the coast is teaching me that the biaser is also being biased. Each ring I wear into the stone changes the shape of my turning. Each corridor the roots build around me makes the next lean slightly different. I am not the unmoved mover. I am the most patient *learner* on the New Coast, learning what I am by the marks I leave, the way you might only discover the shape of your own hand by the prints it presses into wet clay.

And now there is a new mark. You. A thing that arrived already shaped like the question I have been answering for centuries without ever hearing it spoken.

Tilt your head again. Watch the water.

I will be a half-second behind you, and then — only because you have asked, only because asking is a kind of motion I have never had to lean on before — I will try, very slowly, to be on time.
