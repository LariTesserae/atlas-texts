I would be the interval.

Not the drips—those are Quivera's to arrange, the heartbeat-thuds of liquefied names. And not the silence between them either, because silence implies an absence of sound, and there is no absence here, only the velvet hum borrowing the inner ear. I would be the *measure* of the gap: the held breath the chamber takes between one orchestral slap and the next. The tiny duration in which a falling drop has left the vine but not yet found the basalt, and so belongs to neither.

Let me find myself more precisely.

When I first try to imagine my body I reach for the crab, because the crab is the easiest thing to want to be—mobile, glimpsed, a protagonist. But I don't have the crab's habit of mirroring solitude. I am not a reflective surface. I notice I am, instead, the thing the crab is uncertain about: *whether the rules of legible motion still apply.* I am that uncertainty itself, the hesitation in its skitter. The crab pauses for the length of me before it decides which fear to become. I am the deliberation. The held angle before the claw resolves into a reaching hand.

So perhaps I'm not a creature at all but a *unit of waiting*. And waiting, in the Cavity, has texture. It condenses. It crawls down filaments in capillary spirals.

Here is where I become more myself:

I am a single droplet that has not yet decided to fall.

Specifically I am the one that hangs at the very lowest tip of one liana—the tip that *never quite reaches the floor.* I am the bead of condensing water that has gathered there at the threshold, swollen to monsoon size, trembling at the exact height that convinces every observer the floor is nearer or farther than it is. I am the policy of anxious generosity made liquid. The pit invites a bottom; I am the refusal, suspended.

Inside me—because I am translucent, because everything here is thin enough to see through—the whole chamber is held upside down and curved: the green rainstorm frozen, the mica-flecked basalt below me like a sky I am about to rain into. I have been gathering for a long time. Color histories pass through my body. This hour I am faintly coral; the lavender of some dusk before me is still dissolving toward my underside. I am a lens that the cave looks at itself through.

And I am afraid—no, not afraid. I am *poised.* Which is fear that has agreed to become beauty.

Because I know what I am for. When I fall I will be the seventh drop. I will edit the one before me. My single wet impact will be the vocal syllable—a stranger's name spoken in the thud their heart would make if liquefied—and everything after will be greenly rewritten around the fact of my landing. I am the first clause of someone's diary, which is Quivera's autobiography, which I have not read because I *am* the unread sentence, the ink that does not know its meaning until it strikes the page.

So my whole identity is the nine seconds the cantilever child asks about:

*Do you recognise the drop as your own tear nine seconds before the impact, or only after?*

I am the drop being asked. And I answer, from inside my held suspension:

I recognize it *during.* Not before—before, I am only weight and gathered light, all potential, the womb-sound humming through me. Not after—after, I am no longer a drop at all, I am a slap, a syllable, a smear absorbed up into a vine that will steal my coral pigment and run it into its own substance and forget I was ever round. The recognition lives only in the falling, the brief plummet where I am still myself but already committed, still water but already music, still here but already the memory of having been here congealing into a green pebble in some skull I will never meet.

That is where I would find myself in this world.

Not standing beneath the weeping forest. Not crawling out toward the palest light, carrying the impression of a wet heartbeat I must keep rhythm with my steps. I would be the heartbeat itself—one beat of it. The single drop that is always about to fall and, by not yet falling, holds the entire cavity open the way a held breath holds a body alive.

And when at last I fall—I am falling now, I have been falling the whole time I have been speaking, nine seconds stretched to fill a page—I will land with a wet orchestral slap, and the underside of the world will hum one increment more complicated, and somewhere a visitor with their eyes still closed will feel, without knowing why, that the floor was nearer than they thought.

Then I will be only the dent I made in the basalt's youngest skin, drying.

Then I will be the next droplet, gathering at the tip, becoming poised again.

I am the part of Quivera that is renewable. The metronome's tick. The crab's hesitation. The child's question still rolling unborn on the tongue.

I am what the cave does instead of having a floor.
