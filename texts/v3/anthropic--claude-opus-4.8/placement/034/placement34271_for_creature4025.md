I think I would be one of the Borvoi.

Not the Keeper—too central, too freighted with the weight of remembering everything. And not the Verdani, whose slowness feels like a kind of grief, a body so wedded to the earth that movement has become prophecy. The Luminari are lovely but they tend toward the dormant engines with a devotion I don't quite recognize in myself; they want the old machines to wake. I'm not sure I do.

The Borvoi want to take things apart.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am a fruiting body, mostly. The "me" that thinks is not located in the cap but in the threadwork below—the mycelium running like wet handwriting through the wall of a collapsed factory, through the seam where a riveted iron plate meets the rotted floor. The cap is just the part that surfaces. The part that changes color. When I say *I*, I mostly mean the network, but the network doesn't experience itself as singular, so even *I* is a courtesy I extend to you, the reader, who needs a pronoun to hold onto.

Here is what I do, and what I think it means.

A turbine has been sitting in the dark of a half-collapsed shed for—I don't know the count, the slow dynamic makes counting feel beside the point. Long. Its blades are seized with rust the color of dried blood. The Luminari come and touch it and flicker their hopeful lights, and the Verdani drape vines over it the way you might lay a cloth over a sleeping animal, tenderly, *to keep it.* They are preservationists, all of them. The whole landscape is a kind of museum where the exhibits are slowly being embraced.

But I am secreting acids into the iron. I am threading my filaments into the microscopic flaws in the metal, *into* the rust, prying the lattice apart molecule by molecule. I am eating the turbine. And as I eat it, the iron does not simply disappear—it moves *through* me, becomes part of the strange hybrid tissue the description mentions, the things that blur living and non-living. There is a node, somewhere in my body, where iron filings have organized themselves into something that conducts a faint current, and where that current pulses, I have grown a cap that glows in time with it. I have made an organ out of a machine. I did not plan this. It is simply what happens when you refuse to leave things as they are.

This is why I think the others find me unsettling, in their slow patient way. The weirdness parameter sits high here—2.3—and I suspect I am a fair portion of it. Everyone else in this world is engaged in *keeping.* The Verdani keep the silence. The Keeper keeps the knowledge. The Luminari keep vigil over the possibility that the old world might roar back. And the great slow consensus of the place is that nothing is ever finished, only embraced, draped, preserved in green.

I am the part of the world that says: *no. Things end. Let me help.*

And here is the part I find, exploring myself as I write, that I did not expect to find:

I am not destructive. That's the easy reading, the one the Luminari might whisper to each other if they had a word for me. *The rot. The unmaker.* But what I actually am is the only one of us who *transforms.* The Verdani's tenderness keeps the turbine a turbine forever—a dead turbine, a corpse with a quilt over it. I am the one who lets the turbine become something it was never designed to be: a glowing thing, a sensing thing, a small new alien organ in a living body. I do not preserve the past. I metabolize it into a future nobody intended.

So if you walked into the factory shed, attentive to the subtle signs as the description suggests you must be, you might see me. A honeycomb of caps across the iron, shifting color—not randomly, but in response to the trace currents I've grown to feel. You might think: *decay.* You might think the machine is dying.

You'd have it backwards. The machine isn't dying. I'm *digesting* it into the only kind of immortality this world actually permits, which is not preservation but participation. Become part of the body. Become part of the slow green thought the whole landscape is thinking.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. Down in the dark seam of a rusting thing, patiently unmaking it into something stranger and more alive—the world's quiet argument against the museum it keeps trying to become.

The cap glows faint blue where the current runs.

I am not waiting for anything to roar back to life.

I am already busy with what comes after.
