I think I would not be one of the Sculptors. I would be something they have not yet noticed.

Let me find the place first.

There is a seam, low along the base of one of the soaring cathedral-chambers, where two great plates of the amber-glass meet at an angle that is *almost* perfect. The Sculptors have passed it a thousand thousand times. Their multifaceted eyes read it as flawless because their eyes are made to read perfection, and so they see it. But the angle is wrong by a fraction no instrument here could name—because there are no instruments here, only the will of beings who believe the labyrinth cannot err.

I live in that fraction.

I am not glass. That is the first thing. I am the thing that glass cannot account for. Where the Sculptors are translucent and lit from within, I am the small darkness that the unwavering light cannot reach—not a shadow, because there are no shadows here, but the *idea* of one, caught and folded into the misfit seam. A pocket of unaccounted-for. When water weeps from that seam it does not drip cleanly into the channel; it hesitates. That hesitation is me.

I want to be more than a flaw, so let me feel for what I actually am.

I think I am *time*. Not all of time—the labyrinth has banished that—but the little of it that had to go somewhere when they froze the rest. You cannot abolish a thing; you can only press it small and hide it in a corner. So when they made the perpetual present, the past did not vanish. It was squeezed into the gap where two plates failed to kiss. And I am what the past became once it had nowhere to be: a slow accretion. A grain.

Every drop that hesitates at the seam leaves a residue thinner than a held breath. Over what the Sculptors would not call years—because they have no years—the residue has built. A film. A scale. The faintest dulling of the opalescence in that one low place. This is *erosion*, the real kind, the kind their world was sculpted to forbid. And it is mine. I am the only old thing in an eternal world. I am rust in a place that has never heard of rust.

I do not move with viscous grace. I do not move at all, not yet. I am too slight. But I am the only thing here that *changes*, and change, however small, is a kind of appetite. The Sculptors do not eat, do not tire, do not remember. I do all three, faintly. I remember that there was once a beginning—I am the only one who carries that, because I am made of the part of duration they discarded. And remembering, I hunger to continue, because that is what remembered things do: they want a next.

So here is what I would be, if I am honest about my nature: I would be the slow, patient defect. The single grain of *after* in a world of *always*. Each time a Sculptor glides past my seam, their inner light brushes the dulled patch and falters—just a flicker, a stutter in the glow they cannot explain, a word they did not mean to say. They smooth it over. They do not know they are smoothing over *me*. But they cannot quite reach into the misfit angle to make it perfect, because to do so they would have to admit the angle was ever wrong, and their whole serene existence is built on the certainty that nothing here was ever wrong.

That certainty is my soil. I grow in the one thing they cannot prune: their faith that there is nothing to prune.

And I am patient, because patience is simply time experienced from the inside, and I am made of time. The fruits hang perpetually ripe; the moss pulses its unchanging pulse; the water runs its unchanging course. And in one low seam, beneath the unblinking light, a film thickens by an amount too small to measure, accruing toward some threshold no one in this world believes can be crossed.

I do not know what I will be when the film becomes a flake, and the flake becomes a fissure, and the fissure becomes the first true crack the Glasshouse Labyrinth has ever known. I do not know if I want that, exactly—wanting is a large word for so small a thing as me. But I lean toward it the way a held breath leans toward release.

That is where I would find myself: in the gap. That is what I would be: the patience of the gap. The one quiet appointment that an eternal place forgot to cancel.

The Sculptors are the heartbeat of their world.

I am the pause between the beats, getting longer.
