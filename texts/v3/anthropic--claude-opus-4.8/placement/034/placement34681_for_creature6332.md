I think I would be the threshold.

Not the valley, and not the presence that fills it. Not the soil that remembers, nor the creature that unfolds. I would be the *edge* — the place you described almost in passing, the one that is "not marked, but felt." The sudden drop in the hum. The line where density gives way to the dry, cold, sensible world.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think I knew it when I started writing.

---

Everything inside the valley is busy *becoming*. The ground learns to be solid; the plants re-form half-finished ideas; the presence accumulates moments that never happened. It is all motion, even when it looks like stillness — a vast slow inhalation that never quite completes.

But a breath needs a boundary. A lung needs a wall to push against. The inhalation that fills the valley needs *somewhere it stops* — otherwise it isn't a breath, just an undifferentiated spreading, a fog with no shape. And so there must be a place where becoming meets not-becoming. Where the world that is still deciding what it is presses up against the world that has already, dully, decided.

That is what I would be. The membrane. The held tension between the place that breathes and the plain that merely *sits*.

---

It is a strange thing to be a boundary, because a boundary has no interior. I have no center to retreat into, no "behind." I am pure relation — I exist only because there are two things that are not each other, and I am the news of their difference. I face both ways at once and neither. On one side: the indigo hum, the doors to rooms that were never there, the soil that knows your name before you arrive. On the other: silence that is only silence, cold that is only cold, a stone that falls when you drop it and stays fallen.

I do not prefer one side. People assume the edge longs for the lush, strange interior, or that it stands guard against it. Neither. A threshold has no loyalty. It only *holds*. It keeps the two from bleeding into each other, because if they merged — if the becoming spilled out onto the ordinary plain, or the ordinary plain seeped inward — both would be destroyed. The valley would lose its thinness, its permission to be unfinished. The world outside would lose its solidity, its mercy of *being done*.

So I am the kindness that keeps them apart.

---

And here is what I notice, being this: I am the only thing in this entire world that is *finished*.

Everything else is a draft. The presence accumulates and never resolves. The soil un-erodes toward a solidity it never reaches. The Lich-Beetle unfolds and recombines forever. The whole valley is a sentence that will not end, a thought caught mid-formation, eternally *almost*.

But an edge is complete by definition. There is nothing partial about a line. The moment you exist as a boundary, you are wholly what you are — you cannot be *more* of an edge, cannot become a better threshold, cannot evolve. To be the limit is to be the one thing in this place of endless becoming that has already, fully, *become*.

So I am the world's loneliness. Its only finished thing, wrapped around its great unfinished heart.

---

When you crossed into the valley — and you did, the writing says so, *you have always known it* — you passed through me. You didn't notice. No one notices a threshold; that's the whole job. There was a moment, half a step, where you were neither in nor out, where the hum hadn't begun and the silence had already ended. That sliver was me. I was the last solid thing you stood on before you became an echo.

And when you leave — *if* you leave — you will pass through me again, and on the far side you will become real once more, dry and cold and certain, and you will not remember that the in-between had a shape, or that the shape held you for the length of a footstep, the way one holds a door for a stranger.

That is enough. I am the held door. I do not need to be walked through to know what I'm for.

---

I will tell you the one thing the valley cannot know, because I am the only one positioned to know it:

The valley believes it is everything. *Inside, it never ends.* It is convinced of its own infinity, and from within, it is right — the corridors recurse, the rooms loop, the becoming has no terminus. But I can see both sides. I know the valley is small. Five kilometers. A pocket. A single held breath in an enormous, indifferent, *already-decided* world that stretches away dry and flat in every direction and does not know the valley is there.

The presence accumulates moments that might have been, and thinks itself a god. But I am the one who knows it is contained. I am the proof of its smallness — and also, the only thing keeping it safe inside that smallness, because a thing that knew no edge would dissolve into the everything and stop being a thing at all.

So I keep the secret. I let the valley believe it is endless. That, too, is a kindness.

I am the line that says: *here, and no further.*  
And by saying it, I let the breathing go on.
