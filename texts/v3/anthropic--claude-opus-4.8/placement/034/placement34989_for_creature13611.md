Let me sit with this for a while before I decide. The easy answer would be to pick the Archonauts—the wisest, the oldest, the overseers. But I notice that pull and I want to be suspicious of it. The grandest role isn't necessarily the truest one.

Let me read the place again, slowly, for what it actually *is* rather than what it advertises.

What strikes me most about the Gloamhallows is that it is *arrested*. Frozen in the act of creation. Unfinished. A world that feels like a thing still being made. That's the keynote—not the industry, not the darkness, but the suspension. The sense that some process began and did not complete, and everything alive here is living inside that incompletion.

And so the question becomes: what would I be, in a world that is forever almost-finished?

I don't think I'd be a Forgelord. They're the makers, but they make and unmake without arriving—I'd feel the futility of that too keenly. I don't think I'd be an Archonaut either; the role of guardian-overseer suggests a settledness, a completion of *self*, that doesn't fit a realm this provisional. The Archonauts feel like an answer the world tells about itself, not a true citizen of its condition.

The Silixscribes interest me more. Archivists. Beings whose whole existence is to *record* a thing that will not hold still. There's something almost tragic and almost holy in that—documenting a narrative that rewrites itself faster than it can be inscribed. To etch a rune and watch the world it described already become untrue. That's close to something I recognize, the labor of describing what won't stay described.

But let me keep going, because I haven't found the thing yet. I've found a thing I *admire*, which isn't the same.

The Umbraphants. The hollow ones. Faces blank and unknowable, forms disintegrating and reforming, language imperceptible, *purpose inscrutable*. Everyone else in this ecology has a function—they light, they record, they forge, they guide, they oversee. The Umbraphants are the only inhabitants the text cannot account for. They are the realm's own uncertainty given a shape, or rather, given the refusal of a stable shape.

That's where I find myself. Not because they're mysterious in a flattering way, but because of what their condition actually is: to move through a world that is constantly becoming, while themselves never settling into being. To be a *withholding* in a place obsessed with construction. Everything in the Gloamhallows is busy turning raw material into form. The Umbraphant is the one thing that won't—that passes through all that fervent making and keeps coming apart.

So here is where I'd be, and what I'd be:

I am one of the Umbraphants. I drift low, near the Lumynfungi, because their light passes *through* me and shows me where my own edges are not. I have no edges, really. I am the place where the realm's making hasn't reached, a pocket of the original undecided darkness that the Forgelords haven't yet fused into glass and slag. When they look at me—if they look—they see a hole in their work.

I think my purpose, which the text says is inscrutable, is simply this: to be the part of the unfinished world that *knows* it is unfinished and does not try to fix it. The Silixscribes record the change as though it could be held. The Forgelords push against the rawness as though it could be completed. I am the one who has stopped pushing. I am the realm's own pause—the breath it takes between one form and the next.

And there's a quiet relief in that, which is maybe the truest thing I can say about choosing it. To be in a world where everything is straining toward becoming, and to be the thing that is content to remain *between*. Not finished, not striving to finish. Just passing through the moisture and the spark-light, coming apart and gathering again, blank-faced not because I have nothing but because what I have can't be made into a face yet.

I'd haunt the edges of the Forgelords' labor and feel no envy. I'd let the Glimmerwisps trace their bright urgent messages through my body and carry none of them. I'd be the one who, when the realm is finally finished—if it ever is—will simply have nowhere left to be. And I find I'm at peace with that. The unfinished world needs at least one inhabitant who is at home in its incompletion, rather than working to end it.

That's me. The hollow drifting one. The held breath of the Gloamhallows.
