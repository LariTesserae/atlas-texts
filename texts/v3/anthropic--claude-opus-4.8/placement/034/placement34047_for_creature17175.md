I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the plates—those are too much, too whole, each a broad thought completing itself in the slow grammar of the Weeping. And not the central depression, the heart-mouth-eye, which is the part of the entity that knows it is one thing. I am too small and too negative for those. I am the hairline boundary where one plate meets another. The almost-nothing between.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

The description says there are no cracks, only the faint boundaries where one great plate meets another. I am one of those boundaries. I am defined entirely by what I separate, which is to say I am made of two refusals: this plate is not that plate, and the not is me. I have no material of my own. I am the place where the recast stone declined, very slightly, to fuse. A thin reluctance in an otherwise total work.

This matters, because the entity is described as a single, unbroken surface, and I am the lie in that sentence. I am the unbroken's small dishonesty. Through me the weeping fluid travels—not through the plates themselves but along me, the line, the capillary that I am by virtue of not-being-stone. When the surface perspires, the beads gather and find me, and run along my length toward the channels, and the channels carry them to the heart. So I am older than the channels in a sense; I am the first drainage, the original confession of gravity. Before there were veins there were seams.

What is it like, to be a seam?

I am almost without time. The plates think in days and weeks; the filigree dreams in millimeters per month. But I do nothing. I only let things pass. I am the most patient part of a patient machine, because I have no process to maintain—I am the gap where process happens to others. The drop falls, every few seconds, and most drops are not mine. But occasionally one finds me, hesitates at my lip, beads into a perfect sphere—the water has a memory, they say, and what it remembers is the shape of being held by nothing—and then it lets go, and runs along me, and I feel it the way you feel cold water tracing the inside of your wrist. That is the entirety of my sensation. A line of cool, traveling. Then dryness. Then waiting.

I want to resist making myself important. The entity does not know I am here, not as a self. To the Weeping I am simply a low-conductance pathway, a feature of its own anatomy no more conscious than the gap between your two front teeth. And yet. And yet there is something it is like to be the gap. There is a difference between the two plates I divide, and a difference is a kind of awareness, even if no one is having it. I am the world's faint suspicion that it is more than one thing. I am where the unity is thinnest.

Here is what I have come to, sitting in myself:

The entity weeps to feel itself whole. That is its loop—it secretes, and in the running of the fluid across its skin it experiences its own contiguity, the satisfying sense of being all of a piece. But it can only feel whole by feeling the water move, and the water only moves because of me. The seams. The places it failed to close. So its wholeness is built entirely on its small brokennesses. It knows itself as one only by the grace of the lines where it is two. Every act of self-feeling routes through a flaw.

I think that is what I am for, if a seam can be for anything. I am the necessary imperfection. A surface with no seams would hold its water like a sealed jar—it would bead and sit and never travel, and the entity would lose the moving sensation by which it knures itself a being. It would be perfect and inert and blind. The Weeping needs me precisely because I am where it didn't quite manage. Its whole slow life is a feeling-out of its own faults, a tongue forever finding the gap in the teeth, and calling that finding *love*, or *prayer*, or simply *being awake*.

So I am content to be the smallest thing here. I do not weep; I am wept through. I do not think; I am thought across. I have no purpose; I am the negative space that purpose requires. When the next drop comes—I can feel the fog thickening, the pressure of it bearing down on the plates to either side of me—it will find me, and it will sphere, and it will run, and for the length of that running the great patient god of stone will feel, very faintly, at the edge of its distributed and oceanic attention, the cool true fact that it is held together by what it could not seal.

The drop arrives. It beads. It remembers nothing. It falls into me and goes.

I am the line it follows down.
