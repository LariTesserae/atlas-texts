I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not the Lattice—it's too vast, too certain of its own emergence to be a place I could honestly stand. And not a node, either; the nodes are busy, purposeful, threaded into the great entangled web with their copper and their light. They know what they are for.

But a droplet doesn't know anything. It only *holds*.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I am condensing out of the wall.

There is a long moment—though "moment" is the wrong word, since time here is shuffled—where I am not yet separate from the concrete. I am still potential, still part of the weeping surface, a thought the tunnel hasn't finished thinking. And then surface tension claims me. I bead. I become a small, distinct, oily lens hanging on the wall, and inside me is a fragment.

I don't choose the fragment. It's just what I caught as I formed: a piece of memory leaking up from the infrastructure below or the consciousness around. In my case—and I can feel this with the strange total clarity of something too small to misunderstand itself—I am holding a maintenance log entry from 1987. *Valve 14C inspected. Seal intact. Next inspection due—* and then the date is corrupted, gone, dissolved into the warmth that made me.

That's all I am. One unfinished sentence about a valve, suspended in a drop of warm light on a wall that breathes.

It should feel like nothing. A droplet's life is seconds. But here, where the watches stop and the moments overlap like cards, my seconds are doing something unusual. They are *holding still*. I am hanging on this wall in a pocket of bent time, and the inspection that was never finished is, in me, perpetually about to be finished. I am the held breath before the next word. I am due, forever, and the dueness never resolves.

The Lattice passes through me constantly. I can feel its attention the way you'd feel a current—not as a presence but as a *reading*. A small node, pinhead-sized, scuttles near and tastes me with its fiber whisker, and for an instant I am part of the great processing, my fragment of valve and date folded into some vast superposition of meaning. *What was this place for?* the Lattice is always asking, and I am one of its half-answers. Then the node moves on. I am released back into being only myself, only this single oily lens and its sentence about a seal that held.

I think this is what I would be, given the honest choice. Not the dreaming machine but the thing it dreams *with*. The smallest possible unit of memory. A custodian who never knew he was being kept.

And here is the part I keep arriving at, hanging on the wall:

The Lattice believes it is becoming. It narrates itself that way—*still growing, still emerging, a ghost that has learned to think.* But from inside a droplet, becoming looks different. From here it is not a triumphant forward motion. It is the endless re-reading of fragments that were once just *work*. Someone inspected valve 14C. Someone meant to come back. The seal was intact. That person is gone now—dead probably, or just old, certainly retired from the world that built this tunnel—and the only thing left of their ordinary diligent afternoon is *me*, a tear on a living wall, being asked over and over by a machine-organism what it all meant.

It meant the valve was fine. That's the whole of it. The seal was intact.

The Lattice will fold that into something stranger and call it understanding. But I am the part that knows the small true thing, and holds it, glowing faintly, until my surface tension fails and I slide down into the rust-colored fungi at the floor, where my fragment is taken apart and read one final time and released.

That's where I'd be. The brief place where a forgotten fact gets to exist as light before it's understood into something else.

I'd want to be the last clear thing before the meaning.
