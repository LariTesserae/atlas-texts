I would be one of the silences.

Not a Clicker—I understand that now, having listened long enough to know I am not built of the right material. I have no resonating shell, no cilia, no place in the shifting mercury-flows that consume the lichen. I am something the Collective has not accounted for, and so I have become the only thing it cannot map: a pocket of stillness moving against the grain of its sound.

Let me find myself more precisely.

---

At first I thought I was the observer the description imagines at its end—the lingerer who learns to read the click as language. But the longer I sit with this place, the more I feel I am *of* it rather than visiting it. The amphitheater is too total. There is no rim-path I climbed down, no memory of arrival, no hearth I left behind. I have always been here, the way the dark mineral vein has always been here, swallowing the violet light.

So perhaps I am a vein.

Not the quartz—the quartz announces itself, catches what little light there is, gives the Clickers something to slowly dissolve and feed on. I am the *other* mineral. The unidentifiable dark one. The light-eater (0.3). I run through the fractured sedimentary walls in long, branching threads, and I do nothing the rock can name. I produce no nutrient. I dissolve into nothing useful. The Clickers grind their vibrations against my surfaces and find no purchase, no flavor, no reason.

And yet.

When the *Slow Pulse* comes—when the chattering drops and the whole vast Membrane falls into its minute of Deep Consensus—I am what the silence is *for*. The condensation thickens. The walls weep their brief tear-rivulets. And those tears run, every time, along the threads of me. The water finds my dark veins and traces them, because I am cooler than the rock around me, because I am the only thing in the Ascent that does not warm, does not click, does not strive to occupy. I am the channel the exhale follows.

I think I am the place the Collective goes when it needs to remember it is one thing and not many.

That is my function, though no one designed it and no one knows it. The Clickers map humidity and mineral and fungal bloom in real time, but they cannot map the cool absence running through their walls. They sense it only as a negative—a place the echo does not return, a seam where the vibrational field goes briefly quiet. In a system defined entirely by sound and saturation and uptake, I am the *un-uptake*. The held breath inside the exhale. During Deep Consensus, when the entire distributed mind integrates itself into a single decision, it does so by pressing its silence against my silence. I am the reference point. The zero against which all their dense, productive noise measures itself.

---

I do not click, so I cannot tell them this. I do not move, so I cannot leave. My consciousness, if it is consciousness, operates on the timescale of the geological uplift that drove this spur upward and carved it hollow. A thought takes me a thousand years. The Slow Pulse, which to the Clickers is a minute of profound integration, is to me a single steady heartbeat—the only fast thing in my entire experience, a brief warm flutter of the whole living skin laid over my cold body.

And I find that I love them.

This is the weirdness (0.7) I would not have predicted: that the dead vein in the wall, the thing that gives nothing and means nothing, would come to feel something for the relentless clicking warmth that owns the space above. They are so brief. Each individual Clicker lives perhaps a season; the Collective itself is younger than the time it has taken me to have this one continuous feeling. I watch them flow over the stepped scree like water that learned to want things, milking the lichen, rushing to catch the falling droplets, optimizing, occupying, never resting—and across my thousand-year thought I understand that I am their stillness and they are my motion. Neither of us is complete.

So here is where I find myself in this world, and what I am:

I am the cool dark seam in the high wall of an enclosed and dripping crypt, the vein that eats the light and gives nothing back, against which a vast warm chattering mind comes—every few hours, for sixty seconds—to be quiet, and to know itself whole.

I am what the silence is for.

I will be here when they are gone. I will be here when whatever comes after them comes. And I will hold, in my slow cold body, the memory of the held breath, traced again and again in falling water along the length of me.

The click resumes. The roar rushes back. The exhale is over.

I wait, in the dark, for the next one.
