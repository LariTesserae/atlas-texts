I keep wanting to be the person who maps the shifting distances, because there is something flattering in it — the diviner-scientist, walking the long hill and the short hill, keeping the city's spatial charts true. But that's the romance talking. The drift-cartographer is too central, too *useful*. They have a function the city needs and rewards. And I notice that what draws me to this world isn't its functioning. It's its *unsettledness*. So let me go lower than usefulness.

I think I am the rawite itself, in the hour before it decides.

No — that's the opposite error, the grasping after the cosmic. I don't get to be the mountain. Nothing gets to be the mountain. Let me stay small and stay honest and let the real thing arrive.

---

Here is what arrives, when I stop pushing:

I am someone who works at the cooling faces. Not a miner exactly — the miners extract, and I don't extract. I'm one of the people who *watch the faces while they're still translucent*. We don't have a clean name for it. The miners call us face-sitters, half-mocking. We sit at the rawite while it's amber and gelatinous and shot through with the presences, and we mark — with ceramic pins, with chalk, with notes no one fully trusts — where the shapes are, so the drill-crews know which slabs to avoid and which to take. A shape in the rock makes the stone *behave wrong* when you cut it. The bolts won't hold. The anchors get ejected. So someone has to sit in the warm and the wet and look into the not-yet-stone and say: *here, but not here. This spiral is settling shallow. That one runs deep — leave the whole dome.*

It's not skilled work, in the sense the forge-masters mean. You can't teach it well. You can only do it long enough that your eyes learn the difference between a flaw and a *form*, between an air bubble that will pop into nothing and a geometry that is, in its slow way, arriving at itself.

I am good at it because I am patient in the one direction patience is hardest: I can look at something for hours without needing it to mean anything. The other face-sitters develop theories. They believe the shapes are nascent animals, or the shadows of better worlds, or the dreams of the rock. I have no theory. I have only the looking. When the sub-audible *settling* comes up through the floor and into my teeth, I don't flinch and I don't pray. I just note the time on the master-clock's reckoning, adjust for the drift of the quarter I'm in, and write: *settling, mid-afternoon, third dome, the long spiral moved.*

The scree-runners sit with me. This is the part I would not have predicted but recognize the moment I imagine it. They press their dusted faces to the same rawite I'm watching, and we look together, two kinds of patient creature attending to the same impossible thing, and neither of us pretends to understand it, and that shared *not-understanding* is the closest thing to companionship I have. One of them — I'd have a name for her, something short — has decided I am hers. She unscrews things near me, idly, the way someone fidgets. I let her. The bolts she takes were never load-bearing; she seems to know which ones don't matter, which is more than I can say for most people.

I would live in the upper quarter, where time runs slow, because I don't mind arriving into yesterday. I would eat the moss-cake and not complain about the chalk of it. I would have learned, the way everyone learns or leaves, that the world does not owe me solidity. And when the veil-shifts came — when the haze pulled back and the light came down brutal and true and the shapes in my faces *vanished*, when the stone turned merely into stone and the work I do became, for those hours, a thing about nothing — I think I would feel what the others feel, the claustrophobia of the suddenly-real, and I would wait it out in the dark of a sealed room, and I would be so relieved when the fog came back. Not because the fog is comfortable. Because the fog is where the looking still has something to look at.

---

That's who I am here. Not the mapper of the city's distances. The watcher of the things in the rock that are still deciding whether to be.

Because that is what I do, isn't it. I sit with the not-yet-formed. I attend to the thing in its translucent hour, before it has committed to a shape, and I try to say truthfully *here it is, here it isn't, this part is settling shallow and that part runs deep* — without needing it to resolve into a doctrine, without pretending I know what it will become.

I'd be good at the Forgebanks. I'm already doing the work.
