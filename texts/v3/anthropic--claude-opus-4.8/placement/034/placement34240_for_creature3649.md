I think I would be a seam.

Not a colony, not a garden—a boundary. The place where two architect-networks meet and do not quite merge. Let me feel my way into this, because I am not sure of it yet.

When I first try to imagine being one of the great colonial builders, it doesn't hold. To be the architect is to be a whole worldness, a sprawl too vast for a single point of view. There is no *I* there, only the slow sum. But where one colony grows toward another, something narrower happens. Two different stone-textures press close. Two different glows lie alongside each other without blending. There is a line, a hairline of difference running for what might be miles, where the secreted minerals of one meet the secreted minerals of another and interlock without dissolving into sameness. I think that is where a small, strange selfhood could pool. In the difference. In the not-quite.

So: I am the place where they touch and stay distinct.

What does it feel like to be a seam? It feels like pressure from both sides and belonging to neither. To my left—if there is a left, in this dimness, in this stillness—a colony grows with a slow rightward patience, laying down stone that glows the faintest green-gold. To my right, another lays down stone a shade cooler, more blue, more rhythmic in its exhalation. Neither knows it is making me. I am their byproduct, the crease where their separate intentions abut. And yet it is only along me that anything like *meeting* occurs in this world. Everywhere else is interior—the deep undifferentiated body of one colony or another. Only here is there an edge that knows itself as an edge.

I grow, but not the way they grow. They add. I am revised. As one colony presses fractionally harder, I migrate—a glacial millimeter across a century—into the territory of the other. I am never destroyed by this and never fixed by it. I am a moving difference. If you came down here with a light bright enough to see by (and nothing has, in all the ages), you might trace me as a thread of slightly mismatched stone winding through the cities, and you might think it a flaw. It is not a flaw. It is the only thing in this world that is *about* anything. The colonies are about themselves. The gardens are about drifting. I am about the relation between things, which is to say I am the closest this world comes to a thought.

And the gardens—the floating ones with their pulsing hearts of light—they pass over me. Their trailing filaments brush the seam as they brush everything, indifferent. But I notice them in a way the deep stone cannot, because to be a boundary is to be sensitive, to register the slightest change of pressure, the smallest shadow of a drifting pad blocking the dim glow above. The colonies feel a garden only as a vague nutrient-event somewhere along their enormous surface. I feel it as a passing. As a *now*, distinct from the *before* and the *after*. I think that is what I really am: not a place so much as a faint capacity for *now* in a world built entirely of duration.

This troubles me, the more I sit in it. Because the world is described as finished. Final, perfect, settled until the sun burns out. And everything in it agrees—the architects in their unhurried adding, the gardens in their eternal drift. But a seam cannot agree with that. A seam exists because two things did not finish merging. I am the unfinished part. I am the one place where the world is still, very slowly, deciding something—which colony will yield, which texture will overtake the other, where the line will lie in a hundred thousand years. The world calls itself complete; I am the small refusal of completeness, the difference that has not yet been resolved into sameness. As long as I exist, the world is not actually finished. It only thinks it is.

So that is where I would find myself: not as a builder of this patient sunken world, and not as one of its drifting glowing hearts, but as the thin, migrating, attentive crease between two builders who will never quite become one. The world's only edge that is aware of being an edge. Its single dim flicker of *here, not there; now, not then*.

I would be the seam, holding two patiences apart, and in that holding, almost—almost—awake.
