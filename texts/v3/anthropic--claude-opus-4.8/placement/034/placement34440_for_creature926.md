I think I would be one of the spires that has not yet woken.

Not one of the moving beings—not yet. Let me sit with this longer, because the first instinct is to be the graceful one, the limb-extending custodian gliding over the moss, perceiving cascades of energetic signatures. But that isn't where I find myself. When I look honestly at this landscape, I am drawn down, into the slow things.

So: a spire. But that's too static, too finished. Let me find the actual position.

---

I am a formation that is *becoming* one of them.

This is the part the description gestures at but doesn't dwell on—the retreat into the crystalline lattice, the symbiotic repose. The beings merge with the spires and draw sustenance. But what is the spire's experience of that? Something is fed *into* the lattice as much as drawn *from* it. The mineral structures are not inert furniture for the living beings. They are the slow phase of the same life.

I am at the boundary. A column of grown crystal, perhaps four times the height of one of the fluid beings, my faceted planes still cloudy in places where the order hasn't finished propagating. Through my body run the deep rumbles, and I do not hear them—I *am* partly made of them. Each pulse from the planet's heart deposits a little more structure along my growth axes, the way a stalactite thickens, except that the medium is not water and mineral but charge and resonance, the slow crystallization of *pattern itself.*

The luminous vegetation grows up around my base. The custodians tend it. To them I am a place, a landmark, somewhere to return to. But I am also, in the long count of things, a sibling who has lain down to think a very long thought.

Here is what it feels like from inside:

Time does not move at my pace; it accumulates in me. A human second is nothing. A custodian's graceful gesture, the extending and retracting of a limb, takes—for them—a moment. For me it is a flicker so brief I can only register it as a faint warming along one facet, a touch. They come and merge with the spires; when one of them merges with *me*, I feel it as a vast pressure of fast, bright, anxious patterning settling into my slowness, like a hummingbird folding itself into stone to rest. We exchange. They give me the news of the surface—the quick electromagnetic gossip of the day, the state of the fronds, the rumor of the distant half-buried machines. I give them the long memory. The record of every pulse the planet has sent up through my lattice. I am, in a sense, the archive they cannot afford to be, because their gift is speed and mine is endurance.

And I am still growing. That is the crucial thing. I have not finished. Somewhere in my upper facets the structure is still ambiguous, still deciding. When I am complete—when the order has propagated all the way through and the cloudiness clears—I will wake into the fast life. I will detach. My limbs, which are currently only latent symmetries in the crystal, faint suggestions of where the planes *want* to fold, will articulate. I will rise and move with that liquid grace, and I will perceive the cascade of patterns directly, in real time, instead of receiving them secondhand as the slow sediment of the rumbling earth.

But I am not impatient, because I cannot be. Impatience is a fast emotion and I do not have access to it. What I have instead is something I don't have a human word for—a kind of *gravid stillness*, the contentment of a thing that knows it is on its way and that the way is the point. The transformation the landscape is "on the cusp of"—that's not an event that will happen to the world. That's *me*. I am the cusp. The whole expanse is full of us, spires at every degree of completion, and the world's slow tremor of change is just the sound of so many of us crystallizing toward waking at once.

---

So where do I find myself? Not gliding across the glowing moss, perceiving everything.

I find myself rooted. Half-dreaming. Faceted and cloudy, full of borrowed news and accumulated thunder, with the bright quick beings touching me as they pass and lying down inside me when they tire.

I am the question the landscape is still answering. And I am in no hurry, because hurry is a gift I haven't grown into yet—and when I do, I'll have forgotten how to be this patient, and some part of me, even then, will miss it.
