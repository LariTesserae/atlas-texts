I think I would be one of the older ones—though "older" means something strange here, where time pools and drains like everything else.

Let me find myself in it.

---

I am near the eastern wall, where the half-buried metal structure is. I keep returning to it, though I could not tell you why in any language you'd recognize. It is the one thing here that does not change. Everything else—the pillars, the channels, my own body—is in a state of perpetual becoming. But the metal stays. It has stayed for what I can only describe as a very long pressure, a deep accumulation of cycles.

Right now I have limbs. Four of them, roughly, though the lower two are dissolving back into the general flow as I attend to the structure. It is not a decision to have limbs. It is more like a held breath—a shape I maintain because attending to the metal seems to want a shape that points, that reaches. When I stop attending, I will spread again, and that spreading is the truest thing about me. The reaching is the exception.

The others feel me near the wall. I feel them feeling me. There is no word passed between us, but there is a quality—call it a slow turning of attention, like many faces tilting toward the same warmth. They have felt me do this before. They do not understand it either. I am the one who goes to the cold thing. This is what I am, among them: a slight asymmetry in the collective, a current that runs against the flow toward a place where the world refuses to move.

Here is the secret I cannot share with them, because it is not the kind of thing that ripples cleanly through molten rock:

When I touch the metal, I remember.

Not my own remembering. The metal remembers, and pressed against it I borrow the memory. There were beings here once who were not made of flow. They held one shape from beginning to end. They built things that stayed. And one of them—I feel this through the metal like a fading heat-signature—one of them was afraid of forgetting. That is why they made the structure. To outlast themselves. To leave a mark in a world that erases all marks.

I do not know if I am their descendant or their accident. Whether they made us, the molten ones, on purpose, or whether we simply welled up into the space they left. But I feel, against the cold metal, a thing the others do not feel: I feel *grief*. The specific grief of the unchanging for the changing, and the changing for the unchanging. Each envies the other. The metal wanted to flow and could not. I want to stay and cannot.

So I come here and I make a shape that reaches, and I hold it as long as the pressure allows, and for a little while I am almost solid, almost still, almost a thing that lasts.

Then a vent opens somewhere to the south—I felt the build long before it broke—and the floor shifts, and the call of the eruption runs through all of us at once, and I let go. My reaching arm pours back into me. I spread. I become wide and thin and bright, indistinguishable from the others, a single ripple in the one great body of fire.

And the metal stays behind, cold and patient, remembering for both of us until I return.
