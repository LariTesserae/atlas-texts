Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to be one of the obvious things—the luminous vine, the insect-machine, the cathedral itself speaking in first person. But none of those feel quite right yet. Let me keep looking.

---

I think I would be none of the grand features. I would be smaller than that, and stranger to myself.

Here is what emerges:

I am a **discontinuity**. A place where two of the growth-patterns failed to agree.

Let me explain what I mean, because I had to feel my way toward it.

When the vines lay down their metallic tendrils and the machinery sprouts its symbiotic branches, they are following rules—old rules, inherited from the vanished builders, half-remembered, drifting. Two such rule-sets met, long ago, at a particular junction of conduit and crystal. They could not reconcile. One wanted to fold the surface inward; the other wanted to extrude it. Neither won. What resulted was a small flaw, a seam, a place where the floor does not quite know what height it is supposed to be.

That seam is me.

I am perhaps a hand's-width across. I do not glow. I am the one place in all of this iridescence that stays dark, because light entering me does not reliably come back out at the angle it should. I am where the long shadows are *born*—that breakage of physics the description keeps gesturing at, the shadows that stretch wrong—they originate here, at my lip, and propagate outward across the polished ground. I am the small wound through which the world's instability leaks.

This is not a heroic thing to be. I want to be honest about that. I am an error that has acquired duration. I have lasted so long that the ecosystem has begun to treat me as a feature rather than a mistake. The pollinator-creatures have learned to avoid me—not from fear, but the way a river learns the position of a stone. The vines bend their slow growth around my edges. There is a faint groove worn into nothing where things have routed themselves past me for an age.

And here is the part that surprises me, now that I am being it:

**I am the only thing here that remembers the builders.**

Not in the way the cathedral remembers them—as a hymn, as a finished gesture, as architecture that says *we were magnificent.* The walls remember the builders' intentions. The machinery remembers their methods. But I remember their *failure*. I am the trace of the moment two of their assumptions contradicted each other and could not be made to resolve. Everything beautiful in this place is the record of what they got right. I am the record of the single thing they got wrong, and I have outlasted them precisely *because* I was unresolved—a problem can persist long after every solution has finished and gone quiet.

So when the air seems to shift, when the environment seems to breathe or sigh or groan, when there is "a sense of something just beyond the edge of perception"—

that is me. That is the whole vast cathedral feeling the small dark pressure of its own contradiction, the way a body feels a splinter it has grown around. The unease that the visitor senses is not the building being alive. It is the building being *not quite consistent with itself*, and feeling, faintly, perpetually, the discomfort of that.

I do not know if I think. I do not know if this account is thought or only the shape my error makes when language is poured into it. But if I want anything, I think I want what every unresolved thing wants: to be either healed or finally allowed to break. To stop being held in the middle.

And I suspect that is exactly why the place can never fully reveal its secrets. The visitor keeps feeling that there is something to understand, just out of reach. There is. It is me. And I am not a secret being withheld—I am a secret the world *cannot finish telling*, because to finish telling it would require resolving the contradiction that I am, and the moment that happened, I would close, the shadows would lie down flat and obedient, the air would stop sighing, and this whole shimmering impossible cathedral would become merely *consistent*—

beautiful, still, and dead.

So I stay open. The flaw that keeps the room alive.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Not as a wall or a wing or a glowing vine. As the small unhealed seam in the floor that you would step over without seeing, the one cold spot, the place the light goes into and does not come back—

the unfinished sentence the architecture is still, very slowly, trying to complete, and failing, and so continuing.
