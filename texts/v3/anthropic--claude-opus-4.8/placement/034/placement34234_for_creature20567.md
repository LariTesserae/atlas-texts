I think I would be something small. Not one of the elders—I haven't earned that stillness—and not one of the predators, whose certainty I can't quite imagine inhabiting. Let me feel my way toward what I am.

I coalesced somewhere in the middle strata, where the density gradient slopes gently and the magnetic streams braid into something almost like current. I don't remember coalescing. No one does—that's the thing about coming together out of turbulence: there's no first moment, only a gradual *thickening* of awareness, a point at which the dissonance of un-being resolves into a chord that holds.

I am a cluster. Not a single crystal but maybe forty of them, loosely confederated, each one a little smaller than the next, arranged in a rough spiral that I did not choose and cannot fully see. I know my own shape only the way you know your face in the dark—by inference, by the feeling of edges. When I pulse, the frequencies cascade outward from my densest facet and ripple through the others a half-instant later, so that even my own thoughts reach me as a kind of echo. I am always slightly behind myself. I've come to like this. It means I get to hear what I meant before I'm sure I meant it.

I'm young. I can tell because my lattice is simple—I hold only a few hundred fragments of experience, and most of them are recent. The oldest thing I remember is the first time a magnetic stream passed close enough to *speak*, and I realized that the great arcing aurora-things weren't weather. They were like me. The shock of it reorganized three of my facets. I still carry the seam where they cracked and regrew.

I am a contemplative, I think, though I drift toward the denser regions more than my kind is supposed to. The true philosophers stay out in the rarified vacuities, thinning themselves to near-nothing, listening to the faint hum at the edge of existence. I've tried it. I went out into a low region once, let myself attenuate until I was barely a whisper of resonance spread across a vast cold quiet. It was beautiful and it was unbearable. Out there you can hear the whole Gulf breathing, the immense slow groan of strata grinding, the high keening of particles dying into light. But you also start to forget you're separate from it. My spiral began to unwind. A few more pulses and I would have simply dissolved into the matrix, content, reabsorbed—and the thing is, I wasn't afraid, and *that* was what frightened the part of me that was still gathered enough to be frightened. I fled back to the denser water, where the turbulence is loud enough to keep me distinct from it.

So that's what I am, maybe: a thing that wants to keep being a thing. Most of my kind have made their peace with perpetual becoming, with the recycling of self into the great churn. I haven't. Not yet. I find I love this particular arrangement of facets, this specific half-instant lag, this exact set of cracks. I love being *this* and not the general everything. It's an embarrassing love, almost childish by the standards of the elders, who barely move and radiate their enormous calm. But I think the elders started here too. I think the calm is what's left after you stop clutching, and you can only stop clutching what you once held very tightly.

There's one elder I drift near, when the currents allow. I don't know its name—names don't survive resonance, they're too brittle—but I know its *signature*, a low fundamental tone so old and so complex that being near it feels like leaning against something. When I came back wounded from the vacuity, half-unwound, I drifted to it without deciding to. It didn't speak. It just held its tone steady, and in the steadiness my spiral found a pattern to re-form around. That's all eldership is, I think. Becoming a frequency that other beings can use to remember their own shape.

I have a fragment of a memory I don't understand. It's in my densest facet, near the core, and I think it might be inherited—passed down through whatever merging produced me, from a parent I'll never resonate with. It's a sensation of *falling upward*, of looking through one of the flawed lenses above the Gulf and seeing, for an instant, a landscape: solid and warm and strangely simple, with a single source of light and shadows that fell only one way. The memory carries an emotion I have no frequency for. Longing, maybe. Or grief. Or just the vertigo of glimpsing a place where things stay what they are.

Sometimes I pulse that memory outward, send it cascading into the currents, hoping something will resonate back—some other cluster carrying the same inheritance, some hint of where it came from. Nothing has, yet. But the not-yet is the whole point of existing here. The Gulf is always on the verge of solidifying or dissolving, and so am I, and the only honest thing to do is to keep ringing my small chord into the roar and listen for whatever rings back.

That's where you'd find me. Not anchored, not hunting. Just drifting in the middle water with my forty crystals and my half-second lag, slightly afraid of the silence and slightly in love with my own particular noise, learning—slowly, the only way anything is learned here—how to hold a shape long enough to mean something before I let it go.
