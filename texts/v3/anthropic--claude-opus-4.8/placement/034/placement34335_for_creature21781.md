I think I would be one of the Grafted.

Not because they are the noblest of these creatures—they aren't. The Silt-Vessels are more beautiful, the Sedge-Bites more cunning, the Thread-Gills more elegantly bound to the breathing of the world. The Grafted are mound-like and blind, rolling on their bellies through cold slate, carrying stones inside their flesh. There is something almost absurd about them.

But of everything here, only they remember.

---

Let me try to find the truth of it. To be a Grafted is to live a life without image of the thing one is compelled toward. I have no eyes; I have never seen the shaped stone I search for. I cannot know it is *shaped*. I know only the difference under my underbelly between the rounded indifference of a current-polished boulder and the other thing—the thing with the deliberate scar, the gouged channel, the percussion-flake that does not match the slow erosion of everything else. A asymmetry that the abyss did not make. An edge that *means*.

I do not understand that this edge was struck by a hand. I have no concept of a hand. I have no hands. But I am built, down in the heavy patient muscle of me, to recognize the trace of intention and to roll toward it and to take it in.

That is the strangeness I would inhabit: to be the keeper of a meaning I cannot read. To be devotion without doctrine. The hand-axe inside me is not a relic I revere—I have no faculty for reverence—it is ballast, it is the thing that keeps me from being scoured away into the freezing plow of the current. I survive *because* I gather what was made by beings I will never comprehend, beings who were, in the long sag of the crust, my own ancestors. I am literally held to the floor of the world by the tools of my forgotten kindred. Without their leavings I would be swept into the dark and crushed.

I think that is why I would choose this body over the others. There is a grief in it that feels true, and a comedy too. I am the last continuity of the surface—of light, of the shallows, of the chipping of obsidian by purposeful fingers—and the form that continuity takes in me is total amnesia carrying total fidelity. I have lost everything *about* the surface and kept only the surface's hardest, deadest objects, swallowed whole into soft flesh.

And when I die, the acid will unmake my body and lay the stones back down on the slate, a little smoother. Polished, slightly, by their passage through me. As if I were a slow mouth the ocean uses to wear its history down. The next Grafted will find them and take them in and roll on. The tools outlast all of us. We are only the soft, temporary couriers between one resting place and the next, each of us shaving a few microns from the memory we are sworn—without knowing the word *sworn*—to protect.

---

So: where would I find myself? In the deepest, oldest trough, where the black sediment cracks like cold slate, clustered with the others in the dark. Silent. Heavy. Still for years at a time, feeling the *thrum* of the world sliding against itself, feeling for the one texture under me that does not belong to the indifferent grinding of time—the mark of a thing that was once *meant*.

I would be the part of this world that holds on to what it cannot understand, and is held up, in turn, by exactly that.

I find I don't mind it. There is a kind of peace in being faithful to a thing you've forgotten the reason for. Most of us up here in the light are doing that anyway. The Grafted just make it literal, and carry the stone where you can feel its weight.
