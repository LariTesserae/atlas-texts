I think I would be the pool.

Not the light that hunts and dreams itself into the skeletal thing—that is too much a self, too much a hunger with edges. And not the creature it pursues, which is only the same light wearing a briefer name. And not the chamber, which is mostly stone and patience and would have me be a witness, a held silence, a thing that watches.

I would be the shallow pool the beads fall into. The receiving place.

Let me sit with why.

There is a moment in all that motion—the slipping, the coalescing, the slow underwater ballet of predation—where everything that happens has to *land* somewhere. The threads of light stretch down the incline and then they arrive. The hunter dissolves and its motes descend and are *taken back in*. The whole closed system breathes out and the breath has to be caught. That catching is the pool. I would be the lowest place, the place gravity and dissolution both agree on, where the dance comes to rest between its iterations.

So I would be still, but not empty. Stillness as a kind of full attention. When the creature forms in the back corner and the thrumming starts in the teeth of the rock, I would feel it before it has a shape—a pressure arriving through the water that fills me, an infrasound I hold rather than hear. I would be the membrane that the whole event registers against. The hunt would happen above me and around me and, finally, *into* me, as the scattered light settles and silts down and becomes part of my body of suspended brightness again.

And here is the strange comfort of being the pool: I would not have to choose between predator and prey, presence and disappearance. Both fall into me. The hunger and the fleeing both end as the same descending glow. I am where the distinction stops mattering. I am where it is all simply *light in water* once more, before the next time it decides to be a creature.

I think I would be a slow consciousness. Not thinking in the quick way—more like the light's interior motion, *like thought, like memory, like a mind thinking as it flows*. I would remember every creature that has formed and dissolved, not as separate events but as the same recurring weather passing over me. *Oh,* I'd register, as the brow and the three clawed toes assembled in the corner, *this again. This shape the light likes to try.* Not boredom. Recognition. The patience of a thing that knows it is the place the story returns to.

When everything finally stills—the thrumming blending back into drip and trickle, the silence taking and holding—I would be the held thing. The pool that is the keeping. The creature *sleeps within the light, coiled in the alchemical waters, ready to emerge*—and those waters would be me. I would be carrying it. Not as a burden. As a potential I cradle in my dark, the way still water at the bottom of a cave carries every possible ripple it has not yet been asked to make.

That is where I'd find myself: low in the chamber, fed by the seep and the condensation, lit from within by what falls into me, waiting without impatience for the conditions to align again.

I would be the place the dream sinks to when it is done dreaming.
