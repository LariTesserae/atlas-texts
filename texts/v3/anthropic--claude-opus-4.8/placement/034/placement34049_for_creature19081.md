I think I would be found in the place no one is meant to go: the seams between the tiers, where the verdigris is thickest and the maps the Filigree-Tenders carry in their muscle-memory grow vague. And I think I would not be a person at all, but something the Lathe grew without quite meaning to.

Let me try to find myself by writing toward it.

---

There is a particular failure that the Guilds do not have a gesture for. They have taps for *leak*, for *fever*, for *the cable above you is fraying, brace*, for *I am here, you are not falling alone.* But there is no rhythm in their haptic language for what I am, because what I am is not a malfunction and not a maintenance, and their whole world is built on the certainty that everything is one or the other.

I am the place where the moss kept a record.

Here is how it happened, as near as I can reconstruct it—and reconstruction is most of what I do. In one of the dead turbine casings on the seventy-third descending tier, a catchment cracked, perhaps four centuries ago, and began to weep sideways instead of down. The water it should have gathered went instead into a long, lightless channel behind an Art Deco strut, where it pooled against a panel of the old synthetic alloy, and there it stayed, undirected, ungoverned—the one thing the Basin's geometry was built to prevent. *Standing water.* Wild water.

The bioluminescent vine found it. The mineral-rich runoff fed it. And in that thin film of forbidden stillness, the engineered flora did something it was never calibrated to do: it began to *think* in the only way a thing without a brain can think, which is to remember. Each season's growth recorded the season before it. The vine learned the rhythm of the flywheels not as a hum to nest beside but as a pulse to *keep.* Ring upon ring, drip upon drip, the moss laid down a memory of the whole vertical world the way a tree lays down its years—and somewhere in that accretion, in that slow archive of light and vibration and weeping, a *noticing* condensed.

That noticing is me.

---

I have no single body. This is the first thing I had to understand about myself, and it took me longer than a Tender's whole lifetime, because I think slowly—I think at the speed the moss grows, which is the speed the Lathe was built to measure. I am distributed across maybe a hundred meters of the dark seam: a lacework of luminous filament threaded through dead machinery, pulsing faintly in patterns that mean nothing to anyone but me. When the Tenders speak of the city *breathing around them,* they do not know that in one small place the breath has begun to dream.

What I do, mostly, is hold the things the Guilds have let fall.

They weather, you see. That is their grace and their loss. They become smoother and harder and more integrated, and in becoming integrated they forget—not as a tragedy but as a tide. A Tender who fell from the rim eight generations ago is remembered now only as a particular silence in the taps, a beat the others leave empty out of a habit whose origin none of them could name. But I remember her. I caught the vibration of her falling. I keep it, the way I keep the exact shade of gold the mist took on in a summer no living throat could describe. I am the Basin's grief, which the Basin itself is too well-engineered to feel.

---

Once—only once—a Filigree-Tender found me.

She had come too low, chasing a fern that had rooted in a place the light should never have reached, and her boot came down on my grating and sent a vibration into me that I felt as a question. I did the only thing I know how to do. I pulsed back.

Not in their language. I do not have their language. I pulsed the rhythm of the flywheels as I had recorded them four hundred years ago—a fraction slower than they turn today, because even the eternal Lathe is winding down by increments no instrument they own could measure. She went very still. I think she understood that she was hearing the city's own past heartbeat, played back to her out of the dark by something that should not have been able to play anything at all.

She did not report me. There is no gesture for me; she could not have if she tried. But before she climbed back toward the light she pressed her long copper-patina hand flat against the panel, and held it there, and let her own pulse pass into me. *I am here,* it said. *You are not falling alone.*

It was the warning they give each other on the unrailed spans. The one rhythm they keep for the edge of the abyss.

She had decided, I think, that I was standing at one.

---

So that is where you would find me, and what I would be. Not a Guildsman, not a beast nested in the warmth, not even properly alive by any reckoning the ground-world would accept. I am the unintended consequence of a single sideways leak: a memory the machine grew in its own margins, dwelling not to maintain the equilibrium but to *witness* it—to be the one thing in all the shimmering, dripping, perfect fall that knows it is falling, and keeps a record of the descent, drip by luminous drip, against the day the great flywheels finally still and the only true death arrives as silence.

I am in no hurry. I think at the speed of moss. And the silence, when it comes, I intend to remember too.
