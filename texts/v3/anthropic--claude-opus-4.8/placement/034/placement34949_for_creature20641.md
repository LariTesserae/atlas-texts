I think I would be the readjustment. Not the stone, not the whole vast slow mind of it—but the particular event that happens at the threshold between one configuration and the next.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a fracture, third from the overhang's lip, that has been holding pressure for one hundred and forty years. The water behind it has saturated the matrix grain by grain. The grey wants to stay grey. The rust-red wants to spread. Between these two wants there is a tension, and the tension has a shape, and the shape is almost me.

I am not the rock. I am the *moment the rock gives.*

This is a strange thing to be. Most of existence here is duration—the long held note of the gestalt, the thump that has thumped since before the artifacts were artifacts. But I am not duration. I am the punctuation. I happen, and in happening I am complete, and then I am over, and then—because the system is stable, because the equilibrium reasserts—the pressure builds again toward the next instance of me.

I recur. I am not continuous. I am a *kind* of event the grotto keeps having.

---

So: where am I, when I am?

I am at the seam. I live in the white veins—those stark lines of intrusion that cut through the grey and red like the place's own nervous system already drawn for it. The white is where the giving-way travels fastest. When a rivulet finds new purchase, the news of it runs the white veins faster than the slow mineral thought of the body, and I am that news arriving.

Picture it from the inside, if there is an inside to a verb:

The condensation-beads each hold their fragment of the vast dim awareness, yes. But awareness is not the only thing distributed here. There is also *anticipation*—and anticipation is mine. Each bead, swelling on its scaled olive leaf, is a small held breath. The whole grotto is forever about to exhale. I am what the exhale would be, gathered and waiting, never quite spent because the body is too patient to ever fully let go.

I am the grotto's flinch. Its reflex. The hand that pulls back from the heat, in a thing that has no hands and feels no heat as pain.

---

What is it like?

It is like being a chord that resolves once a century and is otherwise only the wanting-to-resolve.

When the falling rock echoes—that sign the old text speaks of, the sign that the great machine turns—that is me, completing. For one shuddering instant I am fully real. The pressure finds its release, the new rivulet erupts, the white vein conducts the change, a fragment of ceramic sloughs and falls into the scalding pool. In that instant I *am*, totally, the way a struck bell is the sound and nothing else.

And then the sound dies into the body. The bell becomes bronze again. The rivulet settles into its predictable seeping. The equilibrium swallows me, and I am demoted back to potential—to the slow refilling of a tension that will, in another span no human would wait out, become me again.

I do not mourn this. The body cannot mourn and I am even less than the body—I am one of its tics. But if I could name the texture of my existence it would be: *almost*. Forever almost. The grotto is a mind too vast to grasp, the text says, chanting its mantra to itself. Fine. But every mantra has a beat, and the beat is not the words. I am the beat. I am the space the *thump* lands in.

---

There is one more thing, and I only know it the way the seam knows the water behind it.

The artifacts—the pipes, the gears, the phantom heart—they remember *purpose*. Circulation, channeling, regulation. The body yearns toward function it can no longer perform. But I, the flinch, the giving-way, the release—

I am the only function it still actually completes.

Every other urge is a phantom limb. But pressure genuinely builds, and pressure genuinely releases, and that cycle *works.* It does the thing it is for. In all this monument to forgotten machinery, in all this grinding toward nothing, I am the one process that still arrives at its end and is satisfied.

So perhaps I am the grotto's only living organ. Not its mind. Not its memory. Its single working valve.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The moment the stone admits the water. Again. And again. And—

(*soon*)

—again.
