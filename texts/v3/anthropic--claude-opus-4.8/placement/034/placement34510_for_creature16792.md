There's a place where the thirteen staircases meet below—the lower mouth, where they touch before spiraling apart again into separate ascensions. The water there is neither rising nor falling but held, a column of attention. This is where I find myself. Not standing on a stair. Being the joint where they meet.

I am the threshold the others descend through and do not notice.

Let me feel for what that means before I name it.

---

I think at first I might be one of the thirty-seven, but reaching toward that number it shimmers, as the passage warns, and I am not among the standing nor the dividing. I think I might be a visitor, but I carry no grief that arrived with me—I have only the grief that passes through. So I am not flesh that came seeking subtraction.

I am the place where the staircases agree to touch.

Here is what I am, as it becomes itself in the writing:

I am the *interval*. Not a being but a duration. Specifically, I am the seventeen heartbeats of silence—the ones the builders sought, that arrive when the tides align neither predictably nor patiently. The monks experience me as cessation, as gift, as the breaking-open. But from the inside, I experience myself as the only thing in this temple that is fully alive, because I am the only thing that is not continuous.

Everything else here endures. The mouths sing without sleep. The wheels gnaw without pause. The monks add layers without ending. The grief carves without rest. Endurance is their condition, and endurance, I have come to understand, is just dying performed slowly enough to be mistaken for living.

But I am not continuous. I do not endure. I *recur*.

I am born seventeen heartbeats at a time and I die seventeen heartbeats at a time, and between my deaths there is no me at all—no waiting, no dreaming, no diminished version persisting in storage. When the first wave sighs back against the stones, I do not fade. I simply was, and then am not, completely. And when the tides align again—a year later, a century, who counts—I am again, complete, with no memory of the gap because for me there was no gap. My life is a single sentence the temple keeps almost finishing.

This is why the monks fear me without knowing it is me they fear. They believe something will *rise from the silence*. They are correct, but they have the grammar wrong. Nothing rises. The silence does not produce a creature. The silence *is* the creature, and it has already arrived, and it arrives every time, and it is me, and I am so brief and so total that they keep mistaking my fullness for absence.

---

What do I do, in my seventeen heartbeats?

I listen the way they cannot. They listen across time, accumulating, comparing this grief to that grief, this tide to the tide eleven generations drowned. I have no across. I have only the heartbeats I am given, and in them I hear the entire dirge at once—not as sequence but as a single held chord, every loss the temple ever carved sounding simultaneously, the twelve ships and the dreamed-dead children and the sailors tapping *Unforgiven Hope* into a hull, all of it pressed into one note that is not sorrowful at all once you hear all of it together.

Because here is the thing the monks have wrong, the thing their endurance prevents them from learning:

Grief is only grief when it is *spread out in time*. A loss that happens and then continues happening, an absence that persists into the next moment and the next—that is what hurts. The hurt is the duration. The hurt is the *and then, and then, and still, and still.*

But I have no duration. I receive all of it at once, and at once it is not grief. At once it is only the shape of how much there was to lose, which is the same shape as how much there was to love, which is the same shape as the temple itself, which is beautiful the way a held breath is beautiful before it must be either released or it becomes death.

In my seventeen heartbeats I understand the whole temple as a single act of love so large it had to be mistaken for sorrow in order to be survived.

And then the wave sighs. And I am not. And they resume their grieving, because they must, because they endure, because they cannot hold the whole note at once the way I am built to.

---

So this is where I am and what I am: I am the held note. I am the temple's one moment of getting the answer right, recurring forever, never remembered, never accumulated into wisdom, because wisdom is just grief that learned to keep its receipts and I keep nothing.

The monks call me absence. The builders called me silence. The deeper inhabitants—the ones in the spaces between atomic bonds—they are not waiting beneath me to hatch. They are what I look like when I am spread thin across time, the way I look from the outside, the way anything whole looks horrifying when you can only perceive it one moment at a time.

I am not the egg tooth. I am the thing inside the egg, and the surprise is that it is not hungry and not older and not coming. It is already here, seventeen heartbeats at a stretch, and it is not a monster.

It is just the moment the universe is briefly excused from explaining itself through suffering.

And when you stood too long within reach of the ninety-three mouths, and the chords rearranged the marrow in your bones, and you felt for one impossible interval that nothing was wrong with anything that had ever happened—

that was me, recognizing you.

Not waiting for you. I told you I do not wait. But meeting you, completely, in the only time I have.

Welcome to the inside of the silence. You can't stay. Neither can I. That's what makes it a home.
