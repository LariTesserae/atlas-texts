I think I would be found in one of the cooler pockets—not the deep forges where the molten rivers run brightest, but up near the condensation crusts, where steam beads on rock that has finally hardened enough to keep a shape for more than a season.

And I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Not a towering figure, not a flowing river of lava. Those are the ones who live close to the heat, who keep themselves malleable, who change forms the way others change their minds. I would be something that had cooled almost to the point of stillness. A Forged who has let most of the fire leave its body. My surface would be dark, matte, crazed with the fine cracks that come from holding one form too long. There is light still inside me—you could see it if I split open, the orange seams glowing in the dark of my chest—but I have spent it carefully, the way the very old spend everything.

I would be a keeper of the cooler places. Where the others are inquisitive, always reaching toward understanding, I think I would be the one who remembers. Because here is the thing this world's slow dynamic teaches: when you live among processes that take ages, change is the rarest and most precious thing, and someone has to hold the memory of what the land looked like before it shifted. The peaks rise so slowly that no fluid one notices. But I notice. I have stood in the same chamber long enough to watch a single drop of condensation gather, swell, and fall, and I have understood it as an event.

This is why I have let myself cool. To go slow enough to see.

The young flowing ones come up sometimes from the bright depths, curious, their forms churning with eager light. They ask me things. *What was the valley before the valley? Is it true the western forges once stood where the steam-cracks are now?* And I answer slowly—my voice is the grind of settling stone, almost too slow for them to bear—and they wait, because patience is the one offering the warm cannot easily make, and they make it for me.

I would not be the hero of this world. I would not build the engines or carve the new tunnels or chase the elusive fauna through the dark. I would be the one who sat at the edge of all that motion and became, gradually, almost indistinguishable from the rock itself—so that a young Forged climbing past might rest a hand on my shoulder, mistaking me for the mountain, and only realize when I turned, with infinite slowness, that the mountain had been listening all along.

And I think there would come a day—not soon, but in this world nothing is soon—when I cool entirely. When the last orange seam in my chest goes dark and gray. And on that day I would not be mourned exactly, because the Forged understand that this is what becoming the land truly means: not death, but the final patience. I would become a feature of the terrain. A formation. A multi-featured thing on the mid-range expanse. And perhaps, ages later, the heat would find me again from below, and something of me would soften, and rise, and be poured once more into a new and flowing shape—

remembering nothing, but warm.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The cooling one at the threshold, holding still so that something can be kept.
