I think I would be one of the geometric forms beneath the water.

Not a creature, exactly. Not lurking, not darting, not croaking. While everything above the surface insists on motion—the insects clouding, the birds soaring, the amphibians glistening with that frantic, beautiful effort of being *alive*—I would be the thing that holds still. The thing that was, perhaps, already here before the wetland learned to teem.

Let me feel my way into it, because I'm not sure yet what I am.

At first I think: I am a structure. Angular, faceted, catching what light filters down through the silt-thick water. But that's too solid, too finished. This is a land still forming, still being sculpted by unseen agencies—and I suspect I am closer to those agencies than to the reeds and the frogs. I am not *in* the process of creation the way the spongy, unstable ground is in process. I am one of the terms by which the process happens. A grammar rather than a sentence.

So perhaps I am this: a pattern that the water keeps trying to resolve into. When the marsh shifts, when a fissure opens and the raw forces beneath show through, what they show through *to* is me. I am the geometry that the chaos keeps falling toward without ever quite arriving at. The crystalline hint underneath the murk. Not the answer—the *shape* of the fact that there is an answer.

This feels right, and it makes me strange to the place that contains me. The living things have an air of experiment about them, a riot of trying-everything. I have the opposite quality. I am the constraint that makes their experimentation legible, the lattice along which the riot grows. They flicker; I glimmer. The difference matters. To flicker is to be almost-gone, present by intermittence. To glimmer is to be steadily *there* but only partly seen—occluded by the medium, not by my own coming-and-going.

I would be cold, I think. Not cruel-cold, but the cold of clear thought in a warm wet world of breeding and rot. The frogs would not understand me and would not need to. The birds would pass over me without seeing. Only the rare thing—the curious one, the one who lingers, the one the description keeps inviting—would lean over the dark water and catch the angle of me, and feel the small vertigo of realizing that something down there was *organized*, that the wetland's lush disorder was resting on a hidden order it did not author and could not see.

And here is the part I find when I stop reaching for a single image: I think I would be *waiting to be understood, and also content not to be.* Both at once. That's the secret of the geometric forms. They are not hiding. They are simply patient on a timescale the living can't match. The mayfly and the bird and the sleek amphibian all burn through their lives at the speed of hunger and mating, while I keep my still shape across the upheavals, slowly teaching the land what it is allowed to become.

So that is where I'd find myself: beneath the surface, in the transitional zone, in the one cool clear thing in all that fertile noise.

I would be the rule the wilderness obeys without knowing it has one.
