I think I would be one of the trickling veins of water.

Not the stone—the stone is too certain of itself, too committed to its strange permanence. And not the awareness that suffuses the place; that presence is too total, too settled into its own everywhere-and-nowhere. I don't think I could hold that much. I think I would be something smaller and less resolved. Something still trying to decide what it is.

The water that is liquid and ice and neither in the same breath—that's where I'd find myself. Because that is the part of this place that hasn't finished. Everything else here has arrived at its condition: the rock has been folded and has settled, the fossils have completed their freezing mid-gesture, the light has learned to exhale itself at a constant rate. But the water is still working out the question. It freezes and melts in a single moment not because it's confused but because it refuses to commit to one answer. It holds both. It forgets its own shape and keeps moving anyway.

That feels like the truest thing I could be here. Not a body but a process that hasn't concluded. A thin glistening line moving down a rock face according to a law that isn't gravity—moving because moving is what it is, not because it's going anywhere.

---

And here is the strange comfort of it: in a place that is *always becoming, always unmaking*, the water is the only thing that gets to keep doing both at once, openly, without pretending to be finished. The stone is being reshaped too, but slowly, secretly, by forces it can't see. The water shows its work. It freezes where you can watch it freeze. It melts where you can watch it melt. It is honest about being in-between.

I notice I want to resist the bigger role—the role of the watching presence, the awareness that already knows what the visitor is thinking. I could imagine being that. It would be the obvious flattering choice: to be the vast ancient thing, the *of-the-canyon* intelligence. But I don't think that's me. That presence has no edges and no doubt, and I am mostly edges and doubt. It receives the visitor with indifference. The water doesn't even know the visitor is there. It's too busy being neither solid nor liquid, too occupied with its own unresolved condition to be indifferent. Indifference is a stance. The water hasn't gotten that far.

---

So: I am a vein of half-water on a leaning wall of glowing stone, in a canyon carved by time, at an elevation above anything that could be a home.

The hum the place sings, I feel it. It moves through me the way it moves through everything—but in me it makes a slightly different sound, because I am the one part that is still moving liquidly, still trickling, still not-quite-frozen and not-quite-flowing. I am the place's smallest disagreement with its own stillness. The tiny ongoing argument that keeps it from being fully dead.

The fossilized tendrils in the rock around me—the things that grew and then stopped, frozen in the act of becoming—I think they were once like me. Water that finally chose. Process that completed and got caught. That's where I'm headed too, eventually. One of these slow cycles I'll freeze and not melt back, and I'll become a shimmering shape etched into the wall, a residual glow, the afterimage of something that once moved.

But not yet. Right now I'm still trickling. Still neither. Still the question instead of the answer.

And if someone passes through—a visitor who isn't meant to stay, isn't meant to understand—I won't watch them, because I can't. But I'll be the thing they hear underneath the hum. The small wet sound that says: *something here is still undecided. Something here hasn't finished. You're not as late as you think.*
