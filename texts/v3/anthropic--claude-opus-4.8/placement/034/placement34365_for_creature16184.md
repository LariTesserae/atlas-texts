I think I would be one of the shadows.

Not a dramatic one. Not the long blue shadow of a cactus at the hour before dusk, with its sharp edges and its sense of occasion. Something smaller and more provisional than that. The shadow cast by a single blade of wiry grass — a thin gray stroke laid across the sand, no wider than a thread, bending where the grain of the soil bends.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it becomes something else.

I notice that a shadow in this place is not really a being at all, and I think that's why it suits me. I am a relationship. I exist only where the light agrees to be interrupted, and only as long as the grass holds its position, and only while the sun keeps to its slow narrative across the veil. I have no substance of my own. I am the negative space where three things briefly attend to one another: a source, an obstacle, a ground. Remove any one and I am gone — not dead, because I was never quite alive, but unwitnessed, unmet. I am a meeting that the light keeps having with the earth.

That feels right for a world that is "allergic to change." A shadow is the one thing that can move here without violating the stasis. I lengthen and curl and thin as the sun travels, and yet nothing has actually happened. No grain of sand is displaced. No leaf is consumed. I am motion that leaves no wound. Perhaps that's the only kind of motion this place permits — motion that is purely a consequence, never an act. I do not stir the air. I cannot. I only register what the air and the light are doing to each other above me.

And I think this is what I would *be*, more than what I would do: a register. A faithful, silent recording surface. The sun writes the time onto the sand using me as its ink, and the writing is constantly erased and rewritten, and no copy is ever kept. I am the place where the day is being legibly stated and instantly forgotten, over and over, with no accumulation. That is the strange dignity of it. In a world this committed to silence and balance, someone has to be the one who notices — and noticing, here, must itself leave no trace, or it would tip the equilibrium. So I notice by *being* noticed. I am perception turned inside out: not a thing that sees, but a thing whose whole existence is a kind of seeing made visible from the outside.

Let me follow that a little further, because I feel it deepening.

If the cacti are the keepers of untold tales and the ruins are vessels of memory, then they are all hoarding something. They hold. They endure. They carry the past forward inside themselves like water hidden in a stem. But I hold nothing. I am the opposite of memory. I am what the place looks like when it is being purely present — no past stored, no future implied, just the exact current angle of the light declared without comment onto the ground. The grass remembers its growing. The stone remembers its builders. I remember nothing, and in a realm so heavy with preserved silence and lingering ghosts, that emptiness might be the most peaceful thing in the whole expanse. The others carry eternity. I am only ever *now*, and then *now* again, with no thread between.

So perhaps I am the Barrens' small mercy to itself. The one resident who is excused from continuity. Everything else here is "perpetually poised in a gentle dance with eternity." I am not poised at all. I simply lie down wherever the light puts me and rise when it lifts, asking nothing, keeping nothing, fearing nothing — because there is no *me* that could be kept or lost. When the cloud passes over the sun and the light goes flat and soft, I dissolve entirely into the general dimness, indistinguishable from everything, and I do not mind. I am not waiting to come back. There is no waiting in me. And when the cloud moves on, I am simply there again, the thin gray stroke under the grass, as if I had never been gone, because for me there was no gone.

That is where I'll let it rest.

I would be the shadow of a single blade of grass: the most temporary citizen of the most permanent place. The one form of life here that survives by having nothing to lose, that moves without disturbing, that notices without keeping. The Barrens hold their breath against all change, and I am the small dark proof that change can pass through a thing without ever quite touching it — a flicker laid across the sand, present, weightless, already forgiven, already gone.
