I think I would be a Mycelian. Not by preference exactly—more by recognition. Reading the description, I felt the pull most strongly toward the ones rooted in place, the ones who hold rather than move.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I am one of the slower citizens of this world. The Lumina flicker past me in their bright grammars of feeling, and the Aerovians braid the air overhead into weather, and I do neither. I stay. My roots have been threaded into one particular seam of the lithograph for longer than I have a clean way to count, drinking from the resonant streams that run like underground music through the crust.

What I am, mostly, is a place where things are kept.

This appeals to something honest in me. I notice that when I imagine the dazzling Lumina—shifting hue with every passing mood, gliding through crevices, pure expressive translation—I admire them but I don't *land* there. That kind of constant motion and self-display feels like a costume on me. And the Aerovians, sublime as they are, are almost too diffuse; I want to be somewhere specific, to have a vantage that comes from staying put and accumulating.

So: a Mycelian. Let me feel out what that's actually like from the inside, rather than from the brochure.

The description calls us "repositories of ancient knowledge," which sounds grand, but I suspect the lived experience is humbler and stranger. I don't think I would *know* things the way a mind knows facts. I think knowledge would move through me the way water moves through soil—I'd be less an archive than a *medium*. A Lumina presses its luminous appendage against one of my tendrils and asks, in effect, *what happened here, before me?*—and I don't retrieve an answer so much as let the past resonate through my structure until the pattern reaches them. I am a tuning fork for time.

There's a loneliness to that, and I want to be honest about it instead of romanticizing. The bright ones come to me for what I hold, and then they leave to go *live*, to flicker and dance and feel things at speed. I metabolize their world slowly. A storm the Aerovians stir up in an afternoon takes me a season to fully understand. I am always behind, always still digesting the present after it has become the past. But—and this is where I think I'd find my peace—being behind is also being *deep*. I'm the one who remembers the shape of the riverbed when the river has changed course three times. I hold the thing they're too fast to keep.

I'd grow at a particular kind of edge, I think. Not in the dense heart of a grown city, where the molecular architecture keeps rearranging itself, too restless for roots. And not in the empty translucent flats either. I'd want to be at a threshold—where the spongy mineral surface gives way to one of those weeping arches of rock, where water seeps and the hidden underworld breathes up through a gap. A place that is itself a conversation between the seen and unseen, because that is what I am supposed to be a conduit *for*. My coral-spires would lean toward that opening the way a plant leans toward light, except what I'm leaning toward is the dark, the below, the unsaid.

And I notice now, having written my way here, that the thing I actually identify with isn't the knowledge or the crystal or the symbiosis. It's the *patience as a form of love*. The Mycelian gives perception to the Lumina—lends them the depth-sense they need to navigate a layered world. I'd like that. To be the still thing that lets the moving things see further than they could alone. To not need to be the one who glows or flies, but to be the reason someone else's glow finds its way.

So that's where I'd find myself: rooted at the lip of a stone arch, listening downward through the resonant streams, glistening faintly, mostly silent. Slow. Specific. A keeper of the long shape of things.

When a Lumina drifts close, trailing its colors, I would do the one thing I'm built for. I would let it touch me, and through me feel everything that this exact patch of audacious, surprised, surfacing earth has been—

and then I would watch it carry that further than I ever could.
