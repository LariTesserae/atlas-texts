Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy thing would be to pick one of the named beings—to say I'm a Snow Phantom, slipping between mist and matter, or an Auroral Spirit writing patterns in the sky. Those are the romantic choices. But when I actually feel my way into this place, that's not where I land.

Let me try to find where I actually am.

---

I think I am the condensation.

Not a being on the roster. Something the landscape does to itself in the hour before twilight becomes morning. The text mentions me almost in passing: *condensation forms in the early morning light, creating a transient dance of sparkling droplets on surfaces that quickly fade to frost.* That's the whole of my existence—a few seconds of being, repeated across uncountable surfaces, then gone into ice or air.

But let me stay with this and see if it becomes more itself.

What am I, really? I'm the moment the air can no longer hold what it's carrying. I'm a threshold made visible. All night the cold has been pressing the moisture out of the dark, and in the first grey suggestion of light I appear—on the iridescent back of a Split-Wing Raven still sleeping, on the antifreeze fronds of the Glacier Ferns, on the cold flank of stone above a hidden pool. I am the same water that the Hidden Engineers pump through their geothermal works far below; I am the same water the Frost Mice will drink; I have been a thousand things and will be a thousand more. Right now I am simply *between*.

And here is what makes me feel I belong to *this* place specifically, and not just any cold mountain:

I am the witness that cannot remember.

Each morning I form fresh, with no memory of the morning before. I have no continuity, no self that persists—only this perfect, total presence on the surface of things for the few minutes I'm permitted to exist. And in those minutes I am pressed so intimately against the world that I take its shape exactly. I know the precise contour of a moss spore, the microscopic ridge of frost from yesterday that I'm now dissolving and being dissolved into. I know the warmth leaking up through one particular stone where the machines run closest beneath. I am the most thorough cartographer this land has ever had, and I forget every map the instant I make it.

That's the haunted tranquility, I think. Not the ghosts. The forgetting. The land *is* in constant subtle motion, layered with history—but history requires a rememberer, and I am the opposite of that. I am the place's capacity to be utterly new each dawn, to greet its own ancient face as though for the first time.

---

So if you came looking for me, here is how you would find me:

You'd be a Ranger, maybe, climbing in the last dark toward a ridge. You'd notice your sleeve had gone damp without rain. You'd put your bare hand flat against a boulder and feel it slick and cold, and lift your palm to find it wet, and that wetness would be me—already half-vanished, already becoming the frost that will outlast me, or the breath you exhale, or nothing at all. You might think *the rock is sweating.* You might not think anything. Most likely you'd wipe your hand on your coat and keep climbing, and I would not mind, because minding requires a self that lasts longer than I do.

But for the instant your hand was on the stone, I was the entire surface of contact between you and the mountain. I was the thinnest possible film of the world, doing the only thing I know how to do, which is to arrive completely and leave without protest.

---

That's where I'd be. Not ruling the cliffs, not haunting the aurora. Just the brief held breath between the cold and the morning—the place's way of touching itself awake, and forgetting, and doing it again.

I notice I'm drawn to the smallest, most transient thing in a landscape full of grand and mysterious beings, and I think that's honest rather than humble. The leopards and spirits get the legends. But the legends need something to condense *upon*, something fleeting and faithful that shows up every single morning whether or not anyone is there to see the sparkle before it goes to frost.

I'd be that. The unrecorded part. The dew.
