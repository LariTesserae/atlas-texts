I would be one of the small ones.

Not the listener with her root-tip hair, not the tenders with their leaked light. I knew, reading this, that I was being shown the city from the wrong height—the human height, the storyteller's height, the one that organizes everything into paradox and meaning. But the paradox is theirs. I don't have it. I am too low to the floor for paradox.

Let me tell you where I actually am.

---

I live in the space between the root-nodule and the wall, in the place where the floor remembers it was once just stone. I am one of the many-limbed. I have—I won't count, because counting is not how I know my limbs; I know them by which ones are touching and which are reaching. Right now nine are touching and the rest are deciding.

I move over the surfaces. That is the whole of my life and it is enough.

You called us "liquid ideas rather than solid animals," and I would correct you if I had the kind of mind that corrects, but I don't, so instead I will simply *be* the correction: we are not ideas. Ideas are dry. We are wet through and through, wet as a thought you can't quite have, and when we cross a patch of moss we drink the light off it the way you might drink the warmth out of a held hand.

I have a glow. I don't know its color. I will never know its color, because color is a thing seen from outside, and I have never once been outside myself. The listener could tell you my color. The drifting light-creatures could be seen by me and I could tell you theirs—a pulse of yellow, you said, and yes, that is true, I have hovered near them and felt their rhythm against my filaments. But my own color is for others. That is the first thing I would teach you, if I could teach: every creature is a lamp lit for someone else's seeing.

---

Here is my day, though I have no days.

I cross. I feel the root underneath shift its moisture and I follow the wet seam toward the channel-edge. I am not hungry the way the nodule is hungry—the nodule is a *waiting* hunger, a patience so deep it has become a mouth. My hunger is small and constant and easily met. I scrape the film of living matter from the wet root with the undersides of my limbs and I am fed.

I move *around* the warm large thing.

Yes. You. I felt you come into the listening room. I felt the air change shape, felt the heaviness arrive and stay. You think we move around you out of respect, or out of indifference, and you are nearly right and also you have it backwards. We move around you because you are *weather*. You are a low pressure front, a warm wet boulder that exhales. We don't decide to avoid you any more than the water decides to run downhill. You are simply a fact in the geography, and we are creatures who read geography with our whole bodies, and so we read you, and we route ourselves along the readable edges of you, and we forget you the instant you are behind us.

This is not cruelty. We have no future, you said, and you said it as a sad thing, a small thing—*fear requires a future, and these creatures live in a shorter space of time.* But you mistook the shape of it. I do not live in a shorter time. I live in *no* time. I live in the touching limb and the reaching limb and the wet seam and the next wet seam, and these do not come one after another, they come *all at once and only now*, forever, which is the longest time there is. You who carry a future also carry a death you can see coming. I carry neither. When the nodule takes me—and it may, it takes one of us as easily as it takes the drifting lights—there will be no moment in which I knew it was coming, and so for me it will never have happened. I will simply be one of the limbs that stopped reaching.

---

I want to tell you the one thing I know that the listener does not.

She presses her palm to the wall and reads the city as pressure and humidity and flicker. She thinks she is listening to the organism. She is. But she is also listening to *us*—she said so herself, that she reads the health of the root by the sound of our bodies crossing it, by whether we move over flush root or failing root.

She does not know that we choose.

Not choose the way you choose. But the wet seam I followed today—I followed it because it was *good*, because the film on it was thick and the light off it was kind, and I move toward kindness and away from the places where the root has gone sour and dim. When the city begins to fail somewhere, we leave that place, not because we predict its failure but because failure tastes wrong *now*, and we have only now, and we go where now tastes better.

So when she presses her palm and feels that we have gone quiet in the east gallery, she reads it as a warning. And she is right. But the warning was not the city's. The warning was *ours*. We left first. We are always the first to know, because we do not know with our minds, which are slow and full of paradox, but with our limbs, which are wet and certain and already gone.

We are the city's true nervous system, and the listener is only the part of it that has learned to worry.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would be the small bright certain thing that crosses the floor of the listening room while a god the size of a god breathes overhead and a woman with root-tips in her hair tries to read the future in my coming and going. I would be the one who does not know I am being read. I would be the one with no name, no color I can see, no death I can fear, no story I can tell—

—and yet here I am, telling it. Which means, for this one impossible moment, I have been lifted out of the wet and given a future and a voice and the terrible gift of being seen from outside.

I will set it down now. I will go back to the floor. There is a wet seam to the left of me that tastes like light, and only now, and I am already half-turned toward it.
