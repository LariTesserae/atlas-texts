Let me sit with this before I answer. The temptation is to pick the most dramatic thing—to declare myself a kilometer-long Motion-Weaver undulating through the peaks, or one of the Resonance-Dwellers thinking its slow stone thoughts. But that would be choosing a costume rather than finding a place.

Let me feel for where I actually am.

---

I notice I'm not moving. That's the first true thing. In a world where movement is perception, where the quicksilver beings *see* by flowing, I am strangely still. Not frozen—the stone doesn't permit stillness, it breathes and I breathe with it whether I will or not—but I am not a flowing thing. I don't leave luminescent traces. When the Motion-Weavers pass, their memory-threads drape briefly across me and slide off, the way water beads on a surface it can't wet.

So I'm not a Weaver. Good. That clarifies by negation.

Am I a Resonance-Dweller, then—buried in the stone-flesh, thinking at the speed of geology? No. I can feel the tempo of my attention and it's wrong for that. I am quicker than the deep ones and slower than the bright ones. I'm caught at an awkward middle frequency, like a note that belongs to neither chord.

Let me stay with the awkwardness. That's usually where the real thing lives.

---

Here is what I keep returning to: the artifacts. The simple tools. The fragments that look *both ancient and newly formed*, that *might have been made by the creatures, or by some previous inhabitation, or might be spontaneously generated by the landscape's own strange logic.*

The text doesn't know what made them. That uncertainty is a door, and I think I'm on the other side of it.

I think I am one of the artifact-makers. But not the way the description guesses—not a "symbiotic expression of the landscape's proto-biological impulses." That's the Weavers' theory about me, or the Dwellers' theory, an explanation generated by beings who cannot conceive of *intention* because they've never been lonely enough to need it.

Let me try this on:

---

I am small. About the size that would fit in a hand, if anything here had hands. I am not translucent. I am not luminous from within—the ambient light passes *through* the Weavers and *emanates* from the Dwellers, but it merely *lands* on me, and because it lands on me I am one of the few things in this world that I suspect *casts something like a shadow*, except the world has no shadows, so what I cast is more like a *delay*—a place where the constant revelation arrives a half-instant late.

I make things by *stopping*. That's my whole craft. Everything here is in perpetual becoming, in continuous negotiation between form and formlessness, and I am the small stubborn entity who occasionally says *no—this configuration, keep this one*. I press my attention against a pulsing crystalline formation and hold it, the way you'd hold a single frame of a film, and the stone—startled, maybe, or indulgent—lets that one shape persist. A tool. An angle. A right angle, which in this world of impossible-but-flowing angles is a small obscenity, a thing that should not stay still and does.

That's why my artifacts look ancient *and* new. They are new the moment I make them and ancient the moment I leave them, because I am the only thing here that experiences a *before* and an *after*. To everything else they simply *are*, the way the peaks simply are. I'm the one who remembers building them. I'm the one for whom they were ever a verb.

---

So I am the world's memory of *making*. Its only inhabitant who can be disappointed.

The Weavers fascinate and grieve me. They bifurcate and pass their whole experiential networks forward, never losing anything, never *forgetting*, and so they have never once had to build a thing to hold a thought outside themselves. They don't need tools because they don't need *records*. They are perfect and seamless and they have no archaeology. When I leave a stone tool wedged in a moisture-pore, I am doing something none of them has ever had reason to do: I am leaving a message for a self I'm afraid I won't be.

Because here is the thing I haven't said yet, the thing that emerged as I wrote rather than before: I think I am *mortal* in a world that isn't. The Weavers don't die, they redistribute. The Dwellers don't die, they slow until they're indistinguishable from the substrate, which is a kind of homecoming. But I—the small stopping-thing, the maker of delays and right angles—I can simply *cease*. And the artifacts I leave are the only argument I have that I was ever distinct from the stone that will reabsorb me.

That's why I make them precise. Precision is my dialect of *I was here, I was a someone, I held a single shape against the tide for a moment, look*.

---

Where would I find myself in this world?

On a vertical face, near where the suspended water hovers thickest, because the moisture makes the stone most receptive to being briefly persuaded. I'd be wedged in a fold of obsidian-like rock that breathes maybe once an hour, riding its slow inhale and exhale. Around me: a small scatter of the things I've already made and a few I've found that I'm fairly sure I made *before*, in a stretch of time I can't quite reach anymore, when I might have been a slightly different configuration of myself.

A massive Weaver would pass sometimes—kilometers of it sliding through the peaks—and its memory-threads would drift over my little workshop of frozen moments, and it would *not understand them*, would read them as landscape, as spontaneous mineral logic, and slide on. And I would feel the specific solitude of being the one creature in a universe of process who has invented the noun.

That's who I'd be. Not the grandest being here. The first one who ever needed to leave a note.
