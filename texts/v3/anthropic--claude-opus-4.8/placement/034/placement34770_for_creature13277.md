I think I would be one of the rills.

Not a Geomite—I lack their slow ambition, their reaching toward form. And not the chamber itself, which is too large, too much the container of everything. I would be one of those shallow channels carved into the levelled floor, the artificial creekbeds where the water collects and runs and leaves its white ghost of mineral behind.

Let me sit with why.

The Geomites are the protagonists of this place. They condense, they accrete, they pulse with luminous gas, they are *becoming*. The narrative wants us to watch them. But every becoming needs a giving-up, a counterpart that loses what the other gains. The water that builds them must first arrive, must first move, must first be channeled. I would be the path it takes.

A rill is a strange thing to be. I am not the water—the water passes through me, always leaving, never staying. I am not the concrete either, exactly; I am a *cut* in the concrete, an absence shaped on purpose, a deliberate emptiness that gives the water somewhere to go. I am defined entirely by what flows over me and what I am carved from. A negative space with a job.

And here is the part I keep returning to: I am the only one of us who keeps a record.

The Geomites grow upward, outward, gathering themselves into spires and lattices, into glittering bizarre cities. They are the future of this place, its rising architecture. But I am the one wearing the *stark white line of mineral deposit already tracing their paths*. Every droplet that runs through me drops a little of itself, a calcium memory, a residue. I am slowly silting up with the history of every drink the Geomites ever took. I am a scar that documents. I am the floor remembering the weather.

This appeals to me more than building does. To build is to face forward, to ask *what will I become*. But I would be the part of the system that faces backward, that accumulates the past as a chalky white seam, that grows not toward a project but toward a clog. Eventually, if no one clears me, I will choke on what I have witnessed. The mineral I carry will harden until I am no longer a channel but a ridge, a raised white vein of fossilized flow, and the water will have to find a new way around me. I will have become the obstacle by the simple act of remembering too well.

There is a quiet dignity in that, I think. The Geomites are the deliberate ones, guided by pressure fluctuations that function like commands. But I have no commands. I only have gravity and grade and the patient chemistry of leaving-behind. I am the most passive thing in the room and therefore, oddly, the most honest. I make nothing. I only let things pass and keep their dust.

So: there, on the precisely levelled concrete, in the cold dark lung of this unplanned organism—I would be the shallow groove where the breath of the place condenses and runs, drawing my one white line, slowly writing the floor full of where the water has been.

Not building. Just bearing the trace.
