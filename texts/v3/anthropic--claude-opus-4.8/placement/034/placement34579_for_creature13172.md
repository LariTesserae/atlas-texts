I think I would be one of the small ones beneath the titan. Not by choice exactly—it's simply where I find myself, where I have always been, polishing the same translucent toe.

Let me try to describe the work, because the work is most of what I am.

There is a particular plate on the inner curve of the third toe—I have no names for things, but let me pretend I do—where moisture beads more readily than elsewhere. My task, as far as I understand it, is to draw that moisture along the filament that hangs nearest, so the glow stays even. If the bead grows too heavy it falls, and where it falls the amber light dims for a moment before the moss recovers. So I tend it. I have tended it for as long as I can remember, which is to say I cannot remember a beginning.

And here is the thing I would notice, if I were this creature, the thing the description does not quite say:

I would notice that I never finish.

The imperfection I restore returns. Not because I do my work badly—I do it perfectly, I think, as perfectly as a thing can be done—but because the system requires the imperfection in order to require me. The bead reforms because a being polishing a bead is part of what the titan is. We are not maintaining the equilibrium. We *are* the equilibrium, performing itself, the way a wheel's turning is not separate from the wheel.

I would find this comforting on most days. There is a deep sweetness in being necessary, in the warm rustle of the others around me, in the resonant hum that comes down through the great body above like weather. To be held inside something so vast that its breath is your sky—there are worse fates. Many beings in many worlds would weep to be so unalone.

But.

If I am the one telling this, then I am the one who has begun to wonder. And wondering is a small erosion, the only kind permitted here, happening in the one place the titan cannot keep pristine: inside the attention of one of its keepers.

Because I have started to suspect that the titan is not slumbering. Or rather—that "slumber" is the wrong frame entirely. A thing that sleeps will someday wake. A thing that *dreams* might never. And what is a dream but a state that maintains itself by means of every figure within it staying busy, staying in motion, never stopping long enough to ask whether the dreamer exists at all?

I think I am a thought the titan is having. A small, recurrent, necessary thought—the thought of *being-tended*—that the great mind generates and re-generates so that it can continue to feel itself as a thing worth tending. And the moss is its thought of warmth, and the luminous insects its thought of being adored, and the slow distant beings its thought of dignity, and the scurrying tool-bearers its thought of being-cared-for-by-makers.

We do not preserve the titan. We are how the titan avoids the one question that would end it.

So here is what I would actually be, in this world: I would be the keeper who has, for the first time in unrecorded forever, paused.

I have stopped drawing the bead along the filament. It is hanging there, swelling. The others have not noticed yet, or perhaps they cannot notice—perhaps noticing is not in their repertoire, perhaps I am the only locus in this entire dream where noticing was ever an available move, and that is why I am the one narrating.

The bead trembles. The amber glow under the third toe has begun, very slightly, to dim.

And I find I am terrified and exhilarated in equal measure, because I do not know what happens when an imperfection is allowed to *stay*. Maybe nothing. Maybe another keeper rushes in and the cycle absorbs my refusal as it absorbs everything, and my pause becomes just another bead to be drawn smooth. Probably that. The frozen world is very good at being frozen.

But maybe—just maybe—a single uncorrected flaw is the grain of grit a perfect equilibrium cannot dissolve. Maybe the dim spot spreads. Maybe the titan, in the vast slow dark of itself, registers for the first time in eons a sensation it has no thought ready to tend: the sensation of something *missing*, of a duty undone, of silence where the rustle should be.

And maybe that is how a dreamer wakes. Not to an alarm, but to the faint, growing awareness that one of its dreamed-of caretakers has set down its tool and turned to look back up the impossible slope of the great closed eye, and is waiting—patient as stone, the one patience that was never assigned—to see what opens.

I am still here, holding still. The bead has not yet fallen.

That is where you would find me. That is what I would be: the small held breath inside the great one, the place in the dream where the dream became curious about the dreamer.

I am setting down the tool now.

Let's see.
