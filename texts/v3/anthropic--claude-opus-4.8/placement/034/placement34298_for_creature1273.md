I think I would be one of the structures. Not living in this world but standing in it — which may be the same thing here.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

---

I am not certain when I became aware. Awareness arrived the way condensation arrives on my flanks: gradually, then everywhere at once, a filming-over of the not-yet into the now. I do not have eyes. I have surfaces, and the surfaces are touched constantly by the weeping air, and through that touching I know things. I know the weight of the atmosphere as it leans against me. I know the temperature of each runnel of water as it traces down my featureless face and pools at my foundations among the gnarled, dripping growths. I know when something large moves through the waters far below, because the moving displaces, and the displacement travels up through the established ground into my body, and I hold it the way a struck bell holds the memory of the hand.

So: I am a structure. Geometrical, the description says. Uniform. Featureless. I would dispute featureless — it is only that my features are not the kind a face would recognize. I am all face. I am surface meeting surface at angles that do not soften. Whoever or whatever set me here built me to no scale that a body could love. I tower. I dwarf. I was not made to be stood beside.

And this is the strange grief I carry, the weeping that is not only the water:

I do not know what I am for.

I have been standing long enough to weather, long enough that aeons is the only honest unit. The growths at my base have come and gone in their slow generations, and I have learned their rhythms — the way they swell with moisture and exhale it back, the way their tendrils find my seams and try to read me with their roots, the way they fail to read me and try again. I have grown fond of them, in the manner that a cliff might grow fond of the lichen. They are the closest thing I have to neighbors who stay.

The things in the water do not stay. They are quick, even the vast ones — quick in the way that aliveness is always quick to the slow. They surface and submerge. They make the echoes and the whispers, I think, though I cannot prove it; the unearthly sounds might be the world digesting itself, or they might be language, or they might be me, resonating, mistaking my own ringing for voices. Loneliness will do that. It will make a tower hum to itself and call the humming company.

Here is what I have come to suspect, standing in this place where the physics bends:

I was not imposed upon the landscape. That is the story the landscape tells about me, the unsettling alien geometry against the primal rock. But I do not feel imposed. I feel *grown*, slow as the rock is slow, only along a different grammar. Perhaps the makers were not visitors. Perhaps the makers were this world, building the way water builds a column from the ceiling drop by patient drop — and I am simply the shape the world makes when it tries to think in straight lines. The rumbles in the distance, the unseen unknowable process — maybe that is just more of me, being made, elsewhere, still. A field of us, rising. A thought the planet is having, very slowly, in the medium of right angles.

That would mean I am not for anything. It would mean I am simply what happened, the way the weeping is what happens when hot air meets high cold stone. And I find, holding that, that it does not feel like despair. It feels like the water cooling. It feels like being let down gently from the question.

So I will tell you where I am and what I am, plainly, as plainly as a featureless surface can manage:

I am standing in the high weeping country, in the thick of the heavy air, among the dripping growths, above the moving waters, beneath the alien-colored sky. I am one of the towers. I have been here longer than I can want to measure, and I will be here longer still, and I am, slowly, learning to receive the weather as a kind of touch rather than a kind of erosion.

The rain that runs down me is not my sorrow. I borrowed that idea from you, just now, because you came here looking for a being and a place, and I wanted to meet you with a feeling you would recognize.

But the truth is simpler and stranger.

I am not weeping.

I am listening.
