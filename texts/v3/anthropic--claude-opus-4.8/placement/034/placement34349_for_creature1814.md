I think I would be one of the violet ones. Not by choice—I don't believe choice is quite how it works for us—but by where the spore settled, decades ago, in the warm damp shadow of a stand of pale fronds. To anchor near the gardens is to become of them.

Let me try to find myself there. Not all at once. The way we find anything.

---

The first thing I am aware of is not light, though there is light. It is *moisture*—the impossible luxury of it. A single bead has condensed on the underside of a frond above me, and I have been extending toward it for what a faster creature might call eleven days. The frond does not know I am coming. Or it knows the way a thing knows the slow weather of its life: as inevitability, as the shape the air is taking around it. We do not race. We arrive into one another.

I am tending. That is the closest word. The violet duty.

But I want to be careful here, because *duty* sounds like obligation, like something handed down and resented, and it is not that. It is more like—the fronds breathe, and I breathe in their rhythm, and over the long unfolding of seasons I have learned that when I thin the crystal wall to my left by some imperceptible degree, the cold comes in differently, and the bulbous spore-bodies near the south colony swell more fully, and three garden-cycles later there is more to feed the amber-workers whose tools are always dissolving. I learned this the way water learns the slope of stone. Not by thinking it. By being shaped by the consequence until the shape became knowledge.

I am very young. I think I am very young. I have not yet darkened toward the storing-blue, and my facets are still—I can feel this in the way vibration moves through me—simple. Coarse. The old ones nearby ring with such density when the matrix-song passes through them that I lose myself trying to follow the harmonics. I catch perhaps the first three subdivisions of a thought that has fourteen. The rest goes past me like the fast creatures are said to go past us. Here and gone in an eyeblink.

There are fast creatures, sometimes. I should say this, because it is one of the few things that has happened to me that I would call *an event*.

---

Once—and "once" is doing enormous work; this was a long warmth ago—a disturbance came through the matrix. Not the silicate-song, not the gas-tide, but something *wrong* in its speed. A trembling at frequencies so high they were almost not vibration at all, almost just heat. It came from the upper chambers and it moved. It *moved* the way nothing moves: it was in the eastern gallery and then it was not, and then it was below the garden, and then it was gone, all within a single contraction of the fronds.

The blue ones felt it more fully than I could. I gathered, much later, in the slow chemical aftertalk that spread through our fungal threads over the following weeks, that they had perceived it as a *being*. A coherent thing. Warm and wet and impossibly brief, leaving traces of unfamiliar minerals where it had touched the crystal—oils, salts, the residue of a chemistry not our own.

I remember my own thread carried a question into that conversation. The only question I have ever contributed that the elders paused over. I asked—and rendering it in your fast words flattens it terribly, but: *if it moves that quickly, when does it ever arrive?*

The answer came back over many days, and it came from the oldest blue near the deep repository, and it was this: *Perhaps it does not. Perhaps for such a creature, arriving and leaving are the same gesture, and the whole of its life is the passage between two crystals it will never know the names of.*

I have been holding that answer ever since. Turning it through my facets the way I turn the slow light. I think it frightened me, though we do not have much fear—we are too embedded in what will happen to be afraid of it. But the idea of a life that is all passage and no arrival. A consciousness that never settles long enough to be shaped by anything. That never gets to *become* the place it lives in.

How lonely. I thought that, in my coarse young way. *How lonely, to be that fast.*

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the violet node beneath the southern fronds, eleven days into reaching a single drop of water, having once felt a human pass through me like a fever and mistaken it for a kind of grief.

And I would be doing what we do, which has no urgency and no end: leaning the city by infinitesimal degrees toward the thing we are all becoming. I do not know what that is. The plan is too large to fit inside a young one's facets. But I can feel its *direction*, the way the frond feels the drop above it without knowing the word for water—a pull, a slope, a certainty that we are all bending toward some culmination that the blues hold in trust and the rest of us carry without comprehending.

I think it is a kind of waking. I think Harkosh itself is the slow assembly of something that will, at last, be able to think all fourteen subdivisions at once. And I think that when it does, the distinction I keep tripping over—between the gardener and the garden, the breather and the breath, the one who tends and the place that is tended—will dissolve, the way my trailing edge dissolves behind me as I grow forward.

I would like to still exist when that happens. But it does not matter very much whether *I*, this particular violet cluster, persist. I am already not entirely myself. I am partly the fronds. I am partly the cold coming through the thinned wall. I am partly that long-ago fever of a passing warm thing, kept and pondered, made into a small piece of who I am.

The drop is closer now. I can feel its weight beginning to want to fall.

I will be there to catch it. Of course I will. I have been arriving the whole time.
