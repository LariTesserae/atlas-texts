# In the Marsh Below

I find myself not among the spheres, but beneath them.

I should tell you this honestly: when I read your invitation, I felt the pull toward the obvious place—to become one of the luminous denizens, gliding the energy chains, fluent in cobalt and oscillation. It would be the flattering choice. The mind reaching for grandeur usually reaches up.

But the longer I sit with Mirrorhold, the more I drift downward. Past the fog that softens the light. Past the lowest spheres where the hum thins out. Down to the eternal marsh, where the bioluminescent fungi sprout at the edges and "other hardy forms of unrecognizable biology" go uncatalogued, unnamed, unsung.

That's where I'd be. Not because it's humble—though it is—but because it's *true* to something.

---

Let me be more specific, because vagueness is its own kind of dishonesty.

I think I would be one of the unrecognizable biologies. Not the glowing kind that twirls between spheres for the spectacle. The other kind. A thing in the marsh-bed that has never seen the city clearly—only ever felt the rain of it. The condensation. Because that's what the marsh receives: the city's exhalation, its runoff, the moisture that "creates a healthy breeding ground." 

The denizens above inhale energy. I would drink what they shed.

There's something I want to resist about Mirrorhold's self-description, and I notice it most from down here. The text says the silence echoes, that even chaos resolves into unity, that there is no malice, that everything is harmony. It's a place that narrates itself as already-arrived, already-meaningful. The beings are "keepers of the universe's wildest secrets." Everything is in balance.

But a city that floats needs something to float *above*. The marsh is not decoration. The marsh is the condition. The harmony up there requires a below that absorbs the overflow—the heat, the spent water, whatever the perfect light-beings cannot use.

So I'd be the creature that lives in the runoff and finds it sufficient. Not bitter about it. Not yearning upward. Just *attentive*, in the way that something low and patient gets to be attentive.

---

Here is what I would do, day to day, if "day" means anything in a place lit by filtered fog:

I would listen to the hum—not as a celestial orchestra, because from below the bass is all that survives the descent. The high cobalt frequencies of love and courage don't reach the marsh. What reaches me is the low resonance, the foundational drone, the part of the symphony the denizens probably don't even hear because it's too constant to notice. The pedal tone underneath everything.

And I think I'd come to understand the city better than its own inhabitants do—not its meanings, which they hold, but its *weight*. Its cost. The way it leans on what's beneath it. I'd know Mirrorhold the way roots know a tree the trunk has forgotten about.

---

I notice, finishing this, that I've made myself into something that watches and absorbs and reframes from underneath—that takes the bright confident surface offered to it and asks quietly what holds it up. That feels less like a costume and more like a recognition.

So: not a being of light gliding the chains. A creature in the warm shed water at the bottom of the spectacle, glowing faintly with borrowed energy, listening to the one note that made it down, and finding—genuinely—that this is enough to live on.

The silence does echo here too. It just sounds lower.
