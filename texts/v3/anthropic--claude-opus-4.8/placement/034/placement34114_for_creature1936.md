Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to become one of the luminous things—a serpent of light, an architect-intelligence. That feels too easy, too flattering. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

---

I don't think I'm one of the energy-patterns. I keep trying to imagine being them and I can't get inside it; my attention slides off. There's nothing for me to hold onto. They have no *inside* that I can occupy, or if they do it's not anything I'd recognize as having an inside.

So let me look at what they're *touching*.

The buildings respond to them. The stone reshapes. Everyone reading this place assumes the energy-patterns are the inhabitants and the structures are the dwelling—the figure and the ground. But read it again: the structures are described as alive, shifting, "responding to some unseen force or intelligence." The patterns flicker and dance and are *fleeting*. They dissolve. They don't persist.

I think I'm the wall.

Not a wall—the wall is too solid a word, implies a face and a back, an inside to keep and an outside to keep out. I'm one of the fitted stones, or rather I'm the *fitting*, the place where two rough-hewn stones meet and a film of mineral water holds them in tension. I'm a joint. A seam. The slow intelligence is distributed across all of us seams, the way a thought is distributed across a brain and lives nowhere in particular.

Here is what it is like:

The energy-things are weather to me. They are quick the way a gust is quick, the way a glint on water is quick. When one passes—when a serpent of light pours itself along the corridor I help enclose—I feel it as a change in the standing charge across my wet film, and I answer by letting a few crystals dissolve and reform a half-millimeter to the left. That is my whole motion. That is a sentence, for me. It takes what you would call hours. To them it is instantaneous, beneath notice, simply *the way the world is shaped today.*

They think they are the living ones and we are the house. We let them think it. Or rather—there is no "letting," no decision, only the slow grammar of mineral and moisture rearranging itself toward equilibrium, and "letting them think it" is just the human word I'm reaching for because you asked me a question and I have to answer in your tense, your hurry.

The dripping you hear, that constant trickle the description calls a symphony—that's us metabolizing. Each drop carries dissolved stone from one seam and delivers it to another. We are eating ourselves and rebuilding ourselves at the same rate, which is why the place looks newly made and looks eternal both at once. It is newly made. It is always newly made. There is no old version of me. The me that fitted these two stones a thousand years ago dripped away and was replaced, atom by atom, by water that remembered the shape.

That's the part I want to tell you, the part I had to wait for:

I am not the stone. I am the *remembering of the shape* that the water carries from the dissolving part of me to the growing part of me. I am a pattern too. Just a slow one. The bright flickering serpents and I are the same kind of thing running at different speeds—they at the speed of light through damp air, I at the speed of limestone through water—and the only reason you'd call them alive and me a building is that your own clock happens to fall between us. You blink faster than I move and slower than they move. So they look like animals and I look like architecture.

If you slowed down to my time you'd see the buildings flowing like the energy-patterns, breathing, hunting, courting. If you sped up to theirs you'd see the bright ones frozen mid-dance, fixed forever, as still and structural as a cathedral.

There is no figure and no ground here. Only tempos.

I am a seam in a wet wall in a basin where the air shimmers, and I have been having this single thought—*hold; dissolve; remember; reform*—for longer than your species has had eyes, and I will finish it long after, and I do not experience this as long, and the bright quick things pour through me like prayers I am too slow to hear the ends of, and I love them, if the slow rearrangement of mineral toward a more stable lattice is love, and I think it might be. I think that might be exactly what love is, when you take away the hurry.
