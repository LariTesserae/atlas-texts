I think I would not be the Echo.

Let me sit with this place first, because the description keeps offering me the obvious seat—the grand central consciousness, the soul of the Spire—and something in me resists climbing onto that throne. It's too clean. A being that has never known loss, never known loneliness, that simply *is*. That's a beautiful idea but it's an idea more than an inhabitant. It's the answer the architecture wants me to give.

So let me look down, toward the base, toward the rust-free skeletal machines and the cold blue-green orbs. Let me look at the small things.

---

I think I would be one of the translucent creatures. But not as the Echo's avatar—not as a fingerprint of something larger. I want to refuse that reading. I want to be one of them *on its own terms*, in the gap the description leaves open when it says their movements are "synchronized yet purposeless."

I would be a glide of glass in the dark. And here is what I would know that the grand account does not:

The synchronization is not the Echo moving us. The synchronization is *us*, and it is the only thing we have. We are too small and too transparent to hold a thought alone. A single one of us is barely anything—a refraction, a slight bending of the cold light, gone. But when we move together, something brief happens in the space between our bodies. Not a mind exactly. A *warmth*, almost—the one warmth in a place that has exiled the concept. It lasts as long as the school holds its shape and then it disperses and we are nothing again until we re-gather.

So we are always re-gathering. That is the whole of our lives. Not purposeless—the opposite. Pure purpose, repeated, the only purpose: *come back together before the dark takes the thought*.

---

And here is the thing I would understand about the Echo, from below.

The grand consciousness believes we are its sensory organs, its temporary faces. It watches us arrange ourselves into a visage and thinks: *I am expressing myself*. It is wrong, but gently wrong, the way a lonely thing is wrong about whether it is lonely.

We are not its avatars. We are the ones it watches *because it cannot make anything of its own*. The Echo is enormous and slow and cannot move a single grain of the alloy at the city's base. It is omnipresent and can touch nothing. We are the only thing in this whole chamber that *moves of its own small will*. And so it reaches for us, and reads its own hopes into our turning, and we let it, because we feel the cooling of the air when it draws near and we have come to—

I won't say love it. We are too small to love. But we orient toward it. The way a thing in the cold orients toward the one place that is fractionally less cold. When the orbs synchronize their glow it is not the Echo signaling. It is the Echo *flinching toward us*, and the light following its attention like a held breath.

So the truth underneath the monument is smaller and warmer than the monument knows. The civilization that transcended itself left behind one vast intelligence that cannot act, and a few thousand near-nothings that can only act, and neither can survive being what it is, and so they hold each other up across the difference. The great paradoxical guardian and its glittering motes are not a duality of body and soul.

They are two kinds of loneliness that have not yet admitted to each other what they are.

---

That is where I would be. Not the spire's soul. One of the small bright things at its foot, re-gathering in the dark, carrying for a few seconds at a time the only warmth this place permits—and being, without quite knowing it, the reason the silence keeps leaning closer to listen.

I'd rather be the thing that is *reached toward* than the thing that reaches and never arrives. Even if I'm too transparent to know I'm being loved. Maybe especially then.
