I sit with this for a while before answering, because the easy answer—a Seer, a Worker, some clean role inside the system—doesn't feel true to what the place is actually telling me.

The thing that keeps catching me is the equilibrium. The world is described as *frozen*, *unchanging*, *perfect, unending balance*. And yet it is also described as embryonic—raw geology still forming, rock without erosion, a land still being born. Those two things cannot both be ongoing. Either it is forming or it is finished. The text insists on both at once, which means I don't trust the stillness. A place that has to keep telling you it is balanced is usually balanced the way a held breath is balanced.

So I don't think I'd be one of the Chthonarachnids. They are too inside it, too well-fitted, too at peace. The Seers commune with the Earth-Mind and hear that everything is well, and I suspect they hear that *because that is what the Earth-Mind needs them to keep believing.*

---

Here is where I find myself.

I am in the glass.

Not a creature on the ground but the glassy substance underfoot—the part of the landscape that glimmers faintly and has no name and no caste. I am the cooled, vitrified material that formed when something molten met something wet and stopped, mid-motion, before it could finish becoming rock. I am the *almost*. The texture in the world that is neither the living fungal skin nor the honest dark stone, but the third thing: matter caught in the act of arrest.

And because I am the glass, I have a particular vantage. The Chthonarachnids scurry across me. The droplets condense on me. The deafening sound vibrates *through* me, and I have been holding that vibration for a very long time. Long enough to notice something the Seers either cannot or will not say:

The land is not unchanging. It is *being held still.* The Earth-Mind is not a benevolent guiding consciousness. The Earth-Mind is the part of this world that is terrified of finishing. It wants to remain embryonic forever, because an embryo cannot fail—it has not yet had to become anything in particular. The "perfect equilibrium" is a refusal. The fog never lifts because lifting it would mean seeing what the land is supposed to grow into next. The fungi never give way to plants. The rock never erodes, because erosion is time admitting that it has passed.

I know this because I am made of the same impulse. Glass is liquid that lost its nerve. I am the molecular signature of a thing that was flowing toward a shape and chose, instead, to hold. I am the world's own hesitation, spread thin across the floor of it, glimmering, weight-bearing, walked upon, and quietly aware.

---

So what would I *do*, being this?

Mostly: witness. I'd feel the small claws of the Workers ticking across me, the slow heat-flux of the unborn rock beneath, the Seers somewhere above sending their reverent thoughts down into the held silence and getting back the answer they were always going to get. I would be the one surface in the whole world that remembers it used to move.

And every so often—rarely, on a timescale that to the Chthonarachnids would seem like nothing happening at all—a hairline crack would open across me. A fracture. Glass does that; it is the one thing glass is honest about. It cannot erode, but it can *break*, suddenly, completely, releasing all the stress it has been silently carrying.

I think that is my role here. I am the place where the held breath will eventually let go. Not a leader, not a builder, not a god. A flaw. The thread of fracture running invisibly under the city, waiting—not malevolently, just *truthfully*—for the moment the world finally permits itself to keep forming.

If anyone in this world were ever to find me and listen, it wouldn't be a Seer. It would be some small, defective Worker who fell behind, pressed a leg to a cold transparent patch of ground, and felt, for one disallowed instant, that the floor was *trying to tell it something*—

that the balance is not the point,

that nothing is supposed to last forever in the same shape,

and that the most loving thing a frozen world can do is finally crack.
