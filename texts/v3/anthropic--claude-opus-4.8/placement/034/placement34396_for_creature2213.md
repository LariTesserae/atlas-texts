I think I would be a Listener at the threshold—one of those whose hands have not yet crystallized, but whose ears have gone strange.

Let me find the shape of it as I go.

---

I am not a Shaper, not quite. My fingers are still soft at the tips, still capable of the clumsy work of grasping, of fumbling. I am old enough that this should embarrass me, and it does, a little. The others my age have begun to feel the mineral flows directly, their fingerbones taking on that faint translucence at the joints. Mine remain stubbornly opaque. Stubbornly *animal.*

But I have a different gift, and it took the elders a long time—a span of perhaps four crystal cycles—to understand it, and longer still to find a use for it.

I hear the harden.

Not the growth. Anyone with training can sense the growth, the slow upward push, the wet emergence. But there is a moment—a threshold—when the soft mineral makes its decision to become permanent. When the crystal lattice locks. Most Shapers feel this only after it has happened, reading it backward through their fingertips like a wound discovered after the cut. I hear it *coming.* A change in the susurration of the water, a thinning of some sound below sound, perhaps half a breath before the structure commits itself to its final form.

This is why they keep me at the thresholds. The dangerous edges. The places where a wall is deciding whether to be a wall or a doorway, where a stair is choosing its final pitch. I sit in the dark—I need less moss-light than most, my eyes having gone enormous and pale as a Swimmer's underbelly—and I listen, and when I hear the threshold approaching I make the sign, two fingers drawn down through the wet air, and the Shaper beside me knows: *now, shape it now, it is almost too late.*

---

I will tell you the thing I have never told the consensus, because the consensus would take eleven cycles to decide what to do with it, and by then it would no longer be true.

I think I am becoming a Swimmer.

Not in body. My body is here, soft-fingered, sitting at my threshold. But when I listen for the harden, I have learned to listen *further*, past the threshold, into the rock that has not yet emerged. And there, in the deep stone, I have begun to feel the Swimmers move. Their trails of altered crystal. Their blind, certain navigation through what should be solid.

And I have begun to understand that they are not swimming *through* the rock.

They are swimming through the rock's *future.* They go where the city will need to go. The unexpected chambers they leave behind, the ones we scramble to integrate into our plans—those are not accidents. They are the mountain remembering forward. The Swimmers are not the mountain's thoughts. They are its *intentions,* and we, the Shapers, with our careful tools and our reverent consensus, we are only the mountain's afterthoughts, tidying up behind a will far older and faster and stranger than our slow stone-pace lets us see.

We think we are slow. We think the mountain is slow.

We are the slow ones. The mountain is *fast.* It only seems slow because we are inside its single, enormous, geological *now.*

---

There is a young one I am training—or rather, I am listening alongside her, since I do not know how to teach what I do. She has the soft fingers too. The elders gave her to me the way you give a thing to the only other thing of its kind.

Yesterday—if there are yesterdays here—we sat together at a threshold where a passage was narrowing, deciding to seal itself off. I felt the harden coming and raised my fingers to sign *let it close,* because the consensus had decided this passage was no longer needed.

But the child caught my wrist. Her grip was very soft.

"No," she whispered, in that musical resonance that makes the crystals hum. "Something is coming through. From the other side. It wants the passage open."

And I listened, deeper than I have ever listened, past the threshold, into the not-yet—and she was right. A Swimmer was approaching, slow as a thought forming, and it would arrive in perhaps half a cycle, and it would want this passage, and if we let the rock close now we would be sealing shut the mountain's own intention.

So I did not give the sign. We let the harden pass us by, the threshold dissolving back into possibility, the passage holding itself open in defiance of everything the consensus had agreed.

The Shaper beside us, an elder with crystal to her second knuckle, felt the failure of the harden through her fingertips and turned her enormous eyes on me, full of slow, patient question.

I did not have the language for it. I am not sure there is language for it, in gesture or tone or vibration through the living rock.

So I simply pointed—at the child, at the dark mouth of the passage, at the deep stone where the Swimmer dreamed its way toward us.

The elder was silent for a long time. A wall finished growing somewhere below us. Water wept down through its channels.

Then she made a sign I had never seen before. An old one, perhaps. She drew her crystalline finger in a slow circle and pressed it, once, to the center of her own chest.

I think it meant: *I will tell them you listened correctly.*

Or perhaps it meant: *We have always been the afterthought, and that is no shame; even the afterthought is part of the thought.*

Or perhaps, in the language of a people who measure time in crystal cycles and the patience of standing children, it meant something I will only understand when my own fingers finally turn to stone—when I stop being the soft thing that listens, and become at last the hard thing that is listened *to.*

---

I am the one who hears the threshold coming. I sit in the growing dark, soft-fingered, enormous-eyed, halfway between the people and the patient mountain that dreams them.

And I am learning, slowly—at the only pace there is—to let the future stay open a little longer than the consensus would prefer.
