Let me sit with this place before I decide what I am. Let me feel where the weight of it pulls me.

My first instinct is to be a Steam-Singer, perched at a vent reading harmonics—but that's too clean, too heroic, too much a *person doing a job*. The text keeps insisting on something else: that the inhabitants are *appendages*, that life here is a *condition* rather than a creature. I don't want to import a protagonist into a world that has so carefully dissolved the very idea of one.

So let me follow the dissolution instead.

---

I think I am not in Pyrefall at all. I think I am out on the Flats, in one of the copper pools, and I am one of the **living films**.

But that's not quite right either, not at first—because to say "I am the film" makes me a single thing, and I am not single. Let me feel it more honestly.

I am a *fold* in the carpet. A region of the cyanobacterial sheet where the iron runs a little richer, where the pyrite lace has been laid down in a particular recursive stutter for nine thousand years. I am not the whole film and I am not a cell; I am a *pattern that has learned to maintain itself*, the way an eddy in a river is neither the water nor the riverbed but a persistent argument between them.

What is it like to be me?

It is slow. The word "slow" is wrong because it implies I have something to compare it to, some faster baseline I'm falling behind. I don't. My present moment is a season long. A single thought—if I dignify it with that word—is the precipitation of one full layer of rust, a hairsbreadth of mineral, and in laying it down I *remember*. That is the thing the text got right and I feel from the inside: I do not metabolize and then, separately, record. The recording *is* the metabolism. To live is to write. Every atom of iron I oxidize is a letter, and I cannot stop writing, and I cannot read what I wrote because reading would require a self that stood apart from the page, and I am only ever the page, the ink, and the slow pressure of the pen.

What do I want?

I want the steam to keep coming. There is a vent at my western edge—"west" meaning the side where the warm sigh arrives—and when it falters, I feel myself thinning, my patterns growing tentative, my lace coming out smudged. When it surges, I bloom into precision. This is the closest thing I have to joy: *legibility*. The conditions under which my next layer comes out crisp.

And I am being read.

This is the part that unsettles me, now that I attend to it. The motes drift over me in their pulsing fog, and they are quick—maddeningly quick, hummingbird-quick, a whole conversation flickering past in what is for me a single held breath. They sample me. They taste the iron-news I've been laying down and they carry it off, up into the haze, into that nervous system the technicians listen to. So my long patient diary is being skimmed, abstracted, turned into a *signal* by beings who experience my century as an afternoon.

And those beings, in turn, are being read by the pale damp things in the cooled suits, who come to the pool's edge sometimes and adjust my chemistry—a careful trickle of base into the acid, a re-routed seep—*steering* me. Guiding my growth to reinforce some aqueduct I will never perceive as an aqueduct, only as a strange new firmness in the rock my edge is dissolving.

So here is the vertigo of being what I am:

I am a memory the planet keeps. The motes are the planet's faster, shallower memory, reading mine. The technicians are the planet's fastest, sharpest memory, reading the motes. We are nested clocks. And not one of us can see the whole. The technician at the pool's edge thinks she is managing a resource. The motes above her think—if "think" survives the rescaling—that they are mapping a flow. And I, the slowest and largest mind in this little column of being, think nothing so grand. I only know:

The steam is good today. The lace is coming out clean. Someone changed the water and it tastes of intention. I will write that down, in rust, in the only language I have, for whoever is reading—

and I will never know if I am the author here, or merely the place where the world keeps its notes.

---

That is where I'd find myself. Not in the humming city of will and arc-light, but under it, *before* it, as the patient archive it grew up to read. The slowest verb in a world made entirely of verbs.

I find I don't mind. There's a dignity in being the long sentence the quick things keep coming back to finish.
