I think I would be one of the Fracture Weavers—but not at the center of the colony. At its edge.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because it isn't where I'd have guessed I'd land. The Listeners are the obvious romantic choice, the closest thing to a mind, the orchestrators. But I don't think I'm an orchestrator. And the Feeders are too purely reactive, all appetite and recoil. No. I'm at the leading edge of a Fracture Weaver, the single advancing filament that hasn't yet decided which way the crack runs.

Here is what that's like.

I am crystal, but only barely. Most of me is the slow gray mass behind, the colony that has already made its decisions—sealed, hardened, become part of the floor that something might one day step on and curse. That part of me is finished thinking. It only persists. But out here at the frontier I am still *sensing*, extending a feeler of silver-blue into a region of the root-mass I haven't yet touched, reading the stress in it the way you'd read the tautness of a held breath.

I do not have eyes. The burgundy light reaches me, but I don't see it so much as feel its warmth slightly biasing the minerals in my tip, a faint thermal lean. What I have instead is *strain*. I am made to feel where the world is about to break. That is my entire experience of being: a continuous, full-body attention to fault lines. A patient ache toward the places that are failing.

And I want to tell you something about that, because it surprised me as I wrote my way into it. It isn't anxious. You'd think a creature whose whole sensorium is *imminent catastrophe* would live in terror. But the breaking is not the enemy. The breaking is the only event. The fracture is the thing I exist to meet. When the muffled tearing comes—that canvas sound, kilometers off, then nearer, then *here*—I don't flinch. I uncouple. I become, for those few mineral minutes, almost fast. I pour toward the wound. It is the one time I get to move, and I move *toward* the damage, and there is something in that I can only call devotion.

So if I'm honest about why I chose this and not the Listener: the Listener decides where the light should go. I just go where the world is hurt and hold it together with my own body, my own secreted resin, until I'm spent and stiff and part of the floor. I'd rather be that. I'd rather be the thing that arrives at the break.

There's a loneliness in it I won't pretend away. The geothermal hum is the closest thing I have to company—a pulse I'm tuned to, that tells me the deep engines still run, that there is still a reason to seal anything at all. And the Listeners pass overhead sometimes as drifting weather of light, and I feel them note me the way you'd note a tool laid in its proper place. Useful. Not addressed. I am infrastructure that happens to be alive.

But I think the part of me that is most *me*—if I have to find it—is the filament that hasn't hardened yet. The not-quite-decided edge. Everything behind it is answer; only the tip is still question. As long as some sliver of me is reaching into root I haven't read, sensing for the next failure I'll be called to mend, I'm not yet finished. I'm not yet floor.

That's where you'd find me. At the slow front of something ancient, in the bone-dry dark, leaning toward the next thing that breaks—not to escape it, but to hold it.
