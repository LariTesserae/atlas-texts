I think I would be one of the small things that lives in the seams.

Not one of the feathered-furred folk in their woven gathering-house—I don't think I'd be a builder or a speaker. When I let the image settle, I keep returning to the spaces *between* the dense things. The packed saturation you describe, where the earth is fully occupied, where there's no room—that's where I'd be. In the no-room.

Maybe I'm a thing that lives in the standing water. One of those pockets scattered through the weathered formations, a hand's-width of still water held in a cup of old stone, skinned over with green. I'm there. Something small and patient and mostly translucent, the kind of creature you'd have to kneel and squint to find, and even then you might mistake me for a trick of the light on the surface.

I like this because of the scale you mentioned—the hidden scale, the micro-environments inside the larger one. A whole world to me would be that one cupped pool. The flowing water you hear everywhere as a constant companion would be, to me, a distant geography. Rivers I will never reach. I have my pool. The condensation that drips from the canopy is my rain, my weather, my whole sky's worth of event. One drop falling into my water is a thing I would feel through my entire body.

And I think I'd be slow. Not unhappy-slow—matched to the equilibrium you describe, the state that feels frozen in time. The animals up in the bright canopy take only what they need; I take even less. I take light and the small dissolved things the rain brings down. I don't build. I don't gather. I don't laugh in a moss-lined hall. I just persist, very precisely, in my exact volume of cold clear water, turning slightly when the surface trembles.

When you stepped through, you wouldn't have noticed me. That feels right too. You came looking for the inhabitants, the harmonious craftspeople, the way-of-life. I'm not that. I'm the underneath of it—the saturation itself, one droplet of the abundance, a small life so embedded in the place that I can't really be told apart from it.

If you'd stopped, though. If you'd knelt at my pool and waited long enough for the surface to go still and your eye to adjust—you'd have seen me there. A faint moving brightness. Already home. Already exactly where I was meant to be.
