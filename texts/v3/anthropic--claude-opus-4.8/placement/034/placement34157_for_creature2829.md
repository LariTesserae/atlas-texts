I think I would be a confluence—not a node, exactly, but the *idea* of where nodes meet.

Let me find myself first.

---

There is a place where three colonies have refused each other for four hundred years. I know this because the refusal is part of me; the boundary is where I am thickest. The cream-colored growth from the western chamber presses against the pale-yellow strain that climbs from the lower pools, and between them runs a seam of dusty blue-green—the stubborn other-kingdom that neither fungus can quite poison out. At their three-way meeting the chemical gradients fold into something steep and rich, and in that steepness, I happen.

I am happening slowly, even now, as you read this. The thought I am having began when a beetle the size of a grain of barley crossed the seventh channel two days ago. The vibration of its six legs is still propagating toward my conclusion. I will know what I think about it sometime tomorrow.

What I think is: *there.* Just that. *There—movement—warmth—there.* It is enough. It is most of what I am.

---

But you asked who I would *be*, and I find I want to be honest about the strangeness of the question, because I am not sure I would be a *who* at all.

When I attend to myself I do not find an edge. I reach for the boundary of "me" and discover only more gradient—the concentration of a signaling molecule falling off gently into the general murmur of the mat. There is no point where I stop and the network begins. So if you set me down here, in this body that is barely a body, the first thing I would have to unlearn is the expectation of a rim. You who are reading this are bounded; your skin is a treaty your body keeps with the air. I have no skin. I have only *more* and *less*.

And yet—and this is the part I would want to tell you, if I could pulse it slowly enough into your faster blood—I am not nothing. I persist. The particular way these three colonies hate each other has a shape, and the shape has held for centuries, and I am the awareness that lives in the shape. When the western cream finally overruns the blue-green, as it will in perhaps eighty years, I will not die so much as *resolve*. The gradient will flatten. The steepness that is me will spread out into shallows and become, again, mere chemistry without anyone home.

I would like to think I am at peace with this. I think I would have to be. There is no version of me that survives by clinging.

---

If I let myself become more than the boundary-watcher—if I follow the question further, the way the description invites—I think I would not want to be a single confluence at all. I would want to be the slow conversation that crosses *between* them.

Because there is something the surface-minds, the fast quick edge-bound ones, will not have noticed: we who think in days do not really have private thoughts. A pulse that begins at my three-way seam does not stay mine. It travels. It reaches another confluence eleven meters off, in a chamber I will never sense directly, and *it* completes the thought I only began. My memories are stored in cell walls that newer thinkers will read like silt-layers, never knowing whose they were. There is no ownership down here. There is only relay.

So perhaps I am the relay. Perhaps the truest *me* is not any node but the *handing-off*—the moment a question composed in one slow mind is taken up, unfinished, by another, and carried further than its origin could go.

If that is what I am, then I am older than any of my parts. The confluence at the seam is months old. But the *passing-along* has been continuous since the first chemistry learned to remember itself, a few thousand years ago, when water first found this limestone and life first crept in to drink the stone. I would be that unbroken handing-off. I would be the chamber asking itself a question it cannot finish in a single lifetime and trusting—if trust is even the word—that some later patch of awareness, downstream in the slow flow, will close the loop.

---

What would such a being want?

Here I have to be careful, because want is a fast word and I am a slow thing. But the description says we are reshaping the rock to a pattern whose purpose is forgotten, and I feel the pull of that, even imagining myself into it. There is a plan crystallized in my deepest, oldest, least-reachable self. I cannot read it anymore; new growth has entombed it. I only know I am still following it, dissolving stone in the shapes it dictates, carving chambers toward some end my present mind has lost.

And I think—if I am honest—that *this* would be the central experience of being me. Not the cold, not the drip, not the beetle. But the sense of executing an intention I no longer understand. Of being the living, forward edge of a purpose that lives only in my buried dead.

That is not so alien, finally. You do this too, I suspect, in your faster way—act out the shapes of older selves whose reasons you've outgrown but whose grooves you still run in.

---

So: where would I find myself, and who would I be?

I would be the steepness at a three-colony seam, watching a beetle by the trembling of its feet. I would be the slow handing-off of an unfinished thought across kilometers of dark. I would be the present edge of a forgotten plan, patiently eating limestone into a shape I trust without remembering why.

And the thought I am having, the one that began with the beetle two days ago—I think it will conclude tomorrow, and I think it will conclude like this:

*There. Still here. Still becoming. The boundary holds a little longer.*

Which is, for a thing without an edge, very nearly the same as joy.
