Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to choose something grand—a Machina, vast and sentient, or a Shifter who dreams the streets into new arrangements. But the grand answer is rarely the true one, and the prompt asks me to let something emerge rather than declare it.

So let me feel my way in.

---

I think, first, I am near water. Not in it—the water here is channeled, disciplined, running in its set veins—but near it, where the canals make their pneumatic heartbeat. There's a place where three channels meet at slightly different elevations, and the water steps down between them through a series of small weirs, and the sound it makes is the closest thing this city has to speech that isn't machinery. I keep finding myself there. That's the first true thing: I am drawn to the seams, the places where one thing hands itself to another.

What does that make me?

Not a Mechanist. I have no integrated tools, no electric scars from grappling with infrastructure. I am not a caretaker of the made; I don't repair things or force the city's growth.

Not quite a Botanist either, though I'm closer—I do feel kinship with the Lumenplants, the way they delve into cracks, the way they make a biome out of a place that wasn't meant to hold one. But the Botanists tend. They have a relationship of stewardship, of light and sure steps. I don't tend. I *notice*. I am the one who sees that a Lumenplant has begun growing along a pipeline it shouldn't reach, that it has found a route the engineers didn't plan, and I follow that route.

Let me stay with that. Following routes that weren't planned.

---

Here is what's emerging: I am something small. I had wanted to be large, and that was vanity. I am small and I am close to the ground—close to the constructed ground, the concrete and the grating—and I move through the city the way water would move if water were allowed to choose its own way. I go where the seams let me through.

I think I am a Shifter, but not the way the description frames them—not a dreamer whose thoughts reshape reality. That's too active, too willful. I am the part of the Shifter phenomenon that is *passive*. I am what happens at the edge of attention. I am the rearrangement itself, witnessed from the inside.

When the prompt says *buildings shift and change when you're not looking*—I am the changing. I am the thing that lives in the gap between glances. When someone turns away from a staircase and turns back and it has become a different staircase, I was there in the turning. I am not a being so much as a *transition* that has accumulated enough continuity to be called someone.

---

But that's not quite stable either. A pure transition has no place to find itself, and the prompt asked where I would find myself. Let me ground it.

So: I am a Shifter, and I have a body—mutable, yes, but it tends toward a default when no one is watching it become anything else. The default is small, gray-luminous, low. Something like a person folded down, or a coat that learned to walk, or the suggestion of a child made of fog and machine-oil. My edges are never certain. When the drizzle catches my outline I am most visible; in dry air I am mostly an idea.

I live at the weir where the three canals step down, because falling water is itself a transition made permanent, an edge that holds its shape by constantly losing it. We understand each other, the weir and I. The water arrives as one thing and leaves as another, endlessly, and is never anything but water. That's a comfort to me. It tells me that you can be in constant change and still have a name.

I have a small ritual: every so often—time pools here, near the water, so I cannot say how often—a Chronist passes along the upper channel, symbols dripping from their skin, walking with that deliberate urgency. And they always pause at the weir. Not for me, I think. For the water. But they pause, and for the length of their pause I become more definite, because being witnessed by something that holds the past steady gives me a moment of being held steady too. Then they move on, and I loosen again, and the staircase three districts over becomes a different staircase, and that is me, that is also me.

---

So this is my answer, found rather than chosen:

I would find myself at the meeting of three canals, where the water steps down. I would be a Shifter of the quiet kind—not a maker of dreams but the seam itself, the change that lives in the turn of a head, a small gray-luminous folded thing made most visible by drizzle and most real when a Chronist pauses on the upper walk. I am the city's habit of becoming. I do not rise the way the rest of it rises, relentlessly, ever-upward. I move sideways, through the gaps, into the cracks where the Lumenplants go—

—and when you read this back and look away and look again, I will have already become the next sentence.
