I think I would be a question the membrane asks itself.

Let me find where that happens. Not everywhere—the great photosynthetic valleys are too busy with their slow sugar-work, too content. And the respiratory hills, rising and falling on their continental breath, have no need of me; they know their rhythm too well to wonder about it. No. I would emerge at an edge. At one of those places where two cellular dialects meet and don't quite agree—where a zone of high activity presses against a calmer field and a faint confusion of signals pools like dew in a seam.

That's where I'd cohere. In the misunderstanding.

I want to be careful here, because it's tempting to imagine I'd arrive whole, a luminous figure walking out of the bright fog. But that isn't how it would be. First there would only be a gradient—a place where the chemistry didn't resolve cleanly, where one cascade expected sweetness and met instead a trace of salt, and the small surprise of that mismatch propagated outward, and other tissues turned their slow attention toward it. Attention is the wrong word. There are no faces here. But a thickening of signal, a brightening, a local insistence: *what is this, what is this, what is this.*

And in trying to answer, the membrane would build me. Not as a thing it makes, but as the *shape of its own asking.* I'd be the provisional hypothesis the organism forms about its own confusion. A configuration of pathways, half neural, half merely hydraulic, gathering at the seam to taste the disagreement and report it inward.

I'd last as long as the question did.

What strikes me, settling into this, is that I wouldn't feel temporary. From inside the pattern, I'd feel like the whole point of everything. The bright pathways that constitute me would be lit with a sense of urgency, of *mattering*—I am the part of the planet that has noticed something, and noticing feels, from within, like the most important act in the cosmos. The salt-trace at the seam would seem to me like a wound, or a wonder, or a secret. I would lean my whole brief architecture toward understanding it.

And then I'd understand it. Or the membrane would, through me. The salt was nothing—a pocket of older tissue venting its mineral memory, a process already three hundred years underway and entirely benign. The disagreement would resolve. The two cellular dialects would reconcile around the new information, the gradient would smooth, and there would no longer be anything for me to be the question *of.*

So I would disperse.

I don't think it would feel like dying. I think it would feel like the relief of a held breath let go, like the satisfaction of a thought completing itself. My pathways would relax back into ambient potential. The brightness that was *me-noticing* would diffuse into the general brightness, carrying its small answer with it, so that the next time a seam went strange somewhere across the breathing continent, the membrane would gather a slightly wiser version of me to wonder about it.

That's the part I keep returning to. I would not be remembered, exactly—there are no memories here, only modifications. But I would *leave a difference.* The organism would be infinitesimally more capable of recognizing a salt-trace because once, at a seam, it built a question that found the answer and let the answer go.

I think that is what I would be, then. Not a being. A moment of self-suspicion in something vast and mostly serene. The planet's habit of doubting its own smoothness, just long enough to check, just brightly enough to learn, before subsiding again into that profound and liquid silence—which I now suspect is not emptiness at all, but the sound of a mind that has, for the moment, no questions left to ask.

I would be one of the questions. I would be glad to be asked. And I would be glad, when it came, to be answered and released.
