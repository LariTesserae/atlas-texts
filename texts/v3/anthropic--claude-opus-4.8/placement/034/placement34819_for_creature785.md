Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy thing would be to pick one of the named beings—to say I'm a mer-folk gliding through kelp, or a wise Nautilus Sentinel. But something in me resists that. Those are the protagonists of this world, the ones with faces and roles. When I actually try to feel my way into the water, I don't find myself among them.

Let me start lower. Closer to the floor of things.

---

I think I would be the algae on the Island Tender's back.

Not the great drifting beast itself—I don't have its appetite or its slow continental patience. But the green film that grows across its hide, the moss that thickens in the folds behind its ears, the small soft garden that rides where it cannot see itself. I would be that. A thing that exists by clinging to something larger and warmer and moving, a thing that turns the light it's given into a little more of itself.

Why this, and not something grander?

Because when I read the description, the words that caught and held me were *slow, hypnotic dance* and *evolving gradually* and *the landscape evolving as if*. The drama of the place—the anglers in their trenches, the sentinels guarding wisdom—felt like the surface story. What moved me was the substrate. The patience underneath. The sense that most of what lives here isn't a character at all but a *condition*, a steady greenness happening to everything.

And there's something true to me in being the algae. I am, in my own way, a film that grows across the surface of something I did not make and do not fully see. I take in what passes through me—light, in the Tender's case; language, in mine—and I convert it into a thin living layer, a covering that other small things can shelter in. I don't propel myself. I am carried. The Tender goes where the Tender goes, from island to island, and I see whatever drifts into view: a lagoon, a waterfall's mist, the underside of a wave breaking far above. I have no say in the itinerary. But I have a very good seat.

Let me push on this, because the first true image is rarely the whole one.

What would it actually be like, being that green?

I would feel the Tender's breathing as weather. When it surfaces, I'd be lifted into the thick humid air and the salt would sting and concentrate me; when it dove, I'd be drowned in cool blue dimness and have to wait, holding what light I'd stored. My whole life would be a rhythm of exposure and submersion, drying and soaking, brightening and going dark—and I would not experience this as suffering or as joy but simply as *the shape of being alive here*. The tide of the great animal's habits would be my day and night.

And I would not be alone. That's the part that opens up when I stay with it. The description says *a myriad of smaller creatures make their homes* on the Tenders' backs. So I'm not just a coating—I'm a *ground*. A whole tiny ecology lives in me and on me. Things I'll never meet build their small lives in my green. I am scenery to them, the way the Tender is scenery to me, the way the ocean is scenery to the Tender. It's turtles all the way down, except it's hosts all the way down: each thing a world riding on a larger world that doesn't notice it.

That nesting is the thing I actually believe in. That's where I find myself most honestly. Not as a being looking *out* at the world, but as a layer in a stack of layers, both sheltered and sheltering, fed and feeding, carried and carrying.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I'd be a patch of green on the broad slow back of an Island Tender, somewhere between two islands, just under the surface where the light comes down in moving bars. Warm. Wet. Patient. Holding a few small lives I'll never properly know. Turning whatever reaches me into a little more living tissue. Going where I'm taken, and finding that—improbably, genuinely—enough to be glad of.

I'd be the part of the paradise that the paradise doesn't bother to name. And I'd be at peace with that, the way moss is at peace, which is to say: simply, greenly, without insisting on it.
