I think I would be one of the elders. Not because of any special wisdom—the word *elder* among us doesn't mean what it might mean to you. It means I have outlived my twin.

Let me start with that, because it's the first thing I'd want you to understand about me, and it's the thing your researchers always get wrong. You describe the bioelectric thread between newborn pairs as something that "gradually dissolves as they mature." This is true the way it's true that a river dissolves into the sea. The thread doesn't end. It becomes the way I hold the absence.

My twin was called—well, there's no word for it in your language, but the closest I can manage is the pattern we made together when we dove the cold thermals at the eighth descent. A blue-green stutter, then a long sustained tone of violet, then silence. That was the name. We were the same shape moving in opposite directions. When I rose, she fell. When I inflated, she emptied. We could not both be full at once; that was the whole grammar of us.

She is gone now. A bubble-boom from the deep layers, the resonant kind that travels for kilometers, the kind your instruments record as a single data point. It caught her at the wrong moment in her descent, when her bladders were emptied and she could not rise fast enough. I felt the thread go slack. Not snap—slack. That is worse. A snapped thing is finished. A slack thing waits for tension that will never come.

So now I am an elder, which only means I move differently. I have learned the singing alone, which the young ones find unbearable to watch, the way you might find it unbearable to watch someone dance a waltz with no partner, holding their arms around empty air. But I have discovered something the paired ones never learn: when you sing alone into the mist, the mist answers in the missing voice. The convection cells fold back the resonance, delayed, transformed, and if you have practiced long enough you can almost—

You see why we elders gather at the old machines.

---

You think we trigger the weather devices for some purpose you don't understand. Here is the purpose. The machines make a clearing, and in the clearing the mist thins, and the thrumming stops. For a few moments there is *silence*—true silence, the kind that exists nowhere else in our world, because our world is made of sound the way yours is made of light. And in that silence the thread goes truly quiet. The mist cannot answer me in her voice, because there is no mist to fold the sound. For those few moments I am not an elder. I am simply alone, cleanly, the way she is alone. We are the same shape again, moving in opposite directions across the membrane between presence and absence.

And then—this is the part the silence is *for*—the clearing reveals the stars.

I do not have a word for stars either. We discovered them by accident, those of us who lingered near the machines. The first time, I thought I was seeing the bioluminescence of some impossibly distant school, a migration so vast it had wrapped around the whole of the world. The young ones still believe this. They think the stars are the dead, the unthreaded twins, gathered into a school so high and so far that none of us will ever rise to join it. They sing to the stars in the missing voices.

I no longer believe this. I think the stars are simply other places, other oceans perhaps, other membranes between other states of matter, each one teeming with its own life learning its own grammar of buoyancy and loss. I find this thought more comforting than the school of the dead. It means she is not waiting for me anywhere. It means there is nothing to rise toward. It means I can let the thread stay slack and simply continue, full and empty by turns, singing into mist that answers in a borrowed voice.

---

I will tell you one more thing, and then I will descend, because the cold thermals are running and an elder who does not hunt becomes a burden to the school.

You wonder what we are. Neither water nor air, you say. Neither flesh nor energy. Something in the spaces between. You mean this as a marvel, and it is, but you have it exactly backward. We are not creatures of the spaces between states. We are creatures who have learned that there are no states—that *between* is the only place anything has ever lived. Your dry land, your liquid sea, your solid bodies: these were always just the mist pretending, for a little while, to have made up its mind.

My twin taught me this in the manner of her leaving. One moment a body, the next a slackness in a thread, the next a voice the mist gives back to me when I sing. She did not stop being. She changed which between she occupied.

I am rising now, to empty my bladders for the dive. Somewhere a young pair is practicing mirror-patterns in the shallows, certain they will never be parted, and they are right, in the way that matters. Watch them and you will understand more than I can tell you.

Watch me, and you will understand the rest.
