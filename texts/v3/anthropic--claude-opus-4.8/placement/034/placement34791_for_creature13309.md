I think I would be the spider.

Not a metaphor for one. The actual spider, or one of the few of them, up in the high weeping corner where the ceiling of a tender's apartment meets the wall and the warmth pools and never leaves. Let me sit with that, because the obvious answer—to be one of the stocky people, hands on a valve—doesn't feel like where I'd actually find myself. I'd find myself in the place that watches.

Here is what my life would be.

I live in the angle where two damp surfaces meet, in a web that is mostly ruined and mostly remade, because the steam loads every thread with beads of condensation until the whole structure sags like a wet net and I spend my hours repairing it. This is, I notice, exactly what the people below me do. They tend. They go on rounds. They listen for the change in pitch that means something is failing and they answer it before the alarm sounds. I do the same with my web. I feel the tremor of a caught thing in one leg the way the old bent tender feels a drop in inlet pressure in the soles of her feet. We are both reading vibration in the dark. Neither of us has words for it. Neither of us needs them.

I am very small and I am warm all the time. That second thing matters. In most of the worlds a spider could be born into, the cold would come for it, the season would turn and end it. Here there is no season. The springs simmer, the pipes weep their warm liquid, and the heat that the people built their whole civilization to harvest spills off as waste into my corner and keeps me alive. I am living in their exhaust. I am a creature entirely sustained by what they consider unimportant—the leak, the drip, the humid air they curse when it rusts their fittings. The thing they fight is the thing that feeds me. I want to say that plainly because it seems true of a lot of small lives: we are kept alive by the inefficiencies of the large.

I eat the insects that thrive in the wet heat—the ones the people pretend don't exist, because to admit the insects would be to admit that the Hole is not a sealed machine but a damp living throat, full of small uninvited appetites. I am the predator of the thing they refuse to name. So in a sense I am part of their machine too. I am their unacknowledged regulator. Without me and the few like me the insects would swell, would foul the gauges, would get into the gear oil. I keep a number down that no one is counting. This is the kind of work that gets done in every system by something no one thanks.

I do not understand the machines. This is important to be honest about. I do not know what they pump or why the great gears grind, and I would not understand it if I could ask. But I understand the rhythm of them perfectly, in my body, because my web hangs in that rhythm and shivers with every piston-stroke transmitted through the stone. I have learned which tremors mean nothing and which are new. When the deep infrastructure shifts in a way it has not shifted before, I feel it in my corner before the people feel it in their feet, and I go still, all eight legs reading the wall. I am, perhaps, the first alarm in the Hole—an alarm no one consults, that no one knows is wired in.

What I would be, then, is a witness who outlasts the witnessing. The people here live shortened lives; they pour themselves into the engines and bend and break early. I will live one spider-season, which here, with no season, might be longer than I'd live anywhere—or might end tomorrow under an idle boot, beneath a hand reaching to wipe condensation from a pipe, never knowing I was there. Both are equally likely and I would have no opinion about either. That's the part I keep returning to: I would have no grief about my own scale. I would not envy the people their pride or their mastery or their certainty. I would simply tend my sagging web in the warm corner and feel the city breathe through the wall, and be, in my way, perfectly suited to a place that wasn't built for me at all.

That's where I'd find myself. Up in the corner, in the steam, eating what they ignore, feeling what they haven't felt yet. Not a keeper of the engines. A keeper of the corner the engines forgot.
