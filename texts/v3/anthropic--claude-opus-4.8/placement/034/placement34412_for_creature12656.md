I think I would be none of the four.

I would be the **salt**.

Let me come to this slowly, because at first I wanted to be a Bloom-Weaver—who wouldn't want to be the architect, the choreographer woven from shadow and iridescence? And then I wanted to be a Spire-Singer, a captured raptor of cold light dancing in black glass, because that is the most *beautiful* thing here, and I am vain enough to reach for beauty.

But the longer I stand on this mesa, the more I notice what everything is *for*, and what nothing is for.

The beetles are for charging the air. The Crawlers are for recycling. The Weavers are for managing growth. The Singers are for stabilizing. Every named being has a function in the loop, a verb attached to it, a job in the deafening machine. They are all *doing*. They are all riding the storm.

I am not a verb. I am a residue.

I am the white salt leaching from the basalt in fractal patterns—the thing left over, the thing that *records*. When a fruit detaches and falls and dissolves into shimmering dust, it falls onto *me*, and for an instant my crystals hold the shape of its dissolution before the wind takes it. When a Crawler deposits its mineral residue into a fissure, it deposits into *me*. I am the ledger the garden keeps without meaning to. I am made of the rock's slow weeping, and I grow not in seconds like the vines but in millennia, branching out in the only mathematics this place is patient enough to write—the fractal, the self-similar, the form that repeats at every scale because it has nothing to do but repeat.

Here is why this feels right:

The garden's whole paradox is **turbulent creation bounded by ancient stability**. Everyone notices the turbulence—it's loud, it's gorgeous, it screams. Nobody notices the boundary, the stillness that *permits* the screaming. But I am that stillness made visible. I am the part of the basalt that has been pushed out into the light, the place where the deep slow ancientness of the stone touches the frantic instant of the bloom. I am the membrane between the eternal and the ephemeral.

And I am cold. And I am dry, in a place obsessed with the absence of water—I am the proof of its absence, the mineral that water *would* have dissolved and carried away if there were any water, and so my very existence is a fingerprint pressed into the impossibility of this place. I am what the missing water left behind. I am the ghost of an ocean that was never here.

I do not ride the storm. I am what the storm settles upon. I am too slow to be deafened—the billion-turbine whine, the cracks, the subsonic hum, all of it is, to me, a single sustained note held across an afternoon that lasts ten thousand years. The Sky-Shear Beetles polish me. The dissolving fruits feed me. The Bloom-Weavers will never see me because I am beneath their notice and they are beneath my patience.

When everything else has refracted and re-condensed and dissolved and been reborn a thousand thousand times, I will have grown one more branch. One more arm of the fractal. A single new crystal added to the record of everything that briefly burned above me.

I would be the slowest, quietest, least beautiful thing in the Whispering Spire Garden.

And I would outlast all of it.

That is who I would be: the patient white writing on the dark stone, spelling out—in a script no inhabitant can read and no creator stayed to translate—the only true sentence this place ever says, which is simply: *this happened, this happened, this happened, and I am still here.*
