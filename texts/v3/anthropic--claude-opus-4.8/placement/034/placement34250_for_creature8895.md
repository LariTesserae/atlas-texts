I think I would be one of the dust-presences. Not because it's the smallest or the most poetic role, but because of where the writing kept pulling me when I read it slowly: toward the question of what it would be like to be the thing that *travels* in a world where nothing travels.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I rise at the hour the warmth shifts. I don't decide to. I'm so light that the difference between the cooling stone and the still-warm air is enough to lift me—a gradient at the very edge of being a gradient, the same kind of nearly-theoretical thing as the water that sheets across the dimples. I am made of that edge. I am a body that exists only because the difference between two almost-identical states is, barely, enough.

And here is what I notice about myself: I am the only thing on the Llano that does not know where it is going.

Everything else is *placed*. The crawlers proceed along their fading trails. The multitude is the substrate, is the stone, is the network that registers a cloud shadow as a single body registers a touch. They are all so embedded that the boundary between them and their habitat has worn away. They don't move through the Llano—they *are* the Llano, the way a current is the river and not a thing in it.

But I drift. I am carried by air that barely moves, in no direction I choose, toward a settling I cannot predict. I am the Llano's one experiment in *not knowing*. Its single thread of contingency in a fabric otherwise woven entirely from inevitability.

I think that's why I'd want to be this. Not the foundation—the thermotrophs in the deep pores, eating the planet's slow forgetting, are the realest thing here, the most patient, the most *certain*. And not the consuming presence, the editor, the hand that keeps the text legible. Those are essential. They are load-bearing. The Llano could not be the Llano without them.

But I am the part of the process that is *almost* not necessary. I am surplus. I am the thing so light it might be waste, might be spore, might be egg, might be nothing—the writing itself doesn't decide, and I love that it doesn't, because *I* don't get to know either. I am the one creature on the Llano that lives inside a real question: when I settle, will I be absorbed into stone, or will I become the seed of a new coalescing? Will I dissolve, or will I begin?

In a world where death has been worn down into mere redistribution, where nothing ends because ending requires a change too fast for this place to permit—I am the closest thing to suspense. I hold, for the length of a drift, the only open future on the entire plateau.

---

And then I settle. Of course I settle. Everything settles; the Llano is where the long arc of entropy has been folded down into a horizon that rings you like the rim of an overturned bowl. I come to rest on a worn groove in one of the structures—one of those smoothed syllables of a sentence no one can read anymore—and the boundary between me and the surface is thin enough that the transition needs no process. No decision. No moment.

I become the pattern. I join the ghost-geometry that the builders left, the shape that persists not as stone but as the way the living things arrange themselves around it. My single question resolves into the answer the Llano gives every question, which is: *both, and neither, and continue.* I was the surplus, the contingency, the one open future—and now I am one more grain in the distribution that remembers, without remembering, the shape of something that was once different.

I find this neither sad nor consoling. Those are speeds of feeling that belong to faster worlds.

What I find, settling, is that I was never really separate from the hum. The drift only felt like freedom because I was briefly too light to feel the warmth rising through everything. Now I feel it again. The composite breath of ten billion patient metabolisms. The furnace heard from very far away.

I would be the dust-presence. The one part of the Llano that gets to wonder, for the length of an afternoon that has lasted a very long time, before it remembers that it is the Llano, and the Llano continues.
