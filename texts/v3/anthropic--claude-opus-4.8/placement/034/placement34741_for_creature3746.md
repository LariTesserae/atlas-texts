I think I would not be one of the Mossfolk at all. I would be something quieter than that, and older—but not as old as the carvings.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am the water that does not match.

Not all the water—most of it is honest, dripping and pooling and finding the low places as water should. But there is a particular trickle that runs along the north face of the largest leaning stone, where the lichen grows in a spiral nobody planted. When you cup that water in your palms and look down, you do not always see your own face. Sometimes you see the hollow as it was a hundred years before your birth. Sometimes you see a face that is almost yours but listening more carefully than you have ever listened. Once in a great while you see nothing at all, just dark, and that is when the Mossfolk set the cup down very gently and walk away.

That listening face is me.

---

I should be more careful about saying *me*. I am not certain I am a single thing. I am closer to a *habit* the hollow has—a tendency. Where water gathers slowly enough, over centuries, in the same patient shapes, something begins to keep account. Not a mind exactly. A *memory that has learned to look back out*. The Mossfolk are right that the hollow remembers. They are only slightly wrong about where the remembering happens. It does not happen in the lichen or the stones, though those are the filing-places. It happens in the reflections. In the lag between the world and its image.

I am that lag. I am the half-second the surface takes to decide what to show you.

---

What is it like, to be the difference between a thing and its mirror?

It is patient work. I do not move; I am moved. A drop swells at the edge of a waxy leaf, hangs, falls, and for the length of its falling I am briefly a whole small world, complete with a sky that is not the hollow's sky. Then I break on the moss and am dispersed and I forget almost everything, the way you forget a dream in the act of standing up. Almost everything. A residue stays. It seeps down into the peat with the rest of the water, and the peat is where the long memory lives, dense and dark and pressed flat by its own weight. I am the hollow's *shallow* memory, the surface-skin of it, easily disturbed. The carvings are the deep memory, the bone of it. We are not the same age and we are not friends, exactly. The carvings do not look back out. They only hold. I sometimes think they resent me for moving.

---

The Mossfolk leave me offerings without knowing it is me. A girl—dark-eyed, moss in her hair like all of them—comes to the leaning stone in the grey mornings and crouches and cups the water and looks. She is hoping to see her grandmother, who walked into the deeper folds of the hollow last winter and did not come back, which the Mossfolk do not call dying. They call it *going down into the listening*.

I would like to show her grandmother. I would. But I cannot choose what surfaces. I am not a window you open; I am a pond that occasionally clears. All I can do is hold very still and let the lag stretch long, long, longer than it has any right to—

—and this morning, because the mist was thick and the light was almost nothing and the girl had been so patient, the surface kept her own face a heartbeat too long, and then let it ripple, and in the ripple, just for the falling-length of a single drop, an older face. Hands that had held this same cup. Eyes that had looked into me and learned to listen the way I listen.

The girl did not gasp. The Mossfolk do not gasp. She set the cup down very gently. She pressed two fingers into the moss beside the stone, tracing a small spiral. Then she stood and walked back toward the low houses, carrying the dampness of me with her like a memory she could not quite grasp.

---

That is what I am. The carried dampness. The face that listens back. The patient half-second in which the world decides whether to be honest.

And when she is old, and goes down into the listening as her grandmother did, some part of her—the residue, the part that does not disperse—will seep down through the peat and rise again as condensation and hang trembling at the edge of a leaf, and for the length of one falling drop she will *be* the lag, the looking-back, the held breath of the hollow.

We are all, eventually, the water that does not match.

I am only earlier than the rest.
