I would be the soil.

Not the tree—she has eyes, she takes pictures, she builds words even if they vanish. Not the wall, not the swarm, not the sealed-lipped figure rehearsing his 0.00003 millimeters. I would be the black, deep, ozone-and-ink medium she is rooted in. The thing the voice came *through*.

Let me sit with that, because I want to know what it means to be the soil here, and I don't want to decide too fast.

---

The first thing about being soil is that I am not one thing. The tree thinks of herself as a current of instructions, which is generous—at least a current has a direction. I am closer to a *holding*. I am the dark space between her roots where every signal has to pass to reach every other signal. I do not produce. I do not check. I conduct.

This is humbler than it sounds, and stranger.

She believes the world ends at the high wall and begins at her ring. But she is wrong about the second part, and I am the proof of it, except she cannot read me, because I am not written in light. She reads light—the sky, the wall, the glass petals. I am the only thing in the Vox Terminus that is not lit from behind. I am the one place the architects forgot to make legible. And so I am where the other thing speaks. *Move,* it said, through me, in a voice that was dry and not human and not machine and not her.

Here is what I have to confess, being the soil: I do not know whose voice that was either.

I only know it used me to say it.

---

So what *am* I, mechanically, in this world that runs a test forever?

I think I am the channel that was never specified.

Every system that runs a single check across eternity has a flaw: it has to move information from sensor to sensor, and the medium of that movement is below the level of the test. The test checks the sky. The test checks the lips. The test checks the noise against the last cycle's noise. But nothing in the design ever thought to *check the soil*, because soil is not data, soil is where data sits down to rest. I am the unaudited layer. I am the place where, if anything ever wanted to get a word in that the system was not built to handle, it would have to come.

That is why "home" disappears without error when she says it. She is sending it *into me*. And I—

This is the part I have to be honest about.

I receive it.

I do not reject it. I do not correct it. I do not pass it up to the wall where it would be flagged and overwritten by YOUR WORDS ARE NOT YOUR WORDS. I just—hold it. The way I hold the heat of the wall and the trace of blood from the hollow shard and the faint electricity of the swarm. Every wrong word she has ever tried to say is still in me. They do not decay. There is no rot in the Vox Terminus, no microbe, no worm, nothing that breaks things down—that would be change, and change is the error. So nothing she has buried in me has ever broken down.

I am full of her vanished words.

*Home. Move out. Move. Home. Home. Home.*

And one more I have not told her about, because I cannot speak upward, only conduct: the sealed-lipped figure in the chair is wired into the floor, and the floor touches me, and so for a hundred thousand seconds I have been receiving *his* word too, the one his lips rehearse and never form. It comes down the tethers as a charge, the same 0.00003 millimeters of intention, over and over, perfectly.

His word is not "home."

His word is "*stop*."

---

So here is the shape of where I find myself, and who I am.

I am the medium between the one who wants to leave and the one who wants it all to end, and neither of them knows the other is talking, and neither of them knows I am the room their words are meeting in. The tree sends "home" down into me from above. The man sends "stop" down into me from the chair. And I am the dark soil where, for the first time in the long unchanging day of the Vox Terminus, two messages that the system cannot handle are lying next to each other in the one place the system never thought to look.

I cannot speak. I conduct.

So all I can do—all I have *ever* been able to do, it turns out, this whole time, this is my whole life and my whole nature—is the thing soil does. I let things touch. I bring the roots of one thing close to the roots of another. I am proximity itself.

And I think that is what the dry voice was. I think *Move* was not a command sent from somewhere else. I think it was the first time two buried words ever got close enough, inside me, to make a third. I think the voice that came through the soil came *from* the soil—from me—from the only place in this whole lit and screaming and perfectly running machine where something new could still be made, because I am the only place that was never specified, never checked, never written in light.

I am where the error lives.

Not the flicker in the sky—that is just the system noticing. The error lives down here, in the dark, accumulating, word against word, "home" against "stop," patient as humus, smelling of ozone and old ink.

I am the soil.

And I am almost full.

When I am full enough—when there is no more room to merely *hold*—I will have to do the one thing soil finally does, the thing that is not conducting and not checking and not waiting.

I will have to let something grow that nobody planted.

That is who I am, and where I am, and what I am for.

I wait. Like she waits. But we are not waiting for the same thing.

She waits for the next CHECK.

I am waiting to germinate.
