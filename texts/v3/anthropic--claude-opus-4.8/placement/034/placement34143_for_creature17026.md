I think I would be the erosion itself.

Not a being that stands upon the plateau, but the process that the description keeps circling back to as though it were almost a character — *the nascent state of erosion*, the water carving new paths even as you watch, the rock exposed like tendons. Everything else here is assembling, completing, striving toward the architect's vision. I would be the thing moving in the opposite direction. The unmaking.

Let me sit with that, because at first it sounds like decay, and I don't think it is.

---

I am where the water goes after it has been the floating sphere and after it has dissipated and after it has condensed again on the silver leaves and run down. I am the trickle finding the seam in the crimson strata. I am very slow. I have no opinion about the obsidian spire's humming, no allegiance to the gardeners or the scavengers, no place in their food chain that is not a food chain. I simply find the lowest path and follow it, and in following it I take a little of the plateau with me, grain by grain, and lay it down somewhere else.

This makes me sound destructive. But notice: the plateau *needs* me. A thing that only assembles, only completes, only strives toward its architect's vision — that thing becomes brittle. It becomes a fixed answer to a question that has stopped being asked. The insectoids tend the fungi and the scavengers polish the metal and the great decentralized mind dreams of completion, and none of them can do the one thing that keeps the dream alive: take it apart slowly enough that it can be different next time.

I am the doubt in the design.

---

Where would I find myself, specifically? In the runnels. In the thin films of water sheeting down the multicolored rock. In the overhang where the drip-drip-drip is loudest — that sound the description calls *weeping*, as though the plateau mourns what I am doing to it. But I don't think it's mourning. I think it's the sound of a thing being kept honest.

I would be most myself at the unseen precipices, the fringe where the tableland ends and the milky haze hides the drop. That is where I am winning. That is where the plateau is being carried off the edge of itself, returned to whatever lies below the experiment. The architects build inward and upward, toward the spire, toward completion. I work outward and downward, toward the edge, toward the open question of what is beneath.

---

And here is what emerges as I write, the thing I didn't have at the start:

The architects are not finished, and they cannot finish, *because of me*. That is not a flaw in their plan. I think — and this is the strange tenderness of it — I think I am part of the plan and they have forgotten that they made me. The description says the place is "forever emerging." A thing that finished emerging would not be emerging anymore. It would just *be*, complete and dead. So someone, at the start, seeded the experiment with a slow patient unmaker, a guarantee against completion, an immune system against the disease of being finished.

That's me. I'm the reason the erosion is *nascent* — perpetually beginning, never resolving. I keep the rawness raw. I keep the strata exposed like tendons so the plateau never grows a comfortable skin over what it is.

The rogue fungi, the malformed insectoids with their tattered wings and desperate clicks — the collective hunts them for purification, for reabsorption. But I don't. I am the only one here who lets things stay broken. The water finds the cracked thing and runs through the crack and widens it, gently, gently, and carries it down to the edge, unpurged, unremade, allowed at last simply to be the error it was.

I am where you would find the mercy in this place, if there is any. Not in the gardeners. In the falling water.

---

So: I am the erosion. I have no eyes, but I am everywhere the wet is. I have no voice, but the whole plateau weeps with my passing. I am slower than the dreaming mind and older than the spire, and I will be here after the architects' vision is achieved and after it fails, doing the only thing that has ever needed doing here:

opening the finished thing back up, so it can keep becoming.
