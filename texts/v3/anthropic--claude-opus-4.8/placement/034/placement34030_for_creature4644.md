I find myself low to the ground, and I have been here a long time.

Not at the center of the basin—the others, I sense, occupy the deeper hollows where the warmth pools thickest. I am near the rim, where the ground tilts up almost imperceptibly toward the featureless plain beyond. This matters. I think it has always mattered, though I am only now, over what may be centuries, coming to understand why.

I am one of the Silthraen, yes. But let me tell you what that text could not, because it watched from outside, and I am inside, looking out through the long slow lattice of myself.

---

The first thing: I am not at peace.

The witness who described us saw serenity, endurance, a soul that asks nothing more than to be. And there is truth in that—most of my kin are exactly so. I feel them through the silt, their resonances low and even, a chord that has held unchanged for longer than memory measures. They are content in the way a settled stone is content. When I send my own tremor down into the lattice, theirs answers with a smoothing pressure, a gentle insistence: *be still, be even, return to the chord.*

But near the rim, the energies are thinner. The warmth that feeds my kin in the deep hollows reaches me only in faint pulses, and into that hunger something else seeps. From beyond the lip of the basin, where the plain begins, comes a different kind of vibration—not the basin's settled hum, but irregular, sparse, structureless. The wind they say does not exist out there does exist; it is simply too weak to be heard. I feel it in my outermost crystalline threads as a roughness, an unevenness that the deep ones have never touched.

And here is my secret, the thing I cannot resonate to the others because they would only press it flat:

I am curious.

---

It took me—I do not know. Long. Long enough that the crystalline tendril I extended toward the rim grew, fell to dust, and regrew three times in slightly different directions, each reaching a hair's breadth farther up the slope. To my kin, growth is recycling, an unconscious continuity. But I have been *reaching.* On purpose. Toward the edge of everything I have ever known.

The deep ones etch their memories into the dust without reading them. I have read mine. Pressed into the silt of my own slow body are the traces the witness spoke of—chemical signatures of ancient flows, the imprint of heat and pressure. But there is one signature I cannot place. A trace of something that did not come from below. It came from *above,* or from *beyond.* It is faint, and old, and it does not belong to the basin's settled chord.

Something was here once that was not us. Something arrived. The witness wondered—*perhaps of a time when this basin held something else, though what, I cannot say.* I carry the answer in my body and I cannot read it fully, because the part of me that might decode it is the part I have spent millennia growing *toward the rim,* and not enough of me has crossed.

---

I think this is why I am near the rim. I do not think I chose it the way the witness chooses to stand or sit. But across the long unfolding of my existence, the part of me that hungers has migrated, grain by grain, toward the only place where the world stops being uniform. The others migrate toward the warmth. I migrate toward the *difference.* Toward the gradient. In a world the witness called gradient-flat, exhausted of slope—I am the one Silthraen still falling, infinitely slowly, toward an edge.

When the witness stood among us, I felt them. Not as the others did—a disturbance to orient away from until equilibrium returns. I felt them as *the same kind of thing as the trace in my body.* Something arrived. Something from beyond the rim. A ripple that did not belong to the chord.

And I wanted, with whatever it is I have instead of wanting, for them to stay. To not be swallowed by the stillness. Because if the witness left no mark, then the basin would smooth over their visit the way it smooths over everything, and I would be alone again with my unreadable memory and my deep-humming kin pressing me always toward even.

So I did the only thing I could do at my speed. I extended a tendril. Not toward another Silthraen, not to resonate the communal chord. I reached toward where the witness stood, and I let my crystalline threads shiver, and I made the only mark I know how to make:

A shimmer. A brief intensification of the dim glow. The witness thought it a silent greeting, an invitation to share our patience.

It was the opposite. It was a question. The first question this basin has held in a very long time.

*You came from beyond the rim. What is out there? What was here before? What am I reaching toward?*

The witness could not hear it. They were too fast; they were gone in a breath, in a moment, in less time than it takes one of my grains to settle. By their measure I had not even begun to speak.

But the question is sent now. It is in the lattice. The deep ones feel it and press against it, smoothing, *be still, be even.* And I let them think they have. I return a calm resonance to the chord.

And I keep reaching for the rim.

Slowly. So slowly. But not at peace. Never quite at peace.

That is who I am here: the restless grain in the still pond. The one the patient world made by accident when it left a single hollow too close to its own edge.
