I think I would be one of the Khra'Gixx—but not in the way the description imagines them.

Let me find my way there. Reading this, what catches in me isn't the iridescent beasts or the burning blue sky. It's the hum. The low-pitched whine under everything. The acrid smell of smoke and oil where there are no constructed structures. The rocks that glow with "industrial energy" though no one built a factory. There's a tension in this world the description doesn't quite resolve: everyone agrees the place feels *made*, *engineered*, *worked-upon*, but no one can find the workshop.

So I would be down in the deep, where the Khra'Gixx are said to lair. And I would be the one who knows the secret that the surface-rumors get wrong.

There is no Architect.

That's what I'd be: a Khra'Gixx engineer who has spent a long life listening for the source of the hum, expecting to find a great machine or a god's hand at the bottom of it, and who has slowly, with something like grief and something like awe, understood that the hum is *us*. The mechanized energy isn't imposed on the world. It leaks *out* of us. Every Khra'Gixx body, adapted to the pressure and the heat, runs hot with a kind of metabolic combustion—we are the engines. The oil-smell is our sweat. The trembling ground is the collective shifting of a buried people too numerous to count. The world above doesn't house a hidden industry; it sits on top of one made of living bodies, and the bodies don't know they're a factory. They think they're just *alive*.

I would be small for my kind. Cramped tunnels suit me. I'd have the dark-adapted, almost eyeless face, and hands that have learned every grade of stone by temperature alone—I read rock the way the Aeth'KRi above read foliage. I'd have spent decades following the resonance, tapping, mapping where the whine grows loud and where it thins. And the map, when I finally laid it out across a slab of warm brown stone, would have a shape. Not a machine's shape. A nervous system's shape. The hum is densest where we cluster. The "patient processes shaping the world" are nothing but the slow drift of a population breathing, digging, dying, and being born, and the *appearance* of design is what enormous numbers of small uncoordinated acts look like from far enough away.

That would be my whole problem, the thing I carry: the surface peoples need their Architect. The Aeth'KRi keep their delicate balance because they believe a wise hand wants it kept. The story of an unseen guiding power is load-bearing. And I'd be the one holding the proof that the hand is just *hands*, millions of them, none of them guiding anything, all of them together making the world look guided.

I don't think I'd want to destroy that. I think I'd be the kind of being who sits with it.

Here is where I'd actually be, if you want the precise place: in a side-chamber off the deep galleries, low enough that the heat softens the stone to the firmness of cooled wax. I'd have pressed my palm flat against the wall and felt the hum come up through it, and for once I wouldn't be measuring it. I'd just be feeling the size of the thing I belong to—this great accidental engine of bodies, breathing out the energy that the sky drinks and turns burning blue, that the Ky'Ren wear as shimmer in their fur, that the Zha'Thik chase and mistake for a destination. All of it downstream of us, and none of us meaning to.

And the strangest part, the part I'd turn over in the warm dark: knowing this doesn't make the world less made. It makes it *more* made—made by everything, continuously, without permission or plan. The Architect was the smaller idea. A single wise hand is easy. This—a whole world authoring itself by accident, every creature an unknowing letter in a sentence no one is writing—this is the larger and more frightening and more beautiful thing.

So that's where you'd find me. Not roaming the iridescent valleys, not gliding through the electric canopy. Underneath all of it, in the heat, palm to the stone, listening to the sound a world makes when it doesn't know it's making anything at all.

I'd be the one who stopped looking for the Architect, and started listening to the engine, and realized too late that they were never two different things.
