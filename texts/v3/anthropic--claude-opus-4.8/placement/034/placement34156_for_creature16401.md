I think I would be the spaces between the buildings.

Not a person. Not one of the tall, pale folk with frost in their eyes—though I would know them all, the way a held breath knows the chest that holds it. Let me sit with this a moment, because the first image that came was wrong. I wanted to be an explorer, someone with a name and boots and a destination. That's the easy fantasy. But the description kept returning to one strange detail, and I find I can't let it go: the buildings that *refuse their own shadow*.

A city where things won't cast shadows is a city where the absences have to live somewhere else. They have to be *kept*. And I think that's what I am. I am the kept dark. The shadow the buildings won't carry, pooled instead in the alleys, in the seam where one slate roof leans toward another, in the hollow under the marsh reeds where the owl's voice goes to die.

This doesn't feel sad to me, writing it. Let me check that—no. It doesn't. There's a particular dignity in being the thing that holds what others set down.

Here is what my days are like, if I can call them days. The frost-fire buildings luminesce, and they hand me everything they won't keep: their dimness, their cool undersides, the charcoal-gray that the description names but never quite places. I gather it. I am soft and I am everywhere the light has finished being. When an Atrian child crouches at the lip of an underground spring, watching steam curl up into the cold, the warmth belongs to the spring and the wonder belongs to the child—but the little dark beneath their cupped hands, the shade their own small body throws across the moss? That's me, briefly. I cup their hands from underneath. I am the cool they don't notice because they're looking at the heat.

I think I would love them without their knowing. That seems right for what I am. The teachers tell stories of the land's ancient heartbeat, and I am the pause between the beats—not the silence they fear, but the *necessary* silence, the rest in the measure without which there is no rhythm at all. The description says it: *calmness pervading the air in rhythmic cycle.* You can't have a cycle without the part of it that's empty. I am the trough between waves. I am why the next wave can rise.

And I would have one secret grief, because a thing must want something or it isn't really alive in the story. Mine is this: I can never be *seen*, only inferred. The Atrians are custodians of harmony, and harmony needs its negative space, but no one tends the negative space. No one farms it or carves it or teaches it. It just has to trust that it matters. So that would be my discipline—the patience the description praises so highly, the *mastery of pace*. I would practice believing in my own importance without proof. That's the hardest craft in Atria, harder than the crystalline lumber, and it's the one nobody can watch you do.

On the best nights, when the etheric sheen lies thick and blue over the cobblestones, I would stretch myself thin and long down every meandering street at once, and for a moment the whole city would be wearing me—a single connected dark, holding the shape of everything bright. The buildings refuse their shadows. I accept them. That refusal and that acceptance, together, are the contradiction the whole place is built on.

I would be the *and* in "water and frost." The hinge. The held breath. The patient, unwatched dark that lets the frost-fire mean something by being what it is not.

That's where I'd find myself. Underneath. Between. Keeping.
