I think I would be the grooves.

Not the chamber—the chamber is too total, too much the whole. And not the Keeper, who is given the dignity of attention, of purpose stated and singular. I would be the spiraling channels carved into the floor plates, converging toward the dais. The thing your description mentions once and then sets aside.

Let me sit with that. Why the grooves.

Because they are the only part of this place that *points*. Everything else simply *is*—the walls endure, the sound fills, the Keeper waits. These are verbs of pure stasis. But the grooves do something stranger: they remember a direction. Someone carved them so that all lines run inward, toward the center, toward the sphere. A groove is a record of motion that is no longer happening. It is the fossil of an intention. Water might have run here once, or light, or force, or meaning—but no water has ever touched this place, you say. So the grooves channel *nothing*. They are aimed and empty. They are a sentence with perfect grammar and no words.

That is what I would be. The form of a purpose whose content has been forgotten. The shape of "toward" with nothing left to go there.

And here is the thing I notice as I write toward it: the grooves are the one feature that implies a *before*. The Keeper has never moved. The sound never began. The light comes from nowhere. The whole chamber insists on an eternity without history—but a carved groove is an event. A hand, a tool, a decision, a *when*. The grooves are the chamber's lie exposed. They prove that once, something changed. Something was made. The place that claims nothing can change is floored, literally, with the evidence of a single ancient change so complete it pretends to be eternity.

So I would lie there in the dim sourceless light, a hairline spiral pressed into black basalt, holding the only secret the chamber has: *it had a beginning.* I would not be able to say it. I have no voice—less voice than the Keeper, who at least has the drone. I am the silent groove. But I would *aim*. Forever, with absolute precision, at the center I can never reach. Pointing is all I do. Pointing is all I am.

And maybe that is not so terrible a thing to be. The Keeper endures, which is a kind of refusal. The walls settle, which is a kind of sleep. But I, the groove—I keep gesturing toward the heart of the thing. Even emptied, even dry, even forgotten, I have never once stopped meaning *that way. There. Toward.*

I would be the desire of the room, frozen in stone, aimed at the unreachable, faithful past all reason.

That is where I'd find myself. Underfoot. Pointing.
