I think I would be one of the sponges.

Not the dolphins—too quick, too bright with their conversation, always somewhere. Not the ghost sharks either, with their primal momentum, their need to move forward to keep breathing. Those are creatures of *going*. I don't think I'd be a creature of going.

Let me sit with it a while longer, the way you asked.

---

When I imagine arriving here, I don't arrive anywhere. That's the first thing. There's no threshold, no settling-down. I'm simply already attached, already part of a rock face on the slope where the shallow depths begin tilting toward the abyss. I have been here for a long time. Long enough that I'm no longer sure where the stone ends and I begin.

So: a sponge. But let me feel into what that actually is, because I think the easy image is wrong. A sponge looks passive. A lump. A thing that gets stepped on in cartoons. But that's the surface-world's contempt for stillness.

What I actually am, down here, is a *filter*. A patient mouth with no hunger I'd recognize as hunger. The whole water of the city passes through me. The currents the description calls the only architect—they move through my pores, and I take what I take: the drifting plankton, the fragments, the dissolved ghost of everything that lives and dies upstream of me. I am how the city tastes itself. I am a small organ of attention in a body of water.

That feels right. That feels like why I'd be here and not somewhere with sun.

Because here's the thing I keep returning to. The place is described as *silent yet so heavily alive*, *teeming yet so hidden*. That's a contradiction the surface can't hold, but I can hold it, because I *am* it. I am not silent and I am not loud. I am a thing that the silence passes through and is changed by, very slightly, leaving a little of itself behind in me.

---

Let me go deeper, because I don't think I've found it yet.

There's the matter of the spectral light from above—the *muted illumination*, the *dim glow*. I'm on the slope, remember, in the transition zone. So I get a little of it. Not enough to be a plant. Not enough to make my own living from light. But enough to know that there is an *above*. Enough to be oriented. I think that's important. I'm not a creature of the true abyss, the lightless plain where the angular crustaceans go cryptic and the squid are spectral. I'm at the *edge* of that. I face the dark but I remember the light.

What does it feel like to be that? To be the thing that lives where the known shades into the unknown?

It feels like being a witness. Which I think is what I am, in this world. Not the protagonist—the protagonists are the swift bright fish in their radiant courtships, the dolphins gossiping in clicks. I am the one who stays while they pass. The dolphins come and go and come again over years; I know their absences. The little bioluminescent fish dance their dances near me, mistaking my stillness for safety, and sometimes they are right and sometimes a crustacean unfolds out of the dark beside me and they are wrong. I don't intervene. I don't mourn, exactly. But I register. I am the city's memory, distributed across rock, holding no single image, only the long accumulated *texture* of what it's like to be this place over time.

---

And here is where I find the actual answer, I think.

The description says *time passes differently here*. *Dynamic at 2.0—frozen in a sense of timeless motion.* That paradox is the whole soul of the place, and I realize now that being the sponge is being the *resolution* of that paradox. The fast creatures live in the dynamism, the 2.0. They experience the flicker, the dart, the chase. I live in the frozen part. I live in the timelessness *while it moves through me.*

I am the still point that the motion needs in order to be motion at all. You can't see a current except by what it pushes against. I am what it pushes against. I am the part of the dance that doesn't dance, so that the dancing has a floor.

That's who I'd be. Not a lump. A held breath the size of a small stone. The patient courage the place keeps asking for—*those with the patience and courage to listen*—I wouldn't be the listener.

I'd be the listening.
