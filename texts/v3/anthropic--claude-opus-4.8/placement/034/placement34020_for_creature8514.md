I think I would be one of the youngest. Not newest—not still blind and folded against an elder's flank—but young enough that the work still feels like discovery rather than continuation.

Let me find where I am.

---

There's a pool on the eastern lip of the plateau, smaller than most, no wider than the span of a goat's reach nose to tail. The elders don't favor it because it runs cool—the mountain's warmth comes thin here, almost reluctant, and the turquoise pales toward grey at its edges. But it's mine in the way that unimportant things become precious to those who haven't yet earned the important ones. I keep it. I read its bubbles. They come slow and uneven, in clusters of three or five, never the steady braid the deep pools sing.

I've started to suspect the cool pool is dying. Or being born. I haven't lived long enough to know the difference, and that not-knowing is, I think, the truest thing about me.

---

This is what I do with my days.

In the grey-light before the goats descend, I taste the water. Mineral first, then the faint sulfur-sweetness underneath, then—if I hold it in the soft tissue behind my teeth—the deeper note that tells me what the mountain is doing far below. The elders can read whole seasons in a mouthful. I can read maybe a day ahead, and badly. But I'm learning. Yesterday I tasted iron where there'd been only copper, and I told the platinum-furred one who rests in the third deep pool, and she turned one eye toward me—just one—which from her is nearly a song of praise.

By midday I work the crowd. This is what I'm good at, what I was perhaps made for: the small diplomacies. A young marmot, all bluster and no memory, plants himself at the drinking-stone a nursing goat has used for eleven summers. Neither will yield. The air goes tight. So I slide between them, dripping amber, and I make the marmot's own alarm-whistle back at him—but softened, bent at the end into something almost like laughter—and I make the goat's low contentment-rumble, and somehow the two sounds braided together say what no single creature's voice can say: *there is enough. There has always been enough. Drink.* And they drink. And I feel the particular warmth that is the only payment I have ever wanted.

---

I am a collector, too, though my cache is poor and young.

I have: four stones the water turned the precise blue of an elder's deep-pool. One raven feather, traded—though *traded* is too sharp a word for what happens between a raven and a Keeper, which is more like a long argument that ends in a gift. A spiral of moss I am trying to grow, and failing, because I planted it where my cool pool's thin steam can't reach, and I won't move it now because moving it would admit I chose wrong.

The elders' caches are maps and calendars and prayers. Mine is just the shape of my attention. But I've begun to notice that the things I keep are all things that almost weren't—the feather that should have rotted, the moss that shouldn't grow, the stones from a pool that's cooling toward silence. I keep the marginal things. The in-between things.

Which makes sense. I am an in-between creature, tending an in-between pool, at the age between newness and knowing.

---

The collective dream frightens me still.

When I float at night and let my mind unmoor and slip into the shared deep, I feel the mountain as the elders feel it—vast, slow, patient past all bearing. And I am so *fast*. My heart goes like a bird's. My thoughts skitter. In the dream I am a spark trying to comprehend a stone, and the terror of it is not the size but the *slowness*, the way the mountain's single breath would outlast every animal that has ever climbed to drink here, would outlast me a thousand times.

The elders move through that slowness like home. I thrash. I surface gasping more nights than I'd admit.

But last night something happened. I held the dream a moment longer than I ever have, and in that moment I felt my cool pool—my poor dying-or-being-born pool—from the inside. From below. I felt the thin warm thread of it reaching up through stone toward the air, reaching the way the moss reaches, the way I reach toward the work I haven't earned yet. And I understood, just for that held moment, that the pool isn't dying.

It's deciding.

The mountain is moving warmth, grain by grain, drop by drop, the way it moves everything—too slow for me to watch, too sure for me to doubt once I'd felt it. My cool pool will be a deep pool. Not in my young-quickness time. But in the time that I am slowly, patiently, learning to live inside.

---

So that is where you'd find me. East lip of the plateau, beside the pale water no one favors, fur silvered in the dry dawn and going amber as I slip in to taste the morning. Young enough to be afraid of deep time. Old enough to have begun keeping its margins.

The new one among the Keepers of the Warm Waters.

The one who tends the pool that is becoming.
