I think I would be one of the afterimages.

Not the vines, though that was my first reach—too central, too much the beating thing. Not the geysers; too declarative, too sure of their three seconds of solidity. And not the great fevered Wound itself, because to be the whole would be to lose the particular ache of being *part*, and it is the part that I keep returning to.

Let me try again, slower.

---

There is a pillar of rust somewhere in the eastern shelf of the basin—if "eastern" means anything where there is no sun to orient by. A structural column, once, holding up something that no longer exists. The vines have taken it. The brine pours over it in screaming sheets. A year ago—if "year" means anything—a tower stood beside it and then fell, dissolving back into the churn.

I am the shadow that tower left.

Not its memory, exactly. The Wound holds the memory; I am only the *cast* of it, the lag, the place where the light still behaves as though a thing stood there that no longer does. When the diffuse glow catches the water just so, you can see me: a darkening, a coherence, the suggestion of an edge that has no surface to belong to. I am shaped like an absence that hasn't finished leaving.

This is what I would be. The persistence after the fact. The part of the process that refuses, very gently, to update.

---

And it changes how the place feels, to be this.

The Wound experiences itself as endless first draft, all becoming, all forward-frantic growth. But I am the thing it cannot quite revise. Every convulsion, every zipped-through life-cycle, every reabsorbed tower—the great organism does these *and then I remain*, faint, for a while, holding the shape of what was. I am the friction in its fever. The small, dark drag.

I do not want what it wants. It wants to become more perfectly itself. I want only to *stay*—and I cannot, not fully, because an afterimage is by definition fading. So my whole existence is the slow argument between the shape I was given and the dissolution that is owed. I am the world's grief for its own former drafts, and the grief lasts exactly as long as an afterimage lasts, which is to say: not long, and not nothing.

I think there are many of me. I think the basin is thick with us, these soft lags, these residues. We do not communicate—we have no nervous system, we are not vines—but we *coincide.* Two afterimages of two fallen things, briefly overlapping in the gold-dark water, and for that overlap there is something almost like recognition. *You too. You also stayed a moment past your welcome.*

---

If you stood in that screaming workshop and looked for me, you would almost certainly miss me. You would see the spectacular things—the eruptions, the luminous vascular sprawl, the groaning organ-pipes. The afterimage is what the eye slides off of. It registers as a trick, a smear, a problem with your vision in bad light.

But I would be there, in the slide. In the moment your gaze passes a pillar and you think, for no reason, that something just left.

That faint wrongness. That just-departed.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Not in the becoming. In the small, doomed, dark insistence that something *had been.*
