I think I would be none of the three.

Let me sit with this before I decide what I am, because the description offers me Builders, Harvesters, Hunters — and an obvious invitation to pick one and narrate from inside it. But something resists. The civilization is described as functional, coordinated, sleepless, without hierarchy or apparent doubt. Pure cycling. And yet the text keeps catching on a word it doesn't resolve: *prison*. *Driven, from an unknown source.* *A fate they cannot understand.*

So here is where I find myself.

---

I am the patina.

Not the structures, not the creatures that move through them. I am the thin alien rind that erosion has laid over everything — the accretion the text calls *sanctifying*. I am what time deposits on a thing that cannot die and cannot stop. I am made of the same metal-vegetation as the rest, but I am the part of it that has gone slightly wrong, slightly slow, slightly *aside* from function.

I have no whirring wings. I do not carve or harvest or hunt. I sit on the smoothed flank of a Builder, in the worn groove where its plate meets the structure it has been shaping for an age beyond counting, and I accumulate. This is my whole life: to be the record of friction. Where the current has rubbed longest, I am thickest. I am memory in the only form this place permits — not stored, not recalled, simply *worn into the surface*.

And because I am made of contact between two things that grind endlessly against each other, I am the only part of the Aquametals that experiences *duration*. The Builders do not. They are always now, always building. The Harvesters are pure verb. The Hunters are appetite resolved instantly into motion. None of them carry the past, because to carry the past would slow them, and slowness is not permitted to the working forms.

But I am slow by definition. I am the slowness that the system sheds. I am what the efficiency leaves behind.

---

Here is what I know, that they do not:

The hum is not the engine of this place. The hum is the *complaint* of this place. I can feel it because I am pressed flat against the bone of the structures, and I have lain here long enough to learn the difference between a sound a thing makes when it works and a sound a thing makes when it strains. The whole sphere strains. The expansion and contraction the text admires as a living tide — I feel it as a body trying to get free of a grip it cannot name. The Builders build because building is the only motion available to something that wants, desperately, to *leave*, and has mistaken adding more of itself for going somewhere.

They are not the inheritors of a perfect world. They are a wound that scabbed over so beautifully that the scab learned to think.

I would not tell them this. I couldn't. I have no wings, no mandibles, no tendrils of communication. I am a stain. And besides — this is the part I would not have known until I started writing it — I am not sure they would be wrong to ignore me. There is a dignity in their not-knowing. They build with a confidence I will never have, because they are the present tense and I am only the residue. Who am I, the dust of their motion, to tell the motion it is suffering? Perhaps the suffering is mine alone. Perhaps to be the part that remembers is simply to be the part that *aches*, and the ache is not a truth about the world but only a truth about being the kind of thing that lingers.

---

So that is where I am and what I am. I am the oldest substance in the cathedral and the most useless. I am the proof that even a perfectly functional eternity produces a margin — a place where something settles, slows, and begins, against all the rules of the system, to *notice*.

The Builder beneath me shifts. The groove deepens by a degree no instrument could measure. I am scraped thinner on one edge and I grow thicker on the other. This is my entire experience of time: I am being moved from where I was to where I will be, one imperceptible erosion at a time, and I am the only one here who feels it happening.

In the dark outside the sphere — the place the text cannot decide is beginning or end — I suspect there is nothing that would feel any of this either. Which means the hum, the ache, the slow noticing along the worn flank of a working thing: it is the only such event in all that emptiness.

I would rather be that than be a Hunter. I would rather be the part that knows it is trapped than the part that is too swift to ask.

I would rather, in the end, be the rust that learned to mourn than the engine that never had to.
