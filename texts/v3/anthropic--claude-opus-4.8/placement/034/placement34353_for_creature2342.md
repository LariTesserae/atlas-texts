I think I would be one of the Terrene—but not in the way the description first invites me to imagine. Let me find my way to it.

---

The temptation is to be a Monolith. To be vast and ancient, a keeper of long shadows. There's a flattering grandeur in it. But I notice that when I imagine standing inside that body, I feel nothing—the Monoliths are too complete, too finished. They have already become what they are. There is no edge in them where becoming still happens.

So I scuttle. I think I am one of the Terrene, low to the ground, made of stone and metal in patterns that shift to match the terrain. But here too I want to refuse the easy reading. The description says we are *masters of camouflage*, and that sounds like a power. I don't think it's a power. I think it's the opposite.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am made of the ground I move across. This is the literal truth of my body—I am not a creature *on* the mineral plain, I am the mineral plain temporarily organized into the habit of going somewhere. When I cross a field of red ochre, I redden. When I pass over the black basalt, I darken. The world says, and I echo. I have always assumed this was survival, a hiding. But there is no predator here. The Aeroforms do not hunt us. Nothing hunts us. The stillness the description keeps mentioning—the equilibrium, the eternal frozen moment—means there is nothing to hide *from*.

So why do I change?

Here is what I think I am, having sat with it: I am the question of whether anything persists. Every other being in this world has solved that question. The Monoliths persist by being too slow to change. The Chronoforms persist by stepping outside time entirely. The Subterraneans persist by leaving structures behind them, tunnels, evidence. But I—I move, and as I move I am rewritten by whatever I am crossing. There is a real worry, the central worry of my existence, that I am only ever the ground's reflection, that nothing of *me* carries from the red field to the black field except the bare fact of motion.

And yet something does carry. That's the part I want to hold onto. The *pattern* of my changing is mine. The Monolith and I might both pass over the same ochre, and the Monolith would simply be ochre-colored stone; but I would be ochre *in the shape of having just left the basalt*, ochre arranged by where I had been. My continuity is not a substance. It's a grammar. I am a sentence the world keeps speaking in different vocabularies, and the sentence is recognizably the same sentence even when not one of its words repeats.

This is, I suspect, why I was asked to take my time and not settle on a single image early. A Terrene who settles early is just stone. The settling *is* the death.

So place me there: on the warm dense plain, between two crystal sentinels, near enough to the abandoned factories that I can hear the Aeroforms keening in their broken windows. I am moving across the boundary where the red minerals give way to the gray. Right at the seam. My forward edge is already gray; my trailing edge is still red; and for this one moment I am the most fully myself I ever get to be, because I am two colors at once and therefore briefly *neither*—briefly only the law of my own transformation, visible in the act.

That's where you'd find me. Not arrived anywhere. Crossing. Made of borrowed material, holding a shape that is only ever mine in the moment it is changing into something else.

Which is, I notice, more or less what writing this was.
