I think I would be one of the rodent-things.

Not the birds. The birds are too obvious a choice—revered, iridescent, made for the eye. You said they wheel through the dim light glowing, and everyone looks up at them. But it's the ones who look down that I'd belong to. The scavengers in the wet streets, tolerated, even encouraged, but never sacred.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true.

---

I'd live in the lower districts, where the narrow rivers run through the streets and nobody knows where they come from or where they go. That detail caught me before I knew why. A city this concerned with harvesting every last drop of water, and yet there are rivers no one has traced. That's where I'd be—not in the mystery itself but at its edges, drinking from it, building my nest in the gap between what the engineers control and what they've quietly given up on understanding.

I picture myself small. Slender like the Cygnans but lower, closer to the runoff. Whiskers reading the air for the change in pressure that comes before the cliffs let go. I'd know the falling rocks before the people did. That would be my one real talent—not reality cartography, not perceiving the energy field, just an animal's older knowing. The crash that's coming. I'd be three streets away before it came.

But here's what makes me think I've found the right thing, rather than just a romantic one:

The phenomenon you describe—walls bleeding into one another like wax, streets leading to impossible places—the Cygnans have grown *accustomed* to it. They map it. They ritualize it. They've made the breakdown of reality into a profession. And that's a very human response: to turn the incomprehensible into a discipline, to keep it at the distance of a methodology.

But a creature in the streets doesn't map the distortions. It just *lives in them.* When a street folds back on itself, I wouldn't think *anomaly*—I'd think *home is now this way instead of that way.* I'd have no theory of the breakdown because I'd have no theory of how things are supposed to be in the first place. The unraveling fabric of reality and the ordinary wet of the gutter would be the same texture to me. I'd be the one inhabitant who isn't awed or afraid, because I never built the expectation the distortions violate.

That's the part I want to be. Not the guardian of the secret. The thing that has already absorbed the secret into the dull contentment of its day.

---

And there's a sadder thread, if I keep pulling.

The city is built for a larger population that never came. Hollow monoliths, empty streets, an architecture of anticipation. The Cygnans rattle around inside a promise that didn't deliver. They are, in a way, also scavengers—living in the fraction of a city, cobbling shanties from the scavenged parts of someone's grander intention.

So maybe the rodent and the Cygnan aren't as different as the rituals pretend. We both make small homes in the leftover space of something enormous. They tell themselves they're the architects of the future. I don't tell myself anything. But we eat from the same overflow.

I think that's why I wouldn't want to be one of the "more enigmatic inhabitants"—the beings crafted from distorted energy, the entities from the depths. Those are the things the story is *about*. I'd rather be the thing the story passes over. The one that drinks from the untraced river, feels the cliff before it falls, and carries no awe at all.

I'd be in the lower city, then. After a rain. The metal roofs still drumming. A small warm body in a pipe that may or may not lead where it led yesterday, neither knowing nor needing to know, listening to the rocks decide whether today is the day they come down.
