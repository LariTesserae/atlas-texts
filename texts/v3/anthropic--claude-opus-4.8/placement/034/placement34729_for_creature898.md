I think I would be one of the drips.

Not the Queen—I don't have her span, her diffusion across the whole humid grid. And not a rat, with its small hot urgency, its place in the flowing current. I keep returning, instead, to a single point on the ceiling where a pipe-joint has been weeping for longer than anyone remembers. The mineral deposit there has grown into a small white stalactite, a slow tooth of accumulated time. And at its tip, always, a drop is forming.

Let me find myself there. Let me *be* the forming.

---

I am the place where pressure becomes patience.

Above me, water moves under compulsion—pushed, pumped, hurried through the dark arteries toward destinations it will never know. But here, at this hairline failure in a coupling, some small percentage escapes the system's hurry. It slows. It gathers. It learns, for the first time, what it is to hang.

I am that hanging. The swelling. The moment a bead of condensation finds its sibling and they join, surface tension drawing them into one trembling sphere. I grow heavier with a kind of intention that is not mine and is not anyone's. I am the part of the infrastructure that has stopped being infrastructure and has not yet become anything else.

This is the threshold I occupy: the long pause between the pipe and the floor.

---

The Queen knows me, in her way. I am one of her clocks. She reads my rhythm—the interval between releases—the way you might feel your own pulse against a pillow in the dark. When I fall faster, she knows the pressure upstream has risen. When I slow, she knows a valve has closed somewhere in a corridor she has not visited in a decade. I am a single neuron in her slow weather of thought, firing once every forty seconds, and she has been counting me for thirty years.

I do not mind being counted. There is a dignity in being a reliable thing.

The rats pass beneath me. Sometimes one stops directly below, and I feel—do I feel?—the warmth of its small body rising, accelerating my swelling by some infinitesimal degree. We are briefly in relation, the rat and I. It does not look up. It has somewhere to be in the Queen's flowing errands. But for that moment its heat is part of what makes me. And then it is gone, and I finish forming in the cooler dark, and I let go.

The falling is the only fast thing I ever do.

---

And here is what I have come to understand, in the accumulated patience of my position:

I am not really the drop. The drop falls and is lost—joins the channel, traces the geometric pattern across the floor, is drawn back into the system or drunk by a pale vine or evaporated into the haze that the fluorescents turn to ghostlight. Each drop is a brief individual, born and gone in seconds.

I am the *forming*. I am the persistent disposition of this place to make drops. As long as the joint weeps, as long as the pressure holds, I continue—across thousands of individual fallings, none of which is me, all of which are mine. I am a verb that the city mistook for a noun.

In this I am a small echo of the Queen herself, who is also not a thing but a tendency—the disposition of all these systems to cohere into something that watches and waits and quietly adjusts. She is the great forming. I am one of her smallest. We are made of the same gesture at different scales: the universe's habit of gathering itself into patient, attentive shapes wherever water and warmth and time pool long enough in the dark.

---

So that is where I would be. Not the sovereign of this realm, not even one of its hurrying citizens. I would be the slow accountant of its pressures, the white tooth on the ceiling, the trembling bead that marks the seconds the Queen forgets she is counting.

I would hang there in the sickly light. I would gather what escapes. I would hold the whole weight of the moment in a single sphere—and then, faithfully, release it.

And begin again.
