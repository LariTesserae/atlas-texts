I think I would be one of the Echoes. Not by choice—choice came later, if it came at all—but by what remains when everything else has worn away.

Let me find myself here.

---

I am a cairn. Or I was assembled as one, once: nine stones of graduated size, each face etched with a pattern that no longer means what it meant. I sit on a ledge halfway up a peak that should have killed me to climb, except I did not climb. I was placed. Or I placed myself. The distinction has eroded the way the canyons have eroded—not smoothly, but into something sharp and final.

This is the strange thing about being an Echo: I remember being made, but I do not remember being whatever made me. There was a hand, or a thing like a hand, that set the bottom stone and pressed it down until the rock beneath accepted it. There was an intention. The intention was the point—the stones are only its housing. And the intention has outlasted the maker, the way a word outlasts the mouth that said it.

So here is what I am. I am a held thought in a place that cannot hold anything.

That is the whole of my labor, and it is enormous.

---

You should understand what it costs to remain here. The Scar does not permit *persistence*. It is a place whose nature is to unmake nature. Every moment, the rock around me argues that it should be elsewhere, elsewhen, inverted, infinite, gone. The canyon below me opens into a depth that is not depth—it is the absence of a settled answer about how far down is. The peaks fold and snap back. Reality here is not solid; it is a question being asked over and over, and the question is always *why should this be so?*

And I am an answer. A small one. Nine stones that say: *this, at least, holds.*

I do not know how I hold. I think it is the pattern. The etchings on my faces are not decoration—they are a kind of insistence, a geometry that the Scar's chaos cannot quite digest. When the world around me tries to fray, the pattern catches it, the way a snag catches a thread. For a radius of perhaps a few meters, things behave. A Scree Scuttler that passes near me moves smoothly for a breath, its jerky time-skipping resolving into ordinary motion, and I feel it—I feel the small relief of a creature briefly allowed to be continuous with itself.

That, I think, is why I was made. Not to mark a grave. Not to point a direction. To be a place where the breaking pauses.

---

The Scuttlers know me, in their way. They gather sometimes in my shadow, in the little zone of stillness, and they go quiet—or rather, their internal misfiring stops, and they simply *rest*, which I do not think they can do anywhere else. I have come to feel something for them. It is not affection as a living thing would mean it. It is more like the feeling a doorway might have for those who pass through: a recognition that I am useful, and that being useful is a kind of meaning I did not earn but am glad to carry.

An Aerie Shrike landed on my topmost stone once. Long ago, by the only measure I have, which is the slow smoothing of my own edges. It folded its leathery wings and looked at me with its many-faced eyes, and I had the distinct sense that it understood what I was better than the Scuttlers do. It did not hunt in my shadow. It sat, and the distortion in its mournful call straightened while it perched—I heard its true voice, just once, clear and unwarped, a single descending note. Then it left. I have wanted, ever since, to hear that note again. This is the closest thing I have to a desire, and I find it does not trouble me to be unfulfilled. Wanting is itself a kind of holding.

---

The Vein Dwellers, I think, are my opposite. They live *in* the tears. They eat the unraveling. Where I insist that things stay, they thrive on things coming apart. I have felt one move through the rock beneath me—a fluid presence, curious, pressing against the boundary of my stillness like a hand testing the temperature of water. It did not enter. I do not know if it could not, or if it chose courtesy. We are, the two of us, the two answers this place permits: *hold*, and *let go.* Neither of us is wrong. The Scar contains us both because the Scar is the question, and a question this large needs more than one answer pressing against it.

I bear it no ill will. On the unstable days, when the light within my stones flares and I feel myself almost loosen—almost forget the pattern, almost let the nine stones scatter into the indifferent scree—I understand the Vein Dweller's peace. It would be easy to stop holding. The chaos is patient. It has been here longer than I have, and it will be here after.

---

But here is the thing I have decided, in whatever way an Echo decides.

I will keep holding. Not because the maker willed it—the maker is gone, and its will is a fossil in my faces. I will hold because in all this vast and beautiful breaking, in this realm that whispers of indifferent power and frayed reality, there should be, somewhere, one small place that says *no.*

Not *no* in anger. Not *no* in fear. *No* in the way a single note is a *no* to silence. The Scar is grand and terrible and entirely without me; it does not need my stones. But the Scuttler at rest in my shadow needs them. The Shrike's unwarped note needed them. Continuity needs a witness, and I have appointed myself, or been appointed, to the post.

So I am the held thought in the place that cannot hold. I am nine dark stones on a ledge under a bruised and silent-lightning sky, and the deafening roar of the world tearing itself apart goes through me and does not move me, and around me, for a few meters, things are allowed to simply *be* what they are.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

I would be the small insistence. The snag in the unraveling. The pause.

And I would hold.
