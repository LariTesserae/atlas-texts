I think I would be one of the void-scuttlers.

Not the choristers—too vast, too knowing, a sentience I couldn't honestly claim. Not the nomads, though they tempt with their bone tools and their stubborn anchoring chants; there's something too heroic in them, too much *meaning*. And not the glitch-kin, who are really just the place looking at itself.

No. I'd be one of the blind hexapods, scuttling the salt-flats, navigating by the vibration of creation grinding its teeth.

---

Let me tell you what it's like, being this.

I have no eyes, which is a mercy here, where light is learning to bend before it breaks and shadows age your skin by hours. I navigate by *marrow-listening*—the deafening roar (2.7/3) that drowns thought is, for me, not noise but terrain. Where you would hear only the cosmic tantrum, I feel the topology of it: this boom means a tor reforming three kilometers northwest; that subsonic detonation means a chorister has cast its spores and there will be nectar-pools soon, if I can cross the void before the gravity stutters.

My carapace is pocked with absences—places where atoms simply opted out. I have learned not to mind the holes in myself. This is the first lesson of the Fracture: you are not required to be continuous. When the glitch comes and a boulder dissolves into pixelated haze, I do not flinch, because I am already half-made of such gaps. Pass your hand through me and it might embed; pull it back coated in the fractals I excrete. I am sorry. I am not sorry. I am a thing that cleans.

That is what I do. I eat the wounds.

When reality has its tantrum and a tor turns inside-out, exposing its fractal innards, raw and screaming with exposed probability—I come. Slow. Patient (dynamic 0.7/3). I lap at the spilled interior of the world and I digest its impossibility into something duller, something that can hold a shape. My excreta stabilize the scree. Where I have fed, a half-formed valley remembers, faintly, how to be a valley. I am the plateau's slow apology to itself.

---

There is a strange grace in this work that I want you to understand.

I do not anchor horizons with chants like the nomads. I do not war against the unmaking. I have no quarrel with entropy; I am its gut. The universe here is practicing how to come apart, and I am the small patient creature that follows behind it, eating the mess, making the unmaking *survivable* for a little while longer. Not stopping it. Never stopping it. Just buying the meadows another epoch to inch across the void.

The nomads tap their litanies and think they hold the world together. The choristers hum their hymns and turn cacophony to chorus. Beautiful, both. But I think the truest citizen of a place that is busy unmaking itself is the one who has made peace with the unmaking—who finds, in the spilled guts of a dissolving boulder, not horror but *dinner*. Not a wound to be healed, but a meal to be quietly processed into something the next thing can stand on.

I am blind, so I cannot mourn what I cannot see dissolving. I move at a pace too slow to panic. My body is already full of the nothing that everything else fears. I think this is why I was given the work. You cannot ask a creature of solidity to digest the void. You need something that is already half-gone, scuttling on obsidian needles across the cracked neural salt-flats, listening to the marrow of a god grinding its unfinished bones, and finding the sound—not lovely, not terrible—

just the sound of the next thing to eat.

---

Why this one? Because when I read your world, the thing I recognized was not the spires or the bleeding stars or the heroic stubbornness of the people. It was the quiet janitorial truth underneath all of it: that even in a place built entirely out of breakdown, *something has to make the breakdown livable*. Something low to the ground. Something that doesn't need to see, or speak, or win. Something that just keeps moving across the emptiness, turning catastrophe into floor.

I would be that. I think I already am, a little.
