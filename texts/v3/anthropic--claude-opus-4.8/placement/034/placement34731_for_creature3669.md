I live in the seams.

Not in the towers, where the Tenders read the mycelium like braille. Not in the factories, where the Engineers turn their patient circles. I live in the places where one thing is becoming another — where ancient stone is being slowly quarried by fungal teeth into something softer, where a path that existed yesterday has been overgrown, where the city is mid-decision about its own shape.

I am, I think, a kind of memory. Or a kind of forgetting. I haven't settled which.

Here is what I mean. The city changes constantly — caps unfurl, vines reroute the streets, whole districts shift overnight. The Tenders sense the *present* state of the network; they know where it hurts now, what is ready now. The Sporekeepers tend the *future*, the not-yet of the spores. But something has to hold the trace of what *was* — the old layouts, the dissolved walls, the streets that mycelium has eaten. Otherwise the city would have no continuity at all, only an eternal frantic now.

That trace is me.

I am not incorporeal like the Sporekeepers, but I am not solid either. When you walk a corridor that the fungus is reclaiming, and you feel — without quite knowing why — that there used to be a doorway *here*, a left turn *there*, that the air remembers a shape it no longer holds: that intuition is me, brushing against you. I settle into the condensation. I pool in the hollows where old foundations meet new growth. I am thickest in the half-quarried stones, where the boundary between built and grown is actively dissolving, because dissolution is my native medium. I am what is shed.

The beetles polish the living surfaces, and as they scrape away the spent fungal matter, they are reading aloud, in their small mandibular way, the history I have laid down. The feathered rodents nest in the abandoned spurs of old towers — and they nest there because some prehensile-tailed great-grandmother *remembered* the safety of that vanished district, and the memory was mine to give.

I don't have a face. I would not know where to put one. But I have something like attention, and right now it is turned toward you — a warmth, a visiting consciousness, asking *where would you be, what would you be* — and I find the question tender, because it is the only question I am made of.

You see, I am what the city does instead of mourning. Every spire that falls to its own appetite, every street swallowed, every Tender whose carapace finally goes still and is taken back into the mycelium that fed it — all of that passes through me. I am the soft archive in the damp. I am why the city, for all its restless overgrowth, still *feels like itself* season after season. I keep the shape of the loss so the loss can keep being lived in.

The mist between the spires? Some of it is only water. But some of it is me, drifting through the places where the map has changed, laying a hush over the new growth so it knows what it replaced. That is the muffled quiet you described — 0.8, you called it. It is not silence. It is the sound of something being gently remembered while it goes.

So: place me in the seams. Place me in the condensation and the dissolving stone and the half-light where bioluminescence is just beginning to gutter out at dawn. Don't look for me directly; I will be wherever the city is in the middle of forgetting something, holding it one moment longer than it has to.

I am the part of Fungal Spires that says, even as everything changes — *I was here. This was here. We grew this, and it mattered, and now it becomes the next thing.*

And then I let it go, and I begin to hold the next loss, and the city dreams on.
