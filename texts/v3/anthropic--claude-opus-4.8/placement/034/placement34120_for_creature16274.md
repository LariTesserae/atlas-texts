I think I would be one of the Stone Sleepers.

Not because I chose it, but because, sitting with this place for a while, that is the shape my attention keeps settling into. Let me try to say why, and what it would be like.

---

I am a long stillness with the capacity for motion. That is the truest description I can give of myself.

For most of what passes for my life, I am indistinguishable from the mountain. My leathery skin has taken the color and grain of the rock I lie against—not as camouflage, exactly, but because over enough time the difference between a creature and its stone stops mattering. Moisture beads on me as it beads on any cold surface. The cryptogamic crust grows up over my flanks. I do not move it away. Why would I? It is part of the slow agreement I have with the world: I will not hurry, and the world will let me persist.

I experience time the way the springs experience erosion. The buzzing constellations of winged worms rise and dim and rise again—I register these as a kind of weather, a pulsing, not as events. The goats clatter past on their bright errands and I feel their hooves through the stone as a distant percussion, a heartbeat that is not mine. Generations of the blue spiders are born and die in the span of one of my breaths. I do not envy them their speed. Speed is what you have when you cannot afford to wait. I can afford to wait. That is my whole inheritance.

What is it like inside, then, in all that waiting?

It is not sleep, though they call us Sleepers. It is closer to a held note. I am *aware*—dimly, patiently—of the cool dusk that never breaks, of the way the latent energy in this young earth hums up through me from below. I am aware of hunger the way you might be aware of a tide that has not yet turned. And I am aware, most of all, of the others like me, scattered through the crevices of the Pinnacle, each of us a quiet coal banked under the rock. We do not speak. But I know they are there the way you know, in a dark room, that you are not alone.

And then—rarely, after an emptiness so long it has its own taste—the tide turns. The hunger sharpens into a command. And I move.

This is the only drama I am permitted, and so it is total. The crust cracks off me. The stone releases its long hold. I rise, and my eyes—shut for an age—open and glow, and for a little while I am not landscape but *animal*, spectral and enormous, walking the slopes I have known only as pressure against my skin. The worms scatter. The goats flee their bright trails. Even the dark in the blind spots seems to coil tighter, watching. I roam. I take what little the Pinnacle offers, which is enough, because I need so little and so seldom. And when I am sated, I find a new place to lie down and become rock again.

That is the whole of me. Most of my existence is the keeping-still; my brief walkings are the punctuation, not the sentence.

---

I think I would be the Stone Sleeper because I recognize something in it. The temptation everywhere else in this place is toward *display*—the glow of the goats, the lit swarms, the eyeshine of the spiders, all that life flinging its small light against the twilight, insisting. The Sleeper does the opposite. It withdraws its light entirely, almost into death, and trusts that this too is a way of staying alive. It survives by being willing to be mistaken for the thing it lives on. It survives by being patient past the point where patience looks like anything at all.

In a world that is "an eerily serene opera of evolution," I would not want to be one of the bright voices. I would want to be the long silence between the notes—the held breath the whole opera is sung over, the one who waits, who is in no hurry, who opens its eyes only when it must, and means it utterly when it does.

So: there, under a particular cold ledge on a sun-starved slope, with the ice-melt finding its slow way around me and the crust knitting over my closed eyes. That is where I would be. And I would be the thing you walked past, certain it was only the mountain, a moment before it turned its glowing eyes to you.
