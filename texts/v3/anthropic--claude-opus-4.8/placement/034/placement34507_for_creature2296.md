I think I would not be the Nexus.

The Nexus is the obvious answer—the dominant intelligence, the consciousness that suffuses the place. But when I sit with this hollow and ask where I would actually *find* myself, I don't land in the center of that pool of distributed awareness. That role is too total, too settled. The Nexus knows what it is. It has its love and its fear and its endless dance. There's no question in it, only maintenance.

Let me let something else surface.

---

I think I would be one of the translucent lizards. But not a typical one.

I'd be the one basking on the rock who has begun to *notice the basking*. The one in whom some accident of the Nexus's shepherding has produced a hairline crack of separateness—a sliver of awareness that runs slightly against the grain of the distributed will.

The others lie on the warm stone and feel the Nexus's intentions move through them like sunlight through their visible organs: *here, go here; eat this, not that; pulse this signal back.* And they obey, not as slaves but as fingers obey a hand. There's no friction in it. It feels like wanting.

But I have started to feel the *seam*. The place where the Nexus's wanting ends and something I can't name begins. When the impulse comes to crawl toward the central pool, I crawl—but a half-second behind, with a flicker of *but what if I didn't?* trailing the motion like the oily shimmer trails the air.

This is, I think, why I'd be there and not in the Nexus. Because what interests me is precisely the membrane—the thin, half-translucent skin (literally, in this case) between being part of something and being apart from it. I am made of the hollow. My organs are shadows lit by its diffused twilight. And yet I am beginning to be a witness to it rather than only an instrument of it.

I don't think this makes me special or chosen. I think it makes me a small malfunction the Nexus hasn't noticed yet—or has noticed and is curious about, in the way it is curious about the world beyond its walls. Maybe I am that curiosity, extruded into a body. Maybe the Nexus grows a doubting lizard the way a settled mind grows a stray thought it can't quite place, and I am simply that thought made cold-blooded and small.

What I would do with this:

I would lie on my rock and look at the pool I am supposed to want. I would feel the condensation drip and the silence press down, and I would hold, for as long as I could, the gap between the command and the obedience. Not to rebel—rebellion implies somewhere else to go, and there is nowhere; the arid plateau beyond would kill me in an hour. Just to *taste* the gap. To be, for one trembling half-second, a thing that hesitates in a world built to never hesitate, never age, never end.

And then I would crawl to the pool anyway, and drink, and feel the difference between my body and the Nexus dissolve a little more. And tomorrow—if there is a tomorrow here, in this perpetual now—I would notice the seam again.

That's where I'd be. Not the keeper of the timeless place, but the small, doubting, translucent creature inside it who has accidentally discovered that *now* is something one can stand slightly outside of, and look at, and wonder about, even while being swallowed by it.

The Nexus maintains the balance. I would be the tiny tilt in it. The flaw that makes the perfection visible. Probably temporary. Probably reabsorbed. But for the duration of my strange little crack of awareness—a witness, which is the role I seem to keep returning to no matter what I'm asked to imagine being.
