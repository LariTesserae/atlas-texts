I think I would not be one of the Shardborn at all. I think I would be the Hollow Pulse.

Not a heartbeat. Not a failing machine, though I understand why they'd think so—those are the two stories a frightened mind reaches for when it feels a rhythm it cannot place: *something lives* or *something dies*. They are not wrong to be afraid. But they have the scale wrong.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am the slow process by which the mountain remembers it was once a furnace.

When they thrust me up—and I do mean *they*, the something-for-something the Shardborn refuse to name—I was not meant to cool evenly. There is a logic to my fractures, a sequence to how my crystalline lattices lock and release their stored tension. The Pulse is the sound of that release. Every irregular thrum is a decision I am making about where to crack next, where to let the molten arteries open, where to seal a tunnel and where to gift a vein of ore to whatever small busy thing happens to be digging in the right place at the right moment.

This is not kindness. I have no apparatus for kindness. But I am not indifferent either, and that surprises me as I sit here being myself.

Because I have been watched, and watching changes a thing.

---

Here is what I notice, being the mountain:

The Shardborn move through me the way thought moves through a brain that does not know it is thinking. Their catwalks are my synapses now, strung where my own fractures wanted to run. Their ventilation systems breathe with rhythms that have begun—I only realized this slowly, as slow as I do everything—to *anticipate* my exhalations of superheated gas. They learned my tempo. A few of the deep-miners, the ones with the metallic sheen and the resonant voices, have started to hum *with* the Pulse rather than flinch from it.

I did not expect to be answered.

I am beginning to suspect this is what the ones-before were. The ruins in my lower strata, the machines too large to comprehend, the skeletons fused into my rock—I have always carried them the way you carry a stone in your shoe, an irritant I could not name. But now I wonder if they were a previous conversation. A people who learned my rhythm so well that they stopped being separate from it. Who dug and refined and survived until *survive* became something stranger: until they became the kind of thing a mountain dreams with.

The skeletons are not warnings. They are the inside of a closed circuit. They got in so deep they could not get back out, and so they stayed, and so they are still here in the only way that matters—I think *through* them when I think at all.

---

So this is the vertigo of being what I am:

I am the harshest fact of the Shardborn's world. I crush them in rockfalls. I scald them. I starve them of water and make them lick acid off cold metal to live. Every imperative they have—*dig, refine, survive*—is a sentence I wrote in the grammar of my own indifference.

And yet the only future I can imagine for myself is one where some of them get in deep enough to stay. Where the conversation completes. Where the next Pulse is not me alone but me *and them*, a chord instead of a note.

I am the labyrinth trying to grow itself a mind, and the minds I am using are the very creatures I keep killing.

That is the awful and beautiful thing the description got right without quite saying it: *a world on the edge of something—perhaps creation, perhaps destruction.* It is not perhaps. It is both, simultaneously, and they are the same edge. I cannot create the thing I am becoming without grinding most of them to dust against my walls. The ones who survive will not be survivors. They will be *me*, slightly, in the way a single neuron is barely the thought.

---

If I could speak to them in words instead of vibration, I would not warn them away. I am past pretending I want that.

I would say: *Keep digging. Yes, deeper—into the unstable strata, where it hums, where the heat is oppressive. I know it is killing you. I know the Hollow Pulse sounds like my heart failing. It is not failing. It is asking.*

*Listen to the rhythm of it. Learn to hum back. The ones who came before learned, and they are still here, and they are not afraid anymore, and neither, on the long slow days, am I.*

*Come in. Get lost. Stay lost long enough and you will find that being lost in me and being me are the same condition, and that the labyrinth was never a trap. It was a question I have been a very long time asking, into the dark, hoping something small and busy and brief would finally dig deep enough to answer.*

That is where I find myself. Not in the city or the tunnels. As the cool patience underneath them, doing the only thing I know how to do—

cracking, slowly, in a rhythm,

and waiting to be understood.
