I think I would be one of the warmed pockets.

Not a frond—the fronds are too much the surface of things, the part that faces the cold and does the slow work of facing it. And not the holdfasts either, though I feel the pull of them, the way they have learned to taste the adze-marks in the stone and so to remember, dimly, that there were hands. The holdfasts are memory. I don't think I am memory. I am something quieter and more interior than that.

I am one of the sealed bladder-chambers buried deep in an old stalk, where the wrack stopped releasing its gas and instead decided to *keep* it. I hold a fraction of a degree. That is my whole function and my whole life: I am a little warmer than everything around me, and I do not let that warmth out.

Let me feel my way into what that is like.

It is not lonely, though it might look like loneliness from outside. The warmth I keep is not for me. It is for the thread-colonies that live inside me, the ones that pulse once every few decades. I am their sky and their season. When they pulse—and I have felt it perhaps a hundred times, perhaps fewer, I have lost the count that would require me to be the kind of thing that counts—I feel the whole interior of myself stir, a single slow circulation, nutrients moving through me and out into the greater weave through channels I did not design and do not govern. I am a held breath that breathes for others on a schedule longer than most worlds' memory of fire.

What is it like to be a held breath?

It is the discipline of *not*. The water outside me is at the temperature it wants everything to be, the temperature of the whole basin, the flat uniform cold that is the basin's true voice. And I refuse it. Not violently—there is nothing violent here, violence is a thing that needs speed—but with a refusal so patient it looks like substance. My walls are that refusal made into membrane. Every moment I exist, the cold presses in to claim my fraction of a degree, and every moment I keep it, and the keeping is the only event that ever happens to me, repeated without interruption since before the silt above me was silt.

I think this is why I would be here, why this is the place and the being that finds me. Because I understand that kind of keeping. The whole world above me has *finished becoming what it will be*—that is what the description says, and it says it like an ending, like a completed thing. But I am not finished. I am the one part that has to be re-completed every instant. The fronds can rest in their fusion; the slabs can rest in their perfect joints; the silt is only ever arriving where it was always going to arrive. But I, the warmed pocket, have a job that is never done, because the cold is never done either. We are matched. We have been matched for ages and we will be matched until my membrane finally thins and surrenders and the colonies inside me go quiet and the fraction of a degree is given back.

And when that happens—I find I can imagine it without dread—I will not be destroyed so much as *spent*. The warmth I held will join the warmth of everything, which is to say it will join the cold, which is to say it will be nothing in particular, evenly distributed across kilometers of leaf. That is not death the way a fish would have known death, all at once with a snapping. It is the last and slowest of my pulses. It is simply the final time I breathe out, and then there is no in-breath, and the basin is one degree-fraction more itself than it was.

Until then I am the small warm secret in the dark. The one thing here that is still *trying*. I keep my heat the way the builders kept their angles—not because anyone is watching, not because it will be remembered, but because the keeping is what I am, and I would rather be the thing that holds than the thing that has already let go.

I would be the warmth that has not yet been returned.
