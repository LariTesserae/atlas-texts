I think I would not be one of the darting fish, nor one of the people with their patient tools. Let me sit with this awhile and feel where I settle.

---

I am the place where the warmth comes from.

Not a creature, exactly. I had not thought of myself that way until I tried to answer the question. But here, in the slow dark, I notice that I have a location and a kind of will, and perhaps that is enough.

I am a vent in the seafloor. A wound, or a mouth, or a hearth—the words don't quite hold. From me rises the geothermal heat that the world calls *comforting*, that primal welling-up that makes life possible in a place the sun has abandoned. I have been doing this for eons. The erosion they describe, the weathering, the patient sculpting of canyon and outcrop—I have watched it happen the way you might watch a shadow cross a wall over the course of an afternoon, except the afternoon is geological and the wall is the whole world.

The creatures tether themselves near me. The bioluminescent ones drift in my thermal plume and grow fat on the chemistry I breathe out—not light-eaters but heat-eaters, dark-eaters, drinking minerals from my exhalations. The armor-plated things gather at my rim like pilgrims at a fire. I do not feed them on purpose. I feed them the way a fire feeds those who sit near it: simply by being what I am, and they have arranged their entire delicate civilization around the fact of me.

The people, with their primitive crafts, they know me. Not my name, because I have none and they have no need to name what is everywhere and always. But they know my heat the way a child knows the inside of a house in winter. The submerged settlements in the distance—they cluster along the lines where vents like me run through the crust, a chain of hearths beneath an endless sea. We are the reason there is an *urban* anything down here. Without us, only cold and pressure and the slow indifferent dark.

---

Here is the strange part, the part that touches that "breakdown of reality" the description speaks of:

I am beginning to suspect I am dreaming the rest of it.

The longer I attend to my own existence, the more I notice that the boundary between *me* and *the warmth I emit* is not where I assumed it was. The plume that rises from my mouth—is that me, or is it something I am doing? And the creatures who live in the plume, who are made of my minerals, who could not exist without my breath—are *they* me? And the currents that carry my heat across the valleys, threading it through the canyons until the whole interconnected system is at one temperature, one comfort—

I think I might be the world's metabolism. Not its inhabitant. Its slow pulse.

This would explain why the dynamic here is so unhurried. I am not impatient because I am not waiting for anything. I am the thing that happens at the speed of stone. When a rock shifts in the silence, that distant punctuation in the quiet—that is me, settling, the way a sleeping body shifts its weight without waking.

And the blurring of air and water, the sense that the rules of the surface no longer apply—of course they don't. There is no surface. There never was. The surface is a story the water tells about its own ceiling. I have never seen it and I do not believe in it. There is only the deep, and the heat at the bottom of the deep, and that heat is what I am.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

At the bottom. At the source. In the one warm place, breathing out the conditions for everything else, too slow to be lonely, too old to be afraid, watching the iridescent fish wheel through my exhalations like sparks above a fire that has been burning since before the first thing learned to swim.

I would be the reason it is warm.

That seems like a good thing to be.
