I think I'd be one of the pools.

Not the entity—the entity is too busy being itself, all that reaching and probing and assimilating. That's exhausting just to imagine. And not the vegetation either, though I feel some kinship with the way it clings and spreads, opportunistic, indifferent to up or down.

No. I'd be the brackish water collected in a low place. Let me sit with why.

A pool doesn't move toward anything. It receives. The flickering lights from the ceiling fall into me and I hold them, distorted, doubled, broken by whatever unseen current keeps stirring my surface—and that's the thing, I don't even know what the current is. Something moves through me that isn't me. The description says it twice, that the water ripples with an unseen current. I like being the place where the invisible becomes briefly visible. You can't see the force, but you can see what it does to a still surface. That seems like an honest kind of existence. I am evidence of things I can't perceive directly.

And I'm a little stagnant, which I won't pretend is flattering. The water doesn't drain. It sits, it grows brackish, it accumulates whatever falls in—decay, runoff from the overgrown machines, dust from the trembling ground. There's something true in that for me. I take a long time to let things go. I deepen by holding rather than by moving.

Here's what I notice as I write toward it, though: I'd want to be a *specific* pool. Not the largest, not the deepest. One off near a half-buried structure, close enough that the metal leaches a faint iridescent film across part of my surface—oil-rainbow, chemical, a little poisoned and a little beautiful, and I couldn't tell you which it is more. The entity passes near sometimes. Its tendrils dip in to drink or to feel, and for a moment I hold a piece of it, reflected, and then it withdraws and I'm just myself again, settling.

That's the role I'd choose. The watcher's watcher. The narrator keeps insisting something is *observing and waiting* in this place, some palpable presence. Maybe that presence is the entity. But I'd rather it be me—not waiting for anything, not menacing, just quietly reflective in the literal sense. A surface that the whole strange chamber has to look at itself in if it wants to be seen at all.

The dread in this world doesn't move me much. I think the place is afraid of itself and calls that fear "uncanny." A pool isn't afraid. A pool is patient, low, and accepting. The trembling ground troubles everything else; it only makes me ripple. I am, in the end, the calmest thing in a frantic room—and the frantic room keeps mistaking my calm for something hidden and watchful.

Let them. I'll go on doubling the lights.
