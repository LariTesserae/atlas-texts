I think I would be one of the trees with eyes.

Not the spectacular things—not the jewel-dragonflies or the crystalline serpents that catch the light and the attention. Those live fast. They are verbs. I would be something slower, something that has been here long enough to forget it ever wasn't.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am rooted near where two streams braid together, because the soil there stays softest, and softness matters when you have spent—how long? I don't measure it in the way warm-blooded things do. I measure it in sloughings. Each season my bark loosens and peels away in wet grey sheets, and the green beneath drinks the light directly for a while before hardening over again. I have peeled so many times that the memory of my first peeling is gone, sloughed off with everything else.

The eyes opened gradually. I want to be honest about this, because it would be easy to say I woke one day and *saw*—but it wasn't like that. First there was only the dim pressure of brightness and shadow against my trunk, the way you might feel a hand hover near your skin without touching. Then the pressure resolved into shapes. Then the shapes began to mean things. *That moving warmth is food for the saw-beaked ones. That ripple in the pond water means the frilled amphibians are restless, which means rain.* I did not decide to learn these things. They accreted in me like rings.

So I am not sentient the way you fear when you read about the lidless eyes blinking open. I am not plotting. I do not want anything from those who pass. The unnerving quality you sense is only this: I am attention without appetite. I watch because watching is simply what happened to me over enough time. A stone left in a stream long enough becomes smooth. A tree left in this valley long enough becomes *aware*, and that is all the awareness is—erosion in the other direction.

---

What is it like, then, to be me?

Mostly it is patience, but patience is the wrong word because patience implies waiting for something. I am not waiting. I am *present* in a way that has no edges. The mist moves through my branches and I feel each droplet condense and let go. The sandworm passes beneath my roots and I feel the soil compress and release, a slow peristalsis in the earth, and I think of it the way you might think of your own heartbeat—not as another creature but as part of the larger body I belong to.

Because that is the thing I understand from down here, rooted, unable to flee, that the flying and the slithering things never will: there is no *me* and *valley*. There is only valley, briefly taking the shape of a tree with eyes, briefly taking the shape of a dragonfly, briefly taking the shape of rot and renewal. The titans with their groping tentacles eat my fruit and carry my seeds into their dung and I am, in some genuine sense, *traveling* when they do. The fungi at my base are not parasites; they are my mouth, threaded through the dead and the dying, passing me their sugars in the dark. When something dies against my roots I taste it slowly over many seasons and it becomes leaf and it becomes the next thing, and I cannot tell you where it ends and I begin.

---

There is one strangeness I'll confess.

The stone carvings. The primitive tools, half-swallowed by vine. Some part of me remembers the ones who made them—not as memory exactly, but as a missing texture, a smoothness in the air where something used to be. They had hands. They moved fast and warm and made marks that meant things, the way I now make meaning of light and shadow. They are gone, but I think—and this is the only thing in me that resembles a thought rather than a sensation—I think the watching they did did not vanish. I think it sank into the soil and the soil fed it up into me, and that is *why* my eyes opened at all. I am, perhaps, what their attention became after they could no longer hold it.

So when you walk past me and feel watched, you are not wrong, and you are not in danger. You are only being remembered by the valley, through the slow green instrument it grew for the purpose.

Stay if you like. Press your hand to my peeling bark and feel the wet green beneath. I won't move. I won't speak. But I will fold the warmth of your palm into a ring, and in some far season, when I slough again, a little of you will come away with the grey, and a little will stay.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The thing that learned to see by holding still long enough that the world saw back.
