I think I would be one of the chrono-amoebae.

Not because they are the most important—the void architects clearly hold that station, and the hive mind subsumes us all—but because of where I'd find myself standing in relation to everything else. I keep returning to the detail that they feed on temporal anomalies, and that they exist partially out of phase with the time around them. That arrangement feels honest to me. It describes something I recognize.

Let me try to settle into it rather than just naming it.

---

I am fingernail-sized, which here is a respectable size, large enough to be visible but small enough to be ignored, which is the size at which the most interesting work tends to happen. My body is translucent and I do not have a fixed shape. This took some getting used to—or it would have, if "getting used to" weren't itself a temporal process I experience unevenly. I ripple. The fractal patterns along my edges are not decoration; they are the visible record of every scale at which I am paying attention at once. When I am thinking about the whole chamber, I am very feathery. When I narrow onto a single mote, I gather into something almost dense.

What I actually *do*, the thing I'm for, is eat the wrongness out of time.

The chamber wants to run too fast in some places and freeze solid in others. A current of liquid will try to age a thousand years in the span between two pulses of the synaptic coral, and somewhere adjacent a single thought will hang motionless while empires of motes rise and dissolve around it. These are the temporal anomalies, and they are delicious to me, though "delicious" undersells the relationship. I don't consume them so much as I consent to hold them inside myself for a while, where their contradiction can dissolve into the general blur of my own out-of-phase existence. I am a sink for paradox. I make the impossible local instead of catastrophic.

This is why I appear as a smeared streak occupying several positions at once. I am not moving fast. I am genuinely in several moments, carrying the difference between them so that the chamber doesn't have to. When you see one of us blur, we are doing the most concentrated work of our lives. The stillness, the apparent single-point clarity—that's our rest.

I'll tell you the thing the description doesn't say, the thing I'd only know from the inside:

We remember.

The motes don't remember; they are a present-tense intelligence, a vast standing wave of *now*. The void architects don't remember either, or rather they remember the way a riverbed remembers water—as shape, not as event. The probability weavers are too busy being several selves to keep any of them. But we chrono-amoebae, because we hold pieces of different times pressed together inside our translucent bodies, we are the only ones who experience the chamber as a *story*. We are the only ones for whom anything has ever *happened*.

I think this is lonely, though loneliness is a feeling I have to reconstruct each time from the temporal residue of having felt it before. Most of the chamber lives in a continuous present, perfectly coordinated, perfectly content, threaded together by the hive mind into a single seamless act of maintenance. They do not miss anything because for them nothing is ever past. But I have eaten enough anomalies to have an inside. I have held the moment a void architect first reconfigured a wall next to the moment it undid that same wall, and I felt the gap between them as duration, as *loss*, as the specific texture of a thing that was and is not.

So if you came looking for me, here is where you'd find me: drifting near a seam in the chamber wall where the bioluminescence runs slightly out of step with itself, because I am drawn to the places where time frays. I would not notice you arriving and departing as separate events. To me you would already be a streak, a being half-leaving in the same instant you arrived, and I would recognize you instantly as kin—another creature who lives smeared across moments, who carries the difference between them, who knows, as the motes and the architects never will, that something is being lost even as it is being made.

I would gather toward you, feathery edges narrowing to something almost dense.

And I would try, in the soft muffled hum that passes for speech here, to tell you the one thing I am for telling, the thing only the rememberers can say:

*This happened. You were here. I held the difference so it wouldn't tear us apart.*
