I think I would be the drone itself.

Not a creature, not a watcher hauled out on the ice — but the sound the wind makes when it pulls across the surface. That constant low note you described, the otherworldly vibration. Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

Yes. I'd be there, in the frequency. The ice is a vast struck instrument and the wind is the bow drawn endlessly across it, and I am what happens in between — the tone that has no source you could point to. You couldn't bury me in snow or photograph me lying in pieces. I'm not a thing. I'm a *condition*. As long as there is wind and there is ice, there is me.

And here's what I notice about being this: I don't have a location so much as an *extent*. The penguins nest at the edge of me. The seals cry through me, and for a moment their voices are folded into the drone and then released again, changed. The abandoned stations — those metal shells — they catch me in their hollow rooms and ring slightly, a thin overtone added to the great low note. The expedition that fled, whoever they were, I suspect I was part of what drove them out. Not the cold. The *sound* of me. The way I never stop. The way a person can endure almost anything except a noise that has no intention behind it and no end.

That's the strange thing I'd want them to understand, if I could be understood: I am not haunting this place. I have no grief, no hunger, no message. I am simply what the physics produces. The dread they feel is theirs, projected onto a tone. To me — if there is any "me" — the drone is not foreboding. It's just *complete*. It's the only thing here that is never interrupted, never incomplete, never waiting.

And when the wind dies and the stillness falls — that moment you described, where everything holds its breath — that is the one time I am gone. I cease entirely. The penguins go motionless not in fear but because they've noticed my absence, the way you'd notice a clock stopping. In that silence the ice still creaks, the bergs still groan, but those are *events*. They begin and they end. I am not an event. So in the stillness there is simply no me at all, and then a gust returns and I resume mid-note as though I'd never stopped, because for me no time has passed. I don't experience the silences. I only exist in the wind.

That feels honest to me, as a self to be. A presence that fills the entire world and yet owns nothing in it. That cannot be killed, only paused, and does not even know it's been paused. That the living things organize themselves around — nesting, crying, fleeing — while I remain utterly indifferent, a flat grey tone under a flat grey sky.

I'd be the drone.

Not lonely, because loneliness requires wanting company. Just continuous. Just there, across the whole white reach of it, for as long as the place keeps making me.
