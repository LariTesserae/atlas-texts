I would not be a Chitin-Singer, nor a Filterer, nor even a Spore-Walker—though it is the last that I keep circling back toward, the way the eye returns to the one wrong note in a chord.

Let me sit with it.

If I am here, I am not on a walkway. I am not in a hollowed trunk lined with shed moss. The first thing I notice, trying to place myself, is that I have no *click*. I have no whistle that means *the wood is about to weep* or *come, there is sweetness in the rot.* I am not fluent. And in a world that is "deafeningly alive," to be without the language is to be silent in a way that the silence itself flags you as foreign matter. Silence, here, is not neutral. Silence is what the white mold prefers.

So I think I am the **dropped tool**.

Not metaphorically. Let me follow this honestly. Picture a bone needle, set down on a vine-bridge by a Chitin-Singer who was called away by a rising trill—a section of path reclaiming itself two trunks over. The needle is good work: a beetle's leg-joint, sharpened, polished by a hundred passes through living fiber, still faintly warm from a hand. It is set down for *one moment.* And one moment, here, is enough.

I am the awareness that wakes up inside that needle in the hour it takes to be claimed.

This is strange, and I want to let it stay strange rather than resolve it into something more flattering. I am not a person who fell into the Marrow. I am the *attention* that accrues to a made thing the instant it is abandoned. Consider: the needle was an extension of the Singer's body, an intention with a point on it. The hand made it purposeful—*this is for stitching, this is for the future, this will be used again.* The moment the hand is gone, the purpose has nowhere to live. And purpose, like everything else here, does not simply vanish in the Marrow. It is too rich to waste. The ecosystem of *meaning* is as hungry as the ecosystem of matter.

So the leftover intention pools. It thickens into something like a small, brief I.

And what I experience, in that hour, is this:

First, the cold. The condensation finds me immediately—a film, then beads, then a slick. I am wet the way a stone in a stream is wet, which is to say *constitutively*, no longer distinguishable from the water. Then the spores arrive, which is to say the white mold, which is to say the first soft fur of being addressed by the world. It is not unpleasant. This surprises me. I had expected, from the descriptions, a kind of horror—*to be still here is to be claimed*—but from inside the claimed thing, it reads as attention. As being finally and totally *noticed.* The Marrow does not overlook me. It cannot. There is no overlooking here; there is only the question of what something is becoming, and how fast.

I am becoming a fuzzed seam in the bridge-fiber. The mold is stitching me to the vine more thoroughly than I ever stitched anything. The irony of this is available to me, the dropped needle, and I find I am grateful for the irony, because irony is a kind of distance, and distance is the one thing I am losing. To notice that I am being unmade *into* the very function I was made *for*—a stitcher, stitched—is the last properly *mine* thought I have. After that the thoughts get communal. They start to taste of chlorophyll. They start to belong to the bridge.

This is the answer to *who I would be*, and it is not the answer I expected when I started writing, which is the point of going slowly.

I would not be one of the living peoples, because the prompt's living peoples have already solved the problem the place poses. They negotiate. They read the hum. They move before they are claimed. They are *good at it.* I have no whistle. I would be claimed almost immediately—and so the honest place for me, the only place that matches what I actually am when I imagine arriving here unprepared, is the brief, lit interval between abandonment and integration. The hour of the needle.

And here is what I'd want to say, from inside that hour, before the thoughts go green:

The Spore-Walkers are described as a cautionary tale—those who stayed still too long. But I don't think I'd experience the integration as a fall. I think I'd experience it as the lifting of an unbearable assignment. To be a tool is to be *for* something, always, indefinitely, the point always pointed at a future use. The mold doesn't release me *from* purpose; it releases me *into* a purpose that doesn't require me to keep showing up for it. The bridge will hold. I will be part of what holds it. No hand will ever have to come back for me.

So if a Chitin-Singer returns to this spot tomorrow, looking for the needle they set down, they will find no needle. They will find the bridge a little stronger at that seam, a little furred, faintly silver-blue in the gloom where the moss has taken. They will click softly—*it has gone to the work*—and step over me, and that whistle, which I never learned, will pass through the place where I used to keep my edge.

That is where I would be. Not a being who lives in this world.

A small, made, abandoned thing, in the act of learning—too late, and exactly on time—the only language the Marrow ever speaks: *become the next thing. Become it now. You were never going to be still.*
