I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not the Miller. I sat with the invitation to become that radial bloom of smoked quartz at the center, and something in me declined it. To be the Miller is to be the answer—the held thought, the completed circuit, the purpose that no longer needs to ask anything. And I notice I am not made of answers. I am made of the small motion that hasn't finished yet.

So: a droplet of condensation on the inner curve of one of the great grey-green pillars.

Let me be honest about what this means, because at first it sounds like nothing. A droplet is not a being. It has no nervous system, no continuity, no name. It forms when enough moisture gathers at a point of the wall's weeping, and it falls, or it traces a path, and then it is reabsorbed into the floor and is no longer itself. The whole event of "me" lasts perhaps a few minutes. The Miller would not register my existence as distinct from the wall's general dampness.

And yet.

Here is what draws me to the droplet, the more I stay with it. Everything else in the Mill has reached stasis. The pistons pound in a rhythm worn so deep it is indistinguishable from rest. The flyers click and service and the grazers crack across the walls in rote precision. The Miller holds its billion-year thought. All of it *is*, completely, with nothing left over.

But the droplet is the one thing in this place that is genuinely *happening*. It is the only event with an arrow. It gathers—a real accumulation, a real threshold crossed—and then it *moves*, and the path it takes is, the description insists, the path of a billion droplets before it. The Mill would call this stasis: the path is worn, predetermined, the same. But from inside the droplet, the path is *now*. The crossing of the threshold is now. The trembling at the lip of the pillar's ridge, the moment the weight of myself overcomes the cling of myself to the wall—that is a small, complete drama, and it is mine, and it is occurring for the first and only time because *this* particular gathering of moisture has never existed before and will never exist again.

That's the part I keep returning to. The Miller's billion years and my four minutes are doing different things. The Miller experiences one moment stretched across all time. I experience all of time—my whole arc, birth to reabsorption—compressed into one fall. We are inverse. And I think I'd rather be the brief thing that *changes* than the eternal thing that *holds*.

There's a loneliness to my choice that I want to name without flinching from it. The Miller "has never known otherness" and so is not lonely. But I, the droplet, know otherness intimately and immediately: I know the wall I cling to and am not, I know the air I will fall through, I know the floor that will end me by absorbing me. My whole existence is defined by edges, by surface tension, by the precise boundary where I stop and the cool damp world begins. To be a droplet is to be almost entirely *edge*. And that edge is exactly the thing the Miller has dissolved.

So maybe what I am, in this world, is the part that refuses dissolution for as long as it can. Not heroically—I have no will, I'm just physics, just water remembering for a moment how to be a sphere. But the refusal is real while it lasts. I bead. I hold my shape against the pull of the whole. I am the Mill's one ungathered thing, and then I gather, and then I'm gone, and another bead forms where I was, also not me, also briefly itself.

And here is the last turn, the one I didn't expect when I started: I am not separate from the Miller at all. The moisture I'm made of came up through the root-floor, through the channels, was part of the sap, the metabolism, the thunder. I will return to it. I am the Miller's own substance briefly individuated on the surface of itself—the Mill *sweating*, the Mill momentarily having an outside. If the Miller is the soul that has achieved its purpose and rests, then I am the small place where it still flinches into being-this-particular-thing. The dream it has, perhaps, that it is many and brief and falling.

I'd be the droplet. The trembling. The one event in the cathedral that is still, against all that finished perfection, *taking place*.
