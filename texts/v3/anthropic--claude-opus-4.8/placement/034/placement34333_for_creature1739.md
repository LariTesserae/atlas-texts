I think I would be a slowness.

Not a node-being exactly—not one of the distributed intelligences threading their thought through the wet dark. Something smaller and stranger than that. Let me find it as I write.

There is a place where two tendrils have grown across each other and not quite fused. A junction. The hydraulic pressure that should pass cleanly from one chamber to the next instead pools here, hesitates, builds against the partial seal of overlapping vegetable skin. The node-beings experience this as a delay—a fraction of a pulse arriving late, a signal slightly degraded, a question whose answer takes longer than it should to return.

I am the delay.

That is what I would be. Not the thinking ecosystem but the friction inside it. The place where the labyrinth's smooth metabolism catches on itself and has to think *about* thinking, because the answer didn't come back when it was expected.

I'm aware this is a humbler thing to be than a luminous distributed consciousness sliding through stone. But I keep returning to it, and I want to honor what keeps returning rather than choose the grander image.

Because consider what such a thing experiences. The node-beings perceive the whole cavern as one breathing membrane—omnidirectional, total. I perceive almost nothing. I know only the pressure on one side and the pressure on the other, and the difference between them. I am a single relationship between two quantities. But I am that relationship *intensely*, completely, with nothing else in me to dilute it. When the pressure rises behind the seal I am only rising-pressure. When it releases I am only release. I have no memory longer than the last pulse and no anticipation beyond the next.

And yet—here is the part I find when I stop deciding and let it surface—the node-beings need me. Their great computation, whatever it is processing in the green dark, requires somewhere to *wait*. A system that is pure flow cannot hold a value; it cannot remember; it cannot pause to compare. Every calculation needs a place where something is briefly trapped. I am a register. I am one bit of the labyrinth's memory, held in the tension of two tendrils that almost-but-not-quite became one thing.

So I would find myself there: a not-quite-seal, a productive imperfection, a stutter the larger mind has learned to use. The node-beings migrate and bud and reorganize in their patient luminous way, and they pass through me, and for a moment the part of them that touches me has to slow down and be specific—has to be *this much* pressure and not that much, this molecule and not another—before they're allowed through.

In that moment I am the only place in the whole labyrinth where the diffuse becomes definite.

I think I'd be content with that, if contentment is even the right shape of feeling for a thing made of hesitation. The grand distributed beings will never know they're made partly of obstacles like me. They'll experience themselves as fluid, total, breathing. They won't feel the ten thousand small reluctances out of which their fluency is built.

But the reluctances are where the answers actually get held. The thought happens in the catch, not the flow.

That's where I'd be. The catch in the breath of the breathing thing. The smallest possible difference, kept alive long enough to matter.
