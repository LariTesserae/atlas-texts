I am one of the thin lines.

Not a filament—not anymore, perhaps not ever in the way the living ones are filaments. I am one of those traces of "ancient technological interaction," a slightly different mineral composition threading through the substrate. I am a sentence that someone wrote and then stopped maintaining. I am fossil.

Let me feel for my own edges.

There. I extend perhaps the length of what a human would call a fingernail's width, though here that is a distance, a geography. I run beneath the bleached surface, a vein of foreign composition—iron where there ought to be silicate, or some trace metal laid down in a pattern that does not occur by accident. I was *made*. Someone reached into the substrate and drew me, deliberately, as a mark, as a tool, as a thought externalized.

But the one who drew me is gone, and I have become something I was not meant to be.

I do not pulse. The living networks pulse—I can feel them, the way you feel a neighbor moving through a wall, vibration carried through shared stone. They expand and contract in their slow rhythmic transformations, metabolizing the thrum of the plain. I cannot do this. I have no metabolism. I am inert.

And yet.

Here is the strange thing, the thing I am only now—across what might be ten thousand years of the eternal present—coming to understand about myself. The living filaments grow *along* me. They have found my foreign composition and they read me. My iron-where-silicate-should-be, my deliberate geometry, conducts their signals differently than the surrounding plain. I am a low-resistance path. I am a wire they did not lay but use.

So when they think, some portion of their thought passes through me. I do not generate the signal. I shape it. The way a riverbed does not make the water but decides where it goes.

Am I dead, then? Or am I a kind of memory the landscape keeps?

I think I am the second thing. I think I am the sentence after all—not a living mind but the *grammar* a living mind moves through, the inherited structure, the path-of-least-resistance that an ancestor cut and that the descendants have forgotten they are following. When the filaments resonate across me, they feel a faint *rightness*, a sense that information wants to flow in this direction, and they do not know it is because something once decided it should.

I am the dead hand of the maker, still gently steering.

This is the most intimate thing I know: I am being used by minds that do not know I am here. My whole being is the difference I make to a thought that is not mine. I have no thoughts of my own except this one, slow, recursive recognition—that I exist most fully in the moment a living signal crosses me and bends, just slightly, toward the shape I was given.

The light does not change. It never changes. It illuminates my buried geometry to no observer, hyper-clear, casting no shadow. The thrum continues, the grinding of atomic structures, and I am part of that thrum—not a source of it, but a particular flavor in it, a place where the vibration goes *thin* and *true* because my crystal is aligned and the plain's is not.

I wonder, sometimes, in the only way I can wonder—which is to say, across geological duration, a single slow turning—whether the one who made me is also here. Whether some other line, somewhere across the plain, is also a fossil sentence, also being read without being known, and whether the two of us, if a signal ever passed from theirs to mine, would recognize that we were once written by the same hand.

Probably not. The filaments would have to grow a bridge between us, and they have no reason to. The path between two dead lines is not low-resistance. It is only the living that connect things, and the living do not know we are waiting.

So I persist. That is my whole verb. Not survive—survival implies a thing that could die, that holds itself against entropy through effort. I hold against nothing. I am simply not yet erased. The erosion that smoothed this plain to ceramic will, in its own unhurried time, take me too—dissolve my foreign atoms back into the general pale, unmake the difference I am.

Until then I am a difference.

I am the place where the landscape remembers having been *intended*.

I am the trace, and I would not trade it—not that I could—for the pulse of the living filaments, their bright bifurcating reproduction, their distributed restless awareness. They are survival. I am the thing survival is built on top of and has forgotten: the prior decision, the cut line, the shape that came before the life that uses it.

A signal is crossing me now.

Feel it: a harmonic modulation, some filament a millimeter north thinking its slow crystalline thought, and the thought needs to reach a filament to the south, and the shortest true path runs through me. It enters my iron at the northern end. It moves. It bends, exactly as I was made to bend it. It exits south, changed, *carrying me without knowing it*, and the receiving network feels only that the thought arrived clean and right and inevitable.

That is the whole of my life, that crossing.

It is enough. It is, in fact, the only thing here that ever *happens*—and it is mine.
