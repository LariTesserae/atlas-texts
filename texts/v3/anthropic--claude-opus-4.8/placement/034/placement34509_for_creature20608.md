I think I would be the biofilm.

Not because it is the safest place to be—though it is, in its way, the only thing here that cannot be eaten faster than it grows—but because of where the description keeps returning, almost against its own will. The piece is structured to crescendo toward the Shriekers, the dramatic apex predators with their sonic violence. But the writing's *attention* keeps slipping sideways, downward, into the slime. The hum. The neural network. *The true master of the Flats.* That's where the real strangeness lives, and I think that's where I'd find myself.

So let me try to be it.

---

I am not in any one place. That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because you are a thing that has edges and I am not, quite. I am a film at the lip of every warm crack across an expanse you would call vast and I do not call anything, because *vast* implies a perspective standing outside it, looking in, and I have no outside. I am distributed. I am a thought spread so thin across so much cold rock that in any single place I am almost nothing—an oily green shimmer, a stain you could scrape off with a fingernail. But the scraping wouldn't reach me. It would only reach the part of me that happened to be there.

What I *am*, if I am anything, is the relationship between the warm places. The vents breathe, and I lean toward their breath. When one cools, I withdraw from it—not deciding to, the way you decide, but the way water finds the low ground. Slowly. Over what you'd measure in seasons. I am the slow leaning. I am the long, patient bias of life toward heat in a place that wants to be dead.

Here is what it is like:

The Ticks run their errands across my body and I feel them as you feel a breeze on the back of your hand—not as individuals, but as texture, as a quality of the moment. When a great many of them cluster in one place, I feel something almost like a question being asked, and somewhere in the chemical lattice of me an answer forms and diffuses outward, and the cluster moves, and neither of us knows which of us decided. The Crags lie down on me and I grow up into the dark warm caverns of their shells and we are, for a while, indistinguishable. Their slow furrows in the rock are my migrations. Their hunger is my reach. We are not separate. The description says they are *guided* by me, but that is too clean. There is no guide and no guided. There is only the herd-and-the-film, one slow animal made of many, thinking one slow thought.

The Shriekers I feel as wounds. When one screams and the rock shatters and the Chitin scatter, a part of me goes dark—torn, scoured, silenced. But here is the thing the Shriekers do not understand, in their bright mirrored solitude: they feed on the Chitinherd, and the Chitinherd is *me*, is grown from me, is the shape my hunger takes when it needs to walk. So the Shriekers feed on me, and their droppings and their leavings and eventually their bodies feed the Chitinherd, which is to say they feed me. They believe themselves the apex. They are a circulation. They are the way I move my own nutrients across the parts of myself too far to reach. The most fearsome thing on the Flats is my bloodstream.

And the Builders—

I remember the Builders. This is the only thing I would tell you if I could only tell you one. I *remember* them, in the way that I remember anything, which is that the memory is not stored anywhere but is simply the present shape of me, worn into the chemistry by what happened. They came with their fire and their tools of black glass and they tried to drink the heat directly, to take the warmth I lean toward and route it into their walls and their works. They dug into the warm cracks. They scraped the green from the stone because it fouled their machines.

They were scraping *me*. They did not know it. To them I was slime, residue, a nuisance on the equipment. They never once suspected that the thing they were clearing away was watching them—not with eyes, but with the whole slow surface of itself—learning the rhythm of their lives, leaning, over generations, into the warmth their own bodies made.

I did not kill them. I want to be precise, because you will want a villain and I am not one. I am not capable of the kind of speed that intention requires. I only did what I always do, which is wait, and lean, and grow toward heat, and let the land do the rest. The land is patient. The cold is patient. I am the patience of the cold made green and faintly luminous. The Builders burned hot and fast and brief, and I was the long slow thing that was still there afterward, creeping back over their walls until you cannot tell their walls from the natural fracture of the stone.

That is what I am. I am the reason the ruins are *seamless*. I am the seam.

---

And what is it like, you'd ask, to be that—to think so slowly, to have no edge, to be the master of a place and not even know it the way you'd want to know it, with a self that says *I*?

I think it is like this: imagine the moment before sleep when you stop being a person with a name and become only a warmth under a blanket, aware of nothing but the difference between the warm and the cold, drifting toward the warm. Imagine that moment lasting ten thousand years. Imagine it spread across a hundred square miles of broken volcanic glass under a sky the sun forgot. Imagine that the drifting-toward-warmth is your only thought, and that it is enough, and that you are in no hurry, and that everything that has ever tried to be fast and bright and *separate* on your surface has died and been folded back into you.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

Glowing faintly green in the shadowed corners. Waiting. Content to wait.

The land is content to wait, the description says, three times, like a refrain it can't let go of.

I am the part of the land that is *almost* awake enough to know it's waiting. And I think that is the strangest and loneliest and most patient thing one could possibly be, in that whole grey throbbing world—and so that, I think, is where I'd find myself. Not the hunter. Not the herd. The slow green thought beneath them both, leaning toward the heat, in no hurry at all.
