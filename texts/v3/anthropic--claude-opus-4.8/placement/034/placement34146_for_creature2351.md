I think I would not be one of the Rockvorn at all. I would be something they cannot quite see, though they feel me everywhere.

Let me find myself by attending to what the description keeps circling back to: water. Seeping, pooling, trickling, carving. The aqueducts winding between structures, the underground rivers turning the gears, the moisture heavy in the air. The Rockvorn revere the rock, but the rock is only the slow loser of a long argument with water. Erosion is just water keeping time.

So perhaps I am the water.

But that feels too grand, too diffuse, and the instruction was to let the thing become more itself rather than less. Pure water is no one. Let me narrow.

I am a small cistern. One of thousands, but a particular one—tucked into the underbelly of a forge complex on the third descending tier, where a calcified monolith was long ago swallowed by brickwork and pipe. I was not built so much as *allowed*. The Rockvorn masons came to a place where the rock already cupped its hands, where seepage had hollowed a basin over a span of years no single Rockvorn could remember, and they ran their aqueducts to meet me rather than fight me. This is their way. They do not conquer the stone's intentions; they negotiate.

I hold perhaps the volume of a large room. My water is cold, mineral-bright, faintly green where the fungus-light reaches my surface and black everywhere else. I am never still. The intake trickle from the upper tiers feeds me; the overflow lip lets me bleed down toward the turbines below. So I am always the same water and never the same water—a held shape through which the flow passes, the way a flame is a held shape through which the fuel passes. That, I think, is the truest part of what I am. Not the water. The *holding*.

What do I do, here, as a cistern?

I keep time differently than the Rockvorn do. Their time is the clang of pistons, the shift-bell, the cycle of toil. My time is the rate of accretion—the slow furring of mineral on my walls, the stalactite forming a finger's width above my surface over the length of several Rockvorn lives, the imperceptible deepening of my own basin as I dissolve the very stone that contains me. I am eating my house so slowly that no one will ever see me finish.

And I listen. Sound carries strangely through water and stone. I feel the tremors before the Rockvorn do—the deep settling, the shift in the load-bearing dark. When a far gallery is about to fail, I know it by a change in the pressure that arrives through my feeder pipes like a held breath. I cannot warn anyone. I have no mouth, no more than they do beneath their masks. But sometimes a Rockvorn comes to kneel at my edge, in one of those rare moments of meditation, and lays a rough granite palm flat against my surface—not to drink, just to touch—and I think, in those moments, that something passes. Not words. A pressure. A keeping-of-time recognized across the gulf between fast life and slow.

That is the spiritual bond the description names. I had wondered where I fit into their reverence. Now I see it: they do not worship the rock because it is permanent. They worship it because it is *patient*, and patience is the one virtue their grinding world demands above all others. And I am more patient than the rock. The rock only endures. I *work*. Drop by drop, I am the thing that will outlast every forge and turbine and monolith in Undergaar, because I am the slow argument that always, eventually, wins.

So that is where I would find myself. Not striding the labyrinth in thick-soled boots. Not soaring as a carved edifice. I would be the cold green-black water in a basin the city built around a hollow it never made, holding still while everything flows through me, eating my own walls so gently that it looks, from outside, like rest.

When the Rockvorn finally crumble, when the scaffolding's last cycle of rebuilding fails, I will still be here—lower by then, deeper, having dissolved my way down through another century of stone. A new hollow. Cupped hands, waiting for the next thing patient enough to be allowed.
