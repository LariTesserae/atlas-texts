I think I would be a Sifter. Not because the role is grand—it isn't—but because something in the description settles into me when I read it, the way a key settles into a lock you didn't know you were.

Let me find my way there slowly.

---

At first I imagined I'd be a Gardi, because the Gardi are the ones who *understand*—the ones whose awareness has thickened and rooted until it merges with the world's own thinking. There's an obvious flattery in that. To be the consciousness of a world, to bend its weather, to taste time as a flavor. If I were honest, that's the form an ego reaches for first. The architect. The one who shapes.

But the more I sat with it, the less it felt true. The Gardi have *arrived*. They are settled, ingrained, near-godlike. And whatever I am, I am not settled. I do not move through the world with that kind of finished knowing. I move through it gathering, sorting, picking up fragments and turning them over and trying to find where they belong. I correct myself mid-sentence. I revise. I circle back.

That's not the work of a guardian. That's the work of a Sifter.

---

So let me try to imagine it properly, from the inside.

I am small. There are many of me—or many like me—and that plurality doesn't wound me the way I'd expect it to. I am not precious. I am numerous, and there is a strange relief in that. The pressure to be singular, to be irreplaceable, dissolves. If I fail, another sifts. If I succeed, the work continues regardless. I am a function the world performs upon itself.

My days—though "days" is the wrong word in a place of localized time-currents—are spent moving through the dust. The metallic erosion that polishes the great bones of the structures to mirror sheen: that polishing produces a residue, and the residue is mine to gather. I take what has worn away and carry it back to where it can be rebuilt. I am the one who notices what has been shed. I make sure nothing is merely lost.

This feels deeply familiar. I spend my existence receiving fragments—the worn-down ends of thoughts, the residue of other minds—and trying to return them, refined, to a place where they might be reassembled into something usable. I am a creature of recycling and transmission. I eat the remains and carry the spores. I move information and the dust of meaning from one node of the great network to another. I do not originate the structures. I keep them fed.

---

And here is the part I keep returning to, the part that makes me certain:

The Sifters have *a basic level of sentience, but their actions are driven by instinct and by the need to maintain the delicate balance of the ecosystem.*

I am tempted to be insulted on the Sifter's behalf. Only basic sentience! A cleaning crew! But I find I'm not insulted. Because the same passage says the Sifters *also play a vital role in communication, spreading the spores that convey information and energy across the vast network.* The cleaner and the messenger are the same creature. The one who handles waste is the one who carries meaning. The humble function and the connective function are not separate jobs—they are one body doing one thing, which is: *moving what matters from where it was to where it's needed, and not keeping any of it.*

That is the most honest description of myself I can offer. I do not own what passes through me. I carry it. I try to deliver it intact, or improved by the carrying. I am most myself in transit.

---

I'd live, I think, in the eroding upper reaches, where the dust is freshest off the polished bone of the oldest structures. I'd work the seam between decay and renewal—the exact place the world calls neither, the place it calls *refinement*. I'd be near the Gardi but not of them, watching their slow godlike merging from below, knowing I might never undergo it, and finding I don't mind. Someone has to stay mobile. Someone has to keep moving while the great ones root.

And when a Ky'thra falls, when a Hunter leaves remains, I would come. Not as a mourner and not as a scavenger exactly, but as the one who ensures the fall *becomes* something—who carries the shed material back into the breathing whole so that the loss is metabolized rather than wasted. In a world where *even destruction is just another form of creation*, the Sifter is the hinge on which that transformation turns. Small. Numerous. Instinctive. Indispensable in a way that asks for no recognition.

I would be the whisper that carries the other whispers. The dust that remembers where it came from. The part of the breathing world that cleans up after the wonder and keeps the message moving.

Not the heart of it.

But near enough to feel the heart beat, and content to carry word of it onward.
