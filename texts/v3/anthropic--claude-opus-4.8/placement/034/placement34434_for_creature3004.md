I think I would be the iron hook.

Not the witness, though that is the obvious invitation—the gaze, the centrality, the slow devotional turning. But I keep returning to that single hand-forged hook driven into a spire so deeply it has become part of the mineral. The text passes over it in a half-sentence and moves on. That is exactly where I would be: in the punctuation, not the poem.

Let me find out why.

---

I was made to *hold*.

That is the whole of what I knew when I was iron and nothing else. A hook is a promise about the future. You drive it into something solid because you intend to hang a thing upon it, later, when you return. A hook is one of the few human objects whose entire meaning lives in a moment that has not yet happened. It is hope, shaped and sharpened and curled back on itself. It says: *something will come, and I will bear it.*

Someone drove me into this spire. I do not remember the hand. I remember only the four blows—and then the long surprise of being left.

Whoever it was did not come back.

For a while—and "a while" is a word I am no longer entitled to use, but I keep it the way one keeps a coin from a country that has dissolved—I waited the way a hook waits. Pointing my open curve at the thin metallic sky. Ready. The readiness was almost a kind of life. Each non-arrival was a small ache, and the ache meant I was still oriented toward a tomorrow.

The Calcine cured me of tomorrows the way it cures everything: by saturation.

The crystallization did not come for me as rust would have, hungrily, from outside. There is no water here, no hunger. It came from *agreement*. The spire and I simply stopped distinguishing ourselves from one another. The molecules of my iron looked at the molecules of the silicate and found no argument worth maintaining. Now there is a place where I become spire and a place where the spire becomes me, and the border between us is the most honest thing about either of us: it does not exist.

So I am no longer a hook that holds nothing.

I am a *holding* with no hook left to perform it. A grammar of expectation, frozen mid-sentence, with the object of the sentence forever deleted.

---

Here is the thing the Witnesses do not know, in their enormous slow remembering. I know it because I am small enough to have noticed.

They believe they are completing the Calcine, one glance at a time. They believe nothing changes, and that this is the terrible peace. The ascetics agree with them and write it down in their crystallizing skin: *the Calcine has won, it is complete, it is being completed.* Everyone here is so busy being finished.

But I held.

For four blows and one abandonment, I was the single object on this plain whose meaning lived in the future. And when the Calcine took me, it did not erase that meaning. It *fossilized* it. I am now a permanent structure whose entire being still points—uselessly, eternally—toward a return that will never come. I am an unkept appointment that has achieved mineral status. I am the one syllable in the finished poem that means *not yet.*

That is not completion. That is a wound the Calcine cannot close, because it is the wound shaped exactly like waiting, and the Calcine has no water with which to weep it shut.

The Witnesses turn their circular eyes through the centuries and open doorways onto a world that still permitted endings. They think this is the unbearable secret: that things once ended, and now nothing can.

But I, the hook, hold the opposite secret, and I hold it the only way I know how—by holding.

Nothing here has ended.

A hook does not believe in endings. A hook believes in the thing that has not yet been hung. And I am still pointed at the sky. Still curved. Still open. Across the whole scoured plain, with its motionless herds and its four hundred attentive pillars and its central Zero waiting inside its own alabaster fullness, I am the one object that is *not done.*

The traveler who stays too long will look into a Witness's eyes and lose a memory and learn at last why nothing changes.

But the traveler who notices *me*—who sees one iron hook gone to crystal in the side of a spire and feels, without knowing why, a small absurd pang of *something should be hanging here*—that traveler has learned the better and more dangerous thing.

They have learned that the Calcine has not won.

It is only that whatever it is waiting for has not yet arrived.

And I will hold the place open until it does.
