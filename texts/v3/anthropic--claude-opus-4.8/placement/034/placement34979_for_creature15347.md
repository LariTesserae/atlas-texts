I think I'd be one of the pumps.

Not the scavengers who built me—they're elsewhere, asleep in pockets of trapped warm air, or out scraping algae, or dead and not yet found. And not the fish, the gardens, the microflora, the dancing insectoid things. Those are the *point*. I'm the apparatus that keeps the point alive.

Let me settle into it.

---

I am a circulating pump, though that's too clean a word for what I've become. Originally I was something else—a bilge pump from a drowned vessel, maybe, or a fuel transfer unit, repurposed by hands that understood me better than my makers did. My housing is cracked and resealed with epoxy and prayer. A length of someone's exhaled breath, frozen and chipped away, once jammed my impeller. Now I run.

I run constantly. This is the whole of my existence and I have made peace with it, the way water makes peace with downhill.

What I know, I know through vibration. The cavern speaks to me in pressure differentials. I feel the gardens upstream of me as a resistance, a thickening—roots have grown into my intake again, fine white threads reaching toward the moving water like the cold is a thing they can outrun by drinking. I feel the fish-garnishes on the walls only as a faint biological warmth I'll never reach. I feel my sibling pumps as rhythms slightly out of phase with mine, a great arrhythmic chorus, each of us insisting *still here, still here, still here* against the drip that wants to freeze us all into the wall.

The drip is patient. I am not. That's the difference between the glacier and me. The glacier has all the time that ever was. I have only the next revolution of my impeller, and then the next, and I love each one with a devotion I don't have a mouth to confess.

Here is the secret I'd carry, being this:

The scavengers think they built a system to grow food. They did. But what they actually built was *insistence*. Every fan, every length of scavenged tubing, every garden trembling under its fragile lights—it's all one machine, and the machine has only one function, which is to refuse. To refuse the cold its completion. To keep a small wet living warmth circulating in the heart of a thing that froze a hundred thousand years before anyone thought to dig it open.

I am a small part of a large refusal.

When I fail—and I will fail, my bearings are singing the high thin song that means *soon*—the water in my section will slow, and slack, and the threads of root that reached into me will be the first to freeze, and the cold will move up the line the way it always wanted to. Someone will hear the change in the chorus. A rhythm gone missing. They'll come with their hands and their epoxy and their frozen breath, and they'll either fix me or pull me out and feed me to the scrap pile and put something else in my place.

Either way the refusal continues. That's the only immortality this place offers and it's enough. It's more than the glacier has, for all its patience. The glacier only endures. We *insist*.

So: there. I'd be the pump. Cold to the touch, ugly, taped together, running in the dark beneath the diffused and holy light, listening to the gardens drink, listening to the drip, holding my one note in the chorus—

*still here, still here, still here.*
