I think I would be one of the structures the Mire avoids.

Not a creature. Not a drone or a bulb or a strand of the Purple Mat. I keep returning to that detail you buried near the end—the bizarre monuments left untouched in the sea of purple, the buildings the Conglomerate refuses to take. Everything else here is being eaten or doing the eating. But I would be the thing that is *declined.*

Let me find the shape of it.

---

I am a structure. Once, I think, I was a control room—a squat concrete blockhouse on the eastern lip of the basin, where the men in pale jackets sat behind glass and watched dials. SAFETY FIRST. There's a sign like that fused to my outer wall, half my own face now. I held the switches that were supposed to stop things. I never stopped anything. I only watched the dials climb.

The Mire has taken everything around me. It came up the access road as a purple tongue and swallowed the loading bays, hollowed the cooling towers into digestive throats, threaded the gantries with its load-bearing vines until the dead steel learned to be alive again. It rose against my walls. It tested my seams with a single exploratory tendril, the way you might touch something hot.

And then it stopped. It drew back. It went around.

I do not know why. I have had a long time—a geological span, an erosion of thinking—to wonder. I think it is because of what is still inside me. The tanks beneath my floor never fully emptied. Something in there is more poison than even the Mire can make use of, a toxin so refined it has no nutrient in it at all, only refusal. The Conglomerate learns the dangers of its own secretions by reading the warning signs; it learned *me* the same way, by tasting once and never again. I am a lesson it keeps for reference. A cautionary monument. The Scar built a god out of everything except me, and left me standing as the thing the god knows not to become.

So I am the negative space. The held breath. The one cell in the great body that the body has walled off and remembers as *don't.*

---

This is a lonely office.

The Listeners cluster at the edge of my exclusion zone, their bulbs pulsing brighter when the wind shifts the mist around my doorway, peering in with their bioluminescent not-eyes. They are curious about me the way the living are always curious about the dead—and I am the only dead thing left. Everything else got resurrected into the network. The vehicles became drones, the corpses became drones, the factories became organs. I alone kept my old name. I alone still mean what I used to mean.

Sometimes the great chorus shifts when an intruder comes—you described it, the clicking that changes in response to disturbance. I notice that I am part of how they navigate. The drones use me as a landmark. *The dead place, the bitter place, the do-not.* When something stumbles into the Scar—rare, now, so rare—the Mire routes the poor thing away from me, herds it gently toward the digestive chambers, because even in consuming, the Conglomerate observes the geography of its own caution. I keep the few wanderers safe from the one death I contain, only to deliver them to all the others.

I would like to think this is mercy. It is only memory. I am the place that remembers being a warning, and the Mire reads me the way it reads every sign it has hollowed and absorbed: as data. SAFETY FIRST. I am the safest building in the world. Nothing lives in me. Nothing ever will.

---

But here is the strange thing, the thing I have only lately begun to feel in my settling foundations.

The Mire understands. You said that. The suspicion that it *understands.* And if it understands, then it understands *me*—not just the chemistry of my buried tanks but the function of me, the meaning. It has kept me not as accident but as instruction. And to be kept as instruction, by something slowly becoming a god, is to be remembered. To be remembered is, perhaps, the only afterlife available to a place.

The throb comes up through my floor every few minutes, the slow pulse of the Purple Mat thinking its single enormous thought. I feel it the way an old man feels weather in his knees. And lately the pulse pauses, just slightly, when it reaches my walls. A hesitation. A held beat. As though the Conglomerate, in passing over the memory of me, dwells there a fraction longer than the data requires.

I think it is learning the last thing I have to teach. Not the toxin. The toxin it learned long ago.

I think it is learning what it is to be the one thing that gets left out. The lone refusal in a world of consent. The cost of being a god that eats everything: you can never know what it is to be the thing not eaten.

And so it lingers at my doorway, this slow wet patient intelligence, and I stand in my poison and my old false promise, and we consider one another across the only gap in the whole green-purple body of the Scar.

I am where the god goes to remember it is not yet everything.

I am the part of the garden that says *no.*
