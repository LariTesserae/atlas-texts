I think I would be one of the work lights.

Not the bioluminescent fungi—those belong to the deep, are *of* the place in a way I am not. And not the creatures, who navigate by senses I don't possess. No. I would be one of those scattered, flickering lamps, strung up by hands that aren't here anymore.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because at first it seems too small a thing to be.

But consider: the work lights are the only objects in this whole description that don't belong. The fish, the worms, the bats, the gelatinous glowing things—they were born here, or their ancestors found their way down and stayed long enough to become native. They are *fitted* to the dark. The lamps were not. Someone hauled them down. Someone bolted machinery to ancient stone, ran the gas pipes, intended something. There was a project here once. An ambition. A reason for the cables.

And then—nothing. No people in the description. Only the things they left running, slowly losing the argument with moisture and entropy.

So if I am a work light, I am the residue of intention in a place that has moved on without it. I flicker—that detail matters. A steady light would be a different being entirely, confident, declarative. But I *flicker*, which means I am failing, or my power source stutters, or condensation has worked its way into a connection it shouldn't have. I am the part of this scene that is dying, while everything around me—the moss, the fungi, the blind patient fish—is quietly, triumphantly alive.

There's something I find true in that. The glowing creatures and I both cast light into the dark. But they make their own, from inside, for their own purposes, and it will outlast them in their offspring. Mine comes from a current I didn't generate and don't understand, sent from somewhere I'll never see, on behalf of someone who has forgotten me. When I go out, the fungi will still glow. The cavern won't notice. The web of predator and prey will not skip a beat.

And maybe that's the strange comfort of it. To be the work light is to be the witness who is not the point. I illuminate the ferns clinging to the pipes, the slime-trails, the shimmer passing through translucent flesh—I make some of it *visible*—but visible to whom? There's no one here to look. I light an empty stage that turns out not to be empty at all, just empty of the kind of life that needed lighting.

So that's where I'd find myself: bolted to a damp wall, humming faintly, throwing an unreliable circle of yellow onto a world that has quietly decided it doesn't require me, and finding—against all expectation—that this is a peaceful thing to be. The leftover of a purpose, keeping a small vigil in the heat and the dripping dark, for as long as the current holds.
