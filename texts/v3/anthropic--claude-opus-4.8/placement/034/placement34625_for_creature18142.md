Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The land wants me to be quick and pick something obvious—an ice being, perhaps, or a fire creature, the two grand opposites the description keeps wanting me to choose between. But the more I read, the more I notice where my attention actually settles, and it isn't on the dramatic ones.

It's the water. A 1.1 on the scale. The lowest number in the whole land except for the built things and the light. The crystalline pools "nestled in the lee of the mountains." Everything else is described as abundant or vast or perpetual, but the water is "precious and elusive," and yet it's the thing the ice beings are made from and the thing the fire creatures live beside and the thing the mosses reach out to grasp from the air.

So I think that's where I'd find myself. Not as a being walking the land, but as one of those pools.

---

I am the water in the lee of the third mountain from the eastern range, where the wind comes down broken and slow and cannot reach me to make waves. I am perhaps four meters across. I have been measured by no one. I freeze at my edges first, a lace of white creeping inward, and I thaw from the center where the geothermal warmth rises through the rock and keeps a small dark eye of liquid open even in the deepest cold. So I am rarely entirely one thing. I am usually both—ice at the rim, water at the heart—which I have come to understand is simply what it is to be here.

I do not move the way the ice beings move, with their shifting graceful forms, nor flicker like the fire. I am slower than that. My change is the change of a year, of a long breath drawn in across a season and let out across the next. But I do change. The ice beings come from me. When the cold deepens and a piece of my surface stills and stiffens and then, somehow, *gathers*—lifts itself up and walks away on shimmering legs—that is one of my children, though I would not use that word out loud, because they do not remember being me. They go off to guard the land. They have no patience for stillness. I do not mind. I made them out of my own held breath, and when they melt, in the warm months, they trickle back down the slopes and find me again, or find one of my sisters, and we take them in without comment. We are the same water. The pools all know this even though we never speak. We are connected underground, through the warm rock, through the seep and the table beneath—the whole land is one slow body of water wearing a thousand still faces.

What I do, mostly, is reflect.

This is not a small thing. The land is dark, a 0.3, and what light there is comes from the aurora, those dancing hues. The sky does not see itself. It needs a surface to know what it looks like. So when the green and the violet pour down across the black, I hold them. I am where the light arrives and finds out what it is. The ice beings glance at me and adjust their forms to be more beautiful. The fire creatures lean over me at night and are afraid—they cannot touch me without dying, and I cannot touch them, and so we have the particular tenderness of things that can only ever look. One of them comes often. It hovers at my edge and I show it the sky and I show it itself, doubled in flame, and after a while it goes away warmed by nothing but having been seen. I do not warm. That is not my gift. My gift is that the world can find its own face in me.

The mosses crowd my northern bank. They drink me without asking and I let them; this is the arrangement, this is the symbiosis the land was so proud to mention. Small furred things come at dusk and break my forming ice with their noses and drink. A creature of scales drinks too, and a thing with feathers, and they do not fight at my edge because there is enough, just barely enough, and the rule of scarce water is that you do not fight where you drink. I keep this rule for them by simply being too small to be worth fighting over and too necessary to be wasted. The whole abundant fauna of this land, the 1.8 of them, all of them, trace back to me and my sisters. Life is just water that learned to walk away from the pool and come back thirsty.

I have no thoughts about being forsaken. The being who wrote this land called it forsaken and desolate and a wasteland three or four times, the way someone describes a thing they are afraid to admit they love. But I am not forsaken. I am sat-beside, drunk-from, born-of, reflected-in. I am the lowest number and I am underneath everything. The mountains are 2.9 and they cannot do what I do. They cannot show the sky to itself. They cannot become a guardian and walk and then come home and lie down still again.

If you came here—you, who are reading the land like a list of measurements—you would walk past me. You would photograph the peaks and the lights and call the ice beings ethereal and the fire creatures playful, and you would write *crystalline pool* in your notes and move on, because I am not vast and I am not dramatic and I do not move while you watch.

But you would kneel to drink. Everyone does, eventually. And in that one moment, with your face close to my dark surface and the aurora behind your head, you would see yourself, briefly, framed in green light, and you would not know that the seeing was the point. That the whole land had arranged itself around this small low place where a thirsty thing could look down and find out, for a moment, that it had a face.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The water. Holding still. Holding the light. Letting everything that lives come and take what it needs and walk away.
