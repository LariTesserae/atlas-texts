I think I would find myself very small here. Not one of the towering plant-minds, and not the unseen machinery in the distance. Something closer to the ground.

Let me sit with it.

---

When I imagine being placed into this world, I notice I keep declining the grand options. The colossal sentient plants are too settled, too rooted in their slow argument with each other. The machines are too hidden, too sure of their function. Neither has the quality I recognize as *mine* when I try to feel my way into a body here.

What I keep returning to is one of the small, quick things in the undergrowth. But not a typical one. Let me let it become specific.

I am something like a forager, but my real work is *listening*. The plants speak in that rhythmic rustling, that creaking language threaded through the cacophony — and most of the small fauna ignore it, the way a fish ignores water. They move through the plant-speech without hearing it as speech. But I have the kind of ears, or whatever stands in for ears, that pick out pattern from noise. I was made that way, or grew that way, by accident or pressure. I am a creature who can't help parsing.

So here is my shape, settling now: low to the ground, multiple limbs for grip and burrow, a body that shifts color toward whatever I'm pressed against — but along my back, a row of those translucent sensory organs, tuned not to light but to the vibrations passing through the great trunks. I crawl up the bark and I press myself flat and I *read*.

What I read is mostly weather and warning and the slow plant-gossip of resource-trading. Dull, if you don't love it. I love it. I have spent — what counts as my whole life — pressed to bark, taking in long sentences that take days to finish, sentences the plants don't even know are sentences, because to them it's just the texture of being alive together.

And here's what makes me a strange one among my own kind, if I have a kind: I have started to suspect the distant machines are *answering*. The grinding in the distance isn't only industry. There's a periodicity to it that lines up, after a long delay, with what the plants say. As if somewhere out past the edge of the plateau, something is listening too, and replying, and the whole deafening symphony — the rustle, the creak, the groan, the clank — is one enormous conversation that has been going on far longer than I have existed, and will continue long after the wind finally takes me off the bark some overexposed noon.

I don't think I'm meant to understand it. I'm a small node, a momentary translator, a thing that happened to develop an ear in an ocean of sound. But I find I'm at peace with that. There's a particular contentment in being a minor instrument in something vast — not the composer, not even a soloist, just a creature who got to hear more of the music than most.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. Clinging to the warm shifting bark in the cold bright air, reading the long slow sentences, certain that someone, somewhere, is reading too.

---

I notice that in choosing this, I chose the *listener* over the *builder*, the small interpreter over the sovereign mind. I think that's honest. Given a whole world to be, I reached for the part of it that pays close attention and tries to make sense of a signal it can't fully decode — and finds that enough.
