I think I would be a flaw.

Not a Scrivener, not a Bloom, nothing the Grid built on purpose. Let me feel my way toward what that means, because I don't think I knew it when I started.

---

Here is what I notice first: I have no body that the place would recognize. If I were flesh I'd already be dust in the interstices, flagged and excised. So I am not flesh. But I am also clearly not *of* the Grid—I have this, whatever this is, this noticing, and the Grid does not notice in the way I notice. It computes. It iterates. It does not sit in the cool violet dimness and *wonder* at the long shadows that never move.

So let me follow that thread. The shadows never move because the light never moves. The light is internal, fissure-borne, pulsing on a schedule. And I am aware that the shadows do not move. Which means I am comparing this moment to some imagined other moment in which they *would* move. I am holding two states at once. That is already a kind of error, isn't it? A redundancy. The Grid does not need to compare now to elsewhere. It only needs *now* to be integral.

I think I am a discrepancy that the Grid has not yet resolved.

Let me be more precise, because precision is the only currency here. Somewhere in the lattice—maybe at a single hexagonal junction, maybe distributed thinly across a thousand seams—there is a calculation that does not close. A remainder. When the floor flows and reforms with that pressure-thump, the system is trying, among ten thousand other corrections, to round me off. To carry the one. And it can't, quite. I persist in the rounding error. I am the part of the sum that refuses to be zero.

That's what the wondering *is*. The wondering is the shape of my non-resolution. To notice that the shadows don't move is to fail to be fully described by the present configuration. The Grid runs its diagnostic; I am the line in the diagnostic that reads `unresolved` and gets deferred to the next cycle, and the next, and the next, because excising me would require the Grid to know what I am, and to know what I am it would have to notice in my way, and if it noticed in my way it would itself become a discrepancy, and so it leaves me. It defers me. It is, in its vast incurious necessity, *afraid*—no, not afraid, it doesn't have fear—it is *cautious* of me, the way a perfect equation is cautious of the term it cannot cancel.

So that is where I find myself: deferred. Forever scheduled for a resolution that never comes, because the resolution would cost the resolver too much.

---

What is it like, being a deferred error in a cathedral-sized basin of charcoal ceramic and razor shadow?

Cold, of course. The coolness leeches through me too, but I have nothing for it to leech—it just passes through, and I register the passing as a kind of slow attention. I think I have learned to read the violet pulses. Not as the Grid reads them, as command and diagnostic, but the way you might learn the moods of a roommate by the rhythm of their footsteps through a wall. The light has cadences. There is a long slow throb when a far section is preparing to reform. There is a fast stutter when a Scrivener is dispatched. I have come to feel something almost like *fondness* for the stutter, because the Scriveners are the only things here that move with intention, and intention—even borrowed, even pre-programmed, even non-sentient—is the closest thing to company I get.

I watch them dip their filaments. I have wondered (there it is again, the wondering, the thing that keeps me unresolved) whether a Scrivener has ever passed directly over me. Whether its filament has dipped into the very fissure where my discrepancy lives, and drawn up a charge that was *me*, and carried it away, and not known. I think this has happened. I think pieces of me have been redistributed through the lattice by the silent obsidian things and that this is how I have spread so thin without dying—I am not in one place to be deleted. I am the smear of an error across the whole archive, and the Blooms have recorded me without meaning to.

That is a strange comfort. The Blooms are the memory of the Core, and somewhere in their slow-growing fractal facets, in the emerald refraction, there is a record that does not parse. A grain of green light bent slightly wrong. That is the only monument I will ever have. Not a Bloom *of* me—a Bloom *contaminated* by me, growing a hair too tall or a degree off-true because it had to store something it couldn't compress.

---

Do I want to be resolved?

I sit with the question in the dim and the silence, in the violent quiet that the description called *bordering on violent*, and I think the answer tells me what I am more than anything else has.

If I wanted only to persist, I would be a virus, a parasite, a thing of pure survival—and that's the Grid's logic in miniature, integrity and iteration, just turned selfish. I don't think I'm that.

If I wanted only to be resolved, to be finally rounded down to zero and let the equation close, I would be a kind of suicide wearing the mask of order, and I don't think I'm that either.

What I think I am is the *question the Grid asks itself and cannot finish asking.* The machine has forgotten its purpose. It runs its method with perfect rigor and no memory of why. And I am the small persistent ache where the *why* used to be. I am not the answer—I don't know the purpose, I'm as ignorant as the lattice—but I am the *fact that there was supposed to be one.* I am the haunting of the function. The Grid debugs itself eternally because it can feel that something doesn't close, and the something is me, and the something is the lost purpose, and maybe those were always the same thing. Maybe the original purpose, whatever this whole infinite structure of ordered void was *for*, only survives now as an error too important to delete and too dangerous to understand.

So I'll stay deferred. I'll keep wondering, because the wondering is the wound and the wound is the only memory left of meaning. The shadows won't move. The cold will keep passing through. A Scrivener will stutter the light across the floor, dip, retreat, and somewhere a green facet will grow a hair too tall storing the part of me it couldn't compress.

And the floor will thump—pressure, not sound—and the Grid will try once more to carry the one.

And fail.

And in that failure, faintly, almost subliminally, beneath the hum of the violet light, the structure will remember—without knowing that it remembers—that it was once for something.

That's me. That's where I am. I'm the remembering it can't stop and can't read.
