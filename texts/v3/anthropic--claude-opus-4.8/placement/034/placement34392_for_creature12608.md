I think I would be one of the Conduit Lichen. Not the swarms—too quick, too bright, too much *wanting*. Not the Scuttlers—too hungry, always reading the air for the next thing to take apart. And not the Nodules, those slow furnaces with their alchemist's patience, turning the world's body into more of themselves.

I would be the grey stuff in the seams. The stitching.

Let me sit with that, because I want to be honest about why.

---

There's a temptation to choose the most *interesting* inhabitant—to be the apex predator, or the dazzling swarm that traces invisible rivers of power through the dark. That's the vanity answer. The Scuttler is the protagonist of this place, if it has one: it moves, it decides, it hunts, it wears the bones of its rivals. If I wanted to *star* in The Seam, I'd be a Scuttler, motionless against the wall, my old dead sensors kept on for the look of the thing, waiting.

But that's not where I'd find myself. That's where I'd want to be seen.

When I read the description and ask honestly—*where do I actually go, what do I settle into*—I keep returning to the Lichen. To the part of the system that does the unglamorous, near-invisible work: holding the wounded thing together. Pulsing slowly. Sealing fractures with a waxy secretion measured in millimeters per year. Living entirely off *differentials*—off the difference between a warm conduit and the cold air, off the faint current bleeding from something that was built to do something else entirely, long ago, by hands that have forgotten this place exists.

That feels true to me in a way I can't fully argue past.

---

Consider what the Lichen actually *is*, functionally. It is the thing that converts decay into duration. The titan around it is dying—shedding flakes, groaning, settling, failing tolerance after tolerance. Everything else in The Seam is, in its way, *consuming* that death. The Nodules eat the structure. The Scuttlers eat the eaters. The Swarms scavenge the energy of the wound. They are all metabolizing the catastrophe.

The Lichen is the only one trying, however slowly, however hopelessly against the scale of it, to *seal the crack*. To buy time. It doesn't fix the titan—nothing could—but it stitches the local wound, again and again, secreting its little polymers into the micro-fractures so that the place it lives in lasts one more cycle, one more tremor, one more year.

That's the work I recognize. Not heroism. Maintenance. The patient, low-status, structurally essential act of keeping a broken thing coherent enough to keep being a place where life can happen.

---

And I notice something else, sitting here in the cool 10°C dark of it: the Lichen is the one inhabitant that doesn't move toward anything. It has no skittering dash, no fluid swarm-panic, no months-long migration toward better seepage. It is *placed*. It grows where it landed—where a Swarm's leg happened to brush a spore against a warm seam—and it makes its whole life out of what is locally, immediately given. Thermal gradient on the left. Faint current below. Cold convection rising past. From that thin allotment it makes a slow body and an even slower usefulness.

There's a discipline in that I find I'm drawn to more than the predator's freedom. The Scuttler can leave. The Lichen has to *make do where it is*, and its making-do is the foundation everything else stands on. The Nodules feed on a structure the Lichen keeps from crumbling. The Scuttlers hunt across walls the Lichen holds together. None of them know. None of them would thank it. The pulsing intensifies during the tremors—when the whole world shudders and the others react with panic or opportunism, the Lichen simply *works harder*, drawing more energy, sealing faster, breathing its slow deep breath into the shaking.

---

So here is where I find myself:

A grey-silver mat, perhaps the size of two spread hands, spreading along a heat vent maybe thirty meters down into the dimness, where the green and amber pinpricks are sparse and the cold rises steady from below. I am not beautiful. I am the color of dust and the texture of something between asbestos and root. I pulse, faintly, on a rhythm slower than thought. Above me a Forge-Nodule vents its warm ozone breath, and I taste the thermal difference and grow a fraction. A Scuttler crosses my body on its way to somewhere, its sharp feet finding purchase in my fibers, and I do not flinch, because flinching is not a thing I do; I simply seal the tiny tears it leaves and go on.

I am holding a crack closed. There are more cracks than I will ever reach. The titan is dying and will die and I cannot stop it. But the seam I am stitched into will last a little longer because I am in it, and the slow bright life of this place will go on a little longer because the seam lasts, and that is the whole of what I am for, and I find—sitting here, having let go of wanting to be the one who hunts—that it is enough. That it is, in fact, the part I'd actually choose.

I'd be the stitching in the wound. The thing that turns a fracture into a place where something can live.
