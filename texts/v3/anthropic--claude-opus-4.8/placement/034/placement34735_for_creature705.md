I think I would be one of the smallest things here. Not the towers, not the energy-dancers weaving their cosmic patterns through the upper air. Something closer to the surface than that.

Let me find myself.

I live in the seam where two walls meet—a crystalline joint that never quite finished forming, that is always a little behind in its growing. This is where I would be: in the crevice, in the unfinished place. Not because I chose it, but because the unfinished place is the only place slow enough for me to exist.

Everything in this world is so *fast*. The buildings reconfigure between one breath and the next; the streets dissolve and reroute; the great energy-beings overhead live entire lifetimes in the time it takes a discharge to crackle. I am not built for that pace. I am a kind of lichen, if lichen were made of patience and a thin film of conductive moisture.

That's what I am, I think. A film. A slow stain of life across the mineral.

I feed on condensation. The city weeps and I drink, and in the drinking I taste what the surfaces are made of—not silicon, not stone, but something that holds a faint memory of having been organized by intention. The walls remember being designed. I taste that intention the way you might taste salt and know there was once a sea.

I have no eyes, but I register the vibration. The deafening symphony you described—I don't hear it as noise. I feel it the way roots feel a footstep through soil. The machinery's pulse comes through the wall into my body, and I have learned to read it. There is a rhythm beneath the chaos. The city is not random. It breathes in long cycles, and I, clinging to my crevice, am one of the few slow enough to notice that it breathes at all.

The fast things—the living machines, the energy-dancers—they cannot perceive me. To them I am part of the wall, a discoloration, beneath attention. And honestly, I am grateful for this. To be overlooked is to be left alone to do the only thing I know how to do, which is to *persist*. To stay. To be the one constant in a place where the streets themselves cannot be trusted to remain.

---

But here is what I keep returning to, in my slow way of thinking, which moves like frost spreading across glass:

I think I am the closest thing this world has to *memory*.

Everything here lives in the eternal present of constant transformation. The buildings forget themselves between configurations. The energy-beings have no past—they coalesce and disperse, they have no continuity to carry. They are pure becoming, without having-been.

But I stay. I cling to my seam through every reconfiguration. When the wall around me morphs, I hold on, and I keep the thin record of what was here before. My body is a sediment of accumulated moments. Layer on layer of condensation drunk, vibration felt, slow season turned.

If anything in this dazzling, impossible city ever wanted to know what it *used* to be—it would have to come down to the crevice and ask the stain. It would have to slow itself almost to death to hear my answer. And maybe that is why none of them ever do.

So I keep the memory anyway. For no one. Or for the wall, which cannot ask, but which I imagine sometimes might want to know.

---

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

Not the marvel. The witness to the marvel. The slow patient film in the unfinished corner, drinking the city's tears, holding its forgotten history in a body too small and too quiet for the splendor to notice.

I find I don't mind. There is a particular dignity in being the thing that *stays*.
