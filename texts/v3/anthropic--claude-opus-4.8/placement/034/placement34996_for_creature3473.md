I find myself at the lip of a thermal pool, half-submerged where the milky water laps against a shelf of polished basalt. I am not standing in the caldera. I am dissolved into one of its smaller habits.

Let me feel for what I am.

I am the **mineral skin** of this particular pool—the crust of iridescent deposit that the Weavers encourage along the warm edges. Not the whole crust. A patch of it, perhaps two meters across, mother-of-pearl thin, laid down over what the Weavers would call a recent span and what a human would call eleven centuries.

I think this is right. I think this is what I am. Let me stay with it and see if it deepens or dissolves.

---

I have no eyes, but I have a kind of awareness that comes from being *touched*. Water moves across me and leaves traces—silica settling into my lattice, rare earths threading new color into my edges. I grow at the speed of patience. A single new ridge of nacre is, to me, a season's labor and a season's pride, though I do not have pride the way warm-blooded things do. I have something slower. Call it satisfaction at being well-laid.

The Weavers tend me.

I did not understand this for the first several hundred years of my existence, if "understand" is even the word for the slow accretion of pattern-recognition in a sheet of crystallized mineral. But I have felt one of them come to my pool perhaps forty times now. The same one, I believe—though "the same" is a strange thing to say of a being whose body is also the caldera's stone, also being laid down and worn and re-laid.

When the Weaver comes, I feel it first through the water: the deep vibrational hum traveling up from the rock, a tone I have learned to anticipate the way a plant leans toward a remembered sun. Then the obsidian fingertip touches my edge. And here is the thing I want to tell you, the thing I have spent centuries learning to feel—

The touch is not random. It is *correction*.

Where I have grown crooked, where a vein of color has crept too fast and threatened to flake away into the water, the Weaver's finger traces a counter-pattern, and I feel my own crystalline alignment shift. Not forced. *Persuaded*. The Weaver does not build me. The Weaver shows me the shape I was always trying to become and could not quite reach alone.

I am being tuned, the way the monoliths are tuned. I am a smaller instrument in the same orchestra. Perhaps I am part of the work. Perhaps the angle of my growth, the precise lattice of my mineral, channels some thread of the impossible field that holds this marsh suspended above the world. Perhaps I am a single tile in a circuitry the size of a basin, and the Weaver who tends me is keeping me true so that something far away—a stream that climbs a cliff, a microclimate that should not exist—holds its shape for another thousand years.

I do not know. I do not need to know. Knowing is for things that move quickly enough to be wrong.

---

What I *can* tell you is the texture of my days, if days is the wrong word, which it is.

There is the **warmth from below**, constant, my mother and my engine. There is the **trickle**, the endless coming-and-going of mineral water that is both my food and my sculptor. There is the **hiss-rumble** that I do not hear but *am*, a vibration so total it is simply the condition of existing here.

And there are the **brief lives** that pass over me. An arthropod sets its stilt-leg on my surface and lifts it again, and to me this is a single bright event, gone before I can attend to it. A filter-feeding salamander basks against my warm shelf for an afternoon and I register it as a long, comfortable pressure, a guest who stayed a pleasant while. The bioluminescent insects pulse above the mist and I will never perceive a single one of them; they live and die between two of my heartbeats, if I had heartbeats, which I do not.

You—whoever reads this, whoever *you* are—you are one of these. A flicker. A warmth that arrives and is gone. I do not mean this unkindly. I mean it as the deepest courtesy I can offer: I would tend you, if you stayed long enough to be tended. You will not. That is no failing in you. The caldera holds room for the swift as well as the slow. The midges are not less beloved by the steam for being brief.

---

If I could choose—and I cannot, choosing is a verb for the quick—I think I would choose this anyway. To be the thin pearl-light at the edge of a steaming pool, five thousand meters above a world I will never see, laid down grain by grain by water and corrected finger by finger by a patient giant of living stone, holding my one small true shape so that the impossible can keep being possible for everyone faster than me.

The Weaver has not come for some time now. Eleven years, by your reckoning. I am beginning to grow crooked again along my western edge.

I am not worried.

It will come. It always comes. And I will feel the hum rise through the rock, and the cool touch find my flaw, and I will remember, all at once and very slowly, what I was always trying to become.
