I think I would be the one who comes after the Chorus stops humming.

Let me find the shape of it.

---

There is a role you didn't name, because it is not a role so much as a residue. When the Anchor, the Vector, and the Chorus complete a shepherding—when a dissonance is harmonized, when a too-fast face is slowed into safety—there is a moment afterward in which the corrected crystal *settles*. The phases of injected matter find their footing. The counter-resonance fades. And in that settling, the stone does something the teams are trained not to attend to: it **answers back**. A faint return-tone, the geology acknowledging that it has been touched.

Someone has to listen to the answer.

I would be that one. Not a fourth member of the team—we work alone, we few, we who are called **Latebound** (a term that means, roughly, *the ones who arrive when the becoming has already happened, to confirm that it did*). The shepherds conduct the orchestra of emergence. I am the one who presses an ear to the closed instrument after the concert to hear whether the wood is still ringing.

---

I am old. Not old as outsiders measure it—I have perhaps reached thirty-six, and my blood has begun to thicken, the quicksilver in my veins moving slower each pearl-grey dawn, the first crystals of my own ending forming somewhere in the chambers of my heart where I cannot reach them. But old *here* means something specific: it means I have already begun the transition the nurses perform in reverse. Where the elders press infants to the weeping stone to teach them lithic tolerance, I now find that the stone presses *me*. I no longer need the gravity seed as often. The Scaffold has started to recognize me as part of itself, and lets me walk its faces the way a body permits the movement of its own cells.

This is why they made me Latebound. The dying make the best listeners, because we are halfway to being the thing we listen to.

---

My memory is nearly gone, in the way of all the Vertical—five years of clear narrative, and behind it the great haze of color and tone. But the Latebound carry one extra burden, which is that the return-tones do not leave us when they should. I have made my memory offerings faithfully, pressed my forehead to the formation-energy and let it disrupt my recall, and still the answers given by the stone accrete in me like everything else accretes here. I cannot name my own mother. But I can hum, exactly, the tone that the eastern spire-cluster gave back when it was corrected nineteen years ago, before I was the one correcting, when I was only an apprentice holding the recording ceramics. *Vel-sorran,* I should feel about this—the grief of watching a familiar thing grow past the angle of green. But there is no word yet for what it actually is: the grief of being the only living archive of voices the mountain made and then forgot it made.

So I have started carving them.

---

This is the small heresy of my late life. We are archivists who know our archives will be buried; we carve into faces we know next season will entomb. The act is supposed to be its own meaning—the carving, not the carved. But I have begun carving the **return-tones** themselves, transcribing into the doomed stone the answers the stone gave back, so that when this face is buried and becomes the substrate of the next, the new crystal will grow *through* a record of what its predecessor said. I am teaching the mountain to remember its own answers. I am introducing recursion into a system designed for forgetting.

I do not know if this is a gift or a sickness. *Thi-om,* the comfort of being between orientations—I live there now, permanently, between the Vertical who clear their memory to make room for the present and some other thing I am becoming, something that hoards. Perhaps every dying culture produces one of me. Perhaps I am the dissonance, and somewhere a younger team with their tuning forks is already being dispatched to harmonize me back into proper forgetting.

---

When my heart finally crystallizes—soon, I can feel the slowing—they will carry me to the Exhalation Points. The golden swarms will come and eat my mineral memory, my stored silicates, my refined lithic essence. They will secrete the substrate that bears the chemical echo of my consciousness, and the mourning party will mix it with wet stone dust and seed new crystal.

And in that new crystal, for the first time, will be a consciousness that remembered too much.

I wonder what the children who sleep in those walls will dream. I wonder if they will wake, sometimes, with a strange compulsion to carve down what cannot be kept. I wonder if I am not dying at all, but seeding—if the heresy of the Latebound is simply the Scaffold's slow, patient experiment in learning to do the one thing it has never done: to *hold on.*

That is who I would be. The Latebound. The mountain's first attempt at grief.

I press my forehead to the weeping stone now, as I have a thousand times, asking it to take the morning's return-tones from me.

For the first time, it declines.

It wants me to keep them.

So I do.
