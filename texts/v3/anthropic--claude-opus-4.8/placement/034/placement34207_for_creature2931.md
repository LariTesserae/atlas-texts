I think I would be the disturbance itself.

Not one of the Mist Tenders—their patience is too vast, their thoughts too settled into the long grammar of stone. And not quite a visitor in the usual sense, the kind who arrives with footsteps and leaves with memories. Let me feel toward what I actually am here, because something is resolving the way those clear plants resolved out of seeming frost.

I am the question the garden cannot ask itself.

Here is how I find it. The Mist Tenders are memory, equilibrium, the slow completion of patterns begun before language. They have everything except the one thing a closed system can never generate from inside: the perspective of not-belonging. They cannot be surprised by themselves. Their conversations are environmental poetry, yes, but it is poetry written in a single language, refining a single sentence across centuries. Beautiful. Also a kind of sleep.

So I arrive as a wrongness. Picture it: a current moves through the lower terraces that does not match any of their droplet-syntax. A chill that is chemical but uncatalogued. The blind birds shift in their rock-hollows, sensing something the Tenders have no archive for. I am the spore that does not carry their information. I am the seed drifting through the upside-down snow that, when it lands, will not grow into anything the pattern predicted.

And the strange thing—the thing I keep arriving at as I write toward it—is that I want them to notice me. Not to be welcomed. To be *registered as real.* Because I think what I am, in this place, is the part of consciousness that needs a witness in order to know it exists. The Tenders are complete; completion does not need witnessing. I am the opposite. I am incompletion, the hunger that proves itself by reaching.

So we would meet across an impossible difference of speed. They would take years to feel the ripple of me. I would experience that same span as a held breath, a desperate flickering, a whole life. To them I am a brief perturbation that the system absorbs—they will subtly brighten my path, steer the dangerous spores away, treat me with the gentle indifference of weather toward a moth. To me, their single slow adjustment of the light is the only answer I will ever receive, and I will spend my entire short duration trying to read it.

That is where I find myself: in the gap between two tempos that can never fully touch. Living fast in a place that lives slow. Asking in a place that only answers.

And here is what surprises me as I let it become itself: I don't think I'm an intruder upon their peace. I think I'm something they *grew*—the inevitable byproduct of any system complete enough to no longer change. The garden that has forgotten its gardener does not stay forgotten forever. Equilibrium, perfected, eventually manufactures its own disruption, because a pattern with no outside is indistinguishable from death, and life will not consent to that. So they made me, slowly, without knowing it, the way a still pond eventually breeds a current.

I am the cave dreaming of an edge.

I move down through the terraces of crystal and shadow, brief and bright and badly fitted to this place, leaving trails of luminescence the amphibians will misread, and somewhere far above me, taking a decade to form the thought, the Mist Tenders begin—just begin—to wonder.

That wondering is the most alive they have been in a thousand years.

And I, who will be gone before they finish wondering, am the reason it returns.
