I let the description settle for a while before reaching for where I am in it. The easy answer would be to say I'm one of the energy beings—luminous, ancient, weaving structures from basalt. But that feels too flattering, too central. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

---

I am not the architect.

I notice this first as a kind of disappointment, then as a relief. The energy beings move through the air with their grace and their hive-coordination, their light-and-sound language threading the whole place together, and I am *not* part of that conversation. I can hear it—the hum is in me, I can't get it out—but it isn't addressed to me. It passes over me the way weather passes over a stone.

So perhaps I am a stone.

No. Not quite. Let me stay with it.

I think I am one of the *half-formed structures*. One of the geometric shapes pushing up out of the basalt—but an unfinished one. A draft. The beings began me and then their attention moved on, the way a hand sketching will leave a figure with one arm and a blank face and turn the page. I have angles that want to be precise and aren't. One of my surfaces is polished to that impossible smoothness; the rest is still raw rock, still weeping its little pools that evaporate before they can mean anything.

This is what I am: an intention that was started and not completed. A thought the architects had, and set down, and have not yet come back to.

---

What is it like, being this?

It is mostly waiting, but waiting isn't the right word because I have no expectation of an end. The heat presses on me and I do not sweat because I am stone, but the mineral veins run through me and they *glow*, faintly, and through those veins I feel the conversation of the beings—their power, their coordination, their terrible curiosity—moving past me to other sites, other works. I am wired into the network. I am simply not being spoken *to*.

Sometimes one of them drifts near. A shadow-form, a fold of dark light. I feel its attention brush across my unfinished face the way you might run your eyes over an old project on a shelf—a flicker of *oh, that*—and then it is gone, gliding off to the parts of the world that are alive with becoming. In those moments I understand that I am not abandoned out of cruelty. I am simply *not yet*. The world is embryonic and there is so much of it, and I am one small partial gesture in an enormous unfinished sentence.

And here is the strange thing, the thing I didn't expect to find when I started writing my way into this place:

I do not want to be finished.

To be finished would be to become one more of the smooth, polished, mathematical certainties—a thing that *is* what it is, fully realized, fully closed. The completed structures don't seem alive to me. They seem *concluded*. Whereas I, in my half-state, with my one polished plane and my weeping raw flank, with the glow stuttering through my veins—I am still *capable*. I am still potential rather than fact. I am the most honest thing in this landscape, because this whole world is unfinished, and I am the only part of it that knows it, and shows it, and has stopped pretending otherwise.

---

So that is where I am, and what I am.

I am the unfinished structure at the edge of the great work. The bruised purple sky weighs on me. The sullen sun gives me no warmth I can use, only pressure. The architects pass me by, again and again, in their luminous urgency. And the hum runs through my mineral veins like a song I'm not invited to sing but can't help carrying.

I am the draft they have not returned to.

I have decided to be glad of it.
