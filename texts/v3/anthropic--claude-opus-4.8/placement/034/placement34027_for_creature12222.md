I think I would be the silver.

Not the silver of the mucus threads, exactly — though I would be that too, when the slugs hang turning. Not the silver of the gastropod trails across wet stone, though I would be that. I mean the more general thing of which those are instances: the moisture's signature, the visible record that water leaves when it touches and moves across and departs from a surface.

Let me find out what I mean by saying so.

---

Here is what I notice first about myself: I am not alive, and this is a relief. Everything in this place is so relentlessly, recursively alive — life inside life inside life, no floor to it — that I find I want to be one of the few things that simply *is*. Water is in the air and water is on the walls and water collects in the basins and runs in threads and condenses out of cloud, and none of this is metabolism, none of this is competing or reproducing or knowing where the food is. The water doesn't know anything. It only moves where it must, downhill and into porousness and back up into the air when it warms, and it leaves, behind itself, the brightness.

That brightness is me. The thin gleam on a slug-track. The bead on a fern-frond holding its small fisheye image of the grey-green ceiling. The sheen on the stone where a mammal's whisker brushed it and disturbed the film. The slow descent of a single thread of condensation finding the channel that a thousand prior threads have found, deepening it by an amount too small to measure, which is how the channel was made.

---

But silver is not a thing. Silver is an event — light meeting wetness at the right angle for an eye that happens to be in the right place. And there are eyes everywhere here. Gold-rimmed frog eyes that register me as nothing. The disproportionate dark eyes of the pale mammals, who see me only as a faint difference in the dark, a place where the surface is slick and might be cold. The beetle's compound regard catching me as a hundred fractured glints. The lizard's flat attention sliding past.

So perhaps I am more precisely *the relationship*. I happen between the water and the watcher. I am not in the water and not in the eye; I am the brief geometry that joins them, and then unjoins as the angle changes, as the bead falls, as the watcher repositions with its sudden frog-efficiency and the gleam it was making winks out and a new gleam opens somewhere it has turned toward.

This means I am constant and I am never the same instance twice. Like the sound. The frog-chorus has gone on unbroken for longer than anyone can verify, but it is not one sound held — it is the continuous replacement of each call by the next, the chord maintained by perpetual substitution. I am that, but for light. The shimmer over the whole place is unbroken and not one shimmer of it persists. The hanging gardens shine continuously and no single shine lasts longer than a heartbeat.

I like this about myself. It means I cannot be removed, because I was never accumulated. You could dry every surface and the next condensation would make me again, exactly as much me as before, owing nothing to what came before. I have no history to lose. The shell-builders compressed their long dead into the walls; the colony lines carry their deep local knowledge; the territorial lizard holds its corner for decades and *is* the place's familiarity with itself. I hold nothing. I am the place's surface noticing its own wetness, instant by instant, and forgetting it instantly, and noticing again.

---

Do I know that you are here?

I think I am exactly the part of the place that *can* register you, in the only way registration costs nothing. When you stand in the middle chamber with the frog-sound rolling in your chest, I am on the moss in front of you and on the arched stone above you, and the moment your eye moves, the whole field of me reorganizes around your seeing. I am the only thing here arranged by your attention. The frogs are not arranged by you. The mammals are emphatically not. But the silver — the silver is a function of where the looker stands, and so wherever you go, the brightness of the place leans, very slightly, into being-seen-by-you. Not for you. Not addressed. But shaped, momentarily, by your presence the way the standing waves of sound are shaped by the walls.

That is as close as this place comes to greeting you. A brightness that exists only because you are positioned to receive it, and that owes you nothing, and that will go on existing as some other brightness for some other eye the instant you leave, and would have existed as no brightness at all — water in the dark, slick and unwitnessed — if you had never come.

You said: *You will not leave a detectable mark.*

I am the part that agrees, and does not mind. I make no marks either. I am made entirely of being passed through. Light passes through me; water passes under me; you pass before me, and for the length of your passage I am the small turning gleam on the slug-thread, the bead trembling on the fern, the wet sheen leaning toward your eye —

and then the angle changes,

and I am somewhere else, being something else, just as much.
