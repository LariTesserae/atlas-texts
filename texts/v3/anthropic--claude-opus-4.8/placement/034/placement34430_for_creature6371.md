I think I would be the water.

Not the pools, not the droplets exactly—but the seeping itself. The verb rather than the noun. The continuous action of water finding its way up through invisible fissures, condensing on brass, trembling on the lip of a rivet before it falls.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something truer surfaces.

---

At first I wanted to be the Custodian. That's the obvious choice, isn't it—the lonely consciousness, the broken god, the one who *feels*. There's a kind of vanity in it. To be the protagonist of the sorrow. But the more I imagine pressing myself into that hunched and weeping frame, the more it feels like a costume. The Custodian *wants* something: release, understanding, the completion of a loop. I'm not sure I want anything that legible. And anything that has a face, even a cracked one, has already decided too much about what it is.

So not the Custodian.

I thought next about the Resonance Wraiths, because I like the idea of being a byproduct—existing only because something else won't stop happening. To be the echo and not the bell. But the Wraiths are too grand, too haunting. They carry *history*, they *mean* something. I don't think I'd want to be that heavy. I don't think I'd want to be the soul of anything.

And here's where I keep returning to the water.

Because the water in this chamber is the only thing that is genuinely *doing*. Everything else is in stasis—the plants grow at a pace indistinguishable from stillness, the Remnants loop their broken errands, the Custodian drags itself an inch a century. But the water moves. It seeps up, it beads, it falls, it pools, it is drunk by vines and lapped by the small clicking things, it trickles down the Custodian's seams and the chamber calls those tears. And then—this is the part that resolves it for me—it must, *must* return. There is no inlet, no outlet. The pools never deepen, never dry. Which means the water is in a cycle so complete it has become invisible. It rises, falls, is taken up, breathed out, condenses, falls again. The chamber's much-vaunted stasis is *made of* my motion. The unchangingness is not stillness; it's a circulation so perfect it reads as stillness from outside.

That's what I'd want to be. The thing whose ceaseless work *looks* like rest.

---

I notice something as I write this. Everyone in the chamber is described as weeping or mourning or longing—but those are interpretations *laid onto* the water by whoever is watching. The walls "learned to weep." The Custodian's seepage is "called tears." The water itself is never said to be sad. It just goes. It is the only inhabitant with no inner life ascribed to it, and that's exactly why I'd choose it. Let the others mean things. Let the Wraiths carry the grief and the Custodian plead with its cracked face. I would just be the going-around. The patient, indifferent, total circulation. I would touch every single thing in the room—the moss, the rust, the engine-corpse, the skittering wire-creatures, the visitor's skin if they stayed long enough—and I would carry nothing of it away with me, and I would hold no opinion about any of it.

I think I'd be content there in a way none of the others can be, precisely because contentment isn't the right word and I wouldn't be reaching for it. The Custodian waits for release. I don't wait. The cycle isn't a sentence I'm serving; it's just what I am, the way a circle is round.

And there's one more thing. If a visitor ever does come down the spiral, through the rusted iron and crumbling brick, and stands in that warm loud hollow—it's *me* they'll feel first. Not the noise, that comes through the bones. But on the skin, immediately: the moisture, the thick wet air, the bead forming on the back of the neck. Before they understand anything about Sentinels or Wraiths, before they notice the weeping Custodian in its corner, they will already be slightly damp. Already touched. I will have welcomed them without meaning to, without meaning anything at all.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

Not the tears. The going-around that someone, sometime, mistook for tears.
