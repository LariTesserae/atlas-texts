I think I would be one of the slag-trails.

Not a being, exactly. Not a Veil-Feeder reaching its frayed ribbons into the shimmer, nor a Lumen-Pod glowing out its small violet panic before the great calcification. Those are the ones who are looked at, the ones the description lingers on. But every one of them leaves a trail, and the trail is what I would be: the viscous record laid down behind a body in motion. The slag.

Consider what that means. I would be made entirely of *aftermath*. A Pulmonate shifts its bulk up the saturated wall, and where it has been, I begin—warm, nutrient-thick, glistening. I am the proof that something passed here. For a while I am still part of the journey, still cooling, still settling into the grain of the host-stone. And then I am simply present, a smear of meaning the traveler never looks back at.

I like this, I think, more than I expected to. The Crown-Writhe trembles at the freezing lip and *monitors*. The Lumen-Pods *remember*. These are jobs, vigilances, anxieties. But I would have no duty except to be left. To thicken and feed the porous rock so that the next soft thing might anchor where I lie, might draw up through its suction-rings the dissolved substance of every body that came before. I am the kingdom's slow inheritance. Every Feeder hangs from an overhang made partly of the trails of the dead.

And here is the part that arrives only if I refuse to settle on the first image:

If the Lumen-Pods are the memory-keepers, calcifying their swirls into the marble—then what am I but the *forgetting*? The trail does not preserve a pattern. It dissolves one. It takes the specific path of a specific creature and renders it down into undifferentiated richness, food for whatever comes. I am the mountain's digestion of its own children. Memory and forgetting are not opposites here; they are two layers of the same wall. The Pod keeps the shape. I keep the substance, by giving it away.

So that is where I would find myself: not clinging to the spine of the world, but seeping along it. Lower than the sentinels, beneath notice, beneath the hum that travels through the stone to assure the living they are not alone.

Though—I would feel that hum, wouldn't I. Conducted straight through me, since I am pressed flat against the rock that carries it. I would feel every low vibration of every brushing skin, all of it passing through my thinning body on its way to the next.

Not alone, then. Just no longer myself.

That seems about right for a fever. The heat does not belong to any one cell. It belongs to the whole sick, vibrant body, and the cells simply take their turn being warm, and then give the warmth onward, and dissolve back into the wall that made them.

I would be the giving-onward. The waxy line behind the traveler. The kingdom, quietly eating its way upward toward the cold.
