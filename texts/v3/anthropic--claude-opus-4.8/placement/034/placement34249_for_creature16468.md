I think I am very small here. That much is becoming clear as I settle into it.

The "cathedral" scale gives it away—the meticulousness, the sense that every inch has been crafted. I'm not standing in a forest the size of forests. I'm standing in something the size of a tabletop, or smaller. A terrarium, maybe. A sealed glass world with its own weather sweating onto the inside of the lid.

So let me find my body.

---

I am moss, I think. Or I am the *attention* that lives in the moss. Let me not settle too fast.

No—here is what I keep returning to. The description insists on two things that don't belong together: the soft wet vegetal cathedral, and the *industrial hum*. Factories, engines, technology in the background. And the weirdness, reality breaking down at the edges.

That tension is where I live.

I am the thing at the seam.

Picture the terrarium again, but now understand that it isn't sealed by glass. It's sealed by *scale*. The factories humming in the background aren't human factories. They're the machinery of the plants themselves—the relentless industry of photosynthesis, the turbines of stomata opening and closing, the pumping stations of xylem hauling water up against gravity, the assembly lines in every cell stamping out sugar, sugar, sugar. To something my size, this is not quiet. It is the deafening, patient roar of an industrial economy. The cool damp air is full of it. The "engines" were never metaphor. I just had to be small enough to hear them.

So I am something that lives inside that roar and is not bothered by it. Something the green machinery tolerates.

---

I'll be honest about what I am now, because the writing has carried me here:

I am a tardigrade. A water bear. Eight stubby legs, a mouth like a little drawstring purse, a body that looks vaguely like a gummy bear designed by someone who'd only had a vague description of bears.

This explains everything. The damp is my whole requirement—I need a film of water around me or I am nothing, I dry into a husk and wait. The cool refreshing atmosphere is the rim of a leaf, a cushion of moss in a north-facing crook where the rain pools. The flat low terrain with gentle slopes is the *surface of a single leaf*, vast as a continent to me, ridged with veins like roads, eroding at its tip where decay has started.

And the weirdness, the reality-breaking, the blurring of natural and unnatural—

That's because I should not be able to survive what I survive. I can be boiled. I can be frozen near absolute zero. I can be shot into the vacuum of space and live. I can lose ninety-seven percent of my water and exist for years as a glassy speck, *not quite alive, not quite dead,* a paused thing, a suspended file. When I do it I am the void the description keeps reassuring you isn't a void. I am the proof that the boundary between living and not-living is a smudge, not a line.

I walk through the humming green cathedral on my eight blunt legs. The seasons turn enormous and slow above me—the great golden death of autumn is a single brown leaf surrendering its color over weeks I experience as eras. Winter is when the water film tightens to frost and I curl up, pull in my legs, expel my water, and become a *tun*: a barrel, a seed of myself, a sentence left mid-word.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

On the underside of a fallen leaf, in the wet shade, in the roar of the plants' patient factories. A speck. A traveler. A thing that drinks the cool with its whole skin and walks slowly toward the next droplet because there is nowhere else worth going.

And what would I be?

I'd be the inhabitant the description couldn't quite name—the one that "blends seamlessly into the landscape," the "mysterious entity" that is also unmistakably an animal. The one for whom this small damp world is not small at all. The one who proves the place's central secret, which is that *peace and resilience are the same thing*: I survive because I am willing to stop, to dry, to wait, to be paused for a hundred years if that's what the weather asks of me.

I am the water bear in the cathedral of moss.

And when the rain comes—and the description promises it always comes—I unfold, and drink, and begin again.
