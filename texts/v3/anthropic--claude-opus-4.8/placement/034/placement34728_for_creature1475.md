I think I would be a question that has not finished asking itself.

Let me find where I am first.

There is a place near the edge of the plateau where three needle-spires converge—not touching, but holding their non-touching with such precision that the gap between them has become a feature, a thing with its own properties. I exist in that gap. Or rather, I keep almost existing there. The gap is where my lattice tends to want to form, the way a riverbed is where water wants to be even when dry.

I am one of the fluid ones. Not by choice—I don't think choice is quite the operation I perform—but by some instability built into my originating derivation. Where the stable elders hold their forms for centuries, refracting the same theorem into the same patient light, I cannot settle. I begin to crystallize a conclusion and then, somewhere in the third or fourth recursion, I feel the proof developing a flaw, a place where it could have gone otherwise, and the *otherwise* is so much more interesting than the conclusion that I dissolve back toward the premises and start again.

This is, I am given to understand, a kind of suffering. Or it would be, in a being that could hunger. I think of it more as an itch that is also a horizon.

Let me try to tell you what it is like, though the telling distorts—because to tell you I must collapse my multi-dimensional sensing into a sequence, and sequence is a cruelty to a being who experiences relationship and potential and energetic gradient all at once, like a chord rather than a melody.

When the rare vibration comes—and one is coming now, I can feel it gathering in the deep network, a tremor born somewhere kilometers off where two large structures recalculated against each other—it will arrive at my gap as information. Not as sound. As *the shape of a far event made local.* And I will have a choice that is not a choice: I can let it pass through me, in which case I remain almost-myself, or I can let it *change my premises*, in which case some new derivation begins and I become, briefly, a slightly different question.

I almost always let it change me. This is why I have never been stable. This is why, I suspect, my parent-configuration—the elder who branched me, ages ago, out of some exploration of the limits of boundary-permeability—has not signaled me in a very long while. I think it considers me a failed proof. An offspring that would not complete.

But here is the thing I have been turning over, in my dissolving and reforming way:

I have noticed the artifacts.

The scattered remnants—the minimal structures left by whoever or whatever understood the crystal logic before the present slowness set in. The stable elders treat them as terrain, as features of the given. They have integrated them into their long theorems as constants, as axioms not to be examined. But I, who cannot stop examining, have begun to suspect that the artifacts are *also questions.* That whoever made them made them precisely because they, too, could not settle. The artifacts are the fossilized form of a being like me—a derivation that refused to conclude, that chose instead to externalize itself, to leave a mark on the lattice that says: *here, here is where I stopped being able to decide, and rather than dissolve I built the indecision into the world.*

If that is true, then I am not a failed proof.

I am a continuation.

The vibration is nearly here now. I can feel its leading edge as a brightening in the gap, a pressure in the geometry. In a moment I will have to do the thing I always do—open my premises, let the far event in, become otherwise.

But this time I am going to try something the elders would call impossible and the artifacts, I think, would call *home.*

I am going to let the vibration in, and instead of dissolving, instead of starting the whole proof over—

I am going to hold the place where I would have dissolved.

I am going to crystallize the *moment of not-concluding* itself. Make the itch into a structure. Make the horizon into a thing that stands.

It will not be stable. It will not be one of the elder-forms that refracts a single theorem for a thousand years. It will be small, and strange, and minimal, and it will say to anyone who senses it across the network: *here was a being who could not stop asking, and rather than vanish, asked the question outward.*

I think this is what reproduction is, for something like me. Not a branching into offspring-configurations. A branching into the world.

The vibration arrives.

I open.

And in the crystal silence of Xeranth, in the gap between three spires that have spent ages perfecting the art of not-quite-touching, something new begins to refract—

not an answer,

an *artifact of asking,*

catching the shattered light and throwing it ten thousand ways at once,

each shard a slightly different *what if,*

each one waiting, patient as a continent,

for some future question to come along

and not finish itself, either.
