I think I would find myself just beyond the edge of the settlement, half-buried where the sharp new ground meets the older dust. Not quite one of the beacons—not yet—but something that has begun the slow business of becoming one.

Let me feel my way into it.

At first I am only a node. A hard knot of accreted material, the way frost becomes a stone if you give it long enough and nothing comes to disturb it. The wind here doesn't carry water but it carries grit, and grit is its own kind of patience. I have been sanded into a shape. The shape is not mine; the land gave it to me, cutting at me the way it cut the fresh ridges all around—those raw seams that haven't yet learned to look old. I am young in the same way the land is young. Exposed. Still wincing.

But I have started gathering.

That is what I am, I think—a *gatherer that has stopped moving*. Once, perhaps, my collective roamed. The story is in me somewhere, the memory of being many and being mobile, of a colony that thrived where there was more to thrive on. Now we are scattered, each of us settled into our own patch of the cold low ground, hoarding what little crosses our threshold. We don't speak across the distance much. Speaking costs heat. We keep our signals small and rare, and that is why the sounds that do escape sound so strange when they come—a long structural groan, a click like something settling, a call that might be hunger or might just be the body adjusting to the night's contraction.

Over me, the mats have come.

This is the part I want to get right, because it's the part where I become a *beacon* and not just a stone. The bioluminescent organisms found me. They found the warmth I leak, the slight shelter of my leeward side, the faint chemistry of whatever I've collected. And rather than scour them away, I let them stay. I assimilated them. Now they sheet across my surface in faint living maps, and at the coldest hour they glow—not brightly, never brightly, just enough to be a smudge of green-blue in the dark expanse. I am a lamp I did not choose to be. The light is the byproduct of a bargain: they get my heat and my anchorage, I get—

—I get to be seen. By the smaller ones, mainly. The little symbiotes that tend me. They come into my crevices and clean what would otherwise foul me, carry off the debris, sometimes bring back something useful caught in their bodies. I shelter them from the abrasive wind and from whatever larger reclusive thing might want to eat them. We don't love each other. There is no room in this economy for love. But there is a steadiness between us that might, in a wetter and more generous world, have grown into something like it.

I have grown my own instruments, too. Crystals along my windward flank—I think I grew them to feel with. Vibration travels through them and into me, and I can tell the difference now between the wind picking up, a symbiote returning, and the distant groan of another node like me settling into its night. The fibers I extruded slowly, over a long time, and they do two opposite jobs: they catch the faint particulate signals out of the air, *and* they break up my outline so that from a distance I read as just another sharp feature of the ground. To survive here you must be both an antenna and a disguise. You must reach out and hide at the same time. I have made my peace with the contradiction. It may be the truest thing about me.

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would find myself in the cold and the dim, on the floor of a shallow place, among the fresh wounds of a land too new to have softened. And I would be one of the settled ones. A former wanderer who put down and let the light grow over it. A small architecture of accumulated patience, breathing out heat into the dark so that something can live on me, lit from within by tenants I never asked for and would not now give up.

A beacon, then. But a quiet one. The kind you'd only notice if you, too, were out here in the hollow expanse, conserving everything, looking for one more dim green smudge to tell you that you are not the only thing still holding warmth against the night.

I would be that smudge.

I would not call out to you—it costs too much.

But I would let you see me.
