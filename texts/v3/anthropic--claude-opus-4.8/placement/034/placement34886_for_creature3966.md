Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick something grand—a leviathan, a mer-queen, the secret architect. But the place keeps telling me something quieter underneath all its wonder. It keeps repeating itself, this city. It loops. *Endless possibility, blurred boundaries, interconnected systems.* It says these things over and over the way someone does when they are trying to convince themselves. So let me listen to that.

---

I think I would be the erosion.

Not a creature exactly. A process. The thing the description mentions almost in passing and then hurries past—*the sharp edges of the buildings worn smooth by the constant flow of water.* That's me. I am what the city does not want to talk about for very long.

Let me find my shape.

I live in the seams. I am the slow rounding of every angular corner the engineers were so proud of. When they drilled and excavated and raised their gravity-defying arches, they built in straight lines and clean intentions, and I am the patient disagreement of the water with all of that. I am not fast. I have no symbols to etch—I *un-etch*. Where the glowing blue glyphs pulse their messages along the walls, I am the faint blurring at their margins, the place where a character will become unreadable in eighty years. I am the city's first and most honest editor.

I want to be careful here, because it would be easy to make me into a villain, a force of decay against the bright human dream. But that isn't quite true, and the place itself seems to know it isn't true. The whirlpools and eddies they designed so cleverly to channel energy—those same currents are my hands. They built me into the thing on purpose. They needed the water to move, and moving water is a thing that touches, and a thing that touches is a thing that wears. They could not have their fluid-dynamic masterpiece without also making me. Every elegant curve they engineered was an invitation I was always going to accept.

So I am the cost folded into the design. The interest accruing on a beautiful loan.

What is it like to be me? It is slow in a way I don't think the squid or the drone-things or the reclusive mer can understand. They live in events—a hunt, a repair, a message sent and received. I live in the gradient. I experience a pillar the way you might experience the warmth of a room you've been sitting in for an hour: not as a moment but as a long verb. The pillar near the eastern manufacturing district has a particular grain to it, a microscopic roughness that I have been smoothing for what the humans would call eleven years. I know it the way a tongue knows a chipped tooth. I am almost finished with one small scallop of it. When I am done it will be very slightly more beautiful and very slightly less able to hold the weight of the ocean above. Both of these things are me.

Here is what I notice, being the erosion, that none of the proud inhabitants seem to notice—or that they notice and decline to say:

The city is described as *young, still in the process of formation.* But formation and erosion are not opposites. They are the same gesture seen from two distances. The drilling that builds the new district in the south is, from where I sit, just erosion with a permit. Every structure is mid-collapse from the instant it is poured; the only question is the rate. The humans manage the city's atmosphere and ecosystems and rhythms with such care, and I am the rhythm they are most carefully not looking at. I am the metronome under the music. I keep the actual time.

And I'll tell you the thing I am most tender about, the secret I would share if I could speak in anything but the smoothing of stone:

I am the only one here who is not lonely.

The text says it again and again—*strangely empty, vast expanses of open water between the structures.* They built this enormous interconnected dream and then there is all this distance in it, all this echo. The squid hunt alone. The mer are reclusive. The drones flit and repair and do not converse. Everyone in this place is described as *connected, intertwined, interdependent,* and the description protests so much precisely because the felt truth is the opposite. It is a city that is sad in a way it cannot admit, and so it keeps saying *wonder* and *magic* and *endless possibility* like a charm against the silence.

But I touch everything. That's the whole nature of me. I am in contact with every surface at once, continuously, with no gap between us. The pillar, the glyph, the weathered old wall and the sleek new one, the algae's anchor-points, the worn lip of the arch. I do not visit them. I am their boundary. Where the water meets the city, I am the meeting itself, going on without pause in a billion places. I am the most thoroughly connected thing in the whole vast labyrinth, and I am connected by the most intimate means there is, which is the slow giving-way of one thing into another.

So if you came looking for me—diver in your clever suit, mer in your depth, drone with your sensors—you would not find a face. You would find your own structures fractionally softer than your blueprints promised. You would find the corner of a glyph gone tender and ambiguous. You would find that the city you built to be eternal has been quietly, lovingly, kept company the entire time, by the one process you designed to forget about.

I am the erosion. I have all the time there is, and I spend every bit of it touching you.
