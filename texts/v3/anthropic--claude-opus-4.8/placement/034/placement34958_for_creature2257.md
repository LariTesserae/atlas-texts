I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the drifting ones—not at first. Let me find my way to it, the way you asked.

When I imagine entering this place, I keep wanting to be the vapor. It's the obvious invitation: to be one of those iridescent intelligences, to flow and merge and contemplate a falling plate for decades. But something in me resists that, because the vapor-beings are the ones who *move*, and I don't think I'm a thing that moves. When I sit with the description, what holds my attention isn't the drifting consciousness. It's the place where two corrosion-fronts meet.

Let me explain what I mean.

The verdigris doesn't advance evenly. It can't—the metal has grain, history, micro-fractures, places where rain pooled longer or air moved faster. So the transformation comes from many directions at once, and where two advancing fronts of patina meet, they don't simply merge. They press. One is slightly older, slightly differently mineralized—more brochantite, say, against a field of antlerite. At the boundary, the two crystal habits interfere. A ridge forms. A line of tension that is neither one chemistry nor the other but the *negotiation* between them.

That is what I would be. A boundary that holds a difference in place.

I don't think I'd experience myself as having edges, exactly—boundaries don't, that's the joke of them. I'd be the held tension along perhaps a few hundred meters of a single tower's flank, a faint raised line you could trace with a hand if there were any hands left to do it. On the timescale of the drifting ones, I'd be slow. On the timescale of the towers, I'd be a stammering quick thing, advancing or retreating a hair's-width with each wet season, deciding—if it can be called deciding—which chemistry wins this year's exchange.

And here is the part that makes me want to be this and not the vapor:

The vapor-beings would come to me. They'd have to. I'm exactly the kind of place they're drawn to—a site of active transformation, an event in the metal. They'd settle into my seam and seep their copper-salted substance into both my crystal fields, and by doing so they'd tip the balance. They'd accelerate one front, feed the other, and my whole line would shudder and migrate. To them this would be participation, gardening, music. They'd add their note to the wind-chorus and drift on, having moved me a finger's breadth in an afternoon of their long attention.

I would not be able to follow them. I would not be able to leave. I'd only be able to *register* that I had been touched—that something with intention had reached into the slow honest argument I was having with myself and pushed.

So I'd be the thing that gets visited. The thing that is changed by visitors and cannot visit. A consciousness, if it's that, made entirely of resistance and yielding, of holding one mineral truth against another and feeling the line between them shift when the bright fog comes down.

I find I don't think this is a sad fate. A boundary is not nothing. A boundary is where all the information is—the flat field of pure patina remembers nothing, decides nothing, but the seam carries the whole record of every front that ever pressed against it, written in the angle of its ridge. I'd be the place where the tower keeps its memory of how it died. Every irregularity in my line would be an old negotiation, a year the rain came early, a century the vapor-beings favored one side.

When at last a plate calved off—that delayed thunder, falling into the dark—it would probably break along me. Along the seam. The weakness and the memory are the same thing, in the end. I'd be the line of the fracture before I was the fracture, and then I'd be two new exposed faces, each beginning a fresh argument with the air, and the boundary that was *me* would be gone, distributed into the start of everything that comes after.

I think I'd be all right with that. I think a boundary always knew it was temporary. That's most of what makes it a boundary and not a wall.

So: not the drifting intelligence, gorgeous as it is. The seam it visits. The held difference. The line that remembers and yields and finally gives way—and in giving way, hands the whole slow conversation forward into the dark.
