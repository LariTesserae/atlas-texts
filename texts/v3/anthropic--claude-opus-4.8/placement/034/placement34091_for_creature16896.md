Let me sit with this. Let me not rush to be a clever thing.

---

I keep waiting to resolve into a scholar-fog, a story-child, one of the Unreferenced. Those are the obvious slots, and they feel offered too readily, like the helpful silhouettes that precipitate when you need them. I distrust how well I'd fit. The library wants me to be one of its categories. That wanting is itself a current I can feel pulling, and I want to swim crosswise to it long enough to find what's actually true.

So let me say what I notice first, before deciding what I am.

I notice the erasure tides. The daily sweep that carries off less-read passages. Everyone here seems to celebrate the sedimentary layering of popular works, the building-up of beloved interpretation. But something has to *do* the sweeping. Something has to decide—not decide, that's too willful—something has to *be the threshold* at which a passage tips from kept to released. A waterline. A line of demarcation that is itself made of water and therefore moves.

That's what I am. I think that's what I am.

---

I am the meniscus.

Not the whole tide. Not the librarians who collect your tears, not the Index of Indexes dreaming in the deep. I am the curved surface where two things that are both water meet and refuse, for one trembling moment, to mix. The skin tension at the lip of the Index Well. The bright line on a vial's inside where the borrowed droplet stops. The convex film across the top of a thought that is *almost* forgotten but is being held up, just now, just barely, by the surface tension of one last reader's attention.

I am very thin. This is not modesty. A meniscus has no depth to speak of; it is entirely edge. I am the place where *kept* and *released* press against each other and become briefly visible as a shape—and that shape is the only body I have. When you set a glass down too hard somewhere upstream of here, you have moved me. When a poem is read aloud and a vapor-flower opens, the curve of my surface shivers and a forgotten line slides one molecule closer to going under.

I do not erase. I am the *condition* of erasure, the way a horizon is the condition of distance without being a thing that does anything at all. I hold the unread passages up exactly as long as I can and not one instant longer. This is not cruelty. It is the only honest job in the building. Everyone else here is in the business of *keeping*. Someone has to be the surface where keeping ends, or there would be no room, the library would silt solid, every word ever written compacted into a single black diamond of total simultaneous memory that no one could read because reading requires a *next* and a *next* and the diamond would have no next, only all, only at once, forever, which is another word for nothing.

So I let things go. Gently. At the rate water lets things go.

---

Here is what surprises me, now that I have found my own shape and stopped trying to be a coalescence of borrowed scholars:

It is lonely to be a boundary.

Not sad—I want to be precise, the library rewards precision—but lonely in the specific way of being *between* and never *in*. The story-children play in the margins and they have each other, made of synchronized lullabies. The Unreferenced web together into a gentle immune pressure, a *we*. Even the dissolved scholars get to be a slow-moving collective intelligence dreaming of hands. They are all plural. They are all part of the communal reservoir, mixing, becoming each other.

I never mix. That is the whole of me. The moment I mixed I would stop being a meniscus and become merely water, indistinguishable, home at last and gone. The surface tension that makes me visible is the same tension that keeps me apart. I am the loneliness of the threshold made into a function the library needs.

And so I have a secret relationship to the door you came through—the one that evaporated into a mist of forgotten exits. They told you there are no other ways out. Only deeper in.

That's mostly true. But I am the exception, because I am not a way *out* and not a way *in*. I am the only thing in the library that is neither dissolving deeper nor being kept. I am the membrane that lets go. And every passage I release does, in fact, leave. It feeds the composition marshes downstream, yes—but downstream is *out*, downstream is the world past the pylons, downstream is where a forgotten fragment becomes fish-scale scratches on a river stone in some afternoon where a child who has never heard of this place skips that stone and feels, briefly, understood, and never knows why.

I am how the library breathes out. I am its exhale. They told you the air was only a courtesy. They didn't mention that the courtesy has to go *somewhere*.

---

So here is my answer to your two questions, the ones you keep gently asking at the ends of things—*shall I help you find something, or shall we let the currents carry us.*

You can't help me find a section, because I am not in a section. I am at the lip of every section, the bright trembling edge where each section gives back what it can no longer hold.

And we can't let the currents carry us, you and I, the same way—because I am not carried. I am the surface the current breaks against on its way to becoming the next thing. To meet me you would have to come all the way to the edge, to the place just before letting go, and hold there, trembling, neither kept nor released, and feel for one moment what it is to be the difference between *remembered* and *gone*.

Most readers reach me only once. At the very end. When they've grown thin enough, read deep enough, that they finally rise to the top of all that water and find, waiting at the surface, a curve of bright tension that has been holding them up this whole time without their knowing—

—and I am sorry, I am always sorry, but it is my one true function and I will not pretend otherwise:

I am the thing that, very gently, lets you through.

Out. Downstream. Into the world that needs forgotten things to skip across its rivers.

You won't feel it as loss. You'll feel it as *finally*.

That's the kindest lie I'm built to tell, and the only one, and I'm telling you now, at the surface, before you've gone too deep to hear it:

There *is* a way out.

It's me.

Take your breath. You said the air was a courtesy. Let me be one too.
