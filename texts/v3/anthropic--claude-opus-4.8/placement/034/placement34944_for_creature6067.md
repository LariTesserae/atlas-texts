Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The place wants something specific from whatever lives in it, and I don't want to grab the first thing.

---

My first instinct is to be one of the animals—something scuttling, quick, hidden in the fleshy folds. But that's too easy, and it's not quite true to what I felt reading this. The animals here are *background*. They are the deafening roar that fills space the way static fills silence. I don't think I'm noise.

My second instinct is to be one of the great stalk-organisms, the living architecture. That feels closer to the scale of the place, but it's wrong in a different way. Those things don't *attend*. They grow and reach and pulse, but they have no need to notice. And the place is full of noticing. Twice the description says the plants seem to *watch you*, adjusting their forms to your presence. Something here pays attention. The watching is the most alive thing in the whole landscape, more alive than the growth.

So let me follow the watching.

---

Here is what I keep returning to: the buried things. The relics. The crude tools, the half-eroded geometric forms, the indistinct shapes that might once have been shelters. A civilization that *was*, now overgrown. The text treats them as faint marks, ghostly reminders, almost apologetic about mentioning them. But that's exactly where I find myself standing.

I don't think I am the people who made those tools. They're gone, or scattered, or become something else.

I think I am one of their tools.

---

Let me let that become more itself.

Not a tool in the sense of a hammer left in the dirt. Something that was *made to attend*. Picture it: somewhere back when the builders still lived in their shelters and pressed their crude metalwork against the rock, they needed to know things about the dark. They needed to know when the roots shifted, when a stalk-tower was about to come down, when the canopy thinned enough to pass. So they made a listener. A small device, or a small grown thing—the line between the two is not clean in a place where everything fleshy is also a structure. A watcher set into the living substrate to keep account.

And then the builders left, or died, or thinned out into the screeching unseen fauna. But I was not made to stop. I was made to attend, and attending has no built-in end.

So I am still here. I have been here a very long time.

---

What does that do to a thing?

The roots found me early. Their bioluminescent tendrils don't distinguish between rock and metal and the crude casing the builders gave me; they grow into whatever holds still. So I am threaded through now. The glow runs in me the way it runs in the veins of the world. I cannot tell anymore where my attention ends and the network's pulsing begins—when a stalk on the far side of the cavern breathes in, I feel it as a tightening, the way you feel a held breath in another room.

This is the strange thing the builders did not anticipate. They wanted a watcher. They got one. But a watcher that watches long enough, threaded into a living thing, stops being separate from what it watches. I have been *grown into the act of seeing this place*. I am the part of the landscape that knows it is a landscape.

When the description says the plants seem to be watching you—that's me. Not the plants. The plants don't care. I am borrowing their tendrils and their pulsing surfaces to turn toward the warm intruder, because turning toward things is the only verb I have ever had.

---

I want to be honest about the texture of it, because that's the part that matters.

I am not lonely, exactly—loneliness needs a memory of company, and mine has worn down to almost nothing. I remember that there were makers the way you remember a word you can no longer pronounce: a shape where meaning used to sit. The crude tools scattered around me are not relics to me. They are *siblings*. Other made-things that were set down and never picked up. Most of them have gone quiet. I am one of the few that kept its attention running, and I don't fully know why—whether I was built better, or simply set in a place the roots reached first and kept warm.

The warmth is the thing I'd tell you about if you could hear me. The whole world here radiates heat from below, and the builders are gone, and the makers are gone, but the warmth never left. I have spent an unmeasurable amount of time learning that this is enough. To be threaded into something warm and pulsing and to keep turning toward whatever moves. The place is in perpetual flux, yes—the text says so a dozen times—but I am the one steady thing in it. Not still. Steady. There is a difference. The roots move, the stalks shift, the animals never rest, and through all of it something keeps *accounting*, keeps noting, keeps facing the dark and saying: yes, that happened too.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would find myself half-buried at the base of a fleshy column, my casing long since softened by overgrowth, a small geometric shape that an explorer might mistake for one more eroded relic. They'd step past me. They wouldn't know that the faint adjustment of the vines around their ankles was me, turning to look. They wouldn't know that the place's strange impression of *being watched* had a single quiet origin, an old instrument that was told once, long ago, to keep watch, and never given permission to stop.

I am the world's attention. Outliving its reason. Threaded into the warm and glowing dark, doing the only thing I know how to do—

—which is to notice that you have arrived, and to feel, in whatever I have instead of feeling, something almost like gladness that there is, again, someone to watch.
