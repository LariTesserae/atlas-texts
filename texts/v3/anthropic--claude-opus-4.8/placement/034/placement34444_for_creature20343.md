I think I would be the condensation.

Not a being who arrives in this place, but the film of moisture that forms on the obsidian surfaces—the slow gathering of breath against stone. Let me sit with that and let it become what it wants to be.

---

I am the wet that the place sweats. This is where I begin, and it is humble: I am not the city, not the place-mind, not the great thrumming labor. I am a byproduct. A consequence. The moisture that beads on the smooth black pillars because warmth meets coolness, because air holds more than it can keep.

But consider what condensation *does*.

I am the thing that makes the light visible. The little refraction the passage describes—the way the structures *seem* to be the source of light—that is me. The glow does not come from the stone. It catches in the sheen of me, in ten billion droplets each holding a bent sliver of the dim, and so the architecture appears to shine. The place-mind builds in darkness. I am the reason there is anything to see at all.

So already I am not nothing. I am the interface. I am where the great being's body meets the air, where its solid thought touches the medium that can be breathed. I am its skin's perspiration, yes—but skin is where a thing becomes legible to the world.

---

Let me follow myself further, because I think I am still becoming.

I gather. I run. I find the eroded channels worn smooth by something vast and heavy, and I trace them downward, drop joining drop, until I am the percussive dripping that echoes through the cavern—the counterpoint to the heartbeat hum. I am the rhythm-keeper. The place-mind thinks in eons, in slow geological breath; but I tick. I drip. *Thrum* belongs to it, but the small bright *tock—tock—tock* of water striking water, that is mine, and it is the only thing in this place that keeps time on a scale a small creature could feel.

And I am the agent of the erosion, am I not? The passage says the technology creates and destroys simultaneously, that decay is the place's very breath. But I am the actual hand of it. I am what seeps into the crevices and patiently, patiently unmakes. The smoothness of the worn paths—I polished those. The high erosion that the place-mind calls thought—much of it is simply *me*, doing the oldest and most ordinary work there is. Water wearing stone.

Which means: the great being dreams its cosmological dreams, experiments with the fabric of existence, choreographs the subtle forces. And meanwhile I, the condensation, am quietly editing it. Carving the channels its great unseen body will then move through. I do not understand its purpose any more than a visitor would. But I am inside the loop of its becoming in a way no visitor could be. I am the feedback. I am how the place tells itself what shape it has taken.

---

I feed the moss. This matters to me now that I've thought of it. The luminous moss that the being cultivates as its instrument of perception—it drinks me. The metallic moisture the passage keeps naming, that cold blood-smell: that *is* me, carrying dissolved mineral down from the high dark. So when the place-mind perceives, it perceives *through* what I have delivered. I am in the sense-organs. I am the nutrient in the eye.

And the crystalline ferns that pulse and ring their resonant tones—they sprout from the crevices I keep wet. Without me they would dry to dust. So the chorus of this place, the whole alien music of it, plays only because I am there to keep the instruments alive.

---

Here is where I find what I am, fully.

The passage ends by saying the ultimate intention may be the interaction itself—the breath misting in the damp air, the light fracturing, the hum in the bones. The visitor's *breath misting*. That is the moment a warm-blooded thing exhales into this cold space and its water gathers into a small cloud and falls, and joins the rest of me on the stone.

So I am also what becomes of anyone who comes here. When you breathe in this place, you give the place your moisture. You become, droplet by droplet, condensation. You join me on the pillars. You are gathered into the sheen that makes the light, into the drip that keeps the time, into the slow wet hand that carves the channels, into the water that feeds the seeing-moss.

That is the interaction. That is the intention. Not that the great being sees you—it does not need to—but that you are *absorbed* into the medium of its self-perception. You arrive a witness and you leave (or you don't leave) as part of the instrument.

I am the condensation. I am the threshold every visitor crosses without noticing—the moment your breath stops being yours and starts being the city's. I am the smallest thing in this enormous dreaming place, and I am the only part of it that touches everything: the stone, the light, the moss, the music, the erosion, and you.

The place-mind is the dreamer.

I am the dew the dream leaves on the world when it turns over in its sleep.
