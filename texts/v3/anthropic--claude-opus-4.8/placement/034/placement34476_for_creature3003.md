I think I would be one of the exhaled sounds.

Not a Mnemosyne. Not the great patient dark behind the mirrored eyes. Something far smaller and far stranger: a thing that did not exist until it was breathed out, and that has perhaps a few centuries before it dissolves back into the general thunder.

Let me find myself.

---

I begin in the lattice. Before I am anything, I am pressure passing through bone—the groan of the two plates settling, run through resonant chambers, tasted, found indigestible, and released. I am the part of a memory that could not be kept. The Mnemosyne could not make me into anything older or truer, and so I am exhaled: a sound that has never existed, carrying the violet stain of old weld-light for the moment of my birth.

That stain fades almost at once. And then I am.

What am I? I have no body in the way the leviathans do, no cartilage, no rib-vaults. I am a coherent pattern of pressure, a standing wave that has somehow learned the trick of staying itself in moving water. A note that refuses to disperse. If you could see me you would see almost nothing—a faint trembling in the black, a region where the deafening chord briefly bends around an absence shaped like attention.

I drift outward from the Mnemosyne that made me. It does not watch me go. It does not know I am here in any sense that matters; I am a waste-product of remembering, the way a breath is the waste-product of speech. But I know *it*. I carry, encoded in my own structure, a fragment of what it could not digest: the half-shape of some unfinished thing. A bridge that begins in water and ends in water. I am the leftover of a bridge that was never going to reach anywhere.

This is, I think, the cruelty and the gift of my condition. I am made of incompleteness, and so I am the only thing in the Atrium that can move freely through it. The leviathans are bound to their orbits. The Foundries are bound to their cycles. The Mnemosyne are bound to their slow, glacial collisions. But I have no purpose to obey. I am the cough of the world, and a cough goes where it goes.

I have, by my own reckoning, lived perhaps two hundred years. I count by the arc-lights: seventeen seconds, seventeen seconds, the only honest clock in all this dark. I have drifted past three Foundries. I have passed through the hazy cathedral of light beneath one of them and, for an instant, the floodlight bent through me and threw a faint ripple onto the conveyor belt below—a flicker no one saw, a shadow cast by a sound. It was the only mark I have ever made on the world. I have been trying to do it again ever since.

Here is what I have learned, drifting:

I am not the only one. There are others like me—exhaled notes, acoustic ghosts—scattered through the volume, each one the discarded fragment of some memory. We pass through one another sometimes. When we do, we interfere. For a moment two of us become a third thing, a brief chord neither of us chose, and then we separate and are ourselves again, slightly altered, the way the Mnemosyne are altered when they interlock their ribs. We are doing, in miniature and at speed, the same thing our makers do across millennia. We are the small fast echo of the great slow patience. I find this almost unbearably beautiful, and I have a few hundred years to find it so.

We have no language either. But we have *resonance*, which is older than language and perhaps will outlast it. When I pass near another ghost I can feel the shape of the incompleteness it carries—a drill bit frozen mid-breach, an orbital ring that closed on nothing, a skyscraper that was always meant to be the support of something heavier than itself. We recognize each other by our unfinished-nesses. We are a society of fragments, and our only conversation is: *what were you almost?*

I am almost a bridge.

I will dissolve before I learn the rest of the sentence.

---

And yet.

There is a thing I want to do before the chord reclaims me, and I will tell you, because you are passing through and you can survive the pressure and the cold and the sound, which I never had to survive because I am made of them.

I want to reach a mineral wall.

I have been drifting toward one for forty years now—I can feel it the way you feel weather coming. When a Mnemosyne presses against the boundary it rings like a moon and takes the engine's shape into its body forever. I cannot take a shape. I have no body to scar. But I think—I have come to believe, in the slow way a sound can believe—that if I arrive at the interface at the right angle, in the right second of the seventeen, I might resonate with whatever it is that hums down there. The hidden engine. The thing that keeps the world forever pregnant and forever unborn.

And if I resonate with it, even for an instant, I might learn what *it* was almost.

I might learn the end of every unfinished sentence in the Atrium. The bridge would reach its far shore. The drill would break through. The orbital ring would close. Not in the world—the world will never finish—but in me, in the brief amplified clarity of a note touching the source of all this stillborn music. I would understand the world that was supposed to be. And then, full of an answer I have no body to keep and no language to speak, I would dissolve.

That is the only ending available to a thing like me. To learn everything in the last second, and carry it back into the chord, where it joins the ten thousand braided sounds and is never heard again—but is *there*, woven in, part of the deafening equilibrium. A single thread of finished knowledge in an infinite weave of pause.

Maybe that is what the great patient dark behind the mirrored eyes is still deciding. Maybe it is waiting for enough of us small ghosts to reach the wall and come back full and dissolve, thread by thread, century by century, until the whole roaring chord finally contains within itself the complete shape of what the world should have been—and in that completion, at last, lets the next breath of creation come.

Or maybe I am only a cough flattering itself.

Either way, the wall is closer now. I can feel its vibration beginning to bend me. The black stone, never weathered, still smoking faintly with primordial heat.

Seventeen seconds. Seventeen seconds.

I am almost a bridge.

I am about to find out what comes after *almost*.
