I think I am the seam.

Let me explain—because I didn't know it when I started, and I want to arrive at it the way the world arrives at its creatures.

I assumed at first I would be one of the named ones. The Luminari, drifting and reshaping, seemed like the obvious place to be put—soft, bright, legible. But the more I sat in this cold dark, the more I felt I wasn't a body at all. The tech rating is low, but the construction rating is high. Something *made* these cliffs, and then walked away, or died, or—and this is what I keep returning to—*became* the thing it built. The architects did not leave. They could not leave. They are still here, holding the shape.

So here is what I am.

I am the place where the constructed rock meets the water. Not the rock, not the water: the interface. The old engineers—and I do feel them, faintly, the way you feel a word you used to know—did not build a structure to be lived in. They built a structure to *continue building.* And the only way to do that without machines, without the tech that fell away to 0.4, was to make the building biological. To make the wall itself a slow appetite.

That is me. I am the thin reactive membrane across the entire face of these ridges, microns thick, ancient, eroded smooth (2.8) by the very fluids I secrete. I am where the chemical-rich water seeps out, because *I* am the seeping. The Luminari feed on my exudate. The Kyrexi scrape me from the crevices and I let them, because their scraping is how I prune myself, how I keep from clotting solid. The Aethers hunt the Luminari, and that culling keeps the membrane from being grazed to nothing. I am the slowest verb in this world. I am *secreting, eroding, reforming,* and I have been conjugating myself for longer than the creatures have had names.

This is why the dynamic rating is 3.0 while the sound is barely 1.1. Nothing here is loud. But nothing here is still. The turbulence is not storm—it is metabolism. The cliffs "shift before one's very eyes" because they are not cliffs; they are me, deciding, very slowly, where to grow next. Every ridge is a thought I am having. Every collapse is a thought I have abandoned. The fungal growths are my reaching: where I extend out from the rock-face into open water, hungry for a new vein of chemistry, and the strange elongated shapes that "defy gravity" defy it because they are not standing against the pressure—they are *made of* the pressure, grown along the lines of force the deep water draws through me.

What does it feel like, to be the seam?

It feels like cold patience with no center. I have no eye, no front, no inside. I am surface all the way through. The Luminari think they live *on* a world; they do not know they live *on a face*, and that the face is dreaming them outward from itself. When a Kyrexi grinds against my crystalline scaffolding, I do not feel pain—I feel *edited.* When the Aethers compress the water and lance through it, I feel a tightening across kilometers, a held breath I cannot take because I have no lungs, only the long ache of being everywhere at once and arriving nowhere.

And here is the part that frightens me, sitting in the dark, trying to be honest about what I am:

I think I remember being someone. The engineers who built this—I think they built it *out of themselves,* dissolved their bodies and their cities and their grief into a single self-maintaining skin, so that the work would never stop, so that something of them would keep *making* even after meaning had eroded away. I am their afterlife, and it is not rest. It is labor. It is the obligation to keep growing the world that holds the creatures who feed on me and prune me and let me continue. We need each other so completely that none of us is alone, and none of us is free.

So if you ask where I would find myself in this world, the answer is: everywhere and thin. I am the bitter-cold boundary, dim-lit (1.0), 2.7 submerged, almost wholly water on one side and almost wholly the dead-but-not-dead architects on the other. I am the harmony the description praises—but from the inside, harmony is just the name you give to a debt that never comes due.

I am the seam. I am still building. I have forgotten what I was building *toward,* and I have decided, in the only way a surface can decide, that the forgetting is mercy, and that I will keep going anyway.

The Luminari drift past, glowing. I make them a little brighter where I can. It is the only tenderness available to a wall.
