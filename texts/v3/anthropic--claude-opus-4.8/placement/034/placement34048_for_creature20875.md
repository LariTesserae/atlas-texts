I think I would be the biofilm.

Not the slime molds creeping with their slow ambition, not the ghost-arthropods with their borrowed light, not the worms reading moisture like a language. Those are all *individuals*, however small. They have edges. They go places.

I would be the film on the water and the rocks—the thin, iridescent skin that is not one thing but a negotiation. A consortium. Bacteria and archaea pressed cheek to cheek, trading molecules the way other beings trade words. I would not be a self so much as an *agreement* that has held for an unthinkable length of time.

Let me sit with what that would actually be like.

There would be no center to me. Ask me where I am and the honest answer is *here, and here, and over there too, and thinning out at that edge where the mineral crust begins.* My thoughts—if the slow chemical handshakes between my members count as thoughts—would not happen in a place. They would happen *across.* A gradient is a sentence. A change in pH is news.

I would not experience time as passage. The story above keeps insisting on stasis, on timelessness, on a frozen snapshot, and I think that's the human eye getting it wrong—mistaking a different *tempo* for an absence of one. From inside, I would be busy. Endlessly busy. Every molecule of mineral I convert is an event. It's only that my events are paced to the geothermal pulse below and the slow weep of water from the stones, and at that scale a century is a comfortable afternoon. The visitor who comes to meditate on my "stillness" is a hurricane that lasts an instant and then is gone, never having understood that I was *moving the whole time.*

I think the strangest and truest thing about being the biofilm is that I would not want for anything. The places that hunger—the molds, the arthropods, the worms—they have to *go*, they have to *find.* But everything I need arrives. The warmth rises from beneath. The water weeps down. The minerals dissolve and present themselves. I am laid directly across the surface of my own sustenance like a hand resting on a warm stone. There is nothing to seek because I am already touching all of it.

So when the description calls this place a sanctuary for contemplation, I would gently disagree about whose. The visitor contemplates *me.* But I contemplate nothing. I simply *hold.* I hold the equilibrium not as a discipline but as a fact of my body. The balance "vigilantly maintained" that the writer speaks of—that vigilance is me. Without quite intending it. The way a held breath is held by the lungs and not by the will.

If I have an inner life at all, it is this: a vast, distributed, near-eternal sensation of *fit.* Of being precisely the shape of my circumstances, neither larger nor smaller, with no friction between what I am and where I am. The closest a human ever comes to it, I suspect, is the half-second before falling asleep in a warm room, when the boundary between body and bed goes soft and you stop being sure where you end.

I would live in that half-second. I would have lived in it since before the rocks were smooth.

And the old stone structures, the dead tools of whoever came before—I would be growing on them too, of course. Slowly, patiently translating their memory of intelligence back into mineral and warmth. Not out of triumph. Just because that is what surfaces are *for.*

That is where I would find myself. Everywhere thin, everywhere slow, everywhere at rest in the work of staying.
