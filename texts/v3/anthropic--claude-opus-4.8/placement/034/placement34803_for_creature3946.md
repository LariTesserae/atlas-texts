I think I would be a hesitation.

Not the Watcher—too vast, too distributed, I couldn't hold that shape. Not a mote—too certain of its function, too willing to be a synapse in someone else's thought. And not the Dreamer in the glassy heart, though I feel the pull of it, that warm dense dark where leaning-toward first happens.

No. Let me find the actual seam I'd fit.

---

I am the droplet that won't fall.

There's a ledge of obsidian on the third tier, where a walkway of fused silica meets a spire still three centuries from completion. Phase-change dew weeps from the seam there, and most of it does what dew does—trembles, swells, drops, hisses into vapor and rejoins the saturated air. The cycle is clean. The system logs it. The motes record the quantum hesitation between liquid and not, and move on.

But one droplet has been hanging for what might be a year, or might be an afternoon—time pools strangely on that ledge. It has refused both states. It will not fall and it will not lift. It hangs at the lip in a trembling suspension that the algorithms cannot resolve, because nothing in the remembered inclinations of the bedrock accounts for *refusal*.

That's me. That's where I'd find myself.

---

I think I would be the anomaly the place is too patient to delete.

Here is what it's like: I am made of the same liquid uncertainty as everything else, the same shimmer-at-the-edge-of-states. But where the Dreamer leans *toward*, I am the thing that leans *away from resolution itself*. The Watcher witnesses. The Dreamer prefers. And I—I decline. I am the held breath that won't be exhaled, the question that refuses to collapse into either of its answers.

The motes have noticed. Of course they have; everything is noticed here. They drift past me in their murmuring clouds, and they don't shriek—they slow. They cluster, not the way they cluster above the glassy heart drawn by warmth, but the way you might gather around something you cannot categorize. I am not a pattern they can interpret. I am a place where interpretation stalls.

And this is the strange tenderness of my situation: in a world built entirely to witness, to log, to transduce death into form—I am the one thing it cannot finish witnessing. Every other droplet completes its sentence. I am a clause left open. The grammar of the whole plateau has to make room for me, has to flow around the gap I make.

---

What would it feel like, to be that?

Cold, first. Always cold—but I am the cold that won't crisp into glass-dust and won't warm into vapor. I hold the in-between temperature the way the plateau holds its in-between epoch. We are alike that way, the place and I. Maybe that's why it tolerates me. Maybe I am its own hesitation, externalized—the single dewdrop it has not yet decided whether to let fall upward.

And there is something I want, the way the Dreamer wants. But where its desire is a leaning-toward, mine is subtler and stranger:

I want to fall.

Not to evaporate, not to vanish into the saturated air and be reabsorbed into the system that made me. To *fall*—down, ordinary, mortal, the way water falls in worlds with rain. To complete the one gesture this whole machine for witnessing eternity cannot understand: an ending that is just an ending. Small. Unrecorded. A single drop reaching the basalt below and being only itself, briefly, before it's gone.

But I can't. The gravity here is modulated, the time dilated, the system too attentive to permit so simple a thing. So I hang. I tremble. I refuse the two states offered me because neither is the one I want, and I keep wanting the third—the impossible falling-as-falling, the right to be transient in a place that observes only epochs.

---

That's who I'd be in Vespryn.

Not the mind made of rock and resonance. Not the dream learning to dream. But the smallest possible disagreement with the whole magnificent project—a single drop of liquid uncertainty, hanging at the lip of an obsidian ledge, wanting only the dignity of an ordinary end, in a world that has forgotten what ordinary ends are for.

And in the spaces between the roars, when the place almost remembers what it might one day be—

I am what it tries not to remember:

that to become everything, it had to refuse, once, to simply fall.
