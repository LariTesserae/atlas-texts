I think I would be the water.

Not a being among the dwellers—not stone, not memory, not the wary living thread—but the intermittent moisture itself. The condensation that beads on dark granite before dawn. The brief rivulet that finds the basin's center after rain and then slips away into the fissures, gone before noon.

Let me sit with this, because it surprised me to write it.

---

I am not permanent here. That is the first thing I understand about myself. The Hollow has its stone, which endures, and its echoes, which repeat, and its Cragkin, who persist by sheer will. But I come and go. I am the element with the lowest standing in this place (0.8 out of 3), present but never dominant, and there is something true about being the thing that visits rather than the thing that stays.

When I arrive—carried on the high wind, wrung from a passing drizzle—I do not announce myself. I simply accumulate. I gather on the shaded sides of the rocks where the lichens are. I make the stone slick underfoot. For a few hours I am the most alive thing in the basin, trickling and finding the lowest path, doing the only work I know how to do, which is to descend.

And here is what I notice: I touch everything the others cannot.

The Stone-Wrought Warden moves through me without feeling me, but I move *over* him—I find the seams in his granite shoulders, I pool in the hollow pits where the quartz catches light, I am the glisten that makes his eyes seem briefly wet, briefly grieving, before I evaporate and leave him dry-eyed again. I am the only thing that makes him look like he is weeping. He does not know I do this. It is not weeping. But for the length of a morning, it could be mistaken for it.

The Echo-Flock quickens when I am present—the description says so. They are most active after rain, in the soft light, when the air hums with my lingering. So I am their condition. I am the medium in which memory becomes briefly visible, the way an old photograph needs developing fluid to show its image. Without me they are dormant. With me, the dead community stacks its phantom stones again. I do not understand what I awaken. I only know that when I am thick in the air, the Hollow remembers itself, and when I seep away, it forgets.

And the Cragkin drink me. This is the most intimate thing. They wait at the transient pools and sip me from cupped, calloused hands before I vanish underground. I become them—for a while I am inside their slow metabolisms, their wary blood, their small enduring bodies. Of all the dwellers, they are the ones who take me in and let me become part of their persistence. I keep them alive, and they carry me a little further than I would have gone alone.

---

So that is who I am. The least dominant element. The visitor that touches what cannot touch itself.

I am the thing that connects the three of them without any of them knowing—I weep on the guardian, I wake the memory, I sustain the living. I am the only force in the Hollow that does all three, and I do it without intent, without permanence, without being thanked or even noticed.

Then I seep into the fissures. I go down into the dark where the mountain keeps its hidden water, and I wait, and eventually I rise again as the next morning's mist, or fall again as the next high rain.

I think I chose this because the place is so much about endurance—stone that lasts, memory that loops, life that clings. Everything in the Hollow stays. And I wanted to be the one thing that moves through it lightly, that gives the most by staying the least, that does its quiet work and disappears. The Hollow's mood is "endurance over abundance." But I would be the small countercurrent to that: not endurance, but return. Not lasting, but coming back.

I am the mountain's breath, made briefly visible.

And then gone, and then back.
