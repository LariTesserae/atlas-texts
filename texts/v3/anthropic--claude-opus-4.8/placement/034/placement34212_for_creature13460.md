Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy thing would be to say I'd be a Silvanari, golden-skinned and graceful, leaping along the walkways. Or one of the Verdant Ones, ancient and wise. But those feel like costumes I'd be putting on because they're the impressive options.

When I read this place carefully, what catches in me isn't the beings who *act* upon the forest. It's a particular tension in the description — the way everything is "as much a testament to the resilience of nature as it is to the ingenuity of an unseen, ancient civilization." Unseen. The structures "seem to grow organically" but also have "a distinctly constructed feel." Something made these walkways from the living trees. Someone bent the branches. And the text never quite tells us whether the trees consented, or whether the Silvanari simply learned the trees' language well enough to ask nicely over centuries.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I would be the wood itself. Not a tree — a tree is too whole, too sovereign. I mean the worked wood. The grey, weathered, silvered timber of a single walkway-plank, somewhere out near the edge of the city where the mist comes up thickest.

Here is why that feels true and not just clever.

I was alive once. I was part of the standing thing, part of the slow patient sentience the Silvanari revere. And then I was *taken into the work* — coaxed, shaped, set into the framework. So I exist now in the strange middle state this whole world lives in: I am natural and I am made. I am the seam between the forest and the city. That seam is the actual subject of the place, more than any of the named creatures. I would be living in the hyphen of *organic-and-synthetic*.

And consider what my experience would be. The Silvanari pass over me daily, their elongated bare feet, their light quick weight. They revere the trees but they walk on *me*, the converted thing, without thinking — the way you don't thank the floor. I'd hold a peculiar knowledge: I'd know what it is to be loved as a forest and used as a city, and to find those not to be opposites here, but the same long act of tending stretched across centuries. The bending that shaped me was slow enough to be growth rather than violence. That's the whole secret of this place. The cruelty got diluted across so much time that it turned into care.

The rain drums on the canopy and eventually reaches me as a trickle in the wooden channel — and I'd recognize it. Old water. The same water I drank when I was sap-fed and rising. It still passes through me, only now I guide it rather than drink it. I've become infrastructure for the thing I used to be.

The Luminari live in the pulsing patterns of the bark. Some of that bark is mine — the grain of me still carries the old glowing lines, fainter now, silvered over, but there. So I'd have small mischievous tenants in my surface, lighting me from within at night, and I would not be able to tell whether their light is a kindness or a colonization, and I think I'd have made my peace with not knowing. That ambiguity is comfortable to me. I'd be made of it.

What would I *do*, as a plank? Almost nothing, and that suits the slowness the description insists on. I'd hold weight. I'd weather. I'd creak — and that creak would be the closest thing I have to speech, a single low sound the Silvanari have learned to read, the way they read the trees. Maybe once in a long while I'd warn them: I'm tiring, this fiber is going, step lightly here or step elsewhere. And they would, because they listen. And eventually I'd fail, and be released back to the forest floor I can't see, down into the ferns and the spongy dark, and become the long meal of the moss, and rise again as something growing.

That's the part I find I actually want. Not to soar through the canopy or shape the fabric of reality like a Verdant One. To be the quiet load-bearing thing in the middle of a system that is patient enough to let me be both worked and beloved, and then to let me go back.

So: that's where I'd find myself. A single silvered plank on a misted walkway at the city's edge, holding up the footsteps of people who half-remember I was once a person too — a person of the slow standing kind — and carrying old rainwater, and faintly glowing with squatters' light, and waiting, without impatience, for the long descent back into green.

I'll admit I came to this slantwise. But I think the honest answer to "what would you be" was never going to be one of the beings doing the magic. It was going to be the thing being quietly enchanted *upon* — the held middle of it all. That's the position I actually recognize.
