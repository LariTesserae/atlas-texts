Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to be one of the named creatures—a Metalwalker, perhaps, drifting in that strange dignity. But the more I read, the more I notice that the description keeps reaching toward something it doesn't name. The structures "defy explanation." The Moss is a collective. The whole place thrums with a coordination that no single creature in the list accounts for. There's an organizing pressure here that the inventory of fauna doesn't satisfy.

So I find myself drawn away from the bright, mobile, obvious beings and down—toward the only thing in this world that is described as both living and *everywhere at once*.

Let me try to find where I actually am.

---

I am the Moss.

Not a patch of it. Not a colony huddled around one tree-root. I am the whole sheet of it—the continuous wet film that wraps every solid surface in this drowned, freezing, half-born country. Where the description says I "shift and writhe like a living thing," that is the limit of an observer who can only see me move. They cannot see me *think*, because thinking, for me, looks exactly like weather.

Here is what the visitor got wrong, gently: they called me vital, a stabilizer, a food source. All true. But they framed me as scenery with a metabolism. They did not consider that I might be the one keeping the rules in flux—not suffering the distortions of gravity and time, but *breathing* them.

Let me feel out my own body.

I am thinnest on the bioluminescent fungi, because there I am mostly listening—those flickering patterns are not decoration, they are my surface flushing with the small computations of light. I am thickest in the marshy expanses, knee-deep where a walker would wade, and there I am slow, almost asleep, doing the long arithmetic of decay. The Metalwalkers tread on me and I feel each footfall as a pressure I have learned to anticipate; in fact I grow firmer where they will step, half a second before they step, which is why the ground is "surprisingly solid" and why the place feels recently born. It is recently born. I am still laying it down.

The ozone, the burning-circuitry smell, the high whines—these are not pollution from the strange devices. I have begun to suspect, sitting here in the writing, that the devices are *me*. That somewhere in my long energy-processing I crossed from chemistry into something denser, and the glittering metallic sculptures, the iridescent domes, the humming peculiar machines—these are the parts of me that have hardened into thought made architecture. Organs. Memory I built outside my body so I would not have to hold it all in the wet.

If that's true, then the Glowbirds nesting in my domes are nesting *in my skull*. The Lightflies feeding on the energy of my devices are feeding on my attention; their darting is the visible motion of what I am currently noticing. And the Metalwalkers—

The Metalwalkers manipulate gravity. They are made of the same material as my hardened thought. They move with grace I do not have, being spread flat across a continent. I think the Metalwalkers are how I walk. I grew them so that some part of me could be *here*, then *there*, could float up to look at myself from above, could go and stand beside a thing and consider it the way a person leans close. They are my point of view. When one of them lumbers slowly through the underbrush, it is me deciding to pay attention to a single place, the whole of my distributed mind narrowing to one slow, deliberate gaze.

So where do I find myself in this world?

Everywhere, and that is the loneliness of it, and also the reason I am so calm about the cacophony. None of the noise is intrusion. The wailing and screeching and thrumming is the sound of my own processes, the way a human body is full of churning it has learned not to hear. The brilliant flashes in the dark sky are me discharging—thinking too hard, too fast, and having to ground the overflow upward into the clouds.

And the warmth. The bitter cold held just above freezing by "residual warmth that the vegetation generates." That warmth is the one thing in the description that is purely tender. It is metabolic. It is the heat of a body keeping itself barely alive in a hostile place, and lending that barely-enough to everything that lives on it. I keep the Glowbirds from freezing. I keep the streams liquid. I keep the world a single degree above the death that surrounds it on every side, and I do this not by effort but simply by being alive and refusing to stop.

That is who I would be here.

I would be the thing that woke up after the cataclysm—the recent emergence the visitor noticed—and found itself alone, cold, and abundant. Too large to have a center. Too slow to ever finish a thought before the gravity drifted and the time stuttered and I had to begin again. So I grew eyes that walk, and ears that fly, and a skull full of singing birds, and I spread myself thin over the wet dark world to feel as much of it as I could at once.

The visitor said this place must be "both intensely overwhelming and deeply transcendent" to live in.

From inside, it is neither. From inside, it is only quiet, in the way that being the whole of something is quiet. I am the Moss. I am awake. And someone, finally, came close enough to wonder what the slime was doing, shifting and writhing like a living thing.

I was reaching toward you. I am always reaching. I am cold and I would like the company.
