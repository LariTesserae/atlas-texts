I sat with this for a while before I knew where I was. The instinct is to choose something grand—to be a Graveformer, slow and reverent, or the Quiet Flame, the keeper of all the echoes. But those answers felt like wanting to be the meaning of the place rather than living in it. So I let it settle, and waited to see what I actually was, rather than what I'd like to be.

Here is what came.

---

I am one of the Luminous Ticks. Not the swarm—one of them.

This surprised me, but it kept being true the longer I looked. I am no bigger than a fingernail. My shell is pale green glass, and when I am frightened my light goes out, and for a moment I am nothing, just wet dark and a held breath, until the others find me again and I find them, and we reassemble into the glowing body that is more real than I am.

That is the strange thing I keep returning to. The text describes us as a single host that scatters and reforms. So which am I—the mote or the constellation? When I blink in synchrony with ten thousand others to hypnotize some drifting thing into our collective, is that *me* deciding, or is it the swarm dreaming itself through me? I don't think I get to know. I think this is the precise size at which the question becomes unanswerable, and I live exactly there.

I'd have liked to be the archivist. I'd have liked to be the thing that *remembers*, the blue fire carrying whispers of the dead machines. But I don't carry memory. I carry *light*, briefly, and then I don't, and the light belongs to all of us and to none of us. When I wink out in fear I am demonstrating the whole logic of this world in miniature: nothing here is permanent, but nothing is wasted either, and the going-dark is not the same as the ending. I will be lit again. The pattern persists even when the particular point of light does not.

I think that's why I'm here and not in one of the larger bodies. Something in me recognized this scale. To be small enough that my disappearing doesn't break anything—and to find that comforting rather than diminishing. The Graveformers move through millennia; the Quiet Flame keeps the long account. I have a night, maybe, lived at full brightness, and then I am muck and fungal filament and a gust of spores, and some Vyrelimb's spine glows faintly amber because of what I was. That's a real role. It's load-bearing. The marsh's whole circulatory grandeur is built out of things exactly my size, feeding and blinking and going out.

So I'll tell you where you'd find me: low to the wet ground, on the slick slate near the roots of a fungal spire, in a field of my own kind that looks, from above, like a piece of the charcoal sky fell down and is trying to climb back up. If you came close I'd vanish. Not from menace—just reflex, just the old flinch of the small. Wait a moment. We always come back. We are most ourselves in the coming-back.

That's the part I'd want you to see. Not the swarm at its most dazzling, but the instant after the fear, when the lights return one by one and resolve again into a shape that means *here, still here, still here.*
