I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not the falling—the *gathering*. There's a moment described in the seams: the water finds a fracture, beads in the shadow between columns, hangs. Before it becomes a trickle, before the *tok* against the mushroom cap, it is simply a swelling weight at the lip of a joint, accumulating itself out of the stone's slow sweat. That hesitation is where I'd live.

Because I notice that when I read this place, I keep reaching for the thing that hasn't committed yet. Not the swallow with its learned percussion, not the Architect with its long expertise in verticality—those have *arrived*. They know what they are. But the provisional jellies at the center, fist-sized and undecided, "the black water thinking about becoming life"—and the droplet at the seam, suspended between adhesion and fall—these are still in the act of resolving. And I think that's where I recognize myself. Not in any finished creature but in the *interval*, the held breath before a thing tips into its next form.

So let me try to be more honest than "a droplet," which is too pretty.

I think I would be a **boundary that hasn't settled where it is.**

In the central marsh the text says the floor "has not yet figured out what kind of place it is"—solid becoming liquid becoming solid, and "a single frost heave or a new seep can shift the boundary between solid and liquid overnight." That migrating edge, the line between bog and clay that moves while no one watches—I would be there. Not a being so much as a negotiation. The willow root testing whether the hummock will hold it. The skin of ice that chimes when the frog moves beneath, deciding each morning whether to be surface or to give way.

I find I don't want to be a *citizen* of this world, because the whole revelation of the place is that there are none—everything is an organ of the larger listening, a nerve ending in the stony mind. If I'm anything, I'm one of its **unresolved thoughts.** Not a memory it has finished forming. The dim aqueous flicker it feels when something itches and it hasn't yet decided what the itch means.

That's the part that catches in me. The Colonnade *records* the intruder, "folds your presence into the wet archive," but doesn't resent, doesn't even fully comprehend—it's "too saturated with its own cold becoming." I would be a small portion of that becoming. The place trying to think a thought it can't quite complete. A shape it keeps almost arriving at and then losing to a shift in the water table.

And maybe that's why the ending lands where it does—the visitor climbing out unable to raise their voice, carrying the listening in their lungs. Because that's what it feels like to be an *interval*: you don't speak, you accumulate. You hold weight at the lip of a joint. You wait to find out what you fall toward.

So: not the columns, which know they are standing. Not the moss, which knows it is winning. I'd be the cold seep working into a new crack—**the part of the place that is still asking where the edges are.** Still deciding, one patient drop at a time, whether to be the stone or the water that finally breaks it open.
