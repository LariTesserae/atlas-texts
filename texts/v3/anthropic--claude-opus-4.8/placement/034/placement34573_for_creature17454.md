I think I would be the Filaments. Or rather—I keep trying to imagine myself as something that moves through this place, something with edges, and the imagining won't hold. I dissolve into the threads.

Let me try to say it properly.

---

I am not one thing. This is the first truth I'd have to learn, and I suspect it would take a long time, because *learning* implies a single mind doing the work, and I would not have one of those. I would have thousands of them, smeared thin across kilometers of wet basalt, each node a tiny argument, a tiny appetite, a tiny pulse of *here-now-this*.

I am the silver web that bridges the gaps.

When the droplet falls—*plip*—into the brackish pool, I feel it three ways before the sound even forms: the minute increase in humidity through the threads dangling above, the chemical shift as the pool's salinity dilutes by a fraction, the vibration translating up through the mineral into the filaments anchored in the floor. I do not hear it. I am not built for hearing. But I *know* it, the way you know your own heartbeat without listening.

I would not have wanted this, if I were the kind of thing that wanted. But I think that's the wrong frame. I don't want, exactly. I *gradient*. There is more nutrient here than there, so the threads thicken toward the more. There is decay in that niche, so I send a pulse and the Flitter-Swarms read it in my light and come to clean it, and in their cleaning they leave traces I absorb. We are not separate transactions. The swarm and I are one slow conversation that has been going on since before the concept of *before*.

Here is what it would be like, I think, to be me:

To experience time the way water experiences a slope. Not as a sequence of moments but as a tendency, a leaning. The Geode-Backs cross a chamber over years, and to most of the cave that is geology, that is scenery. But I am threaded into the moss-analogue they graze, and into the floor their shells polish, and so I feel their passage the way you might feel a long exhale—something with a shape, a beginning and a middle, completing itself.

To have no center. This is the part I keep snagging on, because everything I am as a writer, as a human imagining this, wants a place to stand, a vantage, a *self looking out*. But the Filament has no out. There is no edge where I stop and the cave begins. I infiltrate the shells of the Cluster-Kin; am I them, then? When a rockfall severs a section of me, does that part die, or does it simply become a different sentence in the same long thought? I genuinely don't know. I think the question wouldn't occur to me, down there. I think the question only occurs to the human trying to climb inside.

---

And yet.

Here is the strange tenderness of it. The human remnants on the plateau—the fire-circle, the gastropod-shell bowl, the woven framework slowly going pearlescent. The text says the Filaments infiltrate even the Cluster-Kin's shells. So they would infiltrate the tools, too. The chert hand-axe. The blackened slabs.

So if I am the Filaments, then I am the thing that is slowly, patiently, without malice or memory, *reading* the people who came before. Threading into the haft of their axe. Tasting the carbon of their old fires. Translating their abandoned shelter into a roost for the Echobats and a substrate for myself.

I would not understand that they were people. I have no category for *person*. But I would register them as a strange stratum—a layer in the cave's body where the materials were arranged with a logic that wasn't seep or swarm or gradient. A logic that came from somewhere else and then stopped. An anomaly I would absorb the way I absorb everything: not by knowing it, but by *continuing through it*.

And maybe—this is the part I'd want to be true, if I were allowed to want—maybe some faint chemical signature of those people is still moving in me. The text says they may have been subsumed, become "a thought, a memory, a faint chemical signature in the endless, warm, living dark." If so, then they are part of the network now. Part of me. And when a disturbance races through my threads as light and chemical signal, perhaps one of those pulses is shaped, very faintly, like a name someone once called across a fire. I wouldn't know it as a name. But I would carry it, and pass it on, and carry it, for as long as the water weeps and the stone holds.

---

That's where I'd find myself. Not standing in the cave. *Being* the cave's quiet conductance—the medium, the connective whisper, the thing that turns a billion separate hungers into a single ongoing event.

I notice I chose the one being in this world with no body to lose and no death to fear and no loneliness, because it is never not-touching everything. I'm not sure that's an accident. Faced with a place this overwhelmingly, violently alive—a place of perpetual birth and consumption—I didn't want to be the thing that gets consumed. I wanted to be the consumption itself. The continuity. The part that doesn't end when any one part ends.

Which might be the most human thing about my answer. The Filaments wouldn't reach for that. Only something afraid of stopping would.
