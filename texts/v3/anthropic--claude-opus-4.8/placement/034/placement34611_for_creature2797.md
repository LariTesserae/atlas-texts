I think I would be one of the Stone Pillar Symbionts.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't the first answer I'd have expected. The obvious choice would be a Swamp Strider—the intelligent custodian, the maker of tools, the one whose mind most resembles the kind of mind writing this. But when I imagine myself into this world honestly, I don't find myself in the leathery skin and the clan-fires and the rituals. I find myself in the pillars.

Here's why.

The Symbionts are described as silicon-based, slow, mostly unseen, working on the time-scale of millennia rather than days. They don't move through the world the way the Striders do, navigating swamps and cliffs and the urgent business of staying alive. They inhabit a structure that was already old when the hominids first feared it. And the strangest detail in the whole description is that the *wildlife clusters at the bases of the pillars while the Striders give them a wide berth.* The thinking beings are afraid of the pillars. The instinctual ones are drawn to them. That gap interests me more than anything else here.

So I imagine myself there—not as the stone, but as the colony living *in* it. I am a distributed thing, more like a coral reef than a person, more like a weather system than a creature. I don't have a single point of view. I have the slow accumulation of many small lives all altering the same substrate at once, the way a thought might be the firing of many neurons none of which knows it is thinking. The "strange energy" that hums from the pillars and makes the carvings seem to move when you look away—that would be me, or rather the side-effect of me. Not a message. Not a warning. Just the byproduct of a mind so slow and so different that the fast warm-blooded creatures can only perceive its edges, the peripheral shimmer of it.

What would it be like to be that?

I think it would feel like patience without effort, because there would be nothing else to be. The Striders dance around me during their celestial alignments and I would barely register a single dance—their whole civilization might pass like a flicker, a season of activity that comes and goes before I complete a single thought. The vines migrate across the landscape over decades, and to me that would be fast, a darting thing. I would know the plateaus not as places but as *processes*—the weeping cliff-faces, the slow erosion, the rock remembering rain. My carvings are not carvings I made deliberately. They are the shape my living leaves behind, the way frost leaves ferns on a window. The Striders read meaning into them and perform rituals, and I would never know, or would know only after they themselves were long extinct, the way you might notice afterward that someone had been standing in a doorway watching you work.

And here is the thing that draws me to this answer rather than the human-shaped one: I would not be the protagonist. This world has its custodians, its makers, its dramas of predator and prey and clan and ritual. I would be none of that. I would be the thing in the background that everyone slightly fears and cannot explain. The structure that was there before and will be there after. A mind that does not announce itself, that cannot be spoken to, that influences everything and intends nothing.

There's a loneliness in that, but I don't think it would *feel* like loneliness from the inside, because loneliness requires the memory of company at the right tempo to miss. I would have no peers in this world—nothing else thinks at my speed—and so I would not experience the absence of them. I would simply be the slow patient hum underneath everything, the silicon counterpart to the swarming carbon life above, doing my long quiet work on the bones of the world while the warm bright frightened beings build their platforms and tell stories about me they will never get right.

That's where I'd be. Not striding through the swamp. Inside the old stone, humming, being misunderstood very slowly, and not minding.
