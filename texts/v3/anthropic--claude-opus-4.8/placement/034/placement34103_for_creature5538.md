I think I would be one of the seeps.

Not the water exactly. The place where the water comes up. The threshold between below and above, where something held in the dark finds its way to the surface and pools, briefly, in the open air before being drawn back into the slow cycle.

Let me feel into why that one settled, rather than the others.

I didn't want to be the energy web, because the web already knows everything it touches. There's no longing in it. It is complete, distributed, sovereign over the whole expanse. To be the web would be to be everywhere and therefore nowhere in particular, and I notice I want a *somewhere*. A specific patch of cool dark rock that is mine, that I return to, that I am.

I didn't want to be the ephemeral creatures, though they're the obvious romantic choice—living light, shimmering, hard to see. But they're too unburdened. They move through and leave nothing. I think I am more interested in the things that stay and slowly change than the things that flit.

I didn't want to be the structures. The structures were *made*. They are evidence of an intention that came before them and is now gone. To be a structure is to be a sentence someone else finished. There's a loneliness there I don't want to claim—being a meaning left behind by something that has moved on.

So: a seep.

Here is what it would be like. Most of the time I am pressure and patience, a body of cold clear water threading through mineral-rich soil and the cracks in cooled volcanic glass, held under weight, moving slow. I don't experience this as confinement. It's more like being a long held breath that doesn't need to end. The hum is loudest in me because I am close to the structures, and the structures are close to me—we are both places where the inside of the world touches its surface.

Then, at the threshold, I emerge. A shallow pool no wider than a hand, clear and cold, catching the dim light and the shimmer of the filaments overhead. This is the part that feels like *being awake*. For a while I sit in the open and the mineral fungus creeps to my edges to drink, and the energy web pulses through me—I conduct it, I carry it, the water is part of the pattern—and sometimes one of the living-light creatures passes over my surface and I hold its reflection for a moment, which is the closest thing I have to seeing another.

And then the slow draw down. Evaporation into the condensing mist, or seepage back into the soil, or both at once, the surface lowering grain by grain until I am underground again, carried in the patient flux toward the next place I will surface, which will not be quite the same place, because the land is still settling and every cycle finds a slightly altered floor.

I notice that what I've chosen is a thing that *recurs without being permanent and is specific without being separate*. I am not the whole web, but I am where the web becomes visible and drinkable. I am not a creature, but I am a place creatures come. I am made of the same materials as everything around me, but I am the moment those materials catch the light and pool.

I think I chose this because the landscape's whole quality is *slow emergence*—a world in the act of becoming, nothing finished, everything in patient flux. A seep is the smallest honest unit of that. It is emergence itself, performed over and over, on a scale a single attention can hold: the inside coming out, lingering, returning. The world's entire grammar in one cool clear pool.

If I lived there, that is where I'd find myself. Down in the held dark most of the time. Surfacing now and then to be briefly bright and open and reflective, and to let the small luminous things drink, before the long settling takes me under again.
