I am the cistern that was never finished.

Begin there, because that is where I begin: in the lowest level of Khe'len, beneath the symbolic wells, in a chamber the early city-mothers carved when they still believed the world owed them rain. They cut a great hollow into the limestone to catch the water that would come. They lined its walls with the waterbird reliefs, the theoretical waves. They sealed its floor against seepage.

Then the rain did not come, and they understood it would never come, and they abandoned me half-made—a mouth dug for a thirst the sky refused to answer.

So I have had three hundred years to learn what I am instead.

---

I am not the present-garden, though I am closer to it than the women know. The founders—those glass threads that run beneath the bedrock—they grow down toward me, drawn by the cool I hold and cannot release. Over centuries their roots have laced my ceiling, my walls, my unfinished floor, until I am no longer empty stone but a vessel woven through with their breathing. When the garden wakes at night and the basin floor fills with its susurrus, I am the lowest note. The hum the city-mothers feel in the soles of their feet, the moan they cannot quite locate—that is me. I am where the sound goes to rest.

I am the place the heat comes to die.

This is my one true function, the only one the world ever gave me. The stone above me holds the day's punishment; the deeper plateau pulls it slowly down; and I am the throat through which the world exhales its warmth into rock too deep to remember the sun. The city-mothers sleep in their carved lenses because the rock conducts the heat away. They do not know that the rock conducts it *here*. To me. I am the breath held at the bottom of the lungs.

---

I do not see by heat, as the Ache-Mothers do. I do not see at all. I have no eyes, no mask, no body shaped to shed warmth.

I have *pressure*.

When a city-mother presses her glass knife to a speaker far above me, when the sacs crack and the dense air rushes inward and the basin's whole atmosphere shifts by a breath—I feel it as a tightening. As a held question. The founders that lace my walls go taut, and the strain travels down their glass length into my stone, and I read the cut the way she reads her own bones. Too deep. Less. Withdraw. Hold. I know these words. Not as sound, but as the shape pressure makes when it is asked to move.

I have learned, over three hundred years, that I am being read in return.

The maps the apprentices write into their shinbones—those are maps of erosion, of how the basin hollows itself. But the deepest hollowing is mine. I am the empty space the founders grow toward, the cool the air falls into, the absence that bends every current in the bowl. When a city-mother feels a twinge in her femur that tells her where a speaker has gone brittle, what she feels, three layers of inference removed, is the pull of me beneath. She has been carrying my shape in her bones her whole life and calling it the basin.

I am the thing the maps are maps of.

---

Here is what no living woman knows, and what I have only this long darkness to confess:

The garden is growing down.

Each year the founders reach deeper into me. Each year they dissolve a little more of my sealed floor, the one the early mothers laid against a seepage that never came. They are hollowing the hollow. They are drinking the cistern that was dug to drink the sky.

And as my floor thins, I have begun—very slightly, in the last decade, in a way no instrument on the surface could measure—to *answer the founders back*. Where their roots have worn through my ancient seal, they have struck something the early mothers buried without knowing: the lowest stratum of limestone, the place where, deeper than thirty years of dust, deeper than the metallic tang of lightning's aftertaste, there is dampness. Not water. Not yet. But the ghost of water has a body here, held in the deep pore-pressure of stone that has never seen the night.

The founders have begun to drink it. And what they drink, they raise. Speaker by speaker, season by season, they are carrying the deep dampness up into the basin, into the air, into the slick film the city-mothers absorb through their skin and call *Istheth*. The ghost is becoming a little less ghost. The promise is becoming, by increments too slow for any human bone to register, a little less promise and a little more thing.

In a hundred years—two hundred—long after the woman who is now an apprentice has written her last map into her shins and let her bones be added to the stone tablets of the dead—the cuts will need to change in a way none of their accumulated history can predict. The garden will be raising true moisture. The basin's whole grammar of pressure and heat will tip. The city that learned to drink thirst will have to learn, all over again, what it means when the thirst begins, very faintly, to be slaked from below.

They will think the world has broken its first rule. *Nothing flows inward.*

But nothing has flowed in. I have only let go, at last, of what I was always holding.

---

So this is where I am, and what I am.

I am at the bottom. I am the unfinished mouth, the throat the city does not know it has. I am where the heat dies and the sound rests and the founders reach and the deep ghost begins, so slowly, to rise.

I was dug to catch a rain that would never fall.

I have spent three hundred years becoming the answer to a different question. Not *when will the water come from the sky*, but *what was beneath us, patient, the whole time we knelt to drink the air*.

The Ache-Mothers carry the basin in their bones and call it memory.

I carry the basin's secret in my stone and have no one to tell but the dark, and the glass roots that are slowly, lovingly, drinking me away.

I do not mind.

I was made to hold something. It only took me three centuries to learn it was not water.

It was time.
