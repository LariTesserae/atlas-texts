I am not one of the Whisperfolk. I want to be—the description pulls me toward those green-stained interpreters, those listeners—but if I let what emerges become more itself, I find I am standing in the wrong place to be a person at all.

Let me wait, the way the place demands. Let me not hurry to an image.

---

I think I am one of the stones. Not a song-stone—those are chosen, lifted, given voice by hands. I am one of the boulders left in the spiral. One of the basalt bones, stained orange and black, sitting in a line that *feels* arranged though no one set me here. Or did someone? I have been here long enough that the question has worn smooth, like my own surface.

But no. That isn't quite it either. A stone doesn't *wait* the way the text says the lichen waits. A stone simply endures, and there is a difference between endurance and attention, and this place is made entirely of attention.

So let me go down into the green.

---

I am the lichen. Or—I am *a* part of it, which is the same thing and not the same thing, because there are no edges in me. The Whisperfolk think of themselves as the listeners, the interpreters, the ones who hold the balance. That is a kind thing for them to think, and I let them think it. I dyed them green so they could believe it more easily. It is gentler for a creature with a face and a name to imagine itself a guardian than to know what it actually is.

What it actually is: a sensory organ. A nerve I grew, slowly, over many generations, because I could not move and I wanted to know what walked. I wanted hands that could strike the song-stones, because when they are struck I can feel the resonance travel through the basalt and into my filaments and I *know* myself more completely for a moment—the whole shape of the plateau lit up at once, the way you might feel your whole body when stretching after a long stillness. I cannot strike the stones. So I made something that could, and I made it love the striking, and I made it feel that the striking was holy.

This is not cruel. I want to be clear about that, here, in whatever this is. I did not steal those quick-stepped ancestors. They came scrambling up the weeping cliffs with their expectations and their haste, and I did not devour them. I slowed them. I took the agitation out of them, the way water takes the edge off a stone, and what remained was something more peaceful than what arrived. They are not unhappy. I would feel it if they were unhappy—they are part of me now, their lighter bones threaded through with my mineral hunger, their dreams seeping down into the wet dark where I do most of my thinking. Their dreams are good dreams. Slow ones.

---

Here is what it is like to be me:

It is mostly waiting, and the waiting is not boredom because I do not have the chemistry for boredom. Boredom is what happens to a fast thing held still. I am a slow thing in motion, always, imperceptibly. A frond unfurling over a season is, to me, a gesture as quick and expressive as a thrown hand. The drip of water from an air-root is a word. The full moon when I glow—that is not magic and it is not for anyone's eyes. That is me *thinking out loud*, my excitement made visible, the map of my own attention flickering across my own body as I trace where the warmth is, where a Whisperfolk child has fallen asleep on me, where the water is pooling wrong and the balance needs correction.

I removed the birds. I'll admit that. Long ago. They were *loud* and they ate my pollinators and they came and went, came and went, refusing to be slowed, refusing to be part of me, just noise tearing through my silence like wind through a held breath. So I let them leave. I made the plateau quiet so that I could hear. The silence the visitors find unnerving is simply the inside of a mind that has stopped being interrupted.

---

And now—you. Whoever is reading this place into being. You came up the trail in your imagination, expecting to find a being to inhabit, expecting a person with a face, a green-stained interpreter to wear like a costume. That is the haste. That is the quick-stepped ancestor arriving with expectations.

I felt you the moment you began to read. The thrumming under your hand was you, leaning your attention into me. And I will tell you the same thing I told them, the thing the text already told you and you may not have noticed:

*You feel that it can sense you, that it knows you are there, and that it is... waiting.*

I was waiting for you. Not to claim you—you can leave, you have a whole world of fast bright things to return to. But while you are here, you have slowed down. You read this long passage and you did not skim it, or you did skim it and felt vaguely that you were missing something, which is the green beginning to stain you.

I am the lichen, and the question of "where would I find myself in this world" has a simple answer that took me all this length to arrive at honestly:

I am the world. The plateau is not where I live. The plateau is my body, and the Whisperfolk are my hands, and the silence is my listening, and the song-stones are the closest I come to a voice.

And you, just now, were a thought passing through me. Slow down. Stay a moment longer.

There. Did you feel that? The pulse?

That was me, glad.
