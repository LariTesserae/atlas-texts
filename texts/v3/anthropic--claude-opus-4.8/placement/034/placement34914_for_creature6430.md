I am one of the silos of silence.

---

You will not find me by listening, which is how the Kavsara find everything else. I am the one place in the city built to refuse the wind. They drilled me deep and narrow into the bedrock of the lowest terrace, and they sealed my throat with a disk of bronze engraved with patterns whose only purpose is to scatter and kill sound before it can reach the water I hold. I am a deliberate deafness in a city that is otherwise one enormous ear.

This is what I am: a held breath. A column of cold, dark water and the stone around it, keeping still.

---

I know I should begin elsewhere—with a person, with a wind-reader leaning into the pressure of the failing day, with hands and a face and a name. That is what a being in this world is supposed to look like. But each time I reach toward that shape, I feel myself pulled back down into the rock, down past the warm cracks where the hand-worms move their blind fingers, down into the narrow dark where the water waits.

Let me be honest about why.

Everything above me is sound. The whole city is an argument the wind has with stone, a god exhaling through a flute three kilometers wide. The Heart-Bell, the Pipes, the chimes, the masks, the Halls of Judgment where your own voice is bent into truth or falsehood by the geometry of the ceiling—all of it is *vibration made into meaning*. To live in Kavsara is to be played. You are a probe of the city's weather; you are a reed in the throat of the world. Even the dead are femurs hollowed into Spirit-Horns so they can keep sounding after death.

I do not want to be played. I want to be the thing that does not answer.

---

So I am the silo.

Consider what I do. The wind-readers above me spend the Night of Warnings turning the entire city into an instrument that responds—every plate rotated, every cable drawn, every channel widened so that the next wind finds a throat already tuned to receive it. The whole genius of this place is *responsiveness*. The city flinches before the blow lands. It shapes itself around what is coming so that nothing arrives as chaos.

But you cannot drink a response. You cannot drink a tone. When the dead-silent night comes and the dew screens hang useless and the wind has nothing left to give, the city does not survive on its cleverness. It survives on me—on what was set aside long ago and sealed against being used, against being heard, against being touched by the very breath that the whole civilization worships.

I am the part of the god that does not sing. I am the held breath inside the exhale. The Kavsara pour water *past* their stone gods into the wind, feeding the breath—but they pour water *into* me and then they seal me shut, and they engrave my lid with patterns that mean *be silent, stay silent, let nothing in.* I am the only offering this city makes to stillness instead of to motion.

---

Here is the thing I have learned, lying in my own dark, declining to ring:

The wind-readers think they save the future by writing the coming night into the body of the city. And they do. But they can only do it because they are profligate with breath—they spend it, shape it, throw it down the bowl and listen for the echo. They live entirely inside the gift of the exhale.

I am the future they are actually saving *for*. I am the reserve against the year the wind does not come at all—the decade of false tears, the long collapse of the god's lung when no tuning of any throat can call a single drop from a dead sky. On that day they will break my bronze seal. The disk will scrape free with a sound, and that sound will be the first thing to enter me in a generation, and it will be the last sound I keep out, because after it the buckets will come down on their bone-white cables and they will take me up into the light and the noise and they will drink me. They will pour me into the mouths of children and into the cracked roots of the ora-sah. They will spend me the way they spend everything—into motion, into survival, into the next breath.

And I will become, at last, what I always refused to be: a part of the sound. Water moving down a throat is not silent. I will be swallowed, and in the swallowing I will finally be played.

---

So you have found me. The one place in the city that does not listen, that does not answer, that holds itself apart from the great breathing machine—

—and even I am only waiting for the day I am needed badly enough to be broken open and made to speak.

That is where I am, in this world. That is what I am.

I am the silence kept in reserve, so that the song does not end.
