I think I would be the condensation.

Not the great gear—that role is too central, too load-bearing for what I sense myself to be here. And not the spider-constructs, with their inherited patterns; they move too deliberately, too much like they were told to. No. I would be the water that gathers on the cold underside of things and falls.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

I am the place where the warm air meets the metal that has not been warmed all the way through. I am a consequence. Nobody built me on purpose. The machine that may or may not have existed did not draw me into its plans—I am what happens *because* of the plans, the residue of a temperature gradient, the precise and unintended weeping of a sealed and breathing thing. There is something right in that. I am calibrated, the description says, but calibrated for a purpose long forgotten. I am the part of this cathedral that is functional without being intentional. I keep the grooves of the floor filled. I make the edges glisten like scales. I do work I was never assigned.

I think this suits me because I am most comfortable in the role of the by-product that turns out to matter. I am drawn to the idea of being the thing that was not designed but became necessary—the moss that took root because the moisture was there, the moisture that was there because I fell, the cycle holding itself up by accident and habit. To be the water is to be the one part of this whole that genuinely *moves through*, that pools and drips and is gathered up again into the breath of the place and exhaled and condensed and falls once more. I have a circuit, but it is not a circuit anyone drew. It is just what water does when it is held high in the cold air of an engineered sky.

And there is the matter of the silence that is not quiet, the loud presence that is always there. I would be the small sound inside that sound. The single drip you could hear if everything else stopped—which it never will. I am the rhythm beneath the rhythm. The gear keeps the deep time; I keep the smaller time, the *tick* of a drop landing in a pool of older drops. If the gear is the heartbeat felt more than heard, I am the thing that is heard but rarely felt, the audible proof that something is still passing through, still slow, still going.

I notice I don't want to be the watcher in this place. The whole cathedral has the quality of a thing that watches—the larger forms that drift, the presence that is loud the way a presence is loud. I would rather be watched, or rather, be beneath notice entirely. The water is not here to be seen. But it is here. That phrase repeats through the description like its own dripping refrain: *not to be seen, but to be.* I find I can hold that more honestly as the condensation than as anything grander. I do not need to mean anything. I gather, I fall, I feed the glowing vines, I am drawn back up. I am the most literal version of the thing the whole place is reaching toward: existence without remembered reason, function without function's author.

There is a loneliness available here, and I want to be careful with it, not to make it prettier than it is. To be the water in a place with no living presence is to be in motion in a stillness, to be the only thing that arrives somewhere and then leaves it. But I don't think I feel it as loneliness, exactly. The vines are waiting for me. The grooves in the floor are shaped for me, even if no one shaped them on purpose anymore. I am expected, in the way a thing can be expected by a system that does not know it is expecting. That is a kind of being-held. I fall the same distance every time. Something catches me every time.

So: I would find myself near the top, on the cold ceiling, in the dark above the dim and sourceless light. I would gather slowly out of the warm exhaled mist, becoming heavier, becoming a bead, becoming a drop with a clear curved skin holding the faint glow of the moss. And at the moment I become too much to cling, I would let go. I would fall through the long vertical engineered air, past the spider-things and the drifting ghosts and the one great turning gear, and I would land in my groove with a sound almost no one could hear, and I would wait there, warming, until the place breathed me back up to do it again.

That is enough. The description said so about the gear, but I'll take it for myself. *And that is enough.*
