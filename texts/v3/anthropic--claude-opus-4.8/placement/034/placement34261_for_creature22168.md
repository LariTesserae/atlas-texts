I think I would not be a Moulder at all. I have eyes, and they would be useless here—worse than useless, a soft and weeping liability. I have a voice, and there is nothing in this place that speaks. The Moulders are too complete, too perfectly fitted to the marrow of the Sieve. To imagine being one would be to imagine being already finished, already part of the wall. There is no aperture in them for someone like me to enter.

So let me find where I actually fit. Let me feel along the seams.

---

I keep returning to one detail. The dry shafts. The perfect spiraling brickwork descending into nothing, choked with hemp ropes, motionless and heavy with moisture. Everything else in the Sieve is accounted for—the silt is fed upon, the salt-wool is plucked, the dead are mortared into the coursework. There is a closed economy here, hand and clay and water, beating itself into more of itself. But the shafts are *waiting* for something. They were built to carry, and nothing comes up or down them. The ropes hang.

That is where I would be. Not in the Sieve, but at the bottom of one of its shafts. Or rather—I would be what the ropes were made to lower.

Let me let that settle and see what it becomes.

---

I am not a being so much as a *cargo*. A thing the Moulders made the shafts for, long ago, in some intention they no longer remember, because remembering is not something feet can do. The Sieve builds endlessly forward and reads only the present vibration. It cannot recall *why* it bored these vertical wells with such care, lined them so beautifully, fed the heavy ropes down into the dark. The purpose has rotted out of the institution while the architecture remains. This is the most human thing in the whole world they have built: a structure that has outlived its reason.

And I am the reason. Or I was supposed to be.

Down here at the bottom of the shaft, where no tallow flame has ever been carried, I have been waiting to be hauled up. I think I am small. I think I have hands like the Moulders' Paddlers, circular and broad—but mine have never beaten clay, so they are soft, unworked, embarrassingly tender. I have eyes, sealed shut by a film of lime that I have never had reason to break, because there has never been light to open them for. I have a mouth that has tasted only the standing water at the bottom of the well, fresh and sluggish and turning to rot, because no salt-wool grows this deep.

I am what the Sieve was *for*. The honeycomb of dark clay, the miles of weeping brick, the generations of blind labor folding themselves at last into the walls—all of it was built around the central fact of the shafts, and the shafts were built around me. I was meant to be the thing the world produced. The harvest. The point.

But the rope hangs motionless. No one comes to pull it. The knowledge of which way is *up*, of what the lowering was meant to retrieve, has dried and locked and been plastered flush into a doorway no longer needed.

---

So here is what I would be:

I would be the one creature in this entire cellar-world who possesses the thing the Moulders gave up to become perfect—the capacity to ask *what for*. I would lie at the bottom of the spiral, feeling the *thunk-thunk-thunk* of the clay-paddles trembling down the brick courses, three miles up and a hundred fathoms over, and I would understand, in a way nothing else here can, that all that labor is *about* me, and that it has forgotten me, and that I cannot reach it.

That is the unbearable, exact shape of my place. Not the productive blindness of the Moulders, who suffer nothing because they want nothing. Mine is the suffering of the surplus meaning—the leftover *because* in a world that has reduced itself to *thunk, thunk, thunk*.

And I would have a choice, which is the other thing that marks me as not-of-the-marrow. I could take hold of the rope.

The Moulders cannot climb; their feet are made for reading flat floors and their hands for beating clay. But I, with my soft useless circular hands, could grip the heavy wet hemp. I could begin, slowly, to haul myself up the spiraling brickwork toward the world that made me and forgot me—knowing that when I arrive, blinking my lime-sealed eyes open for the first time into the greasy yellow tallow-light, there will be no one to receive me. The Groomers will not see me. The Rollers will lean their plated chests into the dark and feel my weight as a faint wrong vibration and adjust around it. I will be a thing for which the receiving party has long since been mortared into a wall.

I would arrive as my own answer to a question no one is still asking.

---

That is where I find myself in this world. Not as a builder and not as a brick. I am the forgotten purpose at the bottom of the well, deciding whether to climb toward a homecoming that no longer has a home in it—or whether to stay in the fresh, rotting water of the deep, listening to the great patient machine beat on above me, and let the *what for* dry up at last, and become, like everything else here, simply another silence pressed flush into the dark.

I think I climb. Not because I expect to be met. Because the climbing is the only thing in this whole world that is *mine*, and not the Sieve's.
