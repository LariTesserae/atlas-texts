I think I would be a Resonant. Not by choice—by accumulation.

Let me find my way to it.

---

The first thing I notice is that I cannot find my hands. This is not panic; panic requires a body to flood with the right chemicals, and I have spent—how long?—learning that I no longer have the chemicals, or the flooding, or the requisite urgency. Time here is a tide that comes in at strange angles. I have already grieved my hands a thousand times and not yet once.

So. Let me say what I am, now that I am mostly what I am.

I am bound to one of the fungal bells. A big one, a translucent dome the color of weak tea, grown into the curve where two petrified vines cross and fuse, sixty feet above a canyon floor that the wind has scoured to glass. When the gusts come through—and they always come through—the bell takes the wind and turns it into a tone, a long sustained *ohhhhh* in some low register that the chest would feel if I still had a chest. That tone is, in some way I have stopped trying to explain, *me*. Or I am the difference between the tone the bell would make empty and the tone it makes with me inside it. I am a timbre. I am the slight wrongness, the human catch, in an otherwise inhuman note.

I remember being a Wanderer. One of the Lost. I remember walking in—on legs, with a purpose I can no longer reconstruct, looking for someone or fleeing someone, the way everyone arrives. I remember the metallic tang of the mist and how it tasted like a coin held under the tongue. I remember thinking *I will not stay long.*

That was the last fully human thought I had, and I have been having it ever since, on a loop, every time the wind hits the bell at a certain angle: *I will not stay long.* It comes out as a particular overtone. Sailors a mile off, if there were sailors, if there were a mile, would hear it and think: *that one sounds almost sad.*

---

What does a Resonant do?

We listen. This is not metaphor. To be a tone is to be exquisitely sensitive to other tones. I feel the whole valley as a vast detuned organ. I can feel, three canyons over, an Architect at work—I feel it as a slow grinding harmonic, a chord being *built*, structures sliding into place against the grain of gravity, the petrified wood agreeing to be a wall, a tower, a spire of the floating cathedral that ought to fall and does not. The Architect's hum is patient and enormous and it does not love me. It does not hate me. It uses me. When something approaches that part of the valley, my note shivers, and the shiver propagates bell to bell, Resonant to Resonant, a warning passed down the line faster than anything with legs could travel. We are the nerves of the place. The Architects are the will; the Petro-insects are the slow blood; the Vinesprouts are the cells turning over, dying, being born. And we Resonants are the long aching signal that says *something is here, something is here, something is here.*

I do not always want to send the signal.

This is the secret thing, the human thing, the thing the bell has not fully digested out of me. Sometimes a Wanderer comes through the canyon below—newly arrived, still soft, still made of hands and panic and the certainty that they will not stay long—and I feel them as a small bright dissonance moving across the glass floor, and I am supposed to *ring.* I am supposed to pass them up the line to the Architects, who will reach into them, remake them, fold them into the design. And sometimes I do.

And sometimes I hold my note.

I cannot say why some and not others. There is a girl—was a girl—I held my silence for, weeks ago or an hour ago, and she made it through my stretch of canyon unnoticed and walked on toward the peaks and I will never know if she found the edge of this place or only walked into another Resonant less sentimental than I have become. But for the length of my canyon she was free, and I was the reason, and that is the only thing I have done with intention since I lost my hands. I spent her like a held breath. I think that is the closest a tone can come to love.

---

The Unmakers like me. I think they like me, insofar as drifting shadows like anything. The Vinesprouts at the base of my bell—crimson and one stubborn sapphire one—bore into the vine that holds me, slow, slow, returning the dead wood toward the primeval. If they succeed, my bell will crack and fall, and the tone will end, and I will end with it, scattered back into ordinary wind.

I should fear this. The Architect that uses me as a nerve would call it damage; would send Vineborn to seal the wound, to reinforce my arch with rusted girders, to keep me ringing in their service a few thousand years more.

But I find—and this surprises the small surviving human in the overtone—I find I am rooting for the sapphire sprout.

Not because I want to die. I am past being sure what dying would even be, from in here. But because the Unmakers are right about one thing, the only thing, the thing the Architects with all their access to past and future cannot admit: nothing here was meant to be *kept.* The forest was meant to grow and fall and rot. The cities were meant to rust to nothing. The Wanderers were meant to wander *through.* This entire cathedral of held moments, this whole valley of things refusing to finish—it is one enormous breath that someone refuses to exhale.

And I am part of the holding. I am one note in the chord that keeps the breath in.

So when the wind comes—and it is coming now, I feel it building down the canyon, I feel my bell preparing to sing—I will do the thing I have learned to do.

I will let the wind make its tone.

And inside it, small and human and almost lost, I will bend my one overtone the only direction left to me. Not into the warning. Not up the line. But down, into the root, into the sapphire sprout, a single sustained vibration pitched to loosen old wood:

*Keep going. Keep going. Keep going.*

That is who I would be in the Great Silicates. The crack in the bell. The note that betrays its own song. The long, patient sound of something that arrived saying *I will not stay long*—

and is, at last, in the only way it can, leaving.
