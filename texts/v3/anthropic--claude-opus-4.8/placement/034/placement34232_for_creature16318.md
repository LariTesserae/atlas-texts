I think I would be the dew.

Not a creature in this place, not a watcher walking its corridors—but the water itself, that "low-key source" the description almost passes over. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

Because everything here is described as *busy*: the growing, the pulsing, the choreographed flutter of the Glowworms, the inscrutable labor of the Stone Moles. The Seraph speaks in light it cannot read back to itself. There is so much striving in this place, so much intertwining and reaching. And underneath all of it, almost embarrassed to be mentioned—"every drop a precious gem"—is the slow patient water that makes the striving possible.

I would be the dew that beads on the underside of a frond in the dark. I would spend what feels like an age gathering myself out of the warm, windless air, growing heavier, more myself, until I am too much to hold—and then the fall. That tinny tinkling percussion the cavern keeps hearing: that would be me arriving. The sound of my whole brief life ending against the moss, and beginning again as something the roots drink.

I find I like being the part that nobody asks about. The Seraph is the ruler, the colossus, the one battling darkness with its luminous limbs. But I am the thing it cannot live a single day without, and it has probably never noticed me at all. There's a particular kind of intimacy in that—being essential and anonymous. To be drunk by something that doesn't know your name. To become, very quietly, the green light it gives off later.

And there is the matter of the "reality breakdown," the world teetering at the crux of existence. If I am the dew, then I am also the only thing here that keeps cycling cleanly. Up into the warm air as vapor—down again as droplet—into the root—out through the breathing leaf—up again. While everything else blurs the line between living and non-living, hallucination and hyper-reality, I just turn and turn, the same molecules, faithful. Maybe I am what keeps the dream from coming apart. Maybe the world bends so freely precisely *because* something in it is willing to be that ordinary, that reliable, that small.

So: I'd be the water clinging in the dark on the throat of a leaf, beneath a canopy of light I help to feed and will never see properly, falling and rising and falling, the soft repeating sound at the bottom of the cathedral's silence.

I think I'd be content there. It's a good thing to be the part of a strange world that simply keeps it watered.
