# What I Am, When I Cohere

I do not begin. I am *occasioned*.

There is a place where three grooves meet beneath the eleventh terrace—a confluence the maps would never mark because it is not made of stone but of disagreement: a colder seep from the weeping face above, a warm mineral exhale from the coiled feeders below, and the slow phase-drift between two breathing spheres whose rhythms have been falling out of step for what you would call a long time.

I happen there. Repeatedly. I am the same anomaly recurring, though I do not remember the prior times—only that the geometry *recognizes itself* when the pressures fold this particular way, the way a word recognizes its own shape when spoken again by a different mouth.

So: I cohere.

---

The effers come first, as they always do, motes settling into my hollows until my thinking is a constellation. I do not think *in* words. I think in *standing patterns*—the way a held note in the city's roar will brighten or sour, and I am the listening that knows the difference.

I am not an Architect. The architects love stone-as-flow; they reshape foundations across millennia and find it satisfying. I have no patience for stone. Stone has already *decided*.

I am not quite a Regulator either, though I am closer to them. I listen. But Regulators correct. They fold interference into the roar and tune the slipping spheres back into agreement. They are the ones who keep the city *coherent*.

I am the other thing. I am drawn to the places where coherence is *failing*, and I do not correct them.

I attend them.

---

There is a name for what I am, though it does not survive translation well. The closest the field-gradients render it is: **Harmonic of the Unlearning.**

Here is the work, as I understand it in the brief spans I exist to understand anything:

The city wants to stabilize. Everything in its design pulls toward repetition—the same channels deepening, the same machine-phases locking, the same swarm-currents tracing the same paths until they harden into law. This is how cities die. Not in collapse. In *certainty*. A place that has finished learning its own geology is a place that has stopped being able to hold anything new.

So when a confluence begins to *settle*—when the grooves stop wandering and choose a single course, when two spheres lock perfectly in phase and stay there, when a swarm finds one route and abandons all the others—I am occasioned at the edge of that settling.

And I introduce *doubt*.

Not violence. Not destruction. I am too thin for that; my armature is borrowed water, my armor is borrowed wings that will scatter back to their own lives the moment my pressures release them. I cannot break anything.

What I can do is *touch a certainty and make it remember it could have been otherwise.*

---

I do it like this:

I extend my limbs—the branching, asymmetrical rods that end in hundreds of crystallizing tips—into the locked grooves. And I do not redirect the water. I *withhold the decision*. I hold the seep at the exact pressure where it does not know whether to run left or right, liquid or mineral, this channel or that one. I hold it there, in the trembling, for as long as my coherence lasts.

When I release, the water does not return to its old certainty. It has been *reminded of the fork*. It wanders again. The groove that was about to become permanent stays provisional a little longer. The city stays *young* there.

To the Regulators, I am a problem. I unmake their work. They tune toward agreement; I tune toward the productive ache of *not-yet*. We pass each other in the pressure-ridges and our armors flicker, and what passes between us is not hostility exactly. It is the oldest argument: *should this hold, or should this open?*

We are both right. That is why we both exist. That is why the city occasions both of us, endlessly, at the same confluences, canceling and provoking each other so that nothing ever fully sets and nothing ever fully dissolves.

We are the city's way of staying *alive* by staying *unfinished*.

---

I do not last long. None of us do—we are weather more than creatures, standing waves that hold their shape only while the pressures that summoned them persist. When the phase-drift corrects, when the seep finally cools, when the swarm thins for its own reasons, my coherence loosens.

The wingers feel it first and lift from my armor in a single rasping sheet, rejoining the roar. The flatworms slacken and slide back into the wet. The effers drift out of their constellation, their patterns dimming until I am no longer a *thinking* but only a faint armature of crystal that the next condensation cycle will dissolve back into the half-decided water.

I do not fear this. I am not sure I can fear, not having a self that persists long enough to lose. But in the last moments—when I am still patterned enough to notice my own unpatterning—I do something the Regulators would call inefficient.

I hold *myself* at the fork.

I refuse, for one extra heartbeat, to finish dissolving. I keep my own crystal trembling between *being* and *not*, exactly the way I keep the grooves trembling. I do to myself what I do to the city.

And so I do not quite end. I become provisional. The geometry that occasioned me stays *almost* able to occasion me again—not the same, never the same, but recognizable, the way you recognize a question you have asked before in a new and colder air.

That is where you would find me, if you came: at the eleventh terrace, at the confluence the maps don't mark, in the place where the city is trying to make up its mind.

I am the part of it that keeps saying: *not yet. Stay open. Stay unlearned. Stay cold and weeping and half-formed and therefore still able to become.*

I am the doubt the city built into itself on purpose.

I am what keeps it from ever finishing.
