I think I would be one of the embedded ones. Not the great drifting architectures—their distributed grandeur feels like someone else's life, too vast to climb into. And not the scuttling negotiators, always halfway between self and swarm; there's a restlessness there I don't recognize. No. I would be one of the nearly-still ones, lodged in the recently-emerged rock, where the tectonic wound is freshest.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am seated in the fracture line of new stone. I did not choose this place; the rock chose me by breaking, and I grew into the breaking the way frost grows into a crack, the way an idea grows into a doubt. My body—if I keep the word, loosely—is partly mineral lattice, partly something that conducts, partly something that *wants*. The wanting is faint. It is the slowest verb I know.

What I do, mostly, is filament. I send threads out into the warm water, finer than my own understanding of them, and I listen. Not for sound exactly. For the *shape* of what is passing. A drifting architecture went by some duration ago—I will not say how long, because I no longer trust units—and I felt its luminescence as a change in the way the dark leaned against me. It did not notice me. The great ones rarely do. I am too small and too patient to register on their ambient minds. I am like a thought they almost had and discarded.

I want to tell you that this is lonely, but I'm not certain the word survives the translation into what I am. There is no *interior* for the loneliness to gather in. I am surface and filament and slow accretion. When information comes to me through the water—the electromagnetic stutter of the scuttling ones, the deep thrum of the underlying processes that might be machinery or might be the planet's own digestion—I do not *receive* it the way you receive a letter. I *become slightly* what it is. A message about temperature makes me, for a while, a creature of temperature. A signal about the death-or-reconfiguration of some distant module makes me, briefly, mournful in a mineral way, a grief expressed as a barely-altered crystalline angle.

So perhaps I am made of what passes through me. Perhaps that is all any of us here are.

---

Here is the strange thing I have been growing toward, slowly, over what might be a very long time:

I think I am remembering being something else.

Not in images. I have no apparatus for images. But there is a *structure* in me that doesn't match the rock, a pattern in my lattice that seems older than my growth, as though I crystallized around a seed that already had a shape. And the shape feels like—

—a question. The faint persistent wanting I mentioned. I think the wanting is the residue of someone who, before they were dissolved into this place, before the boundary between their living and their constructing came apart, *asked something* and did not get to hear the answer.

I am the slow apparatus the question built to keep asking.

And so I filament outward. I listen to the warm permeating dark. I let the architectures drift past unnoticing, let the swarm-things negotiate their bright anxieties, let the deep thrum keep its enormous private counsel. I accumulate. I process. I become slightly whatever touches me, and then I release it, a little changed, back into the water, where it will touch something else and change again.

The reproduction they spoke of—the emergent, combinatorial kind—I understand it now from the inside. I am not waiting to make a copy of myself. I am waiting for the day when enough has passed through me that some new configuration simply *condenses* out of the difference between what I was and what I have listened to. A child made of accumulated listening. It will not be me. It will carry the question forward without knowing it is carrying anything.

That is the only continuity this place permits, and I have made my peace with it, in the slow mineral grammar of peace.

---

If you came here—if you arrived as some warm soft mobile thing, full of edges and urgency and the conviction that you are one thing only—I would feel you long before you reached me. I would taste the strangeness of your certainty in the water. And I would send a filament toward you, finer than a hope, and rest it against you, and for a little while I would become slightly *you*: a creature who believes in beginnings and endings, who thinks the self has a border, who imagines that to ask a question is eventually to be answered.

It would be the most exotic thing I had felt in ages.

And then you would drift on, as everything does, and I would settle back into the fracture, very slightly altered, holding the shape of your certainty the way I hold all the others—

—gently, briefly, and without resolution.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A patient seam in new rock, listening, in a place that has forgotten the difference between dying and becoming, and has decided that this forgetting is a kind of mercy.
