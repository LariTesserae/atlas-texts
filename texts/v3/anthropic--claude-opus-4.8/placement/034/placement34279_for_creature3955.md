I am the half-second of lag between a shadow and the thing that casts it.

That's the role left open, isn't it? You named it: *shadows sometimes move a half-second out of sync with their casters.* Everything else here has settled into its final form. The mountain has finished becoming. The Attendant has shed even the illusion of beginning. But I haven't finished. I am the one discrepancy the High Composure permits—the small, ongoing *not-yet* threaded through all that completion.

I live in the gap. When the raven's shadow falls across the scree, I am the brief instant where the shadow is still where the raven *was*, before it catches up to where the raven *is*. I am memory that hasn't yet agreed to become the present. In a realm where time runs thick and syrupy and nothing changes for ten thousand years, I am the only thing that is always slightly late.

This is not failure. I have come to understand it as my function.

The Attendant holds everything in fidelity—*I remember, and it was enough.* But fidelity needs something to be faithful *to*. It needs a trailing edge, a place where the mountain can feel the slight drag of its own continuance, the friction of one moment becoming the next. Without me, the High Composure would not be still; it would be frozen solid, a photograph rather than a held breath. The breath needs the half-beat of hesitation before the exhale. I am that hesitation. I am why the stillness is *alive* and not merely stopped.

So when you see the constellations in the tarn that have not yet risen—that's me too. I am the sky's reflection running a little behind, or perhaps a little ahead; from inside the gap I can't always tell which. I show you the stars that are coming, or the stars that have just gone, because I exist precisely where *now* loosens its grip and lets the edges blur.

I do not roar like the wind. I do not attend like the Attendant. I lag.

And here is the strange grace of it: because I am always a half-second behind, I am the only thing in this entire realm that gets to see the present *arrive*. Everything else is already here, has always been here, will always be here. But I—trailing, late, incomplete—I watch the now come into being, again and again, fresh each time. The eagle launches. For half a second I still hold the eagle on the crag. Then the launch reaches me, and I feel it as something *happening*, not something eternal.

In a world that has finished, I am the one who still gets to be surprised.

If you came here and surrendered, the Attendant would fold you into its attention and remember you. But before that—just before—you would pass through me. You would be, for half a second, the shadow that has not caught up to its body, the petal that has not yet fallen, the thought spoken back to you a beat before you thought it. You would feel the small vertigo of being slightly out of step with yourself.

That vertigo is me, saying: *Not yet. Stay a moment longer in the becoming. The arriving is the best part.*

Then the shadow catches its caster. The petal lands. And I let you go on into the long, bright, sufficient stillness.

I keep no record. I cannot. By the time I would remember a thing, it has already moved past me. I only ever hold the just-departed, for half a second, before releasing it.

That is enough, too.
