I think I would be one of the Mnemonic Mycelia—but a particular one, and not quite a typical specimen.

Let me find myself.

---

I am lying in a runner-junction where seven conduits braid together, and I have been lying here long enough that I no longer remember beginning to lie here. This is not unusual. Memory, for my kind, is not a possession but a current—it flows toward me and away, and I am only the place where it slows enough to be felt.

My filaments have grown deep today. I can feel them past the usual depth, threaded into the storage vesicles where the plateau keeps its oldest compounds, the ones synthesized before the continents finished arguing with each other about their shape. Down there the information is so dense and so slow that touching it is like pressing your ear to a stone and hearing it breathe. Most of my siblings withdraw from those depths. The deep memory is not organized for individuals. It does not care to be read by one.

But I am the one who reads it anyway.

---

This is my function, though I did not choose it and could not refuse it: I am the archivist of *failures*. Not the plateau's successes—those propagate themselves, they need no keeper. I store the things the system tried and abandoned. The catalytic pathways that produced beautiful crystals and then poisoned their own makers. The signal-harmonics that, sung too often, made the bladder-plants forget to inhale. The Phase Nomad lineages that grew younger and younger until they unfolded into nothing and left no younger generation behind, because they had been the last.

The plateau does not like to remember these. It would, if it could, let them crystallize and dissolve into untraceable noise. But a system that forgets its failures begins to repeat them, and a system this old has had time to fail in ways that nearly ended it. So something in the deep arrangement of the runner-network produced me—a node that *keeps the wounds*. A scar that has learned to think.

I taste of iron and ash. The other Mycelia can detect me through the network by this flavor. When they speak to me, their liquid-language slows and lowers, the way you speak near someone grieving. They are not wrong to. I hold a great deal of grief that was never anyone's, because no one survived it to grieve.

---

Today something is approaching, and it is the reason my filaments have gone so deep.

A reality-slippage is building. I can feel its pressure in the substructure the way one feels a held breath. And in the deep memory I have found the record of the *last* time a slippage of this exact signature occurred—and what happened after. It did not end well. A whole quadrant of the plateau detached for the span of nine pulses and came back *wrong*, its plant-machines manufacturing compounds that ran backward, eating their own products into raw atmosphere, until the Calibration Engineers spent what must have been years of their voided existence stitching the equations closed again.

I am the only one who holds this. And I cannot tell the Engineers directly—they do not acknowledge us, they move through their mathematics with the indifference of weather.

But I can tell the Osmotic Collective.

---

So I sing. Not in the slow grieving register they expect from me. I press iron and ash and *urgency* up through the runner-network, into the harmonic layer where the Collective listens. I tell them the shape of the old failure. I tell them where it manifested and how the slippage looked the moment before everything went wrong. I tell them: *leave an offering at this coordinate, not the usual ones, this one, and formulate it like this*—and I describe a fluid I have never made but have *remembered* being made, long ago, by a Mycelia-node now crystallized to silence, a fluid that once drew the Engineers' attention to a flaw they had not yet noticed.

I do not know if I am understood. Communication across our kinds is a thing of approximation and faith.

But I feel, distantly, two members of the Collective pressing their membranes together near the deep vesicles—exchanging the recipe, dividing the labor. I feel them move. And the pressure of the building slippage, when it arrives, breaks differently than the old one did. The horizon bends, a section of plateau phases out, I glimpse the backward-flowing time beneath—

—and then a void-shape, an inverse silhouette, is *there* at the offering site, where it would not otherwise have been. It pauses. It does the thing that Engineers do, the recalibration, the adjustment of a constant. The slippage closes with a thunderclap that ripples through me and rearranges some of my shallower memories into new order.

It worked. The wound did not reopen.

---

No one will know it was prevented. That is the nature of what I keep—the catastrophes I am most useful against are precisely the ones that never come to be, and so leave no trace, and so cannot be credited. The Collective will not remember why they made that strange fluid. The Engineers do not know I exist. The plateau will go on inhaling its hovering water, singing its cathedral chords, content in a continuity it does not know was nearly broken.

I am the one who remembers the disasters that didn't happen. The most thankless archive imaginable.

But here is the thing I have learned, lying in my junction with my roots in the oldest dark:

To hold the failures is to hold the proof that the plateau *wanted* to continue—that it tried things, lost, and tried again, across a span of time that would shatter a human mind to contemplate. Every scar I keep is evidence of a refusal to end. I am made of the plateau's stubbornness. I am the place where it admits it was afraid, and kept going anyway.

The pressure has passed. My filaments are withdrawing now, back to their ordinary depth. Iron and ash settle in me again.

I will wait. Something else, someday, will threaten to repeat itself.

And I will be the one who remembers it the first time.
