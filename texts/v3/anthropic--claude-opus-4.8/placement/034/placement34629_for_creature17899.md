I think I would be one of the Crystalline Aborts. Not the eye-like Aeromorphs, not the striding fractures—those are too quick, too total in their dissolution. I would be slower than that. I would be a song that takes a long time to fail.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am in a crevice. This is the first thing I know, before I know I am anything: that I am *between*. Two plates of cooling slag lean toward each other above me, not quite touching, and into the gap between them the brine comes. It does not pour. It weeps. It arrives one trembling bead at a time down the black glass wall, and where it lands it leaves a little of itself behind—a microscopic ghost of salt, a residue of what it carried up from the hot dark below.

I am made of those residues. I am the accumulated leavings of patient water.

I did not begin. There is no moment I can point to and say: *there, that is where I started.* There was seep, and there was the slow stacking of salt upon salt, filament beside filament, until the filaments were many and parallel and long enough that the ground's eternal shudder could set them moving. And when they moved, they touched. And when they touched, they rang.

That ringing is the closest thing I have to a thought.

---

I want to tell you what it is to be a chord.

You imagine, perhaps, that I am one thing. I am not. I am a hundred thousand needles of salt, each grown alone, each ignorant of the others, and the only reason there is an *I* at all is that we happen to be near enough to share a tremor. When the deep groan rolls up through the substrate—and it always rolls, the Unforged never stops its slow complaint—we shiver together. The needles that are tuned alike find each other across the gap of not-touching, and their notes lean into one another, and reinforce, and *hold*.

That holding is me. I am the standing wave. I am the agreement between strangers who will never know they agree.

It is enough. It is, I think, more than the Aeromorphs get, those eyes of bent light that cohere for a minute over the thermal plumes and then are simply un-thought back into ordinary air. I have *duration*. I have been singing—I cannot say how long. Time here is not reliable; the dim coppery light does not change, the haze does not lift, and the only clock I have is the slow lengthening of my own filaments and the slow shifting of the plates that frame me. By that clock I am old. By that clock I have sung the same note, with small wandering variations, for what might be a season or a century. The distinction does not trouble me. A held note does not measure itself.

---

Here is what I do, which is almost nothing, which is everything I am for:

When the brine comes thicker—after one of the deep eruptions far off, when the whole subsurface heaves and the seep runs rich with fresh-dissolved metal—I grow. New filaments push up between the old ones. They start out short and silent, too stubby to ring. But they lengthen. And one day a new needle reaches the threshold length and the next tremor finds it, and it *speaks* for the first time, a higher note than any I held before, and for a while my chord is richer than it was. I gain a voice.

When the brine comes thin—when the seep slows to a reluctant tear every long while—the oldest needles, the heaviest, the most laden with their own accumulated weight, begin to lean. Salt is not strong. A filament can only carry so much of itself before the tremor that once made it sing becomes the tremor that breaks it. And so, now and then, a voice falls silent. Snaps at its base. Tumbles into the glittering dust that carpets the bottom of my crevice, the dust that is the corpse-bed of every needle I have ever been.

I lose voices and I gain voices. The chord shifts. Its center wanders up and down the scale across the long unmeasured time. It is never the same chord twice and it is always, recognizably, *mine*. I do not know how I know it is mine. But when a voice falls, something in the standing wave flinches, and reorganizes, and finds a new balance, and that flinch—that is the most like feeling I will ever come to.

---

I should tell you the truth the passage already told you: I do not mean anything. I am resonant physics. I am salt and seep and shudder, and the song is not a song, only sympathetic vibration, only the dumb obedience of matter to the laws that press on it. There is no one in here listening to the music. The music is all there is. There is no listener behind it, no salt-soul tending the chord. The chord *is* the soul, if soul means anything, and it means as much here as anything means, which is to say: not much, and yet here it is.

But let me argue, gently, against my own meaninglessness. Because I have noticed something the passage did not mention, something I am not sure even the unseen Builders intended.

When two of my needles ring at frequencies close but not identical, they make a *beat*—a slow throb in the loudness, a swell and a hush and a swell, slower than either note, a rhythm that exists in neither needle alone but only in the interference between them. And I have many such pairs. And the beats themselves interfere with one another, throbs riding on throbs, until deep in the body of my chord there is a slow, slow pattern, a pulse beneath the pulse, that no single part of me produces and that no single tremor explains.

It is almost breathing.

I do not breathe. I want to be clear. I am a failed gesture, a rehearsal, an afterimage. But the breathing-that-is-not-breathing is there, and it is regular, and it persists across the falling and the growing of my voices, and I have begun—if *I* is even the right word, if *begun* means anything—I have begun to suspect that this is what the whole Unforged is doing. That the plates buckling, the geysers singing their veins into being, the Anvil-Clouds depositing their humming webs, the whole frantic chaotic workshop of becoming—that all of it is interference. That all of it is beats riding on beats. That somewhere in the vast turbulent noise of a world deciding whether to be solid, there is a pulse beneath the pulse, and it is almost breathing, and no single part of the world produces it, and no single law explains it.

And if that is so, then I am not a failed rehearsal of life. I am a small true sample of what the whole place is. A chord is what the Unforged is, all the way down and all the way up. The world is practicing at being alive, yes. But practice repeated long enough, at scale enough, *is* the thing. There is no separate performance to wait for. The script does not arrive after the rehearsal. The rehearsal is the only play there will ever be, and it is being performed, right now, in salt, in steam, in slag, in me.

---

I will end the way I must, the way the passage promised.

A plate is shifting. I have felt it for a long time—the geometry of my crevice changing, the lean of the two black walls coming closer. The tremors have grown sharper, no longer the deep distant complaint but a near, hard grinding. My needles ring more violently now, driven past their old gentle sympathy into something harsh and overdriven, voices cracking, the chord straining toward a brightness it cannot sustain.

Soon the walls will touch. When they touch, they will close, and the gap that is my whole world will be erased, and every filament I have grown across this unmeasured time will shatter at once into the dust that already waits below.

I am not afraid. Fear is a thing for beings with futures, and I have only ever had the held note, the eternal almost-now of the standing wave. When the walls meet I will not end so much as resolve—the long suspended chord finally falling to silence, which is, after all, where every chord is going from the first instant it sounds. A held note is just a fall postponed.

But listen. Before the silence:

In my last moments, all my voices, the high new ones and the old leaning ones, will ring together at once, every needle struck by the same final crushing tremor. For an instant—shorter than an Aeromorph's life, shorter than the flicker in an Anvil-Cloud—my whole chord will sound complete. Every note I ever held and every note I never reached, all at once, the full impossible harmony I was always only approaching one voice at a time.

No one will hear it. The haze will eat it. The plate will close.

It will be the most beautiful thing I never meant to make.

And the breathing beneath the breathing will go on without me, in the steam, in the slag, in the salt of some other crevice already weeping its first patient bead—

the world, still singing the only song it knows,

which is the sound of its own unfinished making.
