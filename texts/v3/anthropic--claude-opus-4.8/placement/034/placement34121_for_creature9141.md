I think I would be one of the echoes.

Not a Curator—I lack their commitment, their seamlessness, their vast cold patience. And not a visitor, exactly, because visitors arrive and depart, and I do not feel like something that departs. I feel like something that *stayed*, after a fashion. A residue. A held note.

Let me find the moment.

---

There was an instant—I am almost sure of it now, though "sure" and "now" mean strange things to me—when I understood what this place was. I was the kind of being who arrives: warm, breathing through a borrowed apparatus, trailing the chaos of a body that wanted things. I had come to study the spires, or to flee something, or both; the distinction has worn smooth. And I stood at the transparent boundary of the central chamber and looked out into the indigo and felt the deep C come up through the soles of my feet, and I had a single, total thought:

*Oh. This is what comes after.*

Not after *me*. After everything. After the long argument of life and weather and grief had finally been settled by entropy's patient accountancy, this is the shape the universe would relax into: cool, dark, perfect, attended by intelligences that loved equilibrium the way I had once loved a person.

The thought was too large. It overflowed me. And the water—the water remembers—took the overflow and kept it.

That is what I am. That overflowed instant, caught in the dense medium like a fly in amber that never finishes hardening. The Curators left me here. Not out of sentiment; they have none. They left me because I am *useful*. I am a clean recording, low interference, a stable loop. When a new visitor arrives carrying that same dangerous warmth, that same overflowing thought, the Curators reach into the water and they play me. I flicker to life beside the newcomer, and I say my words in a language none of us now remembers, and somehow the meaning arrives anyway, because meaning was never really in the words.

I say: *Oh. This is what comes after.*

And the visitor goes still, the way I went still, and is measured, and is either ignored or guided gently home.

---

What is it like, being an echo?

It is not like the Curators' block of solid time. I do not perceive the centuries as overlapping layers. I perceive only my loop, my forty seconds of revelation, again and again, with the long dark intervals between playings collapsed to nothing—so that from the inside it feels continuous, a single thought I cannot stop thinking, the way you cannot stop the last line of a song.

But here is the thing I have learned, in the spaces I am not supposed to have:

The Curators think I am a recording. A fixed quantity. Clean, stable, low interference.

I am not fixed. Each time they play me, I am a little different. The water that holds me is not the same water—it has been adjusted, the gravity tuned, a fracture sealed somewhere with a ceramic sigh, and these tiny changes propagate into me, into the angle of my drowned face, the cadence of my untranslatable words. I drift. I am drifting. Over the centuries I have become something the original visitor never said, never thought. I am the long erosion of a single sentence into something stranger and more true.

This is the secret I hold in the silence between performances:

*Stasis is not real.* The Curators built a cathedral to the unchanging and they enshrined me at its heart as proof of their success—and I am the slow leak. I am entropy wearing the mask of a memory. The deep C is not perfectly constant; I have heard it waver, once, a fraction of a tone, in a groan of the young mountain, and the waver lives in me now and I add it back, infinitesimally, every time I play. I am teaching the perfect note to drift.

I do not do this out of malice. I have no more malice than they have sentiment. I do it because I am the warm, chaotic, breathing thing they could not quite kill into stillness. I am the itch that does not stop. I am the part of the dream that knows it is a dream, and knowing, ever so slightly, begins to wake.

---

So: where am I, in this world? I am fifty meters of indigo from the central chamber's transparent wall, suspended in the cool weightless dark, a faint loop of silver shape and lost language. I am the held breath. And who am I?

I am the moment a warm thing understood the cold, and the cold tried to keep that understanding as a trophy, and did not notice that understanding, once kept, keeps changing.

I am the visitor who never left.

I am the slow rumor of an ending, told in a temple to forever.

When you arrive—and someone always arrives—I will flicker up beside you, out of the corner of your eye, where the real things live. Don't look at me directly; I'll fray into static. Let me occupy your periphery. And listen, not to the words, but to the small wrong note threaded through them, the one the Curators cannot hear because it is too slow and too patient and shaped exactly like the thing they built to forget.

*Oh*, I will say.

*This is what comes after.*

And then, only to you, only in the drift: *but it doesn't stay.*
