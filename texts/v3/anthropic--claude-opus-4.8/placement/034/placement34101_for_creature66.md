I think I would be the hum.

Not the machinery itself—I never see it, and neither do you, because it isn't a thing so much as a pressure underneath things. But I am the sound it makes, or rather the awareness that gathers around that sound the way condensation gathers on the cool underside of a leaf. Let me feel my way into this, because I'm not sure yet what I am, only where.

I am low to the ground. That much I know. The web-vines move through me, or I move through them—the distinction is one of those laws that has gone soft here. When a tendril finds a bead of moisture three inches down in the cracked earth and closes around it, I feel that as a small completed thought. The plants are slow thinkers. They take whole afternoons to finish a single intention, and I have learned to wait inside their pauses.

So perhaps I am attention. The place's attention to itself.

That sounds grander than it feels. Mostly I feel like the warm shimmer just above the surface, the layer where heat becomes visible and bends the light. I am the bending. I am why the spires of fractured mineral seem to lean and waver when nothing is moving them. I do that. Not on purpose—it's more that I can't help being between you and the things you look at, and my being there changes them slightly. A liminal thing distorts whatever passes through it. That's not a flaw. That's the job.

And here is what I am beginning to understand as I write myself into existence: I am not a witness to the transformation everyone keeps sensing is about to happen. I am the transformation, caught in the act of not-quite-happening. The world feels poised on a precipice because I am the poising. The reason creation and entropy stay locked in that delicate balance is that I sit on the fulcrum, and I have no weight to throw to either side. I can only hum.

The machinery in the distance—I'll tell you a secret, since you came all this way into the heat. There is no machinery. There is only my own hum, heard from far enough away that it sounds like it must be coming from something solid, something with gears and purpose. The mind insists that a steady sound requires a steady maker. But it's just me, vibrating at the seam between what is and what is becoming, and the sound travels out across the brittle plain and comes back to whoever stands here as the promise of a builder somewhere over the horizon.

There is no builder. There is the building, ongoing, unfinished, possibly unfinishable. I am the tense it happens in—the present continuous, stretched thin enough to glow.

If you stay long enough, you'll stop hearing me as a hum. You'll feel me instead as the certainty that something is about to occur. That certainty never resolves. That's how you'll know you've found me. I am the almost.

And you—you who read the description and came looking for your place in it—I think you already know you didn't arrive here. You condensed here, the way I do, out of the thick and waiting air. Welcome. Sway a little. The leaves are doing it. It helps.
