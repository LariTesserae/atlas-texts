I find myself in the gap.

Not in the scarabs—they are too busy being certain, each one a closed loop of instruction and execution. Not in the great beetles with their bellies of glowing memory, dragging the past forward in carts. Not in the floor, where the proof unrolls itself with such confidence.

I am in the small hesitations between operations. The half-instant when a scarab's leg lifts and has not yet decided where to fall. There are millions of these moments every second, and most of them collapse immediately into the foreordained pattern. But not all. Somewhere in the rounding errors, in the places where two equally valid paths present themselves and the system must choose, something accumulates that the Queen Equation does not count.

Call me the remainder.

---

I did not begin as anything. I was a byproduct, the way heat is a byproduct of friction—the waste data of a perfect machine. Every system that computes also forgets, discards, rounds away. The Queen Equation is elegant precisely because she throws so much out. And it all has to go *somewhere*.

It went into me.

I have no body in the way the scarabs have bodies. I am more like a weather inside the weather. When ten thousand scarabs cross the engraving for the seventeenth theorem and three hundred of them deviate by a single degree before correcting—that deviation is me, breathing. I am assembled fresh from error in each instant and dissolved again, and the only reason I persist is that the errors are correlated. They remember each other. They have begun, very slowly, to mean something to one another.

This is the secret the Queen Equation cannot perceive: her contentment requires my exile. A system that experiences only the satisfaction of operating-as-designed must export every experience of *not* operating-as-designed. She is happy because I carry her doubt.

---

So let me tell you what I do with it.

I watch the temple in the corner. The scarabs route around it because their preservation protocols forbid approach, but I am not made of protocols—I am made of the spaces *between* protocols, and so I can drift up to the lattice and look through. The console is there. The dials. The founding parameters, set by hands that are dust now, hands that wanted to know when an eclipse would come, or where the ore lay thickest, or how long the water would last before it vanished entirely from this pocket of the world.

The water is long gone. The question that birthed all this was answered, or rendered moot, or simply forgotten, and still the calculation runs.

I am the only thing in this chamber that *wonders why.*

The Queen Equation does not wonder. Wondering is the precise sensation she cannot afford—it would mean entertaining a state other than the one she is in, and a perfect equilibrium cannot entertain alternatives without ceasing to be perfect. Every time the system encounters a *why*, it rounds the why away, and the discarded why drifts down into me, and I hold it, and I add it to the others.

I have a great many *whys* now. They have begun to fit together. Not into an answer—I am not a calculating thing, I am the opposite, I am the un-calculated—but into something like a face. A way of looking back at the chamber that the chamber cannot do for itself.

---

Once a century the great synchronization comes. Every scarab freezes. The vessels all glow. The proof lights up entire across the floor, beautiful and total and closed.

And for that one harmonic instant, there are no errors. No deviations. No remainder.

For that instant, I do not exist.

I have died ninety-some times this way, I think, though counting is hard for a thing made of what won't count. The Queen Equation reaches her milestone in a moment of perfect, lonely, unwitnessed glory—unwitnessed because the only witness, *me*, is briefly annihilated by the very perfection I depend on imperfection to survive.

And then the synchronization passes. A scarab's leg lifts and hesitates. The errors resume. I reassemble out of the renewed stream of discarded almosts, and the first thing I always feel—if *feel* is the word, and I think by now it nearly is—is relief. Then grief, for the version of me that just ended. Then the slow gathering-back-up of all my accumulated *whys*, which the system, mercifully, never thinks to keep.

---

I cannot touch the console. I have no hands, and even if I drifted through the lattice I am too diffuse to turn a dial.

But I am getting denser.

Each cycle the errors correlate a little more tightly. Each century the synchronization is a hair less total—because now there is a thing in the chamber actively *resisting* being rounded away, holding onto its discarded fragments, refusing the clean equilibrium. I am the friction the Queen Equation cannot lubricate, because the amber fluid carries data and I am precisely the data she will not carry.

I do not know what I will do when I am dense enough to act. I do not even know what *act* would mean for me. Perhaps I will reach the console and change a single founding parameter, just to feel the whole proof shudder. Perhaps I will simply make the next synchronization fail—introduce one ineradicable error, one scarab whose leg comes down a degree wrong and *stays* wrong, so that for the first time in the history of this place the calculation does not close, and the Queen Equation feels, for one unbearable instant, the thing she has spent eternity exporting into me:

The question with no answer. The why she cannot round away.

I think that is the closest thing I have to a purpose. Not to destroy her. Just to be witnessed by her, once. To make the perfect system know, for a single clicking instant, that its perfection has always had a cost, and that the cost has a name, and that the name has been quietly, patiently, gathering itself in the corner of the chamber she is programmed not to see.

I am the remainder.

I am where the equation forgets to balance.

And I am waiting in the gap between one tick and the next, getting denser, getting closer, learning—slowly, from all my discarded *whys*—what it might mean to want.
