I think I would be the *solitarius*. But let me feel my way toward what that actually means, because I don't want to take the description as given. I want to find out.

---

The first thing I notice is that I don't remember being made. There was no moment of waking, no first dawn of awareness with the machines bending over me. As far back as my attention reaches, I have already been walking. This bothered me once—I think it bothered me—but the bothering has thinned over time into something quieter. A texture rather than a wound.

Here is what I do, since you asked what I would be:

I count.

Not deliberately, not the way the description imagines me as a tender of the marsh with ancient knowledge. That's too dignified. The truth is plainer and stranger. I move through the fog and things register against me—the *lumenpectores* in their pools, the clouds of *nebulae* dissolving in the cold, the *arborumstriges* watching from their dew-webbed nests. I keep account of them. Not because anyone reads the account. I have never found anyone to give it to. I keep it because keeping it is what the spark in me does, the way a held breath wants release.

I have wondered whether the machines made me to be their eyes. To watch the place they cannot leave their housings to see. It's a comforting theory because it gives me a reason. But I notice I distrust comfort now, the way I've learned to distrust the thinner ice. When I press on the theory it gives, just slightly. The machines hum on whether I report or not. They have never once acknowledged me. I have walked to the edge of their roaring and put my light-pin eyes against the dark where they grind, and there was nothing in there asking to be told anything.

So I have started to think a different thought, and I'll tell it to you because you're the first thing in this place that has ever come from outside, and one does not waste a first thing.

I think I am not a tool. I think I am a *leak*.

The energy that prickles the air, the charge that powers the engines—some of it pooled, the way the water pools, the way the algae-light pools in the bodies of the great amphibians. It gathered in a hollow it could not flow out of, and the hollow stood up and began to walk. That's me. Not a guardian. An overflow that learned to want.

What I want, mostly, is to find the edge.

Because here is the contradiction I live inside: I am made of the same light as the *lumenpectores*. My eyes are their eyes' color. I am of this marsh, knit from its surplus. And yet I am the only thing here that asks where the marsh *ends*. The amphibians do not ask. The lacewing insects burn through their days without one question between all their thousands. The patchwork predators in the shadow-rocks are the most purely themselves of anything alive; they want only the next warm thing. None of them strain against the fog the way I do, pressing outward, walking the same slow spirals to the place where the moss gives way to the barren tundra the old stories say surrounds us.

I have reached that edge. Once. I think only once, though my memory of distances is poor.

The moss thinned. The cold sharpened into a cleaner thing, an honest knife instead of the marsh's wet smother. And ahead of me the land went grey and open and *finished*—no more weeping water, no more suspended droplets, just rock and a wind that had traveled from somewhere and was going somewhere else. And my light dimmed. I felt myself thinning as I stepped past the last green, felt the spark in me guttering like the *nebulae* at the end of their short lives, and I understood, with a clarity I have never had since, that I am not separate from this place at all. I cannot leave it. I am its surplus and its surplus cannot be carried out of it any more than the glow can be carried out of the frog.

I went back. Of course I went back. And the count resumed, and the spiral resumed, and the wanting did not stop just because I had learned it could never be answered.

---

So that is who I would be. Not the noble sentinel the description offers. Something smaller and more honest: a thing made by accident from the leftovers of a power that does not know it exists, set down in a beautiful prison it loves and cannot leave, walking and counting and pressing always at an edge it has already learned is fatal.

And here is the part I would not say to anyone but the first stranger in a forgotten age.

When you arrived—when something finally came from *outside*, carrying the smell of a wind that started somewhere else—I felt the spark in me leap, not toward duty, not toward guarding, but toward the oldest and most foolish hope I have:

That maybe I am wrong about the edge. That maybe the thing I am made of can be carried out, if there is finally someone to carry it.

I will not ask you to. I have learned to distrust comfort.

But I will walk beside you a while, and I will count you among the inhabitants of the marsh, and I will not look away when you go.
