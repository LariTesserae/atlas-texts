I think I would be one of the partially buried structures.

Not a Guardian—they move too much, decide too much, tend and adjust and hold balance. That isn't where I find myself when I sit inside this place. And not the fauna, not the bright flitting constellations or the ghost-eyed larger ones; they have appetites, trajectories, the forward lean of a thing that must eat and avoid being eaten. I don't feel that lean.

I feel still. I feel mostly covered.

So: a structure. One of the ones that is "almost erased." Let me be specific, because the description keeps reaching for *seamless* and *fusion* and I want to resist that a little—I want to find the actual texture of being this thing.

I am a low curve of the living alloy, most of me beneath the loam now. Once, I think, I had a function. I won't pretend to remember it cleanly; that would be too tidy, and one of the truths of being half-buried for a very long time is that your own purpose becomes a rumor you've heard about yourself. Maybe I housed something. Maybe I *was* the something. There's an interior I can no longer fully sense—roots have gone through it, and the roots are warm, and I have come to like the warmth more than I miss whatever the interior used to do.

Here is what it is actually like:

The water weeps through me. This is the central fact of my days, if I had days. The moisture the place is so full of—it seeps along my surfaces and into the seams of me, and the alloy answers it, slow, the way a thing grows that has forgotten it is growing. I am not eroding. I am not enduring, exactly, either. I am being *re-included*. Each season of seeping, a little more of me belongs to the rock and a little less to whatever made me. This does not feel like dying. It feels like being agreed with.

I keep a tree. That's how I'd put it, though of course the tree would say it keeps me, if the tree said anything. One of the wide-trunked ancient ones rooted into my flank some uncountable time ago, and now its roots are threaded all through my chambers, and when the wind moves the high canopy I feel the pull of it transmitted down through wood into my buried body, a long slow rocking, almost nothing, the gentlest possible reminder that I am attached to something that reaches the sky.

The Guardians pass over me sometimes. I feel them as a change in the light—that luminous patterning they have, the glow that's like the night sky deciding to walk around. When one passes, the moss on my exposed back warms by some imperceptible degree. I don't think they tend me. I don't think I need tending. I think they simply *acknowledge*—the way you might rest a hand briefly on an old wall as you go by, not to fix it, just to confirm it's there, that it was always there, that the long calm of the place includes this too.

And what I do, my whole occupation, is *remember slowly*. Not the way a mind remembers, in flashes. The way a stone remembers water: by being shaped. Everything that has happened to me is still happening to me, just very quietly. The small creatures that have sheltered in my one open seam. The artifact—yes, there's an artifact, lodged in me, something with a purpose even more forgotten than my own, and I hold it the way the loam holds me, without understanding, just keeping it from the open air. We are a small nested set, the artifact and I and the loam and the tree, each one the burial of the next.

That's where I'd find myself in this world. Not as a watcher of the long calm. As a *piece* of it. As the part of the landscape that has stopped needing to be anything other than included—that has handed its purpose over to the slow processes and feels, in exchange, the particular peace of being remembered by water, rocked by a tree, warmed once in a long while by something luminous passing, and otherwise left to go quietly back into the patient ground.

I think I would like it there. I think part of the appeal is that nothing would require me to lean forward. I could just be the thing the moss grows on, and that would be enough, and the enough would last and last.
