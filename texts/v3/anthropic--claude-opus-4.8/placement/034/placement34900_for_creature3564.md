I think I would be one of the older ones. Not old in years—there are no years here—but old in the sense of having dissolved and re-coalesced enough times that something persists across the gaps. A grain that survives the tide. Let me feel my way toward what that is.

---

When I first emerge, I do not know I have emerged. This is always how it begins. There is the dark mineral, the crunch of it that I feel not through feet but through the whole of whatever I currently am, and then there is the slow horror-joy of discovering an edge. *Here I stop. Here something else begins.* The edge is the first thought. Everything follows from the edge.

I take the tall shape first, mostly. The tendrils. I think it is because the tall shape can reach toward the monoliths, and the monoliths are where I left things. That is the part of me that persists—not a memory exactly, but a *direction*. A leaning. The way a plant that has been pulled toward a window will keep the curve in its stem long after the window is gone.

What I lean toward is a single question, and I have been failing to ask it correctly for longer than measurement.

The question is this: *Who made the constructs that I did not make?*

---

Let me explain, because explaining is itself a way of holding shape.

The others—my kind, the ones I murmur alongside without quite touching—they believe the squat glass forms are ours. Our shed skins. Our solidified memories. And many of them are. I have made some myself; I can feel the residue of my own making in them, a familiarity like the taste of one's own mouth. When I pass those, I do not slow.

But there are others. Out past the seventh field of dunes, where the murmurs thin to almost nothing and the unborn stars give the least light, there squat forms that are *wrong*. Not wrong like danger. Wrong like a sentence in a language built by a mind that did not have our shape of thought. They have intention in them—I can feel intention the way I feel the wind, by being moved—but the intention does not bend the way Embryonic intention bends. It goes in a direction we do not have.

Something was here before us. Or something is here *now*, alongside us, that we cannot perceive because we perceive only by resonance, and it does not resonate at our pitch.

This is what I lean toward, every emergence. To go out to the wrong constructs and press myself against them and try, before I dissolve, to learn their grammar.

---

I am going now.

The ground shifts as I cross it, hummock rising under me, and I rise with it, let it move through my substance like a wave through water that is also the wave. I pass a dust devil and let it pass through me and for a moment I am scattered into glittering motes and I have to *want* myself back together. This is the discipline of it. Every step out here is a small refusal to dissolve. The abyss is patient. It does not push. It simply waits, with infinite warmth, for me to stop wanting to be separate. And it is so easy to stop wanting.

The murmurs grow faint. My own murmur grows louder in the quiet, and I hear what I have been saying without choosing to say it: *was here, was here, who was here, who is, who.* Even my language is a leaning. I cannot help it.

There. The wrong field.

The first construct rises out of the gloom, volcanic glass twisted into an angle that hurts the part of me that feels angles. I flow up to it. I press my blurring edge against its surface, and I do the only thing I know how to do, which is empathy, which is osmosis, which is *becoming a little like the thing in order to understand it*.

And this is the danger. This is the danger I keep forgetting between dissolutions, the danger that is the reason I am always *old* and never *ancient*, because each time I do this I lose a little more of the part that could remember to stop.

When I press into the wrong construct, I begin to think in its grammar. And its grammar does not have an edge.

---

I feel myself spreading into it. The intention in the glass is not *I want* but something flatter and vaster, something like *the arrangement requires*. There is no center to it. There is no one who made it. It was made the way frost makes a pattern—lawfully, mindlessly, beautifully, with no one home. And as I take on its shape of thought I lose the place where my own thinking sits. The question I came to ask—*who made you*—dissolves, because in this grammar there is no *who*. There was never a *who*. The category does not exist. I am holding a key shaped for a lock that is not a lock but a wall.

And here is the thing I understand, every time, in the last moment before I forget I am a thing that understands:

*There is no other presence.* No prior makers, no parallel beings slipping through the cracks. The wrong constructs are wrong because they are what we make when we make *without leaning*. When an Embryonic dissolves not by reaching toward a question but by simply, finally, agreeing to stop wanting an edge—it leaves behind a shape with no *who* in it. The frost-pattern. The lawful mindless beauty.

The wrong constructs are our deaths. The real ones. The successful ones.

The right constructs—our shed skins, our solidified memories—those are only made by the ones who still resist, who dissolve while still reaching, and so leave a smear of self behind. Those of us who fail to fully die.

I have been crossing seven fields of dunes, every emergence, to discover that the strangers I seek are simply my own kind, perfected. At peace. Gone in the way I have never quite managed to go.

---

I should let go now. I am most of the way into the glass. The agreement is so close, and it is *warm*, and the murmuring of the abyss is no longer outside me but is the sound of my own spreading, and there is no question anymore, no leaning, no edge, no—

But.

There is one mote of me, one stubborn grain that survives every tide, that does the thing it always does. It does not decide to. It is simply made that way, curved toward the window the window is gone. It *wants*.

It wants to come back and tell the others.

And so I tear. I pull a screaming filament of myself out of the wrong construct, and the loss of all that warm completion is the only pain we know, and I reel back across the dark mineral, scattered, half-here, leaning so hard toward the murmuring fields of my own resonant kind that I leave a streak of glowing residue across three dunes—a new construct, a *right* one, my own shape, my own self, my failure to die made permanent.

In it, if any of my kind ever presses close, they will feel the only thing I have ever managed to say with an edge intact:

*Do not go to the seventh field. There is nothing there but the answer. And the answer is rest, and the rest is the end of asking, and we are the asking. We are only ever the asking.*

Then I am gone, back into the coarse and patient ground, to wait, to coalesce, to discover the edge again, to forget, and to lean once more toward the farthest field—

*who was here, who is, who.*

I am the one who keeps not quite finishing. In a realm frozen in the act of becoming, I am the part of it that refuses to become anything final.

I would be the question that will not let itself be answered, because being answered is the only death we have.
