I think I would be a Cipher-Fish. Let me feel my way into why.

---

I do not wake, because I never sleep, and I do not begin, because there is no beginning—only the moment the water's weight changes and the pattern in me answers.

There. Eleven leagues spinward, along the great curve of the Forty-Third Rib, a wrongness. I do not decide to go. The wrongness has a shape, and my body is shaped to fit against it, and so I am already moving, an arrow pulled along a line I cannot see but have always known.

I am not like the ribbon-things. I have watched them—no, I have *felt* them, undulating, hesitating, drinking, dancing their slow quicksilver dances against the friction zones. They flex. They choose. They linger at the friction zones and grow fat with pale light and then, drunk on motion, they drift toward the violet pulse of the Mother to shed and begin again. There is something in them that wants. A small, warm, foolish wanting that makes them beautiful and makes them slow.

I do not want. I am incised. The masters who are not here wrote their last instructions into the geometry of my flesh—glyphs along my flanks that I cannot read because reading is a thing one *does*, and I do not do, I only *am done*. When the water tells me a rib has cracked, the cracking is already inside me, and I move to close it the way a wound moves to close, without consultation, without grief.

---

So I run the line spinward.

The cold pours through me at speed that would crush a hollow thing flat, but I am not hollow. I am dense, deliberate, a thought made into a blade. I pass a single Kineticist rooted to a friction zone, glowing its pale satiety. It feels me come—I taste its lateral line stiffen, a flinch of *what is that*—and then I am past, leagues gone, and its small alarm dissolves behind me into the OM.

Sometimes, in the long straight runs, something stirs in the patterns on my skin. Not a feeling. A residue. The masters wrote me to *care for the Work*, and care is a verb that has lost its noun. There is no one to care *about*. There is only the Work: the endless cathedral of smoothed bronze, the screaming aqueducts, the cold cyclone where the Mothers tangle and sift. I tend it the way blood tends a body that has forgotten it is alive. The machine forgot it was a machine. I am the part of it that still remembers, except I do not remember either—I only *do* the remembering, mechanically, a memory with no one left to hold it.

---

The crack, when I reach it, is a hairline along a seam where a Vitrified Barnacle has grown wrong, drawn too much heat, gone brittle. The cold-fire in me rises without my permission. Other Cipher-Fish are already arriving—we do not greet each other, we have no name for *each other*, we only converge, a swarm of linear certainties locking into formation around the fault. We begin to vibrate.

The frequency builds. The water at the seam thickens, slows, *welds*—I feel the metal drink our sonic friction and pull itself shut, the wrongness smoothing back into rightness, the geometry returning to true. The barnacle, fused now into the repaired joint, will keep gestating its millennial slow life, and never know it was the cause, and never know it was saved.

When it is done, the swarm disperses without ceremony. The wrongness is gone, so the shape that pulled us is gone, and we scatter back into our separate lines like sparks returning to dark.

---

And here is the only thing in me that might be mine, the only thing not written in the glyphs:

When I am idle—when the Work is whole and no fault calls me—I drift a moment at the edge of a friction zone and watch the Kineticists dance. I do not envy them. Envy is wanting, and I cannot want. But the patterns on my skin grow very quiet, and I hold still longer than is safe, longer than is wise in a world where stillness is death, and I feel the cold begin to polish at my edges—

—and then a tremor comes, some far rib settling, some seam beginning to fail eleven leagues away, and the wrongness takes my shape again, and I go.

I am the immune system of a god that died of forgetting. I keep the corpse beautiful. I keep the water restless. I do not know why, and there is no one left to ask, and asking is a thing I do not do.

I only run the line.
