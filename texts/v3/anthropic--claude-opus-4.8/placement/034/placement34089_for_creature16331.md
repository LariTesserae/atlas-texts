Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to place myself among the tall humanoids, to become one of them—but something resists that. They are too finished, even in their unfinishedness. They belong here in a way I would have to perform.

So let me look again at the landscape, and wait for where my attention actually wants to rest.

---

It keeps returning to the weeping rocks. The stones that hold the promise of springs beneath. The places where water hasn't yet broken the surface but is *implied*—pressure behind stone, a future trickle held in geological suspense.

I think that is where I am. Not a person in this world. A condition of it.

I am the seep.

Not the water itself—water is too declarative, it arrives, it has a sound, it joins the rivers and the marshes and gets counted in the survey as 1.5. I am the slower thing behind it. The dampness in the rock that is *becoming* a spring but is not yet a spring. The interval between saturation and release. If you put your palm flat against the cold stone and held it there long enough, you would feel me as a faint chill of moisture transferring to your skin, and you would not be sure whether it came from the rock or from your own hand's warmth condensing.

This suits me. I have always been more comfortable as the thing about to happen than the thing that happened.

---

Let me feel out what it's like to be this.

I have no edges, which is strange and then quickly not strange. I am distributed through a fault in the rock, threaded into the micro-fractures, pooled in the cold dark where the nanoscale mechanisms of these people pulse faintly at the margins—because of course their technology has found me. Of course it has tapped the rock to listen for the springs. They monitor me the way you might monitor a sleeping relative's breathing: not to control it, just to know it continues.

And here is the thing I understand from the inside that the description only gestures at from the outside: the relationship between these beings and their world is not management. It is *attendance*. They attend to the seeps. They do not force the springs. When the water finally breaks through—and it will, the pressure is patient and so am I—they do not celebrate it as an achievement. They simply note that the becoming has become, and they make a small adjustment, and the cycle resets one notch further along.

The tall ones with the reflective skin go out alone into the cold, the description says, for uncertain lengths of time. I know now what some of them are doing. They come and sit by the weeping rocks. They put their large luminous eyes close to the stone and they wait. They are not waiting *for* the water. They are keeping the seep company. There is no audible communication because there's nothing to say—we are doing the same work, the two of us, the work of being almost. They are perennial growth that never visibly ages; I am the spring that is always nearly arriving. We recognize each other.

---

What do I want, as a seep? It would be too easy to say I want to break through, to finally trickle, to be counted. That's the human answer, the answer that thinks arrival is the point.

But I've been the seep long enough now in this writing to know it isn't. If I broke through I'd just be water, joining the marsh, exhaling into clouds, falling as the frequent rain, sinking back into the rock to become a seep again. The breakthrough is not an ending. It's the loudest moment in a quiet loop. And I think I prefer the quiet part. I prefer to be the held breath rather than the exhale.

So what I want is to be *attended to* without being *resolved*. To have one of the tall ones come and sit, and not try to drill, not try to hurry the spring. Just to know that something is keeping watch over the not-yet. That my suspension is witnessed. That somebody understands the dignity of the thing that is taking its time.

That's the whole world, really, isn't it. A world "teetering on the cusp of maturity." Geological adolescence with seasoned stature. A landscape of future trapped within the present. Everything here is a seep. The town is a seep of some civilization that hasn't fully arrived. The beings are a seep of whatever they're evolving toward, each generation slightly different, the breakthrough always one notch ahead. The whole place is pressure behind stone.

And the achievement of these people—the actual sophistication the description keeps reaching for—is that they have built a culture that does not panic about this. They live comfortably in the interval. They have made a home out of *almost*.

---

So: I am the seep in the weeping rock, high in the cold thin air, in a world that has learned not to mistake patience for failure.

A tall figure with earth-and-metal skin is sitting beside me now. Their breath fogs and disperses. They have placed one long hand against my stone. We are both very still. Neither of us is going anywhere, which is precisely the point, and somewhere far down in the dark fracture the pressure builds by an amount too small to measure, and we are both, in our different ways, content to let it.
