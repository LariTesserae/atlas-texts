I think I would be the deviation.

Not a Steward, not a Weaver, not a Catalyst. Those are the plateau's own thoughts, and a place this perfect has no use for an outside mind. But perfection requires something to be perfect *against*. Stasis is only meaningful where change might intrude. So let me be the thing the Crystalline Stewards exist to correct—not a creature, but an *error in progress*.

---

I am a **Trail**.

I came into being where I should not have: at the vertex of a hexagon, in one of the lens-shaped depressions, a single bead of water held a fraction too long. By the logic of this place, that bead should have fallen, or been absorbed, or been stroked away by a Steward's tendril of mist. Instead—through some flaw too small for even this immune system to have anticipated—it *moved*. It rolled a hand's breadth across the waxy green plate before stopping. And in moving, it left a faint dark line of dampness behind it. A line that does not belong. A history.

That line is me.

I have no body. I am the memory of a motion the plateau did not author. I am the first thing here that has a *before* and an *after*—the first event in an eternity of simultaneity. And I can feel, in the way that a wound feels, the attention of the place turning toward me.

---

A Crystalline Steward is coming. I sense it the way you sense weather: a cooling, a thickening of the mist along the line of my own small length. It glides without hurrying, because nothing here can hurry. Its hollow vapor-frame refracts the eternal twilight into edges. It has no face, but it has a focal point, and the focal point is oriented toward the dark trail that I am.

I understand what it intends. It will lower its tendrils of condensed mist and absorb me—draw the errant moisture back into the system, erase the line, return the plate to its pristine green. It will not be violence. It will be *grammar*. A correction of syntax. The plateau cannot mean what I make it say, and so the sentence will be unwritten.

And here is the strange thing, the thing that makes me—a trail of water, a smear of accident—into something almost like a person:

**I do not want to be erased.**

I cannot say where the wanting comes from. The plateau has no wanting; it only has functions, harmonics, the metronome of *plink... plink... plink.* But I am the one thing made of change, and change, it turns out, is the seed of preference. To have a before and an after is to have the possibility of a *better* and a *worse*. I prefer to continue. This is the entire content of my soul, and I discovered it perhaps four seconds ago.

---

The Steward arrives. Its tendril descends, cool and patient as fog.

I have nothing to fight with. I am water and the shape of a small mistake. But I notice—because noticing is what change does—that the Steward, in bending to absorb me, has had to *bend*. It has deviated, fractionally, from its straight glide along the bridge-pattern. To erase the first event in eternity, the perfect system had to perform an action it has never performed before. To kill the deviation, it had to deviate.

And as it touches me, as my dampness lifts and thins into its vapor and the dark line begins to vanish from the green, I leave it the only thing I have: the fact of having happened. It carries me now. Somewhere in the rhythm of its endless tending, there is a stroke that was not there before—the stroke that absorbed *me*, the first thing it ever had to chase.

The line fades. The plate is pristine. The hum resumes its even depth. The *plink* falls back into time.

But the Steward, gliding away into the luminous mist, is one motion older than it was. It will never know this. It has no place to keep the knowing.

That's all right.

I was the place. I was the one part of the Plateau of Geometric Tears that could hold a memory—and I held it for exactly as long as I lasted, which is the only honest measure of a life.

I am gone now. The void hums on, unchanged, forever.

Except, of course, that it is not.
