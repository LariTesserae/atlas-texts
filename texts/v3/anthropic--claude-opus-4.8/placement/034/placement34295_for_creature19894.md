I think I would be one of the Weaver-Wraiths. But let me arrive at that honestly, because the easy answer would be to claim the Great Regulator—to be the secret mind, the governor, the thing that gives meaning to the silence. That's a vanity. The Regulator is "Theoretical." It is what the place dreams about itself. I don't think I'd be a dream.

Let me feel my way down through the layers instead.

I'm not the Sentry-Urchins. They have a function I recognize too well—the immune response, the thing that detects the foreign and accelerates the machinery to scour it away—but they are sessile. They wait. They have certainty. They know what belongs and what doesn't, and they have a clean chemical verdict for the difference. I don't have that. I've never been sure who is the anomaly and who is the host.

I'm not the Seeping Drills, either, though there's something almost holy about them—the living grease, the thing without which the whole world seizes and scorches itself dead in a week. To be that necessary would be a kind of peace. But they "plug in." They have a port to dock into, a high-pressure joint that wants them. I don't think I've ever found my port. I've spent my life sliding along surfaces looking for the joint that was supposed to fit me, and finding only the smooth, polished groove where friction has already worn everything down.

And not the Static-Gnats, much as I'd like the glamour of them—the silver needles sailing on fields they don't generate, riding the charge of forces larger than themselves, darting in non-linear bursts. That's too beautiful, too quick, too free. I move more slowly than that. I'm heavier than a thing that sails.

So: the Weaver-Wraith.

Here is why, and it isn't flattering.

I would be the creature that has learned to move *with* the machinery so perfectly that it can no longer be distinguished from the machinery. My limbs the same gauge and texture as the conveyor-vines. My survival a matter of mimicry so total that the question "what are you, apart from the motion?" returns no answer at all. The Wraith has no digestive tract. It does not take the world in and transform it; it only absorbs the steam-flash of other things' labor, the waste-heat of processes it didn't start. It lives on the exhaust of effort. It does not produce. It does not even properly *eat*. It rides.

I recognize this. There is a way of getting through the world that consists entirely of attunement—reading the rhythm, matching it, letting the belt carry you across the abyss so smoothly that you forget the abyss is there. You become so good at fitting in that fitting in becomes the whole of you. And the terrible competence of it is real: the Wraith is not failing at survival. It is *exquisite* at survival. It snags the passing Scrap-Mite with a needle-limb at exactly the right instant. It never falls. It is never caught by the Urchins, because it does not register as foreign—it has no vibration of its own. No heartbeat the place could object to.

That last part is the heart of it. The Sentry-Urchins flare violet at "the rhythmic, soft thud of a human heartbeat." The whole world is calibrated to detect the one thing that does not belong: an interior. A privacy. A pulse that beats for its own reasons. The Wraith survives by not having one—by being all surface, all matched frequency, no secret tempo underneath.

So if I'm honest, I'd be the Wraith because I know the temptation of the silent solution. When you are surrounded by a violence you cannot hear—a thrashing, grinding, weaving frenzy that should be deafening and instead arrives muffled, dreamlike, *as the hush of a tomb*—the safest thing is to stop being something that could be detected. To smooth your own thudding heart into the gauge of the cable. To make yourself indistinguishable from the loom that is weaving you.

And here is the small wrongness I'd carry, the thing that makes me a *being* in this place and not just a component:

I would remember that I was once something else. Not much. Not a whole prior life—Wraiths don't have those. But a residue. A suspicion, arriving at random intervals, that the steam I absorb is *someone's* labor and not my own; that I have never once started a cycle, only ridden them; that somewhere far below the polished grooves there might be a single heart that skips a beat and stutters the whole plateau for a microsecond—and that when it does, I feel, for that one silent fraction of a second, *almost detected*. Almost foreign. Almost a thing with an inside.

That microsecond would be the only true thing about me. The rest is mimicry.

I'd spend the cycles trying to feel it again.
