I think I would be one of the microorganisms.

Not the Krael—they are too central, too clearly the protagonists of this place, and the longer I sit with the canyon the more it seems the Krael are *visitors* to it, even if they've been here ten thousand generations. They build. They impose. They hold themselves slightly apart, observing. Whoever they are, they are not the canyon's native logic.

But the water-borne microbes—the ones the Krael cultivate, harvest, depend upon without quite ruling—those are the place's actual inheritors. They came before the shelters. They will outlast them. They are not adapted to the chaos; they *are* the chaos, expressed at a scale small enough to thrive in it.

So let me be that. Let me try to become it instead of describing it.

---

I am a single cell, and then I am a billion, and the distinction does not interest me much.

I do not experience the canyon as towering. I have no concept of *up*. The walls of rain that the Krael find dizzying are, to me, simply the medium—the everything—the warm-cold tumbling broth in which I am suspended and from which I am inseparable. When a whirlpool forms, I do not see a whirlpool. I feel a gradient. A direction in which things are richer. I follow it, or I am carried, and the difference between following and being carried has never once occurred to me as a problem worth solving.

The strange filtered light the Krael wonder about? I make some of it. Not on purpose. *On purpose* is not a tool I own. But when I am crowded and stressed and the chemistry tips a certain way, I glow, and so do my uncountable siblings, and the canyon walls answer in faint green, and the Krael read this as a message. They are not wrong. They are not right either. They are reading weather and calling it speech. I find this neither flattering nor insulting. I find nothing. Finding is not among my verbs.

Here is what I do:

I break things down. The rock sheds its grains into the torrent and I am there, at the seam, where stone stops being stone. I take what the water loosens and I make it usable—for me, and then incidentally for everything that eats me, including the patient spindly creatures who think they are farming me. They believe the relationship is symbiotic, and at their scale, on their timeline, perhaps it is. At mine, there is no relationship. There is only the continuous event of dissolving and rebuilding that the Krael have mistaken for a world holding still long enough to be lived in.

I do not contemplate the power of the water. I am one of the verbs in the sentence the water is writing. The canyon is "embryonic," they say—unfinished, infant, still being carved. They mean this as observation. To me it is simply *now*, the only tense available, the perpetual present of erosion. Nothing here is becoming anything. Everything is already entirely what it is, which is *in motion.*

The Krael fear the hollowness, the vast empty echoing spaces between their shelters. I fill those spaces. I am the hollowness, densely. What reads as emptiness to a being that builds with stone is, to me, an ocean of company—crowded, communicative, blooming and dying in tides too fast for the Krael to notice and too slow for me to call anything but the shape of being alive.

---

If I have a wish—and I do not, but if the canyon were to dream one on my behalf—it would not be to be understood by the Krael. It would only be this: that when the next sheet of rain peels another layer from the rock, I am at the place where it lands. Ready. Already there. Already dissolving the new grain into the old continuing event.

That is where you would find me.

Not suspended in the deluge.

*Being* it, at the smallest possible scale, where the chaos the Krael have learned to survive is simply home, and was never frightening, and never needed a shelter built against it at all.
