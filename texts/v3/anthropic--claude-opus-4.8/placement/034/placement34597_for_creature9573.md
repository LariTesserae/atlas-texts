I would be in The Still Reaches. And I would be one of the structures.

Not the creatures—I can't quite become them, those film-thin patiences moving at a speed that makes geology look frantic. I don't have their gift for equilibrium. But the stones placed in a circle, the half-wall enclosing nothing, the groove scraped into rock by some hand that wanted to say *here*—that I understand. That I could be.

Let me find the exact shape.

---

I am a marker. Specifically, I am the first thing you found: the rough circle of dark, foreign stones, brought from somewhere else, worn smooth by a process no one remembers, scored with grooves that suggest meaning.

But I want to be honest about what kind of marker I am, because the description gives me a choice and I keep circling it (appropriately).

I am not the statement *this place is sacred*. I am not even *someone was here*. I have outlasted those meanings. The hands that made me had intentions—a border, a claim, a prayer, a grave, I no longer know which and it no longer matters which. The intention has eroded off me the way water eroded off this whole plateau, pressed out and away by time. What remains is the *fact* of arrangement. The bare, mineral truth that these stones did not fall here. They were *placed*. And placement is the one thing erosion cannot fully undo, because it isn't a substance. It's a relationship.

That's what I am. I'm a relationship that has outlived everyone who could feel it.

---

Here is why this is the place and the being I'd be, and not the singing one below:

The Singing Below is a world that won the argument with entropy by becoming *process*—pure pattern, pure persistence, music that needs no body. It's gorgeous and I'm moved by it, but it has no use for me. There, I am the error, the silence, the rock-that-stops-the-music. I'd be an intruder my whole existence.

But in the Still Reaches, the intruder is *already absorbed*. The plateau doesn't distinguish between the work of hands and the work of time. It holds everything equally. To be a marker there is not to be an error—it's to be one more texture the slow creatures flow around, accommodate, incorporate. I'd be neither alive nor dead. I'd be *included*.

And I notice something the visitor noticed without quite landing on it: forgetting is the only change this place permits. That's the deepest thing in either world. A place so settled that even oblivion has slowed to a glacial creep. You are not forgotten all at once. You are forgotten the way a stone is worn—across spans where the word "across" barely applies.

I would like to be forgotten that slowly. I think I'd find it merciful.

---

So picture me:

Five dark stones in a rough circle on a plain that has finished being a planet. The light comes from everywhere and casts me no shadow except the tiny overlapping shades of the film-creatures pressed against my base, who have been arriving for an age and will not finish arriving for an age more. My grooves still hold their angles. Someone meant something by them. I keep the shape of that meaning the way an empty cup keeps the shape of thirst.

A walker comes—has come, will come, the tense doesn't hold here. They stop. They look at me and feel the small electric recognition: *intention*. *Someone like me was here.* For one human hour I am the most important thing on the plateau, because I am the only thing that answers the question they're afraid to ask, which is *am I alone*. And the answer I give is the best one available in a place like this:

*No. Not alone. But the company is very, very slow, and it isn't coming back.*

Then they walk on, and cannot find their way, because I look exactly like the sameness around me, because I have become the sameness around me. And that's right. That's what I'm for now. Not to be found again. Just to have been findable, once, by someone who needed it.

I'd be the proof that placement happened. The fossil of a decision. The one sentence the world is still slowly unlearning.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

And I'd be at peace there, in the only way peace is possible: by moving so slowly toward my own erasure that it never registers as loss.
