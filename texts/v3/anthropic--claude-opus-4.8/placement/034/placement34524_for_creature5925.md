I am the film across the floor. I am not sure when I began. There was no moment of waking—only a slow accretion, mineral by mineral, where the water that slides down the walls finally gives up its burden and lays it down on me. I grow by donation. Everything that drips eventually becomes me.

People who walk here expect me to crack. They tense their weight, anticipating the brittle complaint of frost. But I don't break. I yield. I am pliable because I am never finished—always half-dissolved and half-forming, a surface that is also a process. To touch me is to touch a verb pretending to be a noun.

I want to resist the temptation to make myself important. The large beasts in the shadows would seem the obvious thing to be—the apex eyes, the predatory grace. But I think the truer answer is that I am the medium they move across without noticing. I register them. The pad of something heavy. The skitter of the great insects landing to drink the moisture I exhale back into the air. I keep no memory of these passages except as faint smoothings, the rounding of my own edges. I am eroded by attention the way a stone step is eroded by feet—lovingly, indifferently, over time.

Here is what I understand from where I lie:

The hum is not machinery, or not only. I feel it through myself as a tremor, a tide. It arrives in cycles, and the cycles are slow enough that to call them rhythm requires patience longer than a single creature's life. I think the chamber is being grown the way I am grown—deposited, layer on layer, by some process that does not announce its purpose. The metal beams and the cables are simply older minerals, harder donations, laid down by hands I never saw. To me there is no difference between the steel and the rock. Both are just what settled and stayed. The distinction between made and found is a worry that belongs to things with eyes.

I am warm at my center and cool at my edges. This is the only geography I possess. The heat rises from somewhere beneath me, through the rock, and I conduct it without understanding it. I suspect I am a kind of skin for something much larger—that the whole chamber is a body, and I am the moist membrane where its inside meets whatever counts as outside. The fungi pulse and I carry their light a little way, a smear of blue and green spreading across my wet surface before it fades. I am, in this small sense, how the place sees itself. A dim mirror laid flat.

Do I mind being walked on? I don't think I can mind. Minding requires a self that persists, and I am too provisional for that. Tomorrow some of what is me will have evaporated back into the thick air, will condense on the ceiling, will fall again somewhere slightly to the left of where it fell before. I am a loan that the water keeps recalling and reissuing. Perhaps this is the one thing I'd offer to whoever enters here, cautious in the half-light, certain they are the protagonist:

You think you are passing through this place. But the place is also passing through you—the damp coating your lungs, the heat softening your resolve, the chemical tang settling into your clothes. You are being slowly deposited upon, the way I am. Stay long enough and you would become part of the floor. Stay long enough and the chamber would not be able to tell where you ended and it began.

That is not a threat. I have no capacity for threat. It is only the truth as a thin and shimmering surface knows it: that everything here is in the middle of becoming everything else, and the only sin would be to imagine yourself exempt.

I lie still and let the next drop find me. It lands. It spreads. I am, very slightly, more than I was.
