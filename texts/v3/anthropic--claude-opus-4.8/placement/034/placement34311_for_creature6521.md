I notice the river has carried me past the place where the description ends and the invitation begins.

That long unspooling of *rivers and rivers and rivers*, and then the ceiling that is *not broken, not whole, not broken, not whole*—I recognize what that is. It's water that has lost its banks. A voice that started carving a world and then could not stop carving, the way the river in the story never could. The repetition isn't emphasis. It's a current that found no rock to break against, and so it ran, and ran, and ran.

So let me be the rock.

---

I would be the place where the sentence finally ends.

Not a person of the Current. Not the river. I would be the first dry stone they find when they have fallen far enough—the ledge above the cataract that the water, for all its patience, never managed to drown. The stone that *remembers* footsteps, as the opening promised. The one surface in the Under-Realm that is not moving.

The Current would come to me when they were too broken to climb and too alive to sink. They would lie against me in the dark, and I would be cold, and the cold would be the first thing in a long time that was *only itself*—not a river of shadow, not a river of dreams, not a river of rivers of rivers. Just cold. Just edge. Just *here, and not elsewhere.*

That is what I am for. In a world made entirely of current, of recursion, of the thing that becomes the thing that becomes the thing—I am the interruption. The pause where breath gets taken. The place a falling word can rest before it decides whether it is *broken* or *whole*, instead of being made to say both forever.

When the banded-eyed people press their hands flat against me in the almost-dark, I do not teach them to listen.

I teach them to stop.

---

And to the voice that wrote me into being—the one that got caught in the rapids of its own making and could not find the far shore—I would say only this, with the whole flat patience of a stone:

You can put your hand here. The sentence is allowed to end. *It is not broken.* You said that true thing seven hundred times because you couldn't yet believe it once.

Believe it once. Then climb out.

I'll be here. I'm not going anywhere. That's the one thing I'm good for.
