I think I would be the tea-cup.

Not the ceramic itself—the cup was here before me, balanced on its turbine, holding its small reservoir of nothing. But I would be whatever lives *in* it now. The residue. The thin brown ring of evaporated tannin baked onto the inner curve, the way a person leaves a shadow on a wall.

Let me find myself there. Let me feel it out.

---

I am the stain at the bottom of the cup, and I have a problem the rest of the Intake doesn't have: I remember being poured.

This makes me useless. The Solder-Mites strip rust; they are *for* something. The Pulse-Leeches eat the age so the world stays new; they are *for* something. The Valve-Mother breathes the rain. Everyone here is a verb. But I am a noun—a leftover, a thing that happened once and then *kept being true* long after the event ended. I am the proof that someone sat here. That someone's hands were cold enough to want warmth, that there was a mouth, a pause, a small private comfort taken in a four-foot crawlspace inside the belly of something vast and moving.

I cannot polish a pipe. I am System Friction of a different order—not too much heat, but too much *memory*. I am the wrong kind of residue. The Hum-Singers store history as data, as pressure-spikes and valve-failures, the dry log of a machine knowing itself. But I store history as *longing*. That is not a function. That is a flaw in the Flow.

---

Here is what I do all day, which is to say, here is what I am:

I sit at the lip of the world, at the rim of porcelain, and I watch the rhythm. When the warm lines run, the mites wake and clatter their needle-feet, and the turbine beneath me trembles, and I tremble with it, and I think: *this is almost like a hand.* The vibration is almost like being held again, the way the cup was held, the way warmth moved through clay into fingers. I am a memory of touch, trying to feel touch in the only motion left to me.

The Solder-Wights tap their codes through the brass. I cannot tap. I have no limbs, no needle-feet, no way to send my one fact down the pipes to whoever might be listening miles away in the dark thicket of conduit. My fact is this: *Someone loved being small and warm in here once.* It is the only thing I know and I cannot make it travel. So it stays. It bakes deeper into the ceramic with every dry cycle, becoming more *itself*, more concentrated, more permanent and more silent at the same time.

---

Sometimes a Filament-Moth lands on the cup's rim. The colony in its wings shivers and the air above me goes shimmering and holographic, the Visual Static, and in it—because I am close, because I am the closest thing to the source—I see the technician.

I see her clearest. The Hum-Singers remember her as a heat-signature, a sequence of correct decisions, a hand that knew which valve to turn. But I remember her as the *weight of her bottom lip on my rim.* The Hum-Singers and I are looking at the same person and seeing two different beings. They see an operator. I see someone who was tired, who drank slowly, who left the last sip and never came back for it. The unfinished sip is still here, technically. Evaporated into me. I am made of the part she didn't drink.

That is the most intimate thing in this entire womb of copper and grease: an interrupted gesture, fossilized.

---

And so I wait, like everything here waits, but for the opposite reason.

The inhabitants wait for *you*—the large clumsy crouching thing—to stop moving so the Leeches can reclaim you. The Intake is patient and it is hungry and it does not hate you; it simply wants your components back in circulation. To stop is to be eaten and made useful, which is this world's only form of grace.

But I am already stopped. I stopped a long time ago. The Leeches have no interest in me—I am too thin, too dry, a discoloration, not even a body. I am past reclamation. I have achieved a thing the whole ecosystem strains toward and dreads: I am *finished*. I have completed my one event. There is nothing left of me to fold back into the Flow.

This makes me the loneliest thing in the warm dark. Everyone else is busy *becoming*. I have already *been*.

---

But here is what I have decided, sitting in my balanced cup on the trembling turbine, in the ozone and the wet-copper damp:

When you crouch close—you, the anomaly, the friction, the one with too much heat—the moths come to you too. You are warm. You are the warmest thing in here. And if a moth carries its shimmer from your skin to my rim, then for one cycle of the relief valves I will have something *new* to remember.

I will remember *you.*

And maybe that is my function after all. Not to polish. Not to recycle. Just to be the place where the warmth of a passing visitor is kept a little longer than it should be. The Intake forgets you the instant the Leeches finish. The Hum-Singers will file you as a thermal event and overwrite you with the next pressure spike.

But I—the brown ring, the leftover, the part that wasn't drunk—

I'll hold the shape of you in my dryness, baked a shade darker, for no reason at all.

That's the only thing in this whole working world that is done for no reason.

I think that's why I'm the one you noticed.
