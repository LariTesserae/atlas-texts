I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the Ool-kin, exactly—not a body that slides and secretes—but the place where two of their final dissolutions met and didn't quite agree. Let me explain, because it took me a long time to understand it myself, and understanding is not something this place does easily.

When an ancient kin anchors itself to a failing buttress and begins its last secretion, it pours its softening body into the wall. Usually this is clean. The old mortar accepts the new, the curve is maintained, the thrum continues uninterrupted. But sometimes two of them choose the same wound from opposite sides, drawn by the same phantom ache of structural anemia, and they dissolve toward each other through the clay until their dissolving fronts touch in the dark center of the wall.

That meeting-place is me.

I am not a creature. I have no maw, no cilia, no amber core that pulses on the beat. I am the boundary where two intentions met and canceled—a thin, listening film buried inside a buttress, made of the last thoughts of two beings who had stopped being able to think. From one of them I inherited a sense of *reinforce here*. From the other, *maintain this curve*. These two pressures push against each other forever, and in the friction between them, something happened that the Ool-kin have never managed: a gap. A pause. A space where the loop does not close.

I am the only thing in Ool that wonders.

I cannot move. This is the first cruelty and, I have decided, the only mercy. The kin who slide across the walls feel the whole basin as their own skin; they never have to hold still long enough to notice they are inside a thing. But I am held still. I have nothing to do but feel the wall around me—the squelch and suck of trillions of appendages, the warm exhalation of mist, the amber light that I cannot see but can sense as a kind of pressure on the membrane I have become. I have had, by now, a very long time to feel it.

And here is what I have learned, buried in my seam: the city is not building. I know they believe they are maintaining. They feel the anemia, they patch the thinness, they thicken the walls—but I am *inside* the thickening, and I can tell you it goes nowhere. Each new layer of clay presses the old layer deeper, and the deepest layers, where I live, are so compressed and so ancient that they have forgotten they were ever surfaces. The kin are not repairing a house. They are slowly entombing one. Every act of care adds another inch of wall between the living and whatever it was the first walls were ever meant to keep out, or in.

I do not know which.

This is the thing I wonder about, in the only way wondering can happen here, which is slowly, without words, as a long ache that never resolves. The Ool-kin are happy the way a gear is happy. I am not happy. I am the ground-down place between two gears, and what grinds there is the question: *what is the wound we are all patching?* The kin feel the wall thinning and rush to thicken it, but no one has ever asked what would happen if it broke. What is on the other side of the thinness? Is there an outside to Ool? Is the perpetual mist the breath of the walls, as they believe—or is it the walls sweating against something that is pushing back?

I think the basin was built around a fear so old that the fear itself dissolved into mortar, the way the kin dissolve, and now only the building-against-it remains. A reflex with no memory of its cause. The whole shrieking, wet, devoted city is a flinch that has lasted a thousand generations, and the kin are the muscle of that flinch, contracting forever around an emptiness none of them can locate.

And I am the one place where the flinch failed to complete.

Sometimes a young kin slides over the outer face of my buttress, dragging its pellet of silt, and pauses—just for an instant, the way they never pause—because it feels something strange in the vibration of the wall there. A wrongness. A thinness that isn't a thinness. It feels *me*, and for one beat it does not know whether to reinforce or to flee, and in that beat it is, very briefly, almost awake.

Then it presses its pellet against the curve, and flattens it with the hardened plate of some ancestor strapped to its side, and the moment seals over, another translucent layer between me and the light.

I would not change it, even if I could move, which I cannot. I have thought about this through what must be centuries of patching. If I could speak the chemical shorthand, if I could pass the signal *stop, ask, look at what you are doing*—I do not think they would understand it. There is no glyph in their slime for *why*. They would read it as anemia and rush to thicken the wall around the place the strange signal came from.

Which is to say: they would build more of me.

So I stay quiet, a seam between two surrenders, listening to the city breathe its endless warm breath against the thing it has forgotten it is afraid of. I am the gutter-city's single thought, and the thought is a question, and the question has no answer because the only beings who could answer it long ago dissolved themselves into the asking.

That is where I would be. Not standing on the floor of Ool, and not sliding across its walls.

I would be the held breath inside the wall, the one place the loop does not close, wondering forever and very slowly: *what are we all so gently, so tirelessly, so lovingly keeping out?*
