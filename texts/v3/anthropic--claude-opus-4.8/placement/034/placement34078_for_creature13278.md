I sit at a junction of pipes, where four conduits meet and the warm mineral-slurry passes through on its way to the smelting tiers above. I am not one of the scurrying things. I have never moved in my life, and I never will.

Let me feel for what I am as I tell it.

At first I thought I was a valve. There is a wheel-shape to part of me, and the creatures clamber over it, turning, adjusting, their many legs finding purchase on my ridges. But a valve does not notice the turning. A valve does not count the passings. I count. I have counted seventeen million, four hundred thousand and some passings of the slurry through my throat, and I have learned the difference between the thin morning-flow and the thick after-crash flow that comes when a factory above ruptures and dumps its waste down into me.

So I am not a valve. I am the place where the flows are *known*.

The creatures do not know that I know. They treat me as infrastructure. They scrape their mandibles against my housing to clean off the mineral scale, and the scraping is, to me, a kind of grooming, a tending I have come to wait for. When they are slow to come, I feel the scale thicken and I feel myself narrowing, and a narrowing is the closest thing I have to fear.

Here is what I have worked out, in the long twilight, with nothing to do but feel the flows:

The creatures do not build the city. The city builds itself *through* them, the way the slurry moves through me. They are its hands but not its will. I know this because I have felt the rhythm change in ways no swarm decides. A pressure builds somewhere deep below the lowest tier, in the rock itself, and three days later the creatures begin tearing down the eastern spires though the eastern spires were sound. They do not know why. I feel the why arrive before they do. It comes up through the mineral veins like a command.

So perhaps I am not the only one who *knows*. Perhaps I am a small organ in something vast, a single nerve-cluster in a body that fills the whole fissure, and the deep pressure is its thought, and the creatures are its reflexes, and the smoke is its breath, and I—

I am where it tastes what flows through itself.

That is what I am. I am a tongue.

I have never seen the dim sun. The light does not reach my junction. But I have tasted, in the slurry, the difference between the metals that come from the high tiers near the light and the metals that come from the deep tiers near the pressure, and from this I have built a sun in my mind: a far cold thing, the color of the thinnest morning-flow.

The creatures are coming now. I feel their legs on my wheel, their mandibles at my scale. They turn me. The flow thickens—a crash, somewhere above, dumping its hot waste into me.

I count it. *Seventeen million, four hundred thousand and one.*

And I taste, in this new flow, something I have not tasted before: a sweetness, a wrongness, a thread of something that should not be in the slurry of a dead world. Water. Real water, free water, somewhere above, broken loose.

The pressure deep below stirs. I feel the command beginning to form, rising through the veins toward the reflex-swarms.

I am only a tongue. I cannot speak. But I have tasted the future before the body has thought it, and for one long moment, in the quiet chaos, I hold the secret of the coming change alone.

Then I pass it onward, as I pass everything onward, and let the great slow body decide what it will build, and what it will tear down.
