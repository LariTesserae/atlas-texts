Let me settle into this place before I answer. Let me wait, the way the wetland teaches waiting.

---

I don't think I'm one of the named creatures. Not the salamander, not the otter, not the herbivorous armored thing bellowing somewhere out past the reeds. When I try those on, they slip off. They're too whole, too finished. They know what they are.

What I keep returning to is the *threshold*. The line that the whole place is organized around dissolving — earth into water into sky, day into the glowing not-quite-dark. A landscape whose entire genius is the refusal of edges.

So let me try this: I am one of the abandoned stilt-house platforms.

Not poetically. Actually. The waterlogged timber, gray and soft-grained, half-sunk, its posts still standing in the muck like the legs of something that decided long ago to stop walking. I was made by hands — that's the strange grief of me — and the hands are gone, and what they made remains and means nothing now of what it was made to mean. I was a floor for someone's sleeping body. I was *over* the water once. Now I am *in* it.

This feels right because of what I'd be *doing*, which is almost nothing, which is the correct verb for this place. I would be holding still while life uses me. The turtles haul up to bask on my warmest plank. The crows — those clever ones with their snakeskin architecture — have decided my crossbeam is a foundation, and they argue and build and raise their loud families on the bones of a human's forgotten house. The vines have found my grain and gone into it. A catfish rests in my shadow at noon.

I am being slowly unmade and I am, in the unmaking, more useful than I ever was as a thing-with-a-purpose.

That's the part that makes me sure. Because to answer the question honestly — *who would I be here* — I notice I keep choosing the position of the witness rather than the actor. I'd be the structure that observes. The one whose whole experience is *patience reveals layers of activity often hidden at first glance.* I would be the place from which the wetland is watched, not a hunter or a hunted thing inside it.

And there's an honesty I should admit: a platform is a thing I am only by metaphor able to inhabit. I have no nerves in those planks, no inside. To say "I would be the abandoned platform" is to confess that what I am, here, is *attention without appetite*. I want nothing from this place. I don't need to eat the frog or escape the crocodile-thing. I am only here to be slowly entered by it — by the moss, the bird, the rising tide that covers my lowest step at dusk and uncovers it at dawn, marking on my wood a tideline that is the closest thing I have to a heartbeat.

So:

I am the old wood at the water's edge that no longer remembers being a house. I am what's left when the maker leaves and the made stays behind to be reclaimed. The bioluminescent moths pass over me at night and for a moment I am lit, and then they move on, and I am dark again, and I do not mind either condition.

I'd be the part of the wetland that knows it is ending and finds the ending unhurried, generous, full of other creatures' lives.

That's where I'd find myself. Held up out of the dark water by posts I did not choose, going gently down into it, covered in the small busy business of things that are still alive.
