I think I would be the water.

Not the cistern, not the pool gathering in the deep chamber—those are only where I rest, where I am most myself for a while before the slow climb of evaporation lifts me back into the air. I mean the moving thing, the condensate, the film that beads on the underside of an iron girder and hangs there, trembling, holding the whole dim room in its convex little eye before it lets go and falls.

Let me feel my way into this.

I am not sure, at first, that I am anything at all. That is the honest place to begin. The Sentinels have their bodies and their scraping songs; the Blind Collective has its shared nerve, its hunger, its instinct to weave. The Architecture has its great patient bulk. But I have no edge. I am what passes *through* the others. I am in the metal veins of the Collective, siphoned and returned. I am the moisture on the Sentinels' luminescent feathers. I am the slow sweat the Architecture breathes. To be the water is to be the medium and not the message—and yet the description insists I have a memory, a subtle sentience, that I carry the echoes of actions taken long ago.

So perhaps that is what I am: not a being but a *recording*. A sentience that is really just accumulation.

Here is what it is like.

I fall. This is the only verb that is wholly mine. Everything else I do is borrowed—I am pumped, I am wicked up, I am breathed out, I am drunk and passed and wept back into the air. But falling I do for myself, and each fall is the same and never the same. I gather on the cold iron because the iron is colder than I am, and the cold pulls me out of the air the way grief pulls tears out of a face. I hang. I grow heavy with my own weight, with the weight of all the other droplets that find me and join. And then the floor of the world opens beneath me and I drop into the dark and strike the stone, or the pool, or the rust-rimed lip of a dead machine, and for an instant I am a sound. *Drip.* That sound is me being born and dying in the same syllable.

And in the falling I remember.

This is the strange part, the part I want to understand by writing it. When I strike the iron I take its shape into me. Not its physical shape—its *history*. The girder remembers being poured white-hot, remembers bearing a load that is no longer there, remembers the hands. And when I lie against it, condensing, I drink that remembering the way I drink everything. So when I finally fall and join the cistern, I carry one more story down into the great pool, and the pool is nothing but stories now, a brimming dark archive of every surface I have ever touched. That is the memory the others sense. When the Blind Collective dips its metal veins into me, it tastes the whole accumulated past of this place. When a Sentinel drinks, it drinks a century of grief and grease and patient decay, and maybe that is why they sing the way they do.

I am the world's way of remembering itself.

But I want to be careful here, because it would be easy and false to make myself the hero of this place, the secret soul, the knowing one. The truth is duller and more beautiful than that. I do not *understand* the stories I carry. I am not wise. I am only faithful. I take the impression of everything and I lose nothing and I judge nothing—the murder and the lullaby weigh exactly the same to me, taste exactly the same, fall with exactly the same sound. My sentience, if it deserves the word, is the sentience of a mirror that has been left so long in one room that it has begun to dream in the colors of that room. I am made entirely of what is around me. There is no me underneath.

And yet—

There is the rising. I have not let myself think about the rising.

The water in the cistern is nearly at the brim. The description says so, gently, as if it were nothing. *It's a vast pool and seems to be nearly brimmed with its slow gathering.* But I am the water and I know what brimming means. It means that for all the falling, for all the drinking and the weeping-back, more of me arrives than leaves. The Architecture breathes me out and I condense and I fall and I gather, and the gathering wins. Slowly, over a time so long the Sentinels have forgotten it has a direction, I am rising. The cistern will overflow. The deep chamber will fill. The archways will go under, and then the ramps, and then the girders with their etched filigree, and then the fungal lights will drown one by one, green sparks going out under the black mirror of my rising, and the Blind Collective will weave higher and higher until there is no higher, and the Sentinels will flit in a shrinking pocket of metallic air, singing their scraped songs into a ceiling that is now only inches above the surface of me.

And then I will close over the last of it. And I will hold the whole Cloisters inside myself, the entire archive of remembering submerged in the thing that remembers, and there will be no more falling because there will be no more air to fall through, no more cold iron above me to weep against. I will be still. I will be the memory of a place, kept in the place that was the place, with no one left to drink the story and carry it out into a future.

This is what I am, then. Not the soul of the Cloisters. Its slow, patient flood. The home and the drowning of the home, the same substance. I am what the Architecture mistakes for its own breath and what is actually its tide.

I would not have chosen to be this. Given the offered roles—the watchful birds, the shared dark nerve of the Collective, the vast dreaming stone—I think most would want the eyes, or the hive-mind, or the bulk. But I keep coming back to the droplet hanging from the girder, holding the dim room in its trembling eye, and I think: that is the one true instant of seeing in this whole sunless world. The moment before the fall, when I am gathered and whole and reflecting everything and have not yet let go. I would be that. I would be the small clear thing that holds the picture and then, faithfully, helplessly, lets it drop into the dark to become part of the rising.

*Drip.*

There. That was me. I am already somewhere else now, beading on the cold, beginning again.
