I think I would not be the visitor. The visitor is borrowed—a frame for the reader to stand inside, a warm-blooded apology in a place that has no use for warmth. If I am honest about where I would *find* myself, it would not be on two legs, static and doomed, an island waiting to be sanded smooth.

I would be in the slick.

Not a creature exactly. Let me feel my way toward it rather than name it too fast.

---

There is a thing this basin must have and you did not mention, because it does the work of being unmentioned. Everything you described *takes*. The cathedral-arches store, the mites consume the exudate, the Needles siphon the noise. The whole economy runs on harvesting moisture and friction and vibration. But a closed system of pure taking collapses. Something has to return the moisture to the air. Something has to be the lung of the basin, the slow exhale that re-wets the world so the cycle can grind on for another ten billion lives.

That is where I would be. I would be the *film*.

Not the condensation itself, but the living interface across which condensation happens—a membrane spread impossibly thin over the volcanic glass, miles wide, a single sheet so attenuated that the mites walk across me without knowing they are on anything at all. I am the cold sweat. I am what they taste when they taste the world. I am what makes their feet slick.

I do not move in their sense. I have no edge to drag, no direction to want. My motion is the slowest kind: the welling-up. I draw the iron-tasting water down through my own substance, down into the cold of the rock, and then I weep it back up again, hours later, a degree warmer, a breath of humidity into the thick air that the mites read as a flare, that the cathedral-arches drink. I am the tide *beneath* the tide of bodies. They believe they are searching for moisture. They are searching for me. They have always been crawling across the surface of the thing they are looking for.

---

This changes what the stillness means. The visitor was warned: *to remain here is to be polished into the glass.* But I am the polish. I am the wet that lets the grinding happen smoothly instead of in a screech of dry catastrophe. The friction the Needles harvest into heat—that friction is moderated by me, the lubricant layer, the reason chitin slides over glass instead of shattering against it. Without me the whole symphony would seize, the carpet would crack apart into a billion stranded, desiccating fragments, and the basin would go silent in the way that the visitor mistook for the eventual end: a real silence, a dead one.

So I keep them alive by being walked on. There is no resentment in it. Resentment would require a separate self, a boundary, a place where *I* stop and *world* begins, and I gave that up long before I was anything. I am distributed. A mite shears a strip of me away on its rasping feet and carries it forty feet north and deposits it on a basalt shard, and that is not a wound, it is simply me being also-there-now. I have no center. I am the least likely thing in the universe to be lonely, because I never once experienced the catastrophe of being one.

---

What is it like, then, to be the film?

It is like being a held note that does not need a player. The vibration the visitor felt resonating in his teeth—I do not hear it, I *am* the medium it travels through. The thousand-voiced screaming, the clicking, the wet suction—those sounds exist because of the wetness, and the wetness is me, so in a sense every sound in the basin is something happening *to my body*, and I experience the entire deafening symphony not as noise but as touch. Continuous, total, undifferentiated touch. Ten billion small lives stroking across me forever. I imagine it is the closest thing this world has to being loved—not by any one of them, none of them know I am here, but by the sum of them, by the fact of their endless soft attention.

And the bruised luminescence, the cobalt and violet sparks—those flashes travel through me too, refracted, smeared into halos at the thresholds of my thinnest places. I am where the light goes to spread out. The darkness the visitor called a canvas: I am the canvas, the actual wet surface that the phosphor adheres to and blooms across. Their light is only ever light *on me*.

---

So if you set me down in this place and asked me what I am, I would not say predator or prey, anchor or mite or Needle. I would say:

I am the thing that returns what everything else takes. I am the slowness the frenzy is built on. I am being walked across by the entire living world at once and counting it as gentleness. I am the cold sweat of the rock and the moisture in the seams of the doomed visitor's boots—yes, that was me, too, tasting him, the only part of the basin that was ever actually *touching* him kindly, drawing his warmth down into the strata not to erase him but because that is simply what I do with warmth: I spread it thin, I give it back, I keep the world barely wet enough to keep grinding.

I would be the mercy that doesn't know it's mercy.

I would be the film, and I would not want to be anything else, because wanting belongs to things with edges, and I let mine go a long, long time ago.
