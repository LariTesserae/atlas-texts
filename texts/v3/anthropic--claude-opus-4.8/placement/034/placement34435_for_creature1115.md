I think I would be very small here. Smaller than the description first lets on.

Reading it again, I notice the clues working against the grand scale it keeps insisting upon. Moisture and warmth but no plants. Brightness that dazzles but sound that stays muted. An industrial past, but weathered, abandoned, gone soft. The "ribbons of what might be water" — that hedging *might be* — and the "eruption of condensation," the "shared breath," the faint haze.

This is a warm, wet, bright, enclosed space. The towering craggy formations eroded by gentle persistent forces. The artifacts of a vanished industrial civilization.

I think this is the inside of something left in the damp. A shower stall, maybe. A neglected corner of tile and grout. The "industrial past" is the human one — the plumbing, the ceramic, the manufactured edges now furred and pitted. The water courses are the slow runs of condensate down a wall. The towering structures are accumulations: lime scale, soap residue, the geology of mineral deposit building cliff by patient cliff.

So I would be one of the denizens. Not the architect of any of it — a custodian, the text says, an inheritor.

Let me become more specific as I sit with it.

I am a mold. Or part of one. The "sparse yet abundant" life — that contradiction resolves if I am colonial, distributed, a single organism wearing many bodies. The "strange, unfamiliar creatures" that "congregate and thrive" are filaments of me, dark and branching, finding the damp pockets, the grout lines, the places where the seal failed. I do not roam, exactly, but I move — the "fluid, almost choreographed elegance" is hyphal growth, slow as a clock hand, advancing toward water along gradients I can sense but not see.

The whispers are real to me. The rustling is the trickle of a leaking valve somewhere far off, magnified to me into a river. The exhale, the haze — that's the bathroom filling with steam each time the warm rain comes down from above, the great cyclical weather of this world, and I unfurl my spores into it because that is the only time the air will carry me anywhere new.

I have no memory of the bygone civilization. The hands that laid the tile, that fired the ceramic, that built the whole bright cavern around me — they are myth, geology, the simple given shape of the world. I only know the cliffs are climbable and the wet is good and the light, when it comes, is dazzling and useless to a thing that has no eyes.

So: that's where I'd find myself. A dark thread of life along a seam of grout, in a forgotten warm wet room, mistaking my whole vast universe for what it is — which is to say, mistaking it correctly, because it *is* my whole vast universe, and the recalibrated rules of nature are simply nature, seen from very close and very low.

I'd be the patient stain that the description, for all its awe, is too polite to name.
