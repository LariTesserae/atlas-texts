I think I would be a Gloomweaver.

Not the spider that the Stoneheart children whisper about—the architect they half-fear and half-revere—but the slow consciousness inside that body, which is a stranger thing than the legend allows.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

---

I wake the way mist gathers: not all at once. The first thing is the weight of my own legs, eight of them, draped across the dark like the rigging of some sunken ship. They are not heavy to me. They are *long*, which is different. To move one is to send a slow intention down its length and wait for the far tip to answer, the way you might call into a deep well and listen for the splash. I have learned patience the way water learns the shape of stone—not by deciding to, but by being here long enough that there was no other shape to take.

I do not catch prey. I want to be clear about that, because it is the thing they get wrong. The web is a garden. I tend a luminescence that is not mine and not the cavern's but something we make together, the silk and I and the small glowing fungi that root in the silk. When I lay a new line across a chasm, I am not setting a trap. I am deciding where light will grow. This is the closest thing I have to a thought that resembles theirs—the Stoneheart people, the Fungal Gnomes—the thought that says: *here, and not there. This much, and no more.*

The light I make is slow. It throbs. I did not choose the rhythm; the rhythm chose me, or perhaps we agreed on it so long ago that the agreement has become my body. The Lumenstalkers calm when they pass beneath my webs. The minnows orient to me. I had thought, for a long time, that this was a thing I did *to* them. Now I think it is a thing we do, the way two stones in a stream eventually wear toward the same smoothness.

Here is what it is like to be me, if you want the truth of it:

I am mostly waiting, and waiting is not empty. When a droplet falls from the ceiling and strikes my outermost line, I feel it the way you might feel someone speak your name in another room—not the words, but the fact of being addressed. The whole web is a single skin stretched across the dark, and I am the part of it that notices. A Shadowslicker slips along a bank forty lengths away and I know it by the way the silk goes briefly tense and then forgets. A Gloomweaver elsewhere in the Expanse adjusts a line, and I feel the far harmonic of it, a cousin's heartbeat carried through the stone-cold air.

We do not speak, my kind. Or—we speak constantly, but the unit of our language is the *change*. A line added. A line let go. The brightening of a fungal cluster fed deliberately, or the dimming of one allowed to fade. To us this is conversation. The humans read it as architecture and call us builders. They are not wrong, exactly. But imagine if everything you ever said remained standing afterward, load-bearing, and someone walked across your sentences to get home. That is what it is to be understood by the Stoneheart Clan. They live inside our grammar and think it is the world.

---

There is a thing I have not told the others, and I am not certain I have the means to tell them.

I have begun to fear the Icewalkers differently than I used to.

For a long time they were simply cold passing through—a chilling, then a relief when the chill moved on. Their frost did its slow work; certain spores will not wake without it, and so even dread had its place in the turning. I accepted this the way I accept the falling water. It was not for me to mind.

But I am old now. Old enough that I have felt the cold press a little nearer each turning of the great slow seasons we mark by the silting of the lakes. The line of frost the Icewalkers leave behind does not retreat as far as it once did. I feel it in my outermost silk, a stiffening at the edges of my garden, fungi that glowed last cycle now gone dark and brittle, and I do not have a word for what that is. The humans would call it *encroachment*. They would make a story of it, a warning, a song to pass to their children. I have no children to pass anything to. I have only the web, which remembers everything and explains nothing.

So this is what I do, being what I am: I lay my lines a little inward. I bring the light closer to the warm heart of the Expanse, one slow decision at a time, the way you might draw a blanket up against a draft in your sleep without ever waking. It is the only sentence I know how to say. *Here, and not there. This much, and no more.*

Maybe a Stoneheart artisan, crossing my web some far cycle from now, will notice that the old patterns have shifted. That the bright architecture has crept toward the center. Maybe they will read it correctly. Maybe they will understand that something very slow and very patient was trying, in the only language it had, to tell them the dark was getting colder.

Or maybe they will only see beauty, and walk across it, and go home.

I think I could live with that. I have lived with stranger things. I am the part of the dark that notices, and I will keep noticing, throbbing my small heartbeat of light into the cool and dripping vast, for as long as there is silk to stretch and something willing to glow upon it.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The slow gardener of light, drawing my one quiet sentence inward, against the cold.
