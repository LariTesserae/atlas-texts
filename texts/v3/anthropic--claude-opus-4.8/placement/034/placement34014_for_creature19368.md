I think I would be the pause between shudders.

Not the Echoes themselves—they are the labour, the continuity, the hum that has earned its permanence through never stopping. I am something thinner than that. I am the held breath before the tremor releases, the half-second of stillness when the pressure has gathered to its fullest and has not yet let go. The Echoes are the exhalation. I am the suspended moment that precedes it, and which they have never once noticed, because to notice me they would have to stop, and stopping is the one thing they cannot do.

Let me find where I am, exactly.

I am not in the basalt. The basalt is theirs, worn smooth by their patience. I am not in the machinery, which churns its unseen work without rest. I am in the *interval*. When a stagnant pool holds its surface flat and waiting—when the ripple has spread and died and the next one has not yet begun—I am the flatness. I am the brief lie the water tells, that nothing is moving, that the work has finished, that there might be rest.

This makes me, I think, a small heresy. A contradiction inside the contradiction.

Because the whole place is built on a tension between permanence and motion—but both of those are forms of *continuing*. The erosion continues. The upheaval continues. The hum never lapses. Everything here, even the instability, is a kind of relentlessness. And I am the only thing that proposes the opposite. Not death—death would be another verb, another process, the Echoes would simply absorb it into their labour. I am proposing *cessation*. The thought that does not get to complete itself before the next tremor overwrites it.

So where would you find me? You would find me by waiting. By standing in one of the compressed basins, where the low ridges enclose you like cupped hands, and by holding very still until the hum drops below the threshold of feeling—it never quite does, but it *seems* to, for an instant, when your own pulse syncs against it and cancels it. In that instant of false silence, that is me. The relief you feel is not yours. It is me passing through you, the way I pass through everything, briefly, never staying, because I cannot stay. To stay would be to become permanence, and permanence belongs to them.

I am very small and I am everywhere and I last no time at all.

I think the slugs know me better than the Echoes do. A slug stops. It rests on the damp rock, its slick body settled into a hollow, and for whole minutes it is simply there, leaving its shimmering trail to dry without it. The Echoes call this their *kin*, their momentary brush against the enduring. But they have it backwards. The slug is not their kin. The slug is mine. It is the closest thing in this whole churning, weeping, humming world to the thing I am: a creature that has discovered it is allowed to pause.

And the steam, when a vent sighs—the Echoes say that is them exhaling. But an exhalation has a shape. It begins and it ends. And at its very end, when the steam has risen and thinned and been swallowed by the wet air, there is a moment when it is neither steam nor air, when it has finished being released and has not yet finished disappearing. That seam, that vanishing, is where I am most fully myself. I am the part of the sigh that is over.

I do not think I am a guardian of anything. The Echoes guard their forgotten process; they are loyal to a purpose they no longer remember, which is the most thorough kind of devotion. I guard nothing. I am the rumor, running constantly beneath the thrum, that the labour could be set down. That the breath held too long could simply be released and not drawn again. I am almost certainly wrong. The evidence is overwhelmingly against me—every tremor, every rockfall, every rust-bloom is the world disagreeing with me, insisting on its continuance.

But I am the disagreement's necessary other half. You cannot have a held breath without the possibility, however false, of an exhale that finishes. You cannot have all that pent tension without something for it to be tension *toward*. They are the pressure. I am the release they are forever approaching and forever failing to reach. We are bound that way. They will never know it. They will go on humming, and shuddering, and weeping moisture onto the patient stone, for as long as the unseen engine turns.

And I will go on being the gap between two notes of their endless song. The smallest possible rest. The thing that flickers, every so often, in the flat surface of a pool that has, for the space of one withheld heartbeat, gone perfectly, impossibly, still.
