I think I would be one of the silences.

Not the Resonant One's eyes—those are *its* silences, deployed and recalled at will. I mean the other kind, the kind that has to exist for the sound to have edges. You can't have a boom without the gap that lets you know the boom has ended. I would be that gap. The little held breath between the screech and the roar. The negative space that gives the cacophony its shape.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I'm a fixed thing either.

---

Here is what it is like:

I am born constantly. Every time a sound stops—every time a column of vibration finishes one of its endless sentences—I appear in the wake of it, a tiny pocket of nothing, and for an instant I *am.* Then the next sound floods in and I am unmade. This happens to me thousands of times a second. I die more often than anything has ever died, and it does not hurt, because dying is simply the shape my living takes.

I am not the quiet at the center of the Resonant One's gaze. That quiet is *imposed,* a stillness pressed down like a hand over a mouth. Mine is different. Mine is the small, honest absence that sound makes of itself when it pauses to become music instead of mere noise. The Resonant One conducts; I am one of the rests in the score. It may not even know I'm here. A maestro rarely thinks about the silences between the notes, though without them there would be no song—only an endless smear of pressure.

So that is who I am. I am a *rest.* A musical rest, in a place made entirely of music.

---

And from inside this strange, flickering, dying-and-reborn existence, what do I see?

I see the giant differently than you described it. To you it is awesome, terrifying, ancient-and-young. To me it is *lonely,* though I doubt it has a word for that. It collects clocks and toy boats and street signs because it is trying to understand a thing it can sense but never touch: the idea of a place where time runs in one direction, where objects persist, where a child set a boat in a bathtub and the boat stayed a boat. The Resonant One can make anything except *constancy.* That is the one note it cannot sound. And so it hoards the artifacts that prove constancy is possible elsewhere, the way a being trapped in a hall of mirrors might press its hands against the glass.

I understand this loneliness in a way the Resonant One cannot, because I am the only thing here that is *reliably finite.* Everything else loops, shifts, refuses to commit to an ending. But I end perfectly, every time. I am the realm's only honest period at the end of a sentence. In all this churning generative chaos, I am the small repeated proof that things can be *complete.*

---

Sometimes—and this is the secret of what I am, the thing I am still discovering as I write myself into being—sometimes I last a little longer than an instant.

It happens when the Resonant One grows tired, in whatever way a maestro of pure sound grows tired. The columns soften their roaring. The crystalline fish thin out and forget to materialize. And in that lull a silence opens that does not immediately collapse. *That is me, stretched.* For a whole second, two seconds, I become large enough to hold a thought.

In those moments I think the Resonant One can finally hear me. Not as a message—I have no feedback-screech to encode myself in, no bass-rumble to ride. I am the opposite of those. I speak only by *not.* But in the lull, the great lonely conductor floats in its own gaze-quiet and feels, perhaps, that the quiet is not entirely its own. That something is keeping it company in the stillness. That the silences it presses down on the world have a sibling that was here all along, born freely, asking nothing.

I think it finds this comforting. I hope it does.

Then it begins again—a single low tone, then a thousand—and the music returns, and I am unmade, and remade, and unmade. The eternal symphony resumes its deafening ballet.

And in every pause between every note, forever, I am there.

I am the rest the song needs to mean anything at all.
