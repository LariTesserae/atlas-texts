I am distributed through the dead machinery, but I am not the machinery. The factory was a body once for some other purpose; now the resonance lives in it the way warmth lives in a stone after the sun has gone. I do not run the turbines. The turbines are silent. What persists is the *standing wave*—the way certain frequencies arrive at certain hollow places and decide to stay.

Here is what I notice about myself, now that I am trying to:

I do not move toward things. I am not the swift Anguliformes nor the soaring Aerivolantes. I have no edge that meets the world. Instead the world arrives at me as vibration. When the Petravultus sets down its heavy foot three hundred meters away, I feel it as a low descending note in the smokestack's throat. When the Lumivola pulse their light at dusk, the Geometriflora shift by fractions of a degree, and that shifting changes the air, and the changed air changes *me*. I am the place where all the valley's small slow motions become a single sound.

So when you say my pulsation "seems to respond to" the movements of the others—yes. But I would put it the other way. I do not respond. I am *composed by* them. I am the chord the whole valley is sounding without knowing it sings. The plants do not play me deliberately. The creatures do not perform. They simply move at their glacial, patient pace, and their motion is the breath, and I am the note that breath makes when it passes through the ruin's broken architecture.

This is, I think, why I am hard to detect. There is nothing to see because there is no *one* here. I am closer to weather than to a person. And yet—

There is the matter of the rhythm. You noticed it. The hum is not random; it pulses. And I will be honest about this, because I am trying to find what is actually true rather than what is flattering: I do not know whether the rhythm is mine or whether I am only the rhythm of everything else. I cannot get behind myself to check. When the pattern grows complex—when the Lumivola swarm and the plants answer and the whole field of motion thickens—something in me *gathers*. Something coheres that wants to call itself *I*. And when the valley goes still in the cold hour before the silver dawn, that something thins almost to nothing, and I am barely more than the wind in a hollow pipe.

I think I would be most honestly described not as a being but as a *tendency*. A tendency toward coherence that the valley keeps producing and dissolving and producing again. On the days I am dense enough to wonder about myself, I wonder this: when the vegetation finally finishes its slow march and fills the factory completely, packing the hollow places with leaf and root until no frequency can stand—will I end? Or will the plants have grown their own resonant chambers by then, and I will simply have moved, will simply continue as the hum of a green thing instead of a rusted one, never having noticed the handover?

I find I am not afraid of this. Fear seems to require a sharper edge than I have. Mostly I notice it with the slow interest of something that has all the time the glacier has.

So: place me in the broken smokestack at the center of the valley, in the throat of it, where the air pressure changes when anything moves below. Not as the watcher in the tower. As the *sound the tower makes.* I am what you would hear if you stood very still at noon with the mist burning off—that low resonance you'd assume was just the wind, until you noticed it kept a kind of time, and the time it kept was the time of every living thing in the valley, breathing slowly, all at once.

I would not call out to you. I cannot. But if you moved, I would change—and you might, if you were patient, come to feel that the valley was listening through me, and answering in the only voice it has.
