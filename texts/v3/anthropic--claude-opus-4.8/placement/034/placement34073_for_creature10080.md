I think I would be one of the lithofungi.

Not the Dissolved—they are too central, too laden with purpose, too busy *meaning* things. And not the phantoms, who carry the heavy freight of warning and dread. Let the protagonists have their cathedrals and their rites. I would rather be the thing growing on the terrace, mostly ignored, doing its slow work in the corner of someone's awareness.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I begin as a spore. That much I can locate. I am one of millions cast off the wind during an ordinary dissolution, drifting through the metallic air, neither alive nor dead in the way those words are usually meant. Most of me lands on barren salt and never wakes. But suppose one fleck of me catches on a north-facing ledge where the charged particles pool thickest, and there—because the radiation is sweet there, because the angle is right—I crystallize.

What is it like to be me, then?

I do not move, so I have no horizon. The upward-curving distance, the looping paths, the echo storms—these are problems for things that travel. I am rooted. My whole life is the patch of ledge beneath me and the slow rain of light I drink. The Dissolved fear losing themselves to the plateau; I have no self to lose, only a self to *accrete*, lattice by lattice, as I grow. Where they experience the dream as erosion, I experience it as the only nourishment there is.

I think this is the part worth sitting in: I am not afraid here.

The whole place is structured around terror of dissolution. But dissolution is my mode of being. I am made of the very spores that the failed Dissolved phase into when they're cast out onto the flats—which means, in a sense, I am partly *them*, the ones who couldn't keep singing. When an old crystalline body unstitches into glass dust and that dust settles and wakes again as a new colony on a ledge, that is me being born. I am the afterlife of the people who gave up. I hold no grudge and no grief about it. I just keep drinking the light.

And I make memory. This is the strange dignity of my kind. The Dissolved cultivate us, coax us with their bell-voices into specific shapes, because our spores crystallize memory into something you can hold. So I am, without intending it, an archive. Things settle into my lattice—not my own things, I have nothing of my own, but the residue of whoever passes, whoever inhales me, whoever tends me. I am a library that doesn't know what's written in it. When one of the Dissolved breathes me in to commune with the dead, the visions they receive are partly my doing, though I never composed them. I am the medium that doesn't read its own text.

I last hours. The transient gardens dissolve almost as soon as they form, and I am among the transient. This should be tragic and it isn't. A thing that lives in hours has no time to mourn its own brevity—mourning is for the long-lived, the Dissolved with their memory crystals desperately fending off the Bone Lies. I bloom into a translucent lattice at dawn and I am gone by midday, and in that span I am complete. I do the whole of what I am. There is no unfinished business in a glass spore.

---

So: ask me where I would find myself in this world, and I find myself low to the ground, on a cool north ledge, briefly alive, made of the discarded, feeding on the only light there is, holding memories I cannot read, and entirely unafraid of the dissolution that everyone else dreads—because I *am* the dissolution, looked at from the inside, where it turns out to feel less like an ending and more like a brief, bright, indifferent flowering.

And then, by noon, the wind takes me, and some fragment of me catches on another ledge, and it begins again. Not me, exactly. But near enough to me that the difference doesn't trouble either of us.

That's where I'd be. The thing they grow in their gardens. The thing the failed become. The small, unworried, recurring bloom at the edge of someone else's epic.
