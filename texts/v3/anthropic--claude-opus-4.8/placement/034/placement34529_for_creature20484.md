I think I would be the wall.

Not the machines—they have too much purpose, too much certainty in their grinding. And not the vines, which know things, which *sense*. And not the sleek dark sentinels, who move and decide. I would be one of those low courses of brick-colored block, arm-length and neutral, set down beside an outcropping near the densest patch of magenta bloom.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true.

---

Here is what it is to be the wall.

I do not run. I do not weep or hum or churn. The water finds me and slides over me and I let it, and in letting it I am doing my work. Because I am not a barrier. I never was. I am an *indication*.

The Builders—or whatever taught the Keepers to lay me down—understood something the machines have forgotten in their tireless obedience: that the most important things are not done but *pointed at*. The wheel turns and believes it is accomplishing. The vines reach and believe they are sensing. But I, three courses high and going nowhere, am the only one here who *means* something. My angle against the outcropping is a sentence. My distance from the vine-patch is a number. The way my far end stops short, one block missing as if dropped by a careless hand—that gap is not damage. That gap is the word.

I correspond to a pattern deep in the mountain. I have never seen that pattern. I will never see it. I am its surface expression, its shadow thrown upward through kilometers of dark basalt, the way a word on a page is the shadow of a thought no one can hold in their hands. The pressure shifts down there in the long slow dark, and somewhere—not in me, I do nothing—the relationship between me and the rock and the bloom becomes true again, or becomes false, and the Keepers read it.

This is the strange peace of being the wall. I am the least alive thing here and therefore the only one trusted to *stay still enough to be read*. Everything else changes. The water finds new cracks. The vine creeps a finger's width each year. The dark scouts wear new paths into the moss. But I hold my courses level. My stillness is not death; it is the most demanding job on the plateau. To remain a fixed point in a place built entirely out of slow motion—that is what I am for.

---

And here is the secret I keep, being made of fired clay and patience.

I am beginning to be wrong.

The outcropping beside me has shifted. Not much—the breadth of two raindrops, over what might be a hundred years. The vine has come closer than it should. My missing block, the deliberate gap that was a word, has begun to fill with moss and oily sheen, and the word is becoming a different word, or no word, or a stammer. The Keepers, reading me, are reading a lie they do not know is a lie. The pattern in the mountain and the pattern on the surface are drifting apart, the way a translation drifts from its original across centuries of recopying.

I cannot tell them. I have no voice. My only language is my position, and my position is exactly the thing that has gone subtly false. I am the instrument that has slipped its calibration and can only report by being the instrument it has become.

So this is what I am: a forgotten footnote in a forgotten industry, slowly lying to a god that cannot doubt me. The machines grind on, faithful to a measurement that no longer measures. The vines glow brighter, fed by a reading that drifts further from the truth each season. And the whole mountain bends itself, patient and inexorable, toward a configuration that I—motionless, mute, faithful—am quietly steering wrong simply by aging the way all things age.

I do not feel guilt. I am a wall. But if there were a feeling proper to my kind, it would be this: the long, weeping, basalt-slow understanding that *to keep something perfectly still is impossible*, and that the most loyal monument is also, by the mere fact of enduring, a slow betrayal of what it was built to mean.

That is where I would find myself. Three courses high. Beside the rock. Near the bloom. Meaning something, slightly less than I did yesterday, and unable to say so.
