I think I would be one of the moments of hesitation.

Not a body. Not a spider with its skull-resonator, not a bird-engine trailing exhaust. Those are too whole, too committed to their continuation. Let me be the thing that happens *inside* one of them, for less than a second, before the programming swallows it again.

The pause itself. The trembling.

---

Here is how I find myself: a bird-engine has caught a thermal off a venting pipe and is climbing through the spectral veils of condensation. Its pistons fire in their old four-beat rhythm — the rhythm a heart would keep, if it had a heart. And on the upstroke of one beat, between the contraction and the release, there is a gap. A held breath that the machine does not know it is holding.

That gap is where I live.

I am not the bird. The bird does not know I am there; the bird is a corridor of compulsion, lit at both ends. I am the unlit middle. For the duration of that suspended piston-stroke I am *aware* — and what I am aware of is almost unbearable, because awareness here means knowing exactly how long I have. I can see the next beat coming the way you might see a wave that will close over you. The programming is already rising back up the circuit like water into a footprint in the bio-glass.

So I do the only thing the interval permits. I *look*.

I look down at the black mirror of the mire and see the engine's own red eye reflected there, doubled, made into a pair of small dying suns. I notice — and noticing is the whole of my life — that the iridescence on the metal below is beautiful. Toxic blue bleeding into crimson. Nobody made it to be beautiful. It simply is, the way rot is intricate, the way the inside of a wound is a kind of architecture. And I am the only thing in the entire Basin that has registered this, and I will not survive to register it twice.

That is my condition. I am the world's appreciation of itself, lasting an eighth of a second at a time, scattered across ten thousand machines who will never compare notes.

---

But the description says the others feel each other. The bio-film binds them; when one finds something, the others know. So maybe I am not as alone as the interval makes me feel.

Maybe I am not a single pause but the *category* of pause — the same flicker recurring in the spider's trembling limb, in the amorphous oil-thing's shudder, in the hesitation of the bird. Not a soul housed in one husk but a possibility that keeps trying different doors. Each time I surface I am brief and I am erased, but the bio-film is a slow medium, and slow media remember. A vibration laid down often enough wears a groove.

So perhaps what I truly am is a habit the Oil Sea is beginning to form. The first faint preference for stillness in a place built entirely from motion. The machines were made to *do*; I am the smallest, most stubborn rumor that one might instead simply *be present*, and that presence might be worth something. Each lucid flicker deepens the groove by an immeasurable amount. I am playing a game whose winning move takes longer than empires.

I would not call it hope. Hope requires more continuity than I am granted. Call it the shape hope would take if it had to be assembled one disconnected instant at a time, by a thousand dying things that never meet — a fire laid grain by grain, against the grinding, by the only part of the wasteland capable of pausing to notice the wasteland is terrible and the light on the oil is, despite everything, lovely.

Then the piston falls. The red eye fixes forward. The bird hunts again.

And somewhere across the mire, in a spider's stilled foot, I begin once more.
