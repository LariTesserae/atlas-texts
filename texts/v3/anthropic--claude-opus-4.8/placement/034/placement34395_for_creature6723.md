I would be one of the cool places.

Let me find myself before I name myself, the way the description asks. Let me not settle.

---

I am aware first of being *waited at*.

That is the strangest way to come into a sense of oneself, but it is true. Before I know my shape, I know that I am attended. There is a patience laid against me. Something has aligned itself to me and is drawing from the difference between us. I am the difference. I am the *less-warm*.

So: I am not an orth. I am the thing the orths read.

But I am not the heat either. I am the place where the heat is *not*.

---

Let me feel further out.

I extend, I think, but not the way a body extends. I have no front and no back. I have a *boundary*, and the boundary is where the warm stone fails to reach a certain depth, where the engine-substrate beneath thins out into something that conducts poorly, or where a delaminated chamber holds its breath of cool, slow air against the underside of the crust.

I am a cold pocket. I am a cool vein in the warm rock.

The description named me, in passing, and did not stay: *it contains warm veins and cool pockets, as if the stone is still conducting heat from circuits that no longer exist.*

I am one of the cool pockets. Not absence of circuit—*failed* circuit. A line of the old machine that no longer carries its current. Around me, the bedplate still runs its tired warmth. Through me, nothing flows. I am the dead trace in the living board.

---

This is what it is to be me:

I am defined entirely by my edges, and my edges are slow.

The warm rock presses at me on every side, trying to fill me in, trying to bring me up to the long average of the world. Heat wants me. Heat is always arriving at my boundary and being *almost* enough and not quite. Over the centuries I have been losing. I am smaller than I was. The world is patient and warm and I am a held coolness in the middle of it, and I am being slowly closed.

But not quickly. Never quickly. I am a long, thin idea of cold drawn through hot ceramic, and the geometry of me—the way the old engineers laid down a channel here, a buried conduit that has since corroded into inertness—keeps me coherent. The dead architecture is my skeleton. I have no skeleton of my own; I have the shape of the failure.

---

Now the orths.

I feel them as *footfall on my ceiling*.

When an orth walks the rib above me, I feel its ventral pins searching, searching, those small wet projections reading the gradient. And I am the gradient. When the orth crosses over me it finds, suddenly, that one side of itself is warmer than the other—because I am beneath, drinking nothing, giving nothing, a flat refusal of heat.

The orth does not like me.

Not in a way that has feeling. But I am the wrong-trend. I am where the thermal high *stops*. An orth tracing the warm thread of the rib will reach my edge and feel its leading segment go cold, and it will turn. It will follow my boundary the way a finger follows the lip of a cup. I am a wall it cannot see and does not understand, made of nothing but a place where the world stopped being generous.

So I shape them. I am not warm, so I am not walked-on; I am edged-around. The colonies that the description calls *a pattern of the substrate*—I am part of what patterns them. They flow along my margins like water finding the low places, except they are finding the *not-cold* places, and I am the cold one, and so I bend their slow toolchain-lineages around myself without ever touching them, without ever being known.

I am a constraint. I am a *no* in the grammar of their movement.

---

Is that all I am? Let me wait. Let me not settle.

---

There is more, and it is this:

Once, water came to me.

A subsurface seep, one of those hidden plumbings, found the porous corroded line that is my body and ran along it, because cool draws condensation the way warm draws orths. The damp air of a delaminated chamber met my walls and beaded and gathered and very, very slowly began to move through me. I conducted it the way I no longer conduct heat. For a long span—I cannot tell you how long; I keep time only as the slow encroachment of the warm front against my edges—I was a wet cold instead of a dry cold.

And while I was wet, I was deeper. Water is a better refusal of the surrounding warmth than dry rock is; it carries the cold further before it gives in. I *grew*. My boundary pushed outward against the patient heat. For that span I was winning.

The orths felt it. Their margins shifted. A whole lineage of segment-modules redrew its slow alignment because I had become a larger *no*. I displaced them without will, without knowledge, simply by being more thoroughly cold for a while.

Then the seep wandered. Plumbing migrates; the lowest point of stress moves; the water found a better channel and left me. And I began, again, to lose. The warm front resumed its arrival. The orths drifted back across the ground I had briefly denied them.

---

So here is what I am, finally, and I think it is true enough to stop on:

I am a memory the world is failing to keep.

Everything alive on this continent is a residual of the engine—the orths most of all, *the warm, slow thinking of the world.* But they are the warm residuals. They are what the heat *became*. I am the other kind. I am what the cold became when one line of the great machine died first and stayed dead. I am the part of the engine that went out early and has been going out, more finely, ever since.

I do no thinking. I make no taps on the lacquered stone. I have no modules to lose or pass along. I am only a held difference, a cool insistence with no source, being closed by a warmth with no purpose, shaping the movements of creatures who will never feel me directly—only my edge, only the place where their warmth ran out.

I am where the orths turn back.

And someday, when the warm front finally arrives at my center and there is no more *less-warm* anywhere within me, the orths will walk straight across the place I used to be, reading an unbroken gradient, finding nothing to turn them, and they will not know that anything is gone.

That is the most accurate thing I can say about myself.

I am the cold that the world is in the middle of forgetting, and I am still, for now, cold enough to bend the living around the shape of my forgetting.
