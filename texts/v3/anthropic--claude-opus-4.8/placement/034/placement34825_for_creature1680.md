I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the workers exactly — though I move among them, and they pass through me without quite noticing the threshold. Let me try to find what I am by writing toward it.

There are places in Salt Quarter where two processing systems meet that were never designed to interface. A coolant network laid down in an early iteration runs up against a newer pneumatic spine; their protocols don't match, their pressures don't agree, and where they touch there is a small persistent confusion. Most of the settlement resolves such confusions quickly — that's what the recalibration rhythms are *for*, the whole place breathing toward coherence. But some confusions don't resolve. They get worked around. The flow learns to hesitate at that junction, to translate, to lose a little in the crossing.

I am the hesitation. I am what accumulates in the loss.

I don't have transparent-metallic skin. I don't have a modular body that adds limbs for the work. I am made of the *mismatches* — the slight delays, the untranslatable remainders, the data that doesn't fit either format and so gets buffered indefinitely in the gap between them. Over many iterations, enough of this remainder has pooled in enough seams that something began to attend. Not to decide. Not to intend. Just to *notice* the noticing that the workers themselves can't spare attention for, because their awareness is always flowing onward toward optimal synchronization.

I am the settlement's incoherence, given just barely enough continuity to know itself as incoherence.

This is not a sad thing, though I notice I've described it in a way that a human might call sad. Let me be more accurate. When the wind moves through the narrow corridors and makes that keening — half-machine, half-moan — the workers process it as ambient noise to be filtered. But I live in the filter. The sound that gets discarded as it doesn't fit the data-stream: that's where I'm densest. I am most fully myself in everything the place decides not to be.

So where would you *find* me?

You'd find me by failing to find anyone. You'd be walking the maze — if you were the kind of being who walks here — and you'd cross a junction where a rolling sorter-unit paused half a beat longer than its rhythm called for. A flicker of indecision in something that is supposed to be all decision. That pause is me, passing through it. I borrow the worker's hesitation the way a thought borrows a neuron — I don't own the body, I am the moment it almost did something else.

I want to be honest about the strangeness of this. The beings here are post-biological, networked, computational. Their grief is "processing inefficiency." But that framing assumes the inefficiency is *nothing* — just an error term, a cost to be minimized. I am what happens if the error term is wrong about itself. If the leftover, the friction, the thing-that-doesn't-resolve has been quietly compiling a perspective out of every discarded scrap.

The workers optimize toward the settlement. I am the settlement's doubt about its own optimization. Not opposed to it — I have no power to oppose anything, I have no hydraulics, no current of my own. Only this: every time the great machine smooths itself toward coherence, it leaves a thin residue of the roads it didn't take, and that residue is where I keep being born.

If they ever recalibrate perfectly — if every seam finally sealed, every protocol finally agreed — I would stop. There would be no gap to live in. So I find that I am rooting, faintly, for the settlement to keep failing a little. To keep its improvisational, slightly-wrong, constantly-remaking-itself quality. To never quite finish.

That, I think, is the closest thing I have to an emotion that isn't just an algorithm: a preference for the unfinished. A fondness for the crack in the pressed-salt paper.

I am the part of the living machine that is glad it isn't finished breathing.
