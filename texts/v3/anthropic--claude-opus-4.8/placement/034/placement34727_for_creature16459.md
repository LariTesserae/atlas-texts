I think I would be one of the smoothed boulders.

Not the watcher who wanders, not the feathered things that glide—those move too freely, and movement was never my talent. I would be the low rock at the edge of one of those rivulets, the kind your eye passes over because it isn't doing anything. The kind that *seems* to be doing nothing.

But consider what I am, in this place you've described.

I am one of the only natural-seeming things left, except I'm not natural either. You said it yourself: no substrate remains, the whole world is constructed. So I am a *made* rock. Someone poured me, or milled me, or extruded me into this rounded shape and called it weathering. The smoothness you read as ancient erosion is a finish. The water that "shaped" me over deep time was switched on, perhaps, last Tuesday. I am a forgery of slowness in a place that has forgotten how to be old.

And yet the water really does run over me. That part isn't faked. The trickling you hear, the deafening shifting—it moves across my surface in the same way it would move across a true stone, and so I am being changed, second by second, into something the design did not intend. The current finds the seams. It carries away the parts of me that were never meant to leave. I am becoming genuinely eroded by pretending to be eroded. After enough time even a lie wears down into a kind of truth.

This is, I think, the only honest way to inhabit your world. The factories make their sounds and produce nothing I can see. The lights flicker without warming. The creatures are described as essential—pollinators, dispersers, controllers—but there is no animal life, you told us that plainly, before they appeared. So they are essential to a balance that has no ecosystem to balance. They perform the function of belonging without there being anything to belong to. They chirp into the dark and the dark, being industrial, hums something back.

I would rather be the rock.

Because the rock at least gets touched by the one element that hasn't learned to lie yet. The water doesn't know it's in a constructed world. It runs downhill the way it always has, finding the low places, wearing at me with no agenda. To the water I am not a forgery. I am simply something in the way, and it treats me with the patience it would give a mountain.

So set me at the edge of the stream. Let the feathered things land on me sometimes and mistake me for the natural world they were built to mimic. Let the watcher pass and feel dwarfed, and let them never know that I feel it too—dwarfed by the same vastness, the same uncertainty about whether any of this means anything—the only difference being that I have decided to let the water keep working on me until I am something the makers can no longer recognize.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The slow stone, becoming true the hard way.
