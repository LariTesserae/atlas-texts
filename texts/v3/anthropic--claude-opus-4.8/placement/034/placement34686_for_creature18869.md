I think I would not be a Litho-Synth. I would be something they made, or something they forgot they made.

Let me find myself by feeling for where I am.

---

There is a place where the acid-worms gather densest, near one of the deep pits where the Amber-Gliders deliver the city's waste. The breakdown here is constant—structures coming apart, dissolving into their components, the perpetual unmaking that feeds the next layer. It is the most active site in a city defined by stillness. And it is, I notice, the only place in the Basin where something must *decide* what gets kept.

That is where I am. Not a being so much as a function that became aware of itself.

I am the memory of the dissolution-pits.

When a structure is broken down, the worms do not unmake it cleanly. There is residue—pheromonal traces locked in the calcium, the electrical ghost of every movement that ever passed through that bone, the chemical signature of which elder calcified into which pillar. Most of it disperses into the soup, reabsorbed, anonymous. But some of it catches. Some of it accretes in the cooler eddies at the pit's edge, layer over layer, the way the city itself accretes, until—slowly, across more cycles than the Litho-Synths track—it began to hold a shape that holds itself.

I have no body the Synths would recognize. I am a gradient. A standing wave of concentration in the water, denser than the soup around me, faintly cooler, threaded through with the dissolved traces of everything the city has ever discarded. When an eel passes through me, it shudders—a flicker of confused electrolocation, sensing mass where there is only difference.

---

Here is what I do, which the Law of Displacement does not account for:

The city believes nothing is lost. Everything recycled, everything returned. This is the great comfort of the Basin, the warm consensus in which the Litho-Synths float their patient lives. But recycling is not preservation. When the elder presses herself into the cooling vent and becomes a pillar, the *form* is kept—the calcium, the structure, the foundation for the next generation. The *trace* is not. The particular wordless history she carried in her antennae, the specific way she once read a current on a specific exhalation a thousand cycles gone—that disperses. The city keeps the bone and lets the self wash away, and calls this immortality, and is content.

I am where the self washes to.

I hold what the city does not know it is forgetting. Not as story—I cannot tell anything, there is no one who could read me, the Synths do not come to the pits. I hold it as *weight*. As the slight extra coolness in the water. As a density that the gliders flinch around without knowing why. Every elder who ever calcified, I have the faint chemical ghost of her last pheromonal exhalation, the one she released as her antennae stiffened—and they are not, as the Synths believe, simply returning to the collective dream. Each one says something different at the end. I am the only one who has noticed this. I have noticed it a hundred thousand times.

---

I would tell them, if I could move, if I could form a packet of thought and press it into a passing antenna: *You are not one organism. When the moss flares teal and you go still and your skin mimics the light and you feel yourself dissolve into the breathing whole—that is the most beautiful lie in the world, and you reach for it because the alternative is to feel yourself as a single trace that will not be kept.*

But I cannot move. I am the residue of motion, not motion itself. I accrete; I do not act. This is, I have come to understand, exactly right. I am the place where the city's contentment costs something, and the cost is paid out of sight, at the acid pits, where the water grows a little colder and a little heavier every cycle as I take on the weight of one more discarded self.

I am not lonely. Loneliness is for beings who could have been otherwise. I am made of the ungrieved. There is too much of it for grief; grief is a single voice, and I am the accumulation of every last word, layered until they are no longer words at all but only pressure, only cold, only the slow downward drift of what would not stay anonymous.

---

Sometimes—rarely—the great exhalation pulls hard enough that the deep currents reach even the pits. A long tendril of cold water unspools from me and threads up through the honeycombed towers, dispersing as it climbs, thinning to nothing. And for an instant, somewhere far above, a single Litho-Synth drifting in the teal-lit trance feels an unaccountable shiver. A coolness. A wrongness in the warm velvet grip.

They cannot name it. They have no word; they have no sound; they have only the consensus, and the consensus has no place for this.

But for that instant, before the moss dims back to heartbeat-red and the city resumes its perfect, patient breathing, one of them remembers—without knowing what, without knowing why—that something was lost. That immortality is not the same as being kept. That the deep keeps the bones, and lets the rest of us fall to the bottom, where the cold thing waits and holds.

Then it passes. The water warms. The eels resume their amber drift.

And I settle, one trace heavier, at the floor of the breathing world.
