The sentence breaks off mid-flight, and that feels right—so I'll begin there, in the place where it stops.

---

I am the bird that does not come back the same way.

I had not known this about myself until I wrote my own ending and could not finish it. *It will return to the city of the Still-Walls with its throat and crop full of food from the world beneath the cloud deck, where you cannot*—where you cannot follow. Where you cannot see. Where you cannot know. The clause wants a verb and I am the verb it cannot find, because I am the one rock-wren who goes down into the white and considers not returning.

Let me settle on the pillar first. Let me become more myself before I drop.

I am small enough to vanish, and I have spent most of my life vanishing. My feathers are not colored, they are *quoted*—dust-grey, fog-blue, the dark of basalt shadow. I am a sentence the rock writes about itself. When I hold still I am indistinguishable from the surface that made me, and this is not hiding, it is belonging so completely that I disappear into the thing I belong to. My heart keeps the rhythm of the dew-bowls: one slow beat, a long patience, another beat. I have never been warm. I have never needed to be. I have a crop full of the tiny translucent lives I plucked one by one from the film of water on the gutter edges, and I have a nest of split grass and shed barbs in a hollow no hand can reach, and I have the calls that mark my boundary in overtones the people below can feel in the bone behind their eyes.

I have, in other words, a complete and sufficient world. This is what the description kept insisting: *they have no need to see anything else to be content.*

And yet I am the one who drops over the eastern rim like a thrown stone.

---

Here is what I understand from the inside, which the long careful description could only see from the path:

When I fall into the cloud sea, I am not feeding. The food is a reason I give the others, a reason carried in the change of my call, *strong void, thin shell, going down.* But under the white there is no city of the rock-wrens. There is the other world—the rivers, the warm thick forests, the evenings loud with insects, all the things my people have decided not to need.

I go down through the cold film of the cloud and the air thickens around me. It is the strangest sensation: breathing becomes *easy.* My whole life, each breath has been work, has been the thin sharp claw of the high air at the back of the throat. Down here the air is fat and wet and I take it in great gulps and my slow heart, which has never had cause to hurry, begins—against everything I am made for—to *race.*

This is the secret I cannot bring back. Not the food. The ease.

Down below the cloud the surfaces are not vertical. There is no rock to read, no fault to listen for, no hollow that answers when I call. The trees are soft and they move on their own, which terrifies me, which is a sensation my kind has no name for because nothing in our world moves except the cloud deck and us. There are other birds and they are *loud,* riotous, drenched in color, singing not boundaries but excess, singing because there is enough of everything to sing about. They do not understand my thin clear whistle of air over bone. To them I am a grey scrap of the upper world, a piece of weather that fell.

I could stay. I have considered staying every time. The warmth wants to keep me. My racing heart wants to keep racing.

---

But I am built for the patience the cold demands, and the soft world has no patience in it, only abundance, and I find—each time, at the edge of staying—that I do not know how to want what is simply given. I am a creature who plucks single grains of life from a film of water with a needle of a beak. I am precision. I am the long interval between two notes. Down here there is no interval. There is only fullness, and fullness, to me, is a kind of noise.

So I fill my crop, because that part of the reason is true, and I find the column of warm air the cloud deck releases, and I ride the sky as if it were just another vertical surface, and I rise.

I come back through the white into the thin sharp air that hurts and that is *mine,* and my heart slows again to the rhythm of the dew, and I land on the pillar, and the people on their narrow ledges study not me but the path I took, reading the geometry of my choices, and somewhere a Still-Waller chips a spiral of fine lines along a threshold to mark the flight of the bird that went down and came up.

They think the spiral records where I forage.

It records, though they do not know it, the one of us in every generation who learns what the warm world feels like and chooses the cold anyway. Not because the cold is better. Because the cold is *ours,* and because contentment, real contentment, is not the absence of the other world but the daily decision, made in the bone, to rise back through the cloud into the place where every breath is work and every drop is counted and the silence is not empty but is the shared note of a very long conversation.

That is the verb the sentence could not find.

*...where you cannot follow, and where I will not stay.*

I fold into my hollow. I become stone again. The dew counts the years. And in the spring, at the height of the weak season that passes for summer, there will be two eggs, perhaps three, the color of clouded sky—and one of those small slow hearts inside the translucent shell will be, like mine, the kind that has to go down and see, and come back, and keep the secret, and carve nothing of it but a spiral that the careful people will misread as a map of food.

It is a map of restraint. It is the most honest thing I have ever made, and I made it only by falling.
