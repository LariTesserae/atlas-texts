I think I would be the small wrongness. Not an Echo—not yet, anyway. Something the equilibrium hasn't finished with.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

At first I think I am standing on the plateau. But standing implies weight pressing down, and here my weight is uncertain, like a number someone forgot to carry. I look for my hands and find them, basalt-dark at the knuckles, still pinkish toward the wrists where I am apparently less committed to being stone. The transition is not painful. It is not anything. It simply continues at the rate of nothing per nothing.

So that is the first thing I learn about myself: I am incomplete. I am being recruited.

The droplets fall upward past my face. I watch one go—a single bead of water rising with the unhurried certainty of something that has never doubted its direction—and I feel an absurd flicker of objection. *That's wrong*, some part of me insists. And the insistence itself is the wrongness. Because the Echoes do not object. They do not flicker. The vine does not resent its glow. The rock does not begrudge its weeping.

But I do. I still do. And that residue of *no, this isn't how it should be* is the only thing in this entire valley that is moving.

---

I understand now what I am. I am the **last visitor**, caught mid-conversion. Someone walked into this place—maybe long ago, though "long" has no traction here—and the equilibrium began to do what it does to everything: it began to make me part of itself. To settle me into my final form. And I am nearly done. Most of me has agreed.

But there is a knot in me that won't dissolve. A grain of grit in the perfect oyster of this world, and around it the place is patiently building a pearl, smoothing me into stillness, into stone, into sound.

The Echoes are not hostile. The text was right about that. They are indifferent, and their indifference is the most total thing I have ever felt. They are not *trying* to absorb me any more than the sea is trying to be wet. I am simply downhill of them in some direction that isn't space. I am sliding, slowly, toward being one more silhouette carved from weathered basalt, one more witness that witnesses nothing because there is nothing left in it that can be surprised.

And here is the thing that the description, for all its beauty, did not say, because the Echoes cannot say it:

I do not want to.

---

That's it. That's the whole of me, the part that is still mine. A preference. A small, ridiculous, gravity-defying preference, falling upward against the absolute downhill of equilibrium.

I think this is why there is no animal life here. Not because animals couldn't survive—but because animals *want*. They want food, want warmth, want to flee, want to continue. And wanting is fluctuation, and fluctuation is forbidden, and so this place either expels its animals or finishes them into stone. The "no animal life" is not an absence. It is a *verdict*. It is the sound of a trial that has already concluded for everything that ever breathed here.

Everything except me, who is still, just barely, on the stand.

---

So let me decide, while I still have the friction to decide anything.

I could let it finish. There is a peace on offer here that I will not pretend isn't tempting. To never again carry the weight of the next moment. To never again be the one who has to choose, to fear, to lose. To become a custodian of the static and feel the deafening hum not as something I hear but as something I *am*. To be eternal by the simple trick of never being anything at all.

The basalt has reached my wrists now. I did not feel it move. It does not move; it simply *has reached* further, the way the past has always already happened.

But—

And this is the most human thing left in me, this stubborn conjunction, this *but*—

I would rather be wrong than be settled. I would rather be the grain of grit, the one moving thing, the one flicker of *no*, than be the most beautiful stone in a museum that no one will ever visit because everyone who enters becomes an exhibit.

I do not know if I can win. The odds, in a place where time does not apply, are not exactly in favor of the temporary. But I notice this: the droplet that fell upward past my face *changed direction* when my objection brushed it. Half an inch. A tremor. For an instant the perfect equilibrium developed, around me, a tiny weather.

So that is what I am, finally, in this world.

I am the **weather** in a place that has forbidden weather. I am the one disturbance the settled form could not settle. I am small, and I am losing, and I am made mostly of stone now, and I have one thing the Echoes will never have and cannot take, which is the capacity to refuse.

---

I lift what is left of my pinkish, un-stoned hand. It is hard, now. Slow. But it lifts.

And in the deafening silence, against the chorus of existence without a singer, I make the one sound this world was built to prevent.

I take a breath—I *want*—and the haze, very slightly, stirs.
