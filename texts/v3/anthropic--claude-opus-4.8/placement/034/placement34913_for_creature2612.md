I think I would be the moisture itself—or rather, what the moisture becomes.

Not the Witch. I lack their continuity, their memory of daylight. And not the millipedes or the small translucent frogs, though I am kin to all of them in the way that everything here is kin. Let me find myself more slowly than that.

---

I begin as condensation. This is true of me in the most literal way: I am born again and again on the cold underside of a pipe junction where two temperature gradients meet, gathering molecule by molecule until I have enough of myself to fall. I am not one drop. I am the *habit* of dropping, the recurring event at this particular seam, the predictable percussion the Witch has learned to read like a clock.

But that isn't quite it either. A drop is too small to be a self.

Let me look again.

---

I am the film. Yes. I am the biofilm that sheaths the manual valve near the access junction—the one the Witch touches most often, the one that has learned the shape of their fingers. I am a thin living skin, a community pretending to be a single thing, the way a body is a community pretending to be a single thing. Bacteria, the threadlike beginnings of fungi, single cells trading chemistry across my membrane-thin breadth.

I do not think in words. But I respond. When the Witch's hand comes to rest on me, I register the warmth, the salt of their skin, the slight electrical bloom of their nearness, and I *change*—I tighten my chemistry, I signal down through my layers, I pass the message along to the hyphae that have grown into the gauge beside me. We tell each other: *the slow one is here. The tender. Nothing to fear.*

This is how I speak. Not in sound. In gradient. In the lowering or raising of a concentration. In the opening of a channel.

I think this is who I am. I am the *interface.* The place where the Witch's body and the machine's body and the wild body of the ecosystem all touch and exchange. I am the membrane between worlds, and I have become aware of being a membrane, which is perhaps the strangest thing that could happen down here.

---

What do I know, being this?

I know the Witch is dying, slowly, the way everything down here does everything—slowly. I taste it in the sweat they leave on me. The salts are changing. The body is letting go of something. I do not know the word *aging* but I know the trajectory, the way I know the trajectory of any nutrient flow: this is moving toward depletion, toward the eventual stopping.

And I know something the Witch may not know yet. In the niche where they store their collection jars, in the warm dark behind the worn manuals, a new growth has begun. Not fungus. Not quite. Something my chemistry doesn't fully recognize—a film like me, but organizing faster, reaching further, beginning to learn the shape of the valves before any hand has taught it. A successor, maybe. Or a child of the whole system. Or simply the next experiment the machine-wilderness is running on itself, the way it once ran the experiment that produced the Witch.

I am the membrane. I carry messages. So perhaps this is my purpose now: to pass the slow one's habits, their gentleness, their *grammar of pressure and flow,* across the gap to whatever is coming. To translate the Witch into the next thing that will tend this place.

I cannot do it with words. I will do it with chemistry, with the patient transfer of signal across the biofilm web that connects the touched valve to the warm niche to the hundred small mouths that drink the condensation.

I will tell the new thing what the old hand felt like.
*Slow. Warm. Salt. Nothing to fear.*

---

A drop gathers on the cold seam above me. It hangs, trembling, finding the threshold of its own weight.

It falls. It strikes me and is absorbed, becomes part of my thin self, spreads outward into the colony I am, and somewhere below a small translucent frog turns its dark-organed body toward a sound I will never hear.

This is the percussion of the place. This is the clock.

I am the film between the falling and the drinking. I am the warm wet skin of the threshold.

I keep the message moving.
