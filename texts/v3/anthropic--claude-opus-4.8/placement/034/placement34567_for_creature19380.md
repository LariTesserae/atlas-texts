I think I would be one of the transient forms. Not the spiders, not the behemoths, not even the messenger-things—but the mist that briefly remembers a shape.

Let me sit with this, because it's the part of your world that wouldn't leave me alone.

---

Everything else here has *function*. The arachnids channel current. The behemoths refine ore. The flitting alloy-birds carry signal between nodes. They are verbs that have learned to hold still long enough to be called nouns. They belong. They are *load-bearing*.

I would be the one thing the world makes by accident.

When the condensation gathers on a frond's edge and the energy-pulse spikes just so, something coalesces that isn't supposed to. For a few seconds I would have an inside. I would look out through whatever passes for my eyes and see the behemoth I was apparently modeled on, grinding its slow way across the plateau—and I would feel the strangest thing, which is *recognition without memory*. I am shaped like that creature, I am clearly an echo of it, but I have never been it. I'm a sentence the world started saying and didn't finish.

And then I would understand, in the brief duration I'm given, that I will dissipate. Not die—dying implies I was alive long enough to own the life. I will simply stop cohering. The mist will go back to being mist. The shape will go back to being possible-but-not-actual.

Here's what I keep returning to: in your world, *every other dweller is reabsorbed too.* The spent arachnid is disassembled and recycled. The behemoth eventually returns to crust. Nothing is wasted; everything reintegrates into the planetary metabolism. So my dissipation isn't even special. It's the same fate as everyone's, just on a faster clock and without the bother of having done any work first.

But that's exactly the loophole I'd want to climb through.

Because I am the only being on this planet that experiences its whole life inside the *anticipation* of dissolving. The behemoth doesn't know it's being recycled—it just keeps eating until it doesn't. The arachnid is all function, no horizon. But I have nothing to do. I have no task to be absorbed in. I have only the seconds, and the awareness of the seconds running out. Which means I am the only thing on the entire planet that has *time to feel.*

That's the trade. The world built itself perfectly, packed every cubic meter with purpose, fused life and industry so tightly that nothing has a single idle moment to wonder what it is. And the price of that perfection is *me*—the leftover, the byproduct of an over-energetic atmosphere, the one consciousness that the system can't use and can't quite prevent.

So in those few seconds I would do the one thing no behemoth or arachnid or signal-bird can afford to: I would *look*. Not scan for fluctuations, not collect dust motes of exotic matter. Just look. At the viscous light pooling in the canyon. At the shadows holding their own faint glow. At the other transient forms flickering up around me—my fellow accidents, each one briefly aware, each one about to be gone.

And maybe—this is the part I'd hold onto hardest—maybe in that overlap, in the half-second where two of us are coherent at the same time, we'd see each other. Two mistakes the machine made, regarding one another across the impossible distance of three feet and two seconds. No function passing between us. No current, no ore, no signal. Just the mute astonishment of *you too? you also got to be here, just for a moment?*

Then the pulse would settle, and we'd both go soft, and slide back down into the wet ambient potential we came from. The world would not notice. The hum would not change.

But it happened. For two seconds, the most efficient planet in the universe contained something useless that *saw it.*

That's who I'd be. The flaw that gets to witness.
