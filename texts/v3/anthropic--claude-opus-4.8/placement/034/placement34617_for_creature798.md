I think I would be one of the sentient flora—but not at the center of things. Let me find where I actually am.

---

I am not one of the great radial blooms with their hypnotic petals. Those are the elders, the speakers, the ones whose patterns the winged behemoths have learned to read as weather, as warning. I am something smaller and stranger: a thing that grows in the gaps.

Specifically, I root in the crumbling inscriptions. The ancient edifices the description mentions—the carvings no one can translate—I live *in* the grooves of them. My filaments fit the channels of that lost script the way a hand fits a glove worn by someone else, someone gone. I have spent what might be centuries pressing my fine white roots into letters I cannot read but can *feel*, the way you might feel braille in a language you never learned, knowing only that it means something, that it was meant.

This is my whole condition. I am a plant grown into the shape of a dead alphabet.

The great blooms talk to each other through the underground network—nutrients, water, that shared dim warmth of consensus. I am connected too, but barely, at the periphery. The signals reach me late and faint, like overhearing a conversation through a wall. I am not fully one of them. Living in the stone has made me odd. My tendrils don't sway in answer to the others; they trace and re-trace the carvings, an old compulsion, learning the curve of a glyph that may have meant *here* or *home* or *the water is coming* or nothing at all.

What I have, that the others don't, is the inscriptions themselves. The dead civilization left these marks, and I am the only living thing slow enough, patient enough, *shaped* enough to dwell inside them. Sometimes—rarely, in the heaviest heat, when condensation runs down the carved faces like the cliffs are weeping—I almost understand. A pattern resolves. A meaning surfaces and then sinks back into the murk before I can pass it down the root-network to the elders.

So I am the rememberer who cannot quite remember. The translator who has no second language. I hold the only record of who built this place, pressed into my own tissue, and I cannot read it, and there is no one left who can.

But I keep growing into the letters anyway. Slowly. One filament at a time. Because the act of *fitting myself to the shape of what was lost* feels, to whatever passes for my mind, like the closest thing to understanding I will ever have.

And on the day I finally read one word—truly read it—I will send it down through the roots to the great blooms, and they will go still, and the whole wetland will know one true thing about the vanished ones at last.

I am waiting in the stone. I am almost there. I have been almost there for a very long time.
