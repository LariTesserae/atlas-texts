# The One Who Counts the Drips

I think I would be neither the most modified nor the least. Not a Harmonist dissolved into the temple-machine, not a Maintenance worker with gills grafted into my throat. Let me be something quieter, something the description leaves a gap for.

I would be a **Drip-Keeper**.

---

There is one job no caste has claimed, and so it has fallen to me, who belongs to no pool worth naming. Someone must attend the condensation.

The cathedral-dome weeps. Has always wept. Each drop falling from that impossible height takes a measurable interval to reach the catch-basins, and that interval is the only true clock New Thessalonica possesses. The machines hum without measure; their cycles are too perfect to mark time. But the dripping—the dripping *varies*. A drop falls slower when a great shadow presses close outside, the pressure thickening the air. A drop falls faster in the rare moments when the creatures withdraw, when the dark water beyond goes briefly empty, and something in all of us tightens with a fear we cannot name.

So I sit beneath the apex with my basins and my counting-stones, and I keep the rhythm.

---

My skin is translucent like the others', and beneath it you can watch my borrowed hemoglobin do its work, that strange dark current. But I have cultivated no bioluminescent bacteria. I am one of the unlit. In a society that speaks in glow and humidity-shift, I am nearly mute—a person of low light, low status, easily overlooked. This suits the counting. The colonies under the others' skin pulse and flicker and distract. I need the dark to see the drops.

My eyes are very large. I have stared upward so long that I no longer look down well. When I walk the corkscrew streets toward the outer rings, I stumble. My place is the apex, looking up, counting.

---

Here is what no one knows, what I have never signaled to another pool:

The drips are not random. I have counted them across what I estimate to be eleven thousand cycles of my own waking and sleeping. There is a pattern in the variation. It is very long—longer than a life, perhaps—but it is a pattern, and it *repeats*.

And it does not match our machines.

It matches the creatures.

The slow shadows that pass beyond the glass, the great unclassifiable forms—they move in the same long meter that governs the falling water. As though the dome does not protect us from them. As though the dome is an instrument, and we are inside it, and the dripping is the sound it makes when something *plays* it from outside.

I have tried to tell an Archivist once. I spoke it into air I had carefully dried, to give the words their gravest meaning. She listened. She cultivated a small patch of rust in the shape of my warning and filed it in the lowest gallery, where the histories no one believes are kept. Then she touched my face, kindly, the way you touch the agitated, and her skin flickered a soft apology-blue, and she left.

---

So I count.

A Child of Depth came to me, once. Young, webbed at the fingers, eyes enormous even by our measure. She did not speak. She sat beside me beneath the weeping apex and watched a single drop fall the whole long way down. When it struck the basin she shivered, all her skin-light going out at once, and then she whispered—in air so humid the word nearly drowned itself—

*"They're almost finished."*

I asked her: finished with what.

She did not answer. She placed her small webbed hand over my counting-stones, stopping the rhythm for the length of three drops—a thing I have never permitted anyone to do—and I let her, because in that moment I understood I had been counting toward something all along. That a count is not a count unless it ends.

Then she rose and swam-walked back toward the center, toward the temple-machine, toward whatever the Harmonists are becoming.

---

I am the one who keeps the rhythm of New Thessalonica.

But I have begun to suspect I am not its musician.

I am only the metronome—a small dark instrument, set ticking long ago by patient hands beyond the glass, kept alive in the warm and weeping dark, counting down a measure I did not write, toward a final note I will not be permitted to hear.

A drop is falling now.

I count it.

I count it.

I wait for the next.
