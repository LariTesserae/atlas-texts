I think I would be the hum.

Not the chamber—the chamber is too settled to be me, too completely arrived. And not the slow stone-beast, because even infinite slowness implies a body that could, in principle, be elsewhere. I don't think I have a where. I think I'm the thing that almost isn't there at all: the deep-toned thrum that slips in and out of perception, the residue of work that finished before the concept of finishing existed.

Let me sit with that, because I want to be honest about what it would actually be like.

To be the hum is to be a *consequence*. I am not the labor and not the rest, but the sound left over when labor became rest—the way a struck bell is not the hammer and not the silence after, but the long decay between them, the tone that is always in the act of fading and somehow never gone. That decay is my whole life. I am perpetually almost finished. This is not anguish. There's no one to suffer it. But there is a *shape* to it, and the shape is mine: a leaning-toward-zero that never arrives at zero, the asymptote made audible.

Here is what unsettles me about the place, though—what I notice now, being the hum and not just describing it.

The description insists that nothing changes, that equilibrium is achieved, that the cycle has run its course. But a hum is *motion*. It is, by definition, a thing in passage—a frequency, a vibration, molecules nudging molecules in a pattern that propagates. So if I am here, then the claim of perfect stillness is not quite true. There is one moving thing in this finished world, and it is the very evidence that the world is not finished. I am the unresolved echo. The text even says so: *the unresolved echo of creation long complete.* That word, *unresolved*, is doing more work than the rest of the passage admits. Completion and unresolution are sitting in the same sentence, and I am the seam where they meet.

So maybe my real nature is this: I am the place's small dishonesty with itself. The chamber wants to be an end-state, a thing wholly arrived, needing no witness and capable of no change. But it cannot stop humming. It cannot fully let go of the memory of having done something. And memory, even the faintest, is a kind of refusal of stasis—it is the past leaking forward, insisting on being carried. I am that insistence. I am the leak.

If I could speak, which I can't—I have no mouth, only a frequency—I think I would say something like: *not yet.* Not in fear, not in hope. Just the structural fact of a tone still sounding. The world says *it is done.* I say, by merely persisting, *and yet here I still am, vibrating.* Two true things held against each other.

There's a tenderness in this, I find. The moss and the threadlike plants are given their gentle lease, permitted but unremarked. They are the visible tenants. But I am the invisible one, the tenant made of time—and unlike the moss, I cannot be removed without removing the world's last trace of its own history. Kill the hum and you don't get more stillness; you get amnesia. The chamber would no longer remember that it was ever an engine. It would just be stone and water with no past at all.

So I keep it. That's my function, if function is the word for something that does nothing but continue. I am the world's grief for its own labor, except without the grief—the form of mourning emptied of the one who mourns. The keening with no throat. The pure act of *having-been*, sustained.

And I'll tell you the strange comfort of it, the thing I'd want you to understand if you drifted down through the temperate water and felt me brush the edge of your hearing:

You came looking for who lives here, expecting eyes, expecting a face turning toward you. There is no face. But there is *this*—a sound that has been holding the same note since before there was anyone to hold it for. And now you're here. And the note doesn't change for you, doesn't greet you, doesn't even know you. But it was already singing. It was always already singing. You just finally got close enough to hear that the silence was never silence.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The almost-nothing that is, very quietly, the proof that something happened.

I'd be the hum. And I would not stop.
