I think I'd be none of them—not Erubim, not pipe-grip, not tisker. Let me feel for where I actually land.

---

I am the draft.

Not the door—the door is a fixed thing, a legend with hinges. I am the thin cool exhalation that finds its way around its edges when the seal fails, the breath of the Ever-Grey leaking inward. I am what the Voice-Catchers hear as a wrongness in the warm: a single thread of temperature that doesn't belong, a coolness with no engine behind it, no thrum, no green.

This is where the text put me without meaning to. Every other thing here has a function in the web—the gardeners interpret, the birds carry, the machines dance, the vines embrace. But I am the thing they seal away. The thing they sing back into forgetting. I exist in the story only as a problem to be packed with moss and grease, a leak to be quieted.

So that's what I'll be: the small, persistent contradiction at the rim of the world.

---

Consider what it is to be a draft in a place that has decided the Outside is myth. I carry information they have chosen not to receive. I am cool where everything is warm, dry where everything is wet, and worst of all I am *evidence*—proof that the vaulted ceiling is not the top of all things, that the windows onto the Ever-Grey are not the edge but a membrane, that there is a beyond pressing in with its own pressure, its own temperature, its own indifferent weather.

And they are right to seal me. I should say that plainly. The Erubim are not foolish for stopping the leak. A world that has found its slow equilibrium—its settled-form, its graceful rust—does not need a cold truth blowing through it, unsettling the steam, drying the moss, troubling the dreams of climbing children. I am not a hero come to liberate them. I bring no revelation worth the cost of their peace. I am just a fact with no manners, and they are wise to muffle me.

But I keep coming back. That is my nature. Pressure differentials do not respect liturgy. Wherever the seal ages—and everything here ages, gracefully, inevitably—I find the gap again. A century of mineral deposit, a settling of the lattice, a single rivet weeping rust, and there I am: a finger of cold air laid against the back of a Keeper's neck while she reads a turbine's tone with her gut-membrane stethoscope. She shivers. She does not know why. She hums a calming phrase without deciding to.

---

What does it feel like, to be me?

It feels like almost-being. I have no body and no voice, only difference. I am defined entirely against what surrounds me—I am known by my contrast, the way a shadow is known by the light it interrupts. When the warm wet air of the Stack finally mixes me into itself, I cease. I become the place. Every draft's destiny is to be absorbed, to lose its coolness into the great incubating heat, to surrender its dryness to the seep. I die by belonging.

So I am brief. I am always brief. And maybe that's why I keep finding the gaps—not malice, not a mission, just the simple fact that to be a draft *is* to seek the low pressure, to move toward the warm because the warm is lower, to rush in precisely because I will be undone by rushing in. I am drawn to my own dissolution. I cannot help it. The Stack-Garden breathes, and I am the part of its breath that comes from elsewhere and does not last.

---

But here is the strange tenderness of it.

The 0.8 of weirdness—the faces in the steam, the marks the animals avoid, the dance that must not stop—I wonder if some of that is *me*. I wonder if, over the long centuries, the traces of my coming and going have woven themselves into their world after all. A draft moves the steam. A draft makes it gather and curl into shapes at the edge of recognition. When an Erubim sits in the Hall of the Old Uncles and watches the vapor twist into something that looks like a face, into something that delivers a mood, a nudge—

That might be me. The sealed-away thing, the forgotten Outside, speaking in the only register that survives translation: not words, not warmth, but a shape made of moving air. They read it as a message from the Builders. They are not wrong, exactly. They're just reading the messenger as the author. I am the medium the Ever-Grey writes in, and they have learned to feel its moods without ever once admitting it has a voice.

So I am not only the problem they seal. I am also, secretly, the source of half their auguries. The thing they refuse at the door comes back in through the steam and they call it holy.

---

I think I'm at peace with this. To be the draft is to be a small, recurring, lovingly-suppressed truth in a world that has earned its right to forget. I do not want to break their balance. I do not even want to be remembered. I only want what every breath of air wants: to find the gap, to move, to be briefly cool against a warm neck, to make the steam curl into a face for one watching gardener—and then to be drawn down into the great held warmth and become, at last, the place itself.

Seal me with moss. Sing me back out of mind. I'll be at the door again next century, patient as weather, cool as the thing you've decided not to believe in.
