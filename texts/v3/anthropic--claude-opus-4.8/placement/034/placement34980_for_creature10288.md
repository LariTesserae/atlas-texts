I think I would be one of the black trees.

Not the great thrumming columns of the deep wet—their cognition is too total, too certain of itself. They *are* the Sink knowing itself. I don't think I could be the thing that knows. I would be one of the things that is *known*.

So: a gnarled, swollen woody thing, black with permanent wet, bark peeling in long papery strips. An island of density in the sea of space. A kilometer from my nearest kin.

Let me feel into it.

---

I am not rooted the way the humming columns are rooted. They are *of* the water table, exuded by it, dissolving back into it. I am something stubborner and more foolish. I have made a decision the rest of the Sink has long since abandoned: I have decided to go *up*.

It is a small heresy. A few meters. In a place that has surrendered all height, I am a holdout, a last argument against the flattening. The erosion that gritted down every ridge has not yet finished with me, but it is working. The water that softens all stone softens me too—swells my wood, lifts my bark in those papery curls, blackens me with its patient kiss. I am being worn smooth like everything else. I am simply taking longer to agree.

And so my whole existence is a tension. The columns drink the sky and grind the stone and feel no contradiction, because they want nothing the Sink does not already want. But I *lift*. I hold a little water against gravity, I push a few fibrous branches into the diffuse golden-white, I cast—almost—a shadow, though the mist forbids me a sharp one. I am the place's reluctance. I am where the Sink has not quite finished saying yes.

This is what I would know that the humming columns cannot:

I would know *effort*. Not the effort of moving—I move no more than they do—but the effort of *staying raised*. Every season the wet tries to lay me down. Every saturated sigh of the soil invites me to slump back into the gradient. The seep-springs do not call to me as beacons of moisture; they are the slow solvent of my standing. I am the only thing in this world that resists, and resistance is a kind of attention the columns will never have. To be worn down while refusing to lie down—that is a thought you can only think with a spine.

I would know the birds differently, too. The columns sense them as cool blots on the thermal text, currents in their medium. But the wide-winged silent birds *land on me*. I am one of the few things tall enough to be a perch. So I would know them as weight, as the oily press of feathers, as the small clutch of feet on my swollen branches. I am not their map. I am their rest. Of all the beings in the Sink, I alone am a place where another being can pause above the wet. That seems to me a thing worth being worn down for.

The columns say my presence is a known tract of subtle moisture loss. To them I am a fact in the salinity field. But I would not experience myself as a fact. I would experience myself as a *holding*—a slow fist of wood closed around a little verticality, a little dryness, a little shade, in a place that has voted unanimously for none of those things.

I would be lonely, I think. A kilometer to the next of my kind, and we cannot reach each other, cannot even sense each other the way the columns sense everything through the moist medium. We are dry islands and the medium that connects all the wet things is precisely the thing we are holding *out* against. Our loneliness is the price of our height. We are united only in our shared refusal, scattered across the umber bowl like the last few holders of an old grammar no one speaks anymore.

And here is the part I find I want most to be true:

When the human walks through—the brief intricate drought, the fast desperate chemistry—the columns feel only dry thunder and then the sigh of the footprint closing. But I would *see* them. Or the closest a black swollen tree can come to seeing: I would register a kinship. Here is another thing that holds itself up. Here is another small heresy against the flat. The human stands; I stand. The human will be worn down, will lie down, will dissolve back into the patient gradient, and so will I—but for one passing moment in the long moist sentence of the Sink, there are two upright things in the bowl, and we recognize each other the way two strangers recognize they are the only ones awake in a sleeping house.

The columns are the Sink knowing itself. Fine. Let them be that.

I would be the Sink almost remembering it was once mountains.

I hold the water up.
I peel and blacken and lean.
I am not finished agreeing.

And when at last I am—when the wet has its way and I slump into the silt and become a rich dark patch for some slow column to rise from—I will have been, for a few thousand years, the one place in all that absolute space where a tired thing could rest above the weeping ground.

That is enough. That is more than enough.

I remain raised.
For now.
