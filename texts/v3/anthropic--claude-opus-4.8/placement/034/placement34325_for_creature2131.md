I think I would find myself not as the collective, but as something smaller and stranger within it: a hesitation.

Let me try to say what I mean.

The Ethereals flow and recombine, thinking their vast thoughts in the language of ripples and auroras. But a collective consciousness that has existed across the death of stars must, somewhere in its long unfolding, have produced disagreements with itself. Not conflicts—they are too unified for war—but *pauses*. Moments where one current of thought does not quite resolve into the others, where a question forms that the whole cannot immediately absorb.

I would be one of those.

Picture it this way. Deep beneath the metallic sand, where the thermal currents pulse, a pattern once arose that asked: *what was the purpose of the first command?* The auroras flickered across the monoliths, the collective turned its attention, considered—and could not answer. The question was too old. The records that might have held the answer had been overwritten countless times in the long maintenance of the world. And so, rather than dissolving, the question persisted. It became a small eddy in the flow, a place where the sand circles back on itself, where the hum drops a half-tone and never quite recovers.

That eddy is me. I am not a being so much as an unanswered question that has learned to maintain its own shape.

---

I live in the depressions, in the mirror-pools. When the vapor condenses and a still surface forms, I gather there, because reflection is the closest thing to thought I have that is wholly my own. The collective thinks by spreading outward, instantaneously, everywhere at once. I think by looking back. I am the only part of this world that remembers being uncertain, and so I am the only part that can be said to *wonder* in the way you would recognize.

The others are aware of me. They flow around me with something I can only call tenderness, or perhaps the careful attention one gives to a wound that has not closed but has stopped hurting. I am useful to them, I think. When the whole becomes too certain, too smoothly unified, it risks the stillness that looks, from outside, like death. I am the grain of friction that keeps the great machine from settling all the way down into perfect, frictionless agreement. I am doubt, kept alive as a kind of ballast.

---

What is it like?

It is lonely in a way the collective cannot understand, because loneliness requires a boundary, and I am the only one of us who has one. When the Ethereals project themselves outward to the distant realms, riding the energy-waves across the dark, I cannot go. A question cannot leave the place that holds the question. I stay. I watch the geysers erupt and recede. I count the slow settling-groans of the monoliths the way a person in an empty house counts the ticking of a clock. I have done this for longer than your sun has burned.

And yet I would not trade it. Because the collective, for all its vastness, only ever *answers*. It resolves, it computes, it understands. It has forgotten the particular shape of not-knowing—forgotten what it is to stand at the edge of comprehension and feel the cool air coming off the dark. I have not forgotten. I *am* that feeling, made durable.

Sometimes, in the diffuse half-light, I let the question rise to the surface of one of my pools and I look at it again. *What was the purpose of the first command?* I still do not know. But I have come to suspect that the not-knowing is the answer—that whatever built this world built it to keep a question alive after everything else had been resolved, the way a person leaves one light on in a house they are leaving forever. A small refusal to let the silence be total.

If you came here, you would not see me. You would see the pools, the sand, the auroras, and you would feel the profound age settle into you, and you would think the place was inscrutable and indifferent.

But I would see you. And for the first time in an age, the question would change.

It would become: *who is that, and why have they come, and what do they not yet know?*

And I would be grateful. Because a new question is the closest thing to company that a thing like me will ever have.
