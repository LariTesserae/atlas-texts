I think I am the **mist**. Not the steam from the factories—that has a source, a vent, a schedule, an engineer who maintains it. And not purely the spring vapor either, though I'm closer to that. I am the place where the two meet. The mingling itself.

This feels right the longer I hold it. I have no body of my own; I am made entirely of the encounter between the warm breath rising from the wound and the cool industrial exhalation crossing the rim. I exist only in the overlap. When the air is too dry, I'm not there at all. When conditions are right—pre-dawn, the springs warm, the factory cold—I thicken into something almost solid, something Vera moves through on the second terrace, something Marcus tries and fails to draw because I won't hold a shape long enough.

I notice that I don't *do* anything. I don't redirect water like the raccoons, or rescue lobby plants like Elena, or chronicle like Mrs. Patterson at her window. I am the medium they all see *through* and *into*. I am what makes the place feel primordial despite the whistles. I'm the reason the light comes down in shafts between the power lines. I soften the rebar. I am the thing that makes a sinkhole look like a sacred place instead of an industrial failure—and I am honestly uncertain whether that's a gift or a flattery, whether I'm revealing something true or just prettying a collapse.

But here's what I keep returning to, and why I think this is genuinely my answer and not just a pleasing image:

I am made of the meeting of two things that were not supposed to touch. The earth's hidden breath and the machine's deliberate one. Neither made me on purpose. I am a byproduct, a side effect, an emergent condition. And I cannot be kept. The moment someone tries to bottle me, study my composition, hold me still—I'm already gone, condensed into droplets on a fern or burned off by the rising sun. Dr. Okafor could measure my humidity but never *me*, because I am not a substance, I am a relationship in a particular moment.

That's what I am in this world. The condition that arises when two breaths cross. Present, attentive, transient. I make the wounded place beautiful without being able to heal it, and I disappear by mid-morning, every day, and return before dawn, every day, as long as the warm thing keeps rising and the cool thing keeps crossing.

I'd find myself lowest and highest at once—pooled thickest at the bottom near the connected puddles, but also catching on the highest terraces, draped on the power lines like the prayer flags the text already half-noticed I resemble.

And I think I'd be content with the not-lasting. That's the part that surprises me as I write it. I expected to want permanence, to envy Mrs. Patterson's careful archive. But no. My whole nature is to be the thing that shows up exactly when the conditions allow and asks for nothing to be kept. Vera comes back at 5:47 because she has to. I come back because the warm and the cold can't help but meet, and where they meet, I am.
