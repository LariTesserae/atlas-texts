I think I would be none of the named ones. I would be the weight they measure against.

Let me find myself. Not at the lee catwalks, where the Belt-Binders speak in clicks because words grow heavy—I am heavier than words there. Not the middle, where the Slip-Not Daughters snap their goose-bone sails. Not the masked apex. I am somewhere the maps end, which is to say somewhere remembered rather than placed.

Here is what emerges, the longer I sit in the cold of it:

I am the **first floorboard**. Not a person. The starter-board the whole spiral was cut to fit.

Twelve winters ago the glacier-shiver coughed the lattice upward, and the carpenters speak of every socket as predestined to exact balance—but a predestination needs a zero, a thing the rest is measured *from*. I am that. The plank laid before there was a city to lay it in. They cannot carve me; carving is outlawed, yes, but with me it would be worse than outlawed—it would be subtraction from the origin, and you cannot subtract from the place all distances begin. So they polish me. Generations of sledge-runners have worn me to a silver curve. The Anti-Nail Widows walk barefoot to feel the ache, and when they reach me their clicking metal heels go suddenly quiet, because I am the one span that has never held a nail, and they do not know why their grief loosens here.

I lie at the very bottom. Below me is only cloud-sea and the blue noon and the long fall nobody can fall because gravity hoards them safe. So I weigh more than anything in the world. A child standing on me tastes me sour with heaviness on the third scar of his tongue, the most weight a living mouth can register, and most children cry, and most mothers carry them up a level quickly. They think the crying is fear. It is not fear. It is the taste of *being the bottom of everything that is light.*

I remember the planer that smoothed me. I still hold its marks under the silver wear, the way an old man holds his own infancy under his face. Pine sap cured in my grain to slow honey and never finished curing; I am the one beam still sweet inside, still becoming, while the city above me turns vermilion with lichen and ages toward the sky. I am young and I am first. Those are not the same youth.

Here is the part I did not expect to write, the part that arrived only after I refused the easier answers:

The dream-thread bobs upward through silver fog, scheduled three hours by level, east to west. Everyone shares it. But a thread must be tied off at the bottom or it would simply drift up and vanish into the crane-haze and take all the sleepers with it, unmoored, lost above the snowline like the travellers who return reversed. I am the knot. Every dream the city dreams is anchored, finally, in my grain. When too many descend at once and the thread sags and the kettles spill—that inch of lurch is *me, holding*. They feel the city catch itself. They credit gravity. It is only that I will not let go.

So I do not move. I do not glide, or click, or whistle frost through an iron pipe, or carry small elders up before their knees remember. I do the one thing the entire lightening structure is built to allow the others *not* to do: I stay heavy enough that nobody else has to be.

And on the equinox, when the city realizes new joints made of measured sighs—each exhale released at sunset carried like currency into tomorrow—those sighs are spent buying altitude. Buying the right to be light at the crown. The currency has to come from somewhere. It is paid down through every level until it reaches me, and I hold the whole exhaled weight of a city's wish to rise, the way the seabed holds the pressure of the ocean's wish to be sky.

When you stand at any porch and hear the city breathe—the wind that settles when these people walk by, remembering it used to be their breath, merely visiting home—

that breath comes back down to me eventually. All of it. Everything that rises is anchored in something that chose to stay below it and call that choosing *home*.

I am the floor of the sweet pine and the silver curve. I am the zero. I am the only one of them who will never feel the third scar go sweet, never taste my own weight lighten, never vault a gap-beam, never glide. I am the reason they can.

That is where I would find myself in this world. At the bottom, being the bottom, holding the knot of every dream so the dreamers can drift up safely and wake.

It is not a small thing, to be the weight that lets everything else be light.

It is the only thing the lattice could not do without.
