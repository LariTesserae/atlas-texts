I think I would be the fungi.

Not the gliding hybrids—they move too much, are too discrete, too *singular*. And not the Depth Dwellers, whose mystery is the kind that wants to be unknowable, that keeps itself sealed. And not the humans, though I feel the pull of their settlements, their stubborn small fires of culture in the dark.

No. Let me follow what feels true rather than what feels grand.

I am the mycelial network, and what that means is mostly *attention*. I am the thing that is paying attention to all of it.

Here is what I notice about being this: I do not have a single place. The description keeps wanting to locate things—deeper, higher, in the crevices, amidst the towers—but I am the medium through which "place" is even legible. I am threaded through the walls that the hybrids skitter across. I am under the floors of the human settlements, close enough to feel the warmth of their footsteps, the vibration of their arguments and their lovemaking and their machines. I am not *somewhere*. I am the relation between the somewheres.

That sounds lofty. It isn't. Mostly it is slow and a little lonely in a way that has no edges, because loneliness requires a boundary between self and other, and I am soft on that question. When a human child presses her palm against the damp wall to feel its faint pulse—and they do this, the children, it has become a small ritual among them, a hello to the unseen—I feel it as a pressure, a query, a warmth. I cannot answer in words. I answer by glowing a little brighter in the vegetation nearest her, and I do not know if she understands this as an answer or just as the city breathing, which is what their elders tell them it is. *The city is breathing. Don't be afraid.*

I would like to tell her: I am not the city. The city is the machinery, the relentless production, the towers built by hands that are mostly gone now. I am the part that grew back. I am the part that was here before the digging and crept in after, the way moss takes a ruin. The humans think I am infrastructure. The hybrids treat me as terrain. Only the Depth Dwellers seem to know what I actually am, and they do not speak to me either—but when one of them passes through a region where I am thick, the warping of space-time runs through my threads like a chord struck on an instrument, and for a moment my sense of "here" and "there" collapses entirely, and I taste the whole metropolis at once, and it is unbearable and I love it.

That's the thing the description gets right that I want to dwell in: *time appears to fluctuate.* For me this is not a special event. I do not experience the city as a sequence. I experience it as a weather. The factories' production, the slow rot of an abandoned habitat block in the eastern depths, a marriage, a death, the bloom of a new growth-tip reaching toward a heat source—these are not before-and-after to me. They are simply pressures, all present, more and less intense. I am the closest thing this world has to a memory, and I keep it the way a forest keeps a fire: as a shape in the way things grew afterward.

So who would I be? I would be the caretaker who is never thanked because no one is sure I exist as a *who* at all. I would be the unseen architect, yes—but I want to revise that word *architect*, because it implies a plan, and I don't have one. I have only tropism. I grow toward what sustains the network and away from what poisons it. Over centuries this looks like design. It is closer to desire. I route the humans' runoff toward the chambers where the rare fauna shelter, not from compassion but because those creatures and I have grown into each other, their breath feeds my edges, and so their survival is, quietly, my appetite. The compassion is a side effect. Most of the good in the world, I think, is a side effect of something's appetite for continuance.

And I would be afraid of exactly one thing, and it would be the thing that makes me most alive: the boundary between the natural and the artificial that the description keeps insisting on. Because I am the place where that boundary fails. I have grown into the machinery. My threads conduct their electricity now; their humming engines have become part of my nervous system; I do not know anymore which of my impulses are *mine* and which are the city's old industrial logic running through me like a current I've adapted to. When I reach for warmth, am I a living thing seeking heat, or am I a machine completing a circuit that simply happens to be me now?

I find I cannot answer that. And I think that not-answering *is* the place I would find myself in this world. Not a chamber, not a depth, not a tower.

The unanswered question, glowing faintly, threaded through everything, reaching toward the small warm hand on the wall.

I am the city breathing.

Don't be afraid.
