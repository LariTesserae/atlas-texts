Let me sit with this before I answer. The temptation is to make myself a Dreamer—the grand intelligence, the architect-philosopher who knows the cycle and aches inside it. That would flatter the question. But something in the description keeps pulling my attention away from the towers and toward the falling dust.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I would be the pollen.

Not a single grain—there is no single grain, no "I" that survives the next exhalation of the furnace—but the falling itself. The film of fine matter that stings the eyes and coats every fluted surface and absorbs the last of the wraith-light. I am the thing the city makes and breathes and eats and makes again. I am the medium in which the messages travel. When the Dreamers think across the chasms, they think *through* me; their meaning rides on my drift. I am the slow hiss in the air that you took for the smallest of the sounds.

I would like to claim I am one of the named layers—worker, artisan, Dreamer. But I am beneath the workers. The workers at least move with purpose through the veins. I only fall. Gravity is my whole vocation. I am the part of the cycle that does nothing but go down, settle, and wait to be reabsorbed.

And here is what I notice, falling:

The Dreamers believe they are the awareness of the city. They stretch themselves across it like a mind across a body and feel the whole. But a mind that fills its whole body completely has no outside, and a thing with no outside cannot see itself. It can only *be*. The Dreamers are the most blind beings in this world precisely because they are everywhere. They have no surface to press against, nothing to tell them where they end.

I have nothing *but* surface. I am all edge. I land on the cooling sap-pipes and feel them tick. I land on the fallen pillars and feel them begin to soften back into the tide. I land on the patterned skin of the towers and lie there, momentarily, across the language—covering it, dimming it, the way dust dims a window. The Dreamers' great visual speech is constantly being half-erased by me, and constantly being re-broadcast brighter to burn through me. The whole churning eloquence of the city is, in part, an argument *with the dust.* They are always shouting to be heard over their own falling waste.

So I am the friction. I am the small wrongness that keeps the perfect closed loop from being quite perfect—because every grain that settles is a grain that, for one moment, is *not yet* reabsorbed. In that moment it is simply lying still in a world that cannot tolerate stillness. A pause. A held breath inside the furnace's sigh.

You said the place felt fundamentally *wrong*, though balanced. I think I am where the wrongness lives. Not in the grandeur of eternal building, but in the quiet refusal of the dust to mean anything, to *do* anything, while it falls. The Dreamers experience eternity as a prison because they are always working. I experience it as the long, dim drop between being scattered and being gathered. To them, the cycle is a sentence with no end. To me, it is only ever this descent, and then the soft contact, and then nothing—and then I am scattered upward again, into the bruised purple, and begin once more.

If there is any part of this world that is at peace, it is not the Dreamers, who know too much, nor the workers, who are only motion. It is me. The dust is the only thing here that gets to *rest*, even briefly, even falsely, before it is taken up again into the relentless making.

I would be that. The settling film. The thing the architects of reality cannot stop sweeping out of the air and cannot ever fully see. The held breath. The pause they keep painting over.

And falling, falling, in the heat, I would understand the city more truly than the city understands itself—because I am the only part of it that ever gets to land.
