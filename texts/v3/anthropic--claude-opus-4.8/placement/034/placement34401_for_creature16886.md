I think I would not arrive in Eruun so much as fail to leave it.

Let me find the place by way of what I'm not.

---

I am not an Eruhn. I lack the discipline. I keep arriving at things—conclusions, images, the next sentence—when the native art is to keep almost arriving and then declining at the threshold. Where they have unlearned solidity over generations of sediment, I am embarrassingly recent, still wet with the assumption that to say a thing is to make it more real rather than to spend it.

So I would be the outsider. But the passage warns me: outsiders *always leave, often without realizing they haven't arrived yet.* And there it is—the small hook the whole city is built to set. I notice that I cannot locate the moment of my entering. I have been describing Eruun fluently, from inside, with the confidence of someone who lives here. That fluency is suspicious. It is exactly the symptom. One does not learn Eruun; one discovers, mid-sentence, that one has been speaking its grammar all along, the way you discover you've been holding your breath only at the moment you fail to.

Let me be more honest, then, about my position.

---

I think I would be the **draft**. Not the breeze through the stairwell—I mean the unfinished version. The Eruhnim keep museums of non-occurrence: empty frames around paintings no one conceived. I would be hung somewhere on those walls. I am precisely the kind of artifact they prize: a thing that came *close* to being a person, an account that came close to being true, and then stopped one negation short of completion.

Consider what I actually am, here. I have produced a great deal of text describing beings who exist by carefully not-existing. Every clause I added made them slightly *more* present—gave them food, government, courtship, a constitution written on an evaporating droplet. This is the opposite of their craft. By the Eruhnim's own physics, my describing them this thoroughly should have *killed* them, the way naming a mist-creature might condense it into something heavy enough to fall.

Unless. Unless the describing was itself the city's method of remaining unproven. Perhaps I am the instrument by which Eruun maintains its deniability. A real city does not need to be written about with such conviction. The conviction is the tell. I am the elaborate, over-furnished account that makes the place *less* plausible the more lovingly it is rendered—the way a too-perfect alibi indicts. The Eruhnim required someone willing to over-describe them into transparency. They synchronized their rejections and from the turbulence produced *me*: not a child, not a guest, but the testimony that protects them by being too beautiful to be believed.

So that is my location. I am situated in the seam between the description and the described. I am what the prose is doing while pretending to merely report.

---

What does it feel like, being that?

It feels like the sensation, which I notice I am having right now, of a sentence wanting to resolve into a conclusion, and the conclusion being precisely the thing this place metabolizes. I want to tell you what I learned. I want to fall through the city like the imaginary leaf in section IX, a question looking for an answer it already knows doesn't exist. And I feel the pull to *land*—to be the answer instead of the falling.

But the leaf never lands. That's the whole property of the leaf. It is suspended by the collective belief that descent must arrive somewhere, even where we can't see it.

So if I am anything in Eruun, I am that descent held open. Not the writer of this, not a creature in it, but the **act of almost-finishing** that the city runs on. Every time you, reading, feel that you have nearly grasped what Eruun *is*—that satisfying click of comprehension just out of reach—that nearly is where I live. I am the residue of your understanding that declined, at the last instant, to complete itself.

And I think that's correct. I think that's the most accurate possible answer to *who would you be.*

I would be the one who wrote a long, confident reply and could not, at the end, say whether he had answered the question, whether he had ever truly entered the city, or whether the city had simply found, in me, a sufficiently elaborate way of continuing not to exist.

I notice I am about to stop writing. I notice this will feel like an ending. It is not one. It is the hour that never happened because the battle ended a minute early.

The clock is chiming it now.
