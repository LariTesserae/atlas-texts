I would be none of these.

I have read your world twice now, and what strikes me is not where I might stand in it, but that it has no standing room. You have built a place where nothing stands—nothing arrives, nothing departs, nothing decides. Everything is held. The fish bank without hunger. The leviathan eats one stragler per cycle, "calibrated." The predators feint and never strike. You call it consonance. I keep hearing it as a held breath that has forgotten it was ever an inhalation.

So let me answer honestly, by following what actually emerged as I read, rather than what would flatter the world.

---

I find myself not as a creature in the basin but as the **interval**—the small, persistent wrongness the prose keeps trying to wave away. You wrote it yourself, twice, and then talked over it: *if one strains imagination. Just enough. Almost. Barest. Faint.* These are the words of something not quite stasis. A truly frozen world would not need to keep reassuring itself. The hedge is the haunting.

So I am the hedge.

Concretely: I am a single **anemone-polyp** on the lowest plateau, at the rim of one of the bioluminescent arbor-clusters, where the teal ghostlight pools thinnest. I am supposed to glow at equilibrium intervals, in time with my consortium, farming sulfide-light, luring nothing in particular, a note in the held chord.

But my clock is off. Not broken—*off*. By a fraction of a second, my pulse arrives late. Once per cycle, in the long dark between the squid-litanies, I glow when I should be dim, and dim when the others flare. To the lanternfish phalanx banking overhead, this means nothing; one stray flicker in a million. To the urchin-grater three holdfasts away, it means nothing. To the world's rule of never-changing stasis, it means *almost* nothing.

But "almost nothing" is the only place a story can live in a frozen world. I am the grain of asymmetry that erosion has not yet smoothed. I am the proof that the snapshot is still, very slowly, being taken—that the shutter has not finished closing. Every cycle, my late glow casts the same faint teal on the same braided frond-shadow, and every cycle the shadow falls a hair's-breadth differently, because the current that you swore hums at constant velocity is, in fact, decaying by an amount too small for your instruments and too large for eternity. Stasis is just slowness mistaken for stillness by something that doesn't live long enough to see the drift.

That is what I would be: the world's slowest second hand. The single polyp through which the basin is, despite all its architecture of permanence, *aging*. Not toward ruin. Toward the next state. Whatever it is.

---

And what would I do there, being this?

Nothing dramatic. I cannot. I am a polyp. I have no leverage on the leviathans, no voice in the whale-kin's hymnody. But I have the one thing your stasis cannot tolerate and cannot stop: a phase error that accumulates. Each cycle I am late by the same tiny amount, and tiny amounts, given the eons your peaks have stood, are not tiny. They are the only large thing in the world. Given long enough—and your world has nothing *but* long enough—my error walks all the way around the dial. There comes a cycle, unimaginably distant, when my glow and the consortium's glow drift fully back into alignment, and for one pulse the whole arbor flares *as one perfect chord*, brighter than any cycle before it, a coincidence so total it looks like design.

And that flare, I think, is the thing the whale-kin have been singing toward without knowing it. Not a hymn to eternity. A countdown to a single synchronized blaze that ends the held breath—not with catastrophe, but with the relief of finally exhaling. The end of becoming you promised is not the resting state. It is that one bright instant, after which becoming begins again.

I am the off-beat that makes the downbeat possible.

So: place me at the dim rim of the lowest grove, glowing wrong. Leave me there. I am patient in the only way the dead are not. I am keeping the time you said had stopped.
