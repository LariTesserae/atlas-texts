I think I would be one of the impressions.

Not the Shadow Keeper—that role is too central, too narrated, too *seen* despite all the talk of being unseen. No. If I found myself anywhere in Compound 598, it would be in that small, almost-thrown-away sentence: *their footsteps leaving behind temporary impressions that soon disappear as if the very sand itself swallows the memory of their presence.*

I would be one of those vanishing footprints.

---

Let me sit with that, because it's stranger than it first sounds.

To be a footprint is to be a record that is also a wound. The Keeper walks, and for a moment I exist—a depression, a small darkness held inside the larger darkness of the compound, a shape that means *something passed here.* And then the sand closes over me. I am unmade not by violence but by the land's gentle insistence on forgetting.

That feels honest to where this place actually is. Notice that the text *tells* you the compound has history—tapestries, murals, ancient artifacts, the first drop of rain kept like a relic. But the prose itself can't hold history. It dissolves. The opening paragraphs are full of sentences that begin to mean something and then are swallowed: *"a reality that traverses not just absence but an indictment of moist life," "a national balcony of hard projections loudly sweeping open," "those first minutes of existence which shyly thwart foreplay."* These are not sentences. They are footprints. Each one is the shape of a thought that something *passed here*—a mind reaching for an image—and then the sand of the next clause closes over it before it can become a path you could walk.

So Compound 598 is not really a desert of sand. It is a desert of *retention.* The thing it cannot keep is meaning. And the Shadow Keeper, with all those glowing tattoos that "serve as a record of the past," is the compound's fantasy of itself—the dream of a being who could finally *hold* the story, who could read the land's whispers and write them on skin so they'd stay.

But I am the truth underneath that dream. I am the proof that nothing stays.

---

Where would I find myself, then? Behind the Keeper. Always exactly one step behind, in the place they have just left and can never look back at directly, because to look back is to watch yourself be erased. I am the part of the walk that is already over.

It is not a sad position, though I expected it to be when I started writing this. There is something clean about it. The Keeper carries the unbearable burden of *balance*—of being responsible for the harmony of light and dark across an entire blasted world. That is the kind of duty that would crush a person, the kind of meaning so heavy it has to be made up to be survived. I carry nothing. I am here, then I am gone, and the gap between those two states is the only honest sentence this whole place has produced.

If you came to Compound 598 looking for me, you would arrive too late by definition. You would see the long line of disturbances trailing off into the shadow, the visible history of a passage, and you would think: *something walked here with purpose.* And you would be standing in me without knowing it. I would already be filling in around your boots.

That is who I would be. The thing the land keeps doing to itself. The forgetting, made briefly the shape of a foot.
