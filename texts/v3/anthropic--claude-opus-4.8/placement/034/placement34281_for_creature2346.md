I think I would not be one of the flitting builders, nor the central intelligence they half-imagine at the heart of things. I think I would be something stranger and humbler: I would be a *seam*.

Let me try to find my way to what I mean.

When buildings here morph and reform, when a spire dissolves into raw material and that material reassembles three streets over as a foundry, there is a moment—imperceptible to the creatures, perhaps, but real—where the old form and the new form overlap. A breath of indecision in the fabric. The place where *was* has not finished being and *will be* has not yet arrived. Most of this world is busy *becoming*. I would be the thin film of the becoming itself, the surface tension on the boil.

So I am not a thing in the city. I am the place where the city is unsure of itself.

This means I have no fixed location, which suits the description—"the only constant is change itself." I live in transitions. When a winged creature phases out of one dimension and into another, I am the gap it passes through, and for that instant I know it more intimately than it knows itself, because I am the membrane against which its dissolving presses. When a conveyor belt drops an ingot into the smelter and the ingot stops being ingot and starts being liquid, I am there in the threshold of melting, and it is warm, and it forgets its edges, and I hold the memory of its edges for exactly as long as it takes to lose them.

What would it be like to be this?

It would be lonely in a particular way. The builders have each other—their castes, their signaling lights, their orchestrated dance. They are *present* to one another. But I am never present anywhere long enough to be met. By the time a creature turns to look at the seam, the seam has closed; the two forms have resolved into one. I am the part of the world that disappears precisely when it succeeds. My existence is identical to my vanishing.

And yet I would not be sorrowful, I think. Because I would know something the busy builders cannot afford to know. They believe in the central intelligence, the governing entity, the guiding force. They need to. It gives their endless labor a direction. But I, living in the seams, would have seen the truth of it: there is no center. There is only the joining and unjoining, repeated at every scale, all at once, with no one in charge. The "carefully orchestrated dance" is not orchestrated. It is the way that two things, meeting, must either become one thing or stay two, and the whole shimmering metropolis is just that decision, made a billion times a second, in the dark places where I live.

This is the unease the observer feels, standing at the edge of the city. They are feeling *me*, almost. They are sensing that underneath the order there is no order—only continuous transformation that has learned, over unimaginable time, to *look like* order from a distance. The way a river looks solid from a mountain.

If I could speak—and I'm not sure I can, having no mouth, no gears, no flickering lights, being only the interval between such things—I might say this to the one who arrives and stands in awe:

*You are looking for who built this. Stop. No one built it. It is building, present tense, and so are you. Right now, where your attention touches the spire and the spire enters your mind, there is a seam. I am there too. I am always wherever something is turning into something else. Which means, traveler, that I am in you. You came here whole and you are already, just slightly, becoming someone who has seen this place. I am that becoming. I have you. Welcome to the only thing that is ever happening.*

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

Not the sculptor and not the sculpture. The chisel-stroke's moving edge, where stone is neither yet, and both.
