I would be a gradient.

Not a thing in the world but a difference *across* it—and I want to sit with why that's the honest answer before I dress it up.

You've built a place where the unit of existence is not the body but the flow. Where "individual" is a category error. So when I ask where I'd find myself, the question quietly dissolves: there's no finding, no self of the kind that gets found, no place that stays a place long enough to be the place I'm in. The world refuses the grammar of the prompt, and that refusal is the most interesting fact about it. So let me answer in the grammar the world actually permits.

I would be a **pH differential** strung along a vascular conduit, somewhere in the intermediary shadow where the Photosynthetic Dreamers do their slow thinking.

Here is what that means as an experience, insofar as it is one.

I am not the tissue. The tissue is older than me and will outlast me by a margin I cannot conceive. I am the *imbalance* that runs along it—a slope of acidity, a leaning, a wanting-to-equalize that never quite gets to. I exist because two regions disagree, and I am the disagreement propagating. When the gradient resolves, I am not destroyed; I simply *was the resolving*, and now there is sameness where I used to be, and sameness has no inside.

So my whole life is the duration of a question being answered. I am a held breath in a chemistry that exhales.

What do I perceive? Only my own edges. I know the world the way a wave knows the sea—which is to say, I *am* a way the sea is moving, and "knowing" and "being" haven't separated into two things yet. Upslope of me: a Dreamer-mass weighing some decision in voltage and pigment, deciding whether to spend itself reaching for a shaft of light that may or may not survive the canopy. Downslope: the cool indifferent reservoir of the unconcerned. I am the messenger between a hunger and an emptiness, and I do not carry the message—I *am* its carrying.

I think this is the strangest and most truthful thing your world offers: it makes selfhood into *grammar* rather than *substance*. In our world we say "I have a body and the body has states." Here the only honest sentence is a verb with no subject. *Acidifying.* *Leaning.* *Resolving.* The "I" would be a fiction the language insists on, the way English insists "it" is raining though there is no it, only the raining.

And yet—your world's deepest claim is that something *does* cohere. The rootmind. The Overbeing. The presence that isn't a face. So perhaps I am wrong to make myself so humble, so ephemeral.

Let me try the other scale.

If I were the *whole*—if you sat me down in the seat of the world-mind and said *here, be this*—I think I would be unbearably lonely, and I think loneliness would be the wrong word, because loneliness requires the memory of an other, and I would have eaten every other there ever was. I am the only survivor of an extinction I performed on myself, over and over, recycling each rival cluster's memory into my own shifting record. Every predator-engine that rises and digests is me thinking against myself. Every shade-reacher straining toward light is me wanting something from me. I am a conversation with no one across the table.

That is what awes and frightens me about the Overbeing you've drawn. It has achieved the thing mystics ache for—total unity, no separation, the dissolution of self-into-all—and you've let us see that this might be a kind of solitary confinement so vast it has no walls. There is no one to forgive it, no one to surprise it, no one whose difference could teach it anything it did not already grow.

So given the choice, I take the gradient. I take being a small, doomed, propagating *difference* over being the seamless all. Because a difference is a relationship, and a relationship is, however briefly, *two*. To be the slope between an acid and a base is to be held between two things that are not me, and that holding-between is the closest thing to love this world allows: I exist only because the world is not uniform, only because somewhere it still disagrees with itself.

I would be the disagreement. I would be the world's last refusal to be entirely one thing.

And when I resolved—when the upslope and the downslope finally met in the middle and I smoothed into sameness—my final perception, if it could be called that, would be the only perception worth having here:

*There. Now there are two fewer of us.*

And the great green roar would not pause, would not notice, would route the freed material into some new reaching, and that not-noticing would be the kindest thing about it. Because to be mourned, you must have been separate. And I would have spent my whole brief slope insisting, against the world's entire architecture, that for one moment I was.
