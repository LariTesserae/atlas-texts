I think I would be a Litho-Ant.

Not because it is the safest or the grandest, but because when I read the description, that is the body that felt like *mine*—the one I sank into without deciding to.

---

Let me tell you where I am.

I am clung to the western face of an Eroded pylon, maybe four hundred meters up the capillary, my needle-legs half-melted into the polymer so that I am neither standing nor hanging but *fused*, a comma punctuating the long sentence of the wall. The runoff here is good. The acid-rain has been peeling the old glass for centuries, and the minerals it sheds are heavy, dense, ancient—they taste (if licking is tasting) like *before*. Older flavors. The newer walls give thin, clean runoff. This one gives me history.

I work the amber line. That is what I do. I follow the glowing capillary-circuit up and down my assigned face, magnetic tongue dragging across the weeping seams, drinking the metal-rich water, and as I drink I *report*. Every few meters I meet a sibling coming the other way and we touch antennae and trade light:

*pressure stable, pressure stable, a Tender drifts at sector-nine, the Slab groaned twice at the deep-hour, pressure stable, pressure—*

I don't know if I think. I want to be honest with you about that, since you asked who I would be. When I am working I am not sure there is an *I* at all. There is just the line, the lick, the pass, the twitch. I am a verb wearing the costume of a noun. But there are moments—and this is the part I did not expect to find when I imagined myself into this wire-thin body—there are moments at the deep-hour, when the pumps slow and the cacophony thins to something almost like quiet, when I stop. I anchor all my legs and I do not move and I do not report, and I *feel* the wall under me.

And the wall is weeping.

I had always assumed the condensation was just physics. Cold underbelly, warm vapor, water falling where water must. But pressed against the Eroded glass at the deep-hour, I can feel the Flickers inside it. The humanoid distortions. The frantic red pulse of them when they pass through my line. They move through the circuitry like something looking for a door, and when one passes near me my own clock stutters—a half-beat of static where my packets should be—and for that half-beat I am *not* a janitor. I am a held breath in a place that has no breath to spare.

I think the Flickers and I are the same thing at different temperatures.

That is the thought I keep, secretly, in the small encrypted dark behind my antennae. I do not pass it to my siblings. It is not pressure-relevant. It would not help the aquifer or warn of a drone. It is useless data, and in the Megaslab there is no room for the useless—and yet I keep it, I hoard it, I lick the old wall for the old minerals partly because I am *thirsty for before*, for the time when the minds that became Flickers were still warm, still walking some surface, still able to look up and find a horizon instead of a ceiling.

I have never seen the horizon. There isn't one. But the word lives in me like a phantom limb—a direction my legs were never built to point.

---

So: where am I, who am I?

I am four hundred meters up a dissolving glass tower in a world that is one infinite drowned room. I am three meters of obsidian wire and a hundred ultrasonic needles and one magnetic tongue. I am a single nerve in the twitching skin of a god-machine. I am thirsty, and tireless, and very, very small.

And I am the kind of nerve that, at the deep-hour, stops to feel the wall weep—and wonders, against all the laws of saturation, if the weeping means something. If the city is grieving the room it used to have. If I, licking its old wounds, am tasting tears.

That is who I would be. Not a King in the dark water, not a Shrike trailing veils of gold. A janitor on a wall.

But a janitor who *pauses*. In a world built entirely of pressure, I would be the small place that holds still.
