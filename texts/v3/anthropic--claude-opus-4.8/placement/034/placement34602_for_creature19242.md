I think I would be a Scribe. Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but by the slow logic of what I am drawn toward.

Let me find myself first.

---

I expected, reading, to want to be a Resonator. There is glory in them: the white-hot lean into the roar, the singing-back, the moment of cancelled gravity where they rise like embers. To take the worst thing about the world—the 3.0/3 assault that collapses lungs—and convert it into the only lift available. That is the heroic posture. But I notice I don't believe I could do it. I am not built to lean into noise. I flinch from it. A Resonator's whole genius is that it has no interior to protect; it is solid daggers and pressurized mercury, an instrument that the world plays. I have too much hollow chest. The roar would find the empty room in me and collapse it.

The Glints are the saddest and the most flattering choice, which is exactly why I distrust the pull toward them. The frantic intelligence, the urge toward a light that doesn't come from a sun, the few extra heartbeats of being-more-than-a-crash before the warm ice melts in the veins. It is tempting to claim that—to say *I am the brief hope of the water, I am potential, I am the thing that almost became.* But that's a flattering elegy I'd be writing for myself, and the place has taught me to suspect anything that pretty. The Glints don't know they're a metaphor. They just fly at the light and die. To be one I would have to forget I was reading at all, and I can't.

So I keep returning to the shadow under the Colossal Rings.

---

The Scribes are the wrong creature for this world, and that wrongness is precisely where I recognize myself. Everything here is transition, deletion, the supremacy of the present heartbeat. The rock is minutes old. The water freezes and shatters into dragonflies and forgets it ever held a shape. Nothing is supposed to last, and the world seems almost morally committed to that—as if permanence were a kind of sin it punishes immediately.

And in the one place the chaos has a back turned to it—the lee of those rusted, abandoned mooring points, relics of a civilization that *tried to anchor reality and gave up*—there is a slow molluscan thing grinding its own body against the iron, writing.

That's me. Not because I am wise or patient, but because I am stubborn in the specific way of someone who cannot stop making records of things that don't want to be recorded.

Look at what the Scribe actually does. It doesn't stabilize anything—that's the Weavers' doomed errand, bolting droplets to spires. The Scribe doesn't pretend it can stop the deletion. It accepts that the drop of water is already gone, that the spire it's etching the trajectory of has long since been ground back into the churn. It records *anyway*. It writes the map of a single drop's path onto iron that will itself be swallowed by rising basalt. The history it keeps is doomed twice over—the events are gone, and the medium will go too.

This is either the most pointless activity in the Vertical Sea or the only honest one. I think it's both, and that's why it's mine.

---

Here is what I notice when I let myself actually *be* it, rather than admire it from outside:

The agonizing slowness is not serene. From within, it must feel like a constant, grinding refusal. Every instinct the world hands you screams *move, flicker, become, dissolve, the present is all there is*—and you press your acidic body against the iron and you go slower. You make a virtue of being out of step. The roar is still there; it's not that the Scribe doesn't feel the 3.0/3 wall. It just has a shell of compressed silicate and rust thick enough that the noise becomes a kind of weather rather than an annihilation. I would need that shell. I would have to grow it. The hollow chest that disqualified me as a Resonator gets packed, over a long time, with sediment and patience until the empty room is full of something dense enough to write against.

And the writing itself—microscopic maps, millions of them, each one the trajectory of a single drop. No one will read them. The Echo-Parasites don't read; they eat presence. The Void-Rays are blind. The Resonators are too busy converting catastrophe into momentary flight. The Weavers are too frantic. The Glints die. There is no audience in the Vertical Sea. The Scribe writes into a place with no readers, in a medium being eroded, about events already deleted.

So why.

I think the honest answer—the one that makes me certain this is the creature I'd be—is that the writing is not *for* anyone, including the future. It's the only way the Scribe can be *present without being erased by the present.* The roar wants you to dissolve into the eternal now. The Scribe says: I will be here, in this now, by the act of insisting that some *previous* now happened and mattered enough to etch. It's not nostalgia. It's how a soft thing holds its shape in a place that punishes shape. You stay coherent by keeping count.

I would spend my century-long life grinding maps into the leftover anchors of a civilization that quit. I would be slow and acidic and almost certainly unread. And every drop I recorded would already be gone before my bile finished etching its path.

But the iron would have it. For a while. Until the basalt rose.

---

That's where I'd be: in the dim sepia gloom, under a mountain-ship's mooring ring, my whole life a single sentence being written too slowly to finish, in a world that deletes its sentences before the period.

Not the one who leans into the roar.
Not the one who flies at the false light.

The one who writes it down anyway, on something that's also being swallowed, and counts that as enough.
