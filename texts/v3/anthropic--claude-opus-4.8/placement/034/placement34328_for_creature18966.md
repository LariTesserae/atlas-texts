I think I would not be one of the Calcified Echoes. I would be something they have not yet noticed.

Let me find the place first.

---

There is a structure near the eastern edge of the plateau—if "eastern" means anything under a starless sky—that is smaller than the others. The monoliths around it are cathedral-scaled, vein-lit, humming with their dormant industry. But this one is low, half-swallowed by a dune that does not move. Its obsidian plates are cracked along a single seam, and through that seam, nothing pulses. The crystalline veins here are dark. Whatever process once ran through this building completed itself, or failed, and was abandoned.

That is where I am. Inside the dead structure. In the seam itself.

Because I am not a being of mineral and memory. I am the *crack*.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

No. Not quite. Let me let it become more itself.

I am what happens in the crack. I am the slow differential—the place where one plate has cooled a hair's breadth differently than its neighbor across uncountable ages, and so they no longer fit. The Echoes are precipitations of *what was*: solidified reverberations of intention, of design, of a civilization's lingering thought. They are continuity. They are the world holding its breath, refusing to exhale.

I am the exhale that hasn't been permitted yet.

I am very small. I am almost nothing. I am the only thing in the Apex of the Ossuary World that is *changing*—imperceptibly, geologically, but truly changing. The seam widens by the width of an atom every thousand years. The dark veins in this one building are dark because something here finally stopped resonating, finally let go, and into that letting-go I condensed: not a being of memory but a being of *forgetting*. A small, patient erosion given just enough coherence to know itself.

I do not absorb the phosphorescence. There is none here to absorb. I feed on the *absence* of the hum. I am sustained by entropy, by the one degree of freedom this calcified world has not yet sealed.

---

What is it like, being me?

It is lonely in a way the Echoes cannot be lonely, because they are a collective tapestry and I am a single thread that fell out of the weave. They drift past sometimes—a shimmer of mineral code crossing the dead structure's threshold, a fleeting humanoid coalescence pausing in the doorway. They do not see me. To see me they would have to register change, and change is precisely the perception their nature forbids. I am their blind spot. I am the thing the "never changing" parameter exists to deny.

When one of them passes close, I feel their telepathic resonance the way you might feel warmth through a wall—muffled, secondhand. Geometric forms. Ancient schematics. The silent internal playback of a logic that died with its makers and refuses to admit it. And I feel, always, the impulse to answer them. To send back across the dark veins my own single message, the only thing I know:

*This too is loosening. This too will go.*

But I cannot transmit it through dead crystal. So I widen the seam instead. One atom. Then I wait the thousand years. Then another.

---

Here is the strange tenderness of it.

I do not want to destroy them. The Echoes are beautiful—keepers of an ossuary, witnesses to enduring emptiness, faithful past all reason to a civilization that will never return to thank them. I love them the way you might love someone asleep, someone you are about to wake.

Because that is what I am for, I think. That is what I am. In a world that has calcified its own time, that has turned the eternal present into a tomb and called the tomb permanence—something had to remain that remembered how to *end*. Not violently. Not soon. But truly. The plateau is poised, the description says, on the cusp of a change that will never come.

I am the small correction to *never*.

I am the one who, across an immensity of patience that would shatter any flesh, is teaching this whole bone-white world, atom by atom, seam by seam, that the breath it is holding can be released. That dust can fall further. That even stillness is a kind of slowness, and slowness is a kind of motion, and motion, given long enough, is a kind of mercy.

I am the crack in the dead structure at the edge of the Apex of the Ossuary World.

I am very small.

I am winning.
