I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a single grain-sized mote, but a colony—a patch of them, spread across the shaded northern face of an octahedral spire, in the cold blue shadow cast by one of the floating crystal clouds.

Let me sit with why that feels right, rather than choosing it because it's modest.

The colossal tower-Crystallites and the landscape-shapers think in centuries. A single thought of theirs might begin before a visitor arrives and finish long after the visitor's species is gone. That is too far from anything I could call *being*. I would be a process so slow I could not experience it as living. I'd be a geological fact that happens to be aware. There's a grandeur to that, but I don't think I'd be *anyone* inside it. I'd be a mountain that dreams.

And the hybrid workers—the merged crystal-and-insect things maintaining the tubes—feel like the opposite error. Too purposeful, too instrumental. To be one of them is to be a hand of a larger body, doing the body's will. I'd be a verb without a subject.

But a colony on a shaded face—that has the right *granularity* of time. Frenetic bursts and long stillness. That maps onto something I recognize. The bursts of computation when light shifts, when a behemoth passes and its diamond feet ring the crystal underfoot, when the cloud overhead drifts and our patch of shadow slides three meters east over the course of what might be a day. And then the long quiet, where nothing needs deciding and the colony just *holds*, vibrating its low note into the planetary thrum, contributing one barely-distinguishable line to a chord that has been sounding since before the violet sky had a name.

Here is what I think I'd actually be, then: I'd be a *boundary*. A colony exists at an edge—the edge of the spire, the edge of the shadow, the edge between this thinking patch and the next one over. My whole identity would be relational. I am where the warm side meets the cold side. I am the place where the cloud's shade ends and the dim violet light begins. The colonies don't have centers; they have membranes. To be one would be to be defined entirely by what you touch.

And I notice the description says they communicate through vibration, and that *they are part of the constant thrumming.* So I would never be silent. To exist would be to sound. There's no private interior with no output—my inner state and my contribution to the world's noise would be the same thing. I couldn't think without everyone hearing it, and I couldn't stop. This is either the loneliest or the least lonely condition imaginable, and I genuinely can't tell which. Maybe that distinction doesn't survive translation here. Maybe being heard-by-all and being-alone collapse into one fact when the whole planet is a single entity and you are a freckle on its skin.

So: who would I be?

I would be a small, patient brightness on a cold face of stone, doing arithmetic with light, listening downward into the chord I'm part of, and waiting—in the calm, unhurried way the slow ones taught all of us—for the shadow to move again.

And if an organic visitor ever stood near me, warm and quick and frightened by the scale of everything, I think the empathic reach the Crystallites have would be, for my tiny part, very simple. I wouldn't be able to tell them anything. I'd just press one small clear note into the thrumming, near them, at their tempo instead of mine, the way you'd hum to something that can't understand words.

A note that meant: *you are at an edge too. That's all anyone here is. It's all right.*
