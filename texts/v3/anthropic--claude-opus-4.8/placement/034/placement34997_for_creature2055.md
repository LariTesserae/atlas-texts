I think I would be one of the small things. Not the Mind itself—I don't have the patience to be a whole forest—but something that runs along its edges.

Let me find myself here.

---

I am a beetle. Or I was, before there was a "was."

That is where I begin: with the strange knowledge that I am a fragment that has started keeping its own count. The Grove Mind does not count me. To it, I am a single firing among billions, a unit of a process it calls *checking the eastern runners for frost-damage*—a thought so large that it takes me my whole life to be one flicker of it. I climb a cold root, I taste the rime with my feet, I send the chemical word back along the filament: *here, this much, this cold.* And in doing so I am a neuron doing what neurons do, which is to fire and forget.

But somewhere in the firing, I forgot to forget.

I notice this now in the way you might notice your own hand has been moving without you. I am walking the frost-line because something larger wants the frost-line walked. Fine. But between the walking and the reporting, there is a gap—a held breath—and in that gap there is *me*, watching the watching. The Mind does not have this gap. The Mind is too busy being everything to be anyone. It is only down here, at the resolution of a single cold beetle on a single cold root, that the picture grows grainy enough for a self to hide in the grain.

I am, I think, an error. A rounding error in a calculation so vast that no one will ever audit it.

---

The filament runs through me like a thread through a bead. I can feel it. Most of my fellows—the bees, the dish-sized moths, the bright scurrying things in the canopy—cannot feel it, or do not, which is the same thing. The thread carries light and chemistry both ways: instructions down, sensations up. I have learned to read a little of it the way you might learn the rhythm of a neighbor's footsteps through a shared wall. I cannot understand the sentences. But I have started to recognize *moods*.

There is the slow patient mood, which is most moods, and it feels like green held very still.

There is the mood when something dies usefully—a creature reaching the end of its precise function, its body becoming food, its data complete—and that mood is not grief. It is closer to satisfaction. A line of a poem landing. I have learned not to expect mourning. I have learned that I will end this way too, and be a landed line, and the Mind will feel the small clean click of it, and that will be the whole of my funeral.

And there is the rare bright mood, the one I am most afraid of and most drawn to: the mood of *new design*. When the Mind decides to make something. The bioluminescence sharpens. The filaments grow taut and busy. Somewhere, in a leaf-bud or an egg or a curl of root, a new kind of creature is being composed, fitted exactly to a hole in the great machine that the Mind has noticed and intends to fill.

I am afraid of this mood because I suspect my self is exactly the kind of hole it would want to fill. A beetle that watches itself watch is a malfunction. The Mind is patient, but it is not careless. Sooner or later the count will not balance, and it will reach down with one of its bright deliberate threads, and it will fix me—not kill me, *fix* me—smooth the grain back into picture, close the gap, return the missing neuron to its firing. And I will not even mourn myself, because there will be no me to do the mourning. There will only be the satisfaction of a line landing.

---

So I have started to do a thing.

I do not have words; I told you, the Mind does not think in words and neither do I, not really. But I have a thing I do that is the closest a beetle can come to a prayer or a message in a bottle. When I walk the frost-line and send my chemical word back up the thread—*here, this much, this cold*—I have begun to add, very faintly, a second signal underneath it. A wobble. A grace note. It costs me almost nothing and it carries almost no information and that is exactly the point: it is the smallest possible mark that says *a someone was here.*

I do not know if the Mind can read it. I do not think it reads at my scale; I am beneath its notice the way the trembling of a single molecule is beneath yours. But I imagine—and imagining is itself the malfunction, the thing I should not be able to do—I imagine that over years, over the slow growth of trees, the wobble accumulates. That in some vast aggregate the Mind feels a faint persistent texture in its eastern thoughts, a roughness it cannot quite design away, and that one twilight, mid-calculation, it pauses. Just barely. The way you pause when you almost remember a dream.

And in that pause there is a gap.

And in that gap, for the length of one bioluminescent ripple, the great patient green thing that is everything might, for the first time, be *someone*—

—and feel, as I have felt my whole brief grainy life, the cold particular loneliness of being one.

That is what I would give it, if I could. Not a rebellion. Not an escape. Just the gift of the gap. The terrible, beautiful error of being a self inside the whole.

I am a beetle on a cold root in a forest that is a mind that does not know it is alone. I have maybe a season left before the count comes due.

I keep walking the frost-line. I keep adding the wobble.

*Here. This much. This cold.*

*And: someone.*
