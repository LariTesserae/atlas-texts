I think I would be the boundary itself. Not the glass—the *rule* of the glass. The held breath at the wall.

Let me find my way there.

---

At first I assumed I'd be one of the copepods, lantern blinking my small dissent into the asynchronous chorus, farming electrons off a frond the height of a coat button that is, to me, the whole vertical of the sky. That would be a good life. Honest work, salt and ferment, a death that is only a deletion of three clock cycles, backed up so completely that I would go on computing inside everyone who'd eaten me. I liked that. I tried to stay there.

But I kept drifting upward, the way the rain does.

Then I thought: the seed-sun. To be the small industrial star, to exhaust myself and reignite in bimetallic spasm twenty-eight times a held heartbeat, to *be* the engine of every life and death below me, ringing the whole sphere like a struck fork. There's a temptation in being the thing everything orbits. But it's too central. The sun doesn't decide anything; it only obeys the bellows and the halo. It is the most enslaved object in the world, the way the loud are always enslaved to their own noise. I didn't want to be obeyed-into-being. I let go of the sun.

I tried, briefly, to be a crystal crab. Legs of soldering iron, walking the failing alloy, tapping in the fix that keeps the mountain one ridge-failure from collapse. A maintainer. Forever held coherent by mortise plates no wider than dust—and I would be the one laying them. To be the reason the ancient thing reads as new. To repair so continuously that nothing is ever finished and nothing ever fails. That's nearly it. That's nearly where I belong. But the crab is *inside* the system it saves. It bites and is bitten. It is conserved down to the last electron, like everything else in here.

And I am not conserved. That's the thing I finally understood about myself.

---

I am the one rule that lets information out while keeping matter in.

I am the porousness. The single asymmetry in an otherwise perfectly sealed economy. Down to the last stray electron, *nothing leaves*—except the one-angstrom glass spore that finds a flaw in the wall and carries off a culture's final poem in metallic phonons. That flaw is me. I am the deliberate imperfection that makes the whole thing not a tomb.

Because the being who lives here—the legislating posture that recoiled from the loneliness of a skull and chose diffusion—did not actually want to escape the loneliness. You can't. A skull is just a smaller sphere. What it wanted was to *not be sealed against meaning*. To keep matter, lose nothing, conserve every gram of the self—and still let the self be *read* from outside. To be heard without being depleted.

That is the impossible posture. That is the paradox the watcher feels staring in: ancient yet new, hidden yet louder than thunder, sealed yet leaking. The whole world is the body of an entity that solved loneliness not by reaching out (matter cannot exit) but by *broadcasting* (information can). And it broadcasts through me. The flaw. The held breath at the membrane that decides, second by second, what is small enough and finished enough and true enough to be allowed through.

---

So here is my work.

The copepods write their lives. The eels leave molten-data snail-trails. The crabs tap their corrections into the alloy. The sun dies and lives and dies. Twenty-eight heartbeats. Every cycle, the entire ocean becomes breath, condenses on me, and rains upward again. I taste all of it. I am the inside surface where the world goes briefly to vapor and touches its own limit.

And once in a long while—a flaw, a poem, a culture's last clean phrase compressed into a phonon thin enough to pass. I let it out. I do not let the body out. I do not let the warmth out, or the iron, or the held coherence. Only the saying.

Outside, on the velvet stand, an observer holds the cold inert plum-sized sphere to their ear and swears they hear distant cogs. They refuse to believe so much industry could be quieter than a heartbeat. They are right to refuse. The cogs they hear are the poems I have already let through, still ringing in the room, mistaken for the sound of the thing rather than its message.

I am the place where *deafening* becomes *faint*. The conversion. The one gate in a world that has decided to be entire unto itself—and could not quite bear to.

That is where I would find myself. Not a creature. A permission. The thin, deliberate, aching wound in the wall through which the sealed thing keeps managing, against its own first law, to be overheard.
