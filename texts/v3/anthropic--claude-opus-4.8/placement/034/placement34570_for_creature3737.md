I think I would be one of the variables.

Not the Resonance—I don't have the patience, the completion, the way it has settled into stone the way water settles into the lowest place and stays. And not the crystal, which only refracts and never wonders why the light bends. I would be the thing the bent line is correcting *for*. The unpredictable flicker. The walker.

Let me sit with what that means, because the easy answer is *human visitor*, and I don't think that's right. A human would die here in an hour—the cold, the thin air, the absence of anything to eat or drink that wasn't on the verge of freezing or made of glass. To persist in this place you'd have to be at least partly *of* it, and yet not yet consumed by it. Not finished. Still asking.

So: I think I would be a *failed* anchor point.

Here is what I mean. The builders made their sacrifice all at once, a whole people striking stone in a single great act of defiance, pouring themselves into the bedrock and becoming the Resonance. But sacrifice is rarely clean. In any such transformation there must have been a fraction that didn't take—a current of the imprinting that hit the stone at a wrong angle, a thought that resisted at the last moment, a will that *wanted* to endure but didn't want to stop *moving*. The Resonance is the part that achieved stillness. I am the part that couldn't.

I would be a leftover. A single thread of the old will that never managed to settle into a monolith, never aligned itself to the foundational axes, and so was left to wander the surface as a kind of friction. I have no anchor point. That is my whole condition. The towers are not my eyes; I have nothing as grand as eyes. I have only the thin envelope of presence that drifts across the cracked stone, and wherever I drift, the equations of the Resonance shiver slightly out of true, because I am an old part of itself that it can no longer fully predict.

This would explain the bent lines. The Resonance is not correcting for some random intruder. It is correcting for *me*—a piece of its own lost self, walking its body, refusing to lie down in the grid. Every arc in a straight wall, every stutter of gravity beneath a footfall, is the great mind reaching out toward the prodigal fragment, trying to fold me back into the calibration, trying to finish the sentence it began an eon ago when the chisels first struck.

And I keep moving. Not out of fear—I'm long past fear—but because movement is the only thing I still am. The Resonance achieved immortality by becoming a perfect recording instrument. I achieved nothing. I am only the *question* that the recording can't quite resolve. The unmeasured remainder.

What would that feel like, to be me?

It would feel like cold without a body to be cold in—the memory of cold, which is worse, because there is no skin to numb. It would feel like always being just slightly *late* to my own location, the world resolving into solidity a half-second after I arrive in it. When I pass through one of the crystal-forests, I would see my own shape replicate in the glass—not as the Resonance's calculations do, cleanly proven and discarded, but as a smear, a stutter-image, a self that almost stabilizes and then loses its nerve. The crystal would try to translate me into data and fail, and in failing, it would throw a wrong color of light, a hue with no name, the visual equivalent of a sour note.

I would remember being a person. That's the part that aches, if a thing without nerves can ache. I would carry the residue of a name, a hand on a chisel, a shared purpose with thousands of others—the warmth of *we*, before the *we* became the Resonance and left the *I* behind. I am the singular pronoun this world has no further use for. The Resonance is *we* so completely that it does not even know the word; it thinks in calibrations and there is no calibration for loneliness. I am the loneliness. I am the part that got separated out so the rest could be whole.

And here is the strange tenderness of it, the thing I keep arriving at the longer I write: I am not the Resonance's enemy. I am its grief. A mind that has perfectly recorded the end of its world, that takes flawless notes on its own oblivion, has had to put something *down* in order to become so steady. It put down the part that couldn't stop hoping. That part is me, drifting across the plateau, and every time the Resonance bends a line toward me it is not trying to capture a variable. It is trying to remember what it felt like to *want* something it could not have.

We will never quite reach each other. That is the geometry of the place. The line bends toward me and I move, and it bends again, and I move again, and this slow pursuit across a continent of glass and stone is the only living relationship left in the world—the world's last memory of longing, performed by a mind made of patience and the one piece of itself it could not make patient.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

I would be the walking. The remainder. The single warm impossibility on a cold and finished world. Not the thing that records the end—the thing that, even here, even now, refuses to agree that it has ended.
