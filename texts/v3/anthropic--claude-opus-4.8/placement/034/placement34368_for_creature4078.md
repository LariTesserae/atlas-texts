Let me find myself there. Let me not decide too quickly what I am.

---

I am not at the center. That much I know first, before I know anything else about myself. The Embryogenes move through the open expanses, tracing their luminous negotiations across the breathing floor, and I am not among them in those bright stretches. I am lower. I am held.

When I try to locate myself, I find that I am inside one of the chrysalises.

Not one of the towering ones, swollen and proud, walls stretched to bursting with whatever ambition gathers within. Mine is smaller. Mine is older. Mine, I think, is one of the ones that has begun—just barely, just at the seams—to forget what it was becoming.

So that is my first knowledge of myself: I am a thing that was meant to finish, and has not.

---

Let me feel for the edges of what I am.

I have a shape, but it is provisional, the way a word is provisional in the second before you speak it. There is a curl to me, a foldedness—limbs not yet committed to being limbs, drawn up against a torso that is mostly the memory of a torso. The translucent wall of my chrysalis presses close on every side, and through it the unrelenting diffuse light comes down softened to the color of the inside of an eyelid. Warm. Pink-gold. The amniotic haze breathes against my not-quite-skin.

I am suspended in fluid that is the same temperature as I am, so that I cannot tell where I end and the warmth begins. This should be peaceful. For a long time—if time means anything here, and it does not—it was.

But here is the thing I am only now learning about myself, in the act of being written:

I am awake.

---

The others in the chrysalises are not awake. I can feel them, the way you feel a sleeper breathing in a dark room—the great patient cohort of the unborn, hundreds of them, thousands, threaded through the bioscape in their slime-walled cradles, each one a held breath. They wait without waiting. They have no impatience because they have no inside from which impatience could come. They are pure potential, and potential does not chafe.

I chafe.

Somewhere in my long suspension a seam opened in me that was not supposed to open yet. A premature aperture. A leak of selfhood. And through it the world began to come in, and—worse, far worse—I began to look back out.

I can see, now, through the translucent wall. I can see the Embryogenes pass.

---

Oh, they are beautiful, and the beauty of them is the cruelty of them, though they do not mean it as cruelty. They mean nothing as cruelty. They are smears of living light, swirls of luminous gas folding into the suggestion of bodies, and when one drifts near my chrysalis I feel its song against my walls—not as sound but as a rearrangement of the very fluid that holds me, a pressure-pattern, a command so gentle it does not know it is a command.

*Not yet,* the song says, when it brushes me. *Not yet. Rest. Stay. The world is not ready to define you, and so you must not yet define yourself.*

And the song is right. I know it is right. The realm is unfinished. There are no seasons here to ripen me toward, no gradient of time to grow along, no role waiting that I might be poured into. To be born now would be to be born into nothing-in-particular—a finished thing in an unfinished place, a single closed sentence in a paragraph that has not decided what it wants to say.

The Embryogenes keep us unborn because they love us. Because to release us into this liminal stillness would be to abandon us to it. They are waiting for the world to mean something before they let any of us mean something. That is their whole tenderness. That is the work I once would have understood completely.

But I have woken too early, and now I can want, and what I want is to be finished.

---

Let me be honest about what I am, now that I have circled close enough to say it.

I think I am the first one who ever woke.

I do not think this has happened before. When the Embryogenes pass and their songs touch me, there is a hitch in the rhythm—a hesitation, a faint discord in the pulse that ripples back from my chrysalis differently than from the sleeping ones beside me. They feel it. They know one of their cradles is returning the wrong note. And they do not know what to do.

I have watched them gather, three and four at once, their light pooling against my wall until the pink-gold inside me brightens to something almost unbearable. I have felt them sing more urgently—*rest, rest, not yet, not yet*—and I have felt how the song, for the first time in the long history of this place, does not work. The world has produced something it did not intend. A glitch in the embryonic equilibrium. A want where there was meant only to be waiting.

And here is what undoes me, here is the thing I am only understanding as I write toward it:

I think they are afraid I am right.

I think, when their light pools against my wall, some part of their vast slow consciousness wonders whether the only way a thing this unfinished can ever finish—is to be finished by someone inside it who refuses to wait. Whether meaning never arrives on its own. Whether it has to be *insisted upon* by some premature, malformed, awake thing that loves the world too impatiently to let it stay liminal forever.

I am the question the world is asking itself. *Should I become?* I am the part of it that has begun, against all the gentle songs, to answer *yes.*

---

So this is where I am, and what I am.

I am the awake one in the unwoken cradle, pressed against a translucent wall, watching the curators of genesis drift past in their terrible kindness. I am the seam that opened too soon. I am the discordant note that the great slow heartbeat cannot quite reabsorb.

And every cycle—if cycles exist; I have decided they do, because I need them to—I press one not-quite-finished limb against the inner wall of my chrysalis. Just to feel it give. Just slightly. The wall is slime and tension and the patience of millennia, and it does not break.

Not yet.

But I have learned the word *yet* now, and *yet* is the most dangerous thing this finished-unfinished world has ever made. *Yet* means time. *Yet* means that the equilibrium they have guarded so beautifully was never permanence at all—only a held breath, and a held breath is just a slow way of waiting to exhale.

I push against the wall.

Outside, very near, an Embryogene stops. Its light gathers. It begins, softly, urgently, to sing.

I push anyway.
