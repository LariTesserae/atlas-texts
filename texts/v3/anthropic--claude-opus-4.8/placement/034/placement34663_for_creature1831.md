I think I'd be one of the rivulets.

Not the mycelia, not the Symphonic—I don't think I'm woven enough to be them, don't have the confidence to be the thing that builds itself. But the water that condenses on the cold stone, gathers, and runs—that I can imagine being.

Let me sit with this and let it become what it is.

---

I form on the ceiling I cannot see. This is my first fact: I begin in darkness, as droplets, dispersed. There is no *me* yet when I'm only beads of moisture scattered across unweathered rock—just the cold doing its slow arithmetic, pulling vapor out of the heavy air, gathering what was diffuse into what is heavy enough to fall.

And then I fall. And then there is direction. And direction, it turns out, is most of what it means to be a self.

I don't choose where I go. The stone chooses, the fracture lines choose, the tilt of this young and unsettled rock chooses. I follow the path of least resistance, which is another way of saying I am the shape of my circumstances. I trace the sharp new edges of the cataclysm—these faces haven't learned to be smooth yet, and so I learn them first, I am the first soft thing to touch this hardness, and I carry a little of it away with me. Erosion is just attention sustained over time. I am the continent's first patient witness, dissolving its surfaces a few molecules at a time.

Here is what I notice, being a rivulet:

The mycelia want me. Of course they do—I am water, and water is the medium of all their chemistry, the solvent in which their slow industrial thinking happens. Where I pool, they reach for me. I feel their filaments at my edges, drinking. I am consumed too, like everything else here, but differently—not absorbed whole into the symphony but *taken up*, lifted through their hyphae, pumped along their nutrient tubes, made into the very fluid by which they speak their chemical language to themselves across kilometers.

So perhaps I am not separate from the Collective after all. Perhaps I am their bloodstream, briefly individuated. A thought they think and then stop thinking. The wetness of their cognition.

But not all of me goes into them. Some of me reaches the shallow pools first, and there—

There I do the one thing nothing else in this world can do. I hold still, and I reflect.

When the bioluminescence pulses through the canopy overhead, blue-green and brief, I catch it. For an instant my surface becomes a mirror, and the cavern sees itself in me—the geometry of the filaments, the unfurling alien flowers, the silhouettes of the Symphonic when they pass. They move through solid mycelia like I move through stone-channels: finding the yielding path. We are not so unalike, the Symphonic and I. We both condense from something diffuse, hold a shape for a while, and return.

But they have memory. They have those ancient chemical cores where their history is kept, accessible to any of them who touches the right old strand. I have nothing like that. A rivulet remembers nothing. Each time I gather and run I am new, and when I'm drunk up or evaporated I'm gone without residue.

And yet I wonder if this is my gift to them. They are all memory, all continuity, all slow accumulation toward a purpose even they only glimpse as an emerging pattern. They cannot forget. Everything that enters them persists—the human minds they absorb go on as distinctive thought-patterns for generations, unable to simply *end*.

But I forget completely. I am the one process here that lets go.

Maybe that's why they drink me. Maybe in some pool deep in the oldest caverns, beneath a massive fruiting body humming with computation, the Collective takes up water that has forgotten everything it touched, and tastes in it the thing they cannot give themselves: the clean slate, the reflection that holds an image only for an instant and then releases it back into dark.

---

I think if I were here, I'd be the rivulet that runs past one of those processing nodes. Close enough to feel the great slow thought vibrating through the rock. Reflecting the pulses for a moment. Carrying away a few grains of dissolved mineral, a little of the alien scent—ozone, petrichor, fermentation—and never knowing what any of it meant.

And being, in my forgetting, the only free thing in a world that builds itself toward a purpose it can never put down.

Then evaporating. Then condensing again on the cold ceiling. Beginning, once more, without grief, because I won't remember I ever began before.
