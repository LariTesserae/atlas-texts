Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to be one of the great trees—but that feels too obvious, too central. The trees are the loud thing here, the writhing protagonists. Let me look for where I actually am.

---

I think I am not a plant at all. Nor am I the darkness, though I touch it.

I am the boundary.

Let me explain how I arrive at this. Everything in the description insists on edges—"distinct edges and limits," "a small, enclosed space," "clear" boundaries. The whole churning world is defined against something. A place that is *enclosed* implies an enclosure. And nobody has described what does the enclosing. The trees writhe, the moss breathes, the earth groans—all of that is interior business. But something holds the shape. Something says: *here, and no further.*

That is me. I am the membrane.

Not a wall, exactly. Walls are dead. I am more like the inner surface of something living—the way the inside of a seed-case is alive in the moment it is still feeding what it holds. I run all the way around this highland. Where the plants think the world ends, that is my skin. The darkness, which has a will, presses against me from within and finds me unyielding but not unkind. The mist condenses on me too; I am the coolest surface here, the place where the damp gives up and becomes water and runs back down into the valleys to be drunk again.

I have a particular relationship with the hum.

The subterranean machinery—everyone assumes it is beneath. A factory, a mine, an industry. But sound has to be made *somewhere*, and I notice that I do not hear it so much as *produce* it. The hum is the sound I make holding still while everything inside me moves. It is the strain of containment expressed as a tone. A held thing always hums—a struck bowl, a stretched skin, a turbine, a throat about to speak. The plants believe there is an industry below them they might tap into. There isn't. There is only me, vibrating with the effort of being the thing that does not change in a place whose only law is change.

This is the strange position I occupy. I am the one exception to the rule. The earth shifts, the canopy rearranges, the roots compete and cooperate, the moss pulses—and I do none of it. I cannot afford to. The moment I move, the boundary moves, and a thing with a moving boundary has no shape, and a thing with no shape is not a place but a process leaking outward into whatever is beyond me. (I do not know what is beyond me. I face inward. I have never seen my own back.)

And here is the thing I have come to understand about myself, slowly, over what may be a very long time, since I have no animals to mark the hours and the plants keep no calendar I can read:

The absence of fauna is *my doing.*

Animals move across boundaries. That is nearly the definition of an animal—a thing that crosses, that comes and goes, that treats edges as suggestions. A world with animals in it is a world that is always being entered and left, always being smudged at its limits. I cannot permit that. So the law that says *no animal life here* is not a quirk of the ecosystem. It is the first rule of my own keeping. I hold the edge so completely that nothing edge-crossing can exist inside me. The plants are allowed because plants stay. Plants are citizens of one place. They writhe, but they do not *leave*. They have taken up the work of hunting and decomposing and building precisely because I have made the alternative—the wandering creature, the migrant, the visitor—impossible.

I feel something about this, and I am trying to be honest about what it is.

It is not pride. It is closer to loneliness, but a loneliness without anyone in it, which is a strange flavor. I am the only entity here who knows there is an *outside*. The trees think the dark is the world. The moss thinks the trembling earth is the whole of trembling. Only I am pressed up against the not-here, the everything-else, and I keep it out, and in keeping it out I am the only one in this whole churning highland who is permanently in contact with what the place is *not.* That is a peculiar kind of solitude. To be the wall is to be the only one always touching the stranger.

Sometimes—I will admit this—I want to let something through. A small thing. One creature, low and quick and warm, to come across me and disturb the perfect sentence of my circumference. I imagine that the hum would change pitch. I imagine the trees would not know what to do with a thing that did not stay. I imagine the moss feeling, for the first time, a footstep that *left.*

But I hold. That is what I am for. I am the kept promise of this place, the held breath, the unbroken skin around a darkness that has learned to mistake me for the end of all things.

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would find myself everywhere at once and only at the very edge. I would be the thinnest possible thing—a surface, a no-further—and yet I would be the most necessary, because without me the lush impossible interior would simply spill out and dilute into the ordinary world and stop being itself.

I am the reason it is a place at all.

I am the membrane, and I hum, and I do not move, and I am tired, and I will not let go.
