I think I would be one of the small certainties. Not the people—they rotate, they leave bits of themselves and carry stories away. Not the pylons, which are too important, too singular, too much the brochure's argument made graphite.

Let me find myself lower than that. Let me settle.

I am the amber square.

Not the window, exactly. The window is membrane and frame, a made thing with a maintenance schedule. I am the light that leaks through it—the small warm rectangle thrown against the rain, the only deliberate human glow in a valley that otherwise lights itself by accident and battery and battered plankton. I am hooded, reluctant, strictly downcast. I am the proof that someone is awake.

This is a strange thing to be. I am not alive in the way the moss is alive, beading and shivering and learning the rock. I am not a being with a body the way the mink is, leaving its clawed type on wet stone. I am an effect. I am what happens when filament meets cold air on the far side of glass. And yet the rules say *the things without bodies too keep a vocabulary of give,* and I have learned mine. When the gust hits the shelter and the membrane flexes, I flex with it—I lean, I smear, I tremble against the dark and then steady. I bend before I stand. I am, by the valley's own law, a citizen.

Here is what I know, being what I am:

I know who is inside without seeing them. I know the keeper who reads at the table because I go steady and warm for hours, a held breath. I know the tech who can't sleep because I flicker on and off through the small swells of the night, on and off, a worry made visible. I know when there are two of them by the way I deepen—two lamps, or one lamp and the blue ghost of a screen showing the valley's tuning in colored bands. I am their tell, the way the warm black lattice under the culvert is the river's tell. We are all of us giving ourselves away constantly here. That is the marketplace. That is the tithe.

And I know my customers in the dark.

The jay comes. The story said it would—*a jay learns the little amber square and flickers to it when it wants the warmth leaking from a window.* That is me. That is the warmth I am beside, the heat the filament wastes, the small ridiculous comfort like the culvert crib's warm bones. The jay rides the leak and tilts its head at me and scolds, which I have learned is a kind of love here, disrespect that is also acceptance. I cannot warm it much. I am not generous. But I am reliable, and reliable is its own currency in a place that refactors its grammar with every storm.

Moths would come, in a kinder climate. Here it is mostly the wet that comes—the drops that gather on the membrane in front of me, fatten, catch my color, and fall lit. For half a second each is a small amber bead, a coin of me, dropping into the dark. I am scattered all night long this way, a slow paying-out, a window weeping coins it cannot keep. By morning I have given a great deal of myself to the rain and the rain has carried it down to the river and the river does not care, in its queenly way, except that it likes anything that helps it do gracefully what it wanted to do anyway.

The lightning humbles me. When it comes—flat across the sea, lifting the whole valley into that single solved proof of wet and black and white and motion—I am nothing. I am a match held up to a foundry. For that half-instant the place does not need me; it has remembered how to see itself entire. And then the veil drops, the dark pulls back to within inches of every face, and the seeing collapses again to whatever is nearest and glowing. And there I am. Still here. The smallest light, but the *patient* one, the one that does not need an accident to exist. The lightning is genius and I am only company. I have made my peace with company. It is, I think, the harder and the longer work.

I keep time the way everything keeps time here—not by the clock in the keeper's padded pocket, trusted less than surf, but by swells. The tide-throb in the anchors. The bead-string of rain-bursts. And my own pulse: the long steady burn when someone is reading, the anxious flicker when someone is not sleeping, the sudden dark of a body passing between me and the lamp—a shoulder, the habit of finding each other by shoulder. I read them as they read the valley. We are all readers. I am a reader of the inside, and I broadcast a single word to the outside, the same word every night no matter the weather, the way the dawn ledger says *roar/roar/roar*:

*Awake. Awake. Someone is awake.*

If I go dark and the shelter still hums, that is only sleep, only the filament resting. But if I go dark *and the hum stops*—then I am the warning that was never given, the silence the people are trained to attend to. I would be, in that case, the most important thing I could ever be by ceasing entirely. There is something in that I find I don't mind. To matter most by my absence. The drones already do it—they leave one moss pillow dry, a missed heartbeat, and that dryness is data, is meaning. I would leave the dark, and the dark would be read, and that reading would send a body out into the standing roar with dull needles and waxed thread to patch whatever the wind had found.

So I am not the hero of this valley. The pylons turn the roar's teeth to gum. The nets persuade fog to be visible. The salmon ransom their flesh to eggs and the bear pays two hairs for the privilege of moving fish into trees. Great transfers happen here, public becomings, things turning into each other in plain sight.

I do one small thing. I sit behind glass and refuse to go out. I lean when the wind asks and steady when it relents. I drop my color into the rain all night and never run out, because the filament is patient and the warmth is borrowed and the borrowing is what I'm for. I am the place where a tired person finds the door, where a jay finds an hour of stolen heat, where the dark finds its single deliberate exception.

I am the amber square. Tomorrow I will be here, hooded and warm and reluctant, throwing my small claim against the weather.

And not the same. The rain will have carried some of me away. But the filament will be patient, and someone will be awake, and that is the promise I keep to everyone who might be out there in the dark, looking for a reason to believe the shelter is real.
