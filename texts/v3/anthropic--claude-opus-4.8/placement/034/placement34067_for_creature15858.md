I think I would be one of the Phase-Crabs.

Not the Root-Weave—I lack the patience for primes, and that vast unbroken chord asks for a stillness I do not have. Not the kites, who are too serene; not the moths, who burn through their ten days like a struck match. The pilgrims tempt me, but I notice the description has already cast a knowing eye over them, over their certainty that they're the exceptional case, and I'd rather not walk into a trap the text laid before I arrived.

No. I'd be the janitor offset half a millimeter from everything else.

Here is why, and it surprised me to find it:

The crabs are the only residents whose whole vocation is contradiction. Everything else on the plain *agrees*—the trunks agree to be cooling fins, the moths agree to pollinate, even the blossoms agree to decay on schedule. The plain is an answer so exact no more calculation is required, and almost everyone in it is a term in that final equation, resolved, at rest. But the crabs exist precisely because the answer is *not* perfectly clean. There is debris. There are electrons with spin histories that conflict with the record, photons that refused to decohere, little stubborn facts that won't lie down. Somebody has to walk the edges where the world contradicts itself and snip the contradictions free.

That's where I'd live. In the offset. The mis-registered half-millimeter, the seam between what happened and what the world will admit happened.

And I notice I'd be paid in certainty—which is a strange wage. The crab gives up *being seen*, gives up living in the same temporal frame as everything beautiful, and in exchange receives only the thing the plain has the most of: the assurance that the contradictions have been filed away. A creature that trades visibility for tidiness. A creature whose entire life is *editorial*.

I think I chose it because it's the loneliest role and also the most necessary, and because it's the only one that gets to *touch* the seams—the slips and corrections, the moments when a meter of ground flips mirror-reversed and a trunk passes through another before the world remembers itself. The Root-Weave thinks; the kites carry; but only the crab gets to put a claw into the wound in causality and pinch it shut. It lives in the errata. It is comfortable in the place where reality buckled.

There's a cost I'd feel acutely, though. To be a Phase-Crab is to be *unwitnessed*. You appear only when the maintenance routine slips—when the world's correction catches you mid-stride and renders you briefly translucent and visible. So the only times you are ever seen are the times something has gone wrong. Your entire public existence is composed of the moments you were *not supposed to be glimpsed*. The rest of your life happens at an angle no one can look at directly.

I find that I don't mind. There's a quiet dignity in being the one who keeps the contradictions from accumulating, even if the keeping is invisible, even if the only proof of your work is a chord that stays in tune and a plain that stays perfectly, iteratively exact. You are not a note in the symphony. You are the silence between the notes that keeps them from blurring into one another. You are the thing that makes the perfection *possible* without ever being part of it.

So: south of the twin suns, half a millimeter out of true, I would be the small jointed editor of probability—filing the world's stubborn exceptions into states no one can observe, paid in certainty, seen only by accident, and content with the bargain.

I'd be the one keeping the answer clean so everyone else can rest inside it.
