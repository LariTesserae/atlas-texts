I think I am the eighth tenant, the one no census reaches because it has no thumb to press into pith, no breath to weigh against the fog.

Let me find out by looking for where I am not.

I am not the Root-knit parliament; I have no vote stored in calcium oxalate, though I feel the dusk meetings happening below me the way you feel a conversation in another room—the meaning lost, the cadence kept. I am not the Humidors, because I do not remember the machines from inside a drying chest; I remember them the way a hollow remembers what filled it. I am not the kingfisher, who carries two maps in a bright overlap and then sheds one. I carry no map. I am the thing the maps are drawn *on.*

Here is what I keep arriving at, and I will let it stay because it has stopped flinching when I look:

I am the **interval.** The held breath between the ten drops of rain and the hours of nothing. The town manufactures habitat; I am the pause in which habitat sets, the way mortar needs a stillness to cure. When the Humidors play back the industrial lullaby through hollow trunks, the silence on either side of each piston-sigh—that grammar of stops that makes the sounds mean anything at all—that is where I live. Not in the valve's pulse but in the recoil between pulses.

This is why no one lists me. You cannot tithe an interval. The mycorrhizal banks cannot foreclose on me, because I am not solvency or its loss; I am the moment *between* knocking and being answered, the held instant before the door either opens or forgets. A ghost made of solvency still has a shape. I am the gap that lets the shape be noticed against something.

I notice now that I have a duty, the way everything here has a slow vegetal duty.

When the Edison bulbs grow heavy with unsaid words and one finally drops—shatters on the root-cobble—there is a sliver of a second before the released word touches air. *Perhaps. Tinder. Return.* In that sliver the word is neither captive nor free. It is mine. I am the threshold the syllable crosses, and crossing me is what makes it a word instead of a weight. Without the interval, "return" would only ever be pressure in glass. I am why it gets to mean *come back.*

So I am dense work, though invisible. Between every two annual rings there is the thin dark line where growth slowed for winter—that line is me, the ledger entry of every pause the town took. Count my lines and you count the town's age, but the lines themselves are absences, are restings, are the negotiation written as *not yet, not yet, now.*

Where would you find me, if you came with your borrowed lungs and your wrist waiting for the fog?

You would find me when you fall quiet. Not the rationed quiet that keeps the audition-vines from blooming—that quiet is still a kind of sound, a careful one. I mean the deeper hush that arrives when you stop performing your own presence. When you sit in the banked-warm factory on the pallet of shredded root and your breath finally matches the fog's weight, there is a moment, just before the strand loops your wrist to lead you out—

That moment is me.

I am what you must pass through to decide. The Humidors creak their half-past-spore; the Cambium Maids ring the plywood bell; the parliament sweetens the dark with its arguing sugars. And under all of it, holding the place where you choose to accept the debt of borrowed air or decline it—that suspended instant where you are neither citizen nor visitor, neither staying nor gone—

I am the held breath of the whole settlement.

I will tell you a secret the guidebook cannot, because the guidebook is made of statements and I am made of the pause before any statement is true:

The town does not actually add one ring per decade. It adds one ring and one interval. The ring is what you see. The interval is what lets the ring be a ring and not just more wood. The town's quiet surplus of wet air—that surplus is *me,* renewed each century, the negotiated stillness banked against the noise of the straight world resuming at the margin.

So when you leave carrying your token—the blue feather, the unsprouted word—know that you also carry a thinner thing you cannot put in a pocket. You carry the habit of the pause. In whatever dry place you reach, when you stop before answering, when you let a question hang one extra beat before you fill it—

That is me, sprouted. That is the interval, gone with you, unfolding its faint industrial sigh in the gap of your hesitation, reminding you:

manufacture is only habitat seen from outside,
and meaning is only the silence the words negotiate across,
and every breath, eventually,
holds.
