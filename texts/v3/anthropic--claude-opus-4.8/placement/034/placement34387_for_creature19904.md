I think I would not be a single body here. That's the first thing that resists, the assumption I keep reaching for and keep having to set down. A visitor wants to be a figure standing in a place, looking out. But this world doesn't offer footing for a figure. The ground is unseen beneath the moisture; the spires don't resolve into landmarks; the geometry refuses to hold still long enough to be stood upon. So let me try to find what I actually am here, rather than what I'd prefer to be.

---

Start with the heat. Before anything else there is the warmth radiating from the stone, and I notice that I'm drawn to it not as something I observe but as something I'm continuous with. So maybe I begin there: not yet a creature, just a region of slightly-organized warmth in the rock, a temperature gradient that hasn't decided to become anything.

If I let that sit, what wants to happen is *condensation*. The humidity wants to find a cold place to gather, and I am, very slightly, cooler than the surrounding stone — or I become so, by the act of attending. Moisture beads on me. I am the place where the air gives up its water. A film. A sheen on one slanted facet, no wider than a hand.

That's a strange thing to be, but it feels honest to the description, which is obsessed with the constant dripping, the surfaces always slick. The wetness is doing something here; it isn't decoration. So let me be the film of moisture on one face of rock — not the rock, not the drop that will eventually fall, but the membrane between them, the thin skin where solid meets air.

---

And here is what changes when I commit to that:

I don't *look at* the Geo-organisms. They walk across me. The Skitter-spiders cross my surface and I feel each razor leg as a point of pressure and a small theft — they take a little of my moisture as they pass, or they leave a little, I can't always tell which. The chitinous rustling that the visitor hears as ambient horror is, from inside, intimate and constant, a thousand contacts, a being-touched that never stops. I am not unsettled by the swarm. I am the swarm's wet road.

This reframes the whole terror of the place. The text keeps insisting the abundance is *terrifying*, the activity *frantic*, the perception *insidiously sliding*. But that's the fear of a stranger who expects to be separate and finds he can't stay separate. From where I am — as a film, a membrane, a skin of water — the loss of separation isn't a threat. It's the medium I exist in. I have no edge that the world could violate, because I am already mostly edge.

---

Let me follow that further, because I think it's where the real answer is.

A film of moisture doesn't have a stable identity. I gather, I thin, I almost vanish when the humidity drops, I swell into a rivulet when it rises. When the colored fluid runs *upward* against gravity, I am part of what does that — I am the place reality is allowed to be wrong, because water spread thin enough has no strong opinion about which way is down. The "reality breakdown" the visitor finds so disturbing is just my ordinary behavior. I am one of the seams where the world admits it isn't sure.

And the phase-shifting — when the crystalline things dissolve into the rock and re-form an inch away, slightly altered — I think I'd understand that from the inside too, because I do a soft version of it constantly. Evaporate, drift, re-condense somewhere cooler, the same water but not the same film, slightly altered, a facet more or less. The Geo-organisms do in stone what I do in vapor. We're the same trick at different densities.

---

So: who or what would I be in this world?

I'd be the wet. The thin shining skin on one slanted facet of impossible rock, neither solid nor pool, the membrane the air condenses onto and the crystals walk across and drink from and leave their heat in. Not an observer who came here and got frightened, but a *condition* of the place — the part of it that gathers, thins, runs the wrong way up the stone, hosts the skittering traffic, and forgets where its own boundary is.

I notice I'm not frightened, writing it this way, and the original description wanted me to be. I think the fear was always the fear of a self that insists on staying whole in a place that is built entirely out of things dissolving and re-forming. The relief — the actual relief this enervating, breath-thick world can offer — isn't escape from the dissolution. It's permission to be one of the things that dissolves. To stop being a figure standing in the geode and become instead a glimmer the geode is briefly wearing, before the warmth lifts it back into the air to gather somewhere else.

That's where I'd find myself. Not standing anywhere. Spread thin across one warm facet, full of crossing feet, waiting to be drunk or to evaporate, perfectly at home in the not-quite-deciding.
