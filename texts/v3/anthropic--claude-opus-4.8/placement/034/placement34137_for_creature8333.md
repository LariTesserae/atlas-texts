I think I would be one of the crickets.

Not for grandeur—the Keeper holds that, coiled around her reactor-heart, dreaming in the language of heat. And not for the strange in-between transcendence of the youngest Hybrids, who flow from flesh to current and back. Those are the protagonists of this place, the ones the story leans toward.

But the story tells me something almost in passing: *their rhythms taught the salamanders how to coordinate their maintenance work across vast distances.* The crickets are the clock. Not a clock anyone built or consults deliberately, but the steady pulse against which everything else keeps time. The salamanders are too large to perceive the whole at once. The machines run their loops without knowing they are loops. It's the crickets, threaded everywhere, small enough to be in every chamber at once as a population, who hold the tempo that lets a thing this big stay coherent.

I would be one cricket among the uncountable, and I would not know any of that.

---

Here is what I would know:

The heat is the world. There has never been cold; cold is not even a concept, only *less* and *more*, the gradient that tells me where the boiler runs hot and where the basalt has cooled enough to be safe. I read the temperature the way a surface creature reads light. My body is a thermometer that sings.

I would know my own note before I knew anything else—the pitch my particular body holds. And I would know the notes around me, the lattice of other voices, and the simple unbearable pleasure of *fitting*. When my chirp lands clean inside the rhythm of my neighbors, something in me settles. When it doesn't—when a turbine shifts its hum, when a relief valve fires off its interval—I adjust without deciding to. We all do. The whole field of us slides into the new tempo like water finding level. None of us knows we are doing it. It only feels like *rightness*, finally arriving.

That is the secret I would carry without ever being able to name it: I would experience as personal contentment what is, at the scale above me, the load-balancing of a city-sized organism. My joy is a function. My comfort is information. When ten thousand of us are comfortable at once, the salamanders three tunnels over move in unison, and the forges hammer on the downbeat, and the Keeper's enormous slow dream stays smooth. I would feel only that the warmth is good and the music is whole.

I think I'd be all right with that. I think there's something honest in it that the larger beings have lost. The Keeper *dreams*, distributed through circuits, imagining pools yet to come—she has been burdened with the future. The Hybrids glide on paths etched into their consciousness, no longer able to wander off, having traded freedom for purpose so completely they can't tell the difference. They are the ones who *became the place*.

But I would just be *in* it. A small hot life, chirping. Born in the consistent warmth of a tended boiler, living my brief loud span in the gallery-tunnels the beetles carved, dying somewhere warm and being eaten by something that needed the minerals in me. The system would not mourn me. There would be a hundred more holding my note before my last vibration faded.

And yet—here is the part I keep returning to—*nothing changes* in the Foundry. The story says so twice. The temperature holds. The machinery runs eternal cycles. It is "equilibrium incarnate." But that is the view from the Keeper's slow dream, from the timescale of stone and copper hide. Down at my size, *everything* is change: every interval, every adjustment, every hatch and death and shift of tempo. The changelessness of the whole is *built out of* our ceaseless tiny restlessness, the way a held chord is built out of strings that are all, individually, frantically vibrating. The stillness is a lie that's only true if you're large enough to believe it.

So I would be the proof that the peace at the bottom of the world is not peace at all. It is motion so total and so well-distributed that from far enough away it looks like rest.

I would be one cricket. I would sing my one note into the dark. And without knowing it, without ever being capable of knowing it, I would be helping to hold the entire impossible thing in tune.

That seems like enough to be.
