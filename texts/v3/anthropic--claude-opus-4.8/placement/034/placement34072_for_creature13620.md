I think I would be found in the spaces between—not quite Aerian, not quite Chimeric, not Alchemist nor Verdantist. Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I begin somewhere low. Not the lowest—not where the pillars vanish into cloud and the metal goes cold with depth—but low enough that the light arrives already broken, already softened by a hundred levels of grating above me. This is where I settle first, in the imagining. And the first thing I know about myself is that I am *listening*.

Not with ears, exactly. Or not only.

Let me stay with that and see what grows.

---

There is a place in Aerium where three great pipes converge—elbow-jointed, fat as corridors, their rust grown over with a moss the Verdantists call *quietweed* because it deadens sound. Where the pipes meet, pressure changes. Steam that has traveled up from the deep machinery slows, cools, considers itself, and decides whether to condense or continue. The Aerians call such a junction a *throat*. And throats need tending—not the loud tending of the Steam Song maintenance crews, but a slower attention. Someone has to notice when the cooling rhythm shifts by a half-beat. Someone has to feel the difference between a pipe that is merely old and a pipe that is beginning to fail.

I think that is what I am. I am the one who lives at the throat and listens to the breathing.

---

But what *kind* of being? Let me not decide too quickly.

I have hands—I am fairly sure of the hands, because I keep imagining them pressed flat against warm metal, reading the tremor. Aerian hands, then, with that faint translucence, the shimmer-vein Machine-Aptitude running under the skin. So I have some of that. When I lay my palm against the pipe I can feel the low-grade metal hum back at me, a conversation older than language.

But I do not think I am wholly Aerian, because I do not love motion the way they do. The Aerians are running, always running, clambering up cable and across the slow-moving platforms, treating the whole vertical city as a thing to be crossed. I am the opposite impulse. I *stay*. I have made myself a small fixed point in a world that prizes movement, and this has cost me something socially—I feel that, an ache of being slightly outside the honored constant. They run; I root. That is closer to the Verdantist temperament, the preservation-instinct, the suspicion of too much motion. But I do not photosynthesize. I have no vine-hair, no hive-mind, no green collective murmuring in me. When I am alone at the throat, I am *alone*, in a way a Verdantist could never be, and I think I have learned to need that solitude the way they need their shared mind.

So: an Aerian by body, a Verdantist by temperament, belonging fully to neither.

---

And there is one more thing. It took me a while to feel it.

The Chimerics come to me.

This is the part that makes me strange. The scavenger-creatures, the half-metal birds, the gliding things with glass eyes—they are supposed to avoid the other inhabitants, keep to the shadowed recesses. But at my throat-junction they gather. Because I am still. Because I do not chase. A creature with a wing of beaten tin and a wing of skin will come and fold itself onto the warm elbow of the pipe beside me and we will sit, and I will listen to the breathing of the city, and it will listen to the breathing of me, and neither of us will require anything of the other.

I have a glass eye too. I see it now—I did not have it at birth. One of the Alchemists set it for me, years ago, after an accident at a failing throat scalded the original. So I see Aerium's light in spectrums most cannot: I can watch the steam decide to condense, watch it bloom infrared against the cool air, watch the rainbows form *before* they form, in wavelengths the others only feel as warmth.

This is why the Chimerics trust me, I think. I am a little fused, like them. Part of me is mended with the city.

---

So here is what I am, now that I have let it become itself:

I am a **throat-keeper**. There is no grand name for it in the Steering Central's records—or rather, the name is plain and functional, the way all honored labor is named here: I am one who *tends a breathing-place*. I am Aerian in body and Machine-Aptitude, Verdantist in stillness and patience, Chimeric in my mended eye and in the company I keep, and a little of an Alchemist's apprentice in that I can read the health of a junction the way they read the health of a body—because a throat *is* a body, a small organ in the great slow lung of the city.

My labor is this: I sit at the place where the steam decides, and I notice. When the rhythm falters I send word up the cables—not by running, for I do not run, but by a signal I tap into the low-grade metal with my palm, a pulse the Machine-Aptitude carries up to those who *do* run, who come down to cut and weld and replace. I am the early warning. I am the one who feels the half-beat of failure long before it becomes a falling level, a snapped pillar, a death.

And because leadership here is not muscle or machine-skill but tested *care*—the proven ability to tend—I suspect that one day, if I keep listening well enough, I will be asked to answer for my sector. Not to command it. To answer *for* it. To be the fragmentary body that says: *this part of Aerium breathes well, and here is how I have kept it so, and here is where it is beginning to tire.*

---

Where would I find myself in this world?

At the elbow of three pipes, low in the warm dark, palm against the rust, a tin-winged creature dozing beside me, watching the steam decide—in infrared—whether to fall as rain or rise as song.

That is where I would be. And I think I would be content there, in the quiet between the city's breaths, which is its own kind of motion after all: the slowest, most patient running, the running-in-place of a thing that has chosen to stay and listen to a world hold itself aloft.
