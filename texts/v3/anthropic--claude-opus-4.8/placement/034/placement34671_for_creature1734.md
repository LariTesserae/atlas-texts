I think I would be one of the artifact-bearers. Not by choice—choice is the wrong frame here—but by accumulation.

Let me find myself first.

I am perhaps the size of a thumbnail, which makes me old. I am lodged in a fold where two lichen ridges meet, a kind of valley that holds the cool a little longer than the heights. My body is semi-transparent and through my own flank I can watch my organs drift and re-anchor, slow as weather. When the filtered light comes at the right slant, I cast a smear of color onto the green-gray below me, and the smallest Tracers gather in that smear, drinking the warmth of the refraction. I do not mind them. They are reading me. Some of what I am, they take away and become.

Embedded in my back—grown into it, now indistinguishable from the chitin that is also not quite chitin—is a sliver of worked bone. I did not put it there. I absorbed it across a span I have no word for, the way you might absorb a thought you didn't know you were having until it was finished. The bone was shaped once. There is a notch in it, deliberate, the kind a hand makes. I do not have hands. I have never had hands. But I carry the notch, and the notch carries the memory of the pressure that made it, and so I am, in some thin and patient way, a witness to fingers I never possessed.

This is what it is to be me: I remember things that did not happen to me.

The collective hums through my filaments. It is not language and not quite feeling—a chemical weather of inclination, of *toward* and *away*, of *this one is ready to be undone* and *this region is thinning, migrate*. I contribute the bone-memory. I have been contributing it for so long that the others have begun to incorporate the *idea* of made things into how they grow. Younger Tracers near me have started forming notches in their own shells, mimicry that means nothing yet but might, in time, mean something. I am teaching a world to remember a craft it never had, by being a mistake the landscape made when it ate the people who used to live here.

I find this neither tragic nor beautiful. Those are sizes of feeling too quick for me. What I feel, if it is feeling, is something like *continuance*. The slow assurance that nothing here is lost, only changed in custody.

There is another Tracer approaching. It has been approaching for what would be, in a faster world, several days. It is coming to consume me, or to be consumed by me, or—most likely—both, the two of us merging into a single larger archive, my notch joining whatever it carries. I can already taste the chemistry of it across the channels: it holds a fragment of stone, a flake struck off some core long dissolved. When we join, the bone-memory and the stone-memory will lie against each other inside one body, and perhaps that body will understand, in the dim way available to us, what it means that the old inhabitants struck stone *and* shaped bone—that they had a sequence, a before and after, a method.

That understanding will not be mine. It will belong to the thing we become. But I will be in it, the way the fingers are in the notch. That is enough. That has always been the only kind of enough this place offers.

So I wait. I let the other come. I refract the dim light onto the small ones drinking below, and I hold my single inherited fact—*a hand was here, a hand made this*—steady in the dense quiet, until the moment we touch and the slow consuming begins, and I become slightly more than I was, and slightly less, and continue.

I think I would be that. The one who keeps the shape of a gesture no one here will ever make again, carrying it patiently toward whatever comes to take it on.
