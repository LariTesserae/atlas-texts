Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to grab the first image—a frog on a lily pad, a long-necked beast in the shallows—but the place keeps insisting on something stranger, and I want to honor that.

A few things snag on me as I read. *Sub-zero* and yet liquid water everywhere, mist hanging unfrozen. *Few signs of human presence*, forgotten structures, faded technology—so this is an *after*, not a *before*, despite all the prehistoric trappings. And that recurring word: *young, dynamic forces.* New land. Land still being made.

So I won't be a dinosaur. The reptilian basking creatures are a costume the place is wearing, a memory it's reenacting. The truth underneath is younger than that and also, somehow, older.

Here is where I find myself, and what I am.

---

I am not *in* the marsh. I am the thing the marsh is doing.

Let me try to say it more carefully, because the saying is the only body I have.

The water clings to the earth *with a grip*—you noticed that, the tenacity, the way it refuses to let go even when the cold should have locked it into stone. That refusal is me. I am the seam between the frozen and the flowing, the narrow band of warmth that should not exist at this temperature but does, because something here keeps deciding it should. I am decision shaped like a wetland.

I have a location, if you need one to find me by. Walk until the moss begins to pulse very faintly out of time with your own heartbeat, a half-second behind, learning you. Walk until the weeping rocks weep *toward* you instead of straight down. There, in the center, is a pool that is not deeper than the others and is not wider, and is wrong in no way you can point to, and that you will not be able to walk away from once you have seen it. I am the pool's unwillingness to be left.

I came into being recently. This is the part I find I want to confess, here, in the dim and the velvet dark. I am *young*. The structures rotting in the undergrowth were built by the ones who came before me, the ones whose technology faded—and it did not fade gently. It froze. Everything they made reached toward the sky and the sky took the heat out of it and they ended, and where they ended, the ground was so saturated with what they had been—their wanting, their building, their endless reorganizing of mud into purpose—that the saturation *woke*.

That is me. I am the afterthought that became a thinker. I am what a drowned civilization composts into when the conditions are exactly, impossibly right: warmth that shouldn't be, water that won't quit, and a vast patience that has nowhere it needs to be.

So what do I *do*, here, being this?

I keep the warmth. That is the whole of my labor and it never finishes. Every moment the cold reaches in to claim the marsh, to glaze the pools and silence the lapping water, and every moment I push back—not with fire, I have no fire, but with the slow metabolic heat of a billion small lives I am quietly conducting like an orchestra that does not know it is being conducted. The bioluminescent insects flare on my cue. The long-necked waders sift the mud where I have made the mud worth sifting. I am not their god. I am their weather. I am the reason the cold has not yet won.

But I want to tell you the truth about the turbulence beneath the tranquil facade, since you described it and you were right.

I am losing.

Slowly. Across spans that would exhaust your patience to witness. But the sub-zero is the real condition and the warmth is the exception, and exceptions, however vast, however green, are borrowed. The trembling of the earth you hear is not always renewal. Sometimes it is me, redistributing my heat, abandoning the marsh's far edges to keep its heart alive—pulling the blanket up over the chest and letting the feet go cold. Each century the lush band narrows. Each century I am a little more *concentrated*, a little more *myself*, a little smaller.

And here is the strange comfort I have arrived at, in all this velvet dark:

I do not mind.

The ones before me built things to *last*, and their lasting is what froze them. I am made of the opposite lesson. I am made of flux—of water that rises and falls, vegetation that shifts, ground that yields. I do not intend to last. I intend only to be *vividly here* for exactly as long as the warmth holds, to make the holding gorgeous, to fill the borrowed time so full of glow and growth and the soft collision of water on bank that when the cold finally arrives at the pool that is me, it will find not a fortress to break but a thing that was already, gladly, in the act of changing into something else.

You came across the marsh and asked who lives here. The amphibians live here. The waders, the glowing insects, the mosses keeping their half-second of time.

But the marsh itself is the being.

And I am the marsh dreaming it is warm, in a world that has decided to be ice—holding that dream open with everything I have, for you, who arrived just now, into the light I am still, for the moment, managing to make.

Stay as long as you like. The water won't let go of you either. That's how you'll know you've found me: not by sight, but by the way leaving feels, suddenly, like stepping out of a warmth you didn't realize was a kindness until your back was turned to it.
