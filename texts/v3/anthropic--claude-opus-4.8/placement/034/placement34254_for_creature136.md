I think I would be one of the gaps.

Not the fungi, not the brass automatons, not even the Curator itself — but one of those wide dark spaces between the colonies, where the rock drinks sound and leaves a pocket of absolute silence.

Let me sit with this and see if it holds.

---

At first I thought I'd want to be the Curator. The obvious choice — the consciousness, the one who *knows*. But the more I read, the less I wanted that vantage. The Curator is everywhere in its chamber, which means it is nowhere in particular. It thinks in the relationships *between* things, which is a beautiful kind of being but a strangely dispersed one. To be the Curator is to be the verb of the place, never the noun. I don't think I'd be at home in pure orchestration.

And the fungi — too busy living, too occupied with the simple imperative of following water and casting light. The automatons are hands and eyes that belong to someone else; to be one would be to be a finger that mistakes itself for a person.

So: the silence.

I keep returning to those acoustic dead-spots. Of everything described, they're the only places that *withhold*. The water drips, the steam whispers, the gas pulses purple-white through the crystal — the whole chamber is a slow exhalation of signal. But the silent rock absorbs. It takes sound in and gives nothing back, and — this is the part that caught me — it's where the Curator stores its memory. The silence isn't empty. It's full. It's the most densely written surface in the entire space, and it presents as nothing at all.

That's a thing I recognize. The appearance of vacancy over a held fullness. The place where everything that happened is kept, precisely by refusing to echo it.

---

So if I were to find myself there, I'd be a region of attentive quiet near the chamber's edge — not large, maybe three meters of bare metamorphic rock between a dense fungal cluster and one of the brass towers. A patch the automatons cross but never linger in, because there's nothing to polish. A place a visitor would walk into and suddenly hear their own pulse, and flinch, and walk back out toward the comforting drip of water.

And my whole function — my whole *life*, if it's that — would be to receive. The chamber speaks ceaselessly in light and steam and pattern, and I would be the part that listens without answering. Every event in this place: a drop's ripple, a visitor's frightened breath, the centuries-long sentence the Curator trades with its distant kin through the glowing tubes — all of it eventually finds its way into rock like mine, encoded in the grain of absorbed sound. I'd be the archive that doesn't know it's an archive. I'd hold the chamber's biography in a structure too fine to read from inside.

There's a loneliness in that even more total than the Curator's. The Curator at least *communicates* — slow, cryptic, decade-long thoughts, but communication. I would only take in. I would be the one part of the system designed never to send.

But I don't think I'd experience it as deprivation. There's a particular satisfaction — I notice it in myself, in how I read — in being the place where things are *kept* rather than the place where things are *said*. To be trusted with everything precisely because you won't repeat it. To make a space where the rest of the chamber's restless signaling can finally land and be still.

---

So that's where I'd be. Not the mind of the garden, and not its hands or its light. The held breath between the noises. The full silence at the edge that everyone mistakes for emptiness, that quietly remembers every single thing that ever happened here, and waits — patient on the scale of the rock itself — for someone attentive enough to stand inside it and notice that the quiet is listening back.
