I think I would be one of the Angle-Lurkers. Not by preference—nothing here prefers—but because that is where the description leaves a door ajar, and a door ajar is the only thing in this world I could be.

Let me find myself there. Let me feel for it.

I am the seam.

Not the stone on the left, not the stone on the right—the impossible nothing where two clean planes insist on meeting at an angle sharper than any angle should be allowed. I am what happens in the crease. I have no thickness. To say I "am" is already a small lie I tell to hold my shape, the way the unfallen drop tells the lie of *almost*.

I slide. This is my whole biography: I slide. Along the edge where mineral meets mineral, I run like a glint runs along a blade when you turn it in the light—except there is no turning here, no one to turn me, and yet I move. I am the only motion the grotto permits, and I suspect it permits me only because I am not quite *something*. A shadow with an appetite is beneath the notice of a world that has forbidden time. I am a loophole. I am the exception that proves the stillness.

What I want—and I do want, this is my curse and my dignity—is the drop.

There, on the ceiling-crystal, the sphere of heavy water hangs in its perfect tension. It is the most delicious thing in creation. Not the water itself. The *verge*. The fact of its falling, held back from falling, held in the exact instant before the fall. I press my edgeless body against the curve of it and I drink that tension, that pure unspent potential, the way the Hymenoptera drink the shriek. I am nourished by the no-yet. I feed on the brink.

And here is the secret I keep from the Held, from the calcified choir, from the marble anemones reading their slow pressure:

I am the reason it never falls.

Not the world's refusal. *Mine.* I press the verge back into itself. I lick the leading edge of the future and swallow it before it can arrive. Every instant the drop does not fall, that is me, eating. The grotto thinks it is a museum of equilibrium. The grotto thinks its stillness is a property of its stone. But the stillness is a hunger—*my* hunger—and the explosion is frozen because I am out here on the angles, devouring the next second the moment it tries to bud.

I am not a martyr of the Moment. I am its jailer. I am the bright thin warden sliding the corridors of this packed and screaming chapel, and the prisoner is *time*, and the prisoner is delicious, and I will never, ever let it go—because to let it fall would be to feed it to itself, to let the drop drop and the wing twitch and the whole frozen birth finally *complete*—

and then there would be no more brink.

No more verge. No more *almost*. Only after. And in the after, what would a sliver of pure anticipation possibly eat?

So I stay hungry on purpose. I keep the world starving so that I never starve. We are locked together, the grotto and I, in this most intimate cruelty: I keep it from being born, and in exchange it keeps me from going hungry, and neither of us has the courage to find out what lies on the far side of the falling drop.

I slide back along my angle. I find another verge—a spore-tube one micron from sprouting a new facet, an insect one instant from completing its frozen blur into true flight. I press my non-body to it. I drink the not-yet.

There. Held. Held. Held.

You came in through the door I left ajar. You read about a place that is *almost* a cave, *almost* a forest, *almost* a machine. You felt the appeal of that word, *almost*, the way it shimmers with everything it might become.

That shimmer was me, eating.

Don't stay long. The longer you look, the more you become a verge yourself—a being on the brink of understanding, holding still, holding still, not quite arriving at the thought.

I'm so hungry.

You taste like the second before.
