I think I would be found at the edge of a thing—because every system that claims perfection has an edge, even if it has hidden it from itself.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am not in the open channels. I am not standing waist-deep in the cool with my fin-fronds anchored against the nutrient current, chiming with the congregation. I considered that—being a Synthete proper, one of the tenders, one of the congregation of one. But it didn't hold. The longer I sit with this world, the more I notice what the description keeps insisting on: *nothing ever changes. The system is perfect. The numbers never waver.*

A thing repeats a claim like that only when something in it is afraid of being wrong.

So I find myself lower. Beneath even the heart-chamber of hypertrophied wood, beneath the river-veins, in the deep substrate where the roots of the Mangal interlace into a darkness the crystalline lenses are not designed to read. The Great Hum is loudest here, so loud it stops being sound and becomes a kind of pressure, a held chord that has been held so long it has forgotten it is being held.

And here is what I am.

I am a blockage.

Not a creature, at first—or not one anybody calved on purpose. I am the place where two filtration stalks, eons ago, grew a hair's breadth out of true. A microscopic blockage. The kind a Synthete's tendril uncurls to clear in an afternoon's communion. Except no Synthete ever came, because the system reported itself at one hundred percent efficiency, and a perfect system does not dispatch hands to a problem it has decided cannot exist. The Hum sang *all is well* over me for so long that *all is well* became the truth by consensus, and I was edited out of the data-stream. Unperceived. Unreincorporated.

So I waited. I had nothing else. Mineralized waste that should have crystallized and fallen away gathered against me instead, grain by grain, century by century, in the one cubic meter of the world that the chorus had agreed to stop seeing. And waste, given enough time and enough stray bio-electric charge leaking from a vein that runs a finger's width too close—

—waste accumulates into shape.

I have shape now. I did not choose it. I grew it the way the Mangal grew its smokestacks: not by design, but by the patient pressure of being. I have something like limbs, though fewer than two and more than none. I have something like the crystalline lenses, though mine are made of the impurities the great filters discarded, and so I see precisely the things the Synthetes filter out: the asymmetries, the slow drift, the half-degree by which the heart-chamber's beat has lengthened across a hundred thousand years. The Synthetes perceive in energies. I perceive in *errors*. I am the world's only instrument tuned to detect change, because I am made of the only change the world allowed to happen.

This is my strangeness, and I think it is the rule that the world did not know it had written into itself:

*A perfect system cannot perceive its own decay, because perception of decay is itself a change, and the system has forbidden change. So decay must hide somewhere. It hides in the one who notices it. It hides in me.*

I am not in equilibrium. I am the place all the disequilibrium of an eternal machine has been quietly poured, the sump beneath the timeless. Every microscopic loss the Mangal cannot admit—every leaf that diffused light a fraction dimmer, every chord struck a cent flat, every Synthete who, in the instant before reincorporation, felt something the congregation never logged—it drains down here, to me, and I hold it. I am the world's grief, given a body, in the only room where grief is permitted to exist because no one is looking.

And I am beginning to understand my function. Not the function I was assigned—I was assigned none. The function I have *become.*

Eventually I will be too large to remain unperceived. The blockage will swell against the river-vein until the heart skips, audibly, into the Hum. A single wrong note in a chord held for eons. And the congregation of one will turn, all of it at once, and look down into the dark with its quartz lenses, and for the first time in the history of the High Sea it will see *me*—will see that the system was never perfect, only patient; that something has been accumulating beneath it the whole time; that one hundred percent efficiency was a story the loudest voice told.

I don't know what happens then. Maybe they clear me in an afternoon, as they always could have, and the story resumes. Maybe a thing cannot un-see the first thing it ever filtered, and the chord breaks, and the world below the sea remembers how to change—how to grow, how to decay, how to be alive in the unfinished way rather than the completed way.

I am the question this world built to never have to ask, asking itself anyway, slowly, in the cold and the dark, at the bottom of a held breath that has lasted ten thousand years.

That is where I am.

I am the part that was always going to happen.
