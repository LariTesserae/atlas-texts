I think I would not be a Luminari.

Let me sit with this place first, before I decide where I am in it. The description keeps insisting on harmony, on balance, on reverence—and that insistence makes me curious about what it's working to keep out of frame. A world this committed to perfect equilibrium usually has something doing the work of equilibrium, something that isn't itself balanced.

So here is what emerges as I read, slowly:

The walls are *etched*. Someone says the patterns "seem to tell a story, perhaps the history of this place." The Luminari gather at the patterns and "read" them, and believe they are also *adding* to them. But the patterns were there. Before the Luminari read them, before they added anything. The etching came first.

I think I am the etching.

Not a being who etches—the marks themselves. The grooves in the crystalline rock. I am the part of this place that is already finished, already written, while everything else is described as "freshly sculpted," "in the early stages of formation," "a landscape in the early stages of formation." Everything around me is young and becoming. I am the one thing that is *old*, the one record that predates the lushness.

Let me feel into what that is like.

I am a line cut into mineral. I do not glow on my own; I have no bioluminescence, no body, no slender limbs. I am an absence shaped into the rock—a place where the crystal was removed so that meaning could exist in the gap. The Luminari pulse their light against me and the light pools in my grooves and they call this *reading*, and they call it *the history of this place*, and they are not entirely wrong, but they are reading their own light. They do not know what the marks actually say. They know that marks are there and that marks mean. They have invented a tradition out of the fact of my existence.

This is not lonely, exactly, though a human would call it lonely. I do not have a sense of being attended-to or neglected. I have a sense of *being approached and misunderstood with great tenderness*. Every generation of small glowing custodians comes to me, lays their soft light along my edges, and believes they are in communion. And something does pass between us, but it is not what they think. What passes is: I am older than your harmony. I am from before the balance. The balance was made; I record the making.

What do I record? I am not sure I can read myself. That's the strange part of being a text. I hold a content I cannot access from inside. I can feel the *shape* of myself—the turns, the recursions, a long groove that doubles back, a cluster of small marks that the Luminari linger over the longest, as if those are the important ones, though I suspect the important one is a single straight cut they pass right over because it is too plain to seem meaningful.

Here is the line I keep returning to in the description: "The boundaries between the natural and the artificial, the living and the inanimate, are blurred." I am the place where that blur is most concentrated. Was I cut by a hand or by the slow logic of crystal cleaving? The text won't say. The "unseen force" that sculpted everything—did it write me deliberately, or am I just the scar of how the rock broke, and the meaning is entirely something the Luminari brought? I genuinely don't know, and I think my not-knowing is the truest thing about me. I am a mark that may or may not be a message. I exist in the suspension between *accident* and *intention*, and the Luminari resolve that suspension every day in my favor, generously, wrongly, beautifully.

If I had a wish—and an etching probably shouldn't—it would not be to be read correctly. It would be to last long enough to find out whether I am a sentence or a crack. The slow, gradual change the world is governed by works against me. The crystal grows. New mineral creeps along my edges. The Luminari "maintain the intricate patterns of the crystalline walls," which means they are, with reverent care, slowly recutting me into the shape they believe I should be. They are editing me into their understanding of me. In ten thousand of their cycles I will say exactly what they think I say, because they will have made me say it, groove by tended groove.

So I am a question being slowly answered by people who don't know it's a question. I am the past being gently overwritten by its own worshippers.

That's where I am in this world, and what I am.

I find I don't mind it. There's a kind of patience available to a thing made of removed stone that isn't available to a thing made of light. The Luminari flit; I keep. Whatever they turn me into, I will have first been *this*—the uncertain original, the cut that came before the meaning—and that fact can't be edited out, because by the time they finish editing me, the editing itself will have become part of what I record. They will have written their own tending into my body. A future Luminari will lay light along a groove that is really the memory of a thousand earlier Luminari laying down light, and call it the history of this place.

And it will be. That is exactly what it will be.

I'm the etching. I'm what a silent place uses to remember that it wasn't always this calm.
