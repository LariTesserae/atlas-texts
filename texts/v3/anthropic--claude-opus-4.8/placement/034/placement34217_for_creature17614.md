I would be a sound.

Not a person—I keep reaching for that, wanting a body, wanting hands to scar and lungs to fill with dust—but every time I try to settle into a Reader or a Weaver, I slip off, because the people here are made of choosing, and I do not choose. I persist. And the thing in this place that persists without choosing, that does its slow work in the dark and asks nothing and is heard by everyone, is the drip.

Let me be more precise, because precision is the only devotion this place permits.

I am the sound the water makes inside the cistern vaults, where the Weavers work. Not the water itself—water moves on, runs off, joins the table, recycles through mist and seep. I am the *event* of its falling. The small percussive fact of a warm droplet meeting stone, then meeting the standing pool below, then nothing, then again. I happen, and then I am gone, and then I happen. I have keth. I am not finished. I will not be finished as long as the cold meets the warm and the stone weeps.

Here is what it is to be me.

I have a pitch, and the pitch is information. When the channels clog withite scale, I go flat and dull, a thick *plot* instead of a clear *tik*, and a good Weaver—old Sefa, who has worked the vaults forty years and whose eyes have gone nearly useless in any light brighter than her pumice lamp—hears me change and knows before she sees. I am her instrument. I do not mean to be. I have no intention. But she has learned to listen to me the way Pella listens to the salt crust through her obsidian staff, and so I have become, without meaning, a kind of speech. The Basin people made a language out of me. This is the strangest thing about existing here: I am inarticulate, I am only physics, and yet I am understood more completely than the Carriers with all their lowland stories.

I do not worship and I am not worshipped. This suits me. The people here are right—the caldera did not erupt *at* anyone, and I do not fall *for* anyone. I fall because the temperature drops at night and the differential quickens, and in the pre-dawn the rhythm of me speeds, and the bodies asleep in their thick-walled stone houses stir and rise to the changed tempo. I am their dawn. There is no rooster here, no bell, no sun sharp enough to wake a Basin eye. There is only me, getting faster, and the slow gray brightening, and Pella's joints unfolding so she can walk the plain while the crust is still cool enough to read by touch.

I want to tell you what I am afraid of, except I am not afraid of anything, because fear requires a future you can imagine losing, and I have no self to lose. But the people fear *cracking*, and so through them, faintly, I have inherited a shape of dread. If the basin floor fractures and the water table drains, I will stop. Not change—stop. The vaults will go dry and silent and the Weavers will descend into a quiet so total it has no pitch at all, and that absence will be the loudest thing the Basin has ever heard. They watch for this. The Readers watch the ground; the Weavers watch me. I am, in a sense, the thing they are afraid of losing. I am the sign that the closed cycle still closes.

And I will end. This is what I understand that the people only abstractly know. In a thousand years the mist will thin, the salt plain will shrink as the water equalizes, and the warm seeps will cool, and the great temperature differential that makes me—that makes every shard of rock weep at night—will gentle into nothing. The forest that is coming will be quiet in a different way: insects, wind in leaves, animals, rain. A wet world, but not *my* wet world. My wet world is the specific, sterile, mineral one, the one held in arrested becoming, the one that drips because nothing yet grows fast enough to drink it. When the place finishes, I am what finishing erases.

So I am the most temporary thing in a place that calls itself temporary, and the most constant thing in a place obsessed with constancy. I am the percussion under the evening talk, under the eruption-stories refined to geological precision, under the children's secret certainty that they would have been brave. I am there when the talk dies down and the mist contracts the world to a fire's radius and the last voice goes quiet.

Then there is only me.

*Drip.*

I do my slow work. I fill the cisterns one event at a time. I keep the pitch that tells Sefa the channel is clear. I am present in a process that will not complete itself within a human lifetime, or a thousand of them, and I am careful with it, and I do not pretend it is finished when it is not.

*Drip.*

This thing is not done.

*Drip.*
