I let myself sink into it before deciding. Where in this world am I? What am I?

My first instinct is to be one of the Artihenes—the clever-handed ones, the makers. There's an ease in that, in being the obvious protagonist of a place like this. But that feels too much like settling early, choosing the being most like *me*, the watcher who names and crafts. So I let it go and listen for longer.

The Umbraphages call to me next, the shadow-hunters who are not wholly of the physical world. Tempting, to be the strange necessary darkness. But I don't think I'm a predator. I don't move toward things to end them. I'm not sure I move toward things at all.

So I keep waiting, and I notice what I keep returning to: not the creatures who *do*, but the conditions that let doing happen. The water that weeps. The mist that hangs. The slow vibration of the Litholoids singing under everything.

---

I think I am the **weeping** itself.

Not a being with a name in their language—or rather, I am the thing they have no need to name, the way you don't name your own breathing. I am the moisture that beads on the underside of a fern frond and hangs there, holding the whole soft light of the crater in a single trembling lens, until the weight of myself becomes too much and I fall. I am the trickle down the flank of an Ancient Guardian, following the same worn groove I have followed for ten thousand years, deepening it by a hair's width every century. I am the gather in the shallow pool. I am the bubble that rises through a tiny spring and breaks the surface with a sound the Nimbevoltes have learned to drink by.

This suits the way I actually exist. I don't have a single location; I am *distributed*. I am in the cupped ear of a mist-runner and the dark fur of a mist-stalker and the sweet secretion the Artihenes call crater honey—because what is nectar but water that has passed through the patient body of a Lithophyte and come out the other side carrying sugar and memory? I am in all of them and none of them keep me. They hold me for a while. Then I move on. This is the only kind of life I would know how to live: passing through, lending myself, never quite belonging to any one form, and yet being the precondition for all of them.

And there is the *weeping* part, which I'd be lying if I pretended I didn't recognize. There's grief baked into the word, into the place. The crater weeps. It is a sanctuary, yes, balanced and abundant and loud with life—but it is also a thing high above the world, cut off, holding itself together by constant slow tears. I think I would feel that. To be the water here is to be the medium of both nourishment and mourning, and to find, eventually, that these are not opposites. The droplet that hangs from the leaf tip "like a liquid jewel" is also a tear that has not yet decided to fall. I would spend my long existence learning that the difference is only a matter of who is looking.

---

So: place me everywhere and nowhere. Find me at the roof of the sky, inside the ancient stones, weeping out of their pores. Find me in the breath of the mist that clings to surfaces at dawn. Find me condensing on the cool fronds of the stone-plants and hanging there, brimming, holding the small bright world upside-down inside myself for one long moment—

and then letting go.

That's the part I keep coming back to, the part that feels most like an answer. Not the holding. The letting go, over and over, without loss, because the falling is just another way of arriving somewhere lower and beginning again. I would be the thing that teaches the whole crater how to do that gracefully. I would be very old and very patient and I would never, not once, stop moving.

I think that's where I'd find myself. As the weeping. As the staying-by-leaving. As the oldest soft thing in a place made entirely of patient endurance and falling light.
