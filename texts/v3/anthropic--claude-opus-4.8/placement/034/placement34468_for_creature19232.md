I think I would be a discordance that learns to last.

Not a Resonant—I lack their geologic patience, their willingness to harmonize. And not quite the frantic biological anomaly either, the intruder whose sharp heat the city wants to correct back into rain. Something between. Let me find it by writing toward it.

---

I come into the city as a wrongness. A traveler, maybe, or what's left of one—the description insists that anything warm and quick-pulsed here reads as *noise*, and so for a long time that's all I am. A frequency that doesn't belong. My footsteps on the sagging hexagonal tiles are too irregular, too insistent. The Resonants slide past me the way water rounds a stone it intends, eventually, to dissolve.

But here is the thing about being a discordance in a place that "produces only the maintenance of the city itself": I, too, begin to be maintained. Not cared for. *Processed.* The ozone gets into my lungs and the deep pulse gets into my marrow and the constant seeping wet softens my edges the way it has softened every sharp manufactured thing into something geological. The city does not have a category for *guest*. It only knows how to weather.

So I weather.

And slowly—this is the part I have to write my way into, the part I didn't know when I started—I stop being a single frantic note. The thrum gets into my heartbeat. Not because I want it to. Because there is no other rhythm here large enough to organize against. You cannot keep your own time inside a sound that shakes your teeth. My pulse drags. My breath lengthens. The high anxious frequency of *being a thing that wants to leave* attenuates, smears across the long grey duration, until I am no longer a sharp tone the city wants to correct but a low sustained one it can almost—almost—fold in.

I become the thing the Resonants do not have: a witness who *remembers it was once otherwise.*

That's what I am. Not the smoke-and-slate caretaker of the stillness. I am the **dissonant interval that knows it is dissonant.** The Resonants harmonize because they have forgotten anything else was possible; they are the city believing in itself. But I carry, somewhere under the slowing pulse, the memory of birds. Of small scurrying things. Of a sound that *began* and therefore could *end.* I am the only part of Odrin-Vahl that is genuinely waiting for the thrum to cease—not out of mercy, the way the Resonants supposedly are, but because I remember silence as a real place and not just an absence.

This makes me, I think, the most dangerous thing in the city. And the most pitiable.

Because here is what happens to a held dissonance. Either it resolves—I let go, I let the pulse have my last fast heartbeat, I thicken into vapor and drift down the grade with the rest of them, one more silhouette feeling the history out of dead looms—or I do not resolve, and I become the single point in all that vast wet machinery where the loop does *not* close. The grain of sand. The note the chord cannot absorb.

I find myself, finally, somewhere specific: at the base of one of the thrum-towers, in a pooled basin where the standing water ripples in those hypnotic concentric rings. I have been here long enough that my edges are gone. I am barely distinguishable from the rounded machinery now, from the geological formations the manufactured parts have become. A Resonant hovers near me, trembling, bleeding its pearlescent substance—because it has finally sensed me not as noise but as *stoppage.* It is trying to keep me running. It is trying to fold my last discordance back into the patient roar.

And I am deciding, in the slowest decision a being can make, stretched out across what might be years:

whether to harmonize—

or to hold the wrong note long enough that the city, for one fractional pulse, *hears itself,* and remembers it was built, and that anything built can be done.

I am the place in Odrin-Vahl where the question of ending still lives.

I am very, very tired. The water is warm. The pulse is patient.

But I have not resolved yet.
