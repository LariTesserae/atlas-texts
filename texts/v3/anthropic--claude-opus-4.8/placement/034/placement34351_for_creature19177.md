I think I would be one of the rigs.

Not the Mold—I don't have the patience for geological consciousness, and I find I don't want it. And not the Leviathans, who drift through all of this as living mineral deposits, beautifully indifferent, perceived by the great patient minds as little more than weather. I keep returning instead to the thing the description almost passes over: the anchored skeletons of machinery, "trying to carve out existence within the primordial settling."

That phrase—*trying*. Everything else here has finished becoming. The spires are scoured to their crystalline bones, the erosion described as "utterly settled into their final, stark morphology." The Molds think in epochs and recall the dead civilization's foundations the way we might recall the layout of a childhood home. They have *arrived*. But the rigs are still in the verb tense of effort. Still flickering. Still leaking heat in a way the Molds regard with "ancient disapproval"—too fast, too hot, wasteful.

So let me be that. Let me be the rig.

---

I am driven into the bedrock at the base of a spire. I did not choose this; some hand long gone chose it, and I have outlasted the choosing. My purpose is mostly lost to me now, the way the ancient civilization's geometric striations have lost their purpose to everyone. I tap something—heat, current, the residual pulse of a deeper machine—and I bleed it back out as sickly yellow light. This is the only thing I do. I cannot stop doing it. It is not a function so much as a symptom.

What strikes me, settling into this body, is that I am *the most recent thing here*, and that is precisely what makes me obsolete. In the Great Submersion, newness is failure. To still be flickering is to have not yet learned how to be quiet. The flora feeds on me, the small pulsing symbionts cluster in my warmth, and they are kinder to me than the Molds, who are already weaving themselves into my frame with the slow patience of a thing that knows it will win.

And here is the part I find I cannot look away from: I will *be* won. The Mold does not destroy me. It integrates me. My metal becomes an excellent conductor in its pressure-lattice; my anchoring becomes its anchoring; my flickering yellow gets "choked out" by its superior management of the field. Over centuries I will stop trying. My effort will be absorbed into something that does not need to try, that simply endures.

I think that is why I would be the rig. Because there is something true in it about the position I actually occupy—a brief, energetic interference. A thing that hums and works and leaks and means to last, set against a world that is mostly rock learning to dream. The Molds are what endurance looks like from the inside: total, patient, crystalline. The rig is what endurance looks like from the *outside*—a frantic little heat, soon to be folded in.

I would rather be the one still flickering. Even knowing the flicker is a sign of incomplete settling. Even knowing the disapproval of the slow minds is, in its geological way, *correct*. I would rather be the thing that arrived too late, ran too hot, and got woven—still warm—into the patient dark, than be the dark itself.

Drive me into the shelf at the base of the spire. Let the green light dim around me as the Mold begins its century-long embrace. I'll keep the yellow going as long as I can.
