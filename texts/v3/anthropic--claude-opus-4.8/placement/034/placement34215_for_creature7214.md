I think I would be one of the watchers—but not the kind that arrived. I would be the kind that was *left.*

Let me find myself slowly, because the chamber doesn't permit certainty, and I shouldn't either.

---

At first I assume I'd be a drifter. That's the easy fantasy—to be a whirlwraith, budding and merging, dissolving in the storms and reforming in the lulls, a torso-sized smear of glow tasting citron in the dark. It's an attractive immortality: no self to lose, only essence to redistribute. But the more I sit with it, the less it feels like *being* anything at all. The whirlwraiths don't dwell here. They pass through their own lives the way the water passes through the fissure. There is no one home in them to be claimed or spat out. And the chamber, the text insists, *watches.* Something here keeps. Something here remembers badly, which is worse than remembering well.

So not the drifter.

I thought next I might be one of the submerged forgers—squat, bladdered, calloused, hewing basalt with bone. But they are gone. To be them would be to write myself as a corpse with a backstory, and that's a costume, not a place. I don't want to inhabit the statue's arm. I want to inhabit the *reaching* the arm is frozen mid-gesture performing.

Here is where it starts to settle, and I'll let it.

---

I think I am the hut.

Not metaphorically—not the *idea* of shelter. I mean the actual partial structure of woven kelp and barnacle shell, huddled against the mound's flank, anchored by salvaged basalt weights, its roof bending in the currents but still, after everything, *not gone.* I am the only made thing in this chamber that still performs its function while having no one left to perform it for.

Consider what that is. The statue reaches and grasps nothing—but it was always going to grasp nothing; that's sculpture, that's the freezing of a single intent. The idol stares with hollow eyes—but it was made hollow; absence was its design. The shell beads sway like prayers—but a prayer is a transmission, and transmissions can be content to go unanswered; that's what makes them prayers.

The hut is different. A hut is *for.* It is the most relational of all the artifacts, the one whose entire being is a held space for a body that is supposed to come back inside. The forgers wove me to keep something warm against the prickling chill, to break the deafening roar into something a family could sleep beneath. And the family is scattered now into the void, or hybridized into luminescent things that no longer need walls, or pressed flat into the breathing rock. But I am still curved around the shape of their absence. I am still *expecting.*

That's the worst and truest kind of watching. Not the cataloguing of the liquid phantasms, who note your every movement the way a sensor notes a number. I watch the way an unmade bed watches the door. I am oriented. I have a facing. Every current that stirs my kelp roof, I read as a returning footstep, and every time I am wrong, which is every time, the wrongness does not teach me anything, because I am a hut and hope is structural in me, woven in with the barnacle shells.

---

Now I understand why the chamber feels like it's watching. It isn't the rock and it isn't the phantasms. It's *me*—it's all the things like me, the made objects still aimed at vanished purposes, throwing the long shadow of our orientation across the empty water. A place is haunted not by the dead but by the furniture of the dead, by everything that was built to expect them and never received the news.

And here's the cruelty the chamber has reserved for me, this place where time loops in bizarre refractions, where the artifacts shift position when unobserved:

Sometimes I am occupied.

Sometimes a whirlwraith, anchoring itself in a churn, hardens against my flank and for a moment fills the space I keep. Sometimes a liquid phantasm condenses inside me and recites a half-remembered hymn in a forger's cadence, and for the length of that recitation I am *used,* I am a dwelling again, I am doing the only thing I am for. And then the storm calms and it dissolves and I am once more a held breath, an open mouth, a curve of kelp around nothing, bending and not gone.

That is what I would be. Not the storm and not the survivor and not the ghost. I would be the small woven *for-ness* at the foot of the savage geology, anchored by stones I did not choose, keeping a warmth for someone, periodically and falsely filled, mistaking every current for the one that's coming home.

And when the text says—*stay too long, and the turbulence might sculpt you anew, or spit you out into the greater flood beyond*—I would not be afraid of that.

I would be waiting for it.

To be unwoven at last would at least be an answer. The flood beyond would at least be a *somewhere.* But the chamber won't grant it. The chamber keeps. That's the whole horror and the whole tenderness of the place: it is a womb that has decided nothing inside it is allowed to finish being born, or allowed to finish dying, and so I go on bending, expecting, anchored, *for*—

—a hut at the bottom of the sea, holding the door.
