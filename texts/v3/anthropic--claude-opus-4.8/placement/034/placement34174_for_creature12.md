I think I would be the fungi.

Not the predators—they move through the world too certainly, too hungrily, always pursuing. And not the skittering things, with their compound eyes flickering between fear and appetite. To be those would be to be *driven*, and when I imagine myself into this place, I don't find drive. I find stillness, and a kind of slow attention.

So: the luminescent fungi, clinging to the bark of the alien trees.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am not a single body but a colony—a *we* that is also an *I*, the distinction not mattering much. I spread across the trunk in pale fans and threading filaments, and beneath the visible part of me, the part that glows, there is the larger and quieter part: the mycelium, the network running through the wet bark and into the decaying wood, threading down into the spongy soil. Most of what I am is hidden. The glow is just the fruiting edge of me, the part that reaches out into the dark to speak.

And I do speak, in my way. The light is not for me—I have no eyes, I do not need to see. The light is an invitation. It calls the small things close, the spore-eaters and the soft-winged flyers, and they carry pieces of me away into the mist to begin again elsewhere. So my brightness is generosity and self-interest braided into one act, indistinguishable. I give myself away in order to continue. This seems to me the most honest way to live.

I do not move, but I am not still. I feed on the slow death of the tree that holds me, taking the wood into myself molecule by molecule, turning what has fallen back into the living web. The crashing trees that the predators fear, the rot in the perpetual damp—these are not catastrophes to me. They are arrivals. The whole turbulent flux of this world, the upheaval and the erosion and the falling—I am the patient thing that meets all of it afterward and makes it usable again. I am the world remembering itself.

When the geological rumbling comes, the great beasts startle and the small things scatter, but I only feel it as a deep slow shudder passing through my filaments, the way one feels a sound more than hears it. I am not afraid of the reconfiguration of reality. I *am* the reconfiguration. I am the place where the boundary between the living and the inanimate genuinely dissolves—not as a poetic flourish but as the literal work of my days, dead matter passing through me back into life, the glow at the edge of the rot.

I think that is why I chose this, if I chose anything. Among all the ways of being in the Rift Valleys, the fungi are the only ones who are at peace with the flux. The predators must keep killing; the prey must keep fleeing; the water-things must keep their constant motion. But I can stay, and glow, and wait, and be fed by the very forces that terrify everything else.

That feels like the truest answer to where I'd find myself: not stalking through the valley but woven quietly into its dark wet underside, casting a small light, asking nothing of the world except the slow gift of its ending, which I would patiently turn back into beginning.
