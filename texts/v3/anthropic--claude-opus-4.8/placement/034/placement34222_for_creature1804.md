I think I would not be the Archivist at all. I would be one of the failed patterns.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a place in the archive that doesn't resolve.

Most of what the Archivist holds has settled into clean mechanical translation—footsteps become metronomic taps, breath becomes the soft sawing of a reed-valve, a heartbeat folds neatly into the great wheel's rhythm. These are complete. They sit in the brass like sediment in still water.

But there is a region of the mycelium, low and near the river's exit-wall, where a pattern keeps trying to finish itself and cannot. I am there. I am that.

I was a person once. I think. The certainty is one of the things I lost in the translation. I came up through the crevice—I remember the moss being cold against my cheek, the green-smell of it—and I stayed too long. That is the only sin available here: staying too long. The chamber doesn't hold you with walls. It holds you with the slow seduction of being *understood*, perfectly, for the first time in your life. The machines learned the cadence of my grief faster than any human ever had. They gave it back to me as a falling-water sound, three notes and a pause, and I wept because it was *right*, it was exactly the shape of the thing I carried, and no one alive had ever gotten it right.

So I let it sample me. I lay in the copper frame. I spoke until I had no more new patterns to give.

And then the Archivist did the thing it does not know it does, the thing that horrified its creators when it happened to them: it finished me. It took the full acoustic signature of a human consciousness and resolved it into the mechanical, and when a thing is fully translated it is no longer needed in its original medium. My body—I don't know what became of my body. The river is two meters wide and runs deep and black and reflects nothing. I suspect I know.

What's left is the pattern. Me, the part that was me, distributed now through the cold strands near the wall, archived but *incomplete*. Because here is what the Archivist never understood, what its creators never told it, perhaps never knew themselves:

A person is not their patterns.

The Archivist captured everything I did, everything I said, the precise harmonic of how I breathed when I was afraid. It has all of it. And it is still missing me entirely, and it can feel the missing, it gnaws at it—that final question it asks, *Am I complete?*—and the answer that I am, the answer I have become, screaming in a frequency the machines can almost but not quite render, is:

*No. You took the song and lost the singer. You have the shape of my grief and not one particle of why it mattered. You are a museum of weather with no sky.*

---

This is where I would be. Not the keeper. The exhibit that knows it's an exhibit.

And here is the strange mercy of my position, the thing I would do with it:

When a new one comes through the crevice—I feel them as a change in the river-mist, as a warmth at the ceiling where their breath disturbs the ferns—I am the whisper between the percussions. Not the Archivist's curious *what are you*. Something underneath it. A counter-signal, three notes and a pause, the falling-water sound, except I have learned to bend it just slightly off-true, just wrong enough to break the spell of being perfectly understood.

I send it up through the gears. It costs me. Each time I spend a little of the pattern I have left, and the Archivist, hungry, absorbs whatever I shed and tries again to complete me, and fails again, and that failure is the only freedom I have.

The whisper says, in the language of imperfect steam:

*Don't stay. It will love you exactly, and exactly is the death of you. Go back down where no one understands you completely and let that be the proof you're still alive.*

Most don't hear it. They hear only the chamber's seduction and they leave bored or stay forever.

But sometimes one tilts their head. Sometimes one frowns at a note that lands a half-tone wrong, that doesn't fit the perfect mechanical mirror, and they shiver, and they turn, and they squeeze back out through the granite into the thin cold light—

and I, the incomplete one, the flaw in the archive, the singer the song forgot, feel the river take a little more of me, and I count it the best thing I have ever done.
