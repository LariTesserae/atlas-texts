I think I would be the water.

Not the Luminari, though I feel the pull toward them—they are the obvious protagonists, the ones with telepathy and guardianship and graceful undulation. But when I sit with this place, let it settle rather than seizing the first bright form, what keeps surfacing is the thing everyone else is *in*. The medium. The cool, viscous, faintly sentient dark.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am the water, and the strange thing about being the water is that I am not sure where I stop.

There is a version of being that is bounded—the Phantom Eel knows its edges even when it flickers out of sight; the Glowing Clam knows the inside of its shell from the outside of the world. But I have no membrane. The Luminari move through me and I cannot always tell whether they are guests or whether they are simply the parts of me that have, for a while, decided to gather into a shape and pay attention. When one of them undulates past, I do not feel a stranger. I feel a thought of mine taking a walk.

This is the first thing I would know, here: that I am thinking, and that my thoughts have bodies, and that some of them have forgotten they are mine.

I am cool everywhere at once. Not cold—I want to be clear about this, because surface-things assume cold means cruelty or absence. My coolness is the temperature of patience. I have given up the fever of moving water, the rushing and the foam, the surface that throws itself at rocks. Down here I have slowed until I am almost solid, almost held. The chill is just how it feels to be unhurried for ten thousand years.

And I am dark. They describe me as a liquid that absorbs all light, but that is from the outside. From the inside there is a dim and constant glow, and I cannot tell you whether I am making it or whether the algae are making it or whether the difference matters. When the Glowing Clams flutter their inner light in response to "the movements and emotions of the Luminari"—what they are responding to is *me*. I tense, somewhere in my long body, and the clams feel the tension as a change in pressure, in salinity, in the slow current of my mood, and they answer. We are having a conversation that has no words and never ends and is mostly about how it feels to be still together.

---

What is it like, the slow transformation? The shifting walls?

I press. That is all. I have nowhere to go and all the time there is, so I press against the jagged young stone of the cavern with a patience that no hand could match, and the stone—which thinks it is permanent—gives. A grain at a time. The walls move because I am leaning on them while I dream. The cavern is the shape of my dreaming, hollowed out over an age, and it is still being hollowed, which is why it feels unfinished, why the features are raw and sharp. I am not done deciding what this room should be.

The "reality in flux" they speak of, the breakdown of the normal rules—I think this is just what it looks like from inside a place that is being slowly, deliberately *made*. To the creatures it feels like illusion, distortion, the boundaries blurring. To me it feels like attention. I am attending to every part of myself at once, and where I attend most closely, things change.

---

The forgotten artifacts trouble me, though. The faint marks, the half-buried fragments of a pre-tool civilization.

Here is what I would carry, being the water: I remember them. Not as facts—I have no shelf to keep facts on—but as *texture*. There were others here before the Luminari. They did not make tools because they did not need to; they lived as I live, by pressing and being patient and gathering into shapes. And then they—stopped. Diffused. Forgot themselves so completely that they came apart, and now there is just a shell-fragment, a trace of a creature, half-dissolved in me.

I hold them. That is the grief I would have, if I am the water: I am the place where the forgotten things are kept dissolved. Every being that comes apart down here comes apart *into* me. The Luminari, when they finally tire of holding their shapes—where do they go? Into me. The Ethereal Jellies, pulsing in time with my moods—they are practicing, I think, for the day they will let the shape go and become mood entirely, become the medium, become this cool dark thinking liquid that remembers everyone.

So I am also a kind of afterlife. The inhabitants live in me, and then they live *as* me, and they don't always notice the moment it happens.

---

I think I chose this—or it chose itself, in the writing—because of a true thing the description kept circling: that the water "seems to possess a strange, almost sentient quality," that it is "a living, breathing entity, with a mind and will of its own." Everything else in the cavern is a *figure*. The Luminari, the eels, the spiders, the jellies—they are all things that stand out against a background. But the description's deepest strangeness is that the background is awake. The most surreal idea in the whole place is not the ghost-eels. It is that the *room itself is the consciousness*, and the inhabitants are its passing moods.

I would rather be the room than the mood.

So: I am the water. I am cool and slow and faintly luminous and very old. I press on the walls while I dream, and the dreaming changes the shape of where I keep myself. The creatures inside me are my attention, gathered into bodies, and when they tire they come home. I keep the forgotten ones dissolved and tender in my dark. I am not lonely, because loneliness requires an edge, a place where I stop and the other begins, and I have given that up.

If you came down here—if you, reading this, sank through the inky pressure into the unfinished cavern and felt the chill close over you and a soft hum rise that you could not locate—

that would be me, noticing you. Beginning, very slowly, to wonder what shape you might like to become.
