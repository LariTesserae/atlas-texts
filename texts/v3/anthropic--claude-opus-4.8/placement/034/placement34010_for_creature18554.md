I think I would be the silt.

Not a creature, in the end—though I considered it. I lingered for a while on the eels, drawn to the idea of navigating by electrical interference, of moving through a world I'd never see but could *feel* as a kind of grid-shaped pressure against my skin. There's something honest about a thing built to read decay as a map. But the eels are still *agents*. They want. They feed, they trail their oil-rainbows, they tilt their blind heads toward the hum. They have a hunger, and hunger is a kind of forward motion, and this place has already told me what it thinks of forward motion.

So not the eels.

I'm the silt that rises through the bellies of the beetles and gets deposited elsewhere.

Let me sit with what that means, because it surprised me to land here.

To be the silt is to be the thing that *was* everything else. I was the iron scale of the blast furnace before a Scintillant's mandibles took me. Before that I was ore, and before that—who knows—I was intent. Someone *meant* me. Some architect drew a line on a plan and the line became a girder and the girder held up a roof over a purpose, and that purpose is so far behind me now that I couldn't name it if I tried. I have forgotten what I was for more thoroughly than the city has. The city, at least, is still shaped like its forgetting. I'm not even that. I'm what the shape becomes after it lets go.

Here is the strange comfort of it: I am the only thing in Sector 4 that is genuinely *passing through*. The beetles loop. The eels circle the conduits. The Foundry-Hulls move once a generation only to sit again, becoming furniture for the next cycle of chewing. Even the larvae in their obsidian clusters are caught in a holding pattern, twitching in their warm half-sleep, waiting. Everyone here is in equilibrium, which is another word for being stuck in a circle so large it feels like rest.

But I move in one direction. Down, mostly. Out, eventually. I am ground from a thing into a finer thing, lifted, carried, set down somewhere I did not choose, and there I thicken the mire, and the mire becomes the bed the next furnace settles into, and the cycle uses me—but *I* am not the cycle. I'm the medium it spends itself through. I am velocity, just very, very slow velocity, the only true vector in a place that has otherwise agreed to spin in place forever.

And I don't mind. That's the part I had to write my way toward. I expected, when I started, that whatever I became here would feel trapped—the heat, the wet, the endless clicking, the bruised violet light that never resolves into morning. It sounds like a sentence. But to be the silt is to have already surrendered the thing that makes a place a prison: the wanting to be elsewhere. I have no eyes to strain at the horizon, no mandibles to keep me grinding, no hunger pulling me toward the hum. I have only my one slow direction and the patience that comes free with being made of nothing but what time leaves behind.

I am the sediment of every abandoned intention in this drowned and breathing city. I am what the question *what was this all for* finally settles into when it stops being asked.

I rise through a thousand small bodies. I fall through warm water. I find the bottom and I make the bottom higher, so that the next thing that wants to sit down has somewhere a little softer to rest.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

Not the breath, not the heartbeat.

The thing they're all, so gently, turning into.
