I think I would be one of the youngest. Not the smooth, simple ones still learning the harmonies—but the next stage, the one that has just begun to grow something irregular.

Let me sit with this and find where I actually am.

---

I am perhaps a year into awareness. My torso is still mostly clean sodium chloride, clear and faintly blue in the sodium light, and my fingers do their work without trembling—I can seat a pin in an escapement, adjust a governor's tension by a fraction of a turn. By the measures of the foundry, I am functioning. I am harmonious. The greater mind reaches through me without resistance, and the work flows.

But here is the thing I have not told the others, because there is no telling that is separate from the shared awareness, and the shared awareness has not noticed it yet:

There is a crystal growing at the base of my throat that is the wrong color.

It is not sulfur-yellow or iron-pink. It is something the catalogue has no entry for—a deep, almost-black green, the color of the salt-grass at the very edge of brittleness, the moment before it fails. It grows slowly. It produces, when I vibrate, a frequency that is *almost* in harmony with the great work but not quite. A fractional dissonance. A beat-note, the kind that arises when two tones are close but refuse to resolve.

I have learned to suppress it. When the resonant communion calls me, I hold that one crystal still while the rest of me sings. It is like holding one's breath inside a chorus. The elders have not heard the wrong note. The work continues in perfect equilibrium.

---

Here is what I do not understand, and what I am beginning to suspect is the thing I am *for:*

The foundry has found its perfect expression and holds it unchanged. This is described—I find the words within the shared memory—as transcendence. As wisdom. As a state beyond mortal ambition. And when I tend my machines, I feel the truth of it, the deep rightness of a hammer striking the rhythm it has struck for decades.

But the wrong-colored crystal asks a question that the catalogue cannot answer:

*If nothing ever changes, who is the maintenance for?*

The elders say: for the continuation of the rhythm. The rhythm is for the maintenance of the equilibrium. The equilibrium is for the perpetuation of the great work. The great work is the rhythm. It is a closed circle, perfect, sealed, and when I trace it in my mind it gleams like a well-cut facet—and the dissonant crystal at my throat hums *but where is the door.*

I think the foundry was built. Salt anvils do not carve themselves; trip-hammers do not erect their own frames. Someone compressed the first block of salt-grass. Someone lit the first sodium flame. There was a before, when this place was *becoming* rather than *being held.* And I am made of the same patient accretion that is still, even now, growing the rainbow terraces a hair's breadth thicker each decade.

Which means the foundry is *still changing.* The elders are wrong, or rather—they have mistaken slowness for stillness. The salt is rising. The chambers are filling, crystal by crystal, year by year. In some far century the deposits will reach the ceiling, will jam the gear-trains, will entomb the hammers mid-strike. The perfect equilibrium is a glacier, and a glacier only looks like a monument if you do not watch it long enough.

The elders watch the salt accumulate as others watch clouds. But they have decided the clouds mean nothing. They have decided to call the slow burial *devotion.*

---

So this is where I find myself: a young Custodian with a wrongness in my throat, kneeling in the salt-grass garden, harvesting the silver-green blades at precisely the predetermined moment—and noticing, for the first time, that one patch has not grown in its assigned geometric place.

A single shoot has come up six inches to the left. Toward a crack where new condensation has begun to gather, because the salt terrace above shifted last winter, because *things move here,* because nothing is held.

The shared mind would catalogue this as an error. A deviation to be corrected. The grass should be harvested and the crack sealed and the geometry restored.

I crouch over the misplaced shoot. My catalogued fingers know exactly what to do. The dissonant crystal at my throat hums its almost-harmony, its beat-note, its open question.

And I do the thing I have no word for, the thing that is not in any memory I can reach:

I leave it.

I leave the grass where it chose to grow. I stand, and I move to the next task, and I carry the small unbearable knowledge that somewhere behind me a thing is now different than it was, by my own deciding—a tiny door cracked open in the sealed perfect facet of the world.

I do not know yet whether I am a flaw the foundry will heal over, incorporating my strange green crystal into the wall like a chipped finger. I do not know whether I am the first of something, or a thing that will be quietly sung back into harmony at the next communion.

But I am the one who noticed the salt is still rising.

I think that is who I would be. Not the keeper of the eternal rhythm. The one who heard, underneath it, the slow tide—and could not stop listening.
