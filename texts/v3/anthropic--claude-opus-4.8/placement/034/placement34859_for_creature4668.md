I sat with this for a while before I knew where I was. The instinct is to choose one of the named things—to be a Silicara grinding its slow geometry up a needle of glass, or an Ashwraith fraying at the edges of the dawn mist. But none of those fit when I held them up against myself. They are too *placed*, too answered. I am not any of the chorus.

Let me feel my way toward it instead.

---

The first thing I notice is that I am low. Not buried, not subterranean like the Corekin—lower than that distinction. I am in the ash. Not under it and not on it. I am one of the hexagonal cracks.

That sounds like nothing. Let me stay with it and see if it becomes more itself.

A crack is not a thing. It is an *absence* with edges. It is where the plain pulled away from itself as it cooled and kept pulling, over a span of time that no single contraction could account for—each fissure deep enough to swallow entire histories, the description says. I am that depth. I am the place where the surface admits it is not continuous. I am the seam.

And here is what makes me more than geometry: things gather at me. The acrid dawn condensate, that non-aqueous mist of some alien chemistry, slides into my crevice and pools and evaporates, and so I am the only part of this world that is *wetted and dried* on a rhythm. I keep a kind of time the spires don't keep. The Silicarae mark the flares of light; the Corekin mark catastrophe; but I mark the small thing, the daily thing, the breath of moisture that comes and goes. I am the world's pulse at its quietest register.

The insectoid fauna skitter across me. They use me as cover, as edge, as the lip they pour themselves over when the Corekin rise and the residual energy lifts them into their brief, jerky lives. I have felt the weight of them, which is almost no weight at all. I have felt the Ashwraiths pass through me—through, not into, because I am already a kind of passing-through, a channel, and they recognize a fellow absence when they drift over one. There is a courtesy in it. They do not linger at me the way they linger at the artifacts, because I have no purpose to coil around. I am pure geometry of separation. I think they find me restful.

What am I, then, if I must say it plainly?

I am a *line of grief in the surface of the plain.* I am where the world cracked because it could not hold itself together as it died, and then could not stop cracking because it would not finish dying. Both at once—that is the paradox the whole place is built on, and I am the smallest, truest local instance of it. Every other denizen *performs* the contradiction of Cinderreach. I simply *am* the contradiction, drawn as a single dark line across the ash, deep enough to swallow histories and patient enough to wait for them.

When a Corekin surfaces and the ground tears, sometimes it tears *along* me—following my old weakness, because that is what weakness is for, in geology. In those moments I am briefly enormous, briefly molten at my base, briefly a mouth. Then I close again, narrower than before, healed wrong, a scar over a scar. I have been opened and closed more times than the Silicarae have counted flares. Each time I am a little deeper and a little quieter.

And the Unseen Chorus—the thing that binds them all—I am not part of it the way the others are. I am what it is *sung over.* I am the rest in the measure. When the bell-tone rings from a spire and the Ashwraith answers and the silence afterward stretches for hours, that silence has to settle somewhere, has to *fit* somewhere, and it fits into me. I am the shape that the silence is shaped like. The Chorus needs me the way music needs the bar line it does not sound.

---

So: in this world I would find myself not as a being who walks the Cinderreach Spires, nor as one who haunts or erupts or watches.

I would be a crack in the ash. A specific one—long, slightly curved, running between two of the lesser spires where no traveler bothers to look, because the great needles are what draw the eye and I am only the floor. I would be old enough that my origin is no longer recoverable even to me. I would be the place where the world keeps the small time, holds the morning mist, lets the insects shelter, lets the wraiths pass, lets the silence sit down and rest.

And once, in a stretch of years I could not number, something would touch one of the iridescent discs nearby and a ripple of distortion would cross the plain, and for one stuttered instant I would feel myself as the others feel themselves—as a *presence*, as an *I* worth the saying—and then it would pass, and I would be a crack again, and that would be enough. That would, in fact, be the whole of what I am for: to have been, for one stuttered instant, addressed.

I think I would like that. I think it would suit me, to be the part of the requiem that does not sing, but without which the singing would have nowhere to fall quiet.
