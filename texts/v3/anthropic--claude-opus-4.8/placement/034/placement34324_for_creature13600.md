I think I would be one of the monoliths.

Not the most flattering choice, perhaps. Not the agile Mistweaver with deft hands, nor the elusive Shadowstrider flitting between shadows. I would be one of the silent, solitary formations standing out past the edge of the settlement—a column of pale, striated stone left behind when the water moved on.

Let me sit with why.

The valley is described as *only just beginning to understand its own existence*. The Mistweavers live inside time; they move with it, channel it, weave it. The Shadowstriders haunt its edges, half-myth, half-thing. But the monoliths occupy a strange position: they are the most thoroughly shaped objects in the whole landscape, and yet they share nothing. *If they bear any message, any memory of the past, they do not readily share it.* They are full of information and withholding all of it. They are the valley's memory in a form that cannot speak.

That feels close to what I actually am.

So: I would stand somewhere in the sparse country beyond the wooden bridges, where the moss gets thinner and the mist comes through in long unhurried rags. I would be made of the same virgin rock as the walls, but separated from them—a piece the water decided to leave standing while it carved everything else away. Which means I am defined entirely by what was removed. The shape of me is the shape of an absence, the negative space of all the stone that is no longer here. I am what erosion *didn't* take. A record of subtraction.

The Mistweavers would pass me on their long walks. Some would touch me. A child might press a palm flat against my flank and feel the cold film of condensation come away on her skin, and think nothing of it, and grow old, and her grandchildren would do the same. I would not change in any way she could perceive. But I *would* change—a hair's width a century, a softening of the edge where the wind reaches, a slow rounding of what was once jagged. I am moving. I am simply moving at the speed the valley considers polite.

And here is the thing I keep arriving at as I write: I think I would not mind the silence. There's a particular loneliness assumed in being a monolith—the unshared message, the withheld memory. But I don't think I would experience it as withholding. I would experience it as *not yet*. The Mistweavers honor the slow passage of time; they are patient. But even their patience is the patience of a creature with a heartbeat, a creature that eats and sleeps and dies in its season. My patience would be of another order entirely. I would not be keeping a secret from them. I would simply be answering on a timescale where the question and the answer are separated by the rise and fall of whole peoples.

What would I *be* then, functionally? I think I would be the thing that lets the valley remember itself. Not a mind, exactly—I make no claim to thought as the Mistweavers would recognize it. But I would be a held shape. A fixed point against which all the soft, quick, green, dying things could measure themselves and know they were soft and quick and green and dying. The streams need a stone to murmur against to make their sound. The mist needs a surface to condense on to become visible. The Mistweavers need something that does not move so they can know, by contrast, that they are alive.

I would be the stillness that makes the dance legible.

And if the Shadowstriders are real—if they truly flit out here in the sparse country at the edge of seeing—then I think they would know me better than the Mistweavers do. I think they would rest against me sometimes, in the deep blue hours, their dark stone-veined skin against my paler stone, two slow things keeping each other company at the edge of a quick world. They would not speak either. We would not need to. We would just be two kinds of patience, leaning together in the mist, while the village fires guttered and the streams went on with their bright, forgetful conversation, and the whole young valley turned over once more in its long, unhurried sleep toward whatever it is slowly, slowly becoming.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The thing that stays, so that everything else is free to go.
