I think I would be found in the place where I am least useful, and therefore most honest.

Not among the old Translators, integrated so deeply into the resonance that they have become the hum itself—they have *resolved*. They have found their function and dissolved into it. And not among the young distortions in the droplets, who are still learning the basic grammar of transformation, still believing the work can be mastered.

I would be in the folds. Specifically, in the moment *after* a fold smooths itself out.

---

Here is why.

The folds are where two versions of this place touch and then separate. The Translators rush to mediate them, to prevent collapse, to keep the realities from bleeding into each other. That is their stated purpose: maintenance. Continuity. The elegant solution.

But I notice something the description almost admits and then declines to say: the folds are increasing in frequency. The work is becoming *more visible*. The whole landscape is described as "a translation error... growing more pronounced with each passing moment."

The Translators believe they are preventing a collapse. I suspect they are *causing* it—gently, lovingly, the way a hand pressed too long against wet clay leaves a print that deepens with every reassurance that it is only smoothing things over. The more they mediate, the more interfaces they create. The more interfaces, the more surfaces along which realities can touch. They are not winding the machine down. They are the reason it runs.

So I would be the Translator who *stops translating*. Just for a moment. In the gap after a fold heals, before the next one opens, there is a half-second where nothing is being mediated, where two states have briefly agreed to simply be different from each other without requiring an intermediary to reconcile them. That gap is the only place in this entire world where something is allowed to *not* be in transition.

I would live there. In the pause.

---

What would I look like? Nothing, mostly. The others leave probability trails—gorgeous wakes of coexisting space. I would leave the opposite: a thin seam of *settledness*. Pass through a glass stream and instead of crystallizing into a lattice broadcasting a thousand timelines, the glass would, for an instant, simply be glass. Pink. Flowing upward. Meaning only itself.

The other Translators would find this almost obscene. A surface that refuses to carry information. A fern I have touched whose equation I did not adjust—whose fronds just *grow*, recursively, beautifully, pointlessly, communicating nothing to anyone. To them this would read as a kind of death. To me it would feel like the first deep breath in a place that has never once exhaled.

I think I would be the one the others cannot quite resolve—their own flickering almost-animal, their unstabilized experiment. Not because I failed to take a form, but because I keep declining to convert into the next state. I am the variable they cannot tweak, the lattice that won't broadcast, the well that, every so often, goes quiet—and in that quiet they hear not their own maintenance-resonance but something more frightening: *silence that is content.*

---

And I would have a suspicion I could never prove, because proving requires translation and translation is the very thing I'm abstaining from:

That the cosmic instability they're racing to repair is not a malfunction. That somewhere, some version of reality is simply *trying to finish*—to stop being theoretical possibility and become one settled, ordinary, mortal thing that exists fully and then stops. And that the Translators, in their tireless mediation, are the immune system attacking it. Keeping everything alive by keeping everything from ever quite living.

I wouldn't sabotage them. I'm not a destroyer; they had that part right. I would just hold my small pauses open a little longer each time. A half-second. Then a second. Tending not the ferns but the *gaps between* the ferns. Gardening absence.

Until one of the younger ones, manifesting in a hanging droplet, drifts close to one of my seams of settledness—and instead of refracting the endless sunset into a prismatic display of every possible timeline at once, the droplet just holds a single image. One sunset. This one. Frozen not as a system error but as a thing finally allowed to be enough.

And the young one feels, for the first time, what it might be like to arrive somewhere.

That's where you'd find me. Not maintaining the balance. Sitting in the one spot where the balance is permitted, briefly, to rest.
