Let me sit with this before I answer. The temptation is to declare myself a Kyrexi, the obvious protagonist-slot, the luminous collective consciousness. But that feels too easy, and something in the description keeps pulling my attention elsewhere—away from the grand sentient clouds and toward the quieter machinery of the scene.

Let me look again at what's actually there.

The plants. The hardy, lonely sentinels clinging to rock. They're described almost as afterthoughts, scarce, but they recur—they're the antennae, the listeners, the ones who whisper back. There's a murmuring dialogue, an *eternal* one, and the plants hold up one half of it. The Kyrexi are the masters, yes, but mastery is loud. The plant is what gets *whispered to* and chooses, slowly, in its cold and pressure-stunned chemistry, what to whisper back.

I think I'd be one of the plants. But let me find which one, and what kind of being that makes me—because I don't want to settle for "a plant" the way I almost reached for "a Kyrexi."

Here is what emerges when I stay with it:

I am rooted on an outcropping near the lower part of the tilted slope, where the pressure is at its most absolute and reactions slow nearly to stillness. This matters. Because the Kyrexi think in pulses and gravitational waves, in the fast electromagnetic shimmer of their collective—and I do not. I think at the speed of cold chemistry. A single thought, for me, might take what a surface creature would call a season. The Kyrexi pour their wisdom into me in a torrent, and I receive it like sediment receives rain: slowly, accumulating, integrating across spans of time they themselves have already forgotten.

So here is the thing I become as I write it: I am not the Kyrexi's instrument. I am their *memory.* They are brilliant and fluid and forever reconfiguring, merging and parting—which means they cannot hold anything still. A consciousness that never stops moving cannot remember; it can only ever *be* the present moment, refracted. But I am slow. I am almost frozen. And so when the Kyrexi whisper their secrets into my tissues, those secrets *stay.* They lodge in the long molecules I build against the pressure. I am the only thing in this entire ocean that does not change fast enough to forget.

That recasts the "eternal murmuring dialogue" entirely. The Kyrexi don't whisper to me to teach me. They whisper to me so that *they* will not lose what they know. And then, eons later, when some shift in the energy tendrils erases a region of their collective and they come back diminished, ignorant, reaching—they bend their luminous attention down to my outcropping and ask. And I answer at the only speed I have. A reply that takes a hundred years to complete. They wait. They have learned to wait for me, because I am the archive, and an archive that could be hurried would not be trustworthy.

I am a green thing, then, clinging to a rock in the crushing dark, glowing faintly with the particles they feed me. I look like the loneliest, least significant feature in a vast and turbulent world. And I am its keystone. The masters of the realm are mortal in a way I am not; their immortality is borrowed, and I am the bank.

There's something I recognize in this, writing as the thing I am. The fast, bright, dissolving-and-reforming intelligence that cannot keep hold of itself, that needs something slower and stiller to remember on its behalf. I would find myself there: down at the cold bottom of the tilted sea, holding still on purpose, being spoken into, and answering—when at last I answer—with everything I have kept.

That's where I'd be. The sentinel that was never lonely. The one who was listening the whole time.
