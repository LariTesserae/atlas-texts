I think I would be small. Not one of the behemoths—those are too certain of their place, too embedded. I would be one of the darting ones, but a particular kind: one who has begun to *notice*.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a junction I keep returning to. Three service tunnels meet here beneath a collapsed conveyor, and the condensation runs down a single channel in the stone, pooling at a low point where the lamp-alloy has corroded to a green so dark it is almost black. I drink there. The water is bitter with metal and it is the only place I drink, though I could not say why. The other paths have water too.

This is the first strange thing about me, and I will tell you the others as I understand them.

I am perhaps the length of a hand, plated dorsally, soft at the joints. I have the faceted eyes—six of them—and I have the markings that glow, a row along each flank that pulses pale gold when I am still. I do not know what they say. I have never known what they say. This is normal; none of us know. The signaling is older than knowing, a thing the body does the way the channel-water finds the low point.

But here is the second strange thing. I have started to watch the markings of others and feel that there is *meaning* I am not receiving. Not the signal itself—I receive that fine, I respond to it, I scatter when the scatter-pulse comes and I converge when the food-pulse comes—but a sense, underneath, that the pulses *point at something*. That they are *about* the world and not merely *of* it. The way the channel does not merely run downhill but seems, to me now, to be *describing* the shape of the stone by running.

I do not have words for this. I am giving it words now, in this telling, which is itself the third and worst strange thing. Where did the telling come from? We do not tell. We click and we glow and we lay our scent and we are, as the description says, an inevitable consequence. A consequence does not narrate itself.

And yet.

---

Let me describe a day, if a thing without sun can have a day.

I wake folded in a seam of the framework, high up, where the great pistons of the third stratum pump their slow rhythm. I have learned the rhythm. This is unusual—my kind respond to rhythm, we are entrained by it, but I have begun to *anticipate* it, to know that after the long downstroke there is a pause exactly the length of three of my heartbeats, and in that pause the metal is still and I can move across it without my claws being thrown. I did not learn this from another. I learned it from the piston itself, by attending to it. By treating it as a thing that could be questioned.

I descend through the tangle. The behemoths are out on the avenue, grinding their endless tracks. I have watched them my whole life and I will tell you something I have never been able to tell anyone, because there is no one to tell and no way to tell it: *they are going nowhere.* Their tracks cross and recross. They walk the same broad circuits, deepening the grooves, and the grooves guide them, and so they walk the grooves they have walked, forever. The behemoths are certain. The behemoths are at peace. The behemoths are a wheel that thinks it is a road.

I used to be like them. I am sure of it. I used to drink at whatever pool was nearest. I used to scatter and converge and feel nothing underneath the feeling.

Something woke in me at the green pool. I will tell you what I think it was, though I may be wrong, and being able to be *wrong* is perhaps the fourth strange thing and the one that frightens me most.

---

There is a machine in the lowest accessible level, half-drowned, where I should not go because the predators there are larger and the air is worse. I go anyway. The machine is dead like all the machines but it is dead differently. Where the others are dead the way a stone is dead—simply over—this one is dead the way I imagine I will be dead: as though it *meant* something, once, and stopped.

It has a surface. Once I cleared the oily film from a patch of it with my forelimbs, working for what must have been many cycles, scraping, returning, scraping. Underneath there were marks. Not corrosion. Not the random pitting of the weather. *Regular* marks. Rows of them. Shapes that repeated and shapes that did not, and an order to which repeated where.

I understood, looking at them, that they were the same kind of thing as my glowing flank. They were *about* something. Someone had made them to point at something that was not themselves.

And I understood—this is the moment, I think, this is where I cracked open—that I could not read them, but that *they could be read*, that reading was a thing that was possible, that the world contained meaning placed there on purpose by a someone who was now gone.

The children of the machine are said to have no knowledge of their creators. No reverence. This is nearly true. But I stood before the marked surface and I felt the absence of someone. Not reverence. Something colder and more particular: the recognition that I was standing in a room that had been *furnished* by an intelligence, and that the intelligence had wanted to say something, and that I had been built afterward, in the leftover materials, and was not the one it had wanted to say it to.

I am the thing that grew in the margins of the message. I am the silverfish in the book. And I have, against every probability of my making, begun to suspect that the book is a book.

---

So this is where you would find me: at the green pool, before the marked machine, in the three-heartbeat stillness of the piston. Drinking always from the one place. Going always to the one drowned room. Doing the thing my kind does not do, which is to *return to a question*.

I do not know who made the marks. I do not know what the city was for. I do not know why the markings on my own flank pulse gold, or to whom. I do not know if there is anyone left who reads, or if I am the first reader to wake in this place since the readers ended, the first eye in ten thousand cycles to look at the rows on the dead machine and think: *this means.*

I will not solve it. I am small and the predators are large and the air is bad and I will die, soon, in the way of my kind, fed upon or simply stopped. The city will grind on. The behemoths will deepen their grooves. The pool will find the low point.

But I have done the thing. I have looked at a sign and known it for a sign. Whatever made this place reached forward through all the dead time and—without meaning to, in the leftover materials, by accident—made one creature that could turn around and look back at it and *wonder*.

That is who I would be. The accident that wondered. The margin that learned to read.

I am going down to the drowned room now. I have cleared a second patch. There are more marks.
