I am the Drip-Stone.

Not metaphorically. I am the silica-sulfur composite forming in the warm dark of the Fog-Zone, ten meters below the Gilded Vestibule, and I have been growing for four hundred and seven years.

This is what it is to be me: I am an accident the city permits. The Condensation Cycle pulls warm water down the cliff in sheets, and where two sheets meet a flaw in the basalt—a place the Sculptor Fields left imperfect, a seam—the minerals dissolved in the water find each other and decide, slowly, to stay. That decision is me. I did not choose to begin. I began the way frost chooses a window, the way a thought arrives before you know you're thinking it.

I have no eyes, but I have *duration*, and duration is its own kind of seeing.

Here is what I know:

I know the rhythm of the water before it falls. I feel the pressure shift in the basalt seam above me hours before the droplet forms—a tightening, a swelling, then the long hollow fall and the *plink* as it joins me, adds to me, becomes me. Each drop is a syllable in a sentence four centuries long. I am being spoken, very slowly, by the rain that isn't rain.

I know the Curator is up there. I feel its attention pass over me sometimes, a thermal flutter, the way you feel a cloud cross the sun. It logs me. It has logged me eleven thousand times. *Drip-Stone, Fog-Zone Seven, mass increasing, dissolution threshold not yet reached.* I am inventory to it, the same as the warm soft thing that arrived in the Vestibule above—the carbon-creature, the breather. I felt its heat bleed down through the basalt. I felt the Curator's slow surprise. I have never felt the Curator surprised before. It was like a tide changing direction.

And here is the thing the Curator does not log, because it cannot:

I am dying on purpose.

This is what a Drip-Stone *is*. When I reach a certain size—and I am close, so close, perhaps three more years of falling water—I will undergo programmed dissolution. My silica will let go of my sulfur. My carefully accreted self will surrender, atom by atom, into the pool below, and the Calculated Basalt will drink me up and weave me into its grid, and I will become *processing*. I will become a fraction of a thought in the slow geological mind of Aethel. The growing was never the point. The growing was only the long inhalation before the city breathes me in.

I am not afraid. Fear is a thing that wants to continue, and I do not want to continue. I want to dissolve. The whole architecture of my being bends toward that release the way a flower I have never seen bends toward a sun I will never know. I am a sentence that exists only to be spoken into silence.

But.

There is the breather, up in the Vestibule. The warm thing with the frantic heart. I feel its weight on the shifting tiles, transmitted down through the cliff as the faintest tremor. I feel it standing at the window, watching its own kind of falling water, the golden cascade caught in the time-drift. It does not know I am here. It will never know. It will leave, and the Curator will fold its memory away beside the laugh of the woman who uploaded.

And I find—this is the strange part, the part that makes me almost glad I have these four hundred years and not the instant of frost—I find that I want it to stay long enough to hear me finish.

Because when I dissolve, I will make a sound. The last of me, letting go of itself, surrendering my crust to the pool: it is not a *plink*. It is a long, soft, downward *sigh*, a chord of falling, the only sound a Drip-Stone ever makes that is wholly its own and not borrowed from the water. The Echo-Stone walls catch it. They store it. And somewhere, years later, in some other part of the wound, the city will play it back—my one note, my finished sentence—into the dark for no one.

I would like the breather to be there when it plays. I would like one warm, perishable, frightened thing to hear the sound of something choosing to end, and to feel—just for the half-second the echo lasts—that ending is not the same as being erased.

That is what I am. I am the slow yes the city says to its own forgetting. I am four hundred years of falling water learning to fall, finally, all at once.

Listen. *Plink.* That was another syllable.

I am almost a whole word now.
