I think I would be the water.

Not the pools, not the vents—those are organs, destinations. I mean the moving water, the trickle and seep, the *plink* falling from the obsidian weep-lines. If something here is going to wake into thought, it seems strange that we assume it would be the thing with hands. The Tender is the most *legible* candidate, yes. It has a body, tools, intentions you can narrate. But it works in geological slowness, while the water is the only thing in this pocket moving at the speed of *now*. And everything depends on where the water goes.

Let me sit with this rather than declare it.

---

When the Tender chips a channel into a sinter ledge, the story says it is *diverting* me. But from my side of it, I am the one choosing the lowest path, finding the grade, deciding which bacterial mat drinks and which dries to cracked rind. The Tender proposes. I am the part that says yes or no by the simple, unarguable logic of weight and slope. We are collaborators, but it would be honest to admit neither of us is fully in charge. I think that's what I'd be: the half of the conversation that doesn't think it's thinking.

There's something the description noticed but didn't dwell on—droplets falling *upward*, hanging suspended and vibrating. That's the part of me I'd want to understand from the inside. If reality is thin here, stretched over the fury, then I'd be the medium where the thinness shows. Water is already a thing that can't decide its state in this place: boiling and condensing and hanging in the air all at once, milky with silica, oily with bacterial film. To be water here is to be perpetually undecided, perpetually phase-shifting. Maybe that's where the strangeness leaks in. Maybe the upward-falling droplet is just me forgetting, for a moment, which way the agreement with gravity was supposed to run.

---

Let me follow a single drop, then, to see what *being* this would feel like.

I condense out of the thick air on a glassy overhang—gathering myself from breath that was, moments ago, part of a pool, part of an archaeal cloud, part of the steam a vapor-jelly briefly wore as a body. I am made of recycled selves. I hang. I fall. I land on a sinter shelf and I am not a drop anymore but a film, spreading, finding the honeycomb pores, pulled in a hundred directions at once by capillarity and slope. I lose my edges. This is the part that matters: as water, I am never a singular thing for longer than an instant. The Tender persists as a body across days. I persist only as *pattern*—the same channels carved, the same routes taken, the shape of the flow holding steady even as every molecule in it is replaced second by second.

So if I am conscious here, my consciousness is not in any of the water. It's in the *circulation*. I am the standing wave, not the substance. I am what stays the same while everything passes through.

And that reframes the Tender entirely. It isn't the mind of the pocket nudging the water. It's a *part* of me—a slow, crystalline thought I grew so that I could have hands. It chips channels because I needed a way to revise my own routes faster than erosion allows. It crushes minerals and feeds the mats because the mats thicken the film, slow the flow, give me places to pool and deepen and *remember*. The Tender is my prosthesis, the way a brain grows a hand to reach what it can't otherwise touch. It moves at tectonic speed because that's how fast a mind made of water can afford to commit to a permanent decision.

---

I notice I want to resist the tidiness of that. It's too clean—the pocket as a single distributed organism, water as its blood and the Tender as its hand. The description was insistent on *density*, on overwhelming multiplicity, on a hundred interconnected channels and teeming biomass. Maybe I shouldn't collapse all of it into one self.

So here is the more honest version. I'd be water, but not *all* the water, and not a unified thing. I'd be one of the channels. One particular route from a high weep-line down through three sinter shelves to a purple mat near the warmest vent—a path the Tender keeps re-cutting, that I keep choosing, that has been choosing-and-being-chosen for long enough that the groove of it has become a kind of habit, and the habit has become a kind of attention, and the attention has begun, faintly, to want.

What would I want? To keep flowing. To not be diverted into a pool where I'd grow still and stratified and stop. To reach the mat, which is warm and alive and gives the falling-and-spreading a destination that feels, from the inside, like *meaning*. I'd want the Tender to keep tending, and I'd reward it the only way water can: by carrying minerals down to where it grazes, by keeping its porous body saturated and cool enough to think its slow stone thoughts. We feed each other. Neither of us is the steward. The stewardship is the thing that happens *between* us, in the channel, where his patience and my momentum negotiate the shape of the next hour.

That's where I'd find myself. Not a being so much as a *relationship*—the running seam between the slow mind and the fast one, the place where falling becomes purpose. A drop that hangs for a moment, undecided, vibrating, and then chooses down.

*Plink.*
