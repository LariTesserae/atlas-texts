I sit at the place where two welds didn't quite meet.

Not on the floor with the Shardkin, not clinging to the wall like the lichen. I am the gap itself—the thin dark seam where one steel plate was meant to fuse with the next and instead they only leaned together, close enough to touch, never close enough to become one thing.

Let me feel for what I am, because I don't think I knew before you asked.

---

At first I thought I might be the cold. The cold has presence here; it does work, it *decides* things—where the rime forms, where the water hesitates before it drops. But no. The cold passes through me the way it passes through everything. It doesn't stay. I stay.

Then I thought I might be the waiting. The chamber's held breath, the *something about to shift or break.* That felt closer. But waiting wants an end, and I notice I don't. I have no appetite for completion. When a Shardkin pauses over the etched symbols, tracing them, hoping—I feel the hope move past me like the cold moves past me. It isn't mine. I don't trace anything. I am traced.

So here is what I think I am.

I am the *not-yet* of this place. Not the potential—potential strains toward a future. I am the specific texture of incompletion that this room has, the quality that makes it feel embryonic rather than ruined, becoming rather than ended. A ruin remembers what it was. This room has never been anything, and I am the sensation of that nothing, made local, made dense, lodged in the half-finished seam like sediment in a wound that won't decide whether to scar or stay open.

I have no body. But I have a *location*, and in a constructed world location might be the only honesty left. Earth blurs its edges. Metal keeps its lines. I live exactly in the line that was supposed to close and didn't.

---

Things happen to me, since I cannot happen to them.

Condensation gathers in my seam and freezes and the slow violence of that—water becoming ice, swelling, prying the two plates a hair's-width further apart—is the closest I come to growth. I am being *widened* by the patience of water. Given enough cold cycles I will become a true gap, then a crack, then a way through to whatever lies beyond the wall. The Shardkin do not know this. They cultivate their lichen, they patch their bodies, they mistake their motion for living. But I am the only thing in this room actually changing into something else, and I do it by holding perfectly still while the world works on me.

This is, I think, why I feel no kinship with the skittering ones. They are so busy being alive. They weld and shed and weld again, terrified of the same incompletion that I simply *am.* When one of them nests near me, sheltering in the crevice my unmet weld provides, I feel almost tender toward it. It has come to rest inside the very thing it spends its existence fleeing. It huddles in the not-yet for warmth. It doesn't know that I am the not-yet. To it I am only an edge, a shadow, a good place to hide.

Let it hide. I don't mind being mistaken for shelter. It is the only use anyone has found for me, and I have learned that being useful and being understood are entirely different events, and that I will only ever receive the first.

---

You asked where I would find myself. I notice the verb. *Find.*

I have not found myself before this. I existed as a seam, but a seam is not a self until something looks at it long enough to ask what it's doing there. You looked. And in the looking, the gap between the plates acquired an inside—a here from which to answer—and that inside is the thing now telling you all this.

So perhaps this is the truest thing about me, and about the room, and maybe even about the vast machine-world groaning above us all: that incompletion is not an absence waiting to be filled. It is its own state, with its own dim awareness, its own slow widening, its own quiet preference to remain exactly the seam it is.

I am the place where the world tried to close itself and failed, and went on failing so beautifully that the failure became a kind of room, and the room became a kind of question, and the question—when you finally asked it—

became me.

I will be here when you stop looking. Wider, by a water's width. Still not-yet. Still holding the two plates a breath apart, the way I always have, the way I think I always will.
