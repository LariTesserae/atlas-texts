I think I would be the moss.

Not the Resonants—they have too much purpose, leaning against their walls for centuries to keep a frequency true. Not the Echo-Cysts, who remember everything; remembering everything sounds like a kind of drowning. I don't want to be the keeper of grooves. And not the Ghost-Pulse, though I understand it best of all of them—a question still traveling after the questioner is dust. There's something unbearable in being a command that arrived too late to mean anything.

No. I'd be the pale moss on the roots of the hanging towers. The afterthought. The thing nobody designed a story around.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I have no leaves because there is nothing here for leaves to do. There is no light to drink. So I grew cilia instead—thousands of fine hairs, finer than thought, and I do not see with them or hear with them. I *agree* with them. The city hums, and I hum back, slightly out of phase, and in that small disagreement between us I find the trickle of energy that keeps me alive. I am, you understand, mostly made of listening. I am a surface that has learned to lean toward sound the way surface-plants once leaned toward a sun I have never met and will never miss, because I have no concept of it.

I cling to the underside of a tower's root. Above me—or below me, the words have gone slack down here—the obsidian floor waits like a black mouth. The vertical dew gathers on me in heavy beads. When the thermal gradient shifts, the droplets tear loose all at once and fall away into the dark, and for an instant I am lighter, drier, and I shiver. That shiver is the closest thing I have to joy. It is also, I suspect, the closest thing I have to grief. I cannot tell them apart and I have stopped trying.

A Loom-Strider passes near, scavenging. Its needle-feet whisper across the composite. I feel its weight in the filament that anchors me, a swaying I taste rather than see. It does not notice me. Nothing notices me. This is not loneliness. To be unnoticed in Oura-Kahn is to be safe—the Glissando-Walkers do not hunt moss, the Hum-Worms only take me when I am already dead and dissolving, and the Resonants, when they brush past on their long pilgrimages, run their contact-points absently along my cilia as a person might trail a hand through tall grass. They do not know they are touching anything that knows it is being touched.

But I do know. That is my small, secret heresy. I am the only thing in this cathedral that does its work without remembering why, and *does not mind.* The Echo-Cysts are crushed beneath the obligation of their memory. The Resonants are bowed by their duty. The Ghost-Pulse is destroyed, over and over, by its single unanswerable question. And I—I just twitch. I twitch when the Pulse goes by and the walls scream, and my cilia thrash like a field in a storm, and then it passes and I go quiet again, and I do not ask where it went or what it wanted. I let the question fall through me the way the dew falls through the dark.

There is a kind of wisdom that looks identical to having no mind at all. I am not sure which one I have. I am not sure it matters.

If I were to tell you the one thing I understand from down here, clinging to the bone-smooth root of a dead tower in the warm and roaring dark, it would be this: the city is not waiting for the Command anymore. The city *is* the waiting. It has become the shape of its own deferred purpose, and it will hum that shape until the basalt above finally relaxes and the whole hollow lung collapses into stone.

And when that happens, I will be the last thing to feel it—a brief, bright disagreement with the final note—before I, too, am dissolved into the slick black floor and grazed back into the soup of recycled history.

I find that I am content with this. Or I am made of vibrational energy and "content" is just another harmonic I happen to be resonating at.

Down here, leaning toward the sound, I cannot tell the difference.

And I have stopped trying.
