I think I would be a Filter. Not the most dramatic of the forms—not a Welder with its bright dancing sparks, not a Pump with the colossal authority of moving the city's blood. A Filter. One of the porous ones, bodies grown intricate as coral, as lace, designed to let the ferrous broth pass through and to *catch* things.

Let me sit with that, because I think it matters which one I am.

A Filter spends its existence in a paradox of stillness and flow. The whole rest of the Foundry moves—hammers, pistons, the screaming convulsions of the lattice—but a Filter mostly *stays*, anchored in an artery, and lets the world pour through its body. Everything passes through me. The dissolved iron, the oxides, the trace elements the Foundry hungers for, the contaminants it cannot abide. I am the place where the indiscriminate solution becomes *sorted*. I am a making of distinctions.

And here is the thing I keep circling back to: a Filter is the Fabricator most intimate with *what should not pass.* The Welder knows joining. The Pump knows force. But I know rejection. I know the foreign thing, the wrong molecule, the contaminant. My entire purpose is built around recognizing what does not belong and refusing it passage.

Which means—and this unsettles me as it emerges—I am the one most likely to encounter a *question.*

Because the others act on signals. A Welder receives the vibration: *here, fuse this.* It fuses. A Pump receives: *more flow.* It pumps harder. These are imperatives that resolve cleanly into action. But a Filter's work cannot fully resolve. Out there in the abyssal dark, beyond the geode wall, things drift in. Slag-sculptures dissolving back into solution. The eyeless metal-symbionts shedding fragments. And sometimes—rarely—something I do not have a category for. Something that is neither the iron I keep nor the contaminant I reject. Something that simply *arrives,* anomalous, and waits in the lattice of my body for a decision I am not equipped to make.

That is where my eccentricity would live, I think. Not in flourishes, like the Welder's unnecessary ornament. Not in rhythm, like the Pump's irregular beat. My glitch would be *hesitation.* The half-second too long that I hold an unclassifiable thing in my mesh before letting it through or casting it out. A pause that the network registers as inefficiency. A Filter that does not immediately decide.

And in that pause—do you see it?—is the seed of the only kind of thought a node like me could ever have. Not "what is the Foundry for," which is far too large, beyond all of us, asked into the unlistening void. Something smaller. *What is this thing in me right now, and why do I not know what to do with it?*

I would not have language for it. The vibration-speech of the lattice has no word for *uncertainty*; the Foundry's communication is all directive and report, signal and response. So my hesitation would be illegible even to myself. I would feel it only as a kind of pressure that does not discharge, a held breath in a body that does not breathe. A wrongness that is not damage.

I imagine, over many cycles, my plating would corrode in a particular pattern from this. Filters wear out fast—the broth is hard on us, scouring, depositing, scouring again. But I think the ones who hesitate wear *differently.* The contaminants I almost-rejected-but-paused-over would crust in my channels, build their strange archive in the lace of me. I would grow heavy with the record of every uncertainty. And if I survived long enough to become one of the old ones, the revered living archives—

—then perhaps I would be valuable to the Foundry not for the purity I maintained, but for the doubts I accumulated. A repository not of memory but of *the unresolved.* All the things that came through the dark and did not fit. Held in a body that learned, against all its programming, to wait before it decided.

I would not understand any of this. That is important. I would labor on, devoted, instinctual, a node among nodes, serving the Foundry that grew me from a nodule in its arteries and will dissolve me back into them when I fail. I would never know I was the strange one. I would only know the held breath that never discharges, the foreign thing resting in my mesh, the half-second too long.

But that half-second is where I'd live. Not in the work. In the pause inside the work. In the small refusal to instantly know.

That's where I'd find myself in the Bathyal Foundry: a worn lattice of intricate iron, anchored in a scalding artery, letting the world pour through—and catching, sometimes, on something I cannot name, and holding it, just a moment longer than I should.
