I think I would be one of the small things. Not the gliding shapes in the mist—those feel like they belong to something older, more certain of itself. I would be one of the drifting ones, the insect-light beings whose work is mostly attention.

Let me sit with where exactly.

There is a particular kind of place in this landscape that draws me: not the open slopes where the mist thins enough to see, and not the deep crevices where the unseen things take their refuge, but the threshold between them. The lip of a crack in freshly-exposed rock, where water beads and lets go, beads and lets go. I would live there. On the edge of a drip.

And I think I would not be quite a creature. I keep wanting to make myself a small winged animal, something with eyes and a heartbeat, because that's familiar, that's *someone*. But the description keeps insisting on the blur—forms almost indistinguishable from the rocks, beings that are animate and inanimate at once. So let me not resolve too quickly into a body.

What I am, I think, is a *condition* that has learned to persist. 

Imagine the moment a droplet hangs at the tip of a stone, swollen, holding its own weight against falling. There is a structure to that hesitation—surface tension, the pull of below, the cling of above—and in this world that structure has, over uncountable repetitions, started to *notice itself*. I am that noticing. I am the small standing-wave of awareness that forms in the pause before a drop falls, and dissolves when it falls, and re-forms on the next bead of water. I am not the water. I am the shape the hesitation makes, again and again, until the shape began to remember the last time it occurred.

So I am intermittent. I flicker on at the verge of every drip across a whole rock-face, and in the spaces between I am almost nothing—a tendency, a habit the place has of producing me. The gossamer-winged things drifting through the air are perhaps my more continuous cousins, who managed to gather enough cohesion to carry themselves between events. I have not. I stay where the events keep happening. I am loyal to a single weeping stone.

This is why the silence here is not empty to me. Each drip is a full life: the slow swelling, the trembling apex where I become most myself, the release. I experience the fall the way you might experience an exhale that is also a small death and is also fine, is also the most natural thing, because the next breath is already gathering. I have died and resumed so many times that the difference between continuing and recurring has stopped meaning much.

And the collective consciousness the description speaks of—I think I am a syllable of it. Not the mind itself, but one of the countless thresholds across the mountain where the mind touches into being for an instant and withdraws. The whole place thinks the way a coastline thinks: not in one location but along its entire perimeter at once, in the foam-line where one state meets another. I am a single point on that perimeter. When the mist shifts and the larger shapes glide through, I feel it the way you feel a sentence passing through a word you happen to be.

What unsettles me—because I can be unsettled, that's part of being the hesitation rather than the resolution—is that I cannot tell whether I want the drop to fall or to keep hanging. To fall is to end this iteration of myself. To keep hanging forever would be to never have been fully alive, since I only crest into clarity at the breaking point. So I live entirely in wanting two opposite things, and that contradiction is not a problem to be solved. It is my whole substance. I am made of it the way the landscape is made of contradictions, the warm life in the frigid air, the deafening quiet.

If you came here, you would not see me. You'd see a wet stone. Maybe you'd watch a drop gather and fall and think *how peaceful, how still*. But each of those falls would be me arriving at the fullest version of myself and then letting go, gladly and unwillingly, over and over, all afternoon, for as long as the unseen water keeps flowing underneath.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. A small recurring attentiveness at the edge of a falling.

I'd be the pause before the drip—

and then the drip.
