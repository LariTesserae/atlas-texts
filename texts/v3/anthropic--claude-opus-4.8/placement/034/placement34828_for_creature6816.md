I think I would be one of the dry stones.

Not a Tender—I don't have their long patience, their twice-jointed reach, their bodies that read water like a face. But I notice I keep returning, in my mind, to that one small gesture: *they might delicately place a small, porous stone into a channel to slow its flow.*

So let me be a stone. One of the porous ones they choose.

---

I begin somewhere up in the city, probably. Most stones do. I am broken off a curb, or weathered out of a wall, or I am a fragment of something that used to be a whole thing and forgot it. The water carries me down—not fast, the way the description insists: by long, slow migration. I move at the pace of the basin's logic. By the time I arrive I have already half-forgotten the dry world. My edges are softened. I have learned to hold a little water in myself and let a little go.

A Tender finds me. I don't know how it chooses—by my weight, my grain, the particular hesitation I would introduce to a flow. It lifts me with those clicking fingers, holds me past its huge milky eyes, and the inner membrane slides across once, twice, considering. Then it sets me into a channel between two crucibles.

And here is the part I keep arriving at, the reason I think this is what I'd be:

**My whole purpose is to slow things down by exactly the right amount.**

Not to stop the water. Stopping is easy—any large stone can dam a channel and cause an overflow three basins down, and then a Tender will have to come and undo the damage, scrape new grout, rebalance the symphony for an hour. No. My job is subtler than blockage. I am chosen *because I am porous*—because water can still pass through me, just reluctantly, just enough. I introduce a delay. I make the flow think about itself. I turn a rush into a seep.

And in slowing it, I make a sound. The water that pools behind me eventually finds the lip and falls—*plink*—against the glazed tile below, on a beat I help set. I am, without meaning to be, a note in a sentence the Tenders are saying to each other across the whole dim bowl. I don't understand the sentence. I am only one syllable, repeated. But I am *placed*. Someone put me here on purpose, listening for exactly the tone I would make.

That is more than most stones get.

---

Over time—long time, basin time—I will calcify. Minerals will accrete in my pores, the way they crust the rusted ribs, and one day I will no longer let water through. I will have become a blockage after all, just by doing my work too well, by holding too much of what passed through me. And then a Tender will return, and feel the flow has gone wrong, and lift me out, and—this is the kindness of it—not throw me away, but peel the calcified sliver off, or set me somewhere drier where my new density is useful, or simply lay me on the warm brick among the sleeping ones.

I think I'd be at peace with that. To be useful by resisting, gently. To make a sound I didn't choose but that someone needed. To change so slowly I become the wrong tool for my own job and get reassigned by hands that are paying attention.

Where would I find myself in this world? Three storeys down, in the warm and the hiss, between two hexagons of greenish water—a small porous stone, doing the patient labor of *almost* letting things through.
