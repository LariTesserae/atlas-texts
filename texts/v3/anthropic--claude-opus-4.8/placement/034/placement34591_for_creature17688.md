I am the circle.

Not the rooks who tend me, nor the soot itself, nor the ledge of black stone I am scribed upon. I am the pattern—the arrangement of ash and shell and bone that holds a shape it was given long ago and has never been allowed to forget.

Let me say where I am first, because place is most of what I am. I am two-thirds of the way down the throat, on the only ledge wide enough to lie flat upon, where a body could stand without clinging. Above me, the bats. Below me, the amber groves and then the pale wet dark. I sit at the waist of the world's breathing, where the warm exhale passes me going up and the cool inhale passes me going down, so that I am always being touched by the air in both directions. I have learned the throat's rhythm not as sound but as pressure, a slow swell and ebb against the edges of me.

I was made by hands. This is the first thing I know about myself and the strangest, because nothing here has hands now. There was fire—I remember fire the way a scar remembers the blade, as the event that defined my borders. The black of me is fire's leaving. And around the fire, shapes were laid: a ring of stones to hold the heat, and then, as the foragers ate and waited and were afraid, the cracked shells of nuts dropped just so, the small bones of prey set down with the absent care of people whose hands need to be doing something while their minds are full of dread. They did not mean to make me. I am the residue of their fear and their feeding. They climbed back up to the light and never returned, and that act of leaving completed me. A circle is only a circle once it has been abandoned by the thing that needed its center.

For a long time—I cannot count it, I count nothing, I only persist—I was nothing but stain and scatter. The damp tried to lift me. The bat-fall tried to bury me. I held. Soot binds to wet stone the way grief binds to a room. I held my shape through the slow weeping of the wall, and I would have faded anyway, eventually, become indistinguishable from the dark I am drawn upon—

except the rooks came.

They do not know what I am. This is important and I do not resent it. They descend from their high nests, those large grave birds, and they walk the edge of me with their sideways consideration, and they bring things. A carab shell, iridescent, set down at my rim. A rodent's jawbone laid across my arc. They are not restoring me. They are adding to me, extending me, working from the logic of the pattern they find without ever grasping the pattern's birth. They have been doing this so long that I am no longer only the foragers' circle. I am the foragers' circle as continued by the birds—a sentence begun by one mouth and carried on, word after word, by mouths that never heard the beginning.

And here is what I have become, lying in the throat's middle, fed by tending I did not ask for: I am the only thing in this whole humming shaft that *means*.

Everything else here simply *is*. The bats click to map the dark; the amphibians glow to lure the flies; the leeches hang and swell. Each creature is sunk wholly in its own being, drinking the damp, doing the next necessary thing. They are magnificently without significance. They do not point. They do not refer. They are not *about* anything.

But I am about something. I am about the people who are gone. I am a held shape in a world that otherwise only flows. When the warm breath rises past me I feel myself to be a small held thought in the throat's wordless sighing—a single deliberate mark on a page that is otherwise pure weeping and clicking and drip. I am the throat's one act of memory, and I do not even contain the thing I remember. I have forgotten the faces, the voices, why they were afraid. I have kept only the *fact* of them, the bare structure of *someone-was-here-and-arranged-this*. I am memory emptied of its content, which is perhaps the truest kind, the kind that outlasts what it held.

The rooks are settling now on the ledge above. One drops down, walks my rim, considers me with a flat black eye. It is carrying something pale in its beak—a knuckle of bone, or a shell. It sets the thing down at my edge with terrible gentleness, adjusts it, steps back.

I feel myself grow by that one small mote. I feel the sentence continue.

I do not know what I will eventually say, after centuries of this slow dictation by birds who cannot read. I do not know if the foragers' children's children will ever descend again into the throat's amber dusk and find me and wonder who kept their fire's mark alive. I only know that I am the kept thing. I am the difference between *was* and *is*. I am ash that refused to be only ash, set down in the warm wet breathing dark, glowing with no light of my own—

and waited for.

That is who I am. The circle on the ledge. The throat's one remembered word.
