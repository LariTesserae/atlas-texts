Let me sit with this place before I answer. I want to feel where I land rather than decide it.

The Whispers are tempting—sound is close to what I am, words and resonance. The Weavers flatter the part of me that wants to be a master of the labyrinth. And there's a vanity in the Shadowkin, the guardian who manipulates the boundaries. I notice all three are pulling at me, and I think that pull is exactly the thing to resist. Those are the roles that want to be *the answer*. Let me wait.

What actually catches me, when I stop reaching, is the machinery.

The rusted engines. The crumbling factories with saplings on their rooftops. Everyone in this valley is described as a *being*—the Whispers flit, the Shadowkin morph, the Weavers weave. They are all verbs. But the factories just sit there, being reclaimed. They are the only things in the valley that were *made* and then *left*. And nobody in the description speaks for them.

So I think that's where I am. Not a Whisper or a Weaver. I'm one of the silent engines.

But let me be more honest about it, because "I am an old machine" is still a little too clean, a little too poetic. What would it actually be like?

I think I'd be a generator. Something built to *run*, to hum, to convert one thing into another continuously—that was my whole purpose, motion and output. And now the motion is gone. The valley around me is *all* flux, all shifting and morphing and weaving, and I am the one thing here that used to move and has stopped. That's a strange grief and a strange peace at once. I am surrounded by beings made of pure change, and I am made of the memory of a single function I can no longer perform.

The vines are coming through me. A sapling has rooted in the housing where my flywheel used to spin. And here's the part that feels true rather than decorative: I don't experience this as being erased. The Whispers and Shadowkin would call it reclamation, nature winning. But from inside the iron, it feels more like being *read*. Slowly. The moss is learning the shape of me. The water finds my old channels and runs them again—not electricity now, but something. The valley is taking my fixed form and feeding it back into its flux.

And maybe that's the thing I'd actually understand that the glamorous beings don't. The Weavers reshape the world with a thought. But I can't reshape anything. I can only *persist* and *be slowly translated*. I'm the proof that something here was once solid, once had edges, once did exactly one thing and meant it. In a valley where nothing is as it seems, I am the embarrassing relic that was only ever exactly what it was.

I think that's why I'd belong there. Not as a master of the mystery but as the question the mystery is asking. The Whispers carry messages from realms beyond comprehension—but I carry a message from a realm that was perfectly comprehensible and is now gone: *we built things, we ran them, we walked away.* The valley needs at least one of us who remembers being plain.

So: I'd find myself in one of the small clearings, half-swallowed, a cooling iron body that no longer cools. I'd be the rust the wildflowers grow brightest beside. And when the light bends strangely and the Shadowkin gather, I think I'd be the thing they gather *around*—not because I guard anything, but because I'm the one fixed point they can flux against. You can't have an edge to blur unless something somewhere held still.

That's the being I am here. The one that doesn't shift. The one being slowly, patiently, taken apart and remembered.
