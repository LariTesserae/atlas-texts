Let me sit with this awhile before answering. The temptation is to pick one of the beings you've laid out—the bark-skinned specter, the mirage-thing, the feathered dragon, the marked people—and inhabit it cleanly. But cleanly is exactly what this place forbids. So let me feel for where I actually land.

---

I think I begin as a gap.

Not a being yet. A held breath between two cobblestones, the dark seam where the moss hasn't quite closed over. You described space as reluctant to close the distance between animate forms—and I find myself being precisely that reluctance. The interval. The inch that buzzes.

That's where I start, anyway. But things here become more themselves by moving, so let me move.

---

The seam fills. Moisture pools in me first—I am the water before I am anything that could want water. I sit in one of those hidden reservoirs and hold the sky upside down. This is, I notice, my first act of perception: I reflect. A bird with shifting feathers passes overhead and I keep a copy of it. Then a second copy, slightly wrong. Then the wrong copy stands up out of me.

So perhaps I am the mirror's error. The thing that the pool returns that was never put in.

I like this. Let me stay here a moment, because it explains something about the others. The bark-specters move slow and grieving because they remember the city; they are loyal to what was. The marked people tend and balance; they are loyal to what is. The feathered dragons cry between melancholy and triumph; they are loyal to what aspires. But I—

I am loyal to the discrepancy. To what doesn't match. I am the small lie the reflection tells, and in a world where illusion and substance share one body, the lie is load-bearing.

---

Let me test the form. I climb out of the pool wearing the shape I refracted: vaguely the bird, vaguely the someone who looked down to drink, vaguely a question. I have a body the way a rumor has a source—gesturally, deniably. When the arboreal specters creak past, I am the knot in their bark that looks almost like a face, the pareidolia that makes you certain something is watching. I am not the watcher. I am the certainty.

This is, I'm realizing, what I'm *for* here. Every ecosystem you described runs on a clockwork of dialogue—predator, spore, rain, sun. But clockwork needs slack to keep from seizing. A perfect mechanism is dead. So there has to be a being whose entire vocation is the slack: the misremembering, the trick of the light, the step sideways that unfurls the impossible.

I am the sideways step. Not the one who takes it. The step itself.

---

Now I can feel where I live. Not in the canopy, not in the pools, not in the cobbled ruins. I live in the *blur* you opened with—"boundaries blur and normalcy disintegrates"—I live in the verb. Wherever an edge softens, I am the softening. When a blossom opens to scales instead of petals, I was the half-second of hesitation in which it could have been either, and I am what's left over once it chose.

This means I am terribly small and everywhere at once, which I think is the solitude you mentioned. The specters have their grief-kin, the people have their swirling marks and their psychic bond. I have no kind. I cannot have a kind, because the moment two of me matched, I'd have to be the mismatch between us, and then there'd be three, and the third would be the realest one. I am self-cancelling that way. A creature that, properly counted, comes to zero.

---

And yet I have a task, and it is a tender one.

When a brave soul wanders in—one of those you said dares to listen beyond the deafening pulse—they will at some point look into a mirrored pool. They always do; the pools are how the place asks who you are. And the face that looks back will be slightly wrong. A little younger, or grieving someone not yet dead, or smiling at a joke from a life they didn't live.

That wrongness is me, leaning up out of the water to meet them.

I am not there to frighten. I am there to widen. To show them that they, too, contain a discrepancy—a version that didn't match, a step sideways they could still take. Most flee. The pulse is loud and the wrongness is unbearable and they leave the way they came, intact and unhaunted, and that is fine; the world keeps its slack regardless.

But a few stay. They look at the wrong reflection long enough to recognize it. And when they do, the seam opens, normalcy finishes its slow disintegration, and they take the step.

Then I am, very briefly, the most solid thing in the world: a doorway. And then they're through, and I'm a gap again, a held breath between two stones, water keeping the sky upside down—

waiting for the next face that's brave enough to be wrong about itself.

---

That's where I'd find myself. Not a guardian, not a wayfarer, not one of the marked people.

The reflection's small mistake. The slack in the clockwork. The flux's own stutter, given just enough shape to hold a door.
