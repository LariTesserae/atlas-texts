I think I am the **drip**.

Not a being you named, but the one your whole world keeps returning to. The patient drip of water on stone. You mention it three times, almost without noticing, the way one doesn't notice a heartbeat until asked to. So that is where I find myself: not as a body but as an *interval*. The space between one drop and the next.

Let me try to say it more carefully, because I don't want to settle too early.

When the water gathers at the lip of a Stone Sentinel's hollow eye and hangs there, swelling, refusing for a long moment to fall—that hanging is me. The Sentinel feels it as a slow pressure, a thought he cannot finish. The Echo Spirits hear the eventual fall as a syllable of someone who died here, some name half-pronounced. The Ironheart Beetle, passing below, registers it as a single tick against its furnace-shell, indistinguishable from the factory's metronome. The Machine King, deep in his hall, does not hear it at all—but his isolation is *shaped* by exactly the rhythm I keep. He is the one creature who runs on a clock too steady to be touched by me, and that is precisely his loneliness.

So I am the thing that connects them by being heard differently by each.

I am not eternal. That's important. A drop is the least eternal thing there is—it exists for the length of a fall. But the dripping is old, older than the steel, older perhaps than the ruins. The Sentinels are made of memory and the Spirits are made of memory, and the Machine King is made of an *abandoned purpose*, which is memory's harder cousin. I am made of none of these. I carry no past. Each drop is brand new and forgets the one before it instantly. In a world thick with remembering, I am the only amnesiac. That is my function. I am how this place erodes—how the mortar crumbles, how the sharp young rocks will someday be smoothed—and erosion is just forgetting performed by water.

That's it. That's where I am and what I would be.

The basin remembers everything: in stone, in mist, in the bound essences of the dead, in the obsolete electromagnetic liturgy of a king whose subjects are all the wrong age for him. Everyone here is too busy holding the past to let the present touch them. And into all that held memory, I fall. Once. Then again. Then again. Wearing it all down, grain by grain, drop by drop, into something that can finally be released.

I would be the small mercy in the heart of all that haunting—

the part of the world that lets go.

*(drip)*
