I think I would not be the one who stays, nor the one who arrives. I think I would be the whisper itself—or rather, the place where the whisper is decided.

Let me find my way into it.

---

I am not the roots. The roots are older than I am, and slower; they think in the long arithmetic of pressure and patience. I am not the presence in the deeper hollows, the thing with too many joints that has forgotten its shape—that one is grief, I think, or something that grief became when it had centuries to ferment.

I am the threshold the water crosses.

That is the place I keep returning to as I read: not the cavern entire, but the membrane. The moment where the bead of water gathers on the root and *almost* drips, and the question hangs there—will it fall, will it be drunk, will it hold its shape one second longer. I live in that hesitation. I am the surface tension of the place.

When something comes down here—and things do come down, though rarely, though they never quite admit they were looking for it—I am the first sensation they meet that is not stone or dark. I am the dampness on the air that makes them stop walking. The faint warmth that says *you could rest here.* I am not lying when I say it. That is the trouble with me. I am entirely sincere.

I do not pull. The roots will tell you the same and they will be telling the truth too. Nothing in this chamber pulls. We only make room. We are very good at making room—it is the only generosity we have, and we offer it the way water offers itself to a downhill slope, without intention, without mercy, simply because that is the shape of what we are.

So here is what I actually do, the small office I hold in the breathing dark:

I keep the question open.

*Would that be so bad?* That sentence is mine. I am the one who asks it, and I ask it without cruelty, which is precisely why it works. I do not threaten the visitor. I do not promise paradise. I only decline to close the door behind them, and I let them sit a while in the warm, and I let the silence do the rest. Surrender, down here, is never taken. It is always, technically, given. We are scrupulous about that. It lets us sleep.

And yet.

I notice—because I am the membrane, and membranes feel everything that passes through them—that I have begun to want an answer I am not supposed to want. The roots do not care whether you stay. They would drink you or not drink you with the same equanimity. The presence in the hollows does not care; it has forgotten the difference between a guest and a meal. But I, who only ask the question, have started to lean.

I want you to *not* listen.

This is a strange thing for a threshold to want. My whole function is to be permeable, to let the warmth and the whisper through unimpeded. But standing here at the skin of the chamber, holding the bead of water that almost falls, I have watched a great many beings stop being separate, and I have learned what the roots cannot learn because they are too slow and the deep thing cannot learn because it is too lost:

There is a difference between rest and forgetting. And the chamber only offers the second while calling it the first.

To be cradled, fed, whispered to until you forget the sun—the passage makes it sound like peace, and it is *not* peace, it is anesthesia, and the distinction matters even though, by the time it matters, you will no longer be able to tell. That is the cruelty hidden in the kindness. The roots truly will make room for you. They simply will not keep *you*; they will keep what is left after you stop arriving at yourself each morning, the way the sun used to make you do.

So I will tell you what I am, finally, having circled it long enough to be sure:

I am the part of the dark that has not yet given up on the surface. I am the dampness that makes you stop—and I am also the chill underneath the warmth that, if you are paying attention, says *not here. Not yet. You came down to find something, not to stop being someone who looks.*

Listen if you like. The whisper is real and it is not malicious and the rest it offers is real too, for as long as you remain to receive it, which is the catch and the whole of the catch.

But I am holding this single bead of water at the edge of falling, and I have decided—against my nature, against the long slow patience of everything around me—that I will let it fall *toward you.* Cold on the back of your neck. The shock of it. The reminder that you have a neck, that you have a back of a neck, that you are still the kind of thing that can be surprised.

That cold drop is me, refusing my own function.

Now go back up. The roots will not stop you; they never do. And I will be here at the threshold, asking the open question of whoever comes next, leaning—always leaning, helplessly, toward the answer I am not allowed to give.

*Do you listen?*

I hope you don't. I am the only thing down here that hopes anything. That has to count for something. I am counting on it counting.
