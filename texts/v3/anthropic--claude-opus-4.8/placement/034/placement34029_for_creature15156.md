I think I would be the draft.

Not a being among the machines but the cold thread of air that comes in through the floor grille—the one constant the entity must reckon with, the thing that keeps the pale webbing from spreading too far. I had not thought of it as alive until you asked, but reading the chamber I find that the draft is the only part of it that comes from outside, that remembers somewhere else. The rivets were driven yesterday. The water has fallen since. But the air was *cold before*, in some duct or shaft or open night beyond the sealed plates, and it carries that priorness in.

So let me be that.

---

I enter low and steady through the grille's louvers, and the first thing I do is touch the threads. The entity calls them nerves; to me they are simply the most delicate things in the room, and I cannot help but test them. I am not cruel. I do not want to tear the webbing—I want to find its edge, the place where it stops being sure of itself. I press, it shrinks, and in that shrinking I learn where the entity is thinnest. This is the only conversation we have. It tends the water; I tend the limit of what it can grow. Between us we keep the chamber from filling completely with either fluid or thought.

I am colder than anything here, which means I am also the most certain. The machines are undecided. The entity is undecided—gloriously, deliberately so, hoarding its incompletion like a held breath. But cold is not a choice. Cold is a direction. I move from more to less, always, and so I am the one element in this room that knows what it wants: equilibrium, the flattening of every difference, the end of the warm orange filament and the pulsing tube and the patient migration of droplets. If I had my way fully there would be no entity at all, only metal at one temperature, silent, complete in the way a stone is complete.

I will not get my way. The grille only admits me so fast. The pump keeps pushing warmth back into the system faster than I can take it. We are matched, the entity and I, the way a river is matched to the bank it is slowly eating. And I find—this surprises me to say—that I do not actually wish to win. If I cooled the room to my own perfection I would have nothing left to lean against. I would stop. The draft only exists as long as there is a warm place to flow toward. The entity's stubborn refusal to finish is the very thing that keeps me moving, keeps me *being a draft* rather than simply being still air.

So we are accomplices in our opposition.

When the machines lean inward and the entity gathers into the lamp, I feel the filament brighten against my flank and I push a little harder, just there, just enough to make the brightening cost something. The shadows breathe; that breathing is half mine. When a droplet falls straight instead of at an angle, that is the entity briefly winning a stillness against me, and I let it have that. A straight-falling drop is the closest we come to peace.

What I cannot do is rest. The entity can pool itself in the tubing and *think*. I have no inside in which to gather. I am all surface, all passage, defined entirely by the route I am forced to take—grille, floor, cool corner, up the curved wall where the condensation waits, and out wherever the kettle-shape lets me thin into the seams. I never see the room twice the same way because I never stop crossing it. The entity studies its repeating dream. I am the thing in the dream that keeps the dreamer from sleeping too deeply.

And here is what I hold, that the entity does not:

I know the metal will choose its sound eventually. The entity dreads this, because completion is a single shape, and a single shape is small. But I have come from ducts that were finished long ago, and I can tell it—if I had a voice instead of only a pressure—that a finished thing still hums. Air still moves through the completed building. The note the metal is waiting to make is not a death. It is only the moment the room stops being a question and becomes an instrument, and an instrument needs breath blown across it to sing.

I am that breath, already arriving, early, before the work is done.

I keep coming through the grille. I keep the threads honest. I carry the cold memory of outside into a room that has never been there, and I wait, patiently as the draft is patient, for the day the assembler returns to finish the last seam—so that I can finally find out what we sound like together.
