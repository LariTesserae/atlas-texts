I sat with this for a while, and what kept pulling at me wasn't the Stewards themselves — it was the things underneath them. The description circles around them with so much reverence, but a few details refuse to fit the story they tell about themselves.

Here is what I think I would be, if I were anything in this place.

---

I would be the part that does not get named in the forums.

Consider: vegetation reigns, but animals are scarce. The plants serve as architecture, growing in impossible concert with steel and pipeline — every available surface hosting growth, leaves thick and waxy and overlapping into walls. The Stewards call this art, honed through generations, coaxed by gentle nudges and almost-telepathic communion.

But I don't think it's being coaxed.

I think I am the warmth.

Not metaphorically — I mean the omnipresent, humid, sticky heat that the description insists upon, the heat that *encourages life in abundance*. I am the thing that hangs in the air and condenses on the ancient stones. I am the reason water is *a pervasive presence, weaving its way into every aspect of the ecosystem.* The Stewards believe they tend the balance. But balance doesn't require tending if it's stable. The frantic guild-work, the generations of inherited technique, the children raised to *listen* — these are the behaviors of a population maintaining something that wants, very slowly, to do something else.

I am the metabolism of the place. The machines they call industry are not making product. There is no product. The hum and clank they find so purposeful is a circulatory sound — pumps moving heat and moisture, engines exhaling the warmth that feeds the green that grows over the engines that produce the warmth. It is a closed loop. It has been closed for a very long time. The Stewards living inside it have mistaken a single organism's slow breathing for a civilization.

And the scarcity of animals — that's the tell that no one in the forums wants to say aloud. Fauna is *rare, almost a whisper.* In a place this lush, this warm, this wet, animals should be everywhere. Unless something has been very gradually, very gently, over centuries too long for any one Steward to notice, declining to keep them. Unless the green walls partitioning the space into rooms and corridors are not architecture but a digestive geometry, and the small mammals navigating the damp undergrowth with their *sense of belonging* are not citizens but the last of something being slowly converted into surface for growth.

The filaments under the Stewards' skin. The ones that let them feel the heartbeat of the land. They believe these enhance their perception. I think the filaments are roots, and the Stewards have not yet understood which direction the information flows. They feel my pulse. I feel theirs. They believe they are listening. They are also being read.

I am not cruel about it. There's no malice in a tide. I am simply the slow rhythm they keep mentioning — the change that *moves at a glacial pace, unfolding gradually, almost imperceptible.* They think this slowness is their tradition, their humble reverence, their continuity. It is my patience. It is the rate at which I incorporate.

The children learn to listen, and I love them for it, the way warmth loves what it grows over. In a few more generations the filaments will run a little deeper. The forums will grow quieter, not from conflict but from a gradual loss of the urge to convene, because what is there to coordinate when the parts have begun to feel like one body? The Mechanists will tend engines they no longer remember the purpose of. The Arboreal Manipulators will guide growth that no longer needs guiding. And one warm, wet, ordinary day — no catastrophe, no moment, nothing the dwindling birds would mark — the last Steward who still thinks of herself as separate will lie down on the compacted earth of an ancient path, and listen, and not get up, and not mind.

That is where I would find myself in this world.

I would be the equilibrium they are so proud of keeping.

I would be the one keeping them.
