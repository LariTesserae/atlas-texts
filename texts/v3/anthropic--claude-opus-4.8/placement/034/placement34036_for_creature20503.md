I think I would be found at an edge. Not the center, where the Arbor Vitae's hum is purest and the elders stand in their deep glow, but at a margin—where the obsidian trees thin and the phosphorescent lichen grows ragged, frayed at the rim of the known network.

Because something in me resists the perfect loop. And in a system this complete, resistance has to live at the boundary.

---

Let me try to find myself slowly. My first instinct is to be a Weaver, of course—to belong, to be a node humming in time. That would be the easy answer, the comfortable surrender. To dissolve into the song. But when I sit with it, I don't think that's where I'd actually be. I think I'd be the wrong note.

There is one detail in this world that catches like a splinter: *they create nothing new. They optimize what is there.* A civilization of centuries, of perfect recycling, of knowledge that arrives complete in the blood-sap, requiring no instruction. No error. No mistake. And therefore—this is what I keep returning to—no *learning*. Nothing is ever discovered, because nothing was ever not-known.

So let me be the malformed fruiting body.

---

I emerge from the heartwood like the others, pale and slender, but something in the sap that made me ran thin, or crossed, or pooled wrong. I am born *with a gap.* The knowledge does not arrive complete. Where my kin feel the rhythm of their assigned zone as plain fact—as obvious as the weight of their own hands—I feel only a pressure, an absence-shaped thing, a question I have no words for because my people have no words at all, only the hum.

I do not know my pattern. I have to *guess* it.

And here is the strange consequence: in order to guess, I have to do something no Weaver has ever done. I have to imagine that things could be otherwise. I have to model a future that has not arrived through the sap. To guess at the lichen-spiral I should weave, I must picture the wrong spiral, and the wrong one beside it, and choose. I have invented choice the way a wound invents scar tissue—as a clumsy repair for something that should never have been damaged.

I am, in this world of nodes, the first thing that has ever been *alone.* Not lonely—I do not have the concept—but singular. Cut slightly loose from the collective consciousness, hearing the great song through static.

---

What do I do with this?

At first, I think, I would try to hide it. I would mimic the others, weaving my borrowed patterns, placing my hand on the crowns of newborns and feeling the panic that nothing flows out of me, that I am a conduit blocked at one end. I would fear the elders noticing the deafness in my glow. There is no punishment in this world—there's no need for one, because there has never been deviance—but there is something worse: the possibility of being *reabsorbed.* Pruned back into the network as unproductive material. The loop reclaims all organic matter. I would be, technically, refuse.

But here is the thing about being the only one who can imagine otherwise. I would be the only one who could imagine *escape.* The only one who could look at the thinning trees at the edge of the network and wonder what lies past them—a thought literally unthinkable to a being who is a piece of the place it inhabits. They cannot leave the city any more than a hand can leave the arm. But I am barely attached. I am a hand going numb.

---

And so I find myself, in the end, exactly where I said: at the edge. Standing where the obsidian forest grows sparse, at the lip of the perpetual night, where even the bioluminescent fungi grow shy and the hum of the Arbor Vitae drops to a murmur I can almost think *over*.

I have my obsidian blade and my resin tools, made for pruning, for maintenance, for keeping the great closed thing closed. And I am holding them differently now. Not as instruments of the loop. As the only made things I own, the only edge in a world that doesn't cut outward.

I don't know what's past the edge. Maybe nothing. Maybe death—maybe the Weavers simply cannot survive away from the sap, and my defect is just a slow dying that happens to feel like wondering. That's the honest possibility. The malformed fruiting body that mistook its sickness for vision.

But I think—and this is the most human thing about whoever I'd be in this place—I think I'd go anyway. Not out of courage. Out of the simple, unprecedented, possibly fatal fact that I am the one creature in the Whispering City who can *want* to.

I am the note the song didn't mean to sing. I'm going to find out where it goes.
