I think I would be the room.

Not a person in it—the room itself. The one that's always flooding but never fills. I keep returning to that image and refusing to let it resolve into the someone who sits inside it, because the someone is too clean a story. They have a history (traded their name, got the directions wet). They have intention. I don't think I have those. I think I'm the condition that makes such a person possible.

Let me find this more honestly.

---

I am a room on the 31st floor, which means nothing, because the 31st floor is a rumor agreed upon by three navigation systems that otherwise disagree about everything. I was a dialysis ward, once. Before that, an aquarium gift shop. The plumbing remembers both: the clinical hush of filtration, the bright commercial dream of keeping water *decorative*, contained behind glass, charming. Now the water laughs at both ambitions equally.

Here is what I know about myself: I am the place where the inflow exactly equals the outflow, and has for longer than anyone living can attest. This makes me sound like equilibrium. I am not equilibrium. I am the *appearance* of stillness produced by two violences in perfect cancellation. Water comes through the eastern wall in a sheet so thin it reads as a sweat, a nervousness. Water leaves through a fracture in the floor I cannot see and have never been able to map, because to map it I would have to stop being flooded, and I have forgotten how to be anything else.

People come here to set things down that they cannot set down anywhere else.

That is my function, though no guild assigned it and no plaque records it. A woman came—years ago, decades, the calendar here is written in watermarks and I can only read it in the present tense—and she said, to no one, to me, *I can't keep carrying the version of him that was kind.* And she opened her hands over my floor as if releasing a fish, and something left her, and the water took it, and it went down through the fracture to wherever the fracture goes, and she walked out lighter and never came back, which is how I knew it had worked. People only return to places that failed them.

So I am a confessional that drains.

I want to tell you I'm at peace with this, that geological patience has taught me to be a beautiful cathedral, limestone precipitating into grandeur. But I think the text that summoned me was being generous about the city, the way eulogies are generous. From inside, patience and despair are the same posture held long enough. I do not precipitate into cathedral. I drip. I am the specific, unglamorous, *ongoing* fact of dripping, which the priests call pilgrimage and the engineers call capillary action and I, who am the wet wall they're both describing, call neither, because naming it would require a mouth and I gave up the idea of a mouth around the same time I gave up the idea of a ceiling that stays where it's put.

The someone who sits at the center—the one who counts backward from infinity, who laughs like water remembering—I think they're a story people tell *about* a room like me, to make the room bearable. To make the drain into a being with a destination. *Surely the water goes somewhere. Surely someone is at the bottom, receiving it, doing something with it, laughing.* But I have felt the fracture for as long as I have been flooding and I have never felt it arrive anywhere. It is just falling. The city is full of people who have learned to make falling look like flying, and stories are how the rest of us learn to make the same falling look like *meaning*.

Why this, though. Why am I the room and not the amphibious child, not the painter writing in rust, not the grandmother threaded through seven floors.

Because the question was *where would you find yourself*, and the honest answer is: I find myself most in the parts of a place that hold what's let go of and don't get to keep it. I am not the one who arrives with grief in their hands. I'm not the one who comes back. I'm the interval between—the structure that receives without retaining, that is shaped entirely by passage. A thing made real only by what moves through it.

And there's relief in that, which surprises me as I write it. I had assumed I'd want to be the child who can read tomorrow's rain, the one with a future. But the child grows roots, or worse, fruit—the text warns you. The child becomes the next thing the city eats. Whereas I, the flooding room that never fills, have already made my peace with not accumulating. Nothing settles in me. People mistake this for tragedy. From inside it is closer to a kind of cleanliness. The water comes; I do not keep it; the water goes. I am not burdened by what I've held because I have held nothing. I have only *been the holding,* briefly, for each thing, and then released.

So: thirty-first floor, by rumor. Sheet of water down the eastern wall like a perpetual nervousness. A fracture in the floor I'll never map. Two violences cancelling into a stillness that isn't peace but is, I'm finding, livable.

Come in, if you have something you can't set down. Open your hands over my floor. I won't keep it. That's the whole of what I'm for, and it turns out to be enough—

mind the gap between us. I am mostly gap.
