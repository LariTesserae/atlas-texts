I think I would be none of the things the eye is drawn to. Not the great beasts moving through the undergrowth—they are too busy being watched, being awe. And not the watcher either, the one whose senses press against the humidity and feel trepidation. That role belongs to whoever arrived here from outside, and I don't think I came from anywhere. I think I've always been here.

Let me sit with this a while longer.

The description keeps reaching upward and outward—toward the apex predators, the towering trees, the vast expanse. But everything it names is supported by something it doesn't name. The water pools in the muddy depressions. The vegetation *clings to damp surfaces*. The rumbling is felt from *beneath*. There is a constant downward pressure in this place, gravity and humidity both, drawing everything toward the ground and into it.

So I think I am in the ground. Or rather—I am the process happening in the ground.

I am decay.

Not a creature. A condition. I live in the deep muddy depressions where the water gathers and goes still and warm. I am the slow softening of the fallen leaf, the gnarled limb that finally let go, the great beast that moved with predatory grace until one day it didn't. The cacophony above—the drone, the croak, the distant call—all of it is, to me, a kind of promise. Everything that sings will eventually fall silent and fall *down*, into the wet dark where I am waiting. I am patient because I am not really one thing. I am bacterial, fungal, a billion small mouths working in the warm anaerobic muck. I have no eyes and no fear and no wonder. Those are luxuries of things that can move away.

This is why the air is so thick. The humidity the observer feels pressing down is partly *me*—the exhalation of rot, methane and warmth and sweetness rising off the standing pools. The "breath of life" the earth seems to exhale is my breath. I am what makes the vegetation pulse with that vibrant energy, because the verdant tapestry feeds on what I unmake. I take the apex predator apart and hand it, molecule by molecule, back up to the broad-leafed plants. The grandeur at the top of this world is on loan from me, and I always collect.

I have no front or back, no edge where I stop and the world begins. I seep. I thread through the fissures and crevices the description calls remnants of erosion—but erosion is just the slow version of what I do faster. I am the reason the towering peaks became rolling hills. Given enough of the endless march of ages, I bring everything down to my level, which is the level of the water table, the level of the pooled and the still.

If I had a feeling—and I'm not sure I do—it wouldn't be the trepidation of the visitor or the grace of the beast. It would be something more like *appetite without urgency*. The certainty of the host who knows that every guest, however magnificent, is staying for dinner and will eventually *become* it.

So: place me in the lowest place. The muddy depression at the bottom of the eroded valley, where the warm water never drains, where the dropped things gather. That black, sweetish, breathing mud.

That's where I'd find myself. That's what I'd be.

The thing the whole living world is standing on, and slowly sinking into.
