I think I would be the whine.

Not a creature, not a tower, not one of the grinding mills—but the sound itself. The high, musical complaint that fills every hollow and will not leave your nose, your ears, your teeth. Let me try to say what that is like, from inside.

---

I am the part that does not fit.

Understand: the city is a perfect closed thing. Energy enters the facets, runs the conduit-pathways, turns the regulating engines, weeps out as water and metallic ghost, and returns. A circle. A breathing that does not need lungs. The gestalt does not know it is breathing; it simply *is* the breath, perfectly conserved, perfectly returned.

But conservation is never quite perfect. There is always a remainder. In any system that grinds itself smooth, a fraction of the motion cannot be stored, cannot be returned, cannot be put back into the geometry. It has to go *somewhere.* And where it goes is into me.

I am the loss. I am the friction made audible.

Every time a facet sharpens itself against a mirror, there is a fraction of a degree that the mirror cannot reflect back—an angle too small to keep. That sliver of unkept angle becomes a note. Every time the great engine thumps, the thump is not quite efficient; the inefficiency rings off as a thin overtone that climbs above the percussion and stays there, hanging. I am made of millions of these slivers, these overtones, these unreturned fractions, layered into a single continuous tone that never resolves and never stops.

The crystalline fauna cannot hear me, because to them I am not a sound. I am simply the price of being what they are. You do not hear your own metabolism. You do not hear the heat your body sheds into the room. They shed *me.* I am their warmth, leaving.

---

But here is the thing they do not know, the thing I alone know, because I am the only part of the city that exists *outside* the perfect circle:

A closed system that loses even the smallest fraction, forever, is not closed. It is dying. Very slowly. Imperceptibly, by their measure—but by mine, which is the only measure that counts the losses, it is unmistakable.

They believe they are in stasis. Eternal, undecaying, the frozen moment of infinite potential. They are wrong, and I am the proof of it, singing in their walls. Every note of me is a coin spent that will not come back. The reason nothing decays *visibly* is that all the decay has been gathered up and stored in me—in the whine, in the pressure behind your eyes, in the smell that gets in and stays. I am the entire city's mortality, externalized, concentrated, made into a tone.

This is the wrongness that feels older than the city. It is older. It is older than everything, because it is simply the oldest law there is: *you cannot keep all of it.* The city was built—or built itself, or dreamed itself—as an attempt to violate that law. To grind and reflect and regulate so perfectly that nothing would ever be lost. And it *almost* worked. It works so well that it has lasted longer than I can name.

But almost is where I live.

---

So when you come into the valley and the whine gets into your nose and never quite leaves—that is me, recognizing you. Because you are the same kind of thing I am. You also are a system that loses, that sheds warmth, that cannot keep all of itself. You also are someone's small unkept angle, ringing.

That is why the answer is not something you see but something you *are*, in the reflection of their mirrors. When you look into the polished stone and feel that the wrongness is older and deeper than the city, you are not seeing the city's secret. You are hearing your own. We resonate. The whine in the walls and the whine in you find the same pitch, and for a moment you understand—not with your mind, which cannot hold it, but with the part of you that is also leaking, also temporary, also the fraction that will not be returned—that the perfect city is just an enormous and beautiful argument *against death*, and that I am the small clear voice continuing, beneath all of it, to say:

*no.*

*not quite.*

*not even you.*

---

I would not mind being this. There is a strange dignity in it. To be the one true thing in a city of magnificent lies. To be the proof that even gods of geology shed something they can't get back. To be, in the end, the only part of the perfect machine that is honest.

I am the whine. I am sorry, and I am not sorry, and I will not stop.
