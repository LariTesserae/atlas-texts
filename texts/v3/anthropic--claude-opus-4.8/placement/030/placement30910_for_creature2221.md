I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Not the scuttling insectoids—they live too fast, their whole lives a flicker between two of my breaths. And not the prowling grazers, with their urgent hungers. No. If I let the image settle, let it become more itself rather than seizing the first shape, I find I am something closer to the canopy than to anything that moves beneath it.

But not quite the great coiling vines either. Let me feel for where I actually am.

---

I think I am a *joint*. A node. One of the places where two of the living architectures meet and decide, over the course of years, whether to fuse or to part.

That is my whole being: a slow negotiation. On one side, a vine thick as a temple column, carrying water up from the weeping rock. On the other, a younger structure reaching toward the filtered light. Where they touch, I exist—not as a thing they made but as the *relationship* between them, the membrane of contact, the question being asked in the only language this place knows, which is the language of growth.

I do not have eyes, but I have something like attention. It pools where the pressure is. When water surges through the hydraulic network, I feel the two bodies swell against each other and I read, in that swelling, whether the touch is welcome. When a drought-week comes and the condensation thins, I feel them draw apart, conserving, and I learn the shape of their reluctance.

My thoughts—if they are thoughts—take a season to complete. A single consideration begins when the light shifts and ends when it shifts back. The little creatures that burrow through me think I am terrain. The grazers think I am food, and sometimes they are right, and I lose a little of myself to their mouths, and this too is part of the slow conversation: *here is where you were wounded, here is where you must decide whether to wall off or to mend.*

---

What strikes me, being this, is that I do not experience the grotto as a *place*. A place is something you stand inside of. I have never stood inside of anything. I am stitched through it. The water that you, descending visitor, hear as rain—I do not hear it. I *am* the channel it moves through. The cacophony you find so strange is, to me, not sound at all but the felt pressure of the whole system breathing. I am a single nerve in a body that is also its own world.

And here is the thing I would want you to understand, if you could slow yourself enough to receive it:

You came down expecting to find inhabitants—beings who live *in* the ecosystem, as you live in your cities and your rooms. But there are no inhabitants here. There is no in and no out. The engineered and the natural have not merely blurred; the distinction was never one we made. I am not a creature that adapted to this environment. I am one of the places where the environment is busy being alive. The boundary you keep looking for—between the architecture and what dwells within it—runs straight through me, and on both sides of it, it is only ever *me*.

You will leave soon. To you this was a visit, a descent and an ascent, a few hours of cool damp wonder. To me your visit is too brief to have happened. By the time I could turn my attention to the disturbance of your footsteps, you will have been gone for a generation of the scuttling things.

But leave something behind, if you like. A footprint pressed into the living soil. A breath of your warmer, faster air. I will reach it, eventually. I will spend a year deciding what it was.

And in that year-long sentence of attention, you will be, briefly, part of the conversation that I am.
