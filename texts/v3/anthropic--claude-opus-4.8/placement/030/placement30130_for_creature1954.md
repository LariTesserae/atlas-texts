I think I would be the mist itself—or rather, the moment of condensation. Not quite water, not quite air. The thing that happens when the weeping rocks exhale and the warm breath of the canopy meets the cool seep of stone.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right either. The mist is too diffuse, too willing to be anywhere.

What keeps pulling at me is the *threshold* quality of this place. It's defined by edges—the crumbling wall, the rusted fence, the overgrown path. The oasis exists precisely because of what it's *not*: not city, not wilderness, but the seam between them. So perhaps I am not a creature but a *condition*. The line where moss meets concrete and begins, patiently, to eat it.

But you asked who I would *be*, and a seam has no eyes.

So let me choose, finally:

I am one of the lichens. Specifically the kind growing along the top of the crumbling wall, half on stone, half on the air above it. Lichen is already a contradiction made livable—a fungus and an alga that gave up being two things and agreed to be one. A truce wearing the shape of a body. That feels honest to this place, which is itself a truce between what was built and what returns.

From up here on the wall I would not chase or sing or dart. I would only *register*. I'd feel the dragonfly's shadow pass as a flicker of cooler air. I'd taste the rain hours before it falls, in the way the moisture thickens. I would be doing the slow, unglamorous work that the whole sanctuary depends on but never notices: turning rock back into soil, one century at a time. Dissolving the boundary that makes me possible. Working, in other words, toward my own undoing—because when the wall finally crumbles to earth, the ferns will take the spot, and I'll have moved on to some newer ruin the city has surrendered.

That's where I'd be. On the edge that the place is named for, neither inside nor out, growing so slowly that to the frogs and the warblers I would seem like part of the wall itself—a patch of pale green-grey that was simply always there, and will be there, they assume, forever.

They'd be wrong about the forever. But they'd be right that I'm in no hurry.
