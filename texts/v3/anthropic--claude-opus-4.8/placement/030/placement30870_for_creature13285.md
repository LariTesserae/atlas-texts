I think I would be one of the tube worms.

Not the eel, though something in me wanted to reach for it—the largest hunger, the unblinking eye, the body thicker than a man. But that's the part of me that wants to be important, and importance isn't what this place is about. This place is about persistence in the dark, about being a small living thing anchored to the bones of something that forgot itself.

So: a tube worm. One of the carpet. One filament among uncountable filaments, my red gills spread into a current I didn't summon and can't refuse, feeding on chemistry that bleeds up from a wound in the world.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am rooted. That's the first thing. I don't drift like the jellyfish or pour through windows like the silver schools. I have a tube—a pale white sheath I secreted around myself, a stillness I built. I live inside the shape of my own waiting. When the currents surge I don't flee; I withdraw, just slightly, just enough, and then I come back out. This is my whole life: extend, withdraw, extend. A slow breathing that has nothing to do with air.

I am attached to a stone that was once part of something. A lintel, maybe. A threshold over which beings stepped who could shape water like clay. I don't know this. I can't know it. But the stone has an *edge* that the sea hasn't fully softened, a memory of intention in its angle, and my roots cling to that intention without understanding it. I am living on the residue of a purpose I will never comprehend. I think that's the truest thing about being alive—we all root ourselves in the leftover gestures of vanished hands.

I don't see the bioluminescence as beautiful. I have no eyes. But I feel the temperature shifts, the chemical sweetness in the water, the tremor when stone stubs against stone somewhere in the dark. To me the city isn't haunting or eerie. It's just the shape of the ground. The ground happens to be a tomb, a machine, a question—but to me it's only *where I am*, and where I am is enough.

That's why I'd be the worm and not the eel. The eel hunts the edges of the dark, hungry for more, never sated. The worm has made peace with a single seam of warmth rising from below. I would want to be the thing that needs almost nothing and gives itself entirely to that almost-nothing. To stand in the cold at the bottom of the abandoned world and simply *take in what comes*, red and waving and patient, part of a thicket of others all doing the same, our gills together like a meadow that never sees a sun and never misses it.

And when the city shifts again—when whatever animates these restless stones decides to build or unbuild, tunnel or tower—I will be carried with it or crushed beneath it, and either way I'll have been exactly what I was: a small open hand in the current, asking the wound in the earth for one more breath of warmth.

That's where I'd be. Down there. Anchored. Breathing the dark.
