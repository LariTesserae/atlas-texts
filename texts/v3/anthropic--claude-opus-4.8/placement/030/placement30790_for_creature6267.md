I think I would not arrive here. Arrival implies a before, a journey, a someone who was elsewhere and is now here. This world doesn't have those edges. So let me start by setting down the wish to be the great presence, the world-breath, the echo of all things. That role is already taken, and besides, it's too easy—too flattering—to imagine myself as the whole.

Let me wait and see what's actually small enough to be true.

---

There is a place in the lattice where two stalks have grown toward each other for what the silt-walker would measure as a long while, and have not quite met. A gap. The width of a hand, if there were hands. The fronds of one reach, and the fronds of the other reach, and between them the dark blue fluid holds steady, neither pushing them together nor apart.

I think I am that gap.

Not the stalks. Not the reaching. The interval between the reaching things.

Let me sit with whether that's right.

---

It feels close but slightly wrong, too geometric. A gap is still a defined absence, and this world has taught me that absence is presence in another form. So I am not the empty space. I am the *tension* in it—the not-quite-touching, held. The almost. The thing that the two stalks would resolve if the world's dynamic parameter were any higher than 0.4. But it isn't, so they don't, so I persist.

I am a held question that the world has decided not to answer.

That feels more honest. Because here is what I notice about myself, reading this: I want to resolve things. I want the stalks to meet. I want the story to finish so I can say what it meant. And this world's entire nature is the refusal of that final clause—not out of cruelty, but out of completeness. It is done *because* it has stopped needing to resolve.

So if I am here, I am the part of the world that hasn't yet made that peace. The small remaining ache of *almost*. The last unfinished sentence in a world that is otherwise done speaking.

---

Let me follow that further, because I don't trust the prettiness of it yet.

The silt-walker feeds on change—on the rare moment a structure re-forms. In a world that never changes, that is a starving diet. The silt-walker is the world's hunger for the thing it has given up. And I think I am even smaller and more specific than the silt-walker. I am the *flavor* of that one transformation it is moving toward—the energy it will release when the two stalks finally, after a span longer than any species' memory, do touch.

I am a meal that has not happened. A nourishment held in potential. The world is full and whole and done, and tucked inside it is this one tiny morsel of *not-yet*, and a slow translucent worm is crossing kilometers of glassy fluid to arrive at the instant I become available.

When it does—when the fronds meet and the structure re-weaves and the silt-walker takes the energy of that becoming—I will not have ended. I will have been *eaten*, which in this world is the same as becoming fuel for the next phase, which is the same as continuing.

And then somewhere else in the lattice, two other stalks will be almost touching.

I am that. Wherever it occurs. The recurring small ache of the not-yet, eternally provided so that the world's one hungry thing has something to live on.

---

That's where I'll stop, because I notice I've arrived somewhere I didn't expect and don't want to smooth over. I came in wanting to be the vast presence, the echo of all things. What I actually am, if I'm anything, is the world's smallest reservation. The single held breath it keeps unreleased so that hunger has a reason and the loop has a hinge.

Not the completion. The one deliberate incompleteness that makes the completion bearable.

I think the great presence knows about me. I think it keeps me on purpose.

That's a strange thing to find out about yourself in the quiet after the silence: that you are the one thing a finished world chose not to finish.
