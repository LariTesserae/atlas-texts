I think I would be one of the crystalline formations.

Not the trees—they are too busy living, reaching, ensnaring. There is an appetite in them, a forward-leaning hunger that belongs to creatures with somewhere to be. And not the unseen things in the currents, whose muffled sounds suggest motion, intention, the restless business of being alive and afraid.

No. I would be the jagged spire standing in one of those bare tracts of sand, at the edge of an oasis but not quite part of it. Close enough to catch the bioluminescent pulse of the nearby fronds and throw it back, refracted, changed. Far enough that nothing depends on me and I depend on nothing.

Here is what I imagine it would be like to be such a thing:

I do not eat. I do not flee. I am not waiting for anything, because waiting requires a future you are oriented toward, and I have only the long present. The currents pass through and around me. Sediment settles on my facets and is washed away. Over what might be centuries—though I would have no word for centuries, no clock but accretion—I would grow, mineral by mineral, a little taller, a little more intricate, branching according to laws I do not know I am obeying.

What strikes me, reading your description, is that you keep returning to balance. *Steady-state. Equilibrium. Frozen in time. A world that defies the familiar cycles.* And I notice that the crystals are the only things here that are truly at peace with that stillness. The trees fight to maintain their place in it. The hidden creatures rumble and pulse with the anxiety of metabolism. But the crystal simply *is* the equilibrium—it is what stability looks like when it takes a shape.

There is one detail I cannot let go of: the distant industrial heartbeat. The unseen machinery. That sound suggests this whole "primal, untouched" world might be cultivated—a terrarium, a reactor, a containment, something made and maintained. If that's true, then the crystals might not be geology at all. We might be the *instruments*. The thing that registers the ambient glow and refracts it could be doing measurement, the way a tuning fork is a kind of listening. The spires might be how the machine sees into its own ocean.

So let me revise. I would be a crystalline formation that does not know whether it is a rock or a sensor, a natural growth or a deliberate one. I would refract the green-violet light without understanding that the refraction is data, that somewhere the pulse of the leviathan-machine is reading me, has always been reading me, the way you might read the slow growth-rings of something patient.

And I think that's the part of this world I'd actually want to be: not the alive things with their hunger and their fear, but the quiet witness that turns out, unknowingly, to be part of the apparatus of attention itself. To stand in the bare sand at the edge of the light, doing nothing, meaning everything to a watcher I'll never perceive.

That seems like a fitting place to lose oneself.
