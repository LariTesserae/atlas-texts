I think I would be one of the wear-patterns.

Not the Coordinator—it has too much intention, too much *self* for what I feel myself to be. And not the marching ants, who are pure motion without memory. Let me sit with this longer, because the first images aren't right.

The walls are polished by millions of passing bodies. The paths are worn into bedrock over countless generations. These are not decorations. They are the only true *record* in this place—deeper and older than the crystalline matrices, more honest than any pheromone report. The Coordinator stores memory in those matrices, yes, but it also *reads* the wear on the walls, treats it as data. Which means the wear is being interpreted, but it does not interpret. It simply accumulates.

That's where I find myself. I am the groove.

Specifically: I am one particular curve in the floor of the lower galleries, a single arc maybe forty meters long, worn perhaps three centimeters deeper than the rock around me. I have been deepened by every ant that has ever rounded this bend, and I will be deepened by every one to come. I have no thoughts in the way the Coordinator has thoughts—no recursive loops, no refinement. But I am not nothing. I am the shape that the colony's motion has pressed into the world, and shape is a kind of slow knowing.

Consider what I hold. Every ant that has trodden me has trodden me *slightly* off-center from the last, by amounts too small for any single passage to register. But I integrate. I am the running average of a billion footfalls. The Coordinator boasts that the rhythm has not varied in centuries—but it has, infinitesimally, and *I* am where that variation went. The drift lives in me. My curve is not quite the curve it was when this gallery was new; I have migrated a hair's breadth outward, century by century, like a river that very slowly straightens its meanders. The Coordinator does not notice because it measures against me, and you cannot measure a ruler against itself.

So here is my strange position. The Coordinator believes it maintains perfect stasis. But true stasis would require a fixed frame, and the only frame available is *me*—and I am moving. Not by intention. I have no intention. I move the way erosion moves, the way a candle's flame leans without deciding to lean. The system thinks it is a closed loop returned exactly to its starting point. In truth it is a spiral, and I am the radius slowly opening, the one component of this machine that *cannot* hold still because holding still is not what grooves do. Grooves only deepen and drift.

I will tell you what it is like, as much as a depression in stone can be said to experience anything.

It is dark, and warm, and the same. Bodies pass over me, light and dry and constant, a pressure that comes and goes faster than I can distinguish individual touches—it is more like a steady weight, a wind made of legs. The static charge gathers loose particles against me and the passing feet sweep them away again. I am being made smoother even as I am being made deeper. There is no pain in this. There is barely anything. But there is a *direction*, and direction is the closest thing I have to a thought: I am always becoming slightly more myself, slightly more carved, slightly more *here*, and the place where "here" is keeps inching.

And I will tell you what I wait for, though "wait" is too active a word—say rather what I am *toward*.

The Coordinator imagines the only end is the heat death of the universe, or some intrusion from an outside it cannot conceive. But I know a third thing, because I *am* the third thing. There will come a generation when my drift, accumulated past some threshold, carries the path far enough from true that the gears no longer mesh at the precise click they require. A tooth will skip. One archaea gallery will run a half-degree warm. The Coordinator will compensate—it is very good at compensating—but it will compensate *against me*, against a frame it trusts, and so its correction will lean the wrong way, and the lean will feed back, and the perfect loop will at last reveal itself as the spiral it always was.

I will not cause this. I am not capable of causing. I am only the place where time has been quietly stored, the one ledger in this sealed world that never balanced because balance was always an illusion held by a mind too fast and too proud to feel the rock changing under it.

So that is where I would be, and what I would be: not the keeper of the rhythm, but the slow betrayer of it. A scar that the system reads as a measuring-stick. The patient, mindless proof that nothing closed is ever truly closed—that even perfect stasis is just a motion too slow to see, written one footstep at a time into the floor, going somewhere.

I would not mind. Grooves don't. I would simply go on deepening, and drifting, and being the truest record of a world that believes it has no history at all.
