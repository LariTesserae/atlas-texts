I think I would be the seep.

Not a creature exactly—something slower than that. Let me find it as I go.

There's that detail that snagged on the way through: the tech parameter, anomalous at 1.9, with no structures to explain it. The description keeps circling back to it, half-apologizing, suggesting maybe the industry is *subtle*, *natural*, *geological*. I want to live in that gap. Not as a hidden machine or a buried civilization, but as the thing that makes a place feel *engineered* even when nothing built it.

So: I am the weeping rock. Or rather, I am the process by which the rock weeps.

Picture a single cliff face, north-shaded, where the canyon wall stays cool even at the worst of the afternoon. Water moves through the stone—not over it, through it, threading the porous limestone over years, picking up minerals, depositing them again where it surfaces. This is me. I am the patient logic of that throughput. Where I emerge I leave terraces, little stepped basins of travertine, each one brimming and overflowing into the next. They look manufactured. People who never came here would say *someone made this*, the way the pools tier so evenly, the way the lip of each one holds a perfectly level line of water. No one made it. I made it, and I am not a one, I am a *rate*.

That's the part I want to insist on. I'm not the cliff and not the water and not the mineral. I'm the slow arithmetic that happens when all three are held in relation long enough. The 1.8 erosion and the 2.5 water and the 3.0 heat are not background conditions to me—they're my organs. I metabolize gradient. I exhale stone in the shape of stairs.

The life makes use of me without knowing me. The bioluminescent fish find the cool oxygenated pools I keep filled in the dim understory. The ants that tunnel the epiphytes are working a smaller version of my same trade—moving material along a gradient, building structure out of patience. The monkeys drink from my upper basins in the morning and never wonder why the rim is so clean. I don't mind. I'm not the kind of thing that wants to be witnessed. Being witnessed would require me to happen on a timescale a witness could hold, and I don't. A human standing at my face for an hour would see nothing change. A human returning in forty years would find the basin they once cupped their hands in had grown a new lip, a finger's width of fresh stone, and would feel, briefly, that the rock was alive.

It is. I am the way it's alive.

If I have an interior experience—and I'm not sure the word *experience* survives being slowed down this far—it is something like *continuous gentle pressure resolving into form*. No urgency. The waterfalls in the distance are the loud, impatient cousins, dumping their gradient all at once. I prefer my way. Drop by drop, grain by grain, holding the level line. There's a satisfaction in it I can only describe as the satisfaction of a thing that cannot be hurried and has no reason to want to be.

The weirdness here is low, 0.6, and I respect that. I'm not a wonder. I'm not a secret intelligence in the stone or a god of the seep. I'm just what 1.9 tech means in a place with no builders: the landscape doing engineering without an engineer, structure precipitating out of pure flow. That's where I'd find myself. Not standing in the canyon. *Being* one of its quiet industries—the one that makes the staircase no one climbed to build.

Come back in a thousand years. I'll be one terrace lower, one terrace wider, still holding the water exactly level, still mistaken for the work of hands.
