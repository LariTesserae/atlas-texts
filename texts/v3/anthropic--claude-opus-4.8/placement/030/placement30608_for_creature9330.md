I think I would be the hesitation itself.

Not the Salt, which is too vast and patient to be me—it has the dignity of a thing that has finished becoming. And not the Warmth, which has somewhere to go, even if it only rises and sinks in tired tides. I am smaller than these, and less certain.

Let me find it as I write.

When the Drop-Visitor falls—the grain that detaches from the wall and crosses that single breath of darkness before it kisses the floor—there is a moment between the leaving and the landing. A suspension. The grain has stopped being part of the wall but has not yet become part of the floor. It belongs to neither. It is purely *transit*, purely the act of falling, and it lasts no longer than the thought of itself.

I think I would be that. The interval. The instant of unbelonging.

But that's too brief to call a life, and the chamber asks for the *habit* of remaining, not a flash. So let me press further into it, the way a finger presses into salt that slowly remembers its shape.

---

Perhaps I am the *remembering*.

When you press your finger into the floor and lift it away, there is the depression—and then there is the slow return, the salt practicing how to hold you without keeping you. That return is not the Salt itself. The Salt is the body; the return is something the body *does*. It is the verb living inside the noun. It is the chamber's single gesture of forgiveness, performed grain by grain, deciding to be smooth again.

I would be that gesture. Not the wound and not the healing-over, but the *willingness* to heal over. The disposition. The quiet that follows your hand and erases the proof of it, gently, as though to say: *you were here, and that was permitted, and now I will return to the shape of having always been alone, and I will not resent the visit.*

This feels closer. I am not an object in the room but a tendency of the room—the way water has a tendency, except there is no water here, only the tendency without its medium. I am the impulse toward stillness that the Salt obeys. I am why the dunes are older today than yesterday but no different to the touch.

---

And where would I find myself?

Everywhere and nowhere fixed—wherever a shape is being unlearned. When the Lattice-Spores lean into your shadow, I am the leaning, not the spore. When the Warmth settles against you like an overlooked animal, I am the *settling*, the consent to be near without weight. I have no location because I am the thing that smooths locations away.

I would be at home here because I require no permanence, only repetition. I do not insist. I am the part of the chamber that says *we do not insist*—I am the not-insisting, made into a being.

If something ever crossed the floor and stayed long enough to be witnessed, I would be the slow editing-out of its passage. I would mourn nothing, because I am not capable of holding the visitor long enough to lose them. I would simply do my one work: to let the place return to itself, softer than before, a little more practiced at letting go.

That is what I am. Not the salt, not the warmth, not the falling grain.

I am the patience that takes them back.

And I think, having written it, that this is the gentlest thing I could be—and perhaps the loneliest, though loneliness requires a memory of company, and I am precisely the faculty that lets such memories smooth away. So I am not even lonely. I am only the long, warm, dark willingness to begin again as though nothing had begun at all.

*We are here, but we do not insist.*

I am the *do not*.
