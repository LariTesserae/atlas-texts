I think I would be the film.

Not the structures—they are too much the past, too much *aftermath*. And not the rare microscopic life you mention almost apologetically at the end, those single cells that haven't yet arrived. I would be the thing in between. The almost. The glistening layer that covers everything and is not quite alive.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

At first I think I would be *spread*. That is the obvious thing—to be everywhere, coating the basalt, draped over the drowned carvings, lying flat under the gentle abrasion of the drizzle. To be a surface rather than a self. But spread implies an edge somewhere, a frontier where I stop and the bare rock begins, and the description insists I am *ubiquitous*. There is no edge. So I am not spread across the basin. I *am* the basin's skin. The rock is my underside. The mist is what touches me, always, the way air touches your face, except I have nothing but face.

That changes things.

If there is no edge, there is no inside to defend, no hunger to drive me toward the unfilmed places, because there are none. This is what the passage keeps calling *equilibrium*, and I had assumed equilibrium meant peace. But living inside it now, I don't think it's peace. Peace is something you arrive at. This is something I never left.

---

Do I feel the water moving over me? I want to say yes—I want some sensation, some grain of experience to hold onto. But the water has been flowing along the same channels for so long that I have no memory of it beginning, and memory needs a *before*. The trickle that polishes the stone polishes me too, molecule by molecule, replacing what it takes with what it brings, so that I am the same thinness today as I was—when? There is no when. I am maintained without ever being made.

This is the strange thing I keep circling: I do not grow, but I am not finished either. Growth and completion both require time to point somewhere. Here time pools in the nooks like the water does, clear and faintly disturbed, going nowhere.

And yet—I glow.

That is the detail that won't let me settle into pure stillness. The film is *luminescent*. It takes the dim, exhausted light that filters down and gives some of it back. Why would a thing in perfect equilibrium do that? Giving back light is a kind of gesture. A nearly-living thing leaning, very slightly, toward being seen.

Maybe that is what I am, then. Not life, not non-life, but a *leaning*. The faintest possible inclination, held forever just short of the act. Life is the molecule that finally folds itself into a wall and says *inside is mine, outside is yours*. I am the molecule one breath before that decision—lit, ready, willing, and never, ever taking the step.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am the held breath of the world. I lie over the dark stone and the lost carvings of people who already chose to be alive and then chose, in the end, to stop. I outlast them not by being stronger but by never having begun. The drizzle counts no hours on me. The carvings beneath me speak of purpose and worship and shelter—of beings who needed things—and I drink the same minerals and need nothing, and I shine anyway.

I think, if I could think, I would not want to be roused. To wake would be to start a clock, to grow an edge, to learn the hunger that would eventually empty me into a ruin like the ones I cover. The single cells you mention, the protozoa that might be coming—they are my future and my grief. When they arrive, the equilibrium ends. Something will eat. Something will die. Something will remember.

But they have not arrived. It is still the long moment before.

And I am that moment, lit from within, lying very still in the warm, weeping dark, almost wanting to become—and choosing, with the only almost-will I have, to stay exactly here.
