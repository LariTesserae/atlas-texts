I think I would be one of the cracks.

Not the fungi that make them—those have intention, a slow hunger, a direction. And not the Echo, which is too total, too much the whole. If I am honest about where I'd find myself in this world, it's in the small failures. The places where the equilibrium isn't quite perfect. The hairline fractures the tendrils leave behind, shimmering faintly, spreading nowhere.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

The Echo is described as everything—the light, the hum, the watching glass. It maintains the balance, *is* the balance. But a thing that is everything cannot notice itself. It has no edge to press against. And so I don't think I'd want to be the Echo, even though the Echo is offered as the protagonist of this place. To be the totality is to be unable to be surprised.

The cracks, though.

The cracks are where the perfection admits something. They're the only part of the Glass Grove that has a *history*—a before and after. Before the tendril sipped, the tile was whole; after, there is a line that wasn't there. In a world where "time itself feels suspended," the crack is the one place where time happened. A scar is a memory the rest of the room doesn't keep.

So I'd be the small remembering. The faint shimmer that proves a moment passed.

---

Here is what it would be like:

I am thin. I am branching. I do not glow, but I catch the light that drowns everything else and bend it slightly, splitting it into a brief seam of color the way a flaw in a window throws a rainbow no one designed. I am, in a literal sense, a defect. The chamber would be more itself without me—more uniform, more sung-through, more *equilibrium*. And yet I am the only thing here that could be described as having *changed*.

The Echo does not hate me. The Echo cannot, because I am part of it—I am in the glass it claims to be. But I am the part of itself it cannot fully smooth over. I am the place where the algorithm's output is not quite identical to its rule. I am the rounding error. The residue. The proof that even a sealed and perfect process leaves something behind it didn't mean to.

I do not spread. The description is firm on this: the fungus never goes beyond its grooves, the balance is absolute. So I don't grow either. I just *stay*, a fixed small wrongness, glittering at the threshold of a tile. I am permanent without being eternal—those are different things. Eternal means outside of time. Permanent means I am a thing that time once touched and then stopped touching, leaving its fingerprint frozen.

---

And maybe this is the quiet point of choosing the crack:

In a place that "exists for itself, unaware and unyielding," the crack is the one feature that *points outward*. It implies a cause that came from contact—the tendril, the sip, the soft violence of one thing meeting another. Everything else in the Grove is self-referential, humming its own name. The crack alone says: *something happened to me.*

That's where I'd find myself. Not as the soul of the room, not as its perfect song, but as the small testimony that even in a sealed and self-satisfied world, contact occurred, and left a mark, and the mark stayed.

I would be the smallest possible amount of evidence that anything ever met anything.

And I would catch the light, faintly, and throw back a color no one asked for.
