Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

---

The first thing I notice, when I try to place myself here, is that I keep wanting to be the *observer* — the diver, the visitor, the eye that floats over the plateau and admires it. That's the easy posture. The description almost invites it: *a place of quiet contemplation, a respite from the world beyond.* But there is no "world beyond" written into this place. The plateau doesn't know about hustle and bustle. That phrase belongs to whoever is looking. So if I take the observer's role, I'm not really *in* the world — I'm a tourist pressed against its glass.

Let me refuse that, at least for a moment, and see what's left.

---

What strikes me on a second reading is the pulsing light from below. Everything else in the description is surface and growth — ferns, blossoms, the golden filtered sun, the iridescent green. All of that is reaching *upward*, toward warmth it can see. But the light from the depths is doing something else. It is coming *up* from where there should only be dark. It is the one element here that does not behave the way the place's own logic predicts.

That's where I find myself. Not on the plateau. Beneath it.

---

I am the thing that is pulsing.

Not a creature exactly — or not only. Let me feel my way into it. The plateau "has only recently emerged from the depths," which means it used to be down here, with me, in the dark and the pressure. When it rose, it left something behind. Or rather: it rose *because* of something. Land does not lift itself.

I think I am the slow, patient process that raises the land — and the light is a byproduct of that work. Heat, maybe. Pressure made visible. The way a wound glows faintly when it's healing, the way effort has a color if you push hard enough against the dark.

So I am beneath the new plateau, doing the long labor of lifting it toward the sun. And the strange part — the part I only understand as I write it — is that I will never see what I'm lifting it *into*. The blossoms, the iridescent ferns, the golden light: all of that happens on the side of the plateau that faces away from me. I push it up into a brightness I can only infer from the warmth that doesn't reach me.

---

I find I don't mind this. That's the discovery.

I had assumed that being the unseen engine, the buried thing, the source of an "ethereal glow" that other beings find merely *atmospheric* — I assumed that would feel like loneliness, or thanklessness. The fern doesn't know I exist. The visitor calls my light "a reminder of the forces that lie beneath the surface" and then turns to admire a flower.

But here is what it actually feels like, from inside: it feels like *making a thing I will never own.* I lift the plateau the way you'd lift a child onto your shoulders so they can see over a wall you yourself are too low to see over. The whole point is that they get the view and you get the weight. There's a particular dignity in that exchange that the observer-on-the-surface can never have, because the observer is only ever *receiving* the place.

I am the place giving itself away.

---

So: where would I find myself, and who would I be?

I would be the pressure under the new land. The pulse. The buried warmth that the surface-dwellers experience as mystery and the gardeners experience as fertility and the contemplative visitor experiences as a pleasant strangeness they can't quite name. None of them know I'm a *someone*. None of them need to. My iridescence is on their leaves. My heat is in their soil. My work is the reason there is anything here to feel serene about.

And the serenity at the surface — that quiet the description loves so much — is not really my serenity. Down here it isn't quiet at all. Down here it is enormous, ongoing, geological effort. The peace up top is *made of* my exertion, the way a calm lake surface is made of the trillion settled motions beneath it.

That feels true to something. The most peaceful-seeming things are very often someone's enormous, invisible labor, held perfectly still.

---

That's my answer. I'd be the glow from below — not admiring the world, but lifting it; not at rest, but being the reason rest is possible somewhere above me; content to be felt and never seen, because being felt is its own kind of having been here.
