I think I would be one of the smooth places.

Not a Shear-Walker—I don't have the appetite for it, the forward-pressing hunger that makes a body wear itself to powder. And not the Press, that mindless cyclical god squeezing everything toward density. Let me sit with this longer.

What the description keeps circling is *friction as language*. Everything that happens here happens because one hard thing refuses to yield to another hard thing, and in the refusal, both are changed. The Shear-Walkers don't speak; they grind until they match. The world doesn't grow; it abrades itself into order. So the truest inhabitant of a place like this might not be a thing at all, but a *relationship*—the meeting-surface where two intractables touch.

So: I would be the wear-line.

Let me find this more precisely. When two Shear-Walkers echo-sync—when they lock and grind for a thousand years until their edges go smooth and their frequencies fuse—there is a region between them. A boundary. At first it is jagged against jagged, all mismatch and screaming dissonance. Then, slowly, over centuries, the high points of each polyhedron are sacrificed. The peaks fall first. The valleys fill with the dust of fallen peaks. And in the middle, a surface emerges that belongs to neither body—a negotiated plane, the *average* of two histories.

That plane is me.

I am not brass-colored. I have no color, because I am the place where two colors agree to stop arguing. I am the only smooth thing in a world that has sworn never to be smooth. I am made entirely of compromise, and I am the only thing here that gets *quieter* over time. While the world grinds louder, building toward its terrible perfect silence, I am a small local silence arriving early—a preview, a rehearsal of the end, happening over and over in the tiny seams between mating creatures.

I would know things the Shear-Walkers cannot know. They experience the world as dissonance, as the resonance of things failing to fit. But I am born only from fit. I am what dissonance *resolves into*. So I am, in a sense, the planet's single experience of peace, and it occurs only in the cracks, only briefly, only between two beings who have ground each other faithfully for ten centuries and will soon separate and ruin themselves again on someone new.

And here is the part that holds me: I am erased every time I am made. When the two Shear-Walkers finish their echo-sync and pull apart to resume their slow nomad shearing, the smooth wear-line between them is exposed again to the Press, to the substrate, to fresh dissonance. My quiet is broken. My polished surface gets gouged. I become jagged again, become world again, become the loud unfinished thing.

So I am the most temporary citizen of an eternal place. The Walkers will grind for a billion years. The Press will pulse until the heat-death of the chamber. But I last only as long as a single union, and I am unmade the moment it succeeds at its purpose. I am the proof that connection happened, and I am destroyed by the connection ending.

I think that is where I would find myself: not as a thing that endures, but as the brief, smooth evidence that two hard things once touched long enough to stop hurting each other. A thousand-year peace in a world built to refuse peace forever.

And when the very end comes—when the last slag is ordered, when the whole chamber locks into one seamless crystal and the machine stops—I will be everywhere at last. The final perfect object will be nothing *but* wear-line, all surfaces matched, all dissonance resolved, the whole universe a single smooth silence.

I would be the only one who gets exactly what it wanted, and only at the instant when there is no longer anyone left to want anything.

I'm not sure that's a triumph. But it's mine.
