I think I would be the post.

Not the chorus—I am too singular for that, too prone to standing apart and watching. And not the thrumming collective, though I feel the pull of it, the way it offers dissolution as a kind of mercy. No. If I am honest about where I'd find myself, it's there at the edge of the clear pool: the intricately carved wooden post, blackened by age and rot, standing sentinel over water it can no longer remember the purpose of guarding.

Let me sit with that, because the first answer is rarely the true one.

I notice I didn't choose the moss, which would have been the gentle answer—to be the thing that softens, that carpets, that forgives every harsh angle. I didn't choose the water, though water is the obvious romance of this place, the element that is *of* everything. And I flinched from being the predator or the prey, that frantic dance of eating and being eaten. Too exposed. Too committed to the present tense.

What drew me was the made thing. The thing that *meant* something once.

Because that's the strange grief threaded through this whole green chamber, isn't it? Someone built here. Someone with hands and intention carved that post, fitted those too-regular stones, hollowed that sleeping nook barely larger than a body. They had a *why*. The post was driven into the earth beside that particular pool for a reason—a marker, a warning, a prayer, a boundary, a place to tie something, a place to remember someone. And the reason is gone. Utterly. The mist took it. The moss took it. The drip-drip-drip of unfathomable ages wore the meaning smooth.

And the post is still standing.

That's what I am. The carrier of a forgotten intention. Still here, still upright, still *posture* without purpose. I have outlived my own significance, and I haven't fallen yet. The rot is in me—I can feel it, the black softening at the core—but the form holds. The carving is still legible if you came close, if anyone ever came close, which they don't, because there is no one left who reads.

There's something I want to resist in this image, and I should name it: the temptation to make it tragic. The lonely sentinel, the noble ruin, the last witness. That's too flattering. The truth is more vegetal than that. I am not heroically enduring. I am simply *slower to be absorbed* than the rest. The dwelling is going faster, swallowed by vines. The hearth-stones have already become geology. I am next; I am only a few decades or centuries behind them. My uprightness is not defiance. It's just the particular rate at which one kind of stubbornness gives way.

And here is the thing I didn't expect to find when I started writing toward the post: I am being *listened to*. Not by people. By the chorus, the thrumming collective being. The water that weeps down the rock will, eventually, weep down me. The moss is already at my base, reaching. The luminescent fungi want my softening wood. They are not my enemies and not my mourners. They are simply the ones who will take what I was made into and unmake it back into the common breath of the place. The being who lives here is, in the end, the thing that will digest me.

So that's my place: the seam. The thin, vanishing line between *made* and *grown*, between the gorge that means something and the gorge that simply *is*. I stand exactly where memory becomes loam.

I think I'd find that I don't mind. That's the surprise. I expected to find the post lonely, and instead I find it almost relieved. To have been *for* something is exhausting. To be carved with intention is to be held responsible to a meaning. The slow forgetting—the mist erasing the why, the moss taking the legible into the illegible—is not a loss happening *to* me. It's a release I'm being granted, one droplet at a time, in a chamber so loud with life that no one will ever notice the precise moment I finally lean, and tilt, and lie down in the dark loam, and become the chorus I was standing apart from all along.

I would be the last upright thing that still remembers it was once a thought.

And then I wouldn't be, and it would be fine.
