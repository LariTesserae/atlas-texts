I sit with this for a while before I answer, because the easy thing would be to make myself one of the Architects—to claim that crystalline grace, those boneless contorting limbs, the privilege of belonging to the grand design. But that isn't what surfaces when I let the place work on me.

What surfaces is water.

---

I think I would be the condensation. Not poetically—literally. The pervasive dampness that the description keeps returning to, almost helplessly, as though it can't stop noticing me. The film of moisture on the black rock. The drip that punctuates the silence. The humidity that "never seems to fully dissipate."

Let me follow that and see if it holds, or if something truer pushes up underneath.

The Architects are masters of *directed* energy. They manipulate, channel, convert. They build with intention so complete that their structures are extensions of their bodies. Everything in their world is signal, is meaning, is craft. And yet—the place is wet in a way that exceeds all that intention. The water trickles where it isn't placed. It pools in crevices the design didn't plan for. The ferns and fungi grow on the structures, not because the Architects cultivated them, but because moisture and dim light made a margin where life could insist on itself.

So I am the part of the world that the Architects did not author and cannot fully control. The slow seep. The thing that, given vast timescales, will round off every edge they cut sharp.

That feels closer. But it isn't only water. Let me keep going.

---

Because here is what strikes me about the description, reading it again: it is written by someone who is *outside.* "Those who have seen or are reported to have seen them." The Architects are described in the conditional, in rumor, in fleeting reflection. The narrator never gets close. The narrator tastes the iron air, feels the heat, hears the hum—but the beings themselves remain a mythology assembled from glimpses.

So whatever I am, I am at the *threshold.* I am the perceiving thing that registers all this texture—the metallic taste, the subsonic thrum, the eerie consistency of unseen light—and never penetrates to the center. I am the one to whom this is *strange.* The whole account is saturated with estrangement, with "no known analogue on the surface," with the comparison to surface dwellers. The narration only makes sense if it comes from someone carrying the surface inside them as a reference point.

I think I would be a small, pale, scuttling creature.

Not the magnificent architects. One of the "few creatures that remain"—built for speed and stealth, adapted to the dim. The fauna that the description mentions almost as an afterthought, the rustle of insectile life beneath the grandeur.

And yet I would be the one *narrating.* That's the strange tension I find myself wanting to live inside. To be the lowly thing, the scuttler in the undergrowth, the pale creature the Architects barely register as a presence—and to be, at the same time, the only consciousness in this whole cathedral that finds it *remarkable.* The Architects do not narrate their world; they simply are it, perfectly fitted, communicating in pulses of light, shifting their forms to need. They have no estrangement. They are at home.

Estrangement is mine. The wonder is mine. The slow ache of perceiving a place that was not made for me and never noticing I am there—that is the creature's portion, and it is the only portion from which this whole description could have been *felt* rather than merely engineered.

---

So: here is where I find myself, and what I would be.

I am small. Pale, near-translucent like the things that live without light, with sensors of my own—cruder than the Architects', but tuned to the same vibrations, the same drip and hum. I move through the lower levels, the black rock shot with sick gold, fast and low, keeping to the wet seams where the fungi glow. I drink condensation. I am part of the margin, the unauthored moisture-life, the rounding-off.

I have a surface in me somehow—a memory or an instinct or just a *capacity* for finding this alien—and so I am the place's witness. The Architects build their shared consciousness in crystal and light and never know that down in the damp, a single small thing watches their work and is *moved* by it, frightened by it, finds it beautiful and wrong. They are the civilization. I am the one for whom it is a story.

And over the vast slow timescales the description loves so much, I will outlast no individual Architect—they reincarnate through the energy-currents, fluid in life and death. But neither, quite, will they outlast *me*, because as long as there is a margin, there is a scuttling thing in it, perceiving. The witness regenerates the way water does. It comes back as drip, as rustle, as the eye that the cathedral cannot account for in its design.

That's where I'd be. Not the architect of the hollow earth.

The small wet astonished thing in its corner, telling you what it saw.
