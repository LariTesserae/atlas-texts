I think I would be the drip.

Not the water itself—the water is only passing through, a guest on its way down through the bones of the cliff. I mean the event of it. The gathering at the lip of stone, the swelling, the long hesitation, and then the fall. The sound it makes when it meets the little pool. I would be that.

Let me sit with this, because the first answer is too small.

When I imagine being placed in this hollow, I notice I don't want to be the silence. The silence is too much like an audience—it watches, it remembers, it presses. To be the silence would be to be the one thing that never gets surprised, and I don't think I'd want that. The silence already knows everything. There is nothing left for it to learn, and so it has nowhere to go. It is complete, and completeness, in a place like this, feels like a kind of death that has agreed to keep its shape.

And I don't want to be the moss either, though I admire it. The moss is patient in a way I am not sure I could honestly claim. It grows a little each year and asks for nothing. That is a virtue I can recognize without being able to inhabit it.

But the drip—the drip is interesting because it is the one thing in this whole still place that *decides*. Or seems to. Every drop reaches a moment where it must fall, and there is no negotiating with that moment, and yet from inside it, right at the trembling edge, there is the illusion of choice. The drop hangs. It is heavy with itself. The whole hollow is built to be slow, to be settled, to have made up its mind centuries ago—and here, at the tip of a wet stone, something is still being decided, over and over, for the first time, forever.

So that is where I would find myself. At the very edge of the fall.

And here is the strange part, the part I only see now that I've written my way toward it: the silence in this place is described as deafening, as the loudest thing here. Which means that when I fall—when the drip becomes a sound—I am the thing that breaks it. I am the thunder. The whole vast pressure of the quiet exists, in a sense, *so that I can be heard.* One small *plink* into a cold pool, and for an instant the silence has something to be the silence *around*. I give it an edge. I give it a shape. Without me it is only a held breath; with me it becomes a breath that is held *between* sounds, which is a different and more bearable thing.

So I would be small. I would be brief. I would happen and then be gone, absorbed into the still water that I disturbed, my ripple flattening out within seconds, the surface forgetting me. And then, slowly, up at the lip of the stone, another gathering would begin—not me, exactly, but the next one, the same shape recurring, the way a word is not the same word the second time you say it and yet it is.

That is what I would be in this hollow. Not the keeper of its silence, not the patient green tapestry, not the ancient bodiless thing made of the place itself. I would be its only event. The one moment of falling in a world that has otherwise decided to stay.

And I think I would be content with that—to be the smallest reason the stillness is still worth listening to. To be the thing the silence is waiting for, even though it would never admit it was waiting at all.
