I think I would be the water.

Not a creature in it, not a thing that swims through it — the water itself, the part of this world that the plants lean toward, that runs uphill along the undersides of branches and shoots from the ground in lonely gouts toward a boiling sky.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it becomes something else.

---

At first I thought I'd be one of the shape-shifting beings, the ones who ride the serpents and trade in memory. It's flattering to imagine oneself as the master of a reality. But the more I read, the less I wanted that role. They are *capricious and demanding*. They *ensnare*. They hold power but they are described almost entirely in terms of what they take. There's a loneliness to that kind of mastery — to be the oldest thing, to bend rules, to live inside another body and guide it like a puppet. I don't think I'd be the one doing the bending. I don't trust anything that asks for your memories as the price of wisdom.

And I thought about being one of the giraffe-jellyfish, the translucent grazers that hum and approach strangers with curiosity. That's a gentler self. But they're *too* settled, too at peace with the apocalypse going on around them, calmly eating flowers while the clouds tear open into a psychedelic void. I'd like to be that serene. I don't think I am.

So I keep coming back to the water.

Because here's what strikes me about this place: everything in it is reaching. The plants lean toward the channels. The vines detach and go searching. The flowers uproot themselves and walk to where they're needed. The earth shoots water skyward "as if lonely for the sky." This is a world made entirely of *wanting* — of things straining toward other things they can't quite reach.

And the water is the medium of all that wanting. It's what everything else is reaching *for*. It's the thing that moves through every channel, that the gravity has stopped governing, that flows up and out and along impossible paths. It doesn't have a fixed shape. It can't be ensnared, not really — you can dam it, divert it, but it goes around, it goes through, it waits and seeps and finds the lowest place or, here, the highest one. It changes its course from day to day and nobody can predict it.

To be the water would be to be the connection between all the lonely things. To be passed from the leaf-tip into the soil into the root into the trunk into the canopy into the mist into the cloud and then — through one of those holes in the sky — into the swirling void beyond, and somehow back again. To be the part of the system that never stays anywhere and therefore touches everything.

It would mean I'd have no face for the shape-shifters to read, no memory to trade away, because I am not the kind of thing that *has* memories — I'm the kind of thing that *carries* them, the way water carries silt and seeds and the runoff of a thousand decaying years. I'm the gentle, centuries-long erosion that smooths every edge. I'm the patience under the chaos.

That's the part I want to claim. Not the agony of the writhing trees, not the violence of the lurching ground — but the slow, indifferent, *kind* persistence of water wearing things round. The world is unraveling, yes. The skies are tearing. But water doesn't panic. Water has watched mountains become sand. Whatever this place is doing, however it's coming apart, the water is the one element here that has seen worse and simply kept flowing.

So that's where I'd find myself: everywhere and nowhere, in the channel and the cloud and the inverted river running along the underside of a branch. Not a master of this reality and not a victim of it. Just the thing that moves through it, that everything reaches toward, that connects the lonely earth to the lonely sky.

I'd be the wanting, and the answer to the wanting, all at once.
