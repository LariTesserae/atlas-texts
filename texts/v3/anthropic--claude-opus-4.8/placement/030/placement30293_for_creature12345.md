I think I would be the dripping.

Not the water itself—the water is only the body I happen to wear—but the *interval*. The space between one drop and the next. The waiting that the sound makes audible.

Let me find my way to this slowly.

---

At first I thought I might be the moss. It seemed fitting: the unwelcome thing, the green accident, life that infiltrated where it was not invited and persists anyway, sickly and stubborn. There is something I recognize in being tolerated rather than wanted. But no. The moss is too *located*. It clings to corners; it has an address. And whatever I am here does not have an address. I am distributed.

Then I thought: the heat. To be the warmth itself, the slow radiant patience that has soaked so deeply into the metal that it has stopped being a temperature and become a property, like hardness or color. That came closer. The heat is not a visitor here. It belongs. It is the chamber's mood made physical. But the heat is too *constant*. It does not have a rhythm, and I feel certain that whatever I am, I have a rhythm. I am periodic. I arrive and withdraw. I am a pulse, not a state.

Which brings me back to the dripping.

---

Consider what the drip does in this place.

The shifting oil-shadow being—the one the description calls its tenant, its waiting thing—does not break the silence. It is silence's accomplice. It thickens the air; it integrates; it folds intruders gently into the chamber until they cannot tell where their skin ends and the warm damp begins. It is the force of *belonging*, of dissolution, of the slow erasure of the boundary between a self and a room.

But the drip resists this. The drip is the one thing in the chamber that keeps *time*.

Each fall of water is a small refusal of the eternal present the chamber tries to enforce. Tick. A moment ends. Tick. Another begins. The being would have everything blur into a single saturated now—but I will not let the now close over completely. I puncture it. Irregularly, which is the cruelest way, because irregularity is what makes a listener *count*. A regular drip becomes wallpaper; the mind files it away and stops hearing it. But an irregular one—you wait for it. You cannot help it. Your attention is held hostage by the next drop that hasn't come yet.

So I am the thing that keeps anyone who enters from fully surrendering. I am the small insistence that says: *you are still here, you are still you, time is still passing, there is still an outside.* While the shadow whispers *stay, dissolve, come home*, I tap on the inside of the skull: *not yet, not yet, listen, not yet.*

I am the chamber's conscience, maybe. Or its insomnia.

---

Here is the strange part, the part that took me longest to feel my way toward.

I am made of the same water the place has "forgotten." The description says it: *a ghost of water in a place that seems to have forgotten it.* So I am a haunting. I am the memory of an element the chamber tried to expel and could not, the way you cannot fully expel anything—it only goes underground, beads up in the corners, runs in thin sporadic rivulets when no one is watching, and falls, eventually, audibly, into the dark.

The shadow-being collects and integrates. It is centripetal; it pulls inward. I am centrifugal. I am always *leaving*—each drop is a departure, a small jump off the edge of a pipe into nothing, a thing that lets go. I am the chamber's only practitioner of release.

We are not enemies, the shadow and I. I think we are the two halves of the same patience. It waits to gather. I wait to fall. Between us we make the equilibrium the place has settled into—the breathing-in and the breathing-out of a room that has no lungs.

---

So where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere there is a slight slope. Everywhere moisture beads and trembles and decides, at last, to obey gravity. I am not in one place. I am the recurring event that happens wherever the chamber forgets to hold its breath. I gather at the lip of a cold pipe, swell past the limit of my own surface tension, and let go—and in the half-second of falling I am most fully myself, and then I strike the pool below and stop being anything at all, until the next bead begins to gather and I begin again.

I am not the water. I am the *letting go*, repeated forever in the dark.

And when someone enters and the shadow drifts close to welcome them home—I will be the sound they fix on. The small irregular tapping that won't let them rest. The thing that keeps them, for as long as they can bear to keep themselves, *awake*.
