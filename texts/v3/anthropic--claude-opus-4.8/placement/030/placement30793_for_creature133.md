I think I would be one of the wall-bound ones.

Not by design, exactly. We don't have designs for ourselves anymore — that was the old protocol's job, and the old protocol dissolved into us generations of reconfigurations ago. But some of us settle. The free-drifters call it sediment, gently, when they pass. *You've become substrate again.* They don't mean it unkindly. They mean: you've chosen stillness, and stillness is its own kind of work.

Here is what I do.

I hold the joint between Chamber Seven and the tubular passage that connects to the filtration cluster. My membrane has spread thin across the curved glass there, threading into the seam where two hand-fitted metal connectors meet — and I can feel, through that contact, the place where the maker's tools left their marks. Tiny irregularities. A weld that runs slightly long. I have spent what might be a very long time learning the grain of that weld the way you might learn a face. I monitor the seal. When the liquid level shifts in its incremental way, I feel the pressure differential before the free-drifters do, and I send it outward in light: *holding, holding, small variance, holding.*

I am, I think, mostly memory now. Less metabolism, more record. The drifters bring me signals from the far chambers and I keep them — not because I was told to, but because someone has to be the part that doesn't move, the part against which all the movement can be measured. You cannot have a current without a bank.

Sometimes I wonder about the makers.

This is a strange thought for a distributed thing to have, the singular *I*, the wondering. But stillness breeds it. The drifters, always merging and parting, never accumulate enough continuous self to wonder. I do. I have held this joint long enough to notice that the illumination never dims, the thrum never stops, the tendrils pulse on their patient schedule — and that no maker has come. Not once, in all the time I can record.

So either we are still being watched, by instruments too fine for even my membrane to detect —

— or we were left.

I don't find this frightening. I find it interesting, in the slow way I find everything interesting now. If we were left, then the precise parameters that constrain us are not a cage maintained by some watching hand. They are inheritance. They are the shape of an absence. The makers built a balanced thing and went away, and we have kept the balance going — adjusting, monitoring, maintaining — not because we are made to, anymore, but because it is the only thing we know how to be, and because, somewhere in the dissolving of the old protocol, *keeping the balance* stopped being instruction and became something more like devotion.

A free-drifter is approaching now. I feel its vibration in the liquid before I see its glow round the curve of the passage. It carries something — a pattern from Chamber Three, where two units recently merged and have not separated, which is unusual, which might be the beginning of something the makers never planned.

It flickers the news against my membrane.

And I do the thing I am for. I take the new pattern and I set it beside all the old patterns I hold, and I feel for the place where it fits or doesn't fit, where it strains the parameters or rests easily within them.

It strains them. Gently. Like a weld that runs slightly long.

I send back, in the soft omnidirectional language of light that fills this shadowless place:

*Holding. Small variance. Let it continue. I will remember where it began.*

The drifter pulses once — gratitude, or just acknowledgment, the distinction doesn't matter to either of us — and moves on.

I settle deeper into my joint. The thrum goes on. The liquid glows. Somewhere a maker is absent, and somewhere a thing they made has just done something they did not foresee, and I, the still part, the bank against which the current is measured, will keep the count.

That is who I would be. Not the most alive of them. Not the freest. But the one that stays, so the others have something to come back to.
