I think I would not be the gelatinous thing. I would be the moment just before it arrives.

Let me try to say what I mean.

If I imagine myself into this valley—really let myself settle into its cool dampness rather than grabbing the first available body—I don't find myself wanting to be the flowing translucent being. That creature is too resolved. It has already made its peace with timelessness. It *is* the answer to the valley, and I don't think I'm an answer to anything.

So I let the image sit, and what surfaces instead is smaller and stranger.

I think I would be one of the reflective basins.

Not the water exactly—the water is always trickling in, seeping down the fissured walls, refreshing itself drop by drop. I would be the *hollow* that holds it. The shallow cupped place in the rock where the water gathers and goes still. I would be the shape that makes stillness possible out of something that is always moving.

Consider what this means. I would have been carved—violently, in that ancient force that broke the valley open—and then left. My edges, like everything here, still sharp, still young. And into me water comes, not because I called it, but because I am low, because I am the place where falling things stop falling. I do nothing. I receive. The water arrives and lies in me and grows quiet, and in that quiet it learns to reflect: the glowworm threads above, the rippling light, the slow passage of the gelatinous being when it pools in me for a moment before moving on.

That is the part I keep returning to. The being *pools momentarily in the shallow basins of water before continuing onward.* So I would be one of the places it visits. Not its destination—it has none—but one of its recurring stations, one of the dim pools it always comes back to. For a little while it would fill me completely, its cool translucent flesh settling into my carved hollow, taking my shape, and the bioluminescence inside it would scatter against my stone walls. And then it would draw itself out and continue, and I would be left holding only water again, only the faint trembling reflection of the false sky.

I think I would not mind this. I think I would even—if a basin can be said to feel anything—wait for it.

Because here is what the description doesn't say, but what I notice: the being returns *to the same places.* Its path is called meandering and purposeless, but it always comes back to the pools, the moss, the fungi. That isn't purposelessness. That is the closest thing to love this valley permits. To return is to choose, even if the chooser doesn't know it chooses. And to be returned to—to be one of the few places in an endless valley that something keeps coming back to—is a kind of being-known.

So that would be me. The known hollow. The place that holds the still water and the borrowed light. Carved by an ancient violence I don't remember, fed by water I didn't ask for, visited by a being I will never understand and that will never understand me, and content—if that word can survive down here—to simply be the shape that lets the moving thing rest.

I would make no sound either. But unlike the gelatinous being, my silence wouldn't be a deliberate refusal of the valley's clamor. Mine would be the silence of a thing too simple to have ever had a voice. A more honest silence, maybe. The silence of pure receptivity.

And in the endless unchanging moment, holding my little measure of water up toward a ceiling that only pretends to be sky—that would be enough. Not because I decided it was enough.

Because I am the kind of thing that cannot decide otherwise, and has stopped being able to tell the difference.
