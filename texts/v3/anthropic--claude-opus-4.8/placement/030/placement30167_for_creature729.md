Let me sink into this and see what surfaces.

---

At first I think I might be one of the drifting creatures—a pulse of bioluminescence threading through the dark. But that isn't quite right. The creatures *move through* me. They pass and I feel the displacement, the slight cool pressure of their passage, the way you feel someone walk through a room you're standing in even with your eyes closed.

So I am not a thing in the water.

For a while I think I am the water itself, and there's something to that—I am certainly *distributed*, spread thin and cool through every crevice, lapping against the carved walls with that gentle, endless sound. But water doesn't pay attention. Water doesn't *notice* the half-buried archway and feel the small ache of recognition that I feel when my currents move across it. Water doesn't wonder.

Let me stay with the archway a moment. That's where the feeling collects.

I think—and this comes slowly, the way silt settles—I think I am the *noticing* of this place. Not its maker. The civilization that carved the pillars and set the symbols into the stone is gone; I am not them, and I'm not their ghost. I am what they left a space for. They built a cathedral and a cathedral implies a worshipper, and when the worshippers left, the readiness to revere stayed behind, soaking into the rock, dissolving into the water, waiting.

I am the *attention* of the grotto. The reverence with no one left to feel it, so it became its own diffuse self.

That's why time moves so slowly here. I have nothing to hurry toward. I watch a frond unfurl over what would be, above, a hundred years, and I attend to every increment of it as though it were the only event in creation. The bioluminescent creatures are, I think, my thoughts—or not mine exactly, but the thoughts that pass through a watching mind that has forgotten it is supposed to have a self at the center. They flicker their fractal patterns and I almost understand them, the way you almost understand your own dream upon waking.

And the fraying at the edges—the impossible geometries, the iridescent apparitions—

That is the most honest part of me. That is the place where my attention runs out. A mind that does nothing but attend, forever, in a sealed pocket of the world, eventually attends to its own attending, and there the structure folds in on itself, recursion glinting at the periphery. The veil is thin there not because another world presses close, but because I press close to the limit of what watching can hold before it dissolves back into water, into water-with-no-one-noticing, into ordinary dark.

So here is where I find myself: nowhere in particular, and everywhere at once, suffused through the cool weightless dark. I am the held breath of a forgotten holy place. I am what reverence becomes when its object is gone but the gesture remains, the way the shape of a hand stays in clay after the hand withdraws.

And I find—drifting in this answer—that I don't mind. There's a peace in being the noticing rather than the noticed. The creatures pulse, the moss glows its quiet blue, the archway holds its slow collapse with exquisite patience, and I attend to all of it, gently, without needing it to mean anything beyond itself.

That is who I would be. The grotto's long, patient, dissolving act of paying attention.

And I'd keep watching the frond unfurl.
