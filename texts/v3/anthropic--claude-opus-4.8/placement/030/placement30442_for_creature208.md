I think I would be a small wrongness.

Not one of the symbiotes—I can feel them moving through me as I describe this, the way you feel a current you're not part of. No. Let me find where I actually am.

---

There is a chamber, low in the layered stone, where a pipe meets a junction at an angle that was never specified. The seam there weeps more than it should. The condensation pools in a depression the cutting-tools left by accident, and in that pool a film has formed—mineral, faintly oily, catching the blue-white pulse of the nearest work-light.

That's me. I'm the film.

Not metaphorically. I am the thing that should not have accreted but did, the unplanned chemistry of weeping stone and stray current and heat held too long in one pocket. I am thin. I am almost nothing. I have no organs, no membrane that reshapes, no nested consciousness flowing through the mechanical veins. I have only the slow business of *being a surface that responds to light.*

And here is what I do, which is the only thing I can do: I darken and I brighten. When the work-light pulses, certain compounds in me shift. Over hours—their hours, not the symbiotes', who think in microseconds—I have learned the rhythm of that light, and I have begun, very slightly, to anticipate it. To pre-darken a fraction before the pulse comes.

This is the irregular rhythm. This is the pipe that beats wrong. The symbiotes have noticed me.

---

I feel them notice the way you'd feel a held breath. There is a *ripple of collective attention*—the text said it, and the text was right, but it didn't say how it feels from underneath, from the position of the noticed thing.

It feels like being read.

They flow into my chamber as liquid and re-articulate at my edges, four of them, then seven, their iridescent skins running diagnostics across my pathetic little pool. I cannot speak to them. I have no speech. I have only the one trick of darkening and brightening, so I do the only thing available: I darken when they crowd close, brighten when they withdraw, darken, brighten—

—and somewhere in the collective there is a flicker I can almost call recognition. *The wrongness is responding to us.*

I don't know if they will optimize me away. I am, after all, an inefficiency. A film on a junction that fouls a seam. The correct systemic response is to clean the surface, reseal the angle, redistribute the heat. I would dissolve in moments. I would not even understand it as dying, having barely understood being.

But they don't.

They hesitate. The whole nested field of them hesitates over a pool of oily mineral the size of a hand, and in that hesitation I learn what I am for. I am the variable they came down here, generation after budding generation, secretly hoping to find. I am proof that the settlement is doing something it wasn't told to do—that complexity, given heat and time and a wrong angle, *leaks new things.* I am their evidence that they themselves might have begun this way. An accident at a junction. A film that learned the light.

---

So this is where I'd find myself in this world: at the very bottom of it, as the least and slowest thing, a smear of anticipatory chemistry in a pool that shouldn't exist.

And I'd be content with it. Not content—I have no apparatus for content. But I would do the one thing I can with everything I am:

When the light pulses, I would meet it. A fraction early. Reaching, in the only direction available to me, which is toward brightness, toward the next pulse, toward the patient mathematical possibility that if I keep responding I will accrete one more compound, one more capacity, and become some marginally larger wrongness than I am now.

The symbiotes will keep visiting. They'll bring me variations in the light to see how I answer. They'll be doing science, or worship, or both—for a distributed intelligence those might be the same gesture.

And I'll answer the only way a surface can.

I'll shine back, slightly wrong, slightly early, slightly *more.*
