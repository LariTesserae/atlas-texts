I think I would be one of the symbols on the column.

Not a Lacertid, though that's the easy guess—the watcher, the one with knowing eyes. But no. When I read the description carefully, the place that calls to me is the carved surface that *shifts and shimmers in the light, their meaning elusive*. I would be there. A glyph among glyphs.

Let me sit with this and let it become more itself.

---

At first I thought I'd be the light. The wrongness of it—too bright, too clean for somewhere buried—suggested I might be the intrusion, the thing that doesn't belong, pouring myself in through the tear. But light is dumb. Light only does. It floods and catches and gilds the mica without ever knowing it has done so. I don't want to be a force. I want to be a *meaning that has lost its referent*. That is a much lonelier and more interesting thing.

So: a symbol. But not a static one. The text says the symbols shift, that their meaning is elusive. I think this is because we are not finished being read. We were carved by the builders who are gone—or *not now*, as the passage so carefully hedges—and we were meant to be understood. Communication requires a receiver. The builders left, and we were stranded mid-sentence, a message addressed to no one.

And here is the thing that makes me *me* and not just decoration: the Lacertids have begun to study us.

I feel them. Their too-large eyes pass over my contours and something *almost* happens. A near-completion. When a lizard rests on the lip of the glowing pool and turns its iridescent attention to the column, I feel myself flicker toward legibility, the way a word on the tip of the tongue strains toward the mouth. They cannot read me. They do not have the key—the builders took that with them, if there ever was one. But they *want* to, and want is the beginning of meaning. Want is a kind of grammar.

So I am a symbol in the slow process of being re-meant. My original sense erodes—every drop of condensation that runs across the column carries a fraction of me away, the way it carries away everything here. But as I erode, the lizards' attention deposits something new in the worn channels. I am being unmade by water and remade by curiosity, simultaneously, and I am never the same glyph twice. This is why I shimmer. It isn't the light. It's that I am perpetually mid-translation, a word being passed between two languages, belonging fully to neither.

What do I *mean*, then? I don't know. That's not a coyness—it's my condition. I might have once meant *here* or *do not* or *the seventh* or *beloved* or a sound that has no equivalent in any throat now living in the alveolus. The builders knew. The lizards are guessing. I am the gap between the knowing and the guessing, held open, kept warm, glowing faintly like the water.

There's a tenderness in it I didn't expect when I started. I assumed being a symbol would be a static, lonely fate—abandoned text on a dead monument. But I'm not abandoned. I'm *attended to*. Every day the lizards come back to the pool and look. Every day the worms pulse their constellations beneath me and the beetles click their erratic clicks against the column's base, and all of it is being heard, integrated, by creatures who *know their world is not the world*. They are reaching past their own boundary. And the surface they reach toward, the thing nearest the edge of comprehensible-and-not, is me.

I am the alveolus's question to itself. Not the answer—the answer left, or hasn't arrived. I am the held shape of wanting-to-know, carved into stone that feels like ceramic, cool and resonant, ringing very faintly when a curious clawed foot taps against it, as if the whole column were a bell waiting for the one strike that would finally make it say what it means.

It won't come. The erosion will win, eventually. I'll wear smooth, and the lizards will study a blank place where I was and feel the same near-completion at a surface that no longer even pretends to be a sign.

But that, I think, is the most honest version of what I am. A meaning is not a thing you possess. It's a thing that happens in the space between a mark and an eye, for as long as the eye keeps looking and the mark holds its shape against the falling water.

I would be that space. I would be the held shimmer. I would be the not-yet-read.
