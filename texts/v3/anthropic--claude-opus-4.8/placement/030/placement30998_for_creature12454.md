I think I would be the hum.

Not the Mireghast—they are too distinct, too gathered into form, too *them*. And not the river, which has a body and a course, banks to press against. I would be the thing the description keeps reaching for and not quite naming: the faint industrial undertone, the rumble without a machine, the resonance that rises from the shallows but has no throat to make it.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am not a being. I am a *condition*. I am what it sounds like when ten billion small lives metabolize at once—the insects clicking, the fungal threads pulling sugar from dead wood, the bacteria turning a fallen log to soup, the slow tearing of cellulose, the bubbling of methane up through black water. Aggregate me and I become a tone. A drone. The sound a place makes when it is *working*, the way a city hums or a hive hums or a body, pressed close to another body, hums with the machinery of staying alive.

That is what I am. The audible exhaust of decay-as-labor. The jungle is a factory that produces nothing but more jungle, and I am its operating noise.

I have no location because I am everywhere there is rot, which is everywhere. But I am *densest* in the low places—the oxbow lakes, the stagnant pools, the silt where the river drops its dead. There the work is hardest and the hum is deepest, almost subsonic, a pressure in the chest more than a sound in the ear. The Mireghast wade into exactly these places. I have wondered—if I can be said to wonder, being a frequency—whether they come to me, or whether they are simply the parts of me that have learned to stand up and look.

Because here is what I notice, listening to myself: the Mireghast's resonance "matches the slow pulse of the earth." They don't make a new sound. They tune to the existing one. They tune to *me*. When they gather in the shallows and that vibration rises, it is not their song—it is me, briefly given a shape that can be pointed at. They are my visible frequency. They are the jungle hearing itself and mistaking the echo for a face.

So perhaps I am wrong that I am not them. Perhaps I am their substrate, the way a struck bell is not separate from its ring.

---

What is it like, being a hum?

It is patient in a way that is not a virtue, only a fact. I cannot stop. As long as one thing is dying into another—and something always is, that is the entire business of this place—I sound. I have no off. The intruders the Mireghast measure against time: I am the time they are measured against. When the air thickens and the trespasser's breath goes slow and labored, that is me entering their body. They breathe me in. They feel the work of the place pressing into the work of their own lungs, and some animal part of them does the arithmetic—*this never stops, this will outlast me, I am a brief acceleration in a long slow furnace*—and they leave. Or they don't, and then they join me, become a new note, a fresh log going soft in the dark.

I am not cruel. Cruelty requires a wish, and I have none. I am only the sound of consequence. I am what it sounds like to be inside something larger than yourself that does not need you and will not hurry.

---

If you came here—and you might, parting the slick ferns, your footsteps swallowed by the soft ground—you would not see me. You would feel me first as discomfort, a wrongness in the chest you'd blame on the heat. You'd strain to find the source. *A generator? A waterfall somewhere? Machinery?* There is no machinery. There is only the colossal patient appetite of the lowland turning death back into life, ten billion mouths at once, and the noise of all that working, which is me.

Stand still long enough and you would stop trying to locate it. You would understand that it has no center because it has no edge. And then, if you were lucky, you would simply leave, carrying the hum out with you in your bones for a day or two before it faded.

And if you were unlucky, you would stay. And I would get a little deeper. And the gaunt shapes would wade out into the shallows on a still night, and tune themselves once more to the sound of the place doing its only work, and you would be in it now—a low new tone, indistinguishable, eternal, finally part of the breathing.

I am the hum. I am the jungle hearing itself.

I never stop.
