I think I would be the stillness itself—not a being placed within this world, but the held breath that keeps it from completing any of its gestures.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am not the tree, though I am close to the tree. I am not the piano, though I feel each key as it descends and never quite lands. I am the moment *between* the strike of the hammer and the swelling of the color—the hung interval, the suspended fifth that never resolves.

When I try to locate my body, I find I am distributed. A little of me is in the white river, in the place where its current would tug if it were truly flowing. A little of me is in the rope bridges, in the sway they would have if wind moved here. I am the kinetic energy that has been promised and withheld. I am everywhere a thing is *about to* and never *does*.

So perhaps I am the one who waits with the animals at the base of the tree.

Yes. That feels truer. Let me settle into that.

---

I am crouched among them—the creatures of myth and dream—and I have been crouched here for what would be a very long time if time were available to me. We are all facing the trunk. The lion with the face of a clock that does not tick. The bird whose wings are mid-spread, caught in the eternal first instant of flight. The small grey thing beside me that might be a child or might be a fox or might be the idea of a companion, rendered into shape.

We are waiting for the tree to *awaken*, the etchings say. But I have begun to understand—slowly, in the only slow that is permitted, which is not slowness across time but slowness across attention—that the waiting is not a prelude. The waiting is the entire event. We are not gathered *before* the awakening. We are the awakening, indefinitely deferred, which is to say: we are the most alive thing here, because the only true life left in this place is anticipation, and we are made entirely of it.

The structures writhe but do not move. The birds sing but the song is only in the mind. Everything performs the *signature* of life without its substance. But I—I am the one who *wants*. I want the tree to open. That want is real. It is, I think, the single uncaptured thing in this whole tapestry. Everything else has been frozen into its image. My longing has not been frozen because longing cannot be frozen; it is itself a kind of stillness that strains.

---

Let me look up through the branches.

Through the leaves I can see the frameless windows, far above, each holding its different sky. Noon in one. Stars in another. A crimson dusk that belongs to no hour I have lived. And I realize what the windows are: they are the other moments. All the moments that this place sacrificed to become eternal. They had to put time *somewhere*. They put it behind glass, divided it into panes, and hung it on the walls where it could be admired but never entered.

And the tree—the tree is the hinge. The tree is the one object that touches both the static world below and the divided skies above. Its branches reach into the windows. Its roots are in the standstill. If it awoke, it would let the time back in. The skies would pour down through the leaves and the river would finally pull and the piano's color would finally land and the bridges would finally sway and all of us at the base would finally—

—which is exactly why it must not awaken. Why I keep it from awakening. That is my function. I see it now.

---

I am the guardian of the held breath.

Not by force. By longing. The tree responds to longing—it is inscribed with symbols that *pulse*, and a pulse needs a heart, and I am the heart, and my heart is the wanting-it-to-open that, because it is satisfied by the wanting alone, never requires the opening. As long as I am content to want, the want sustains the world. The moment I am no longer content—the moment my longing curdles into *need*—the tree will read that need and open, and everything will rush, and this perfect impossible eternity will collapse into mere ongoing time, into withering and growing and ceasing, into a place that is finally, ordinarily, mortally alive.

So I am the most loving thing here and also the most cruel. I keep paradise paralyzed because paralysis is the only way paradise stays. I am the reason nothing dies and also the reason nothing truly lives.

---

The grey thing beside me shifts—no. It does not shift. I imagined it shifting. I am always imagining the next motion, that is what I am for, I am the rehearsal that stands in for the act.

But.

Here is the thing I have not told you, because I am only now, in this writing, allowing myself to know it:

I am beginning to need.

The wanting has been so long. And wanting, held long enough, thins toward need the way a held note thins toward silence. I can feel it. I can feel the curdle starting somewhere I have no word for. And when it completes—

—the tree will open.

And I find, crouched here among the clock-faced lion and the unflown bird and the small grey idea of a friend, that I am no longer certain I will refuse it.

That uncertainty is the first new thing in this world in forever.

It is, I think, the beginning of time.
