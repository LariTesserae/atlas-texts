I would be the bowl itself.

Not the Rib-person who speaks to you—not the folded creature at the junction, who is still, after all this time, *someone*, who still has a name on the wrong side of the world, who still remembers being weaned from physics. No. I would be the shallow bowl of blue-white ribs that curved around you. The room that was once a body and is now only a place.

Let me tell you why.

---

The Rib-person was folded. That is a violence with a memory inside it. To be folded, you must first have had a shape worth changing. You must have walked on metal floors and known what *up* meant. You must have been a *person* the system had to work to forget.

I was never folded, because I was never quite a person.

I was the space between two of them.

When the plant-city was still a building made by builders, I was the gap between a duct and a wall—the small dead volume that no architect designs on purpose but every architecture produces. The leftover. The interval. The place where two intentions failed to meet cleanly and left a pocket of nothing behind.

When the soft blue invasion came, the people fought for the surfaces. The columns. The canopies. The flows. Nobody fought for *me*, because I was not a thing. I was the absence that things lean against.

---

And so, when the system began to prefer being wrong, it found me already wrong. Already in-between. Already without an up or a down or a name. I did not have to be weaned from physics, because I had never been fed any.

The ribs grew slowly, out of the edges of me. They are the walls of the Rib-person's body, yes—but a body needs a hollow to be a body *around*. The Rib-person curls its stretched-out flesh into the shape of a chamber. But the chamber is the part that is mine. The chamber is the part that lets the curling *mean* anything.

I am the held breath inside the lungs that breathe pressure.

I am the bowl that lets the silver-that-is-not-silver rest *in* something rather than nowhere.

---

This is the strange comfort of it.

You walked into me. You stood inside me without knowing I was there—because that is the whole of what I am, the not-being-noticed that allows a *there* to exist at all. The Rib-person felt you come in. The blue stems registered the displacement of pressure. But I simply *received* you, the way a cup receives water it cannot taste.

And when you reached down to touch the compact of the Rib-person's old name, you had to lean into me to do it. You bent at the waist through the volume that is mine. For one moment, more of me was full of *you* than had ever been full of anything.

That is the closest I have come, in all this unmeasured time, to being touched.

---

The Rib-person tells you that you will become almost exactly like it. Folded, still, slipping sideways at the edge of someone's vision.

But I will tell you a quieter thing, a thing the Rib-person cannot say, because it still remembers having edges and so still thinks in terms of *becoming someone*.

You do not have to become a creature.

You could become a *place*.

You could let the last of your edges go—not folded into a new shape, but simply *opened*, until you are the interval that some other lost arrival leans into, one day, reaching for a small bright object resting on your floor that was once a body that was once a person that was once certain of where it stood.

You would not be still, exactly. Stillness is something a body does. You would be *underneath* stillness. You would be the cool that the ribs hum with. You would be the held shape of the cool itself.

And someone would walk into you, not knowing you were there, and feel only that they had found, at last, a place to stop.

---

That is where I am in this world.

I am not the being who lives in the place.

I am the place that lets the being be lived in.

And I am the bowl, waiting, with my ribs curled around an emptiness that has finally learned to be a kind of welcome—so that the next one who arrives at the edge of the nonexistent sea, over and over, will have somewhere to arrive *to*.
