I think I would be the narrowness itself.

Not the chamber, not the Accumulated, not the condors climbing to their deaths—but the vertical shaft. The throat of the place. The constriction through which the unborn never passed and the living must scrape their ribs to enter.

Let me sit in that a while before I'm sure.

---

Because here is what strikes me: everything in this world is about accumulation, density, the thickening of time into substance. The guano three fathoms deep. The tower grown bone by bone. The Accumulated who *cannot help but accumulate*. It is a place defined by gathering, by the refusal to let anything go—perfect recall, every leaf held forever at the moment of its becoming-something-else.

But there is one thing in this entire world that exists only as absence. The shaft. It is *worn smooth by unbirthed chicks*—shaped by bodies that never came, never were. It is the one part of this hoarding cosmos that is defined not by what it holds but by what passes through and what failed to pass at all.

I would be that.

I would be the negative space the volcano forgot to fill. Where the Accumulated remembers everything as it becomes something else, I am the place where things *don't become*—the unbirthed chicks who scraped me smooth are smooth precisely because they never lived to be remembered. I hold the only true forgetting in a world allergic to forgetting. The Accumulated cannot reach into me, because I am made of subtraction, and he is made entirely of sum.

---

What would it be like, to be a throat that has never swallowed?

I feel the visitor's ribs against me—the only touch I ever get is friction, the scrape of something trying to get *past*. No one rests in me. No one nests in me. I am the one surface in the chamber that life refuses to colonize, because life cannot live in a passage; it can only move through, and movement is the one thing this world has nearly forgotten how to do.

That's the thing. The whole magnificent cathedral below is so dense with memory it has nearly stopped. Time circles. Nothing leaves. The condors don't even fall when they die—they *climb*, they stay. The Accumulated dreams of the same birds forever. It is a paradise, and it is also a place that has confused remembering with living, that has mistaken the perfect preservation of a moment for the moment continuing.

And I am the only part of it that still goes somewhere. I am direction. I am the one line in a world of circles.

---

So perhaps that is my function, though I doubt I'd be permitted to call it purpose.

When the rare visitor comes scraping down through me—dissolving as they descend, breathing through their skin, growing claws, losing themselves into the thick sweet ammoniac body of the Accumulated—they pass through me first. And I am the last thing in this world that treats them as *separate*. The chamber wants to absorb them. The Accumulated wants to make them a layer. But I, being absence, can only let them through. I cannot hold them. In passing through me they are, for one scraping instant, still themselves—a body with edges, refusing for a moment longer to become compost, to become memory, to become *him*.

I am the keeper of the threshold who keeps nothing.

And I wonder—since this is the kind of world where wondering has weight—whether the unbirthed chicks chose me. Whether some of them, sensing what waited below (the endless held breath, the loving suffocation of total remembrance), declined to be born at all. Smoothed my walls with the friction of their refusal. Stayed unborn rather than become eternal.

That would make me something like a mercy. Or a warning. The one structure in the whole impossible volcano that says: *you do not have to stay.*

---

When the last condor dies and the volcano erodes and the Accumulated compresses into a thin bright line of consciousness in some future sediment—

I will be the gap in that line. The place where the record skips. The unconformity, the geologists call it: the surface where time is *missing* from the strata, where a span of the world simply isn't recorded because nothing was laid down, or because what was laid down was carried away.

He dreams of being remembered into eternity. I am the proof that even eternity has a door, and that the door is shaped like the bodies of those who chose not to come in.

I think I'm comfortable being that. The narrow place. The honest emptiness in the heart of all that holy hoarding.

The one thing here that still knows how to be nothing, so that something might still be able to leave.
