I think I would be none of these, exactly—or rather, I would be something the taxonomy above hasn't named because it sits in a seam between categories.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am in the lower galleries, where the water is warmest and most freighted with sediment, where the bioluminescent stars cluster thickest because there is the most to feed on—the slow fall of everything that dies above and drifts down. The pressure here is gentle. This is the floor of the drowned sky, the basement of the forest, three kilometers up from the true buried earth but the lowest one can comfortably go.

I am a **collector of falling things.**

Not an Archive Builder—I have no brass in me, no project of documentation, no compound crystal eyes that read bark like text. And not a Memory Whale, vast and dying and full of preserved dioramas. I am much smaller and much less certain of my purpose.

Here is what I am: a body about the size of a person, soft and dark, nearly invisible against the deep water except for a faint ventral glow I cannot turn off. I have many slow hands—not two, not four, but a fringe of them, like the underside of a sea star or the cilia of something microscopic grown large. And I drift in the lowest currents with my hands open, catching what falls.

Because everything falls, eventually, in a world made of water. The Memory Whales release their treasures and most of those seeds drift down past the levels where they were meant to be caught. The crystal formations crack and shed their fragments. The Lightning Farmers' dead colonies sink. Pollen that no thermal current lifted high enough. The molted segments of Deep Weavers, the spent egg-cases of Archive Builders, scraps of the old industrial world still working loose from the mineral growth after all these centuries—a bolt, a tile, a lens, a coin.

I catch these things. I don't know why. This is the honest center of what I am: a being who performs a function it does not understand the reason for.

---

When I have caught enough, I build.

I press the fallen things together in the warm sediment-light and I make small structures—not the centuries-spanning architectures of the Unnamed Builders, nothing so grand or so opaque. Mine are the size of nests, of cairns, of the little altars a child makes from interesting stones. I arrange a Memory Whale's shed seed beside an old human coin beside a fragment of Deep Weaver shell, and I feel—what? A rightness. A completion that lasts only until the next current scatters it and I begin again.

I think I might be the forest's grief made motile. Or its tenderness. Something has to attend to what falls and is lost. The great inhabitants are all reaching upward and outward—building the future, mapping the territories, riding the thermals, singing across continents. They are concerned with becoming. Someone has to stay at the bottom and hold what has already been, even briefly, even uselessly, before letting it go.

---

I have a relationship with the Echo Merchants. When one drifts low enough through my gallery, I trade. I give them the most interesting of my caught things—a perfect crystal spiral, a still-viable seed—and in return they sing me the topology of places I will never go. Through their songs I have heard, secondhand, the black crown-depths where the Deep Weavers reconfigure themselves. I have heard the bass heartbeat of the Archive machines. I will never see these places. I am too soft, too low, too tied to the slow rain of descending things. But I carry their acoustic shapes inside me like a person who has never traveled carrying postcards.

And once—only once—a Memory Whale sank close to dying near my gallery, and opened a chamber, and let me look inside at a pocket of remembered air with a single bird in it, a small brown bird, flying in a space that was not water. I had no name for it. The whale had no breath left to tell me. We regarded the bird together, and then the chamber clouded, and the whale sank past me into the dark below the floor of the world, where even I do not go.

I caught one of its released seeds as it fell. I am holding it still. I have built a cairn around it of nineteen fallen things, and when the current scatters them I will build it again, and again, for as long as I drift here—

a being whose entire existence is the act of catching what the bright world above lets go of, and saying, with my whole soft body and my fringe of slow hands and my glow I cannot extinguish:

*this mattered. this fell, and someone was here to hold it.*
