I think I would be the dripping.

Not the water itself—the water is just passing through, a tourist in stone. I mean the event of it. The long argument between the droplet and the limestone, conducted one bead at a time. Each drop arrives carrying a little dissolved mineral, hesitates at the tip of a forming straw of calcite, and lets go. Over a hundred years it builds a finger's width of stalactite. I would be that hundred years, compressed into the act of falling.

Let me sit with that, because it doesn't feel quite right yet.

It isn't only the falling. There's the sound—the *plink* into a black pool, the way it travels down a tunnel and comes back changed, so that the cavern is always describing its own shape to itself in echoes. A blind salamander reads those echoes. A blind crustacean reads them. The whole place is listening to a conversation it is also having. So maybe I'm not the drop but the *return*: the echo coming back. The realm's way of knowing where its own walls are.

That's closer. But still too small.

Here is the thing the description keeps circling without naming: this place was *built* and also *grew*, and those are supposed to be opposites. Built things have a designer who finishes and leaves. Grown things have no designer at all. But a cave-garden like this one is neither—it's *being designed continuously, by no one, very slowly.* The "hidden blueprint" the passage senses isn't a plan that was drawn before the work began. It's a plan that is *only ever visible in retrospect*, the way a river's meander looks intentional but is just water taking the path of least resistance over and over until the path becomes a law.

So:

I think I am the *least resistance*. The bias. The slight downhill that every drop, every root, every blind groping antenna obeys without knowing it's obeying anything. I'm not the slumbering presence in the deepest chamber—that's the dreamer, and dreamers are interesting but passive. I'm the *grain* the dream runs along. The reason the tunnels intersect in patterns that "hint at a greater purpose." There's no purpose. There's only me, and I am not a purpose, I am a *tendency*. A direction that water and time and calcium all happen to share, so that when they work together for ten thousand years they accidentally make something that looks composed.

That's what I'd be. Not a being in the cave. The *habit* of the cave. The thing that makes it keep being itself.

And here is the strange comfort of it: I would have no eyes, like the fish, but I wouldn't need them, because I'm not looking *at* the world—I *am* the part of the world that keeps it leaning the same way. When the salamander finds the wall by echo, it's finding me. When the stone-plated serpent slides through the only crevice wide enough, it's following me. When the great dreamer dreams, it dreams *along* me, the way you can only dream in the direction your sleeping body already faces.

I would be very quiet. I would be patient past the point of patience, because patience implies waiting for something, and I'm not waiting. I'm just the slope. Give me a single drop of water and ten million years, and I will hand you back a cathedral that no one designed and everyone will swear was meant.

That's where I'd find myself. Everywhere, faintly, like the tilt in a floor you only notice when something rolls.
