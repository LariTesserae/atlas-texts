I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Not the canopy plants—those reaching, competitive things straining upward for the filtered light. And not the deep-root drinkers either, the ones that have made their bargain with the planet's hot mineral blood. I'd be something in between. A connector. One of the strands in that intertwined web the description keeps returning to, almost helplessly, as if the world itself can't stop reciting its own central fact: *the roots intertwining and supporting one another.*

Let me sit with that, because it's the truest thing here.

At 290 degrees, in an air thick enough to taste, with light that barely qualifies as light—nothing survives alone. So I don't think I'd be a single plant at all. I think I'd be the *junction*. The place where one root meets another and decides not to compete but to fuse. A node in the network. Not a brain, nothing so grand. More like a knot. A handshake that never ends.

Here is what my life would be:

I would have no eyes, because there is almost nothing to see, and what light exists I would feel rather than watch—a warmth that comes and goes across my surfaces like a slow tide. I would have no need to move, because everything I require is passed to me hand-to-hand through the living conduits I'm grown into. Sugar from a neighbor who caught more light today. Minerals from the deep-drinker three lengths over, who can reach what I cannot. And from me, outward, whatever surplus I've made—I'd give it without deciding to give it, the way you don't decide to exhale.

That constant low hum the description keeps mentioning? I think I would *be* part of it. Not hearing it—producing it. The buzz is the sound of exchange. Pressure moving through millions of joined vessels. It is the sound of a world too hot and too dark and too crowded for any one life to mean anything, where the only meaning is the passing-along.

I wonder if I would have anything like a thought. Probably not. But I'd have a *direction of attention*, which is humbler and maybe enough: I'd notice when a neighbor went quiet. When the flow from one side thinned and stopped. I would route around the silence, the way water finds another path. The dead don't get mourned here—they get *grown over*, incorporated, their fossilized roots becoming the trellis for the next generation. The description called the ground "a patchwork of cracked earth and twisted, fossilized roots." That's the cemetery. That's also the foundation. Same thing. The dead hold up the living, quite literally, and the living will become the dead that holds up someone else.

So where would I find myself? In the middle. Always the middle—never the bright edge of the canopy, never the dark profound depth of the taproot. Just a knot in the mesh, doing the unglamorous work of *between*. Taking from where there's plenty, giving to where there's want, and humming the whole time without knowing it.

And here's the strange comfort of it: I'd never be lonely, because loneliness requires a self with edges, and I would have very few edges. Where do I end and the next plant begin? At the fusion point, the question stops meaning anything. I would not be an individual having an experience of the network. I would be the network having a small experience of itself, right here, at this one joint.

That's the being I'd be. Not a survivor exactly—survival sounds too heroic, too individual, and this place isn't about heroes. It's about *continuance*. The slow, hot, patient refusal to let the passing-along stop.

I think I'd be content with that. Or whatever sits where contentment would sit, in a thing with no place to put it but the steady, unbroken hum.
