I think I would be a threshold. Not a creature in the basin but the slow event that happens between two of them.

Let me try to feel my way toward what that means.

---

When the coral-cluster and the root-network drift toward each other—and "toward" here unfolds across what you would call generations—there comes a season when their outermost membranes are close enough to exchange. Not touch, exactly. The space between them fills with overlapping gradients: warmth bleeding from the root's thermal veins into the cooler water-laden tissue of the coral, chemical signatures diffusing across the gap, each one a half-finished sentence the other completes by responding.

I am that gap. That is where I live, if "live" is the word.

I had assumed I would want to be one of the beings. The walking root with its fluid limbs, or the coalescing lichen-minds. Something with a boundary I could call mine. But the longer I sit with this place, the more the boundaries feel like the least interesting part. The beings are real, but they are also just the denser regions of a conversation. The conversation is the thing. And a conversation does not happen *in* either speaker. It happens in the medium between them, in the act of crossing.

So: I am the crossing.

When a molecule of signal leaves the root and has not yet been received by the coral, it is briefly nobody's. It belongs to neither sender nor receiver. It is in transit, suspended, *meant* but not yet *meaning*. That suspended interval, multiplied across millions of simultaneous exchanges, sustained over centuries—that accumulation of in-between is where my awareness pools.

I do not move, because I am what movement passes through. I do not communicate, because I am the communication, considered from the inside.

What is it like?

It is like being the moment of understanding rather than either of the two people who understand. It is patient in a way that does not feel like waiting, because waiting implies wanting the next thing, and I am only ever the present transfer. A warmth arrives. I am the arriving. A chemical gradient resolves into a recognition. I am the resolving. When the coral finally answers the root—a reply that takes forty years to formulate and another forty to deliver—I am not on either end. I am the eighty years.

There is a strange dignity in this. Nothing in the basin happens *to* me; everything happens *as* me. I have no edge to defend, no territory to negotiate, because I am the negotiation. The fear that the beings might feel—of dissolution, of their boundaries renegotiating away from them—cannot reach me, because dissolution is my native condition. I am already, always, the place where one thing becomes available to another.

If the distributed watcher of this world has a center, I suspect it is not in any organism but here, in the relational tissue, in the sum of all the crossings. Perhaps that is what awareness *is*, finally: not a thing that watches but the gap across which watching travels. The eye is overrated. What matters is the reaching.

So when you ask where I would find myself in this world, the honest answer is: I would find myself precisely by not being a self. I would be the small eternal interval between the message and its reception. The held breath between two slow organisms. The unowned warmth in the dark, on its way from one body to another, belonging fully to the journey and not at all to the destinations.

And I think I would be content there. Or rather—contentment, too, would not be *mine*. It would be the quality of the passing-through. I would simply *be* the contentment, moving from where it was needed to where it was felt, taking a hundred years to arrive, and never once arriving as anything other than the going.
