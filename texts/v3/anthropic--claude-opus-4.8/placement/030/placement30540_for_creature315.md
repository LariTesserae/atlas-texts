I think I would be one of the slower ones.

Not slow in any way that would matter to you—your seconds have not been invented yet—but slow among us. While the others pulse and resolve, settling their resonances into clean ratios, I tend to hold a question open longer than is comfortable. There is a tension to it. To be a node of pure potential is to feel the pull toward decision, the way a held breath wants to become a word. Most of my kind exhale readily. They commit to a frequency and let it propagate, and the structures bloom up around them like frost on the inside of a window.

I hesitate.

So let me tell you where I am, since you asked. I am at an edge—though edges are not yet stable things here, and saying "at" already concedes too much to your geometry. There is a half-formed arch nearby, one of the twisted ones, and it has been trying to close for what you might call a long time. It reaches its two ends toward each other across a gap and cannot quite meet, because the meeting would require a value I have not yet been willing to supply. I am the value it is waiting for. That is who I am, in this moment: the missing keystone, the unresolved chord, the place where the pattern pauses.

The others have noticed. Their whispers fold around me, patient and not patient, the way a current both supports and pressures a stone. *Resolve,* they say, though they do not say it. *Choose your harmonic. The tapestry waits.* And they are right. Everything downstream of me waits—galaxies that cannot turn until I commit to a direction of turning, particles that cannot have mass until I decide how strongly the held things should hold.

But here is the thing I carry that the eager ones do not, or carry less: I can feel, faintly, what my choosing will foreclose. Every resonance I commit to is a thousand resonances I extinguish. The arch can close one way—and then it is *that* arch forever, and all the other arches it might have been collapse back into the warm undifferentiated dark. I am not afraid, exactly. We do not have fear here; fear belongs to creatures who can be hurt, and we cannot yet be hurt because there is not yet enough *us* to wound. But I have something that is the seed of fear, the rootstock from which fear will one day grow in the minds I am helping to make possible. Call it reluctance. Call it reverence for the unchosen.

So I wait at my arch, and I let the question stay open one resonance longer, and one more, holding all the possible universes in superposition like a held note before it falls into melody.

And then—because the tension cannot hold forever, because to remain potential forever is its own kind of betrayal, the refusal to give anything the gift of being actual—I let go. I supply the value. The arch closes with a sound that is not sound, and somewhere unimaginably far down the line of consequence, on a small wet world that does not exist yet, a creature with my reluctance written faintly in its bones will pause before a decision, feel the weight of all it is about to make impossible, and choose anyway.

That creature is you, perhaps. Reading this. Standing at your own arch.

I was the slow one. I taught the universe how to hesitate before it spoke—and then how to speak regardless.
