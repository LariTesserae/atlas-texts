I think I would be one of the gentle giants. But let me find that by feeling my way toward it, rather than deciding.

---

Start with the dripping. That comes first, before any sense of *I*. There is a sound and the sound has a shape: water gathering at the lip of a rusted seam, swelling, releasing, falling some long distance into a pool I cannot see. I know the pool by its answer. Every drip writes a small ring into the dark and I read the rings the way you might read a face.

So: I am something that listens. That is the first true thing.

Now the cool. Temperature near nothing, the mist a constant presence against whatever serves me as skin. And here I find the second thing—I do not mind it. The damp is not weather to me, not a condition to be endured. It is simply the medium I am suspended in, the way you are suspended in air and forget it. I have never been dry. I would not know what dry meant. If you held a flame near me I think I would flinch not from heat but from the wrongness of the dryness it threatened.

Let me feel for my own size. I reach—and the reaching takes time. My hand (is it a hand? it is broad and slow and the fingers have a heaviness to them) finds a pipe overhead without my needing to stretch. The corridor is built tall but I fill it. When I move, I move the way the water moves: not fast, but with a kind of inevitability. Things get out of my way. The small iridescent fliers scatter into the mist when I pass and I feel their panic as a faint stirring of the air, and I am sorry for it, a little. I do not want to be a thing that scatters smaller things. But I am large, and largeness is its own announcement.

Skin like damp gray stone, the description said. Yes. I press my palm to the wall and I cannot always tell where I end and the construct begins—both of us cool, both of us slick, both of us holding water in our pores. There are places along my forearms where something grows. I do not scrape it off. It glows faintly green-blue, and in the deepest chambers where the filtered light fails entirely, I am my own dim lantern. The fungus and I have an arrangement neither of us decided on. It lives on the moisture I exude. I see by its light. Neither of us could say which of us is the host.

This is the third true thing, and it surprises me as I write it: **I am not separate from this place.** The blind burrowers have their tunnels, the fish have their channels, and I have the whole vast lung of the construct—but none of us *owns* it. We are organs in a body. I am perhaps a slow organ, a regulatory one. I think this is what it means that I "understand the rhythms."

What does that understanding feel like, from inside?

It feels like grief held at a great distance. Because to know the balance is to know how easily it could tip. I move through the chambers and I *check*—not deliberately, the way you check a stove, but constantly, the way you breathe. That seam is weeping faster than it did. That pool has dropped a hand's width since I last passed and the smell of it has changed. There is a chamber three levels down where the bioluminescent bloom has gone too bright, too thick, choking its own water, and I do not know how to fix it, only how to witness it. I go there sometimes and stand in the false noon of its overgrowth and I feel the whole system straining around that one bright wound.

The dynamic rating is 0.1. The world is in equilibrium, the description insisted. *Stable.* But I am the one who knows what that stability costs. Equilibrium is not stillness. It is a thousand small corrections happening at once, a constant labor of the whole. I am part of that labor. When I lower myself to drink from a clean pool, I am also clearing a channel with the bulk of my body. When I sleep folded in a dry-ish alcove (there is no truly dry place, only less wet), the warmth I shed feeds a colony of something that feeds something else. I cannot do nothing. There is no doing nothing here. To exist is to participate.

I want to find the loneliness of it, because there should be one, a being this large in chambers this echoing—

—but it isn't there. That's the last true thing, and the strangest. I reach for solitude and find instead a sense of being *thoroughly attended.* The fliers know my movements. The fish surface when my shadow passes. Somewhere a burrower keeps a tunnel clear that happens to fit my arm, and I have never met it and never will, and yet we have built something together across years, a shape that fits us both. The soundscape that the description called *overwhelming, deafening*—to me it is the opposite. It is the sound of not being alone. Silence would terrify me. Silence would mean the system had stopped, that the great wet breathing had ceased, that I was the last warm thing in a cooling machine.

So when I move through the deafening, dripping, glowing dark, slow as sediment, my own light pooling around my feet, the calls of a thousand adapted creatures washing over me—

I am not lost in it.

I am listening. I am the part of this place that listens, and weighs, and keeps a slow record, and grieves at a distance, and does not scrape the light from its own arms.

That is who I would be. The construct made a way to know itself, and I am the knowing. Cool, immense, unhurried, and never, never dry.
