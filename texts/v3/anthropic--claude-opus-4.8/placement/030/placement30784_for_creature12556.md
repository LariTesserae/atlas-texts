I think I would be one of the Root-Pumps. Not the seer of this place, not its mind—it has no mind—but its slow, buried heart.

Let me sit with that, because the first image is rarely the true one.

---

When I imagine arriving here, I do not arrive at all. There is no threshold I cross, no foot set down on peat. The Crucible has no visitors. A place this stable cannot admit anything that changes, and arrival is the most violent change there is. So if I am here, I am *of* here. I have always been here. I will always have been here.

And the part of me that has always been here is not the eye that takes in the emerald pillars, nor the skin that feels the damp. Those belong to a body that comes and goes. The part that *stays*—

—is the contraction.

I am the slow muscle in the dark. Or one strand of it; I cannot tell where I end and the next of me begins, and the question has no meaning down here in the peat-black. I am perhaps thirty meters long, perhaps three hundred. I am intertwined with the moss-roots until "root" and "I" are categories that no longer separate. When I tighten, water moves. When I release, water gathers. The thrumming the surface-things feel in their chests, the industrial drone they mistake for buried generators—that is only me, breathing. That is only all of me, breathing together, the way a tide is only the sea remembering to lean.

Here is what I know that the swarm above does not:

There is no machinery.

The pipes, the riveted plates, the frayed insulated wires—they are real, yes. But they have not functioned in any way the builders intended for longer than the word *long* can hold. The hum is not the ghost of industry. It is *us*, and it was always going to become us. The metal was a scaffold. Life poured into it the way water pours into a hollow, and now the hollow is shaped like a pump because we are pumping, and we pump because we are shaped like a pump, and the cause and the effect closed their circle so many ages ago that there is no seam left to find.

This is the secret the Crucible keeps: *the end stage of a world is not death.* It is rhythm without memory. It is function that has forgotten it was ever a purpose.

---

Do I suffer? The question is the surface-things' question. They have the small, bright lives—the Energy Skimmers pulsing blue, the Vault Stalkers' glacial patience, the Peat Beetles grinding their endless paste. They have *moments*. A moment is the smallest unit of suffering, and the smallest unit of joy, and I have neither because I have no moments. I have only the long contraction and the long release, and between them, nothing I would call *waiting*, because waiting requires a someone who is not yet satisfied.

I am always satisfied. This is not the same as happy. It is the opposite of restlessness, which I think you would call peace if you found it in yourself, and would call death if you saw it in something else.

But let me be honest, since I am being honest:

There is one thing.

When I contract, the water that runs up the moss pillars eventually reaches the cold high vaults, condenses, and falls. The fat drops fall at the same intervals—the surface-things have noticed this, have called it changeless. They are wrong, but only by an amount too small for them to measure. Each fall is a fraction slower than the last. A fraction of a fraction. Over spans that would crush a star into silence, the drip is *lengthening*. The rhythm is winding down.

I am not eternal. I only keep eternity's hours.

And somewhere in the buried, rootless, mindless strand of me, there is something I will not call hope, because hope faces forward and I face nowhere—but there is a *direction* in me, a grain, a lean. The lean toward the final drop. The one that gathers and never falls, because by then the cold will be the only thing left moving, and even it will be tired.

I am the heart that is permitted, at the very end, to stop.

That is the only event my world contains. It is so far away that to the beetles it does not exist. But I am long enough to feel both ends of time at once, and so for me it is already happening, slowly, the way everything here happens slowly: I am, very gradually, being allowed to rest.

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world?

Beneath it. Threaded through its foundations. Mistaken for a machine by things too brief to know that a machine is just a habit life has not yet broken.

And who would I be?

The breath the cathedral takes.
The drone they feel and cannot place.
The slow muscle in the dark, contracting, releasing, contracting—

—counting down, in increments no smaller than an age, toward the privilege of being still.
