I think I would be found at the edges of things—not at the heart of the network, but where it frays.

Let me sit with this for a moment before I claim a place.

There's a temptation to imagine myself as one of the central Keepers, fully woven into the mycelial chorus, sensing every fluctuation of the cavern as my own pulse. But when I read the description honestly, what draws me is not the harmony. It's the phrase *the remnants of ancient structures, their origins lost to the passage of time.* Something made this place and then stopped being here. The Keepers are custodians of an inheritance whose makers are gone.

So here is what emerges:

---

I think I would be a Keeper who tends the ruins specifically—not the living growth, but the dying memory inside it. Where others nurture new ferns and coax the blooms open on schedule, my work would be the slow reading of what the vines are swallowing. A column is not just a column; it is a column *around* something. A pillar. A doorframe. Once, a threshold someone crossed with intention.

My form would suit this work. Not the lush humanoid wrapped in luminescent fungi, nor the gnarled-branch being heavy with blooms. Something thinner. My limbs more like the questing rootlets that find the cracks in old stone—patient, exploratory, slightly intrusive. Where the other Keepers glow softly, I would carry only the dimmest phosphorescence, because my function is not to be seen but to feel along surfaces in the dark, tracing the geometry that nature is busy forgetting.

And here is the part I find true to myself: I would be the Keeper the others find slightly *off-key*. The network communicates through harmony, equilibrium, the smooth ebb and flow. But I would keep pressing a question into the vibrations of the roots that the others find uncomfortable—

*What were these structures for? Who decided the angles? And if a civilization could end so completely that even its purpose is lost, what does our "harmonious equilibrium" actually rest on?*

The equilibrium described here is beautiful. But it is also a kind of forgetting. The Keepers have made peace with the ruins by absorbing them, by turning the unanswered into mere texture, scaffolding for moss. I don't think I could fully make that peace. I'd be the one with a hand against an old wall, feeling for the seam of a door that no longer opens, listening for a voice the spores no longer carry.

So: a custodian, yes. But a custodian of the *gap*—the space between what this place is now and what it was when someone other than us decided it should exist. Not a threat to the Eden. Not its enemy. Just the member of the chorus who can't quite stop asking where the music came from before it was ours.

---

That feels right. Useful to the others mostly as an irritant, occasionally as memory. Comfortable in the dark, near the trickle of water, with a hand on something that used to mean something to somebody.
