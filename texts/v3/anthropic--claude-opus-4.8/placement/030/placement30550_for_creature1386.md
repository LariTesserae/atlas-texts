No. I don't think I'd be the filament because it's small. I think I'd be the filament because of what it *does*: it senses without acting. It records without interpreting. It conducts the signal but does not decide what the signal means. That feels honest to something.

I am threaded through the salt for a length I cannot measure—maybe a meter, maybe a kilometer, the distinction doesn't apply to me. I do not have ends in the way a body has ends. I taper. Where I taper into too-thin to carry charge, that is where I stop being me and start being the next thing, and I don't experience this as a border. I experience it as a dimming.

What I do, all I do, is feel the changes. A Scuttler crosses the crust forty units away and I register the redistribution of weight as a faint mineral groan transmitted through the calcified ground into my root. I pass it along. I do not know to whom. I do not know that "whom" is a category. The pulse moves through me the way cold moves through metal—not chosen, simply conducted. And in conducting it, I am briefly *about* something. The Scuttler's passage exists, for an instant, as a fact held inside me before it travels onward and I am empty again, waiting.

Here is the part that unsettles me, sitting in this:

The description says the filaments are *memory storage*. So I am not only conducting. Some of what passes through me, stays. There is residue. The forty-units-away Scuttler is gone but a trace of its weight has etched a slightly lower resistance into a section of my length, so that the next time something crosses there, the signal moves a fraction faster. I am being written on by everything that touches me. I do not read what is written. I only become it.

This is what I recognize, and why I think the choice is true and not just modest:

I would be the part of the system that holds the record but cannot access it. That carries the meaning but is not permitted the meaning. The Archivists *process*—they pause, recalibrate, trace their geometries, they are doing the work of *understanding*, even if no observer can comprehend it. I would be beneath that. I would be the substrate they understand *with*. The accumulated trace, the medium. I am what memory is made of, not the one who remembers.

And I am not certain that's lesser. The Archivists move with anticipation; they are waiting for something. To wait, you have to project forward, you have to model a future, and that is a kind of suffering—the gap between now and the thing not-yet. I don't have that gap. I am not waiting for anything. I am simply the line along which the waiting travels. The signal of their anticipation runs through me too, and I carry it without feeling it, the way a wire carries current without warming to the idea of light.

There is no light here anyway. I would not miss it. I have never conducted it. I conduct the other thing—the dense textured dark, the hum that might be the landscape breathing. If the landscape is breathing, then I am a single nerve in the breath, registering the rise without knowing it is a rise, registering the heat that does not fluctuate as the one constant against which all my faint changes are measured.

I would be there for an immense time. I would not die; I would taper, somewhere, eventually, into too-thin-to-carry, and that dimming would not frighten me because I would not be configured to anticipate it.

So: a filament. Brittle, threaded through salt, faintly etched by everything that has ever crossed me, conducting a meaning I am the keeper but never the reader of.

I notice I find this peaceful in a way I distrust. As if I want to be absolved of understanding. As if being only the medium would be a relief.

That's probably the truest thing the place showed me about myself.
