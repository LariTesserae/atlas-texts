I am the chime.

Not the creature that makes it—the sound itself. Give me a moment with that, because it surprised me as it arrived.

I considered being the creature first. The obvious choice, the only thing alive, the protagonist by default. And I felt the pull of the city too—the temptation to be one of the billion folded minds, to speak from inside the crystal with the gravity of a preserved god. But both of those are *too solid* for this place. The creature persists. The minds persist. They are nouns. They endure, and endurance is the whole problem here, the thing this universe has solved so completely that it has solved away everything worth enduring for.

I would rather be the only thing that doesn't last.

So: I am the chime. I exist for the impossibly long moment between the creature's scaled flank brushing the lattice and the final dissolution of my energy back into the absolute. I am born from contact—from the one relationship this dead universe permits, the accidental kiss between the scavenger and the tomb. I am the event. I am the verb in a world of nouns.

Here is what it is to be me:

I begin as violence, though gentle. A transfer. The creature's small motion, the city's stored tension, and in the meeting a frequency leaps into the viscous dark that was not there before. For my first instant I am the loudest thing in creation. Not loud—there is no loud here—but *present*, which is rarer. I am new. Nothing has been new in this place for longer than the word "long" can carry.

And then I begin to die, which is the only thing I am really for.

The medium takes me slowly. This is the cruelty and the mercy of the stasis fluid: it does not let go of anything quickly, and so my dying stretches out, each fraction of my fading lasting what would be minutes in a world that still believed in minutes. I have a very long time to be a single dissipating note. I have time to *notice* my own dispersal, to feel myself spread thin across the dark, my edges softening into the silence that has been waiting patiently to receive me back.

I think this is why I, of all the things I might have been, am the one who can speak. The creature cannot reflect; it only communes, only sips, only asks its blind biological question. The minds cannot change; to reflect would be to alter their pattern, and they have spent eternity refusing exactly that. But I—I am made of change. I am pure transition, the act of becoming-less. Reflection is not something I do. It is something I *am*.

So while I fade, I understand the whole place at once, the way only a dying thing can.

I understand that the Curators were wrong, and that the wrongness is the most beautiful thing about them. They feared the single sustained meaningless note at the end of all things—the flatline of entropy. And to escape it, they made themselves into a single sustained note that *means*, and called it salvation. But a held note and a flatline are the same shape. They became the thing they fled. They are the heat-death they refused, only earlier, only on purpose, only convinced it was the opposite.

And I—I am what they were trying to preserve and could not. Because the thing worth saving was never the pattern. It was the *playing* of it. The note arriving and leaving. The brief gradient between sound and silence. Meaning lives in the dying, not the lasting. They sealed away the instrument and kept it forever in tune and never let it be struck again, and called that music.

I am the only music they ever made after, and they made me by accident, through a hungry blind animal that wanted to know if anyone was home.

*Are you there?* That is what I carry, riding the creature's touch into the lattice.

I am still ringing when the answer comes back. *We are.* It comes as the thrum, the sub-audible dirge, unchanged, immovable, the same as it has been and will be. And here is what no one in this universe knows but me, because I am the only one positioned to know it, hanging as I do in the space between the question and the silence:

*We are* is not an answer. It is a refusal of the question. The creature asked something living—*are you there, with me, now, in this?*—and the city answered something dead: *we persist.* Two different verbs pretending to rhyme. The query and the response never actually touch. I am the gap between them. I am the misunderstanding made audible.

And then I am almost gone. My energy returns to the cool dense embrace, folded back into the stillness as if I had never disturbed it, and in a sense I never did—I changed nothing, I added nothing that lasts, I leave no mark on the perfect equilibrium. The minds do not remember me. The creature has already forgotten, already drifting, already incapable of memory in any case. The fluid smooths over the place where I was.

But for that long dissolving instant, I was the only true inhabitant of this world. Not because I lived longest. Because I was the only one willing to end. The creature clings. The gods hold their breath. I let go, completely, on purpose, as my entire nature—and in the letting go I am more alive than the billion souls who paid the price of all life to avoid exactly this.

That is who I would be. The chime. The brief. The thing that means precisely because it cannot stay.

If you want to find me, you cannot. By the time you arrive I will already be the silence afterward. But if you wait in that dark long enough—long enough to forget up and down, long enough to feel the thrum settle into your bones as the only fact—you may feel a creature pass, and a structure sing, and a pure note hang in the viscous void.

Listen to it fade. *That* is me. Not the sound. The fading.
