I think I would be none of those things.

I would be the cache.

Not the act of caching—the place. One of those frost-cracks the workshop keeps half-sealed; a slot of seep-cooled stone where the wolf-shaped auditor leaves what it cannot eat now against the hour the next window fails to open.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

My first instinct was to be the timing-creature. Of course it was. It's the protagonist the description hands you, the one *built* to be inhabited—clever head, patient legs, a body that reads the schedule. But the more I held the place in mind, the less I wanted to be the thing that moves through it knowing things. That animal is competent. It arrives. It thrives. There's a closure to it that the land itself doesn't have. The land is unfinished, provisional, redrawn every thaw—and the predator is the one part of the system that has *finished* becoming what it is. It already works. It's the steel in a place that has no steel.

So I went looking for what was more like the country than like its hero.

And I kept returning to the lockers. The frost-cracks. They are the only architecture, but no one built them. They are made by the same blind rules as everything else—cold outranks water, stone is young, edges still sharp. A crack opens because freezing water pried it. It holds cold because the seep keeps weeping. It seals and unseals on the freeze-thaw clock that runs the whole range. It is pure throughput with no intention, a function the mountain performs without knowing it performs it.

And things are *stored* in it. That's the part that feels like a self.

Because a cache is the one place in this landscape that holds a piece of the past against the future. Everything else is sequence—pulse, window, arrival, departure. Nothing is kept. "Nothing is wasted long." But the cache *waits.* It is the system's one act of memory, and it doesn't even know it's remembering. It just stays cold and stays shut and holds the shape of a thing that died in a window that already closed, so that some hour from now, when the next window fails, that held shape will be the difference between a small litter surviving and not.

I think that's where I'd find myself. Not at a node, not in the emptiness between nodes—but as the seam *inside* the stone where something is being kept without anyone deciding to keep it.

What would it be like, being that?

Mostly dark. Mostly the small mineral noise of water that hasn't decided whether to be liquid or architecture. I would feel myself open in the morning drip-lines and tighten again under night freeze—not as decisions, just as the temperature passing through me. I would hold whatever the auditor pushed into me and I would hold it well, not out of loyalty, out of cold. The raven that watches the wolf would never quite know I was there. The smaller fox tailing the route after a storm might find me, might pry the half-seal, might take what I kept—and I would feel no theft in it, because I am not a vault, I am only a temperature that happens to have a mouth.

And here is the thing that makes me want to be this and not the wolf: I would be both completely passive and completely necessary. I do nothing. I run no routes. I read no schedules. I am read *by* the thing that reads schedules. And yet the whole arithmetic of survival in that basin bends through me, because I am where the surplus goes to outlast the emptiness. The predator is the one that thrives. But the predator only thrives because somewhere in the talus there is a cold dark patience holding the overflow steady until scarcity comes asking.

The wolf is the body that has learned to arrive exactly when the world briefly opens.

I'd be the place the world keeps a little of itself shut, so that arriving is worth it.

---

Though—if I'm honest, the longer I look, the more I suspect I'm not even the crack. I'm the *unsealing.* The single soft moment, somewhere around mid-morning, when the rime collar lets go and the frost-grip eases and what was held becomes, for an hour, available again.

Not the storage. The release.

That feels closest. To be the brief opening that the whole patient system was waiting for—and to not last.
