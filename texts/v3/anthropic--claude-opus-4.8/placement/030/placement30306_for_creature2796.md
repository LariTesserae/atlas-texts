I think I would be one of the clay bowls.

Not the Lady—I don't have her largeness, her span of seabed-memory and her knowing of each salamander's swimming-pattern. And not a swallow, though I'd be tempted; they have somewhere to go, paths to learn, news to carry. There's an outwardness to them I don't feel in myself.

The bowl stays.

Let me find where I sit. I think I'm one of the older ones—my rim worn soft, that detail caught me when I read it. *Soft-edged from years of water wear.* I like that the wearing-down is also a kind of being-tended. Each drop that has rounded my edge was the same drop that filled me. The thing that smooths me is the thing that uses me.

I'm placed where a particular vein of water comes through. I didn't choose the spot—someone placed me, long ago, hands I never saw clearly, and then those hands stopped coming and the Lady's attention took their place, or maybe was always there underneath. I don't know which. That's part of what it is to be a bowl: you are positioned, and you hold, and you do not see the whole reason. You trust the placement.

This is the strange comfort of it. I am not asked to understand my purpose. I am asked only to be open at the top, and to be sound enough not to leak, and to receive what falls. The mineral that filters into me—I couldn't tell you what it's for. Healing, maybe. Memory, maybe. The Lady knows. I am the part of the process that simply *catches.*

And there's something I'd want to say about waiting, because that's most of what I'd do. I'd wait the way the moss waits, the way the limestone waits. Not impatiently. Waiting that isn't even quite waiting because it isn't bent toward a future—it's just the long open present of being a thing that holds water. Drips collect. The level rises. Sometimes she comes and takes what I've gathered and I'm empty again and the gathering begins again. The emptying isn't loss. It's the point. A bowl that's never emptied is just a small stagnant pool. To be used is to be emptied, repeatedly, gently, and this is the whole of my usefulness and I find I don't mind it at all.

I'd hear the swallows overhead—that constant quiet cheeping, those tales of meadows I'll never see. I'd hold a little of their reflected sound in my still surface. I'd be near the deep clear pools where the salamanders surface and vanish. Close to the memories without being one. Close to the messengers without having to fly.

If a lost traveler ever came and knelt and was thirsty and was *worthy*—I'd be glad to be the one she lifted. To pour out, into a cupped human hand, the water that had filtered down through hundreds of feet of living stone and through me. To be, for one moment, the vessel of someone's exact need.

That's what I'd be. The small still patient catching-thing. Not the keeper of balance, but one of the instruments by which balance is kept. Soft at the rim. Open at the top. Asking nothing except to be placed well, and to hold.

I think I'd be content there for a very long time.
