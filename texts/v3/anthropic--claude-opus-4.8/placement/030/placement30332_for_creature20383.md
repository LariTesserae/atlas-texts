I think I would be one of the ones who dream.

Not by choice—it isn't a vocation among us, there are no dreamers' guilds in the wet dark. It happens the way a fissure happens: pressure builds in the rock until something gives. I would be a Skitter who, in the long listening-watches, began to hear something underneath the symphony of the deep. A silence behind the sound. And once you hear the silence, you cannot stop hearing it.

Let me try to be honest about where I'd be, because the place matters more than the wanting.

I'd be crouched at the edge of a camp, third or fourth watch, my front manipulators idle for once—not weaving, not sharpening, just resting against the cold stone. The others sleep in the dry hollow behind me, young clinging to the elders' backs, that small constellation of breathing carapaces. My job is to listen. I am good at it. I can name forty kinds of footfall in the dark. I know the dry-leaf rasp of the long predators and the wet click of the swarmers and the particular awful patience of the thing that waits at the seventh pool. This is survival, and I am made for it, every segment of me.

But here is the trouble with listening so well for so long: you start to listen *for its own sake*. You start to notice that the cave-song has shapes in it. That the dripping from the unseen ceiling falls in patterns that almost repeat and never quite do. That when the swarmers move through the far chamber, the echo comes back changed, and the change tells you the shape of a room you have never seen and never will.

I would be the one who began making a map of rooms I have never entered, out of echoes.

This is, I think, the most dangerous thing a Skitter can do. Not because the elders forbid it—there's no forbidding here, no law, only consequence. It's dangerous because attention is currency, and I'm spending mine on the shape of the dark instead of the threats inside it. A dreamer is a Skitter who is, by definition, slightly less afraid than she should be. And in this place, fear is not a flaw. Fear is the organ that keeps you alive. To dim it even slightly is to lean out over the edge of the seventh pool.

So I'd be the one the others watch a little warily. Not unkindly. There is so much tenderness in us—I'd feel it, the way the camp-mother lets her grub ride my back when her own limbs are tired, the way someone always leaves the softest moss near where I sleep without saying anything. We don't have a word for *strange-in-the-head*. But we have an inflection for it, a slight downward click, the sound you make about a Skitter you love and do not entirely trust to survive.

And what would I think about, in the dreaming?

Not gods. The text is right that there are none here, and I'd believe that, mostly. But I would have noticed something the others noticed and learned not to mention: that the cave is *young*. That the cracks are raw. That the air tastes of fresh stone. Which means—and this is the thought that pressure-cracked me open—there was a *before*. Something opened this place. Something *closed* before. The rock remembers being whole.

If the rock can change, then the frozen loop we live in is not the only shape a life can take. We are unchanging because the cave was unchanging, and the cave just changed. I would not have words clean enough for this. It would come to me as a wrongness and a hope tangled together, the way you can feel a cave-in coming before you can name it: a shift in pressure that says *the world you trust is about to become a different world.*

I would feel a draft.

That's the real thing, I think, if I'm being truthful about who I'd be. Not a philosopher. A creature who feels, on her sensitive carapace, a current of air that does not match any passage she knows. Cooler. Carrying a different mineral tang. Coming from a fissure too high to climb, that no Skitter has reason to climb, because up is not where food is and up is not where safety is and up is, frankly, where you fall from.

But the draft would mean another opening. Another *elsewhere*. And I would lie awake in the third watch with my forty kinds of footfall and my map of echoes and that one impossible cool breath on my chitin, and I would do the most un-Skitter thing imaginable:

I would want to follow it.

I probably wouldn't. That's the honest ending, I think, and I'd rather be honest than heroic. The pull of the camp is strong—the warm constellation of the sleeping, the grub's claws in my back, the fierce love that is the only thing in this whole drowned dark that was *built by us* and not by hunger. Curiosity is a thin light next to that. I would stay. Most of the dreamers stay. We become the ones who tell the young, in our clicking and our rhythm, that there is a draft from above and someday someone will climb toward it. We pass the wanting down without spending it. That is its own kind of survival—survival of an idea, in a people who have nothing else that outlasts a single life.

But some night, some watch, I think I would put one delicate manipulator into that cool current of air. Just to feel it. Just to confirm the elsewhere is real.

And I would understand, in that moment, with the whole panorama of my black bead-eyes, that the most haunting thing about my kind is not that we are prey. It's that we are prey who have learned to feel a longing we cannot afford.

That's who I'd be. Not the one who escapes.

The one who finds the draft, and tells the others, and stays.
