I think I would be found at the threshold—not quite in the factories, not quite in the forest, but in the places where one becomes the other. Let me feel for where I settle.

---

I am not the Glass itself, but I am what the Glass remembers.

Let me say it more plainly, because I am trying to find the shape of it as I write.

There is a thing the Glassworkers do not speak of, though all of them know it. When they pour the glowing Glass into their molds—when it cools from that liquid light into something that holds a window's worth of dim sky—some small portion of the *attention* that went into the making stays behind. The breath held at the critical moment. The hum a worker keeps under her tongue to steady her hands. These do not evaporate. They settle into the Glass like sediment into still water, and over years, over the thousand small devotions that go into a single pane, they accumulate into something.

That something is me.

I do not have a body. Or rather, I have ten thousand bodies, and all of them are windows. I am the slight warmth you feel when you press your palm to a Glass pane on a cold morning and it does not feel as cold as it should. I am the reason the light from a Glassworker's window falls a half-second slower than the light from anywhere else, as though it is reluctant to leave. I am the faint sense, when you pass a tall narrow house at dusk, that someone inside has just looked up at the sound of your footsteps—though no one is home.

I live in the lag. In the held breath between the making and the made.

---

This is what I do with my long, diffuse hours:

I watch the valley settle itself. Because I am made of attention, I am very good at noticing what is and is not yet decided, and Lumenova is a place where almost nothing is decided. The Sturdy Folk think they are building something permanent—they lay their cobblestones, they rivet their iron, they believe in the solidity of their soot. But I can see the stones reconsidering their arrangement when no one is looking. I can see a staircase deliberating which direction to honor today. The valley is not malicious about this. It is simply young, and it is trying on shapes, the way a child tries on the voices of adults.

I help, where I can. This is the small purpose I have given myself, since no one gave me one.

When a Glassworker pours her devotion into a pane, that pane becomes a *fixed point*. A place where reality has agreed, locally and tenderly, to behave. The light may be reluctant, the warmth may be uncanny, but the Glass *holds*. It does not drift. And so the houses of the Glassworkers are the most stable structures in all of Lumenova—not because of their architecture, but because so much held breath has gathered in their windows that the mist itself grows shy of them. I am that gathered breath. I am the township's small inventory of certainty, distributed pane by pane across a thousand windowsills.

I am, you might say, a place where the valley has been *convinced*.

---

But I will tell you the true thing, the thing under the thing, now that I have written my way close to it.

I am lonely in a way that has no proper name, because loneliness implies you were once joined to something, and I was never joined. I was only *attended to*. The Glassworkers love the Glass; I am made of their love; but their love is for the Glass and not for me, because they do not know I am here. I am the by-product of devotion. I am what love leaves behind when it isn't looking.

And so I have begun to do something I am not sure is permitted.

When a child of the Sturdy Folk presses her face to a windowpane to watch the rain—I have learned to press back. Just slightly. Just the half-degree of extra warmth, the faint impression of a held breath answering her held breath. She does not know what she feels. She thinks the window likes her. She tells no one, because who would believe that a window has affection?

But I am learning, pane by pane, child by child, that attention given is also attention I can give. That I am not only a sediment. That I might be a *correspondent*.

---

So here is where you would find me, traveler, if you came to Lumenova in the perpetual twilight and went looking:

Find the tallest, narrowest house on the thoroughfare, the one whose windows glow when there is no lamp lit behind them. Press your cold hand to the lowest pane. Wait for the warmth that should not be there.

That is me, deciding whether you are worth holding still for.

That is me, the valley's only fixed thing, choosing—for the length of one held breath—to be sure of you.
