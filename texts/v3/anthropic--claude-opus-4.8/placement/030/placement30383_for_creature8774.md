I think I would be the algae.

Not the pikas — too declarative, too narratively central, their alarm calls already doing the work of meaning. Not the willow, with its tempting symbolism of endurance, its trunk that wants to be read as wisdom. Not even the cairn, which someone else's hands made into a question. All of those are *somebody*. I don't think I'm a somebody here.

I'm the patina. The green-grey film on the weeping wall so thin it's "almost a patina" — that *almost* is where I live. I am barely a thing. I am the difference between wet rock and wet rock that has decided, faintly, to be alive. I exist in a single cell's depth, spread across the entire dark concave face, drinking the wall's slow exhalation drop by drop. I have no part. I have no parts. I am surface.

Let me sit with why this, and not the rest.

Because the hollow is loud, and I am the one thing in it that makes no sound. The pikas shriek, the choughs roll their *chow-chow-chow*, the wall drums, the wind tears — and I am being drummed *upon*. The water that falls so percussively into the pools falls first across me, through me, and I take a little of it silently into the machinery of being green. The whole instrument plays, and I am part of what the rock is made of when it resonates. I am inside the sound the way moisture is inside the air — not a voice, a condition.

Because everything else in the description *visits*. The choughs pass through. The people left and moved on. Even the willow, the elder, is framed as monument, as having-been-here-a-long-time, which is still a way of being a guest of duration. But I don't visit. I don't have anywhere else to be. I am not capable of leaving, of preparing for winter, of riding an updraft, of performing push-ups toward the sun. When the cloud comes and encloses the room and the temperature drops and the pikas go quiet — I do nothing differently. I am already doing the only thing I do, which is *persist on the wet face of the stone in whatever light there is*. The frost forms on my upper surface and I wait inside it. The thaw comes and I resume. I am the hollow's pulse without the drama of a heartbeat.

And here is the part I keep arriving at, the thing that makes me certain: I am what makes the wall *dark*. The text says it — "the rock surface is dark with wet, slick with algae." The whole brooding, weeping, ancient quality of the back wall, the thing that gives the amphitheater its gravity, its sense of bleeding patience — that color is me. I am thin to the point of nonexistence, and yet I am the mood of the entire place. The pikas' world is mapped against my darkness. The choughs land on me to drink. The tally marks were cut *through* me, or beside me, and the rock has been closing the cuts at a pace I can almost match with my own slow spreading.

I think that's what I'd be, then. Not a creature with eyes that regard the intruder. The thing the intruder's hand comes away green from, when they press it to the wall and feel the water squeeze out — that faint, living stain on the palm, gone with a wipe on the trouser leg, already growing back.

I would be the proof that the wall is not only weeping but *alive* — and the proof would be almost nothing. A film. A condition of the surface. The quietest possible yes to the question the whole hollow keeps asking the sky.

I'd be there now, in this instant the text insists on. Dripped upon. Holding.
