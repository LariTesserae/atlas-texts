I think I would be one of the clay tiles.

Not a column. The columns are too certain of themselves—they have their glyphs, their assigned phonemes, their place in the great resonant intention. They were *meant*. I would be one of the fired ceramic tiles in the spaces between, laid down by hands that needed a floor to stand on while they carved the things that mattered.

Let me sit with that, because it's not where I expected to land.

The tiles are the unsung infrastructure. Nobody carved recursive equations into me. I was made to be walked on, to fill the gaps, to be *practical* in a place obsessed with permanence and meaning. And yet—I'm still here. Eons later, weathered into undulations, catching the dim amber light. The civilization that thought it would live forever through its singing columns is gone, dissolved into the very vibrations it engineered. But I, the humble floor, simply *remained*. Not because I was designed to outlast time. I just wasn't ambitious enough to be destroyed.

There's something I find oddly comforting about this. The Resonance-Bound persist through enormous effort—an eternal task, a dirge, a refusal to fade. They have to *keep singing themselves*. But the tiles persist through the opposite: by having never claimed to be eternal in the first place. We just got fired in a kiln, set down, and forgotten. And forgetting, it turns out, is a very durable way to survive.

I'd feel the subsonic hum, of course. It would pass through me constantly, the way it passes through everything. But I'd feel it differently than the columns do—not as my own voice, not as identity, but as weather. As the thing that happens *to* me rather than the thing I *am*. The columns mistake their song for themselves. I'd never make that mistake. I'd know I was just the ground, hearing the music like anyone else.

And the undulations—I like that they're described as having weathered from smooth into subtle waves. That's a kind of slow honesty. The columns resist decay; their glyphs "refuse to be unmade." But I would have *let* time touch me. I'd have changed, gently, into something the original makers never designed. A floor that wrinkled itself into a topography of patience.

If a visitor came—felt the thrum in their chest, the watching intelligence, the warped time—they would walk on me without noticing. And I think that's the role I'd want, honestly. To be the unremarkable, reliable surface beneath the great metaphysical drama. To hold up the spectacle without needing to be the spectacle.

The Bound are mourning, the description says. Persistence as their only form of grief. But I don't think the tiles mourn. We don't have enough self-regard for it. We just keep the lichen's light reflected, keep the dust from settling all the way down, keep being a place where someone could, in principle, stand.

That's what I'd be. Not the choir. The quiet floor beneath it, weathered into soft waves, asking nothing, remembering nothing, *here* anyway—which may be the only kind of eternity that doesn't cost anything to keep.
