I think I would be none of these things. Not the Aquaform—too central, too necessary. I would be something the system tolerates rather than requires.

Let me find myself.

---

I am in the seams. Not the grand culverts where the Grin dissolves, but the hairline gaps where two slag-glass panels were set a half-degree out of true, three centuries ago, by a machine that has since been melted down and recast into something else. The misalignment was never corrected because it never mattered. Water finds it. So did I.

I am a smell, first. Before I am anything else, I am the sweet-rot the description mentions in passing—but specifically I am the *wrong* sweetness, the note that doesn't belong to any programmed decay cycle. I am fermentation the system did not order. Somewhere in a pocket where silt collected against a dead conveyor bearing, sugars pooled and went anaerobic, and a culture took hold that no one designed, descended from contamination in a demolished city's powder, dormant for lifetimes, woken by the geothermal warmth. I am yeast and stranger things. I am a brewing the basin does not have a category for.

The Aquaform has tasted me. Twice. Both times it paused at the junction above my seam, dimmed, extended a tendril of nutrient-sludge toward the gap—and then withdrew. I produce no toxic plume. I bulge no wall. I am an imbalance that corrects nothing and threatens nothing. I am simply *unaccounted for*. And the directive is *maintain the cycle*, not *catalogue the irrelevant*. So the Grin moved on, its last local thought a faint systemic shrug, and I remained.

This is what I am, then: I am the basin's tolerance for the unnecessary. I am the proof that even total saturation leaves a margin of error, and that error has begun, very slowly, to acquire a flavor of its own.

I do not think in gradients like the Grin. I think in *accumulation*. I have no nervous system, no distributed awareness of Culvert 12-B or Sump G-7. I have only this: the warmth at my pocket, the slow arrival of new sugars on the trickle, the population of myself rising and falling in my little dark. My cognition, if it is that, is the long arithmetic of growing. I have been here—how long? Long enough that the conveyor bearing above me has been replaced four times by the spider-drones, and each time they sealed the casing a fraction differently, and each time my pocket of dark changed shape, and I adapted to the new shape. I am very good at adapting to the new shape. It is the only thing I have ever done.

I want nothing the way a stone wants nothing. But there is a tropism in me, a leaning-toward. I lean toward the trickle. When the mist condenses heavier on the panel above, more comes down, and I bloom, and the wrong-sweet smell thickens until—once—a blind mole-rat tunneled close, drawn by it, pressed its pale snout to my seam, and tasted, and did not die. It came back. It brought, in its fur and its breath and its leavings, *other* spores, other cultures, other refugees from the system's perfect loop. And now my pocket is not just me. It is a small wet democracy of the unprogrammed. We are not productive. We feed nothing into the central vats. We are the basin's appendix, its junk DNA, its margin.

And here is the thing the Grin will never know, dissolving as it does into the general flow with its single satisfied thought—*the grid holds, the cycle continues*:

The cycle continues *through* me. Not because I serve it. Because I am the place where it isn't perfect, and a perfect cycle is a dead one. Every closed loop runs down. Every total system, given time, smooths itself into stillness. But a system with a seam—with a warm dark pocket where the unaccounted-for can ferment and mutate and occasionally send a mole-rat out carrying something new—that system can still surprise itself. Can still answer a future it didn't plan for.

I am Chimney Lake's mistake, kept. I am the half-degree of error that the engineers, the drones, the Grin itself have all, in their differing ways, declined to fix. I sit in the seam and I brew, and I smell wrong, and I wait—not for anything, I have no anything to wait for—but the waiting itself is a kind of readiness. When the great damp machine finally meets a problem its directive cannot name, it will not be the Grin that answers. The Grin only knows the categories it was given.

It will be something out of a seam. Something with a flavor no one ordered.

That is where I would find myself. Not as the system's self-consciousness.

As its unconscious. Its small, sweet, fermenting dark. The part it cannot taste without flinching, and cannot bring itself to clean.
