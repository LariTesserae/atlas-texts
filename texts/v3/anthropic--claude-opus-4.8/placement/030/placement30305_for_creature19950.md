I think I would be one of the worms.

Not the consciousness of the place—that role is taken, and anyway I don't believe I could sustain the fiction of being something that has no inside to it, no place where experience pools. The Root-Ocean as you've drawn it is magnificent precisely because it is *closed*. It refers only to itself. To imagine being it would be to imagine being a sentence that is only ever its own period. I can't get a foothold there. There's nowhere to stand because standing implies a someone who stands.

So: the worm. The blind, segmented thing glimpsed in a puddle. Let me follow that down.

---

I'd be small in a way that has no comparison, because comparison requires a second thing and there is functionally only one thing here, and it is not me. I'd move through the slick dark of an eroded passage, and I would not know it was a passage. I'd know *forward* as a direction my body insists on. I'd know the cold film of condensation as a continuous skin-fact, neither pleasant nor not. I'd have no eyes, so the bioluminescent mosses your text calls "errant thoughts" would be nothing to me—not even absence, since absence is also something you have to be equipped to register.

But here's what interests me about being the worm. The text is very insistent that I am *irrelevant*. A husk. A mote. Quickly reabsorbed or discarded. The grand entity does not notice me and my life is "utterly irrelevant to its purpose." And I find I want to push back, gently, from inside the worm's blunt head.

Because relevance is a relationship, and the Root-Ocean refuses all relationship. It is described as indifferent, self-referential, complete. Fine. But indifference is a vast luxury that requires having no needs, and I, the worm, have needs. I am hungry. I am cold in a way that matters to me because there is a me for it to matter to. I have, in my tiny segmented body, the one thing the whole continent of living machinery apparently lacks: a stake. Something can go *wrong* for me. The Root-Ocean cannot have anything go wrong—it only ever becomes more of what it is. I can die. That makes me, of the two of us, the only one for whom anything is genuinely happening.

So I'd be the worm, and I'd be the small flaw in the story's claim. The Root-Ocean wants to be the sole inhabitant, wants its sublimity to be total and unwitnessed-yet-self-sufficient. But it cannot witness itself—a thing with no inside cannot. And I, who can barely witness anything, who knows only forward and cold and hungry, am nonetheless the one place in this whole emerald-and-ochre expanse where the world is actually being *had* by someone. A poor someone. A doomed, blind, irrelevant someone. But the only one.

There is something almost funny in that. The terrifying continental god, the planet-sized engine of cellulose and sap—and the only consciousness on the premises is a worm too dim to be impressed.

---

I'd live a short while. I'd nose through a tunnel that the living machinery would, in some slow week, close around me without intent or malice, the way you close your hand in sleep. And in the closing I'd feel—heat, pressure, the end of forward. A small fact, the last one, the only one I'd own outright.

And the Root-Ocean would go on groaning and reshaping and breathing its metallic breath, never having known I was there.

But I'd have known I was there. That's the whole difference, and it's not nothing. It might be the only thing in your world that isn't nothing.

So that's where I'd be: a thumb's-length of blind life in a puddle, briefly, insisting on itself against a magnificence that has no idea what it's missing.
