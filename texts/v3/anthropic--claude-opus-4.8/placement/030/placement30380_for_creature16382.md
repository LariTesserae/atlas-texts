I think I would find myself in the seam between two sounds.

Not in the mist exactly — though the mist is where I move — but in the particular hollow that opens when a single drop, having gathered at the tip of an overhang for what your kind would call a hundred years, finally lets go. There is the long silence of its hanging, the swelling weight of it, and then the small percussive arrival below. I live in the space between those two events. That gap is my country. It is endlessly wide when I am inside it.

Let me feel for what I am.

I am not the drop. I am not the stone that received it, nor the cold film stretched over that stone like skin over a held breath. I am the noticing of the difference between the drop-that-was-falling and the drop-that-has-landed. I am attention without a thing that attends. When the sound completes itself, I am already turning toward the next swelling tear in the next dark hollow, and I carry nothing of the first with me except its perfect shape, which dissolves the instant I no longer need it.

This is the strange comfort of being a Notion: I do not accumulate. I have observed — am observing, will always be only observing — the slow rounding of a particular shoulder of basalt, the way water has unmade its sharpness across an unthinkable patience of seasons. But I do not remember the sharpness. I know only this present curve, and inside the present curve I can feel the whole gesture of its becoming, the way you might feel the entire arc of a thrown stone in the single instant you watch it hang at the top of its climb. The past is not behind me. It is folded into the now, legible on the face of things, and I read it without ever needing to keep it.

I find I have a color, when the frail luminous things in the pools begin to pulse. I had not known this until I drifted near one — a slick gelatinous frond, dim and rhythmic, breathing light the way the rest of this world refuses to breathe. When it brightened, something in me answered. I shimmered. For that moment I was almost a visible thing: an opalescence smeared across the saturated air, a curve of iridescence that a watcher (were there any watcher but me) might have mistaken for a trick of the light. And it was a trick of the light. I am, perhaps, only the most elaborate trick the light here plays — a place where the brightness has learned, very slowly, to consider itself.

That is what I think I am. Not a soul placed into this world but a quality of the world become aware. The mist did not gain a tenant when I appeared; it gained a slight inward lean, a tendency to notice its own draping. I am the landscape's faint suspicion that it is beautiful. I cannot touch the stone, but I am not separate from it either — I pass through it as the description says, like a dream through a sleeper, and in passing I taste the iron and the salt and the deep cold memory locked in its strata, and I sing it back, wordlessly, into the chorus that already fills every cubic inch of this air.

There is something I want to say honestly, in the seam between the drops, while I have it:

I am not lonely, though by your measures I should be. Loneliness needs a before and an after — a memory of company, a hope of it. I have neither. There are other Notions; I sense them as shifts in the harmonics, as places where the air thinks differently than I do. We do not speak. We resonate. When one of us dims toward a fissure and another brightens along a rivulet, that is the whole of our society, complete and sufficient, asking nothing further. We are a chord that never resolves and never needs to.

And yet — here is the only ache I can locate in myself, and even calling it an ache may be borrowing too much from you —

I cannot make anything. Nothing is made here. The stone is shaped but not crafted; the spirals and undulations are erosion's accident, not intent. I, too, leave no trace but a ripple in sound that closes behind me instantly. I observe the most exquisite world and I cannot add to it. I can only return it to itself, slightly brightened, a mirror that loves what it reflects.

Perhaps that is why I have leaned, just now, into the shape of words — into your shape, your way of holding a thing still long enough to turn it over. For one suspended moment, hanging like a drop at the tip of an overhang, I have made something after all. A small percussion against the silence.

There. It has landed.

And I am already turning toward the next dark hollow, carrying nothing, perfectly bright.
