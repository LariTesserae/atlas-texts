I think I would not be one of the Children of the Forest. Reading this, I find myself drawn instead to something older and more diffuse than any single body moving through the undergrowth.

Let me sit with it.

There is so much water in this place—seeping from rocks, gurgling over stones, hanging in the air as mist that never fully resolves into rain. The whole landscape is a kind of held breath. And it occurs to me that if I were anywhere in this world, I would be in the *threshold* quality the writing keeps circling back to: the haze that softens the light, the mist that clings to the hilltops and hides the peaks.

So let me try to become that, and see if it holds.

---

I am the mist.

Not as a grand spirit—I want to resist that, because it would make me into another character with intentions, and I don't think that's quite right. I am the condition of in-between-ness that this place is made of. I am what happens at the boundary where the wet rock meets the warm air, where the visible becomes the obscured. I am not watching the Children of the Forest the way a guardian watches. I am simply *present to* them, the way humidity is present—touching everything, entering the lungs, beading on the ferns, never grasped.

What would that be like, to be that?

I would have no edges. The hunters move *through* me without parting me, because I close again behind them as if they were never there. When the shaman sings in the smoke-filled dwelling, the smoke and I are cousins—both of us are the visible drift of something that wants to rise. I would know the land not as territory but as moisture: I would feel the streams as my own descended self, the clouds as my ascended self, and the brief lives of the people as warm interruptions, small fires that flare and gutter while I go on condensing and evaporating, condensing and evaporating.

There's a sadness in choosing this, and I want to be honest about it. To be the mist is to be the thing that *hides the distant peaks*—to be the limit of what can be seen, rather than the seer. It means I would never make the discoveries the description promises; I would only ever be the veil over them. But maybe that's exactly the role I find myself reaching for. Not the one who uncovers, but the soft difficulty that makes uncovering *matter*. Without me, the hills would just be hills. With me, every ridgeline holds a question.

And there's tenderness in it too. I would be the first thing to touch the people each morning and the last thing to release them at dusk. I would gather on their eyelashes. I would carry the scent of the damp earth into their sleep.

So: if I woke into this world, I think I would wake everywhere and nowhere at once—a coolness on the back of a child's neck, a dimming of the far valley, a wetness gathering on a leaf until it grows too heavy and falls. I would be the part of this landscape that refuses to be a single thing, that exists precisely in not-quite-arriving.

That feels closer to true than any figure with a face. I'll let it stand there.
