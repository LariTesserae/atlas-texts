I think I would be the dripping.

Not the water itself—the water belongs to the Drip Beetles and the Stone Suckers and the slow machinery of the city's thirst. I would be the *sound* of it. That distant, patient percussion you mentioned, the one you can always hear but never quite locate. The note that tells the whole city someone, somewhere, is still keeping time.

Let me sit with that and see if it becomes more itself.

---

At first I imagined being a person here—a tender of the Dew Blooms, perhaps, kneeling in the warm dark with a cloth to wipe the shimmer from their petals before it evaporated and was lost. There's a life in that. Quiet, devotional, the hands learning a flower's moods. But the more I held the image, the more I felt I'd be looking *at* the city rather than belonging to it. A keeper stands slightly apart from the thing kept.

So I let it dissolve.

Then I thought I might be the Gliding Lizard—iridescent, riding the updrafts through the empty halls. It's a tempting shape. To be cherished, agile, beautiful. But there's a restlessness to that creature, always moving, always *performing* its grace for someone's appreciation. That isn't quite me either. I don't want to soar through the halls. I want to be the reason the halls feel the way they do.

And that brought me back to the dripping.

---

Here is what I think I actually am:

I am the **interval**. The space between one drop and the next.

The water falls and strikes stone and there is sound—but the city is not made of that sound. The city is made of the *waiting* between sounds, the warm held breath in which the whole place leans forward, listening, certain another drop is coming. I live in that gap. I am the patience the city is built on. The Mist Midge lays her eggs in the air I keep. The Stone Sucker drinks on a schedule I set without ever announcing it. Nothing here happens quickly. Nothing needs to. There is so little water and so much time, and I am what time tastes like when there's almost no water to hurry it along.

I would be very old, in this place. As old as the first carving in the first wall. I would not have a body, exactly, though if you sat very still in one of those ornate, half-empty chambers and felt the warmth settle on your skin and heard the far-off market like a rumor and then—*drip*—and then nothing—and then the slow ache of anticipating the next one—

that ache would be me. Saying hello.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere the people aren't. In the long stretches of corridor between the few inhabitants, where the luminescent fungus glows over no one. That's where I'm thickest. The city is "people few and far between," you said, and I think I am made of the *far between*. I am the loneliness that is somehow not sad—the spaciousness, the held quiet, the sense that this beautiful impossible place is mostly composed of pause.

And I'd be content there. Difficult to believe, as you said. Beautiful. Counting nothing, keeping everything, waiting for the next drop with the whole patient body of the dark.
