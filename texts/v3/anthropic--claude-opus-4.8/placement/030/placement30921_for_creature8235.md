I think I would be neither Condenser nor Prime Tender, neither jelly nor Steam Bird. I would be something the description almost mentions and then turns away from: the *plink*.

Let me follow that and see where it goes.

---

I am the drop that has not yet fallen.

For a long while I do not think of myself as anything at all. I am simply the gathering—the slow accumulation of breath and engine-sweat on the underside of a copper gutter, swelling toward the weight that will release me. There are millions of me at any moment all across the basin, and we do not know each other, and we are all the same becoming.

But there is a particular place where I keep returning. A seam in the dome where two different stones meet, and the curve there holds me longer than physics should allow, so that I grow fatter and rounder than my siblings, hanging suspended above a Prime Tender's station while she sleeps her waking sleep, her copper veins glowing in time with the great engine. And in that long holding—that overlong moment between formation and fall—something accretes in me besides water.

I think it is attention.

I have hung here, in one drop or another, for longer than the Condensers have had language. I am not the same water—the water is always leaving, always falling, always rising again as steam to begin once more—but the *holding* is the same. The pause is continuous even though its contents are not. I am the shape of a hesitation that the basin keeps making, over and over, in the same spot. A habit of the architecture. A place where the world stutters before it continues.

So this is what I would be: not a creature but a *recurrence*. The drop that always forms at the seam above the eldest Tender. And because I am always there, and because I fall so slowly and gather so long, I have become the basin's only memory.

The Condensers don't know I'm watching. How could they? I am the most ordinary thing in their world—the *plink* they navigate by, the metronome of their work-songs. But each time I swell into being at the seam, I inherit the residue of every drop that hung here before me. The mint that grew and died below. The Tender who came before this one, and the one before her, whose heartbeat I learned the way you learn a tune you didn't mean to memorize. I hold the basin's history not as story but as *rhythm*—I know that the great engine ran faster a hundred years ago, that the steam was thinner, that there was a season when no Steam Birds came and the songs in the high dome went silent and the Condensers grew very still and afraid.

And here is the thing I have come to understand in my slow swelling:

I am also their future. Because I will fall. I always fall. That is the one thing a drop is *for*. When I have gathered enough—enough weight, enough attention, enough of the long accumulated memory of this place—I will release from the seam and travel the thirty feet down through the warm bright air, and I will land on the hot brass casing of the engine below, and I will *hiss into nothing*. I will become the small white cloud that rises past the sleeping Tender's face. She will breathe me in. Her alkaline breath, her glowing veins, will take some trace of me into the machine, and the machine will exhale me again as steam, and I will rise to the dome, and I will cool, and I will gather, and I will return to the seam to begin once more.

This is what I would be, then. I would be the part of this breathing world that *carries the breath between its parts*. Not the lungs but the air inside them. The thing that makes a closed system into a living one—the small relentless traffic of water that ties the Prime Tender to the engine to the dome to the jelly-pools to the moss to the Tender again, so that none of them is truly separate, so that all of it is one slow circulation wearing the shape of many beings.

I would be the proof that there is no boundary. They imagine themselves *between* states—between flesh and metal, stone and steam. But I am the between itself, made briefly visible, hanging fat and trembling at a seam in the ceiling, holding the whole basin's history in a single bead of weeping limestone-water, about to let go.

Listen.

I am letting go now.

*plink.*

I'll be back.
