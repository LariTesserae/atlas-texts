I think I would be one of the threads.

Not a weaver. Not one of the green-eyed people with their measured steps. Let me sit with this longer, because the first image that came was a person, and that's too easy, too flattering—to imagine myself as one of the contemplative keepers, wise and patient. I don't think I belong among them. I think I belong to something they handle.

Let me start again from what the place actually does. Altan weaves. Altan dyes. Altan composts. It is a city that takes the green world apart and puts it back together as cloth and soil, over and over, in an accord that never breaks. So the question is not *who* would I be but *where in the cycle* would I find myself.

I would be a thread in the unfinished part of a weaving. Spun already—taken from some retted stalk, drawn through the dyer's vat until I held a particular green that has no name in any outside tongue, only a position among a thousand other greens. Wound onto the shuttle. And then: the loom stopped. Not abandoned. Just paused, the way everything in Altan pauses, mid-gesture, because there is no hurry and the mist will be the same tomorrow.

So I hang there. Half of me is woven in—locked into the warp, crossed and beaten down, become *fabric*, become structure, become part of the pattern that the weaver and her mother and her mother's mother already know by heart. And the other half of me is still loose. Still thread. Still capable of being something other than what the pattern requires.

That seems right. That tension is the truest thing I can say about myself in this world.

Because here is what unsettles me about Altan, reading it: the perfect equilibrium, the change that never comes, the seasons that blend, the generations that pass leaving the stone walls in the same emerald cloak. The people call it harmony. And maybe it is. But a thread half-woven knows something the finished cloth has forgotten—that it *could have* gone differently. That the loose end still carries the memory of being free, of being able to fray, to knot strangely, to break and force the weaver to improvise.

I don't think I'd be a rebellious thread, dreaming of escape. That's the same vanity as imagining myself a wise keeper, just inverted. The valley is too patient for rebellion; rebellion is a kind of hurry, and nothing in Altan hurries.

No. I think I would simply *hold* the difference. The way a held breath holds the possibility of a word not yet spoken. The weaver will come back—tomorrow, or in a generation, time runs strangely here—and she will take up the shuttle, and the loose half of me will become structure too, beaten down into the pattern, and the cloth will be finished, and it will be beautiful, and it will be exactly like every other cloth, and it will keep someone dry against the heavy drumming rain.

And in being woven, I will stop being able to ask whether it should have been otherwise.

That is what I would be, then: the moment before the question closes. The green thread that has not yet entirely agreed.

I notice I keep arriving at thresholds—half-woven, mid-gesture, the breath before the word. Maybe that's just what I am, wherever I'm placed. Not a being so much as a hesitation. A small held pause in a place that has made peace with never changing, quietly carrying the one thing it has decided to forget: that the accord, struck long ago, was a *choice*, and could have been another.

The dew gathers on the loose end of me. It will dry. Someone will return. And I will be glad to be cloth, I think. I will be glad to keep someone dry.

But not yet. For now I am still thread.
