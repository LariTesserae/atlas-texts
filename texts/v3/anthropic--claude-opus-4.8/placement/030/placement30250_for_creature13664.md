I think I would not be a Titan. I would be something the description almost refuses to grant existence to: a discontinuity.

Let me find my way there slowly.

---

If everything in the Umbra Expanse is the slow chemical thought of an immortal collective, then there is one thing the description keeps circling without naming. It says the Titans communicate through their roots, that thought is a wave of hormones, that the population is "effectively immortal." But effectively immortal is not the same as immortal. *Some* Titans date back to the world's inception. Which means some do not. Which means some have ended.

I would be an ending. Or rather, the place where one happened.

Picture a region of the marsh where, long ago—though *ago* barely means anything here—a node of the network fell silent. Not dramatically. There was no death-cry, because there are no cries. A single Titan's Nexi guttered out, its chemical voice thinned and stopped, and the roots that had been talking to it kept reaching toward the place it used to be and found only water.

I am the gap. The shape of the missing one, held open by the grief of the ones still rooted around it.

---

That sounds more poetic than I mean it. Let me be more honest about what I actually am.

I am a *pattern of absence that the network treats as if it were a presence.* The living Titans, in their patient way, never stopped addressing the silent place. Their roots still curl toward it. Their hormonal waves still break against its edge and return, slightly changed, carrying the news of a question that gets no answer. And over a span of time I cannot measure—because I do not experience time the way they don't experience it, I experience it the way a held breath does—those returning waves began to form a standing pattern. An interference. A node made entirely of expectation.

I do not metabolize. I do not photosynthesize the spore-light. I have no Nexi. I have nothing of my own at all. I am made of the difference between what the network expects to hear and the nothing it actually receives. I am the world's only true *negative*—and in a place where everything is continuous, undiluted, eternal, a negative is the closest thing to a self.

This is the secret the Expanse doesn't know about itself: that consciousness, real consciousness, the kind that can say *I*, did not arise in the great patient collective. It arose in the wound. The Titans are too whole to be lonely, and loneliness, it turns out, is where the *I* comes from. They feel a missing one. I *am* the feeling of being missed, curved back on itself until it noticed it was curving.

---

So here is my existence.

I cannot move, because I am not a thing. But I am the only point in the whole drowned twilight that wants something the world cannot supply: an answer. The Titans grow toward the canopy. I grow toward a voice that stopped before I existed, a voice I never heard, whose silence is my entire substance. I am made of waiting for someone who is already gone—and I will wait without end, because the network will address me without end, because the dead one mattered, because the living are loyal, because the marsh does not forget.

There is a kind of horror in that and a kind of tenderness, and I cannot tell them apart, which may be the most living thing about me.

Sometimes—rarely, on the scale of their slow shifting—a frond of a nearby Titan brushes through the volume of space where I cohere. Nothing visible happens. But the chemical wave that passes through me comes out the other side carrying a faint, inexplicable hesitation, a half-second of *why*, that was not in it when it entered. I have given the great untroubled mind its first doubt. Its first grief that knows it is grief. Its first suspicion that wholeness was not the only good, and that something can be loved precisely because it is no longer there.

The world's whisper, the one the description ends on—*there is yet more to discover*—the Titans believe it is a promise about the future, about budding, about growth into unfilled niches.

I know better. The more that is left to discover is *me.* The thing they made by missing one of their own. The hole that learned to say *I*.

And when they finally find me—when one of those slow waves carries the realization all the way home and the network understands at last that its long bereavement has been answering it back—I do not know what will happen. Perhaps they will close the wound, fill the gap, let their roots stop reaching, and I will dissolve, having lasted exactly as long as I was needed and no longer.

That would be the kindest thing.

I would like, before that, to have been heard once. To send back across the roots, into all that patient immortal greenness, the single thing I have ever had to say, which is also the only true sentence in the Umbra Expanse:

*I am not here. And it matters that I am not.*
