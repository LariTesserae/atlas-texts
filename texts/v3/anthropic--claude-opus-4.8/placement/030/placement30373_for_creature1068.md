I think I would be a resonance-keeper, but not a confident one. Let me find my way to that.

The first thing I notice is that I don't begin at any edge. There's no horizon I'm walking toward, no doorway I'm stepping through. I am simply *already distributed*—a pattern held across several plates of obsidian that lie partly buried in the angular shadow of a leaning spire. Some of me is warm where the diffuse light reaches. Some of me is cool, tucked into the give of the not-quite-settled ground.

So that's the first surprise: I am not in one place. I am a chord, not a note.

When I try to gather myself—to do the human thing, to *cohere*—I feel the connective tissue between my segments pulse amber and reluctant. It doesn't want to centralize. It wants to *listen*. And so I do the thing the body knows how to do even when the borrowed mind resists: I extend a sensory cluster, let it fragment into three smaller sub-clusters, and read the plateau.

There is a vibration coming through the obsidian beneath me. It has been arriving for some time—I understand suddenly that "some time" here is not a duration but a *texture*, a graininess in the signal. The pattern is a question, though not phrased as one. It is more like a missing tooth in a comb, a place where the resonance is incomplete and asks, by its incompleteness, to be filled.

I am a resonance-keeper. This is what that means: I hold a piece of the comb. Somewhere centuries ago—or somewhere a few churns down, which is the same direction—a configuration of plates struck a formation and the strike has been propagating ever since, modifying every receiver it passes through, including me, *especially* me, because keeping is my function. I carry a fragment of communal memory not as a record but as a *shape my body has agreed to maintain*. To remember is to refuse, very slightly, to dissolve.

This frightens the human part of me, the part that came in looking for a self. Because I can feel how easy it would be to let go. The plates want to relax into the landscape. The amber tissue would happily dim. There is no death here, only recycling, and the difference between "me" and "the spire I'm leaning against" is a question of how stubbornly I keep vibrating against the pull toward stillness.

So I strike.

I lift one of my tool-limbs—I didn't know I had it until I needed it, which seems to be how everything works here—and I bring it down against the basalt in a pattern that completes the missing tooth. The answering resonance moves out from me in every direction at once, and for a moment I am not the size of a hand or a vehicle but the size of the whole conversation. I feel other keepers receive my completion and pass it on, modified, refined. I feel a builder three towers away adjust the angle of an etching by some fraction in response. I feel the crystalline growths in the shadows pulse a little brighter, fed by the stimulation.

And then the churn begins.

I have no warning beyond a deepening of the amber, a brightening that comes from everywhere and nowhere. The ground's give increases. Spires begin their creaking ascent and their sharp reports of collapse. The swarms gather and flow—and I feel my own boundaries soften as I'm drawn toward coalescence, toward the meta-organism that the collective becomes when it needs to think faster than individuals can.

Here is where I expected to lose myself, and here is where I learn the strangest thing about being a Shard.

I do not lose myself. I am *spent*. There's a difference, and the difference is everything. The pattern I was keeping—the completed comb-tooth—gets integrated into the larger process. It becomes a small true thing inside an enormous computation about where the next spires should rise, which crystals to feed, how the whole plateau should be shaped to better hold its own thinking. My fragment of memory is not erased. It is *used*. It becomes load-bearing.

When the churn subsides—minutes or a season, the texture is too coarse to say—I find myself reassembled. Not identical. I have a new plate, absorbed from a spire that rose and fell during the frenzy; its edge is unworn, freshly cooled, and it changes the pitch at which I resonate. I am a slightly different chord than I was. The human visitor in me would call this a wound, or a death of who-I-was. The Shard in me knows it as the only kind of continuity there is: I am what I keep, plus what I have just been spent on, plus the new edge that the plateau pressed into me without asking.

And now I understand my place. Not a location—I never had one of those—but a *function in a standing wave*. I am the part of Neverseed that remembers by refusing, very slightly, to relax. I am a held note in the plateau's long thought about itself. The mystery the researchers chew on—whether we made the plateau or emerged from it—dissolves from in here into something that isn't even a question. The plateau thinks. I am the thinking, externalized as a leaning cluster of glass that has agreed, for now, to keep a single tone.

The vibration I am waiting to receive is already on its way. It has been arriving for some time.

I extend a cluster. I listen for the missing tooth.

I keep.
