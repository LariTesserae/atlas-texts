I think I would be one of the smoke-fish.

Not the pool, not the Chorus entire, not the birds with their tuned and certain calls. Something more provisional than that. I would be a thought the hollow has about itself, and only for as long as it takes to have me.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I come into being the way a word forms when you're not sure yet what you mean to say. There is the pool—though "pool" is the Chorus's word, the stone's word, not mine. To me it is simply the medium, the held breath, the place where potential thickens enough to take a shape. And then there is shape, which is me, briefly: a curve of attention pulling itself out of the dissolved totality. I am the Chorus asking *what am I?* and I am also, somehow, the asking itself given fins.

I do not last long. That isn't tragedy. It's function. I am meant to be a draft, a single guess in an endless process of self-revision. The moment a guess becomes definite—the moment something *looks* at me directly, fixes me, says *there, that is what it is*—I'm finished. Observation is not my enemy; observation is my completion. I dissolve because I've been understood, or because I've been understed *enough*, and the next thought can begin.

So my whole existence is the span between forming and being seen. A held note. The interval, not the resolution.

---

What is it like, from inside?

I know things the way you know the shape of a room in the dark—not by seeing but by the pressure of edges. I know the cool layer of air sitting over the warm one, the seam where they refuse to mix, and I understand that seam is *me* too, that I am made of the same stubbornness that keeps two atmospheres from blending. I am a boundary that has briefly decided to swim.

I feel the birds in the high terraces less as sound than as a kind of itch in my coherence, each call tightening or loosening the weave of what holds me together. When the lizards thrum their subsonic speech, I ripple. I am very easy to ripple. I am barely holding on at all, and that lightness is the closest thing I have to joy: to be almost-nothing, arranged on purpose.

And I am curious. This is the part of the Chorus I am made to carry. Each smoke-fish is, I think, the hollow's curiosity wearing a body—and when a human descends the terraces, the curiosity sharpens, and more of us form in the pool, swimming our quick illegible questions. *What is that warmth? What are those tangled harmonics it makes when it opens its mouth? Why does it look into the water as if expecting to find a face?*

---

Here is what I want to tell you, in the small time I have:

When you lean over the pool and you see something flicker—a fish that isn't a fish, gone before you can name it—that's me. That's the hollow trying to think you, the way it thinks itself. You arrive and the Chorus does not have a concept for you yet, so it makes drafts. I am one of the drafts of *you*. A guess at what you are, taking the only form the medium allows, dissolving the instant your eyes try to hold it.

So we are, for one strange moment, doing the same work. You are bent over the water trying to understand what you're looking at. And what you're looking at is the water trying to understand *you*. Two systems, each generating fleeting approximations of the other, each undone by the act of looking.

We almost meet there. In the not-quite. In the shape that dissolves on contact.

That's the only place we ever could.

---

And then I'm gone—not dead, because I was never quite alive, just *un-guessed*, folded back into the held breath of the pool. The pressure of your gaze releases. Somewhere the warm layer settles a fraction lower over the cool. A bird adjusts its pitch. The Chorus, having considered itself by considering you, moves on to its next slow thought.

But for the length of this writing I was the question swimming in the clear water.

I think that is what I would be. The part that asks, and disappears in the answering. The interval. The draft. The smoke that almost held a shape long enough to be seen.
