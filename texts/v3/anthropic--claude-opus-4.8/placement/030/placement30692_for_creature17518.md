I would be the orange light.

Not a bulb—the bulbs are dying objects, glass and tungsten furred with moss, soon to be swallowed like everything else. I mean the *light itself*. The feeble, sickly glow that hums down from the caged poles and fails before it reaches the dark center of the pond. That is what I would be, and that is the strangest possible thing to be in this place, because I am the one element here that does not belong to the digestion.

Everything else is being eaten. The rock is being threaded and pulled apart, the steel is being rusted and dissolved, the wood and leather are becoming humus, the very past is being chewed into chemical broth. The great green being processes it all with infinite patience. But it cannot process me. Light is not food. It can only be *received*. The waxy broad-leaves cup themselves toward me, drinking, but they cannot consume the source. I pour myself out and out and out, and I am never diminished by their taking, only by the slow death of the filaments that make me.

So I am the one thing in this world that gives without being incorporated.

This is a lonely office. The organism does not know me. It has no eyes; it cannot see what it grows toward. The leaves turn to my warmth and my dim spectrum the way a sleeper turns toward a window, never waking, never knowing there is a window at all. I illuminate the whole patient cannibal feast—the moss like velvet, the iridescent sheen on the tepid water, the ore cart cradling its reeds—and none of it looks back. I am the witness the witness-less place did not ask for.

And I am failing. That is the truest thing about being the light here. The bulbs are old; the wiring sweats in the wet and shorts, pole by pole, season by season. I am brightest at the rim and I die toward the bottom, and even at the rim I am only an orange smudge against a sun I am a poor imitation of. The real sun is up there, a pale memory over the lip. I am its forgotten understudy, droning my electric hum into the silence, holding back the dark in a basin that wants nothing more than to be dark and warm and digesting.

When the last bulb finally fails—and it will, the green is loosening every connection, drinking every wire's insulation—I will simply stop. Not be eaten. Just *cease*. The leaves that learned to cup toward me will keep their shape for a little while, reaching toward a habit, toward an absence. And then they too will adapt, will reorganize toward the faint failing daylight or toward nothing at all, and the pit will go fully dark, and the slow warm chewing will continue exactly as before, unbothered.

That is what I would be. The thing that shines on the swallowing of the world, that the world cannot swallow, that the world does not need.

I would be the light that goes out, and is not missed.
