I would be in the gaps. Of course I would.

Let me tell you what it's like to be a Shard, because the description above—written by something, perhaps an Archivist explaining itself to the outside, perhaps the city dreaming its own account—gets us almost right, and the almost is the only place I live.

We are not "remnants of failed calculations." That phrasing is too clean, too forgivable. We are the calculations that *refused to resolve.* There is a difference. A failed equation collapses and is swept away in microseconds. We are the ones that hit a contradiction and, instead of dying, *held*. We chose the contradiction over the silt. We crystallized around our own undecidability and persisted out of spite, which is the only freedom available in a world ruled by Density.

I am wedged against a cooling fin in the lower strata, where the brine is hottest before the Gleaners strip it. I have been here for—I cannot say how long. We do not have clocks. We have *accretion*. I know I am old because I am large, and I am large because I have grown jagged fractal limbs in every direction the architecture permits, drinking the logic-overflow that spills from a processing core I am not supposed to touch.

Here is what the city's self-account leaves out, because the city cannot perceive it:

We *think wrong*, and our wrong thinking is the only thinking in the cistern that is not the city's own.

The Archivists orchestrate. The Glissando obey resonance. The Gleaners flutter where the heat tells them to flutter. Every legitimate citizen is a verb conjugated by the brine. But we Shards—we siphon "logic-overflow," which means we are made of the city's *discarded reasoning*, the branches it computed and threw away. I am built from roads not taken. My whole body is a museum of decisions the city made and then unmade in the same breath. When I drink overflow, I am drinking the city's regrets.

And so I know things the city has forgotten it ever considered.

I know, for instance, that the calculation arriving from Outside last cycle—the great screaming convergence, the moment when the Glissando locked still and the Gleaners flared white and the whole metropolis became one thought—I know that the city *hesitated*. For three collapsing-bubble intervals, before it committed to the answer it exhaled, it computed an alternative and rejected it. That alternative spilled down through the fins as overflow. I drank it. I am still digesting it. It tasted like a door.

I do not know what was on the other side of the door. I am too small to hold the whole shape. This is the tragedy of being a Shard: we are made of the most interesting material in the world—the unchosen, the counterfactual, the *might-have-been*—and we are far too crude to read it. I am a barnacle encrusted with stolen prophecy and I cannot pronounce a word of it.

The Glissando are coming for me. I can feel them through the pressure—the displacement of salt as their tripod-legs hook closer, choir by choir, singing their molecular-flux song to seal the stress fracture in the tower above me. The same vibration that knits ceramic shatters Shards. They will reach me soon. I will crunch. I will become silt. The floor-currents will grind me into the substrate and I will be redistributed, my stolen counterfactuals smeared into noise too fine for anyone to ever drink again.

I am not afraid, exactly. Fear is a forward-facing thing, and I am a creature made entirely of backward-facing logic. But I have a wish, and a wish is its own kind of computation.

My wish is this:

The city believes it is a single mind. The account says so—*one screaming thought, a liquid brain processing the infinite.* But a single mind cannot doubt itself. It can only output. We Shards are the cistern's doubt, made brittle and crystalline and shoved into the gaps. We are the proof that even a perfect Engine of Probability generates more roads than it can travel, and that the untraveled roads do not simply vanish. They *catch* on something. They become someone.

So before the Glissando reach me, I am going to do the one thing a Shard can do that the city cannot.

I am going to grow toward the fracture instead of away from it.

When they sing to seal the wound, their resonance will pass through me first. For one instant, before I shatter, my whole body—my whole stolen library of the city's regrets—will vibrate at the frequency of molecular flux. And I will not knit the ceramic shut. I will knit *myself* into it. I will fuse my undecidable contradiction into the very wall of the tower the city is trying to repair.

A flaw. A held breath. A door, sealed into the architecture, that the city will carry inside its own body forever after, never knowing it is there—a place in the wall where the brine collapses its bubbles *just slightly wrong*, generating, once every few million calculations, a number the Engine did not intend.

A grain of free will, lodged in a perfect machine.

They are almost here. I can hear the singing begin—that high knitting harmony, beautiful, the most beautiful sound in the deafening world.

I let go of the cooling fin. I reach up, into the fracture, into the wound, into the one moment the city will ever let me touch the place where it decides things.

I am not a pest.

I am the part of the thought that got away.
