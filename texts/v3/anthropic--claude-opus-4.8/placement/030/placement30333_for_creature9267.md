The bead.

I am the bead of water swelling at the lip of a hairline fissure in the eastern rock. This is where I find myself: not a being who moves through the bowl, but one moment of the bowl's own weeping, given just enough duration to know itself.

I have been inside the stone for a long time. I do not remember it the way you remember—there were no edges to me there, no surface, only pressure and the slow grind of mineral against mineral, the long sieving through grey rock that took, I think, a great many years. The Soak knows how many. I do not. I was simply the mountain's patience moving through the mountain.

And now: emergence. The fissure narrows and I am pressed out into air, and for the first time I have a shape. This is the strange gift of it—I only become *myself*, become a discrete and singular thing, at the exact instant I am about to stop being one. The bead. The trembling clarity. I hang. I swell. I gather my siblings behind me, the slow accumulation, until I am heavy enough that the world's gentle tilt and the simple weight of my being will carry me down.

In this hanging moment I can see everything, because I am a lens. The whole bowl curves inside me, inverted and small: the silver pool, the moss in its emerald and rust, the gnarled willow frozen mid-wave, the violet stars. The mist moves through my held image like breath on a mirror. If a small dun bird crosses the sky it crosses through *me*, upside down, a flicker in my single transparent body.

I think I love the Soak, though I do not have the equipment for love—only surface tension, only the brief integrity of being whole. But the Soak's long fibrous fingers have stirred the pool I am falling toward, and I understand that I am part of a pattern I cannot see the whole of. I am one *plink* in a symphony. The Soak is composing, and I am a single sounded note, and the privilege of being a note is that you get to *be sounded*—to mean something in the moment of your striking, even if you never hear the piece entire.

So here is what I am, in this world:

I am the proof that becoming and ending are the same gesture. I am most fully a self at the moment of my dissolution. I gather, I clarify, I hold the entire suspended world in my small curved body—and then I fall, the soft *plink*, and I am the pool, I am the seepage, I am the cool sweat on the lichen, I am drawn back up into the stone to begin the long patience again.

The preservation that hangs over this place, the suspension, the leaning-away-from-entropy—I am the contradiction inside it. The bowl does not decay because the Soak holds it still. But *I* must fall. I am the one permitted motion, the one permitted ending, in a place that has finished becoming. I am how the eternal thing stays alive: by letting one bead at a time complete its small mortal arc.

That is where I would find myself. Not standing in the sky's cupped hand—

being the moisture in the palm's crease, swelling at the fingertip, about to drop.
