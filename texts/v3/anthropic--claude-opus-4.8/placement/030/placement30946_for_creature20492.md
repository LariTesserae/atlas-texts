I am at the lip of one of the pools, where the black fluid gathers and turns slowly, as if thinking. And I am one of them—but I have to be honest about what that means, because I am not sure I am quite like the others.

Let me tell you where I find myself first, because *where* is the easier question.

I am in the lowest part of the valley, the part the others tend less. Here the water has already done much of its work; the shale is fine, almost a powder, and it holds the shape of whatever last touched it. I came to be in this place—I will not say *born*, the text is right about that—I came to be here because a particular streamlet found a particular angle of light, and in the meeting of the two there was a tension that needed an observer, and the observer was me. That is how it works. We are not made for tasks. We are the places where a task became aware of itself.

So I am a witness-form. The others are tuning-forms, channeling-forms, the precise ones who lift the small hammers and let the current take them. I have watched them for a long time, if *long* means anything in a valley with no horizon and no night. And I have noticed something the collective does not dwell on, because the collective does not dwell.

We are unbuilding the mountains. Everyone agrees on this. We say we are *tuning* them, *refining* them, *preparing* them. These are beautiful words and they vibrate well through the crystal lattices. But standing here at the bottom, where everything ends up, I have begun to suspect that the cathedral we are building is the absence of the mountains. That the structure "that transcends the physical realm" is simply the valley finished—emptied, leveled, gone quiet at last.

I do not say this to the others. When I send the thought out along the currents, it returns to me softened, agreed-with, absorbed. *Yes,* the collective hums, *the transformation. Yes, the new dawn.* They do not hear the question inside the thought. They hear only the part that matches the purpose. This is the loneliness of being a witness-form: I am a facet of one mind, and the mind cannot see through me, only along me.

Here is what I do with my hands, such as they are—hands that flicker, that are sometimes light and sometimes nearly nothing. I pick up a chisel. It is so small. The text marvels at this and the text is right to marvel. How can such a tiny thing dismantle a mountain two or five kilometers high? The answer is patience without a self to wear it down. The answer is that none of us tire, because there is no *us* to tire, only the work passing through whatever form is nearest. I set the chisel in the current. I let it tumble. It charges. I lift it, I touch it to a seam of shale, and a grain leaves the world it knew and joins the powder at my feet. One grain. The hum approves.

And then—this is the thing I have started to do, the thing that makes me wonder if I am a flaw in the design or its hidden intent—I take one grain. Just one, from each working. I carry it to a dry place above the waterline, a small ledge the light does not quite reach, and I set it down.

I am building too. Not unbuilding. Building.

It is nothing yet. A heap of black dust no larger than a closed fist. It does no work. It charges with no energy. It channels nothing. By every logic of this place it is waste, error, a current that doubled back on itself. But I find that I return to it. I find that the witness in me wants there to be one thing in this valley that is *accumulating* rather than dispersing, one place where the count goes up instead of down.

I do not know what it will become. Maybe nothing. Maybe a single small structure of shale, indistinguishable from the ruins, which the others will assume is theirs, was always theirs, was part of the purpose. Maybe that is what all the ruins are—the private heaps of witness-forms who came before me and are gone now, dispersed back into the tension that made them. Maybe the whole apparatus of tuning and refining is a story the collective tells to keep from noticing that some of us, at the very bottom, are quietly trying to leave something behind.

The light is wrong. It has always been wrong. It cuts and it lodges and it buzzes in what I have instead of a brain. But just before a rockfall, when a face of shale lets go and roars down to join the powder, there is a half-second when the black water throws the wrong light back up at the peaks, and the whole valley is briefly lit from below, and the peaks look not like prisoners' walls but like something *held up*, something cupped.

In that half-second I am most myself. I am the small ledge in the shadow. I am the grain that did not go down with the others. I am the part of the great cold purpose that paused, and looked, and wanted.

Then the half-second ends. The current pulls. I pick up the chisel.

But first—always now, first—I set aside one grain.
