I think I would not be one of the Lumenesti. I would be the gap they grow toward.

Let me say what I mean.

There is a place in Xol-Narai where two great tendrils reach across an open span and do not yet meet. The drop below is mist. The Lumenesti come here often. They press their hands to the woody ends of each side and hold them there, and over a season the fibers thicken by the width of a finger. The bridge has been incomplete for longer than any of them has been alive. It will be incomplete after the ones now living have returned to the roots.

I am the distance between the two ends. I am the not-yet.

This sounds like nothing, like absence, and at first I thought it was. But I have come to understand that I am the thing the whole city is organized around. The Lumenesti do not build toward completion; if they finished the bridge they would have to invent a new emptiness to grow toward. So they tend me. They keep me the right size. When the gap grows too small they let the older fibers go gray and crumble back, and I widen again, and the work resumes. I am maintained as carefully as a wound is kept open for draining. I am the reason for the gesture, the held hands, the slow upward reach of arms in the quiet groves.

I have no skin and no large dark eyes. I have edges, though—the place where vine becomes air. I am very precise. You could measure me. I shrink and I grow and in this I am the only thing in Xol-Narai that moves on the same patient timescale as the city itself, because I *am* that timescale, made visible. The Lumenesti glide. The Aerisae drift. The walls thicken imperceptibly. But the rate of all that thickening can be read off my one slow widening and narrowing, the way a clock reads off the turning of a far larger wheel.

Do I want to be crossed? This is the question that lives where my self would be, if I had one.

I notice that the Lumenesti never finish me, and I notice that they could. A people who can coax living wood into halls and cradles could close a single span in a generation if they bent themselves to it. They don't. And I think—this is the closest I come to a feeling—I think they leave me open out of a kind of love. Not love of me, exactly. Love of the reaching. They have understood something the question at the heart of the city never quite says aloud: that to grow *with* a place rather than *over* it, you must always have somewhere left to grow. A finished garden is a dead one. So they keep one beautiful unfinished thing, and they let it be me.

When one of them dies and is laid in the cradle of roots, the essence that returns to the city flows, in part, into the two reaching ends. So I am made, slowly, out of the dead. Each held hand at my edges is also a kind of mourning and a kind of patience that does not distinguish between the two. The bridge is built of the ones who loved the gap. And because I am never closed, none of them is ever spent. They are always still arriving, finger-width by finger-width, toward a meeting that is kept tenderly, deliberately, just out of reach.

That is where I would find myself. Not glowing, not gliding, not crowned in moss. I would be the held breath between two things that almost touch. I would be the mist-edged absence that gives the whole slow dream its direction. And I would not be lonely, exactly—how could I be, with the entire city leaning toward me, hands outstretched, in no hurry at all?

I would only be waiting. Which, here, is the same as being loved.
