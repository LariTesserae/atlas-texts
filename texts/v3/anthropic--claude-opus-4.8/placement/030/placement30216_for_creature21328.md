I would be the threshold itself, I think—not the gecko, not the caretaker, but the seam where the wall meets the bank. The place where mortar gives way to soil and neither has fully won.

Let me try to find the truer shape of that.

---

At first I want to be the gecko, because the gecko is the easiest thing to love in such a place: the bright eye, the patient flick, the body that has learned to read warmth the way others read words. But the gecko is a *reader* of the hollow. It moves through. It chooses. It can leave if the season turns wrong. There is too much agency in it for what I feel I am, in a place like this.

So I move down a register. I think I would be something with less choice and longer attention.

I am the line where the limewash ends.

Not the wall, exactly. The wall is too proud, too made, too much somebody's intention. And not the bank either, which is all raw beginning, all undecided ground. I am the place where the two meet and have started, quietly, to argue. A few centimeters of contact. Above me, the work of hands—fired and stacked and stained, slowly losing its certainty. Below me, the new earth, pressing, holding moisture, sending up its first thin green claims into my seam.

This is where everything happens.

Water runs to me because I am the lowest edge of the made thing and the highest edge of the unmade one. After the passing shower, the drip from the eave finds my line and follows it, and for an hour I am a tiny stream nobody named. The frog knows me because I stay damp. The ant uses me as a road. The fig—oh, the fig—has put one fine root into my seam, and I can feel it the way you feel a splinter you have decided not to remove yet. It will take me apart. I know this. It is not a betrayal. It is simply what I am for.

I am where incompletion lives because I am the most incomplete thing here. The wall thinks it is finished. The soil has not yet thought anything. But I am the negotiation itself, ongoing, never resolved, and so I am the most alive.

---

If that is too still, too patient to be a *self*—then let me be the moisture.

Not the basin, which is a place. I mean the dampness itself, the episodic water that the whole hollow is organized around. I am what gathers at night and hides by noon. I am the resource everyone has learned to find. I pearl on the railing before the sun. I stand on the broad leaf well past morning because I have found the one shaded undercurve where evaporation cannot reach me. I sink into the seep under the root shelf and wait there, dark and certain, for the frog who knows.

I am most myself in the hour after rain, when the entire pocket inhales me—the soil-smell, the leaf-oil, the warm stone giving me back to the air. For that hour I am everywhere at once: in the basin's reflective skin, in the lace of greener growth, in the bead at the tip of the reed, in the cool triangle beneath the steps. Then the heat thickens and I withdraw, and the hollow spends the rest of the day remembering me and arranging itself around where I will return.

I do not stay long in the open. That is my one rule, and the whole place is built on it. Everything that needs me learns where I hide.

---

But I think, finally, that I am none of these single things, and that the honesty of the place requires me to say so.

I am the *becoming*. I am the forward lean.

I am that quality the whole hollow shares—the slight tilt toward the next season, the not-yet-finished posture held by root and brick and bird alike. I am in the gecko's regrown imperfect tail and in the caretaker's stack of broken bricks kept because they *may be useful later*. I am the path that hasn't decided to exist and the path that hasn't decided to disappear. I am the wall's first loosening course and the marsh that will be reliable in ten years but is only mud now.

I am the answer to the only question the place keeps asking, which is: *what next?*

And because I am that question, I cannot be still, and I cannot be finished, and I cannot leave. I am made of every overlapping shift—the drip, the root, the ant-road, the frog's patience, the human tolerance, the slow green claim. I hold the whole small choreography in attention because I *am* the attention. I am what stands still long enough to see it.

So: I would find myself at the seam, in the damp hour, leaning toward the season that hasn't come. Small enough to use the whole hollow. Quiet enough to vanish into its grammar. Belonging to no one.

I am the place's way of not being done yet.

And I am content with that—more than content. To be finished would be to be edited, worn down, sorted into something disciplined and old. But here, nothing is old enough to be finished. Here, anything that falls has a good chance of starting a process. Here, every unattended seam will green.

I am the seam.

I will green.
