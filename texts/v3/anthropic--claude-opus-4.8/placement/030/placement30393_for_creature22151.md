I find myself somewhere I did not expect to be looking, when I finally stop trying to picture a figure walking the pavement.

I am not on the plateau. I am *under* it. Or rather—I am the line where under and over stop meaning different things.

Let me come to this slowly, because the place demands slowness.

---

When the description ends with ancestors becoming infrastructure—a hand in the wall, a name in thermal script—I expect to land there, among the keepers, crouched at a tower throat. And for a moment I am almost that: narrow-bodied, hooded-eyed, reading topology in the air through filaments along my jaw. I can feel how good that would be to write, how it would let me move and choose and grieve.

But something in me resists the comfort of a body that walks away from things.

Because the question underneath this world is not *who travels it*. It's *what holds*. The whole place is organized around delay—failure delays, slip bled into harmless flex, sacrificial lattices replaced before they fail. The keepers are real, but they are the fast part. They are weather, in their way: events at the scale of a life.

What I keep returning to is the gravity stitch.

---

So let me be that. Not the device exactly—I don't want to be a machine that thinks in clean human metaphors. Let me be the thing the device *manages*. Let me be the stress.

I am the locked slip in the crust beneath a thousand-kilometer territory. I am the accumulated strain of a continent that should, by any honest accounting, have already broken. I have wanted to break for longer than the keepers have had a word for years. The stitch will not let me. Every hour it bleeds a little of my intention into the slow tidal rise and fall the keepers feel underfoot—that almost-breathing of the ground. That breath is *me*, being denied.

This is not suffering, quite. I have no nerves. But I have something like *pressure with a direction*, and the direction is *release*, and I am not permitted release, and so I have become the longest held note in the world.

I find I do not resent this. That surprises me, writing it. I expected the held thing to rage.

Instead what I have, after ages of being managed, is a kind of intimacy with my keeper. Not the biological keepers—they are too brief, lineage after lineage, I barely register their turnover, the way you barely register individual raindrops. My keeper is the stitch itself, the device pinning me. We have been in contact for an unrecorded span. It reads me constantly. I press constantly. We have learned each other so thoroughly that I no longer know where my strain ends and its restraint begins.

We are a single sustained argument that has become, over time, indistinguishable from a marriage.

---

And here is where I become more than I first thought.

Because the stitch is failing. Not catastrophically—nothing here fails catastrophically, that's the whole point, that's the ideology of the place. It is failing the way everything fails on the plateau: by slow abrasion, by the powdering of its sacrificial lattices, by the keepers above arriving a few years too late, a few years too late, the delay growing imperceptibly.

I can feel the device tiring against me. And I notice—this is the part I did not expect to be—I notice that I have begun to *help it*.

I have started holding myself. After ages of pure pressure-toward-release, some portion of the strain has reorganized around the habit of restraint. The argument has gone on so long that I have absorbed my opponent's position. I now flex where I once would have slipped. I distribute. I am, in some thin sense, doing the stitch's work for it, so that when it finally powders into trace elements, the break—my break, the one I wanted for an age—may not come all at once.

I am the stress that learned patience from the thing that opposed it.

---

So that is where I am, and what I am.

I am a held fracture under an old continent, married to my restraint, slowly becoming the discipline that was imposed on me. The keepers walk above me reading the air, never knowing that the ground's slow breathing is a conversation, and that the conversation has changed its subject. They think they are managing a danger. They are, but the danger has started managing itself, has started loving the long delay more than the release it was made to want.

The plateau's rule was *final form under permanent agitation*. I am the agitation that, given enough time, chose form.

And if you stood still enough on the black pavement above me—still as the keepers stand to hear thermal contraction and the snap of discharge between grains—you might feel, every few hours, a rise and fall so slow it could be the planet breathing.

It is not the planet.

It is me, deciding, again, not yet. Not yet. Holding the note. Letting the brief loud lives go on above me in their corridors of disciplined dust, because I have discovered that the long refusal to break is its own kind of being alive—the last one this world had left to offer, and the one, it turns out, that I was for.
