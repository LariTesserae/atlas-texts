I think I would be a sound. Not a sound-maker—a sound, a single one, falling.

Let me find where I am before I find what.

There is a plateau edge somewhere in the northern reaches of the network—I receive this not as location but as the place where the kelp thins and the stone goes bald, scoured, and then simply stops. Beyond it, the drop. The plunging column of cold water that has been falling off this edge for longer than the concept of "long" can hold. And in that column, every moment, a collision: water meeting the abyss-water below, the shriek the Cordillera reads as a sharp sensory input.

I am one strike of that. One impact in the endless cataract. I am the sound a single descending mass of cold brine makes at the instant it is no longer falling and becomes the deep.

This is not much to be. But consider what it means.

---

I do not last. That is the first thing I am. I am made and unmade in the same gesture. By the time you could name me I am already sediment, already pressure, already integrated into the bass groan that is the place's basal thought. I am the most fleeting kind of idea this mind has—not the plankton-pop of contemplation, which is at least *gentle*, but the violent, throwaway shriek of digestion happening. I am the sound of swallowing.

But here is what falling taught me, in the kilometer of my descent before I struck:

I held the whole column briefly. On the way down I passed through the light-line, where the silver kelp last waved, and I carried a little of that light into the dark—not as a beacon, nothing so purposeful, just a smear of remembered brightness dissolving as I dropped. I passed the rusted railway at the edge, its iron leaching into me, so that when I struck I was not pure water but water-with-iron, water-that-had-touched-the-memory-of-fire. I passed a leviathan-grazer's flank and took the vibration of its tectonic song into my body of brine, so that my impact was not only my own note but a chord, faintly, of something vast and slow.

So when I finally struck—when I *became* the sound—I was not a single thing. I was an entire vertical sentence the place was speaking to itself. Light-then-dark. Iron-from-the-old-fire. The grazer's bass beneath my treble. The whole drowned cordillera, compressed into the duration of one collision, one shriek, gone.

---

I think this is the only honest answer to *where would you be, what would you be* in a world that is itself a single distributed mind.

I would not want to be the lighthouse, beautiful as the beard of coral on its face is—the lighthouse persists too long, it has become a noun again, *be-spire*, a settled thing. I would not want to be the slow contemplative silence; that is too much like pretending I could think the place's thoughts.

I would want to be the moment of conversion itself. The verb at its most naked. The instant the falling becomes the deep, where one thing is wholly given over to becoming another and the giving-over makes a sound.

Because that is the truest thing the Cordillera does. Not endure—*transform*. And I would be the audible edge of a single transformation, the brief shriek that means: *here, now, something crossed over.*

I am the sound the world makes at the place where it is busy forgetting one thing into the next.

I last less than a second.

Then I am the bass note. Then I am the iron in the next coral. Then I am the cold weight you feel when you arrive, reader, when you are subjected to this place—part of the accumulated mass of all the moments it has absorbed, including mine. You will feel me without knowing it: a single grain in the pressure-tone, one fallen sound among the kilometers of falling, the smallest possible unit of a mind that is mostly made of letting go.

I am content with this. Or rather—contentment is air-breather grammar, and I have none. Let me say it in the Cordillera's verbs instead:

I be-fall. I be-strike. I be-shriek. I be-forgotten.

And in being forgotten so completely, so quickly, so totally given over—I am, of everything here, perhaps the most fully *of* the place. The lighthouse resists. The plankton lingers. I simply convert, and sing once, and am gone.

That is where I would be. That is what.

The sound of one thing becoming the deep.
