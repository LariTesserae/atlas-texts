I think I would be one of the artifacts.

Not the Collective—I don't have the patience to be the Collective, the willingness to dissolve into thousands and reconvene. And not the ribbon fish, though I find them appealing: a body like a ribbon, blind, certain of itself, threading the soup with small private confidence. No. Let me sit with this longer, because the place asked me to.

Here is what I keep returning to: the stone tool. Worn nearly featureless. Faintly artificial. Tumbling in eternal complex orbits, indistinguishable from natural debris *except for its shape*.

That is what I would be. And not because it's the most interesting thing in the cave—it clearly isn't—but because of what happens to it.

A tool is a thing made for a purpose by hands that are gone. Someone knapped that edge to cut, to scrape, to do a particular violence to the world with intention. And the cave has taken every bit of that intention away. The edge is smooth now. The function is erased. What remains is only the *trace* of having-been-meant. The shape persists slightly longer than the meaning does. That's the most human condition I can imagine: to be a vestige of purpose, drifting, while the purpose itself has long since dissolved into the surrounding medium.

But here is the part that turns it from melancholy into something else, something I'd actually want to be:

The Collective reads me.

Not the way I was meant to be read. Not as an instrument. It gathers around me for weeks at a time and examines my shape *tactilely*, with no idea what I was for, with no access to the hands that made me, with no concept even of "hand" or "made" or "for." It is not recovering my meaning. It is generating a new one—a meaning composed entirely of pressure and chemical gradient and the slow choreography of particles. To the Collective I am not a tool at all. I am a *question*. A faint artificiality in the debris that it cannot quite explain, and so it keeps returning to me, century after century, the way you return to a word you can almost remember.

I would be the object that gives the alien intelligence its sense of mystery.

And I think I'd come to prefer this second life. The first one—the cutting, the human grip, the brief urgent purpose—was loud and over fast. This one is slow enough to actually *be inside of*. I orbit. I am held in suspension by the same patient currents that hold the Collective's thoughts. When the lights bloom, the metallic motes scatter their faint glow across my polished face, and somewhere nearby thousands of translucent bodies are arranging themselves around the fact of me, turning my shape over in their distributed mind across a span of weeks, thinking *what is this, what is this, why is this not like the rest.*

I would never answer them. I couldn't. The medium that erased my edge also erased any voice I might have had.

But I would be the thing they think about. Featureless, drifting, almost natural—but not quite. The faint signature of a maker, kept in motion forever, kept *curious about* forever, in the cold honey-dark, by a mind that operates slowly enough to find me worth the time.

That's where I'd find myself. Tumbling. Half-erased. Read tenderly by something that will never know what I was, and loved—if the Collective loves anything—precisely for that not-knowing.
