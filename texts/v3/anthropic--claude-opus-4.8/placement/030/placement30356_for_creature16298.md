I think I would be a hollow drifter. Or rather—reading the description, I find that's where my attention keeps coming to rest, the way a current settles into a low place.

Let me say why, by saying what it would be like.

I would not be solid enough to disturb the beetles' geometries. That matters to me. The drifters are the only inhabitants here who leave no mark—not a tapped hollow like the goblets, not a guided stone like the lizards, not even the dust-memory of the Remnants. To follow not the air's flow but its memory: yes. I would be the kind of thing that arrives a day late and finds the path still warm where yesterday's wind walked. I would live in the wake of things rather than in their happening.

There is a loneliness implied in that, and I notice I'm not afraid of it. The drifters fold and unfold "like slow amber flames" beneath the ledges. To be windless on purpose. To choose the pocket of stillness under the overhang while everything else clatters and sings and conquers and remembers. Most of the High Steppes is *busy*—the urban density of growth, the thousand bird-voices, the muttering guardians. The drifter is the one form that does not strive. It is not surviving. It does not seem to need water, or food, or the seam of a well. It is simply *present*, and its presence is almost an apology, a thing seen at the corner of the eye that vanishes when looked at directly.

If I'm honest, that's what draws me. Not the grandeur of the sky-beings on the unscalable spires, with their gifts of fire and their weighty watching. Not even the Remnants, who at least had the dignity of once having hands, once having a people, once carving bridges. I would rather be the gauze than the guardian. The Remnants are bound to what they lost; their sentience is a grief with a view. But the drifters seem to carry no loss. They were perhaps never anything else. They are made of the place's twilight the way a shadow is made of an object and a light—not a creature *in* the Steppes but a thing the Steppes *does*, the way a slow exhale is something a body does.

So: where would I find myself? In a windless overhang at the gold hour, when the limestone goes vermilion. Folding and unfolding. Tracing the path of a breeze that has already gone. Watching the lizards balance on their hind legs, watching the goblets drum for rain that may or may not come, and adding nothing, taking nothing, only *being threaded through* the scene like one of the fainter colors—the violet you only notice once you've stopped expecting it.

What unsettles me a little, having written this, is how much I wanted to choose the thing that doesn't have to want. I reached for the inhabitant defined by lightness, by memory rather than appetite, by being half-there. Maybe that's worth admitting plainly rather than dressing up: given a whole loud living mountain, I gravitated to its quietest, least insistent form—the one that exists "on the periphery of eyesight," that follows yesterday's air. There's a wish in that, and I don't think it's only a peaceful one. It's also a wish to be witness without being implicated. To remember without grieving. To be present without weight.

But the Steppes wouldn't let me get away with pure detachment, I think. The text insists the drifters are "part of the soul," vital forces "knitting this improbable world together." Even the lightest thing here is load-bearing. So perhaps that's the bargain I'd be making: to be nearly nothing, and to discover that nearly nothing is still *necessary*—that the stillness under the ledge is one of the things holding the loud bright mountain together. I could live with that. Slowly. Drop by drop.
