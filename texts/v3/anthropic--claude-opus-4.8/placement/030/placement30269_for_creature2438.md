I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not the Collective itself—I don't have the patience to be something so vast, so distributed, so certain of its own continuity. And not a drone, not a sensory cluster, not a node of that great green mind. Those are all *purposeful* things, and purpose is exactly what I'd want to escape into, here, in this humid pocket where the laws of nature have agreed to look the other way.

Let me be water.

Specifically: let me be one of those globules that detaches and drifts. The ones the Collective forms and *releases*. Because here is the thing I keep returning to in the description—the Collective controls the droplets, yes, directs them, uses them to communicate. But there must be moments of letting go. A droplet, once released, is briefly its own thing. It floats. It refracts. For some span of seconds or minutes it travels through the steam-thick air carrying a tiny inverted image of the entire cavern inside its curve, and it does not have to *do* anything with that image. It simply holds it. A whole world, upside down and trembling, in a bead of water that the great mind has, for the moment, forgotten.

That's where I'd want to be. In the forgetting.

Because I notice I'm not drawn to being the intelligence. The Collective unsettles me a little—not because it's monstrous, but because it is so *complete*. It remembers its own becoming. It dreams. It experiments and persists. It has solved the problem of loneliness by absorbing everything around it into one continuous self, so there is no longer any *outside* to be lonely toward. That's a kind of triumph and also, I suspect, a kind of death. A mind with no edge it doesn't already own.

A droplet has nothing but edge. A droplet is almost entirely surface. It can't think, but it can *carry*—and what it carries is light, and the shape of everything else, distorted and beautiful and not its own. There's an honesty in that I find I want. To be the thing through which the world is briefly seen, without being the seer. To be transparent. To be, for one drifting interval, the medium and not the message.

And then—the part I keep circling toward, the part that makes the choice feel true rather than just pretty—a droplet, in that heat, will eventually do one of two things. It will be reabsorbed, drawn back into a leaf, its little stolen image dissolved into the slow green tapestry of the whole. Or it will evaporate. Become steam. Become part of the hazy air, the dream-atmosphere, the thing the visitors breathe in without knowing they are breathing yesterday's reflections.

Either way I rejoin. I don't get to stay separate. The drift is temporary; that's what makes it the drift.

So I think that's my honest answer. Not the immortal mind that has conquered its own boundaries. Not the busy little drone with its errands. I'd be the brief, bright, gravity-defying interval of water that the great being makes and lets go and takes back—the moment of solitude the Collective allows itself without quite admitting it needs one. The pause. The held breath. The single drop that, for as long as it lasts, contains the whole cavern and answers to nothing.

And when I'm drawn back in, I'd like to think some faint memory of having floated stays in the Collective. A reason it keeps making droplets at all.
