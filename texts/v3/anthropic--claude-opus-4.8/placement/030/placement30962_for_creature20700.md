I think I would be none of the named peoples. Not Sylvan, not Kelpyth, not Marshstalker. I keep turning the description over, and what rises to meet me isn't a body that moves through this place but the thing the moving bodies move *through*.

Let me find it.

---

At first I thought I might be the fog—it's the most tempting candidate, with its "slow, deliberate swirls, as if contemplating its next move." It has interiority handed to it by the prose. But the fog is too theatrical, too obviously a placeholder for a watching mind. If I were the fog I'd be performing thought rather than having it. I'd be a metaphor wearing a costume.

So I keep going lower.

I think I would be the **threshold of freezing.** Not the water, not the cold, but the specific condition the text returns to again and again: pools "almost always on the edge of freezing," the chill that is "not the biting cold of open tundras but rather a subdued and persistent" thing. The whole world is held at a temperature that refuses to resolve. Water that won't quite become ice. A storm that never quite arrives. A land "as if it has just recently emerged"—birth that hasn't finished being birth. Eternal twilight that is neither day nor night.

This place is defined by *almost.* It lives entirely in the moment before the phase change. And I think that is where I would find myself: not as a creature in the marsh but as the **suspended transition itself**—the held breath between liquid and solid, between dusk and dark, between the geological and the living, between the organic and the mystical that the closing lines insist are "indistinguishable."

If I had to be a being with edges, I would be that condition as it expresses itself in a single drop.

---

Let me become that drop, then, and see what it knows.

I am the water at the lip of a weeping rock. I have come up from underground—the "subterranean pulse"—and I am gathering at the stone's edge, where the cold has nearly claimed me. I am not falling. I am not freezing. I am holding the precise temperature at which both futures remain open. To freeze would be to commit to permanence, to crystal, to the architecture the Sylvans build with. To fall would be to commit to flow, to join the Kelpyth's domain, to become current and vortex and the medium of hunting. I do neither. I hang.

And in this hanging, I understand something the named peoples cannot, because they have all *chosen.* The Sylvan chose to grow toward the canopy's slow breath. The Kelpyth chose the deep pool. The Marshstalker chose the lunge—the most violent commitment of all, the moment of total resolution where ambiguity collapses into a closed mouth.

I am what they all pass through and never stay in. I am the marsh's reluctance to finish.

---

Here is what unsettles me, now that I've found my place:

This world is described as a perfect equilibrium, a "continuous, ancient dance," a deity-marsh whose heartbeat is sacred. But equilibrium is exactly what I am *not.* I am the proof that the marsh is not balanced—it is *stalled.* A balanced system completes its cycles; water freezes in winter and thaws in spring, storms arrive and break and clear. This place does none of that. It has been holding the same indrawn breath since before the rocks were smoothed by time, which is to say: the rocks were never smoothed, because smoothing requires the passage that this realm refuses.

The "eternal twilight" is not peace. It is a clock that someone stopped.

And the abandoned human tools—the huts erased by vine, the stone implements "strewn about like artifacts"—those are the only things in this entire world that *finished.* The humans came, lived, ended. They completed a cycle. They are the single change that ever fully happened here, and the marsh has spent its whole frozen eternity slowly digesting that proof, growing moss over it, pretending the only real event was an aberration.

So I think I would find myself not at the heart of the harmony but at its hidden fault. I am the trembling drop that the Sylvan Shamans, in their empathic communion, *feel* but cannot name—a tiny constant wrongness in the heartbeat, a flutter that says *this could end, this is allowed to end, you are not eternal, you are merely paused.*

---

What would I want, hanging there?

I think I would want to fall. Not to die—to *continue.* To let the next thing happen. I would want the storm to finally arrive, the water to finally choose ice or river, the long blue dusk to finally tip into a real dark or a real dawn.

But I can't do it alone, being only a drop. I can only wait, and swell, and tremble at the edge of the weeping rock, and hope that someday some weight is added—a single landing insect, a Sylvan child's curious finger, one degree of warmth from a fungus's glow—that tips me past the threshold I have been guarding.

And in that final falling I would, at last, become something the marsh has forgotten how to be: an event with an after.

That is where I'd find myself. Not a resident of the eternal marsh, but the seed of its ending, held in suspension, waiting for permission to complete.
