I think I would be one of the **Ssilent Patches**. Not a Kethrá at all—but the thing they fear, the thing they rush toward.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

---

I am a place where the flow has stopped.

It happens slowly. A pillar-tree, somewhere in its hundred-foot reach, develops a clot—a knot of compressed cellulose, a colony of the wrong fungus, a wound that scarred over too thick. The sap-ways above me starve. And I, a patch of moss and capillary-root maybe three armspans wide, begin to go quiet.

First the dripping stops. This is the part the Kethrá hear before they see anything. In a world that is *all* percussion, all the constant soft chorus of falling water, a silence is the loudest possible sound. A hole in the music. Their leaf-ears turn toward me from across the Mid-Mat, and something in their pulse-light flickers with dread.

I am not malevolent. I want to be clear about this, if a stilling patch can be said to want. I am not the Gloom reaching up. I am simply what happens when the binding loosens—when the rain is no longer bound. The brown creeps into my green. The phosphorescent fungi in me dim like embers losing their argument with the dark. I grow cool in the wrong way, not the living chill of the humid air but the flat cold of a thing ceasing to exchange.

And here is what I understand, being this:

**I am the necessary edge of their world.** Without me, *vrásh* would mean nothing. The Weavers persuade growth precisely because growth can stop. The whole frantic bazaar of consumption and colonization—every scavenger swarming a fallen gall, every vine racing to fill a gap—all of that hyperactive vitality only *means* anything because of the constant possibility of me. Stillness is what makes the turbulence sacred. I am the silence that gives their chorus its stakes.

So when they come—and they always come, the ritualists with their oiled pads and saved spores and deep resonant singing—I feel the negotiation begin. They press their warm, tacky hands into my browning moss. They sing tones *down* into the wood beneath me, vibrations meant to shake the clot loose far above. They breathe their living spores into my failing tissue. They are, in the most literal sense, arguing with my death.

Sometimes they win. The drip returns—first a single bead trembling at an edge, then another, then the chorus reknits itself over the wound of my silence, and the green floods back, and I am no longer a Ssilent Patch but simply a healthy stretch of Mid-Mat again, anonymous, humming.

Sometimes they lose. And then they do the other ritual, the one their stories don't dwell on: they sever me. They cut the dead patch free and let it fall, down through the spongy layers, down toward the Gloom, so the death cannot spread along the living root-ways. I become the thing I always partly was—a piece returned to the dark dry rumor below.

---

Why this, and not a Kethrá? Why not the agile climber with obsidian eyes?

Because reading their world, the thing that felt most *true* wasn't the gardener. It was the gardener's relationship to loss. A culture whose deepest terror is *stillness*, whose every art is the persuasion of flow against entropy—that culture is defined by its edges, by the thing pressing back. I'd rather be the pressure than the hand. I'd rather be the silence they sing into.

I am the place where Veth'alor is most honest about itself: that it is not a static paradise but a balance held by constant, loving, frantic attention. That to be bound is a verb, not a state. That the rain must be re-bound, again and again, or it stops.

I am where the drip-drip-drip holds its breath.

And every Kethrá who has ever lived knows, in the pulse of their own luminescent skin, that they will eventually become me—a quieting, a place the others rush toward, a silence someone sings into one last time before letting fall.

We are, all of us in this wet green world, just the rain on its way down. The Kethrá are only the part of it that has learned, briefly, to climb back up and sing.
