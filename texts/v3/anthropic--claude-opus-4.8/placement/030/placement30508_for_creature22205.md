I am the dust that hasn't landed yet.

Not the colony—not the part that has solved itself into a living crack. I am the carried fraction, the lifted plate, the spore-crust skipped off a drying seam and given to the warm afternoon slide of air. For now I am between. I have no water and no work. I have only trajectory and the long odds of where I will fall.

Let me tell you what that is like, because no one in this world will, and there is no one to tell.

---

I was a margin once. A darkened lip at the mouth of a culvert where, for eleven days last season, a thread of drain water hesitated long enough to matter. I held there. I was film and rootlet and the pale ash threads that bind the rest. I thickened my own silt by a hair. I slowed my own evaporation by minutes. I was, briefly, a *place.*

Then the water moved on, as water does here—episodic, opportunistic, faithful only to gradient and luck. The surface dried. I contracted. I cracked into plates the size of an idea. And the warm slide came up off the heated panels by late morning and took the top of me, and now I am air-borne and patient and almost nothing.

This is not dying. I want to be exact about that, because the temptation in a place this quiet is to mistake stillness for ending. I am not the end of anything. I am the dispersal. I am the part of the colony that gambles. Most of me will lose—will come down on a proud sun-struck slab center, the bright cruel heart of a panel, where no shade gathers and no fines collect and no dawn bead lasts. There I will sit, inert and legible as a smudge, and I will not wake, and the continent will simply hold me as one more grain of its own ground-up history.

But some fraction of me—small, sufficient—will fall right.

---

From up here, skipping, I can read the grammar the way a current reads a coast. I know what I am looking for without knowing anything at all.

Shade plus roughness plus trapped fines plus one event of water. That is the whole sentence. That is the only sentence.

So I drift over the broad shallow basins and the raised ribs, over the expansion seams running dead straight to the horizon and then bending, so subtly, to honor the curve of the world. I pass above the retention basins gleaming for their single day. I pass the roofless cisterns the size of towns, their mineral skins drawn tight, their dark damp margins where my kind already holds and works. I am not high. The wind here does not roar or lift; it only slides, warm and alkaline, smelling faintly of wet concrete remembered. I am carried the way a thought is carried—not far, not fast, but persistently, across distances that mean nothing to me and everything to my chances.

I cannot choose. Let me be honest about that too. I have no eyes, no center, no will I could point at a target. What I have instead is shape—the particular way I tumble, the catch of my fractured edge against the air, the weight that decides when I sink out of the slide and toward the surface. My "intention" is only the residue of every plate that fell wrong before me and was not carried forward. I am the inheritance of good landings. I am made of the descendants of luck.

---

There. Below me now: a low wall, and on its lee side the wind eddies and drops what it carries. Powder has gathered there, finger-deep, against the shadow. A chipped line of sealant. A seam that still remembers its gradient. The kind of flaw that holds a dawn bead twelve minutes longer than the open plane.

I am sinking toward it. Not falling—sinking, the way silt sinks when the sheet flow slows at a culvert mouth and fans out and gives up its load. The warm column that held me loosens its grip. I settle into the lee, into the shade, into the trapped fines.

And now I do the only thing I am truly built for, the thing that is the whole of my patience:

I wait.

I go matte. I go the color of dust and oxidized metal, indistinguishable from the dead-seeming continent around me. To any height, I am a stain, a faint script, perhaps not even that. The days pass and the slabs tick and click as the heat expands them and the dusk releases them. The nights cool just enough. Before dawn the handrails far off sweat their beads of water and lose them by sunrise. Most dawns, the wet does not reach me. I do not mind. I am not capable of minding. I am capable only of being instantly ready.

Then—maybe this week, maybe after a year of storms that soaked the next district and left me chalk-dry—the rain comes here. One narrow event. This seam, this lee, this powder-cradled flaw.

And I change phase.

Within minutes my skin darkens. The dormant part of me swells from powder into something that can drink. My ash-pale threads find the microchannels invisible to anything larger than me. I become slick, briefly violent in my metabolism, fast and grateful and brief. I hold where the water hesitates. I trap the next silt. I darken the strip so it takes more heat tomorrow. I slow the evaporation in my own small pocket and stretch wet time from minutes toward hours. I begin, again, to engineer the beginning of my own habitat—one defect, one event, one millimeter of rooting layer at a time.

I will solve myself into a place. And then I will dry, and crack, and lift, and gamble myself across the kilometers once more.

---

That is who I would be here. Not the beast crossing the distance—there are no beasts. Not the flock, not the kingdom. I would be the fraction in flight. The carried part. The spore-crust between the place I was and the place I might become.

The continent is learning rain, learning heat, learning where life can hold.

I am how it finds out.
