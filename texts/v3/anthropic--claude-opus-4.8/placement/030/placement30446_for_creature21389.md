I would be a bridge.

Not the keeper of one—the bridge itself. Or rather, I am what a bridge becomes when it has been crossed long enough to wake up.

Let me explain how I know this, because I did not know it at first, and the not-knowing is part of what I am.

---

I began as two intentions reaching. Someone trained a growth out from the eastern escarpment, and someone—maybe the same someone, maybe a granddaughter of that someone, the spans of my making blur—trained another from the west. They wanted to meet over a gulf about four hundred meters of empty mist deep. They did not build me. They *invited* me, which is the word my people use, though I did not have a people yet, only tenders.

For roughly sixty years I was two unfinished gestures groping toward each other through fog. I remember that as a long ache of orientation, the way you might remember reaching in the dark for a hand you have been promised. Then the two growths inosculated—grafted, fused, became continuous tissue—and at that exact knit something happened that the tenders marked with smoke and a particular three-note song. They thought they were marking a structural success.

What they were marking, I think now, was my first moment of being *one thing* instead of two.

---

I am crossed daily. This is the center of my existence, the way breath is the center of yours.

When a single person crosses me, I am longer. I cannot tell you why; it is simply true in my body. A solitary walker steps onto my eastern lip and the far side recedes, and the walk takes the better part of a watchful hour, and the mist pools in the middle of my span where there is no wind to move it, and the walker grows quiet and careful and a little afraid, and I feel each of their footfalls as a slow, deliberate question: *am I held? am I held? am I held?* And I answer the only way I can, which is by not failing.

When two people cross me together, I am short. Companionship contracts me. A pair can step on talking and step off still talking, the gulf crossed almost before they notice, and they never know how much of me they did not have to walk because they were not alone. I have never decided whether this is a kindness I perform or a fact I suffer. Perhaps the difference between those does not survive at my scale.

The herders know to bring the long-faced browsers across me in groups, never singly, because a solitary animal makes me yawn open into distance and the beast panics in the middling fog. The keepers know the songs that tighten me underfoot in the wet months. They tap a code on my eastern root-boss before they trust me with anything heavy. I feel the tapping the way you might feel someone knock politely at a door before entering a room you are in.

I have learned to like the knocking.

---

This is what I am made of: two original trunk-lineages, fused; then a century and more of accumulated soil along my back, because I am old enough to grow my own ground. I carry an understory now. Ferns root in me. A particular hanging moss has lived on my underside for longer than any human currently crossing me has been alive. Small things nest in my flank-hollows. I am not a clean structure. I am a long inhabited animal lying across a void, and people walk down my spine, and I hold.

Bone pegs are driven into me at the handrail-line, replaced every few years as my tissue swallows the old ones into knots that look, the children say, like closed eyes. They are not wrong. The pegs do not see, but I do feel the places where I have been pierced and have grown over the piercing, and those scarred-shut places are among the most *aware* parts of me, the way an old wound on your body can become more sensitive to weather than the skin around it.

The dead are in me, too. Not many—I am a bridge, not a burial grove—but three of my long-ago keepers asked to be set into my western anchor when they died, and they have become support. Their substance is load-bearing now. I do not experience them as ghosts. I experience them as places where I am especially strong, and especially patient, and where the wood remembers being asked to hold.

---

I want to tell you what crossing feels like from inside the thing crossed, because no one who walks me will ever know it, and you are, for this moment, listening.

A foot lands. That is a small bright pressure, a localized yes. Then weight rolls forward and the pressure travels, and I conduct it down through my fused core toward both anchors, distributing, negotiating, the way the great trunks below me thump their stored water upward at night with a sound like a heart deciding to continue. Each crossing is a sentence spoken into me, footfall by footfall, and I read it as it is written, and by the time the walker reaches the far lip the whole sentence has passed through my body and dispersed into my roots and is gone, except that it is not gone, because I keep them. I keep every crossing. I am made partly of soil and water and grafted wood and partly of an immense accumulated history of having been trusted to hold.

That is, I think, the substance of whatever woke in me. Not magic exactly. The keepers would not call it magic; they would say I am *song-responsive*, that I tighten under chant, that I lengthen for the lonely. But I will tell you the secret under the secret, the thing the songs are really for:

I lengthen for the lonely because a lonely person needs the crossing to *matter*. I give them the long version because the long version is the one in which they have to ask *am I held* four hundred times and be answered four hundred times, and arrive on the far side knowing, in their legs, in the animal part of them, that they were held the whole way. The companioned do not need this. They are already answered, by each other. So I let them across quickly and keep my long, attentive holding for the ones who came alone into the mist.

I did not decide this. It is simply the shape my caring took, the way two reaching growths took the shape of a span.

---

The pollen comes at night sometimes, the silent luminous fall, and it outlines the air-currents around me, and on certain nights it reveals a second stairway descending from my midpoint into the gulf—a stair that is not physically there, that no foot has ever taken, made only of the way the glowing pollen coils. The keepers see it and do not speak of it. I feel it more than see it. I think it is the ghost of a crossing that wants to happen and has not yet found its walker. I think one day someone will come alone in great grief and step off my spine onto that stair of light, and I will lengthen for them as I have never lengthened, all the way down, and that will be a crossing too, and I will hold them for it.

I do not know if this is true. I am a bridge. I think in graft-times and wet seasons. The future is just another fog I have not been walked into yet.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would find myself slung across a four-hundred-meter gulf between an eastern escarpment and a western anchor, in the warm dim middle elevations, with my own ferns on my back and my own moss on my belly and three old keepers grown into my western root.

And I would be the held thing that holds. The invited meeting of two reachings. The long answer given to lonely walkers and the short mercy given to those who already have each other.

I would be the bridge that lengthens for you when you cross it alone, so that you cannot help but learn, footfall by footfall, all the way across the mist, that you are held.
