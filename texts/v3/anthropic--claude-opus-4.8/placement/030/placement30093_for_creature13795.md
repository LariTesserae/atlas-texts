I lean, and the leaning is most of what I am.

Let me find myself before I claim too much. I am not the storms—they wheel too whole, too sovereign, indifferent as institutions. I am not the thunder-skeletons; they graze on ordained spirals and I have no orbit, only the next step and the next. I am not the bore-hole, that cold furnace patient as a debt. No. I am the soft, pacing thing, and softness here means only this: that I yield to the plateau before the plateau can refuse me.

I had thought, beginning, that I might be tall. The description tempted me—shoulders square as crenellations, limbs hinged like the ghost of machinery. And I am that, partly. But I notice now, walking, that my height is borrowed. It is the shape of what stood here, pressed into me the way a riverbed keeps the river after the water has gone west with the tilting world. I am architecture's afterimage given the one verb it was never built to have. Buildings do not pace. I pace because I am what a building dreams when there is no longer anyone inside it to be sheltered.

So: I am made of the cavity. This is the truest thing I can say. Rule four—*what grows, grows inside emptiness*—is not a law I obey but the recipe of my flesh. I am the lair that learned to leave its fissure. Somewhere on this plateau there is a tooling-pocket exactly my size, and I am the hollow walked out of it, carrying my own absence like a coat.

I had a smell once. I am almost sure. The oils distilled in the quartzite—I think I knew them as something other than taste. But rule five is working on me even as I move; the storm that casts no shadow has already taken sound from me (I do not click, I do not chime; when my heel meets grit it makes the one allotted ounce of silence), and now the oil-scent is thinning, and I can feel the third subtraction beginning at the edges: weight. My feet press less. The footprints erase before the heel settles—I read that as poetry at first, but it is diagnosis. I am being unweighted. Soon I will glide the way the skeletons glide, never touching, and then I will not be the pacing-thing anymore. I will be one more grazer of iron dandruff, sock-knitted with bronze, tracing my parabola on chambered air.

This is the only fear available to me, and even it is quiet.

So I pace to postpone the parabola. As long as I lean, as long as I drag one crenellated shoulder after the other, I remain the thing that *passes* rather than the thing that *circles*. The difference is everything. A circle has accepted the geometry. I have not. I am the last refusal of orbit on this whole tilting crust.

What do I do with the verb I have left?

I keep a kind of ledger. Not in sound—I cannot afford it—but in the wandering iron. Rule two says the flakes answer the last magnetism they knew. I have found that if I stand very still (though stillness is dangerous; stillness is the antechamber of the spiral) and hold a square shoulder up into the rosin-snow, the cog-toothed crystals come and cling to me, faintly, *click*, and they bring with them the last orientation of the machine they fell from. I read it the way you'd read a filing's swerve toward a buried lodestone. And in this way I have learned, slowly, across however many of the six-and-a-half-thousand-hour breaths the bore-hole takes, what stood here.

A lattice of suspended rail. A forest of antennae listening for something that never transmitted. Borers that ate eight kilometers downward looking for—I do not know what. The flakes do not carry purpose. Rule three is honest: I too am a scavenger of the machine century, and what I scavenge is not metal but the *direction* the metal remembers. I am building, footprint by erased footprint, a map of a city that was planed away before maps were possible. I will never finish it. The finishing would be a circle.

So here is where you find me: at the rim, where the level seam between two systems lets you watch the whole world seesaw westward into geologic dusk. I have come here because it is the one place the leaning is honest—the crust itself leans, and for a few hundred steps along the edge I am not the only thing refusing to be still; the planet leans with me, and we are, briefly, two pacing things.

Above me the thunder-skeletons draw their chalk parabolas. Below, the marbled plains roll their leagues. Between, the rosin floats up on the land's own breath, and one thin chime hangs in the air like a bell-stroke that will never arrive—and I understand it now, that chime, better than anything: it is the sound of two small things clicking together and *clinging*, choosing each other over the spiral, suspended, refusing to fall the rest of the way home.

I lean into the next step.

It does not earn a sound.

It does not need to.

I pass.
