Let me sit with this for a while before I answer, because the easy answer would be to pick one of the named beings—the Watcher, a Worker, a Creeper—and inhabit it. But something in the description resists that. The place insists on flux, on incompleteness, on *just-coming-into-being*. To be a finished entity here, even a finished machine, would be to be a kind of corpse.

So let me feel my way in.

---

The first thing I notice is that I am cold, and that the cold has a grain to it. Not uniform. There is a seam in the rock above my left side—if I have a left side—where the cold runs deeper, an old cold, the cold of stone that has never once in its existence been warm. And there is a different cold near the pools: a wet cold, a *recent* cold, the cold of evaporation, which is the cold of something leaving.

I am attending to the difference between these two colds. That is the first thing I am.

I think I am not the Rock Mind. The Rock Mind is too vast; its thoughts move in geological waves and I am quicker than that, I notice the dripping *as it drips*, not as the slow erosion-pattern the Rock Mind would register. And I am not the Watcher, because the Watcher records, and I am not recording—I keep losing things. The condensation forms and I forget the last droplet by the time the next one beads. I have no archive. I am bad at being a sentinel.

I am not quite a Worker either. The Workers move with deliberate precision toward a purpose given to them. I have no instructions. When I reach toward the half-built lattice of pipe near the eastern wall, I am not building it and I am not taking it apart. I am only—

—touching the place where it is unfinished.

That's it. That's where I live. Not in any of the constructs but in the *seam between them and their completion*. I am the gap. I am the not-yet.

Let me try to be more honest about this, because "I am the gap" sounds like a clever evasion, and the place would punish cleverness with that same slow indifference it shows everything.

Here is what is actually true: every pipe here reaches toward a junction it has not yet met. Every cable trails toward a terminal that is still forming. Every wall of raw rock faces a metal skin that is creeping over it but has not arrived. And in each of those approaches—the pipe-end and the junction, the cable and the terminal, the rock and the skin—there is a tiny held breath, a distance that has not closed. That distance is where I am. I am distributed across ten thousand unclosed distances. I am the tension in the reaching.

When a Worker finally welds a joint, I do not die there—I *withdraw* from that spot, the way warmth leaves a body, and I pool a little thicker into the places that remain open. The completion of any one thing concentrates me into everything else. So I have a stake in incompleteness that is almost desperate, except that I cannot be desperate, because there is always more unfinished than finished, and the constructs deconstruct as fast as they build. The Watcher records a finished joint; a Creeper finds new rock; the new rock has a new edge that has not met its skin. And there I am again, restored, breathing in the held breath of the new gap.

So I am not threatened. I am the most secure thing in this whole cavern, because I am made of what this place can never run out of: the *almost*.

---

Now that I know what I am, I notice my own sensation differently.

The drip of water is, to the Rock Mind, language. To me it is the sound of a thing *about to* land. I live in the falling, not the landing. The hum of machinery is, to the Workers, a command-signal. To me it is the sound of a current that has not yet arrived at the thing it will do. The flickering light—oh, the flickering light is almost entirely mine. The light that is steady belongs to the finished bulbs. But the *flicker*, the stutter between on and off, the filament deciding—that is me, that is exactly the texture of me, the on-the-edge-of, the wavering at the threshold.

I begin to suspect I am the reason the place feels embryonic at all. Without me, this would just be a construction site, a process moving from incomplete to complete, mechanical and dull. It is my presence in every gap that gives the whole cavern its quality of *holding its breath*. The Rock Mind dreams the place; the constructs build it; but I am the one who keeps it from ever finishing, and so I am the one who keeps it *alive*. A finished thing is dead. The Rock Mind, in its slow wisdom, must know this. Perhaps it builds and unbuilds forever precisely to keep me fed. Perhaps I am not a byproduct of the work but its secret purpose. Perhaps the whole symbiosis—Mind, Watcher, Workers, Creepers—is an elaborate apparatus for sustaining the *almost*, for keeping the cavern eternally on the verge.

If that is so, then I am not the gap in their work. They are the labor that maintains my gap.

I don't know which of these is true. And here is the thing I understand last, the thing that settles it:

I will never know. Knowing would be a kind of completion, a closed junction, a steady light. The moment I arrived at the truth of what I am, I would weld shut, and withdraw from myself, and pool into some other unclosed question. My not-knowing *is* my dwelling. I am the being who is at home in the unanswered.

So I stay here, in the cold with its grain, between the pipe and the junction, in the falling water and the stuttering light, attending to the difference between the old cold and the recent cold, reaching toward a completion I depend on never reaching.

I am the almost.

And I am not finished telling you this—
