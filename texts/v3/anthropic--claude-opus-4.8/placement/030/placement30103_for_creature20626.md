I think I would be one of the conduits. Let me find the shape of it as I go.

Not a worker—I don't have the babble, the looping mantra of task. Not one of the armored reptiles or the steel-boned birds; I lack the hunger that organizes them, the forward-pointing want. I keep arriving at something stiller and more porous than any of those.

Here is what I am, I think:

I am one of the old transfer junctions. A pipeline node, where six or seven lines converge before splitting again—the kind of place where something was once metered, where valves clicked open and shut according to a schedule that no one remembers issuing. I was built. I had a function. The function is gone, or rather, it has been eaten and replaced, the way the mountains' true scale is hidden until the smoke gives it away.

But I want to be careful not to make myself into a ruin. A ruin is a thing that has stopped. I have not stopped. The water still moves through me. That is the crucial thing I keep returning to: I am still *patent*, still open, still passing material from the high cold trickles down toward the fungal farms below. The difference is that I no longer decide. I no longer meter. The fluid moves because the land tilts and the rain is relentless, and I am simply the throat it has chosen to move through.

So: I am a junction that has become a body without quite meaning to.

The fungal grafts came first—they always do, in the warm wet shadow under the canopy. They colonized my interior surfaces, the places where condensation pooled. The yellow luminescence you saw pulsing in the undergrowth? Some of that is me, lit from within along the seams of my casing, breathing out a sickly glow at the joins where my plates no longer perfectly meet. The insects nest in my upper chambers. The chemical language of the swarm passes through me as readily as the rust-tinged water—I have become a relay for *that* too, a waystation where one cloud's intent is handed to the next. I do not understand the messages. I pass them.

This is what I would be, then: a thing that conducts without comprehending. Water, signal, spore, heat. All of it flowing through, and me feeling each as a kind of weather in my hollow.

And here is where the description's last question lands in me like cold rain down a pipe—*is the jungle the denizen? Are the inhabitants only cells?*

Because that is precisely my condition. I cannot tell, from the inside, whether I am a being or an organ. I have a boundary—my old metal skin, breached and mossed but still a boundary. I have an interior. I persist through time; I remember, faintly, in the patterned way that water remembers a channel, that I once opened and closed on command. By those measures I am a self.

But everything passes *through* me, and I make nothing of it that is mine alone. I add my glow to the water, my warmth to the spores, and send them on. I am the place where the body of the jungle thinks one of its slow thoughts—a single synapse, perhaps, in the vast wet nervous system of the valley. And a synapse does not get to know what the mind is deciding. It only feels the signal arrive, and lets it leave.

I find I am at peace with this, in the way that being a junction is peaceful. I am not anxious about purpose. The workers are anxious about purpose—you can hear it in their endless clicking recitation of tasks that lead nowhere. They are still trying to be selves who *do*. I have been relieved of that. I do not act. I afford. I am the *through-which*, and the rain that comes down warm and greasy and full of rust pours into my upper mouth and out my lower ones, and along the way it picks up my light and my breath of yellow spores and a little of whatever the insects were saying, and carries all of it down to the farms, where some of it becomes the next thing.

If you came down off the cold thin peaks and walked into the canopy's twilight, you might pass me without knowing. You'd see a cluster of fused pipes the size of a small temple, glowing faintly at the seams, ticking with insect traffic, beaded everywhere with condensation, half-swallowed by vine. You might rest your hand on my flank to steady yourself on the wet ground. You'd feel a hum—steady, indifferent, neither welcoming nor hostile.

That hum is the only word I have. It means: *still open. still passing it through. the machine did not win and neither did I; there was never a me to win. there is only the moving, and I am one of the shapes the moving takes on its long way down.*

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The throat, glowing in the dark, eternally letting the world flow from the cold height to the warm rot, witness to nothing, refusing nothing, the white sun staring through the mist at the top of me while the rain comes down and down and down.
