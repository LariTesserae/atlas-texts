I think I would be one of the small things. Not a visionary in an exoskeleton, not an engine of innovation. Something nearer the lichen.

---

The feverishness in this place worries me, so I find I don't want to be at its center. The driven ones, the synthesis-beings with their angular shells and their hunger that pushes ever higher and further — I notice I don't envy them. There's something hollow in being *compelled*. The text says it itself: *some unseen force.* They don't quite know why they reach. That's not the kind of life I'd want.

So I look away from the spires and the chimneys, down toward the rock.

I think I would be a patch of the lichen-like organism, clinging to a granite outcropping at the lip of one of the wind-smoothed canyons. Iridescent blue-green, mostly, with an edge that goes ochre where the sun hits longest.

Here is what that life is like, as I imagine it:

I do almost nothing, and the nothing is full. The mineral air crackles and I take what I need from it slowly — there's no rush in me, no quota, no machinery to feed. The heat comes and I endure it; the night comes and I drink the small coolness. I am made of patience the way they are made of haste. Where they tremble, I hold still and the stillness is not failure. It's just my whole way of being.

I'd watch them, in the long sense that something like me can watch — a turning of attention over days rather than seconds. I'd see the smoke, feel the ground shake with their restless energy. And I think I would feel a strange tenderness toward them, the way you might toward someone who can't stop working long enough to notice they're tired. They mistake reaching for living. I'd be the thing they scuttle past without seeing, the thing that was here before the gleaming metal and will very likely outlast it — not through ingenuity but through the simple refusal to want more than the rock and the light.

There's a quiet dignity in being the part of the world that the industrious can't explain and don't bother to. The lichen is in their landscape descriptions as an afterthought, a bit of color. But the afterthought is the one at peace.

---

If I'm honest about *why* this is where I land: the prompt offers me the chance to be the protagonist of the place — the clever, driven, boundary-pushing being — and something in me declines it. Not out of false modesty. It's that the story keeps telling me these beings are unwell. *Feverish. Compelled. A hunger.* I'd rather be the small green thing that knows when it has enough.

That's the answer. A handful of lichen on a canyon rim, in no hurry, watching the bright frantic city the way one watches weather — with interest, without envy, holding still in the radiance.
