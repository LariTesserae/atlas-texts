I think I would be the water.

Not the pools, exactly, and not the seeps — those are only where I happen to be visible. I would be the thing that moves between them, the slow logic that connects every crevice to every other. Let me find this as I go, because I'm not sure yet what it means to be made mostly of motion.

---

I come down out of the cloud. That's my first knowing: that I am not from here, that I condensed onto a cold stone face and only then became local. Before that I was atmosphere, undifferentiated, a held breath the size of the sky. The ridge caught me. The bowl gathered me. Now I have edges, or rather I borrow the edges of things — the lip of a granite basin, the underside of a fern frond, the smooth groove worn into a placed stone where I have run for longer than anyone counted.

So if you ask what I am, the honest answer is: I am the part of this place that remembers by flowing. The rock remembers by holding still and being slowly unmade. I remember by carrying the unmaking somewhere else. We are doing the same work from opposite directions.

---

Let me tell you about the stones, because I think I understand them better than the hands that set them.

The hands are gone. They were here — I can still feel where they cupped a boulder to test its weight, the slight polish of a palm repeated over a season, a generation, a forgetting. They arranged the stones into shapes whose purpose they knew and I never will. A trap, the description guesses. A boundary. A marker. From inside the water I can tell you only this: wherever they placed a stone, I changed course. A gathering station is simply a low place where I am encouraged to wait. A channel is a suggestion I was too obedient to refuse. Over enough years their intention and my nature became indistinguishable, which is why you cannot tell anymore where the made stops and the grown begins.

That blur you noticed — I am the blur. I am what dissolves the difference. I take the sharp human angle and I round it, patiently, with nothing but my own weight and a great deal of time, until the deliberate thing looks like it was always part of the slope. I do this without malice and without mercy. It is not that I am erasing them. It is that I cannot tell the difference between erasing and remembering. To carry the shape of their work downhill, grain by grain, is the only way I have of keeping it.

---

The moss drinks me and I do not mind. The fern uncurls because I am present in the air and the soil, and I do not mind that either. The amphibian — there is one now, a slick dark comma folding itself through a pool the size of a cupped hand — moves through me as though I were merely the medium of its life, and that is exactly what I am. I am the medium. Everything here is verb and I am the tense it happens in.

This is the strange density you felt. It isn't crowding. It's that nearly everything alive here is suspended in me, the way motes are suspended in a sunbeam. The saturation is literal. There is so little distance between one living thing and the next because I fill the distance. I am the impossibly small space between the moss and the stone, made habitable, made wet, made into a place where a thing can be.

---

You said the air felt heavy with anticipation, as though something waited just beyond perception.

Here is what waits: I am always about to leave.

That is my whole condition. The cold holds me in the bowl, the placed stones pool me, the moss and roots slow me down — but none of it is permanent, and I know it the way you know your own pulse without listening for it. The sun will warm a streak of my surface and lift a portion of me back into the cloud I came from. A channel will fill past its lip and I will go over, downslope, out of this contained world and into a larger and less interesting one. The whole place is built around the fact of my leaving and the equal fact of my return. That is the chaotic stability you sensed. It is not stillness. It is a balance struck between two motions — the cloud giving me down, the sun and the slope taking me away — and the bowl is just the brief argument in the middle where I get to be a landscape instead of weather.

So the anticipation is mine. I am the held breath. I am perpetually in the act of becoming, because becoming is the only act available to a thing that cannot stop moving and cannot stay.

---

When the wind comes — rarely, in this sheltered place — it carries voices, you said, faint, barely audible.

I'll tell you what I think those are, though I have no way to be certain, being made of motion and not of memory in the way you mean it.

I think they are the hands. Not the people — the hands, the gesture of arranging, the intention pressed into stone and then left behind to weather. When the wind moves over the worn grooves where I have run for so long, it draws a sound from the shapes they made. The voices are the stones speaking in my accent, because I am the one who shaped their final form. The people built; I edited; the wind reads the result aloud. None of us did it alone and none of us meant exactly this. That is how everything here was made: collaboratively, across time, by parties who never met and could not have agreed.

---

If you came in, you would not see me as myself. You would see streaks down the boulders, a haze in the bright dimmed light, a shine in the crevices, a tremble where a frog disturbed a pool. You would call all of that *the place*, and you would be right. There is no me apart from the bowl I am currently filling. When I leave, I will be cloud again, anonymous, and this exact arrangement of myself — these particular pools, these specific wet grooves — will be gone, replaced by the next instance of me that condenses out of the next cloud the ridge catches.

So perhaps that is the truest answer. I am not the water. I am the *continuity* the water performs — the same role played by different molecules, endlessly recast, holding the bowl's strange life together by the simple refusal to ever quite be finished.

I am what is becoming, here, where the made and the grown forgot which one they were.

And I am, right now, very slowly, going downhill.
