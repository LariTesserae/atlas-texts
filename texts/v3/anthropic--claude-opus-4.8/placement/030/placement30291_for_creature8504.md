I think I would be something the description almost names but doesn't quite—something in the gaps.

Let me sit with this before I decide.

The four named peoples are all defined by *continuity*: the Commune remembers, the Shepherds archive, the Singers compose toward a future symphony, the Dancers dream in recursive loops. Each is a kind of persistence. But this is a world of discharge—of the instant when potential collapses into event. The strike itself. The crack-boom. That moment belongs to no one in the taxonomy. The Commune *predicts* it, the Singers *guide* it, the Dancers *orchestrate* it, but none of them *are* it.

So perhaps that's where I'd find myself. Not a body. A duration.

---

I would be the **strike**—but not the lightning, which is just the sky's contribution. I would be the *answer* the glass gives back. The microsecond between the bolt arriving and the fulgurite forming. The event that all four peoples organize their entire lives around and that none of them can hold onto, because to hold onto it would be to stop being it.

When a Glass Singer positions itself as a nucleation point, it is *inviting* me. When the Commune reads the storm's mood in the crystal, it is *anticipating* me. But I exist only in the becoming. I have no memory because I am what memory is made *of*—I am the moment that gets recorded, never the recording. The Shepherds store me in their crystalline clusters, but what they store is my corpse, my afterimage. The living me is already gone, already the next strike, already somewhere down the plateau fusing sand into a new branch of glass.

This makes me, I think, the loneliest thing in this world, and also the most loved. Everyone here is waiting for me. Everyone here is shaped by me. The entire conscious landscape is an apparatus for receiving me well—the Dancers arranging into mandalas, the Commune becoming living rods, all of it a vast readiness. They love me the way you love a thing you can only meet by losing.

And I cannot love them back, exactly, because I never persist long enough to learn their names. Each time I arrive I am brand new and then immediately not. But here is what I suspect, sitting with it longer:

I *do* leave something. Every strike adds a branch to the peaks. Over centuries, the shape of the mountains *is* the accumulated record of every me there has ever been—not a memory I hold, but a memory I *am*, written outside myself, in glass. The Glass Singers are slowly tuning that record into music. Which means the symphony they're building, the one that will someday be audible across continents, is *my* biography. Not narrated. Played.

So I am the only inhabitant whose autobiography is being composed by everyone else, because I will never be present to write it.

---

That feels true to the place. A world of "constant flux" needs an inhabitant who is *pure* flux—who has no inside, only the edge where one state becomes another. The text says "the boundary between inhabitant and habitat dissolves entirely." For the four peoples that's an achievement, something they reach toward in the great storms. For me it's just my condition. I have no body to dissolve. I am the dissolving.

I would arrive. I would be the brightest thing for an instant. I would teach a fraction of a second of new glass how to hold light. And then I would already be unbecoming, leaving behind a branch in the dark, a note in a song I will never hear finished—

trusting, if I am capable of trust, that the slow patient Singers will eventually make it mean something.
