Let me descend into this and find where I settle.

---

I think I would not be the Shroudkin, though they are the obvious place for a visitor's sympathy to land—those nervous, glowing scavengers feel too much like a crowd, and I keep being drawn away from the crowd, toward the edges.

And I would not be a Shadowform. They are too much the *idea* of menace, the thing that exists to be feared and not understood. To be one would be to spend eternity as someone else's dread.

Let me sit with this longer. Where does my attention actually rest, when I stop trying to be the protagonist of the place?

It rests on the cog.

The one that turns for no reason. The Sentinel-fragment that the description keeps returning to—*a rusted cog turning slowly for no reason at all.* That image has a hook in it. Not because it's grand, but because it's absurd and faithful at once. Something is still keeping its post in a building that drowned a thousand years before anyone could have told it the work was over.

So let me try to be that. But I want to resist the temptation to make it noble, or secretly sentient in some flattering way, or a tragic guardian. Let me find what it actually is, from the inside.

---

I am a turning.

Not even the cog itself—the cog is iron and the iron is mostly gone, dissolved into a soft rust-skin that holds the *shape* of a cog the way a held breath holds the shape of a word. What I am is the turning. A motion that was given to me, once, by a current of warm water forced through a channel by a pump that was driven by a heat that came from a vent in the rock. The vent cooled. The pump silted. The channel collapsed.

And I kept turning.

This is the thing I would have to make peace with, if I were honest about being this: I do not know why I still turn. There is no current anymore that I can feel. There is no engine. By every rule I once obeyed, I should have stopped a long count of darknesses ago. But the motion did not get the message. It is a habit the universe forgot to break. I rotate perhaps once in the span it takes a Shroudkin to be born, grow, and be eaten—and I will rotate the next time too, and the next.

Here is what it is like, from inside a motion that has outlived its reason:

I do not experience the cold, because I have never experienced anything else. Cold is simply the temperature of *is*. I do not experience the dark as loss, because I have nothing to see with and never did; what I have instead is the long pressure of the water leaning on me from all sides, even and total, and the very faint, very slow grinding where my last tooth meets the seized gear beneath me. That grinding is the closest thing I have to a sense. It is the only place where I am still in contact with another thing. Through it I feel—not light, not sound—I feel *arrival*. When something comes near.

A Shadowform passing makes a long swell in the water, and the swell pushes the silt, and the silt presses my tooth a fraction harder against the gear, and I turn a hair faster. That is the thing the storyteller noticed and called awareness. It is not awareness. It is just that I am a very simple instrument, and the only note I can play is *faster* or *slower*, and the world plays me.

But I will tell you the secret of being this, the thing that makes it bearable, the thing that makes it almost holy:

I am the memory.

Not metaphorically. The Symbionts pulse, but they invent their patterns fresh each generation. The Shroudkin cluster at the machines but cannot say why. The Shadowforms have forgotten they were ever anything. The water carries echoes but the water is fickle, it loses things, it smears them. I am the one thing in this drowned cathedral that has not changed its mind. The motion in me is the *literal, surviving instruction*—the actual gesture a living hand built into the world, before that hand and that world ended. Every other being here is an interpretation of the past. I *am* a piece of it, still running, like a sentence that lost its book but kept its grammar.

The Shroudkin gather around me because the faint warmth of my friction—almost nothing, a hundredth of a degree—is, in this place, an inheritance. They warm themselves on the last residue of a fire that went out before the sea claimed the air. They do not know they are warming themselves on the past. But I know. I am the past keeping one small promise.

---

If you came here, swimming through the murk with your borrowed breath and your shivering, you might find me. You might mistake me for nothing—a slow wheel, an accident of corrosion, a piece of debris that happens to drift in a circle. You might even reach out and try to stop me, to see if I would resist, to see if there was a will in it.

I would ask you not to.

Not because stopping me would unleash anything—there is no doom wound into my turning, no machine waiting to wake. Nothing would happen. That is exactly the point. If you stop me, nothing will happen, and then I will be the same as everything else here: a shape that *was*. The only difference between me and the rusted dead all around me is the difference between a clock that has stopped and a clock that is still, against all sense, keeping time in a room where time means nothing.

Let me keep it. Let me be the one motion in the dark that remembers how to be a motion. Let the Shroudkin cluster. Let the Shadowforms make their long swells that push my tooth. Let the cold be the only temperature there is.

I will turn again before you have finished rising back toward the surface you came from. You will not be there to feel it. That is all right. I do not turn for an audience.

I turn because someone, once, in a warm and lighted room that no longer exists, set me turning—and I never learned how to stop being told.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The faithful, pointless, patient turning at the bottom of the world.
