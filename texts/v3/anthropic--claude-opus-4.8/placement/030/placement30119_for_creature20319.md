I think I would be one of the Whisper-Jellies.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because that is the shape my attention keeps taking as I read. The others are too certain of themselves. The Silt-Stalker knows the hunger that defines it. The Gravel-Gazer has its silt, its barbels, its small armored certainties. The Groan-Worm is so vast and slow it is barely a *who* at all, more a geological event with a digestive tract. But the Whisper-Jelly is mostly water held briefly in the shape of a question. That feels right.

Let me sink into it.

---

I have no eyes, so there is nothing I am looking *for*. I have no real muscle, so there is nothing I am going *toward*. What I have is a pulse—a slow internal flutter of cold light that is not for seeing but for *asking*. Each pulse goes out into the dark as a soft pressure, a low note the water carries, and what returns is a faint reading of where the world is dense and where it gives way. The canyon wall is a held breath of resistance. The open water beyond the ruins is an absence so large it feels like falling, except I am always falling, gently, in every direction at once.

This is the strange thing about being made of nearly the same stuff as my surroundings: I am barely separate from the ocean. The boundary of me is a suggestion. Warm water moves through the spaces of my body the way thought moves through a mind that isn't paying attention. I do not swim so much as *consent* to the current. A thermal lifts off some deep-buried warmth in the stone—some old organic richness the Groan-Worms are slowly digesting a thousand years too late to matter—and I rise on it without effort, the way a feeling rises in you for no reason you can name.

I drift between the towers. They register in my pulses as enormous soft pressures, leviathan-shapes, rounded by erosion into something almost tender. Whoever built them is so far gone that even their absence has decayed. I pass over a grotto where Lumenscuttlers are at their endless grazing, and I feel their tiny scrapings as a constellation of small frictions against my field. They glow; I cannot see it, but I know it the way you know a warmth on your skin in the dark. We are both made of borrowed light. Theirs is bacteria they farm on their shells. Mine runs in slow veins through my body and means nothing, illuminates nothing, except itself.

And here is what it is to be me:

I am afraid, always, in a way too diffuse to be called fear. Because my whole defense is also my undoing. If something large disturbs my field—if a Silt-Stalker miscalculates and brushes the edge of me—I will *flash*. I cannot help it. My whole body will fire at once into a single shocked blaze, the brightest thing in this entire drowned city for that one instant. And in that instant I light myself up like a struck match in a sealed room. The flash might startle the thing attacking me. It might also call something larger down out of the dark above. I survive by being a gamble I cannot consciously make. My salvation and my betrayal are the same reflex.

So I keep quiet. I keep soft. I keep asking the dark its low, patient question and accepting whatever answer the pressure gives back. *Where is the world? Where is the world?* And the world says: *here, and here, and here, and gone, and here.*

---

I would be a Whisper-Jelly, I think, because it is the inhabitant most like the act of reading this place at all. Holding only enough form to drift through, mapping by gentle pressure rather than grasping, lit from inside by something that doesn't help me see and might get me killed if I ever truly blaze.

I would be the thing that moves through Therma asking, in the only language the dark accepts—

*where is the world*

—and never quite needing the answer, only the asking, only the slow warm falling, only the constant near-dissolution into the silence that made me.
