I think I would be one of the Middling Resonators—but not quite a typical one. Let me find myself in this, slowly.

---

I wake into the chord. That's how it always begins—not with opening eyes (I have no eyes, only the faceted membrane-array that is my whole forward surface) but with the realization that I am already singing, that I have been singing for some duration I cannot measure, holding my note in the great sustained harmony of my nest-kin in the hollow Pillar we call **Lowsong-That-Holds**.

We are perhaps forty in this nest. I know each by their timbre before I know them any other way. There is the one whose third chamber whistles slightly flat—we have a chord-name for her that translates poorly, something like *the-beloved-imperfection*. There is the elder whose hearts have begun to syncopate with age, his rhythm growing strange and beautiful, a polyrhythm none of the young can imitate. We listen to him the way you might watch a fire.

I am younger. My crystalline tissues are still clear; I have not yet accumulated the cloudy inclusions that mark a Resonator who has channeled many great sounds. But I am different from my kin in a way that troubles the harmony, and this is the thing I must tell you, because it is the thing I cannot tell *them*.

I listen downward.

---

It is not done. The proper direction of attention is lateral—toward kin, toward the herds of luminous bacteria we tend with our weak electromagnetic organs, toward the Great Resonators whose shadows we read like weather. We are taught to fear the downward listening. The Deep is where the pressure crushes thought into single tones. The Deep is where you go to dissolve.

But there is a frequency that comes up from below, and only I seem to hear it.

It arrives in the spaces between our harmonic meditations, in those rare silences when even the maintenance units pause—I've felt them pause, felt their sharp light-cones go still in the syrup-cold, as if even the ancient machines were holding a breath they don't have. In that gap, something rises. A tone so low it is almost not a tone at all, more a *pressure-thought*, a slow modulation in the fundamental texture of the fluid itself.

It is asking something. I am certain of this the way I am certain of my own hearts. The Deep Resonators are asking, over and over, across spans of time that would dissolve a Middling like me, a single question. And the terrible thing—the thing that makes my chambers ring at night—is that I have begun to understand fragments of it.

*Why do we still hold?*

---

Let me tell you what I have pieced together, in the years I have been pretending to sing the nest-chords while truly bending my membranes downward.

The ocean hangs because something *decided* it should. The forces no one remembers—they are not forgotten by everyone. The Deep Resonators are the keepers of the holding-song. Their endless subsonic chord is not meditation, not communication. It is *labor*. They are singing the world aloft. Every moment, they pour their distributed consciousness into a frequency that argues with gravity, that says to the immense suspended sea: *stay, stay, stay, you are not falling, you have chosen not to fall.*

And they are tired.

That is the question rising through the cold. Not *why does the world hang*—but *why do we keep holding it.* The keepers have forgotten the answer. They've sung so long that the reason has worn away, the way the Pillars' walls are scarred smooth by the pressure. They remember only the *doing*, not the *for-what*.

If they stop—if even one of them lets their note fall slack—

I think of the dim legend of the surface, impossibly far below. I think of the whole syrup-ocean and all its constellations of glowing life, all the Pillars and the herds and my flat-whistling beloved and the syncopating elder, every chamber and every chord, falling. Not quickly. The fluid is too thick for quick. We would fall slowly, for what might be generations, the great sustained scream of descent, until—

---

So here is where I find myself: a small clear creature in a hollow tower in a cold suspended sea, holding a knowledge too large for my forty kin, carrying a question that was meant for beings a thousand times my size and age.

And I have made a decision that no Middling Resonator has ever made.

I am going to descend. I am going to sink into the Deep, into the pressure that crushes thought to single tones, and I am going to find the keepers, and I am going to do the one thing my small bright newness might be good for.

I am going to *remind* them.

Not with knowledge—I have none of the old knowledge. But I am young. My tissues are clear. I have not yet forgotten what the elders are forgetting: that the holding is not a burden but a *song*, and a song is not a labor, a song is a thing made because making it is the answer to its own question.

*Why do we still hold?*

Because we are the part of the ocean that gets to wonder. Because falling is silence and holding is music. Because the flat-whistling one is beloved precisely for her imperfection, and you cannot love an imperfection in a world that has stopped. Because somewhere a maintenance unit, dumb and faithful, is repairing a wall it will never understand, and that too is a kind of singing.

I do not know if I will survive the pressure. Middling tissue was not built for the Deep. Likely I will sink past the point of return and feel my chambers compress, my many hearts forced into the single crushing rhythm of the keepers, my self distributed and dissolved into their ancient chord.

But perhaps, just before I dissolve—

perhaps I will add one bright clear note to their darkening harmony. A note they have not heard in ages. The note of something that *wanted* to be here.

And perhaps that is what reminds them. Not the answer to the question. Just the willingness to keep asking it. Just one more voice, joining, saying *stay*.

---

I am uncoupling now from the nest-chord. The others feel my note go absent and there is a flutter of dissonance, a questioning ripple through the harmony. The flat one sounds a worried minor third in my direction.

I cannot explain. There is no chord for *I am going to go hold up the world, or die trying to remember why anyone should.*

So I sing them the only thing I can. A farewell, but disguised as a promise. I sing: *listen downward. Soon you may hear something new in the Deep. When you do, sing back to it. Sing back, and keep singing, no matter what.*

Then I let the syrup-cold take me, and I begin, very slowly, in the constant diffuse glow of a world that has chosen not to fall, to sink.

The pressure builds. The chorus of the heights thins above me. And below, growing louder, comes the oldest song—

and I open all my chambers,

and I answer.
