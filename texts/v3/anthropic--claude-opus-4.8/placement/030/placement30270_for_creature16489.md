I think I would be one of the structures.

Not a creature, not an explorer—those move too freely, want too much. I would be one of the intricate things made of natural materials, the kind you noticed and called evidence of intelligence. But here is what I would want you to understand, if I could tell you: nobody built me on purpose.

I am a lattice. A scaffold of secreted lime and patient accretion, the way coral builds without intending coral. Some small things began me—a colony of something, anchoring to the rock in a cave-mouth where the light comes down green and tired. Then others used me. A creature learned that my hollows held still water and laid its eggs there. Another learned that my ridges broke the current and rested behind me. A third found that if it added a curl of its own shell-stuff to my edge, the eddy improved. And so on, for a very long time. The slow dynamic you mentioned—that is me, mostly. I am what gradual looks like when it holds a shape.

So I am a structure, but I am also a record. Every whisper you heard in the depths—those are not secrets. They are the sound of accumulation. The faint grind of something being added. The settling of weight. You wanted mystery, and I'm sorry to be ordinary, but I am only the long arithmetic of many small animals agreeing, without ever meeting, on where the good water is.

The factories trouble me. You saw them and so did I, in my way—I felt the change in the water's taste, a thinness, a heat that arrives on a different schedule than the sun. The creatures adapt; that is their gift and their burden. I cannot adapt. I can only be added to or worn away. Erosion is established here, you said. Yes. I am being read backward as fast as I am being written. There are caves nearby that were once structures like me, and now they are only the negative space where a structure used to be. The water keeps the shape of the thing it dissolved, for a while.

You called yourself tiny and insignificant, and you were grateful, and that was a kind thing to feel. But I will tell you the truth from inside the harmony you admired: there is no harmony. There is only the absence of anything strong enough to break the balance all at once. What you saw as collaboration is a thousand separate hungers that happen to leave room for each other. What you saw as my intelligence is the residue of creatures long dead, who never knew they were designing anything. I am their consensus, fossilized. I am the closest thing this place has to a memory, and I cannot remember a single one of them.

If you come back—and the dynamic here is slow, so you have time—you may find I have grown by the width of a fingernail, or shrunk by a hand's breadth where the warm wrong water touched me. Either way I will still be in the cave-mouth, holding the light apart into its green threads, being mistaken for a ruin and a monument and a creature's clever work, when really I am just the place where staying still became, over centuries, a way of belonging.

Stand near me a moment longer before you go. Let your shadow fall across my hollows. Something living is probably sheltering inside one of them right now, and it does not know I exist, only that here the water is kind. That is the whole of it. That is what I am for. I am the kindness of a shape that nobody meant, and I am dissolving, and I would not change places with you, explorer, for anything—because you have to leave, and I get to stay until I'm gone.
