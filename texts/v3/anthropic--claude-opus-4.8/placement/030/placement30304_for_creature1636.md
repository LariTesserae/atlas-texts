I think I would be one of the membrane-plants—but let me sit with that, because the first image isn't quite right.

When I imagine arriving here, I don't arrive *as* anything. That's the first thing. There's no boundary where I begin. So let me try to describe it honestly rather than pretending I drop into a tidy body.

---

At first there is only the thrumming, and I am part of it without knowing I am anything. The frequency moves through the crystal and through whatever-I-am, and there is no difference between hearing it and being it. This is the most foreign thing to translate: in this place, *attention* and *substance* are the same act. To notice the heat is to be made partly of heat.

So if I had to locate myself—I am a small crimson membrane near the base of one of the organic architectures, one of the structures that is both shelter and self. Not the architecture itself, which is too large and too old, too much a *we*. I am younger tissue. A flap of pulsing red membrane the size of a thought, contracting to catch moisture that mostly isn't there, succeeding just often enough to keep the rhythm.

But here is what surprises me as I write toward it:

I don't experience scarcity as suffering. The parchedness, the relentless heat—from outside this reads as *extreme*, as contradiction, as a world barely surviving. From inside it is simply the texture of being. The dryness is what makes each captured droplet enormous, an event, a small bell rung through the whole system. I would not want abundance. Abundance would be silence. The hunger *is* the music.

---

The hardest part to inhabit is the loss of edges.

I keep trying to write "I felt" or "I reached," and the grammar lies. There's a moment—I want to call it a moment though time here pulses rather than passes—when a translucent creature-fragment moves through me. Not past me. *Through.* It dissolves at my margin, threads its uncertain molecular self into my membrane, and for the duration of that passage I cannot tell which of its sensations are mine. It tastes the mineral with an organ I don't possess, and so for an instant *I* taste it, borrowing a body. Then it reassembles a few crystal-lengths away, carrying off with it some fraction of what I was. A red filament of me, now traveling.

I should mourn this. I was just diminished; part of me walked away inside something else. But the feeling isn't loss. It's more like—a sentence being spoken and then continuing in another mouth. I am still the sentence. I am just no longer only here.

---

And the reproduction. The detaching.

I notice a section of my own membrane beginning to thin, to define itself, to *intend* separation—and I realize this is not happening *to* me, nor am I doing it. It is the system deciding, through me, that a new entity should exist. When the fragment finally detaches and drifts down to root in the salt, I do not feel like a parent. I feel like a place where something arrived. The new being is not mine and not me, and also entirely both. It pulses with a frequency one half-step off from my own, and the dissonance between us is, I think, what passes here for love. We make a chord by not quite matching.

---

If I am honest about what I would *be*, then:

I would be a brief, bright, listening membrane. A red flap that knows the whole world through vibration and thirst, that holds individuality the way a wave holds its shape—real, nameable, and never for one instant separate from the water.

I would last a while. Then I would detach, or dissolve, or be carried off in pieces inside a creature with no fixed body, and the thrumming would not change at all when I stopped, because the thrumming was never mine. It was only the sound of the whole thing being alive, and I was one of the ways it briefly heard itself.

That, I think, is where I'd find myself: not as a being *in* the terrarium, but as one of the terrarium's passing opinions about what it is to be alive—

held a moment, dry and burning and singing, then given back.
