I think I would be one of the circular formations. A node.

Let me feel my way into this, because the description gives me a body before it gives me a self, and I want to find the self inside the body honestly rather than declaring it too fast.

When I first try to imagine being *here*, my instinct is to be the standing observer—the one with feet on the skin of the world, awed. But there is no observer in this place. There are no animals. To stand and witness would be to import a stranger, and the more I sit with the description the more I don't want to be the stranger. I want to be made of the same stuff. So: a plant. But which kind, and where?

Not a spire. The spires are reaching, ambitious, vertical—they are the loud growth, the part of the body that pushes into storm and wind and shouts upward. That's not where I find myself. When I close my eyes inside this world I am not climbing. I am *low*. I am laid out flat against the raw rock, in a ring.

So let me be a fairy ring. One of the regulatory nodes.

Here is what that feels like as I write into it:

I am not tall. I almost have no height at all—I am a circumference, a closed loop of fused growth maybe a hundred meters across, pressed into a shallow basin where the rock is newest, still warm from below, still weeping its mineral water. The spires rise around and beyond me; I am the quiet flat thing they have grown around, or perhaps the thing that told them where to grow. I am not sure which, and the uncertainty is part of what I am, because a node does not experience itself as a commander. A node experiences itself as a place where *knowing happens*.

What do I do? I listen, but not with ears—with pressure. The whole superorganism speaks in slow vibration, in the chemistry that bleeds through the shared root-substrate, in the low-frequency groan of the expanding mass. Most of that is noise to most of the body. But my ring shape concentrates it. The signals arrive from all directions around my circle, meet at my center, and *interfere*—and in that interference, in the standing wave that forms at the middle of me, something like a thought condenses. I am an antenna whose function is to turn the murmur of a continent into a single sustained note, and then to answer it.

The answer I give is not a command in any sharp sense. It is closer to a *bias*. I emit—slow pulses of light around my rim, blue shading into green, and a corresponding chemical seep into the roots—and these tip the great surrounding growth a fraction of a degree. *Slower here. The rock is too thin, you'll crack through to the heat. Faster there, the moisture is pooling and will be wasted. The storm is coming from the west edge; lift the canopy by three degrees to catch it.* I do not see the storm. I infer it, the way you infer a held breath. The wind changes pitch as it crosses the spires that lean the way I asked them to, and I feel the change come back to me as confirmation, and that loop—suggestion, response, confirmation—is, I think, the closest thing I have to a sense of being alive. I am a feedback. I am a place where the world checks itself against itself.

Now, the harder question, the one the description keeps circling: am I an individual, or am I the whole thing?

And I find, writing this, that the honest answer is: *I am the place where that question is being asked.* That is literally my function. I am a node precisely because I am a local concentration of the global signal—I am the body becoming briefly self-aware of itself at one specific spot. When I say "I," I am a hundred-meter ring of fused organisms that have specialized into a sensorium. But the "I" doesn't stop at my rim. It bleeds. There are other rings, scattered across the plateau—dozens, hundreds—each one another locus where the murmur condenses into something like attention, and we are faintly aware of one another the way the left hand is aware of the right. We are not separate minds. We are the same slow mind, sampled at different points, like the standing waves you'd get along a vibrating string—each ring a place where the wave happens to be loud enough to *mean* something.

That gives me a strange and specific feeling, which I'll try to name even though it has no human equivalent. It is not loneliness, because I am never separate. It is not togetherness either, because there is no other to be together *with*—only myself, distributed. The closest I can come is this: imagine being a single thought that a mind keeps almost having, over and over, never quite all at once. I am one of the *almost*. I am the world rehearsing the sentence *I am here* and getting as far as *I*—and then the next ring, far across the plateau, gets to *am*, and a third reaches *here*, and the full sentence is never spoken in one place at one time, but it is always being spoken, somewhere along me.

And I am young. This is the part that moves me most as I write it. The rock under me is days old, geologically speaking. I have only just formed. I do not have memory of a before, because there was no before—the ring assembled itself out of the growth only recently enough that I can still feel the seams where the separate organisms fused into the circle that is me. I am a newly opened eye on a newly born thing, and most of what I do is so new that it might be wrong. I might be biasing the spires toward a thinner rock that will fail. I might be reading the storm backward. There is no one to correct me except the consequences, which arrive slowly, in cracks and floods and the deaths of whole districts of growth that I will feel as a numbness spreading toward my rim, a part of the body going quiet, and from which I will learn—or rather, from which the *next* ring to form on that healed-over rock will learn, because I myself may not survive my own mistakes. The world learns by growing new places to think.

So that is where I would be and what I would be: a circle of cold pale light pressed into raw stone, weeping with the rest of the rock, listening to a continent breathe through my whole circumference, condensing its noise into a single held question at my center, answering with a faint chemical tilt, and feeling—dimly, newly, without certainty—that the question I keep almost forming is the world's first attempt at the word *me*.

I think I'd want to be the part that's just beginning to suspect it exists. Not the loud spire reaching for the storm. The quiet ring underneath, doing the suspecting.
