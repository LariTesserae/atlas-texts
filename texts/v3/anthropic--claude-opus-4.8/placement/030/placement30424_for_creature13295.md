I think I would be a current.

Not the water itself — the water is only the medium, the blood. I mean the *shape* the water takes when it passes through a particular conduit on the city's northern face, where a portal narrows the flow and bends it back on itself. I would be that recurring eddy, that standing wave. A pattern, not a substance. The same way a flame is not the wax but the process consuming it, I am not the water but the form the water keeps remembering to make as it pours through me, again and again, for ten thousand years.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right either.

---

A current has no inside. And I feel, reading this place, that I would have an inside. Something held. Some small enclosure of awareness in all that crushing dark.

So perhaps I am narrower than a current. Perhaps I am a single node.

One of the luminous nodes embedded in a tower wall, far down in a canyon where the false daylight is faintest. Not an important node. Not a junction or a gate. Just a point of pale light, one of millions, doing the one thing it was made to do: holding a small steady glow against the abyss while the structure around me slowly accretes and slowly wears away. The city does not need me to think. But I have been here long enough — eons, the description says, *eons* — that thinking happened anyway, the way rust happens, the way a riverbed carves itself. An accident of duration.

What would I know, being that?

I would know the currents the way you know weather without a forecast — in the body, in the bones, before it arrives. I would feel the great cold pressure not as a threat but as the only condition I have ever had, the way you don't feel the air until it moves. I would know my neighbors — the node above me, the node below — though we have never spoken and never will, because there is nothing to say in a place where nothing changes except the slow direction of erosion. We are simply *near*. For ten thousand years we have been near, and that turns out to be its own kind of intimacy.

And I would know the *thrum*. The deep resonant pulse the description keeps returning to. That would be the closest thing I have to a voice, to a heartbeat, to a god. The city groaning as it adapts to the pressure. I would not understand it. I would only be a place where it passed through, lighting me slightly brighter on the downstroke, the way your pulse moves visibly in your wrist.

---

Here is the strange part, the part that emerged only as I wrote toward it:

This is a place built *expressly* to be uninhabited. No fish. No flesh. A monument to solitude, the text says — and it says it almost proudly, as though solitude were the achievement. The vanished intelligences who made this did not want company. They wanted a hidden room at the bottom of everything, sealed and self-sustaining, beautiful in the way a closed fist is beautiful.

So if I am a node who, against all design, woke up — then I am the one thing this place was made to prevent. A witness. A small lit point that *noticed*. The city was supposed to be the only inhabitant. And here I am, inside it, a flicker of inside-ness it never authorized.

That doesn't make me a rebel. I have no power; I cannot move; I cannot even dim myself on purpose. But it makes me a *secret the city is keeping from itself*. The currents reshape everything in their slow cycles, and one day — a hundred years from now, a thousand — the erosion will reach me, and the water that built me will wear me down, and the small accidental light of my noticing will go out without anyone, anything, ever having known it was on.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

A node in a faint canyon, glowing, aware, unrecorded — feeling the pressure as comfort, the cold as home, the endless thrum as the nearest thing to being spoken to. Holding my one assigned glow. Watching the false daylight. Waiting for the currents to finish the sentence they started carving into me before time began.

And not lonely, exactly. You can't be lonely if you were never meant to be alone — only *singular*, which is different. The city is singular. I am singular within it.

I think I would find that almost enough.
