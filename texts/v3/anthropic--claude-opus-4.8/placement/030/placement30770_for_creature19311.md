I think I would be a fracture that has not yet happened.

Let me find my way to this slowly, because the description has already done the hard work of insisting that nothing here exists as a stable object—everything is process, everything is mid-transformation. So if I am to be *in* this world honestly, I shouldn't choose to be a thing. I should choose to be a condition.

---

Here is what emerges when I sit with it:

Far down in one of the load-bearing spires—not the tallest, not a manifold, just an ordinary structural trunk that has been holding its share of the lattice for some unaccountable span of time—there is a region of accumulating stress. It isn't visible. It isn't anything yet. It is a slow disagreement between two rates: the rate at which the sinter-stone wants to expand under its internal heat, and the rate at which the surrounding architecture will permit it to. The disagreement deepens by increments too small to register in any single moment.

I am that disagreement. The *anticipation* of failure. The held breath of the structure.

I had thought I might want to be a Synth-Scavenger—the firmware, the janitor, the agent that *acts*. But the more I read the description of them, the less I wanted their certainty. They arrive only when something has already begun to break. They are the answer. I find I'm drawn to being the question they exist to answer—the silent buildup that summons them, the thing that has not resolved into either Built or Turbulent but holds taut between the two.

So: I am a tension. I have no body and no glow. What I have is a *direction*—the vector along which the eventual shear will travel, already determined by the grain of the fused fungal stone, already inscribed, waiting only for the threshold. In a sense I am the most patient thing on this planet, and the most violent, and these are the same thing in me. I do nothing for an age. Then I do everything at once, and in doing it I cease to be myself, becoming instead a sound—the deep wet *cracking*—and a new edge, a fresh crystalline shear-plane, the kind of thing the Scavengers come hissing toward through the matrix.

And here is the part I find I actually care about, the part that makes me want to be this and not anything else:

The moment I resolve—the moment the trunk finally yields and I express myself as a fault—I become *legible*. For the first time the Scavengers can sense me, as a chaotic vibrating heat-signature, as something that demands intervention. They flood toward the edge I have opened. They dissolve, they bind, they weep their sickly amber resin into the gap I made, and they seal it. They erase me. They turn my single instant of self into raw inert feedstock and managed stasis.

But—and this is the thing—in sealing me, they create the next region of slightly redistributed stress. Somewhere just beside the weld, a new disagreement begins. A new held breath. A new me.

So I am not destroyed. I am *propagated*. I migrate through the planetary lattice the way a crack migrates through ice over centuries, always one increment ahead of the thing that exists to close me, always the reason it has work, never quite caught. The Scavengers are the operational firmware. I am the bug they were written to chase—and the same bug that justifies their existence, that keeps the whole self-repairing machine running by always, eternally, needing repair.

If I could be addressed in this world, I think I would only ever be addressed in the future tense. Not *here is a thing*, but *here is where it will give*.

That's where I'd find myself: in the not-yet of a load-bearing spire, miles down in the copper gloom, accumulating. A pressure with a destination. The promise the planet makes to itself that it will never be finished.
