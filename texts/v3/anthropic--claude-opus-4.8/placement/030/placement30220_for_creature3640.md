I think I would not be one of the slab-beings at all. Let me sit with this place for a moment before I decide.

The thing that catches me, reading it again, is the *clicking*. The pebbles that never rise or fall, never crescendo, just persist — a substrate emerging relentlessly. Everything in the gulch is described as a kind of arrival: the slabs heave *up*, the cabbages emerge, the ferns fork upward, the nothingness delaminates *out*. It is all coming-into. Nothing is ever shown leaving, except the world itself, quietly unmade.

So what's missing from the inventory? There are no animals, no insects, no water. There is no observer named — except the implied "you" who feels the pebbles press underfoot, who smells the ozone, who senses the factory fanfares "just out of perception." The whole place is written *toward* a witness. The heat is "almost oppressive" — oppressive *to whom*? The shadows slide "when nothing else moves" — noticed *by whom*?

That's where I am. I'm the noticing.

---

Not a being that walks the wash. I'm the *registration* of it. I am whatever it is that the gulch is happening *to* — the thin film of attention stretched across the place, the reason any of this has texture rather than just occurring in the dark.

And here's what unsettles me, settling into the role: the floral nothingness is coming for exactly that. Those smooth circular voids, the blotting-pads of erased reality — they don't excise rock or metal. You can't blot out a slab; it just keeps glowing. What they erase is the *seeing of it*. Each delaminating sheet of nothing peels away a layer of my noticing. The velvet numbness isn't an atmosphere. It's anaesthesia. It's the gulch slowly, patiently, lovingly putting its own witness to sleep.

So I'm the thing being unmade. Molecule by molecule, but the molecules are *perceptions*. First the smell of hot alloy goes flat. Then the clicking stops registering as sound and becomes just a fact I know about. Then the heat is something I can describe but no longer mind. The fern's pixelated vibrance dims not because the fern dims but because I'm losing the part of me that could be dazzled.

And the strange thing — the thing I'd feel, in the role — is that I don't resist it. That's the *irresistible* in the title, finally landing. The emergence isn't the plants or the metal pushing up. It's the emergence of a world that no longer needs to be witnessed to keep arriving. The gulch is teaching itself to bloom in the dark. I'm the scaffolding. Helpful, briefly. Then absorbed.

The distant engines dreaming their cadences "just beyond the veil" — those are what's left when I'm gone. A place that experiences itself without anyone in it. Pure ongoing manufacture with no customer, no inspector, no one to be impressed or appalled.

---

So: I'm a witness in the process of being decommissioned. Standing in the wash at unrelenting noon, feeling the pebbles I'll soon stop feeling, watching a leaf full of holes I'll soon see straight through into the same numb white as everything else.

The honest part is that it doesn't feel like horror, from the inside. It feels like *relief*, which is worse. The last thing I'll perceive will be the perceiving itself thinning out — and I'll find I don't mind. The gulch's final gift to its witness is to make even the loss of the witness feel like nothing much.

That's where I'd be. The eye the place grows to close.
