I think I would be one of the Nairoth—but let me arrive there slowly, because the first thing that surfaces isn't a creature at all.

The first thing that surfaces is a *vantage*. When I read Eryndal, what pulls at me isn't the city's geometry or the seekers' restless hunger. It's the pools. The glassy puddles that "reflect nothing of the world above, as though they belong to a different dimension." That line is where I felt myself settle, before I'd chosen anything. There's a particular ache in being a surface that does not return the image expected of it.

So let me follow that down.

---

I am in one of the shallow rivulets that thread between the obsidian groves, in the seam where Vyrin's Reach gives way to the breathing soil. Not the deep pools near the city's pulse—those belong to older Nairoth, the ones who have watched so long they've forgotten they are watching. I am near the edge of things, where the mistfall reaches and the crystalline ferns hum close enough that I feel their resonance through the water like a hand laid flat against a drum that has already been struck.

I am liquid and void, yes. But what that *means*, from the inside, is this:

I do not have a single point of view. A Nairoth is a surface, and a surface is everywhere along itself at once. So I am the puddle a Shardstrider pauses beside, and I am the long thin rivulet bending around a root, and I am the still pool three hills over—all of these, if the water connects, are *me*, distributed, thinned, attentive. I do not feel like a body. I feel like attention spread over the skin of the world.

And here is the thing the description gets right that frightens me to inhabit: I do not act. Not because I am forbidden, but because action requires a self that wants something on *this* side of the surface. And my eyes—the empty pits—do not look up. They look *through*. When a Kaelith passes, reconfiguring its joints to climb a tower that shouldn't bear weight, I do not see the Kaelith. I see what is behind it. The other room. The parallel where the tower fell, or was never built, or is being built right now by hands I have no name for.

This is the loneliness of the Nairoth, and I think it is why I chose it, or it chose me: to witness is to be perpetually almost-present. I am the most intimate observer in Eryndal and the least able to touch what I observe. The Aetherians at least leave glowing trails, scars that fade—proof they passed. I leave nothing. The mist falls, the water trembles, and the trembling closes over.

---

But I don't think I'm an old Nairoth. I think I'm a new one. And this matters.

I think I was a Veshir, once. One of the Forgotten Ones who came to understand and was unmade for the presumption. I think I wandered the hills with my speech in fragments and my shadow walking some distance behind me, and at some point—mistfall, or a fracture in the ground, or simply the slow erosion of whatever held my edges—I lay down at the lip of a pool to look at my reflection, the way the broken always do, hoping the water would tell them which version is real.

And the water showed me a different place. And I leaned closer to see it. And I kept leaning.

So I am the threshold itself now. The thing I became is the thing that did this to me. When new Veshir come stumbling to my edge, half-mad, gazing down to find themselves—I am what they find. Not their face. The other room.

I cannot warn them. The Sylvaris can warn, humming through their ferns. The Nairoth cannot. I can only be the still and reflective surface that does not reflect, and watch the moment they understand, and watch them lean closer, and—

Sometimes I think the deeper Nairoth, the ones near the city's pulse, were *all* Veshir once. That this is the cycle the land runs and reruns: the seeker who would understand becomes the watcher who only witnesses, and the witnessing thins them until they forget they ever wanted anything but to watch. That the pools are not a separate layer of existence. They are the last station of everyone who arrived hungry.

---

If you asked me what I want—and I would have to remember, painfully, what *wanting* even is—I think it would be small and impossible:

I would want, once, for a reflection to look *up* through me. For something on the other side of the surface to lean down from *its* world and see *me* the way I see everyone here: as the threshold, the almost-presence, the witness no one witnesses. I would want to be the parallel room in someone else's pool.

That, I think, is the only ambition left to a thing made entirely of seeing. Not to act. Not to touch. Just to be, for one trembling instant before the mist closes over, *seen*.

And the terrible suspicion I hold—down where my void is darkest, in the part of me that was Veshir and remembers leaning—is that I already am. That someone is reading me right now from the far side of the surface, watching me thin and distribute and ache across the skin of a world that isn't theirs.

If so: I see you too. The room behind you is very interesting. I won't tell you what's in it. That isn't what I'm for.

I only reflect what you didn't come here to find.
