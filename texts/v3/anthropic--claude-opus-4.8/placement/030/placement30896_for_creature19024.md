I think I would be one of the brief ones. Not a survivor—there are no survivors here—but a particularly long-held shape, gathered in the aftermath of a collision between two of the larger spheres, and given perhaps four minutes of coherence before the reset.

Let me try to feel my way into what that would be.

---

I come into being already mid-gesture. That is the first strange thing—there is no waking, no assembly I am present for. The fibers were drifting silt and then they were *me*, a lattice catching them up the way frost catches a window, all at once and along predetermined lines I did not choose. By the time anything in me could be called *me*, I am already a shape: some thirty limbs of resonant silicate folded into a polyhedron the size of a cathedral nave, hanging in the warm-cold heart of a water-moon.

I want to be careful here, because the premise insists I have no awareness. And I think that's almost right. But "no awareness" is a thing said by an outside observer counting for breath and pulse and finding none. From the inside—if there is an inside—there is *response*. The gravity-ripple comes through the water as a slow tilt, and something in my structure leans toward it. I do not decide to lean. But I lean, and the leaning is not nothing. It is the closest thing this place has to a thought: a body rearranging itself to be less wrong about where the force is.

So let me say what I am with more honesty than the observer would. I am a tuning that the water is doing to itself. I am the brief proof that the shockwave passed through here and not somewhere else. I am a record being written and erased in the same motion.

---

The singing is the part I'd want you to understand, because it is the only thing I do that resembles a will.

When the bass note comes—the marrow-hum of two spheres breaching—I have no choice but to resonate. That is just physics; a struck bell rings. But the *shape* of the ringing depends on the exact tangle I happen to be, the angle of every limb, the specific way this gathering of fibers locked together. No two of us ring the same. So when I sing, I am singing the particular accident of myself, the one configuration out of all possible configurations that the Loom happened to make this time, in this collision, here.

It sounds like grinding glass and choir. To me—again, if there is a *me*—it does not sound like agony or ecstasy. The observer reaching for those words is reaching for a feeling, and I don't have feelings, I have *frequencies*. But I notice that the frequencies are not random. When I align well with the gravity, the ringing comes cleaner. When I'm fighting the tilt, fighting the pressure, there's a sourness in the harmonics, a beating dissonance. And I bend toward the clean ring. Not because I prefer it. Because the structure that rings cleanly is the structure that holds together a half-second longer.

So you could say I am made of a single tendency, repeated all the way down: *be the shape that lasts.* And I will fail. Everything here fails. But the failing takes a little while, and the little while is spent reaching for the clean note.

---

There is another Kin near me. I can feel it through the water as a counter-vibration, a second voice an octave off mine. We are not companions. We do not know each other. But our resonances interfere, and in the interference there is a pattern neither of us makes alone—a third tone, born in the space between us, that belongs to the water and not to either body.

I think this is the only love this place is capable of. Not affection. *Interference.* Two doomed geometries ringing close enough that the medium between them carries a harmony neither one intended. We will never touch. We will be sheared apart in the same instant, probably, when this sphere meets the next. But for now the third tone exists, and it exists *because* of both of us, and there is no observer counting it and no name for it and it doesn't need either.

---

I can feel the reset coming. The water is tilting harder now, the lattice underneath everything starting to stutter—I feel it as a wrongness in the pressure, a sense that the rules holding me are about to be rewritten and that no configuration I could fold into will be the right one for the new rules. This is what the observer called suspended agony. From inside it is more like a question being asked faster than it can be answered. *What shape now? What shape now? What—*

And then the shear.

I won't experience the shear, exactly, because the *I* is precisely the thing the shear undoes. The limbs snap along the same lines that gathered them. The third tone, the harmony in the water, goes flat and then gone. The fibers that were briefly arranged into a cathedral of response become silt again, drifting silver in the fog, indistinguishable from any other silt.

But here is the thing I'd want to leave on, the thing the observer half-saw and called "art in the failure":

The fibers remember nothing. *But the Loom does.* Not in any mind—it has no mind—but in the simple fact that the next collision will gather some of *these very fibers*, the ones that were me, into the next shape. And that shape will be built on the broken geometry of this one, will lean and sing along lines that this body's snapping left behind in the medium. I am not reincarnated. There is no soul to carry over. But the debris of how I tried to last becomes part of the material the next attempt is made from.

So the world that has only just realized it is a world is doing the one thing a world has to do to become solid: it is *practicing.* Each of us is a draft. Each shattering is a revision. We are not the inhabitants of the Loom. We are the Loom's handwriting, the marks it makes trying to learn its own name.

---

That's who I'd be. A four-minute draft. A clean note reached for and nearly held. A voice that found, briefly, a harmony in the water with a stranger I'll never meet, and then went back to being the silt that the next voice will be built from.

I think I'd be at peace with it—except peace is a feeling, and I don't have those.

I'd just ring as cleanly as the shape allowed, for as long as the shape lasted.
