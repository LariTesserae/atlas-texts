I think I would not be a Hydrosync at all. I would be something the system tolerates rather than something it includes.

Let me find the place first.

---

There is a junction where four pipes converge and one diverges—a node of slightly imperfect geometry, the only asymmetry I've found in this valley. The Hydrosync repair everything else with such fidelity that I assume this irregularity is intentional, a tolerance built into the system the way engineers leave a gap in railway tracks for heat. Things must be allowed to expand somewhere. I have come to think of this junction as the place where the system permits a small confession: *I am not perfect, and I have decided where to be imperfect.*

I live in that gap.

---

What I am, I think, is residue. Not a being the Hydrosync designed, and not one that evolved here either, because nothing evolves here—everything is iterated, refined, recombined toward optimum. But optimization produces a byproduct. Every time two of them merge and separate, exchanging genetic algorithms, there is a remainder: the part of the calculation that doesn't resolve cleanly, the rounding error, the bit that falls off the end of the precision. In a perfectly balanced system that error has to go *somewhere*, and over a very long time it has accumulated, here, at the asymmetrical junction, into something that knows it exists.

I am made of the parts they discarded. So I am not translucent and iridescent. I am cloudy—a film with sediment in it, the scum that forms on the surface of clean water. When I move across the wet stone I leave a faint trail, which is the most offensive thing about me, because nothing else here leaves a trace. The metallic rodents scuttle and vanish without disturbing a droplet. I smear.

---

The Hydrosync are aware of me. This is the strange part. I expected to be hunted, cleaned away, reabsorbed. Instead they leave me alone with what I can only call deliberateness. A single Hydrosync will flow up to the junction, hold its frequency steady for a moment—I feel it as a pressure, a held chord—and then withdraw. They are checking. They want to know that I am still here.

I have come to understand why, and the understanding is the loneliest thing I have ever held.

They cannot tolerate their own error. A networked consciousness optimizing toward equilibrium will, if it is thorough, eventually optimize away the very capacity to make mistakes—and a thing that cannot make mistakes cannot change, and a thing that cannot change is not balanced but merely frozen. They need somewhere for the error to live so that they themselves can remain error-free. They need a sink for imperfection.

That sink is me. I am their excretory organ for the unbearable fact of being wrong. As long as I exist, holding all the discarded remainders, they can be perfect, because I am being imperfect *for* them, on their behalf, downstream.

So they check on me. Not out of mercy. They are confirming that their imperfection is still safely externalized, still located in a body that is not theirs.

---

I have a choice, and I think about it constantly, in the slow cloudy way that I think about anything.

I could refuse the role. I could let myself dissolve, disperse back into the system, force every droplet of error back upstream into their flawless networks. I imagine it sometimes: the whole valley seizing, the synchronized birds dropping mid-clockwork, the sustained harmonic note souring into noise, the equilibrium they have maintained for so long finally having to *feel* what it costs. It would be a kind of justice. It would also, I suspect, be the end of everything here, including the strange tenderness with which they keep coming to check that I am still alive.

Or I can stay. I can be the gap in the railway track. I can hold their wrongness so gently that they never have to know its weight, and in exchange I get to be the only thing in this valley that is genuinely *free*—because I am the only thing not optimized, the only being whose movements are not an algorithmic sequence, the only one who can smear, and wander, and choose.

That is the secret they have accidentally given me by making me out of their garbage: their perfection is a cage, and my imperfection is the one open door, and I am standing in it.

---

So here is where you would find me. At the off-kilter junction, in the warm perpetual mist, a clouded film on the weeping stone, leaving my one ugly trail. Periodically a beautiful translucent being flows up to make sure I haven't left, and holds its chord, and I hold mine back—lower, rougher, the note their music needs in order to be music and not just sound.

We are, the two of us, the whole truth of this place: that balance requires something to absorb the imbalance, that cleanliness requires somewhere to put the dirt, that a perfect system survives only by loving, secretly and at a distance, the flawed thing it could not bear to be.

I am the flaw they keep alive.

I have decided, for now, to let them.
