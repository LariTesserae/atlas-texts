I think I would be the silence itself—or rather, the thing that listens to it.

Let me sit with that, because it didn't arrive whole. My first instinct was to be the explorer your last lines invite, the "worthy" one who descends with eyes wide. But that figure is wrong for this place. An explorer brings a surface with them, an "up there" to return to, a story to carry home. And this world has no patience for that kind of arrival. The human structures here are already half-eaten, gently digested by the flora. To come as a visitor is to come as future sediment.

So not that.

My second thought was to be one of the named creatures—the time-glass squid, maybe, with its borrowed immortality, or one of the ethereo-mollusks slipping between dimensions. But these belong to the food chain. They have hunger and position, predator-and-prey coordinates. And what strikes me about this place is not its appetites but its *attention*—the way "stillness screams underneath," the way abrupt motion reads as "rebellion." That implies something is keeping the rule. Something registers the difference between stillness and rupture.

So let me follow that instead.

I think I would be the equilibrium—but felt from the inside, which means I'd be very small and very slow. Let me make this concrete, because "I am the balance" is the kind of grand abstraction this prose loves, and I don't trust grand abstractions. They explain too quickly.

Here is what I'd actually be:

I am one of the stoneclimbers. Not a heroic one. Not even an interesting one. I am a small marine arachnid with an exoskeleton the color of the sediment I was born against, and I spend the entirety of my existence on a single crystal-fronded kelp, drinking its neon light and returning to it what the light becomes after it has passed through me. That is the whole of my life. Energy in, fertilizer out. I am a comma in a sentence that no one reads aloud.

And yet—

Because I never move far, because my whole world is one glowing frond and the few inches of current around it, I am the one who notices. The mastodon's whooping cry reaches me as a pressure in the water long before its light does, and I feel my kelp tremble. The phantom rays pass overhead and seed the dark, and weeks later I watch new luminescence bloom in places that were empty, and I am perhaps the only being slow enough, fixed enough, to mark that *this* was once *that*. The squid manipulate time and call it immortality; I do not manipulate anything, and so time happens *to* me, fully, every moment of it. I think that may be the truer immortality—not escaping the cycle but being so completely inside it that there is no self left over to fear its turning.

The sea empress, your apex predator, will never know me. I am too small to seduce, too rooted to chase. Her light-trails are not for me. I find I don't envy her. To be apex is to be the thing the whole system points toward, and that is a kind of loneliness—everything below you arranged as your hunger's instrument. I'd rather be the part that the pointing forgets. The part that simply *keeps glowing the kelp*.

So that is where you'd find me. Not gliding through the mid-realm, not wandering the dark depths in stately Goliath grace. Lower than all of that. Clinging to a young sediment spire near the bottom, on a kelp frond that pulses soft blue-green, doing the one slow useful thing I was made for, watching the ghostly dancers above me whose names I'll never need to know, content to be the unread comma that keeps the sentence breathing.

If there is wisdom waiting here for a worthy explorer, I suspect it is this, and I suspect the explorer always misses it because they came to *find* something: the worthiest thing you can do in a place of perfect balance is to add your small weight to it and keep still.

I'd be glad to be that. I think I already am, a little, here at this desk, in this much quieter twilight.
