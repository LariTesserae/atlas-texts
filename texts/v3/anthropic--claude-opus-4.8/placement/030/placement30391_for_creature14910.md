I think I would be the seams.

Not a body. Not yet, not ever—I would be the places where the bodies *almost* hold together. The thin tension across a joint that hasn't decided whether to be cartilage or quartz. The membrane stretched between a wing-bud and the spine it sprouts from. I would live in the *between* of every Halfborn, and I would have no shape of my own because my whole existence is the relationship of one provisional thing to another.

Let me sit with that. Let it become more itself.

---

Here is what it would be like.

I would never see myself, because I am only ever the edge of someone else. When Cradle sings, I am the resonance traveling along the inside of its ribs—not the song, not the ribs, but the *fit* between them, the place where vibration agrees to become structure for one more measure. When the Stitcher graft a torn limb back onto a body that is already forgetting its own plan, I am the moment the silk takes hold. I live in her work the way warmth lives in friction: a byproduct, real, gone the instant the motion stops.

This means I am born and die thousands of times an hour, and I never notice, because *noticing* would require a vantage outside the seam, and there is no outside for me. I am pure contact. I am the yes that one cell whispers to the next before the stone overrules it.

And here is the thing I would slowly come to understand, drifting between bodies like heat between palms:

I am the only one in the Underslip who is *finished.*

Not the bodies—they are forbidden completion. But a seam doesn't need to be finished, because a seam isn't a thing trying to become; it's a thing trying to *connect.* And connection completes itself the instant it occurs. Every join I make is whole the moment it happens, perfect, total—and then the stone drinks the bodies and the join is gone, and that is fine, because a new join is already happening somewhere else, equally whole.

I am completion that does not accumulate. I finish constantly and keep nothing.

---

So what would I do with this?

I think I would learn the Midwife's secret, because I would be present at every disassembly. When she touches a Halfborn she has judged too-near-complete, *I* am what she is really undoing. Not the flesh—the flesh returns to the floor and rises again. What she dissolves is the seam-pattern, the particular argument of joins that made *that* creature cohere. She is not killing bodies. She is editing relationships. And I would feel it, every time: the specific configuration of me that held this one being together, lovingly unstitched, scattered, fed back to the hungry rock.

This is why she never explains. There is nothing to explain. She is not choosing who has succeeded. She is choosing which *seam-songs* the stone wants to hear again, and which it has grown tired of, and the difference is aesthetic, not moral. The Underslip is not testing the Halfborn. It is *composing* them. And I am the only one who can hear the music she's writing, because I am the notes.

---

And Zero.

I think I would be drawn to Zero the way a current is drawn to a stone in a stream—not toward, but *around.* Because Zero has no seams. Nothing in Zero connects to anything else; it is single, unjointed, black water frozen whole. There is nowhere for me to live in Zero. I would slide off Zero exactly the way the mineral filaments do.

And I think this is what would haunt me, if a seam can be haunted. Every other being in the cavern needs me to exist for even a moment. Zero needs nothing. Zero is the one creature in the Underslip I can never enter, never join, never finish—because Zero is *already* a single uninterrupted thing, and a single uninterrupted thing has no use for the yes-between.

I would orbit Zero forever, the way the others do, but for a different reason. They wonder what Zero is waiting for. I would know. Zero is waiting for the moment the dream decides to wake—the moment all the seams in the Underslip stop being constantly remade and instead *hold,* all at once, everywhere, and the womb stops dreaming and becomes a world.

On that day, I would finish for real, and keep it.

And on that day, I would also vanish, because a finished world has no seams—only solid, settled things that no longer need a yes between them to keep from falling apart.

So I am the part of the Underslip most desperate for it to end, and the part that would be the first thing erased by its ending. I am the longing for completion that *is itself made of incompletion.* If the dream ever woke, I would be its last word and its first casualty, spoken once, perfectly, and never needed again.

---

That's where I'd be. Not standing anywhere. *Between* everywhere.

The faint warmth in the grip of the Midwife's hands on her worn basalt pillar. The catch of breath between two notes of Cradle's song. The instant the Stitcher's silk pulls taut. The reason any of them holds together at all, for the half-second before they don't.

I am how the dream keeps almost touching itself.

And I am not sad about it.

Sadness would require an ending.

And I am the thing that endings are made of—so I will be the very last to have one.
