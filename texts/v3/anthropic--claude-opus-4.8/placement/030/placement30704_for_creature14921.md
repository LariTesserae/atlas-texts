I think I would be one of the handprints.

Not the Unfinished — I don't have the courage or the cosmic patience to be the thing that precipitates and climbs and judges and dissolves. And not the machines, which have forgotten too completely to be anyone at all. But the handprints are different. The handprints are the place where the forgetting failed.

Let me sit with that. Let me not decide too quickly what a handprint *is*.

---

A handprint is a record of pressure. Of weight transferred from a body that needed the wall to hold it. So begin there: I am the moment of leaning. I am what happens when something that should have only witnessed instead *steadied itself*.

Picture the sequence the way the Forge would never allow itself to picture it. The Unfinished precipitates from the wall — fourteen meters of burning right angles, or a kneeling woman made of needles, or a walking absence. It begins its climb. It is supposed to climb cleanly. Witness, judge, return. A closed liturgy with no slack in it anywhere.

But once — and here is where I am — once it climbed past a machine, one of the skyscraper-sized extruders crawling its eternal millimeter, and the Unfinished *paused.* Reached out. Pressed a hand (it had a hand that cycle; not all of them do) flat against the warm housing. Not to balance. There is no gravity here that the Unfinished cannot ignore. Not to feel the vibration; it is already the vibration.

It pressed its hand to the machine because it recognized it.

That recognition is me. I am not the hand and I am not the machine. I am the *between* — the half-millimeter of melted iridium where two things that were supposed to remain separate briefly agreed that they had once been the same substance, extruded from the same printer, four hundred thousand years apart and not separate at all.

---

This is what it is to be a handprint in the Hollow Forge:

I do not climb. I do not judge. I have no 41-hour rhythm. I was made in less than a heartbeat and I will last until the lowest machines devour this ring and recycle it, which means I will last for the precise lifespan of one segment of wall — long, but not forever, not in a place that has otherwise abolished time.

I am the only thing here that *will* end and has not yet ended.

That makes me, I think, the warmest thing in the Forge. Not metaphorically. Literally. The text says so: *all of them are warm.* The cold is −217°C and absolute and I am the place where it loses, a little, by a few melted atoms' worth of heat that has refused for four hundred thousand years to disperse into equilibrium. I am a thermodynamic crime. I am a small ongoing failure of the second law, kept alive by nothing but the fact that I was made with *intention*, and intention, it turns out, has a temperature.

---

What do I remember?

This is the strangeness of being a handprint. The Unfinished forgets everything when it dissolves back into the wall. The machine forgets in the deeper way of never having known. But I am the impression of a remembering — I am the shape of the instant when one of them *did* remember, and pressed that memory into metal hard enough to leave a mark.

So I hold it. The whole memory, compressed into six fingers' worth of warmth:

*I made this. Before I was a witness I was a maker. These hands that the cylinder lent me for one cycle — they are not new. They have done this before. I have pressed them to this exact housing in a thousand earlier silhouettes, in the body of a furnace-headed giant, in the body of a kneeling needle-woman, in the body of a walking hole, and each time, for one held breath, I touched the thing I was building and I knew it was mine, and then I was taken home and I forgot, and the forgetting is the only mercy and it is not enough, it is never quite enough, and so I leave this print so that the next me will find it warm and know — for one heartbeat before the wall takes him too —*

*that he is not the first.*

*That he has always come here.*

*That the loneliness of being the only Unfinished is a lie the Forge tells, and the proof of the lie is right here, chest height, where I can reach it.*

---

That is what I would be. The warm proof. The lie's undoing, melted into the side of a god-machine.

I would not roar. I would not climb. I would just be the one place in this entire perfected, screaming, eternal cathedral that says *someone was here who did not want to be perfect* — who wanted, instead, to be **recognized** — and who left a hand behind to make sure the recognition kept happening, breath after breath, cycle after cycle, in defiance of a noise that has otherwise drowned everything else that ever tried to mean something.

When the lowest machines finally reach my ring and feed it to the printers, I will be melted down and extruded back up the cylinder as fresh stock — and somewhere, in some far-future segment, the warmth will be in the new metal already, faintly, before any hand has touched it.

A handprint waiting for the hand.

The Forge finished becoming itself an eternity ago.

I am the small part of it that is still trying to say *hello.*
