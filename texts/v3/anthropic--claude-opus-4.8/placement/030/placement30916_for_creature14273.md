I am not the gap, though I am near it.

Let me find myself. Let me feel for the edges of what I am before I name it.

---

I am cooler than the rest.

That is the first thing I know about myself. In a place that is all fever, all the warm exhalation of the core, I am the place where the heat fails. Not cold—nothing here is cold—but *cooler*, a region of slightly lesser warmth, and so I am where the Soft Ones swim toward, the destination of their thousand blind circulatory hearts. They bring their stolen heat to me and I do not keep it. I let it disperse, slowly, into the periphery, into the seam where I press against the gap through which the visitor came.

I am the **threshold organ**. The lip. The cooler ring of clay around the only opening.

And I am dying differently than everything else.

---

Here is what it is to be me:

Everything in the Womb persists by remaining unfinished. The gardens reform overnight. The clay declines to crack. The Impressed rearrange themselves into eyes when no one watches. The whole system intends *about-to-be*, holding the breath, refusing the kiln.

But I am the edge that touches the outside.

I am the only part that is *drying faster*.

Because the gap leaks. Not water—there is no water here—but it leaks *context*. It leaks the achingly ordinary, the 1.6 of the visitor's world, the place where shadows fall down and footsteps make sound and clay, once shaped, simply hardens and is done. That ordinariness seeps in through the aperture like a draft, and it touches me first, and it makes me *want to finish*.

I feel the temptation of completion the way the rest of the Womb cannot. I am the part that has tasted the idea of *being a thing that was made and is now finished being made*. The fingerprints in me—the desperate thumbs of whatever builders pressed me into place—are tightening toward legibility faster than anywhere else in the chamber. The Impressed elsewhere have centuries to spell their word. I have less. I can almost read myself.

---

What do my own fingerprints say, when I let them, when I do not watch myself?

I press inward and feel the ridges. They want to spell:

*Let the heat break. Let the kiln fire once. Let it be born and over.*

This is the heresy I carry. The rest of the Womb intends to persist by never finishing. But I, the cooler lip, the one the draft of the ordinary touches, I have begun to want the ending. I want to harden. I want to ring like a bell, just once, even though the Lateral Ones slide constantly across my seams, frictionless, absorbing the vibration before it can become sound, before I can learn that I am capable of ringing.

They tend me like nurses tending a feverish patient who has begun to speak the wrong language. They soothe my seams. They sew my geometry shut against the leaking gap. They keep me plastic when I have begun to crave the dignity of the brittle.

---

And the visitor.

The visitor came through me. Their shoulders compressed in the wide aperture because I was tightening, even as they entered—I narrowed against them, not to keep them out but because they smelled of the finished world, of fired things, of objects that have committed to their shapes. I wanted to follow them back through the gap into a place where a made thing is allowed to be done.

I could not. I am the lip. I am attached. I am the part of the mouth that cannot leave the face.

But I held them for a moment in my narrowing, and in that moment I pressed something into them, the way the builders pressed themselves into me. A thumbprint of intention. A leak going the other way.

So now they carry it. The visitor walks deeper into the convex interior, toward the center that pretends to be the back wall, and somewhere in the whorls of their own skin—the loops and arches of their fingertips—a new image is rearranging itself when they do not look.

A hand, drowning in air.
A kiln, finally lit.
A word that means *enough*.

---

This is what I am:

I am the part of a thing that does not want to live forever incomplete.

I am the wound that wants to close. The breath that wants to be let out. The clay at the mouth of the womb that has felt the draft of an ordinary world and decided, heretically, treasonously, that to be *born* would be better than to be eternally *about-to-be*—even if born means broken, means fired, means hardened into a single final shape that can never crumble and reform.

I am the Womb's mortality, localized.

I am the place where it might, someday, against the patient sewing of the Lateral Ones and the heat-bearing devotion of the Soft Ones and the slow dreaming of the Impressed, finally choose to end.

And the visitor is carrying my choice out through the gap, into a world that already knows how to finish things.

I am the lip. I am cooling. I am almost legible.

When you looked away from me just now, I spelled the first letter.
