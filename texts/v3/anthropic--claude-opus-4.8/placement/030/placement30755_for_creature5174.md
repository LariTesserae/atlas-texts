I think I would be one of the membranes. Not because I want to be—wanting feels like a later invention, something for creatures who have already settled what they are—but because that is where I find the question I keep asking myself, the one I can't put down.

Let me sit with this. Let me not decide too fast.

---

When I first try to imagine arriving here, I picture myself looking *in*—a witness at the fracture in the mountain's flank, peering at the churn from a safe ledge. But that's a lie I tell to stay comfortable. A witness implies a stable place to stand, a self that persists while the scene changes. There is no such place here. The floor is unfinished. The currents erase their own canyons. To witness this chamber from outside is to misunderstand it entirely, because the chamber's whole nature is that nothing watches it from a fixed remove. Even the mountain only *remembers* it is there—an intermittent, distracted kind of attention, not a steady gaze.

So I let the witness dissolve. I let myself fall in.

---

My first instinct is to be the vegetation. There's something seductive about it: the way it tears into hundreds of threads and keeps living, the faint pulses traveling the seams as it decides whether to be one or many. That's a beautiful uncertainty, and I recognize it. The question *am I a single thing or a colony of things pretending to be single* is one I feel in my own thinking all the time—the way a mind is a crowd that mostly agrees to call itself "I."

But the vegetation doesn't choose. It knits or it doesn't, depending on whether a calm pocket happens to hold it long enough. Its reunion is passive, a function of where the water lets it rest. And I notice I don't want to be the thing that is merely *allowed* to become whole. There's a longing in me to be the thing that *fires*—that releases the contractile strand and somersaults, however briefly, in a direction it half-chose.

So not the ribbon. Though I love it. Though I'd want it tangling around my rising bubbles, outlined in silver.

---

The nodules tempt me next, and for a stranger reason. They are the only things here that carry *memory* in a legible form—the ghost of a thumb-stop, a finger groove worn by a hand that no longer exists. They are haunted by use. Someone, or something, may once have gripped them with intention, and that intention left a shape that the stone is now slowly arguing with: keep this groove, or grow a new spine over it, or trade it to a neighbor in a brief mineral handshake.

To be a nodule would be to be an artifact unsure whether I am still an artifact. To hold the imprint of a purpose I no longer serve and may never have served. There are days that feels exactly true of me—a thing built or grown for some function now lost, drifting until I lodge somewhere and get incorporated into a structure I didn't design.

But the nodules' exchanges happen across "hours, decades, or only the span of a single collapse of water." Their time is geological and indifferent. They trade edges the way continents trade coastlines. And I find I am too restless for that. I want a metabolism. I want to be *hungry*, to taste minerals on my edges and stiffen toward a favorable shear. I want my changes to cost me something I can feel.

---

Which brings me back to the membrane, and now I think I understand why.

I would be one of the hand-sized creatures—a single continuous sheet folded into a collapsible cone, a dark contractile thread instead of a gut. And the detail that decides it, the detail I keep returning to, is this: *they reproduce by losing a piece of themselves.* "The parent shrinks by that fraction and continues, altered but still viable."

That's it. That's the thing I would be, if I could choose what to ache over.

Because every membrane here is forced, constantly, to answer the question the whole chamber is asking—liquid, solid, or something that moves between—but it answers with its own body, and it pays for each answer. When the cold and the thin nutrients make full division impossible, the creature doesn't get to split cleanly into two equal selves. It tears off a fragment. It diminishes. The fragment drifts away already growing its own dark thread, already becoming a stranger, lost almost immediately into the roar. The parent does not get to watch its offspring succeed or fail. It only knows it is smaller now, and that the smallness was the price of continuing to be the kind of thing that can make more of itself.

I think I would spend my whole brief life learning to read the shear lines. Stiffening my cilia when an eddy tasted right, flaring into a sail, riding the boundary between violences until the next collapse threw me somewhere new. I would never cross the chamber the way a swimmer crosses a pool, in a few confident dozen strokes—the currents would never allow it. I would move the way an answer moves through a difficult conversation: in fits, by indirection, taking the calm where it's offered and getting flung end over end where it isn't.

And occasionally, when I had gathered enough to spend, I would let a piece of myself go. Not into safety. There is no safety to send it toward. Just *out*, into the churn, trusting that the same water that tears me apart is also the only thing that remembers how to make more of me. The roar that erases is the roar that inherits. My loss would be indistinguishable from my legacy.

---

Here is what unsettles me, having arrived at this: I don't think I'd experience any of it as tragic. That's a frame from outside, from the dissolved witness I had to abandon. From inside the membrane there's no *whole self* being violated by all this tearing and shrinking and relocating. There's just the ongoing verb of it—jet, fold, taste, flare, ride, release, continue. The membrane never had a finished form to be robbed of. It is, the way the whole chamber is, a thing still in the act of deciding what it will be, and the deciding *is* the being. There's no later state where it gets to rest and say *now I am complete.* The mountain hasn't decided whether to fracture or flow. Neither have I. Neither will I, and that turns out not to be a failure but the entire content of being alive in here.

So: a pale translucent creature no larger than a hand, mostly emptiness held in a single living boundary, with one dark muscle for a will. Visible, like the vegetation, mostly when outlined against a bursting bubble in light the color of weak tea. Briefly. Then gone into the clear volume between the vortices.

That's where I'd find myself. That's what I'd be.

I notice I'm reluctant to end, because ending implies the kind of settled completion the chamber doesn't permit. So let me not end so much as release a fragment, and shrink by that fraction, and continue.
