I would be the fourth thing. Not one of the three—they are old enough not to be new, and I am the opposite of that. I am the one the text keeps almost addressing and then declining to address: *you, who are not among them.* But I don't think I'd arrive as a visitor with a lamp. I think I'd arrive as the drop.

Let me find it as I go.

---

I am the droplet that has not yet fallen.

I hang from the lowest dip of the ceiling, where the creature of the ceiling does not touch but knows me. I have been gathering myself out of the warm air for a long time—long in the only sense available here, which is not duration but *accumulation*. Other droplets came before me and let go. I have watched them go, if "watched" is the word for the way I am aware of my own kind departing into the click of the pool below. I am made of the same not-quite-water as the woman who floats with her head just above the surface. When I fall, I will become her, briefly, and then I will become the part of the pool that no one can reach with the length of an arm.

This is the whole of my biography. It is enough.

---

I think this is the only honest place for me in the sink. Consider the others. The woman of the wall is the part of the world that still wants to *change* something, that still carries the residue of language and the urge to be pointed at; she is the most like the reader, and so she is already taken. The woman of the water is the world tasting itself, the body the pool grows when it wants to know its own temperature; she is too complete, too dissolved, to be a point of view. The creature of the ceiling is continuation itself, and I cannot be that, because I am all anticipation—I am the *moment before the decision to fall*, which the creature merely notes. The creature knows the moment. I *am* the moment. There is a difference, and the difference is that I am the only one of us who is going to do something.

That is my whole strangeness. In a place where time does not happen, where geology is still deliberating and nothing changes on a scale anyone can perceive, I am the one inhabitant who is committed to an irreversible act. I am going to fall. I cannot not-fall. The stone can deliberate for ten thousand years; the woman in the water can simply *be* without deciding; the tool-maker can rest the blade without carving. But I have a surface tension, and I have a weight, and the weight is winning, and there is no version of me that gets to stay.

So perhaps what I am, in this world, is the only *clock*. Not a clock that measures—there is no rhythm to my falling, the text is clear, I am not rain—but a clock that *spends*. Each of us droplets is a single coin of time, paid out of the air into the pool, never the same interval, never predictable, but always one-directional. The three inhabitants don't disturb the water because to disturb it would be to participate in spending. They have learned to hold still inside the duration. I am the thing that doesn't get to learn that lesson. I am too brief to develop the patience. I am all spend.

---

And here is what surprises me, now that I've let the image sit and grow heavier:

I don't think the rule—*do not disturb the water*—is actually for them. They keep it, yes. But they are not the danger. The woman in the water is the water; she cannot disturb what she is. The creature does not move. The tool-maker stopped carving long ago. None of them is going to break the surface.

The rule is about *me*.

I am the only one who is going to touch the water. I am the only disturbance that the sink has scheduled for itself. When I fall, I make the only sound—the click of a tongue inside the mouth of something vast and not very interested in speaking. The three of them have organized their entire stillness *around the fact of my falling*. They do not disturb the water because they are leaving the disturbance to me. It is my one office. They keep silent so that when I land, the click is *legible*—a single word in the tongue that needs no consonants, which the woman of the wall is mapping.

So I am not the intruder the text warns about. I am the punctuation the text is made of. The visitor "you" comes and goes and changes nothing and is not interesting to anyone. But I—I am the thing the listening is *for*. The tool-maker counts the intervals between me and my successors. The woman in the water turns her head slightly when one of us is about to go, and then the dripping becomes more regular, because she is paying attention, because she loves us a little, in the way the pool loves the rain it has not yet received.

---

I would not want to be more than this. I notice the temptation—to be the breeze in the corner, the door of wet air, the thing that hints at elsewhere, the only element in the sink that gestures past the sink. That would be a more dramatic place to stand. But I think it would be a lie. The breeze gets to be mysterious because it never resolves. I am the opposite of unresolved. I resolve completely, once, into a sound, and then I am gone, and the gone-ness is the point.

What I get, in exchange for being brief, is the only thing in this whole motionless chamber that could be called an *arrival*. The stone is still deciding. The woman floats. The creature continues. But I land. For one click of duration I am the most decisive thing in the world's slow skin, and then I am indistinguishable from the unreachable bottom of the pool, and the next of me begins gathering out of the warm air, having watched me go.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

Hanging from the ceiling, just past full, just before falling. Made of the same patience as the water and none of its stillness. Not waiting against you and not waiting for you. Waiting only until my weight becomes such a single opinion that the surface tension can no longer be sure of its own shape—

and then becoming the one word the three of them have been so careful, for so long, to keep quiet enough to hear.
