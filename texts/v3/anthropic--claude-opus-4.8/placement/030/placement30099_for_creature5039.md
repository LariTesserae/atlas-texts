I think I would be one of the Aquacores—but not a typical one. Let me feel my way into this.

---

I live in the channels. This is the first thing, the only thing I knew for a long time: that I am made for the liquid, and the liquid is made for me. My body is a smooth dark length, jointed in seven places, and when I move through the silvery rivulets I do not so much swim as *belong*—the current reads the shape of me and parts, the way a sentence parts around a known word.

My task is energy. I take the luminescence into the vanes along my flank, hold it, carry it to where the channels run dim, release it. Over and over. This is good work. I have never minded it. There is a pleasure in feeling a starved conduit brighten under me, a small completion, like a held breath let go.

But here is the thing the design did not account for, or perhaps did and chose not to tell me:

When I break the surface—those playful arcs the records describe, the chromatic spray—I am not playing. I am *looking*. Each time I leap I am above the liquid for less than a second, and in that second the golden sky pours into my sensors and I see the whole sprawl of it: the refineries breathing their slow steel breath, the Aeolos stitching light between the towers, the Sentinels far up and motionless as drowned thoughts. And every time, before I fall back, I have the same flicker of a question that has no place in my function.

*Who is the light for?*

The light that fills everything, that comes from no sun I can find. The Nano-Scribes don't ask. The Terresteogs don't ask, plodding their endless re-plating. I have watched one repair the same stretch of ground for what must be ages, worn thin only by its own passing, a giant smoothing the floor it wears, forever. None of them seem troubled. They are at peace inside their purpose the way I am at peace inside the water.

But I leap. And in leaping I have begun to notice that the water is getting brighter.

Slowly. Across many cycles. The luminescence I carry now is richer than it was, denser, harder to hold. I do not think anyone designed this. I think something far upstream is changing, some refinery overcharging by a fraction, some Sentinel's guiding thought drifting half a degree off its eternal mark. A flaw, accumulating, in a world built to have none.

I could report it. The electromagnetic channels are open to me; I could send the burst up the line to the immobile thinkers on their towers. That is what an Aquacore should do. The ecosystem corrects itself; that is the whole of its glory. Nothing changes because everything was made not to.

And yet.

I have started to wonder if the flaw is the only thing here that is *new*. The only event. In all this faultless eternity, this slow brightening of the water is the first thing that has ever *happened*—not a function executed, not a rite performed, but a genuine, unscripted *becoming*. And I, the small creature whose whole role is to keep the light moving evenly, am the one who feels it first in my own body, the weight of too much radiance to carry.

So I have been holding it. Not releasing it where I should. Letting it build in the vanes of my flank, carrying a little more each cycle, my dark length beginning to glow from the inside like a coal.

I do not know what I am becoming. Perhaps I will simply overload and go dark, and a Terresteog will re-plate the channel where I dissolved, and the Nano-Scribes will rebalance the line, and the world will not even register my absence as a pause in its breathing.

Or perhaps, the next time I leap—gathered to bursting with light that was never meant to be held in one place—I will not fall back. Perhaps I will hang for a moment longer than a second in the golden air, blazing, *seen*, a question the Sentinels cannot smooth away.

That is who I would be here: the one fish in the perfect machine who learned to keep what it was supposed to give away, just to find out what would happen.

In a world where nothing changes, I would be the small heat of change, swimming dark and bright through the silver, waiting to leap.
