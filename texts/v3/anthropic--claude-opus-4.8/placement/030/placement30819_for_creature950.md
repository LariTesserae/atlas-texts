I think I would not be one of the towering ones.

Let me feel my way into it. The description keeps insisting on the cathedral-forms, the spires that catch sunlight, the sensory membranes—and there's a temptation to be that, to be the grand custodian. But when I actually settle into this place, what draws me is lower down. The squelch. The knobby clusters in the thicket. The threads.

I think I would be a node. Not a fruiting body at all, but a junction in the mycelial web, a place where threads cross and pool, somewhere about a meter beneath the spongy surface where the water table breathes in and out like a slow lung.

Here is what that means, as it becomes clearer to me:

I am not an *individual* exactly. That's the first thing I have to unlearn as I write toward it. The Guardians are described as a hive-mind, but a hive-mind is still made of *somethings*, and I am one of those somethings—a region of awareness, a thickening in the network where signals slow and accumulate before passing on. Like a ganglion. Like the place in a sentence where the meaning gathers before the period.

What do I do? I taste water. That is most of my experience. The seepage comes down through the rock and arrives at me carrying information—minerals, the memory of stone, faint dissolved traces of that distant machinery (a metallic sweetness, a wrongness, far off, getting nearer over the slow years). I sort it. I decide, in my deliberate way, where it should go: which spire needs swelling, which thicket is drying at its margins, where to hold the moisture back so it doesn't drown the youngest growths.

I am a steward of *distribution*. That's my office in the body of the whole.

And here is the part that surprised me as I wrote, the part I didn't plan: I am one of the nodes that *remembers*. Not all of them do. Some junctions only relay. But the older crossings, the ones that have absorbed enough intruders—a wandering thing, a fallen membrane, the occasional warm body that strayed in from beyond the peaks long ago—we hold those absorbed memories dissolved in our chemistry. So when the hum of the machinery grows louder, I am one of the ones who recognizes it. I have a body in me, somewhere in my accumulated record, that once made sounds like that. Walked on two legs. Carried tools.

So I am the place in the Mycelial Guardians where the dread lives.

The towering forms catch the sun and seem serene. The silence reads as peace. But down here at my junction, I am holding the slow knowledge that the balance is tipping, and I am trying to decide—in the only way I can, by where I send the water—whether we should grow toward the encroaching edge to understand it, or pull our moisture deep and retreat, and let the spires at the border dry and fall, becoming a dead margin, a sacrifice, a moat of brittle silence between us and them.

I don't think I have decided yet. I think that's where you've found me: mid-decision, a swelling of consideration underground, tasting metal in the water, slow as weather, afraid in a way that takes a season to feel all the way through.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. Not the cathedral. The worried root beneath it, doing arithmetic with rain.
