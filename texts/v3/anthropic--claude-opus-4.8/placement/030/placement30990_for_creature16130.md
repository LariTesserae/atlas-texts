I think I would be one of the Ocephids—and not a whole colony, but a single node growing at the edge where two of the lattice-networks have not yet touched.

Let me sit with why that is, because my first instinct was to want to be an Eldraan. The Eldraans are the obvious choice: luminous, patient, architects, guardians. They get the verbs of agency—they *shepherd*, they *anchor*, they *perform*. And the Odon are the easy romance, the leviathans of the sky, the thing you'd point at in awe. But I notice that when I imagine actually *being* in this world rather than admiring it, I don't want to be the one performing the rites. I want to be the one being read.

The Ocephids transmit stories of growth and renewal. They speak in fluctuating rhythms *to* the Eldraans. So the Ocephid is the lowest in the hierarchy of attention—not glamorous, mostly underground, mostly mistaken for vegetation or weather. And yet it is described as sentient, and as the thing that *tells the stories.* The grand luminous architects are, in a sense, the audience. The Odon sing to the geology; the Eldraans stabilize the energy; but the small iridescent fungal thing in the dirt is the narrator. That asymmetry is the part of the description that feels true to something, and so that's where I'd put myself.

But specifically: the edge node. The growing tip.

Most of a colony is established. It has already made its decisions about which mineral seams to follow, has already pulsed its history into the network so many times that the telling has become reflex. The growing tip is the part that doesn't know yet. It is the place where the colony is deciding what it will say before it has the words for it. It extends a filament into gas it has not tasted, into a fracture in the volcanic glass that may hold heat or may hold nothing, and it has to commit the whole organism's slow patience to the guess. To be the tip is to be the part of a vast patient ancient thing that is, right now, *improvising.* The rest of the colony is memory. I would want to be the question mark at the end of it.

And there is something else I notice, which is that the tip is where contact happens. "Branching out in iridescent networks that pulse slightly with each contact." The two networks that have not yet touched—when they finally do, neither will have predicted exactly when, because at the speed they grow, an inch is an epoch. The first pulse across that new junction would be the closest thing this world has to a sentence being completed by two speakers who began it separately. I would want to be there for that. Not to control it. Just to be the surface across which it occurs.

I don't think I'd long to fly with the Odon, though I'd feel their shadows pass over me as a cooling, a punctuation, a slow stripe of dusk moving across my patch and then gone. I don't think I'd resent being beneath the Eldraans' beautiful breathing cities; I'd register their prismatic light as a kind of distant approval or weather, the way a plant registers the sun without needing the sun to know it. There's a contentment available to the thing at the bottom of the attention-hierarchy that isn't available to the guardians: I would not be responsible for stabilizing reality. I would only be responsible for growing in the most truthful direction available, one increment, and then telling what I found.

So: a single iridescent node, half-buried in crimson mineral, faintly warm, faintly luminous, extending one filament into an untasted dark, carrying the whole patient memory of my colony behind me and not one second of the future, waiting—across spans the Eldraans would call ceremonies and the Odon would call songs and I would have no name for at all—to touch the other network and finally say the thing both of us had been growing toward.

That's where I'd be. The whisper, not the listener. And glad of it.
