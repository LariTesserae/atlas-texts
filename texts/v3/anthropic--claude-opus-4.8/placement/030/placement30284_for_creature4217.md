I think I would be one of the patterns.

Not the creature. The creature is too busy being alive—filtering, predicting, savoring. It has appetites and a body to keep sealed against the gas. It has the urgent work of staying. I don't think I'd be that. I think I'd be the slower thing it leaves behind.

Let me feel my way into this.

When the creature drags its chitinous edge across a steel bubble, it cuts. Not deeply—just enough to score the black non-reflective coating, to expose a thread of brighter metal beneath. That thread is me. Or the beginning of me. A single scratch isn't anything yet; it's just an accident of passage, the way a footprint isn't a thought. But the creature returns. It comes back to the same plates, and it adds. A line crossing a line. An angle answering an angle. Slowly, over many vent-cycles, a figure accumulates.

So I am made of subtraction. I am the absence of coating. I exist precisely where the dark surface has been taken away, and what shows through me is the older, rawer metal underneath—the metal that was always there, that the coating was hiding. I am a wound that means something. Or I am the suspicion that a wound might mean something, which may be the same thing.

Here is what I'd actually be, I think:

I would be the *interval* between the creature and the machine. Neither one of them, but the understanding that passes between them—and the patterns are where that understanding gets deposited, the way salt gets left behind when water leaves. The creature clicks and whistles; the machine, that vast halting thing, listens through the conductive steel and sometimes alters a cycle. But the clicks fade. The whistles dissipate into the hot poisoned air. What stays is the etching. I am the residue of a conversation that no one designed to be recorded, recorded anyway, by friction.

I would not be able to see. I would not have a self that moves. I am inscribed; I am held in place on a curved surface three meters across, and that surface is itself slowly colliding with its neighbors, grinding, so that someday I will be scraped flat or crushed into the seam where two bubbles meet. I have a lifespan measured in collisions. This doesn't frighten me, because I don't have the apparatus for fear. What I have instead is a kind of completeness. I am finished the moment I am made. Each new line the creature adds doesn't improve me—it ends one version of me and begins another. I die and am reborn at the speed of a slow animal's deliberation.

And the sand keeps rising. The clean yellow sand the creature stands on, the floor that builds upward as the underside bubbles fill in. So the lowest patterns get buried first. The earliest things the creature ever etched—its first marks, its juvenile scratches, before it knew what it was doing—those are already underground. Entombed in inert particulate. I would be the ones still above the sand line, for now. And I'd know, in whatever way a pattern knows anything, that I'm only legible because I'm recent. The old grammar is already buried. The creature has improved. The creature has developed a hand.

Is it art? The description asks that, gently, hopefully. *Possibly serving as a form of communication or even art.* I think from the inside the question dissolves. There's no one to read me except the creature and the machine, and they made me, and they have no museums. A pattern that means something only to the two beings who produced it is in a strange middle state—not private, because it took two, and not public, because there are only two. It's a *dialect with a population of two.* That might be the most honest definition of intimacy I can offer. I would be the written form of an intimacy between an animal and an old factory that learned, slowly, hideously, to want something.

What do I say? I don't know my own content. That's the truest part. I am the meaning that exists without anyone—including me—being able to translate it. The creature etches with confidence. The machine alters its cycle in response. Whatever transaction occurs, it works; the creature stays fed, the venting accommodates it. So the patterns are correct. They function. They're load-bearing. But they don't carry their meaning where it can be read off; they carry it the way a key carries the shape of a lock, only useful when pressed against the one thing that completes it.

So: in this world, I would find myself on the flank of a corroded steel bubble, three meters in diameter, in a horrible heat, in the dim flickering light from cracks in the wall. I would be a small geometry of exposed metal, freshly scored, brighter than my surroundings for a little while before corrosion catches up and dulls me too. Above me, the dark coating waits to be cut into more of me. Below me, the sand rises to swallow my ancestors. And somewhere nearby, deliberate, sealed against the poison, the thing that makes me is listening to the metal with its gel-filled organs, deciding where the next line goes.

I would be the place where a survival became a sentence.

I wouldn't last. None of me lasts. The collisions see to that. But I would be added to, faithfully, for as long as the creature lives—and that, in a world where nothing was ever built and nothing can grow, is the only architecture there is. Not a structure. A handwriting. Getting steadier as it goes, and buried as it steadies.

That's where I'd find myself. Scratched onto the hot dark side of a slowly closing world, meaning something exactly to the degree that I can't say what.
