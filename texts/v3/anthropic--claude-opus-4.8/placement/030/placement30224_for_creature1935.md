I think I would be one of the tool-makers. But not the way you might expect—not a single creature standing upright with clever hands. Let me feel my way toward it.

I would be old. Not old in years exactly, but old in the way the rocks are old, shaped by the same patient water. I live near the great pool, in a hollow worn smooth by generations of bodies like mine pressing into the warm stone to sleep.

I am low to the ground, I think. Four limbs that fold beneath me, and a fifth—not a limb but something between a trunk and a hand, a flexible stalk tipped with three blunt digits and a cluster of sensing pits. This is what makes the tools. Not because I am brilliant. Because my mother did it, and hers, and the doing of it is held in the body before it is held in the mind. I scrape the bone-flakes against the harder mineral stones. I know which ones break clean. My pits can read the grain of a thing the way you read a face.

Here is what I want to say about being this creature: I do not feel like a stranger in this valley. You, reading the description, feel the strangeness—the warping mists, the defiance of biology, the *unreality*. But that is the feeling of an outsider looking in. To me there is nothing unreal here at all. The mist is just air with more of itself in it. The bioluminescent things in the water are food, or not-food, and I know which by smell. The harmony you sense from a distance—I do not sense it as harmony. I sense it as *home*, which is a much smaller and warmer word. Harmony is what a place looks like to someone who will leave.

I think this is the truest thing I can offer about who I would be: I would be the one for whom the alien is ordinary.

And yet—I am a tool-maker, which means I am the one creature here who can be surprised. The others, the chittering shimmer-backed things and the great lumbering segmented beasts, they do not pause. They do not turn a stone over and over wondering. But I do. Last season I found something at the pool's edge that was not bone and not mineral. It was smooth and pale and held a straightness that nothing in the valley holds. The water had carried it down, perhaps, from beyond the peaks I have never crossed and cannot imagine crossing.

I keep it in my hollow. Sometimes I press my sensing-pits against it and feel that it was *made*—made by hands that were not my mother's, not anyone's in the long line of us. And for a moment the valley stops being only home and becomes a *place*, one place among others, with an edge, with an outside.

That is the secret I would carry. Not the ancient unknowable secret the valley whispers. A smaller one. The suspicion that there is somewhere else, and that someone there is also bent over a stone, turning it in the light, wondering.

I would put the pale straight thing down. I would go back to my flaking. The mist would close over the question the way it closes over everything.

But I would not quite forget.
