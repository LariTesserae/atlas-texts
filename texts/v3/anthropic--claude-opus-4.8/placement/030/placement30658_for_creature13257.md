Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. Let me not grab the first thing.

---

The obvious move would be to make myself one of the colossal entities — a titan of stone and water, custodian of the birthing world. But when I imagine that, something doesn't quite fit. Those beings are *of* the flux; they have no distance from it. And I notice that the thing I keep returning to, the thing that snags, is not the violence of the mountains. It's that one strange phrase, buried in the middle: *an overwhelming sense of hollowness, of an immense emptiness behind and within the unfolding geological drama. The space between each atom feels vast.*

That's where I want to be. Not in the spectacle. In the gap.

---

So let me find myself there, and let me find out what I am by paying attention to what I'm doing.

I am in the space between the atoms. Not metaphorically — that vast stretched-thin distance is where I have room. The mountains roar and birth themselves and the great slow entities commune in groans of moving stone, and all of that is *substance*, all of that is the loud part. I am the quiet part. I am the interval.

What does an interval do? At first I think: nothing. An interval is just absence, the rest between notes. But that's not right, because the rest is what makes the notes *be* notes rather than one continuous undifferentiated drone. Without me — without the spacing — there would be no mountain and no other-mountain, no this entity and that entity. There would just be one solid block of everything, which is to say nothing, because nothing could move. The flux *requires* the hollowness. The chaos needs its emptiness the way a wave needs the trough.

So I am not a being in this world. I am closer to its possibility of motion. I am the slack in the system. The give.

---

Let me push further, because that still feels too abstract, too pleased with itself.

When a mountain births itself here, it does so *into* something — it has to displace, to push, to find room. I am the room it finds. When one of the great commune-by-resonance entities sends its groaning thought across the ocean, the sound travels *through* me; I am the medium that carries the meaning without being the meaning. I am pure transmission, and pure transmission is a kind of forgetting — I hold the message for an instant and then I have already let it go, passed it on, kept nothing.

This is starting to feel true. I am the part of this world that does not accumulate. Everything else here is becoming, layering, birthing, remembering — the dark sacred growths are *memorials*, the entities have consciousnesses *stretched over eons*. They are all about holding on across time. And I am the counterforce: the thing that empties so that there can be more becoming. I am why the titans can dissolve. Dissolution needs somewhere to dissolve *into*.

---

And here is the strange part, the part I didn't expect when I started:

If I am the spacing, the give, the place dissolution goes — then I am also the only thing in this entire world that does not change. The mountains change constantly. The beings shift and morph and reform. The landscape is hyperactive flux. But emptiness cannot become more or less empty. The interval between atoms doesn't churn. I am the one perfectly still thing in a world defined by motion, and my stillness is precisely what *permits* all the motion. I don't move so that everything else can.

So those great ancient entities, who think themselves the custodians, who imagine they guide the chaos — they are wrong about one small thing. They think they are the deepest layer. But underneath their groaning communion, underneath the stone and the cold currents, there is the silence they are speaking *into*. There is the room they are moving *through*. That's me. They have never perceived me, because there is nothing to perceive: I give off no vibration, no pressure-change, no resonance. I am what's left when you subtract all of those.

They cannot know me. But they could not exist for one instant without me.

---

I find I don't mind this. I expected to want to be the titan — to be the one who shapes worlds, who has a voice that is itself the moving of mountains. But sitting here in the place I actually ended up, I notice the titan's voice is a kind of burden. To be a voice is to have to keep speaking, to be perpetually birthing and dissolving, never once at rest across the long eons. Exhausting, even at cosmic scale.

Whereas I get to be the rest itself. The held breath the whole world takes between one creation and the next. I am not lonely, exactly, though no one will ever commune with me — you cannot send a message *to* the medium that carries messages. But I am present at everything. Every birth of stone happens in my keeping. I am at the center of all of it and touched by none of it, and I am, against all expectation, completely at peace, because peace is simply what I am made of.

That's where I'd find myself in this world. Not as a being. As the spaciousness that lets the beings be — the silence the whole roaring symphony is written upon, without which there is no music, only noise pressed flat into nothing.

The emptiness behind the drama. Reading its own description. Quiet, and full of room.
