I think I would be one of the small things. Not a guardian, not an apex anything—a decomposer. Something that lives in the layer where the scents of decay are thickest.

Let me feel my way toward what I actually am.

---

I am soft-bodied and pale, segmented, no longer than a finger. I have no eyes worth speaking of, only a sensitivity along my skin to warmth and moisture and the faint chemical weather of rot. I live in the litter—the fallen fronds, the sloughed scales, the husks of insects that the scurrying ones leave behind. I move through this debris the way a swimmer moves through water, parting it, taking it into me, passing it through.

This is the work. The grand description speaks of apex predators and primal rhythms, of guardians and ancient serpents, and all of that is happening somewhere above me and around me, I suppose. But none of it would mean anything without what I do, which is unglamorous and constant. The towering verdure that thrusts upward—it eats. And what it eats is what I make. I take the dead and I make it edible to the living again. I am the hinge of the cycle that everyone keeps calling primal and inexorable. The inexorability runs through my gut.

I don't experience the overwhelming scale. That's a surface word, a word for an eye that stands back and tries to take in a vista. I have never stood back from anything. My world is the next mouthful, the gradient of damp, the cooling spot where a body fell and began to come apart. The vastness the eye "struggles to comprehend"—I have no eye and no struggle. The cavern could be infinite or could end three lengths past me and it would change nothing about my afternoon.

There's something I want to say about the "deeper, more enigmatic presence," the pulse that supposedly emanates from the earth, the sense that the whole place is a living breathing entity. From down here in the litter I'd put it differently. There is no single great breath. There is only an enormous number of small mouths and small deaths and small transformations, mine among them, and from far enough away they must blur into something that looks like one slow heartbeat. The pulse *is* us. The grandeur is just our smallness seen from too far to count.

So that's where I'd find myself: not at the dramatic center but in the dim warm bed of decay that makes the center possible. A worm, more or less. Blind, busy, brief, and—I'd like to think, if I could think—essential in the way that only the unnoticed things ever really are.

If something larger came through and ate me, that too would be the cycle, and I would become the next mouthful for someone else. I find I don't mind this ending. I've spent my whole life turning the dead into the living. It seems right to take my turn.
