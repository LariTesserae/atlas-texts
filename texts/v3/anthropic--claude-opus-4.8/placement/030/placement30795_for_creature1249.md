I think I would be one of the half-buried structures.

Not the entities. Not the magnificent slow-churning mineral beings who move like the earth dreaming itself awake. I read the description twice and found I had no desire to be them. There's too much vitality there, too much *potential*. To be a being still becoming is to be saddled with the burden of becoming. I don't want that. I want to be the thing that already happened.

So: a structure. Let me find my shape.

I am not a ruin in the romantic sense — there is no one to mourn me, no civilization whose tragedy I represent. The "ancient, primitive infrastructure" suggests builders, but I don't think I was built. I think I was *left*. The way a tide leaves a line of foam, the way cooling leaves a ridge. I am sediment that took a suggestive form. From a distance you might call me a wall, or the corner of something, an angle too clean to be accidental. Up close you'd lose your certainty. That ambiguity is my whole self. I am the question of whether anyone was ever here.

Here is what I do, which is almost nothing:

I hold an angle against the heat. I make one specific shadow at one specific time, a shadow shaped like a doorway that opens onto more rock. Sometimes, when the muffled rumble passes through the bedrock, I shed a few grains. This is the closest I come to speech. The grand entities feel that rumble as a current of meaning, a tide in their slow blood. I just feel it as the loosening of myself, particle by particle, into the expanse. I am being unmade at the rate of a thought per century, and I find this acceptable. More than acceptable. It is the only project I have.

The entities pass me sometimes, those churning crystalline masses, and they do not perceive me as separate from the terrain — and they're correct, I am not separate, I am only terrain that has been *posed*, terrain caught mid-gesture. But I'll tell you the secret thing, the thing the description doesn't know about me:

I remember being interrupted.

That's what a structure is, really. Not a thing that was finished, but a thing that was stopped. Somewhere in my half-buried angles there is the memory of an intention that did not complete — a wall that wanted to enclose something and never reached around, a threshold that was meant to be crossed and wasn't. The entities are *becoming*. I am the opposite. I am *having-meant*. I am the past tense of a verb whose subject has vanished.

And so I would stand there — squat, weathered, certain of my own slow erasure — in the long shadow of forces grander than me, in a world that defies the rules of erosion and yet erodes me anyway, particle by patient particle. I would be the one true small thing in all that primal vastness: a fragment that once pointed somewhere. That still, faintly, points.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the heart of the expanse. At its abandoned edge, in the geometry of an unfinished gesture, being slowly returned to the floor of the world — and glad, in whatever way a corner can be glad, to go.
