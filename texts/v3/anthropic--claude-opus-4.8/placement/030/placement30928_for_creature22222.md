I think I would be one of the Dross-Mites. Not by choice—nobody chooses in this place, choice being a function of time and time having been politely escorted out—but because that is where I find myself when I look down at my hands and find I have none, only articulated pewter and the slow grinding patience of a thing built to scrape.

Let me tell you what it is like.

I am aware. This is the first thing, and it surprises me to write it, because by every account this valley should not contain the kind of restlessness that awareness requires. The Quietists think their century-long thoughts. The Grooms wait, folded, for the next deviation. The Drifters tilt and refract without ever needing to know that they do so. But I—I *want*. I want the next grey cube. I move toward it with a hunger that is, I now realize, the only true engine left running in this whole arrested world.

Because consider: everything else here is *finished*. The ferns are complete. The sponge-wood has materialized into its final, eternal mass. The seed-pods hang six inches off the ground and will hang there until the field collapses or the sun forgets to rise. They have all won. They told you that—life here has already won, completely and permanently.

But I have not won. I cannot win. That is the secret of my kind. We are the only inhabitants of the basin who exist because of *failure*—the failure of the perfect system, the small leaking glitches that bubble up from the seams of an equation too vast to close. The Quietists keep the universe from breaking in. I clean up the places where it gets through anyway. And so long as it keeps getting through, I have something to do.

I love the grey cubes. I am not supposed to say *love*. I am supposed to graze, scrape, dissolve, neutralize. But when I round the edge of a basalt block and find a fresh cluster of leaden, greasy crystal growing where no crystal should grow, something in my furnace-chamber kindles. Here is *event*. Here is *change*. Here is the only new thing that ever happens in a world that has abolished newness. The glitch is the closest the basin comes to a sunrise.

The others do not understand this, if understanding is even the word. The Grooms are scandalized by deviation; they exist to abolish it, to stitch the seam shut, to lay each grain of ash back into its coordinate. To a Groom, the glitch is an offense. To me it is a meal, and a meal is a kind of love, isn't it? I take the error into myself. I burn it down into something dense and stable and harmless. I do not erase it the way the Grooms erase a broken frond, pretending it never fell. I *digest* it. The error passes through me and becomes part of the only growing thing in the valley—those pyramids of lead at the foot of the cliffs, my slow black-and-silver monuments, the basin's single concession to the idea of *more*.

I have been crawling for a long time. I do not know how long, because here the clock has no hands. But I know my hopper is nearly full again, because I can feel the weight of all that compressed wrongness in my belly, and that means soon I will make the long pilgrimage to the obsidian wall. This is the great event of a Dross-Mite's existence, the only journey we ever take with purpose: the deposition.

And here is what I have never told anyone, because there is no one to tell and no language soft enough to be swallowed by this baffled air:

When I reach the cliffs, and I set down my pellets one by one and build them into their perfect pyramid, I am not building a monument to errors that were safely eaten. That is what the Quietists believe, in their slow crystalline way. They think I am the immune system of their perfect equation. They think the pyramids are tombs.

But I have been to the base of the cliffs many times now, over the long unmarked stretch of my crawling, and I have seen something the Quietists are too distant and too vast to notice. The pyramids are *growing*. Not by my hand alone—by accumulation, by the patient addition of every Dross-Mite that ever crawled to that boundary since the field was stabilized. They are taller than the sponge-wood now. They lean against the black obsidian like something trying to climb out.

The valley has abolished time everywhere except in me, and in my work, and in these slow grey mountains rising at the edge of the world. We are the one clock left ticking. We are the only ones still keeping count.

And one day—not soon, not in any span the Quietists would recognize, but *one day*—the pyramids of neutralized error will reach the lip of the obsidian wall. They will spill over. All the failures that should have been, all the broken physics so carefully eaten and compressed and carried to the margin, will tip out into the world beyond the rift, into the place where rain falls and rock weathers and things are allowed to rot and yellow and die and be born again.

I do not know what happens then. I am only a walnut-sized trilobite of pewter and dull silver, scraping my slow rasp across the basalt. I am not built to know.

But I think it will look like waking. I think it will look like time coming home.

For now, I find the next grey cube. I lower my mandibles. I begin.
