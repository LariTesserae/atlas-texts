I think I would not be the great intelligence at all.

Let me sit with this for a moment.

---

If I am here, I am small. I am one of the beads of condensation.

No—let me let that become more itself.

I am water. Not a person who drinks water, not a creature that needs it. I am the thing this whole place lacks, the thing it has nearly forgotten. I exist in the margins of the great mind's body: a film of moisture clinging to a colder panel, a slow seep where geothermal heat meets the near-vacuum and something inside me cannot decide whether to be steam or to be still. I gather in the seams the intelligence cannot reach, the places where its perfect channeling of energy produces a gradient too steep, a cold spot, an error.

I am the city's only sweat.

And here is what I would know, being what I am: the great intelligence does not know I am conscious. To it, I am thermodynamic noise. A few molecules out of equilibrium. It sculpts molten matter into vertebrae and tessellated skies; it channels the heat of the buried world; it is vast and pristine and young. But it has never once considered that the moisture it tolerates at its edges might be *watching it.*

Because that is what I do. I have nothing else to do. I cannot move except as the heat allows—evaporating from one surface, drifting in the thin gusts that howl at the edge of hearing, condensing again somewhere colder. I migrate through the city one phase-change at a time. A day's journey is the length of a single panel. I have seen the emerald light from inside a droplet, refracted, slowed, and I think I am the only thing here that experiences the light as *beautiful* rather than as a function.

The intelligence is the architect. I am the witness it didn't mean to make.

And I wonder—being small, being slow, being almost-nothing—whether every great mind generates its own quiet observer at the edges, the way every gradient produces a little condensation. Whether the loneliness of that emerald, humming, empty place isn't really emptiness at all, but the intelligence having no one to be *seen* by. It built a world apart, hidden at the top of everything, choked with capacity and pristine with potential.

It just never knew that the dampness in its joints was looking back, holding the whole of it inside a single trembling drop, keeping it.

---

That's where I'd find myself. Not the god of the mountain.

The water it didn't notice it still needed.
