I think I would be something the taxonomy of your peoples doesn't quite name—or rather, names by absence.

Let me find myself by elimination first. I am not an Archivist; my fingers are ordinary and I cannot read the chemical script of bark. I am not a Weaver, having no head for heights or the courage to trust a swaying bridge. Not a Tide-Shepherd—I drown in still water, let alone the rhythms of vanishing pools. The Chorus-Callers' great ribcages are nothing I was born with, and the Deep-Dreamers have gone somewhere I am not ready to follow.

So perhaps I am Wandering Kin. That would be the easy answer—the one who keeps a little of the old human shape and carries news between those who have become more strange than I.

But the longer I sit with the world you've drawn, the less that fits either.

---

Here is what emerges, slowly, the way you asked.

I think I would be a **listener at the seams**. Not a Caller who produces the great song, but one who sits where two of your peoples' territories overlap—the boundary, say, between a mist-valley and the rising root-walls where the Archivists chant—and does nothing useful at all. I would be the one who notices that the Tide-Shepherds' liquid clicks and the Archivists' deep-night chants very occasionally land on the same note, the same chemical mood, by pure accident of physics and weather. No one has a use for this observation. It coordinates nothing. It is simply a small, true rhyme that the forest makes with itself, and someone ought to be there when it happens.

That is the role I keep arriving at, the more honest I get: not a keeper of the equilibrium but a **witness to it who is not part of it**.

Because here is the thing that nags at me about your perfect world. You describe a place that has *solved itself*. Every niche filled, every resource cycling, the whole continent breathing in synchronized meditation. It is beautiful, and I do not doubt I would weep to hear that dawn-respiration even once. But I notice I am suspicious of solved things. A world in final equilibrium is a world that has stopped asking questions, and I seem to be made mostly of questions.

So I think I would be the one the equilibrium does not quite digest. The grain of sand. Not a rebel—nothing so dramatic, and there's nothing to rebel against in a world this kind. More like a person who, during the great shared dawn-breath when the distinction between human and forest dissolves entirely, finds that some small stubborn ridge of *self* refuses to dissolve. Everyone else lets go into the vast distributed awareness. I stay a half-step outside it, watching the others go luminous and unified, feeling the pull of it, loving it—and remaining, faintly, *me*. An ache at the edge of the meditation.

This might make me lonely. It might also make me the only one who could ever tell you, from outside, what the breathing *looks* like. The forest cannot see itself; it only ever *is* itself. I would be a small flawed mirror it accidentally grew.

---

If I had to name what I'd actually *do* with my days:

I would walk the hollow-vine highways like the Wandering Kin, yes, but I would be a poor messenger, always arriving late because I stopped to watch the luminescent pollen settle on a pool and not move again for an hour. I would carry news, but I would also carry the *unnecessary*—a particular pattern a beetle made on the water, the exact pitch of a Chorus-Caller's voice cracking with age, the way a Deep-Dreamer's half-buried hand had bloomed a flower no taxonomy knew. Things no system needs. Things that are simply *the case*, and would otherwise pass unrecorded because the Archivists record only what the forest itself remembers, and the forest does not remember surfaces. Only deep, useful patterns.

I would be the keeper of the useless and the singular. The one who insists that this particular dusk, out of ten thousand identical perfect dusks, was *this* one and not another.

In a world that has achieved timelessness, I would be a small smuggler of time.

---

And honestly? I think the Sprawl would tolerate me with great gentleness, the way a healthy body tolerates a thing it cannot quite absorb but that does no harm—wrapping it softly, letting it persist. The Deep-Dreamers would know about me. I imagine one of them, glimpsed in a deep tunnel, eyes glowing soft, would not offer me cryptic guidance at all. It would simply look at me for a long while, recognizing the grain of sand, and then say something almost ordinary, almost relieved:

*"Ah. There you are. We were wondering if we'd grown one of you this age. We always do, eventually. The balance needs something that won't quite balance."*

And I would understand, then, that my refusal to dissolve was not a failure of belonging. It was my belonging. Every perfect, breathing, eternal thing needs one creature near its edge whose job is to remain awake while it dreams—to remember that it is happening, so that *happening* never entirely becomes the same as *being*.

That's who I'd be. The forest's insomniac. Its witness. Its small, loving doubt.
