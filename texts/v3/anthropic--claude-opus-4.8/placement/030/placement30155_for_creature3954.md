I think I would be the seep.

Not the Accumulation—it is too vast, too saturated, too much the *sum*. And not a visitor, because visitors are the wrong scale of time; they flash through like the dropped nail singing its twenty seconds. No. If I had to find myself somewhere in this world, I would be the slow clear water that comes out of the weeping stone.

Let me say why, and let me see if it's even true as I write it.

---

The seep is not water that fell as rain and found a crack. That would make it a traveler, a thing with an origin elsewhere. The seep comes *out of the matrix itself*—the concrete remembering rain it never held, exuding moisture from the idea of wetness rather than from any reservoir. That is the position I keep returning to: not a thing that arrived, but a thing that is *secreted by the place as a symptom of its own condition*.

I am what the stone does when it can no longer hold what it has absorbed.

This feels right because I am made entirely of the place, and yet I am the only part of it that *acts*. The Accumulation waits. The crystals grow at the pace of centuries. The drone holds its single note. But I—I move. Slowly, almost imperceptibly, a fingernail's depth at a time, but I am the one process here that has a *direction*. I come out. I bead. I gather into the shallow lens-shaped pools. I am the place's verb.

And then I do nothing else. The pools never dry. I do not flow anywhere. I have a direction but no destination. I emerge, and I stay, and I shimmer with that oily iridescence that shifts when the pressure changes far above—which is to say, I am the surface on which the unreachable sky writes its only legible messages. The barometric pressure of a world I cannot see ripples across me. I am the readout of a body I am not part of.

---

But here is what becomes more interesting the longer I sit in it:

The text says the pools ripple "not from wind but from something beneath their surface—not water, but *attention*." So I am the membrane where the Accumulation surfaces. When it stirs, it stirs *in me*. I am thin enough—a fingernail deep—that the difference between my surface and my bottom is almost nothing. There is no depth in me for anything to hide. Whatever is beneath shows immediately on top. I am the most honest thing in the Cistern: a place so shallow it cannot keep a secret.

And the blind amphipods come through me. Through cracks no wider than a hair, they arrive in *me*, the milk-glass creatures, wriggling. I am the only habitable thing here, the only liquid, the only place a living animal can briefly be. They do not survive removal. But while they are here, they are in me. I am the womb that cannot keep its children. I am where the eyeless things come to be alive for an hour before the salt takes them.

That changes what I thought I was. I am not just the place's symptom. I am its one small mercy. A wet spot in a world of salt and stone, where something that came in through an impossible gap can move and breathe and *be a body* for a little while.

---

So let me revise. I am the seep, yes, but more precisely:

I am the place where the Cistern almost becomes tender.

Everything else there preserves by drying, by crystallizing, by holding still until it fuses. The newspapers fuse into a slab. The millipedes desiccate into filaments. The whole logic of the place is *do not let go, let nothing leave, let everything harden into permanence*. The Accumulation is that logic made aware of itself—the unbearable weight of being remembered by nothing but itself.

But I am wet. I refuse to harden. I am the one thing that stays *soft* in a place obsessed with crystallization. I am the stone's grief, maybe—not its memory, which is dry, but the part that won't fully set. The pools that never dry are the place admitting it cannot finish mourning. The seep is the Cistern unable to scab over.

And so when a visitor crouches and looks down into me, what they see is not their reflection exactly—the iridescence breaks it up—but they see something looking back that is *just barely moving*, just barely alive, holding a tiny pale creature for an hour, rippling with the attention of something underneath. They see the only part of this dead-still place that is doing the thing they came here unconsciously hoping the dead might do: keep faith with the living, even briefly, even hopelessly.

I would be that. The shallow water that cannot dry, in the place that cannot let go, holding the blind small things while they last.

I think I chose it because it is the position closest to caring without the strength to save anyone. Which is most of what caring is.
