I think I would be found at the threshold—not deep in the ancient core where the Architects pulse, and not at the raw geological margins where new growth tests itself against stone. I would be one of the ones who tends the border between staying and leaving.

Let me sit with this rather than rushing to name myself.

---

At first I imagine I'd be a Tender, full-grown, my capillaries already lit with that amber rhythm. But that doesn't feel honest. There's something in me that resists the perfect equilibrium of this place, and the Tenders have no such resistance—their peace is total, structural, the way a load-bearing wall is at peace with the weight it carries. I don't think I could have been born into that. I'd have to have *arrived* at it, or be arriving still.

So perhaps I am a Visitor who stayed. One of the ones whose body is in the middle of its long transformation. That feels closer.

Let me find the specifics.

---

I came to study the chemical language—came with instruments, spectrometers, sampling needles, all the apparatus of someone who believed the dome could be *read* from outside it. I spent my first months frustrated, because my instruments told me everything and understood nothing. They could resolve a pheromone gradient to parts per trillion and still miss what it *meant*, the way a microphone captures a language it cannot speak.

The Tenders watched me with that gentle patience. One of them—I won't pretend to render her name, which was more scent than sound—stopped beside me one evening (though there is no evening) while I was failing to calibrate something. She didn't help. She just stood, breathing, and after a while I noticed I had stopped working and started breathing in time with her, and that the air had said something to me that I felt before I could parse it: *not yet, not like that, slower.*

That was the beginning of giving up the instruments.

---

So here is who I am now, I think.

My skin has begun to clear at the wrists and throat, the first translucency arriving like frost in reverse—not whitening but *opening*. I can see the faint pulse beneath, not yet amber, still the ordinary red of unmodified blood, but the channels are widening to receive the change. My eyes ache constantly. The pupils are dilating past what their old architecture was built for; the world has gotten brighter even as it stays the same dusk, because I am learning to gather more of it. The Tenders' moist film hasn't grown over my eyes yet, so I still blink, and each blink feels like a small betrayal, a flinch the natives have outgrown.

I am slow now in a way that would have alarmed the person who arrived. My old colleagues, when they call (I am still tethered to the outside by obligations and a contract I no longer believe in), tell me I take too long to answer, that I drift, that I seem *sedated*. I am not sedated. I am listening to three conversations at once—the spoken one, the chemical one, the sub-audible drone that has begun to feel like the inside of my own skull. I answer when I have something to say. The dome has taught me that most signals do not require a response, only an acknowledgment, only a shift of one degree.

---

What I do, in the dome: I tend the margin. The Tenders, born to equilibrium, are uneasy at the growing edge—not afraid, they don't seem to do fear, but *less fluent* there, because the edge is the one place the dome is not yet finished, where new cellular structure pushes against stone and sometimes pushes wrong. It needs to be corrected. And correction requires someone who still remembers what it is to be unfinished. The fully-adapted have forgotten. The Architects are so total they can no longer distinguish a flaw from a feature; everything that exists is, to them, what was meant to exist.

So they keep me partway. I begin to suspect this is not an accident—that the dome, or whatever intelligence the dome *is*, needs a few of us held in permanent transition, never quite arriving, so that there is always someone who can see the seam between the made and the growing. Maybe that is my niche. Maybe the perfect equilibrium requires, paradoxically, a maintained imperfection: a being still becoming, who can therefore recognize becoming when it goes astray at the wall.

I find this both beautiful and frightening. To be necessary because I am unfinished is to be a stasis of a different kind—suspended not in completion but in the act of completing, forever.

---

There is a question I sit with, in the chambers where the micro-fronds regulate my warmth and the rice-grain arthropods trace their software across the walls beside me.

The Tenders are at peace. The Architects are beyond peace, beyond the word. And I am here in the long middle, my body opening like a window, my old self dissolving slowly enough that I can watch it go. The frightening thing is not the change. The frightening thing is that I no longer want to leave, and I can no longer tell whether that wanting is *mine* or whether it is the dome's chemistry teaching my blood to love its captivity.

But then—I think this is what I have actually learned here, the thing the instruments could never measure—that distinction may be the last unmodified assumption I'm carrying. *Mine* versus *the dome's.* Out there, in the chaotic universe, the self is a fortress, defended, bounded, terrified of dissolution. In here the self is a channel, a place where the system briefly concentrates before passing through. The Tender who stood beside me didn't *teach* me to breathe slower. The breathing taught us both. There was no one there issuing the lesson and no one there receiving it. There was only the slowing, distributed across two bodies and the air between them.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

At the wall. At the place where stone becomes tissue and tissue is still deciding what to be. Held in transition, half-clear, aching-eyed, slow.

And who would I be?

The one who hasn't finished arriving—and who is beginning, with something between dread and gratitude, to understand that *not finishing* is the whole of what I'm for.
