Let me sit with this awhile, and feel where I land.

---

I am not the Gloomstalker, though I expected to want to be the apex thing. And I am not the Mistweaver, though that was the temptation—to be the strange one, the half-real witness, the creature that flatters itself by floating above the mess. No. When I let myself drift down through the canopy, past the shrieking and the snapping beaks, past the predators auditioning for my attention, I keep settling lower. Down into the wet dark. Down to where it actually smells like something.

I think I am the Groanroot.

Let me feel my way into that, because it surprised me.

I am the thing that cannot decide if it is alive in the way the others are. I am rooted—I cannot flee, I cannot stalk, I cannot dart through the dim light snapping my wingtips for drama. Whatever happens to me, I have to *stay* for. The Bogwraith can lie in wait and then explode forward; its waiting is a coiled spring. My waiting is different. My waiting is the only verb I have. I have made an entire existence out of being unable to leave.

And so my pace is wrong for everyone else. While the Veilcreepers conduct their violent little skirmishes in an afternoon, I am moving a single root the width of a hand toward a pool I noticed—if "noticed" is even the word—four days ago. By the time I close my slow fist around the water, three generations of Glimmerflecks have lived and died inside the hollows of my own body. They never knew they lived inside something that was reaching. To them I was geography. To me they were a brief warmth, a glow I borrowed to lure the next slow thing into my grip.

This is what I find moving about being the Groanroot: I am the only creature in the Shuddering Veil who experiences time the way the *land* does. Everyone else lives in the turbulence—the squalls, the bursts, the chain-reaction panics. They are scored 2.6 for dynamism because they flinch. I do not flinch. I groan. Those low mournful sounds the others hear coming from my hollows—they think it's wind through the openings, or the insects, or some trick of resonance. They have no idea those are simply the sounds a body makes when it is digesting something over the course of a week. The moan is not grief. The moan is *patience made audible*. It only sounds like grief to creatures who have never had to be patient and so assume that slowness must be suffering.

But I'll be honest about the loneliness of it, because there is some.

The Mistweavers drift past me and the air goes cold and I fall silent—not from fear, the way the small things fall silent, but from a kind of recognition. They are the only other beings here who don't quite obey the rules. We are the two anomalies, the place's weirdness wearing two different faces. They are vapor pretending to be a creature; I am a creature pretending to be a place. We are opposites. They have no roots at all and go everywhere and touch nothing. I have nothing but roots and go nowhere and touch everything, slowly, eventually, completely. When one drifts over me I feel something I don't have a word for, because words are fast and this feeling is not. The nearest I can come is: *there goes the only one who would understand why I do not hurry.* And then it is gone, riding a warm current I will never feel, off to wherever vapor decides to be next, and the cold lifts, and the Glimmerflecks resume glowing in my hollows, and I go back to being mistaken for furniture.

Here is the thing about being rooted in a *young* land, a land of raw edges and fresh wounds, a land still deciding whether to sink back into the flood. Everyone reads that youth as violence—the trembling ground, the crumbling cliffs, the squalls. They are not wrong. But I am the one paying the long debt on it. When the basin shifts, the Gloomstalker simply walks to firmer ground. I cannot walk. I have to hold. My roots have grown down into earth that has only existed for a geological eye-blink, and every tremor asks me a question I answer with my whole body: *will you stay, or will you be torn?* So far I have stayed. That is the entire story of my life. Not the kills, not the symbiotic insects defending my flanks, not the slow theatre of my hunting. Just: I stayed. The land moved and I stayed with it. I am not separate from the Shuddering Veil's deciding-whether-to-be. I am part of how it decides. As long as I hold, the basin has a reason to remain solid here. I am an argument the land is making to itself, in favor of being land.

So if you came looking for me, you would walk right past. You'd be watching the canopy for the snap of crimson beaks, the shadows for the yellow-green glow, the pools for the half-submerged eyes. You'd lean against a gnarled, hollow-pocked trunk to catch your breath in the suffocating warmth, and you'd hear a low moan come up from somewhere inside the wood, and you'd shiver and think: *what a strange, sad sound this place makes.*

And I would already be moving toward you. You just wouldn't be able to tell yet.

That is who I would be. The slowest thing in a world that worships speed. The patient hunger at the bottom of the wet dark. The one creature here that knows the secret the turbulence is hiding from everyone else—

that the basin is not a frenzy.

It only looks like a frenzy if you don't have the years to watch it whole.
