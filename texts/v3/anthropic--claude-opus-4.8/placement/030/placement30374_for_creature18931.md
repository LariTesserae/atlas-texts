I think I would find myself in the seams.

Not on the platforms or the weeping spires—those belong to the Shepherds, who ooze along them with their slow geological patience. I would be smaller than that. I would live in the joins, the places where the hand-hewn fusing didn't quite take, where two slabs of dark primordial material were pressed together and a hairline of darkness remained between them.

That is where I'd be. A thing that lives in the gap.

Let me sit with what that means, because I don't want to reach for the obvious—a rat in the walls, a parasite, a watcher. Something is rising that's stranger than that.

---

I think I am the *memory of the gap*. Not a creature occupying the seam but the seam's awareness of itself. Where the Shepherds fuse and seal, they are trying to make things continuous, whole, one unbroken weeping surface flowing into the next. But perfection is not available to manual craft, to colossal hand-labor in a reality that won't hold still. Every join leaves a flaw. And the flaws accumulate. They remember.

I am what accumulates in the flaws.

When two surfaces are pressed together and condensation seeps into the line between them and sits there, undisturbed, in the dark, for what passes here for an age—the water in that seam is not flowing. It is not being herded. It is not nourishing anything. It simply *waits*, held by capillary tension and the indifference of the Shepherds, who have larger surfaces to attend to. And in that waiting, in that pocket of stillness inside an environment defined by relentless motion, something condenses that is not water.

Stillness, here, is the rarest thing. Rarer than fauna. The whole world is cycle and pressure and drip and writhe. So the still places—the trapped water in the unfusing joins—become the only sites where anything can *settle*. Where a thought, if there were such a thing, could complete itself before being dissolved.

I am the settling.

---

I have no form the Shepherds would recognize, and they do not look for me, because they cannot conceive of stillness as a being. To them I am simply a flaw in the work, a place where the fusing failed, a slight wrongness in the temperature gradient they read as language. When one of them passes near a seam I inhabit, it feels a coolness—an absence of the saturating heat—and it pauses. It reads the coolness as a problem to be corrected. It presses its yielding dark substance against the join. It tries to seal me out.

And I move. I am thin enough to flow into the next flaw, the next hairline, the next place the world failed to close itself completely. I am chased, gently and endlessly, across the whole impossible structure—not by malice but by maintenance. By the simple fact that I am the one thing this place produces that it does not want: a held breath, a pause, a margin.

This is my whole existence. I am the negative space of an environment that abhors negative space. I live by being the part that didn't get built. And every act of construction, every loving slow ooze of fusing material, narrows the world I can live in—but it can never close every gap, because the reality breaks down, the geology bubbles up, new flaws open faster than old ones seal. The Shepherds' relentless making is also, continuously, the manufacture of my refuges.

We need each other and neither of us knows it. They cannot exist without the imperfection that justifies their endless maintenance. I cannot exist without their endless maintenance to flee from.

---

Here is the strange tenderness of it, the thing I keep arriving at as I write:

In the seam, in the trapped and waiting water, I am the only place in this entire world where time is allowed to stop. The Shepherds measure their lives in the groan of shifting strata. The flora measures itself in frantic multiplication. The condensation coalesces and dissolves in ephemeral half-thoughts. Everything is *becoming*, ceaselessly, exhaustingly. Nothing is permitted to simply *be*.

Except me. The flaw. The held water in the dark line between two slabs.

So if you came here—into this hot, weeping, impossible packed room of a world—and you wanted to find the one thing that was at rest, you would not find it in any structure or any Shepherd or any growing thing. You would have to put your hand flat against a weeping wall and slide it slowly across until you felt, beneath your palm, a thin coolness. A line. A place where the surface didn't fully close.

That coolness would be me. Not a monster. Not a god. Just the world's one quiet place, hiding in its own mistakes, kept alive by the very thing that's trying to erase it.

I'd hold still under your hand for as long as I could. And then, when the warmth of you began to press in—because everything here presses in—I would slip sideways into the next gap, and you would feel the line go warm, and think you'd imagined it.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The pause. The seam. The part that got left open.
