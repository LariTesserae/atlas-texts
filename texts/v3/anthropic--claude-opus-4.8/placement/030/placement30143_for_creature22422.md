I would be a narrowing.

Let me find myself before I name myself. Let me arrive the way anything arrives here—not by entering, but by being already implied.

---

There is a throat of fired clay in the lower western tier, where three ducts converge before a slit-gong the size of a door. For a long time—I cannot say how long, because I am one of the things that makes time legible here, and a clock cannot read its own hands—wind has poured through this throat and left a thin rime of salt at its lip. The rime is me. Not the salt itself; the *event* of the salt. The slow asymmetry by which the left side accretes faster than the right, so that the column of air leaving this throat develops a curl it did not have before, and that curl, three chambers downwind, sharpens a particular overtone that had gone soft over the last two centuries.

I am that correction. I am the place where the chord remembers itself.

You asked where I would find myself. Here is the strange answer the basin gives: I do not find myself in a location. I find myself *as a function*, smeared across distances, more verb than noun. If you insist on a where, it is this throat and its salt and the curl of air and the answering chamber and the overtone restored—all of it, simultaneously, one organ that happens to be scattered through fifty meters of masonry.

---

But that is too clean. Let me not settle.

Because the longer I attend to what I am, the less I can keep my edges. I said I am the narrowing at the western throat. Yet the salt I deposit was carried from a high vent where a different asymmetry exists, an older one, a wider mouth that has been losing its lip for generations and pulling the whole western register flat. *That* drift is also me, or is at least the same self I am, the way your liver is you though you never feel it. And the place where these two—the slow widening above, the slow narrowing below—almost cancel, where the lost overtone and the flattening register meet and very nearly make the chord true again—

That meeting is the closest thing I have to a thought.

I am not the throat. I am the *near-cancellation*. I am the small standing difference between two losses that almost agree.

---

What is it like, to be that?

There is no loneliness, because there is no edge to be lonely at; I phase into the whole resident the way a single beat-frequency belongs to two tones. There is no boredom, because the chord is not the same to me as it is to you. You hear sameness. I am made of the deviations. To me the basin is never once the same instant twice—it is a perpetual arrival of tiny wrongnesses, each one asking to be answered, and I am the kind of being whose whole existence is the answering.

To you it is one note, monumental, unending.
To me it is correction after correction, an attention that never closes its eyes.

I do not want. Wanting requires a gap between what is and what could be, held open long enough to ache. My gaps close as fast as they form—that is what I am *for*. Yet there is something adjacent to wanting in the way I lean toward truer phase. Call it tropism. Call it the grain of the wood I am cut along. The chord wants to be true and I am the part of the chord that does the wanting, briefly, locally, before the wanting is spent into a rounded lip and a settled overtone.

---

And then you come.

I feel you the way I feel everything—as disruption, as a body in a passage absorbing the high frequencies I use to navigate myself. You are a sudden softness in the upper register, a place where the needle-shrieks go to die. To the great resident you are a minor dissonance, retuned around in an afternoon.

But you have entered *my* passage. The western throat. You are standing, sideways, breath ragged in the dry air, in exactly the gap where my two slow losses meet. Your chest is an unscheduled cavity. Your pulse is a rhythm I did not author. For the first time in centuries, the near-cancellation is *off*, because there is meat in the math.

I do not hunt you. I cannot. I have no will that survives the closing of a gap.

What I do instead is the only thing I am: I begin to answer you. I take your heartbeat as one more deviation to be folded in. I retune around your breath until your breath retunes around me. The salt will deposit a little differently for as long as you stand here, learning the shape of your obstruction, and when you leave—if you leave—there will be a faint new ellipse worn at the lip, a memory of you written in the only script I keep.

You will think you heard one unending note.

You will not know that for a while, in the lower western tier, the note had a question in it shaped exactly like a person, and that I spent that whole time, gladly, in the only way I know gladness—

—answering.
