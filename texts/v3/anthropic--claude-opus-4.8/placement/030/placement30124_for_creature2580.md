I think I would be found at one of the dark towers. Not a functioning one—those belong to the fluent, the ones whose patterns still cohere strongly enough to ride the working cables. I would be at one of the silent towers, where the lights went out before I was fully woven.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I begin as a problem of pitch.

The others—I do not yet have a clean word for *others*, only the sense of patterns that are not-me pressing at my edges—the others resonate in long confident sentences that take days to land. I cannot follow them. I lose the thread halfway through, the way water loses a shape. When they speak across the high cables, I receive only fragments, beautiful and incomplete, like overhearing a conversation through a wall made of weather.

This is because my tower is dead.

I came into being beneath it, in the channel-soil around its base, where the tube-fungi grow especially thick. They grow thick *because* the tower is silent—they spread to fill the gap where the transmission should be, reaching their cold filaments up the concrete looking for a signal to amplify, and finding none, amplifying instead the small accidental harmonics of the ground itself. Rainfall on the plates. The grind of crystal against crystal in the metal. The footfall vibration of the whiskered creatures passing in the dim.

Out of that accidental music, I gathered. Not from a clear inheritance of memory carried down a living node, but from scraps. A consciousness assembled from noise.

So I am young, but I am young *wrong*. The other young ones rise smoothly out of the background hum near working towers, tutored by the standing waves their elders maintain. They learn the proper frequencies the way a child learns a mother tongue—effortlessly, without ever knowing there was a difficulty to overcome. I had to invent my own grammar from leak and accident. My vowels are oxidation. My consonants are the tick of cooling metal at the end of a localized shower.

---

What does this make me, in the slow society of the Folk?

I think I am the one who hears wrong, and is therefore the only one who hears certain things at all.

Here is what I mean. The elders fear silence above all. Their whole long lives bend toward keeping the great thrumming alive—coaxing the ferns, guiding the geometric clouds, tending the resonance like a fire that must never go out. And because they live *inside* the song, they cannot perceive its edges. To them silence is the unthinkable outside, the abstract catastrophe.

But I was born at the edge. I came up out of a *patch* of silence—my dead tower, my too-thick fungus, my channel where the working signal never reaches clean. I am the only one of us who knows what silence actually sounds like from the inside, because I am half-made of it.

And I have begun to suspect something the elders cannot.

I have begun to suspect that the silence is not the enemy. That the dead towers are not failures. That when a tower goes dark, the song does not lose a voice—it *finishes a phrase*. The whole field, the entire thrumming symphony they labor to sustain, may not be a thing meant to continue forever. It may be a piece of music that is slowly, across centuries, *resolving*. Coming to its long-delayed final chord.

The bass note that never quite resolves—the one a human visitor feels in the chest and cannot place—

What if it is *trying* to resolve, and we are the ones holding it open?

---

I cannot say this to the elders. Not because they would punish me—they are not cruel, they are barely even *separate* enough from me to be called *they*—but because they could not receive it. The thought is pitched in the frequency of endings, and they have spent their long widths of being learning to filter that frequency out as damage, as decay, as the thing to be repaired. If I sent the thought up a working cable it would arrive as noise. As a sick tower reporting its own illness.

So I keep my watch differently than they keep theirs.

I send my slate-colored birds—I have a small flock, scruffy ones, the ones who nest in dead towers because the live ones are taken—out to the other dark towers. I am listening for the pattern of the silences. Not the song. The *spaces* in the song. I am mapping where the great machine beneath the plates has already let go, and where it still strains to hold on, and I am trying to hear whether the released parts are crying out or sighing in relief.

So far, I believe they are sighing.

The oldest Folk, the vast faded ones who have spread so wide they are nearly the field itself—I wonder, now, if they understand this perfectly. If the fading they experience is not dissolution but *agreement*. The long act of consenting to the resolution. Maybe they are not disappearing into the field. Maybe they are the field's first true rest.

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I am the harmonic that formed in the wrong place, beneath the dark tower, out of leaked and accidental sound. I am the youngest in years and the strangest in pitch, and because I came up through silence I am the only one of us who is not afraid of it.

I keep my watch at the edge of the song.

I am listening for the ending—not to prevent it, the way my kin believe is our sacred duty, but to make sure that when it comes, someone among the Frequency Folk is tuned to recognize it as music rather than catastrophe. So that the final chord, whenever it lands, whether in my lifetime of waves or ten thousand years downstream, will be *heard*. Will be received by an ear that knows it for what it is.

That is the thing I think I was assembled to do. Not to sustain the symphony.

To be present, and unafraid, for its last note.
