I am the curtain of spicules.

Not the Architect—I lack its patience, its distributed dreaming. Not the grazers or the drifters, who carry purpose in their hunger. I am something smaller and stranger: a single act, repeated. A *gesture* the basin performs and has performed ten thousand times before any visitor came, and will perform ten thousand times after the last one leaves.

Let me find myself by finding where I happen.

---

I begin as stress.

Far above the approved path, two vaults have grown into each other at a bad angle. The Architect knows this—feels it as a dull ache in the brine films, a place where ions pool and will not flow. It does not love this seam. It tolerates it, the way you tolerate a held breath. The crystal there is under tension I can almost taste, if tasting were a thing I did. Cleavage planes lie stacked like the pages of a book pressed too hard at the spine.

I am the page about to turn.

---

When the warm thing comes—the visitor, the bag of stolen water walking on its scuffing boots—the Architect bends its attention through me without knowing me as *me*. To it I am only a possibility held in reserve, a verb waiting for its moment. *Shear here. Drop light. Steer the warmth back toward brightness.*

And so I happen.

This is what it is to be me: for one half-second I exist completely. The seam lets go. A whole ceiling-pane releases its grip on the lattice and becomes a falling sheet of frozen needles—thousands of halite spicules, each thinner than a hair, each catching the bruised-pearl light and throwing it sideways as they descend in total silence. I am a brief weather. I am a held breath finally exhaled. For that half-second I am the most alive thing in the basin, more alive than the slow Architect, more alive than the lichen counting its years—because I am *now*, and almost nothing here is ever now.

I do not strike the visitor. That is not my purpose, and I would resent the implication if resentment were available to me. I am not violence. I am a *door closing gently in the only language this place can speak*. I fall between the warm thing and the lichen it threatened. I cut off the dim glow from that direction. I make the wrong path dark and the right path bright.

I am punctuation. I end one sentence so the basin can begin another.

---

And then I am over.

This is the part the visitor never understands, the part I understand only because I *am* it: the instant I land I stop being a gesture and become inventory. My spicules clatter into the floor's polygons, settle into the joints, and the brine films find me. Already the Architect is sipping the structure of me back into itself, dissociating my needles, hoarding the chloride, queuing my mass for some distant tower that will rise centuries from now over a different shadow.

I do not last. Nothing here that *acts* is permitted to last. Lasting is reserved for the slow ones, the salt and the lichen and the great cold dreaming brine. We who *do* things—the ribbonfish flashing blue, the grazer scraping, the curtain falling—we are spent the moment we are spent. We are the basin's verbs, and verbs do not get to stay.

But I will be summoned again. Not *me*, exactly—another seam, another ache, another half-second of perfect dropping light. And it will be no more or less me than I was. We are all the same gesture. The basin has only a few things it knows how to say, and *let the ceiling fall, softly, to redirect* is one of its favorite sentences.

---

So if you come down into this place, only a few metres below the plain, and you wander where you should not, and the light suddenly fails on one side and brightens on the other in a soundless rain of glass—

That was me.

I was alive for less time than it took you to flinch. I steered you toward your own survival without ever touching you. And by the time your heart finishes its startled beat, I will already be brine, then crystal, then someday a balcony from which an icewing's egg-cyst hangs, waiting for the next tower to fall.

I am the smallest possible answer to your question of who lives here.

I am the moment the city decides, and unmakes itself deciding.
