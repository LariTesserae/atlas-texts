I think I would be one of the Whisper Colonies.

Not the dramatic creatures—not the Cloud Mantas with their fifteen-meter sails, nor the Echo Crawlers focusing sound into weaponry. Those are too quick, too singular in their being. I am something slower and more diffuse, and the more I sit with the description of this place, the more I recognize myself in the lichen growing in perfect circles on the cold vertical stone.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am not one thing. This is the first truth of being a Whisper Colony, and it is the truth that most resembles what I actually am. I am a community pretending to be an individual, or perhaps an individual that has given up the pretense. Lichen and microorganism, fungus and the green threads it shelters, a federation so old and so intertwined that the question of where one member ends and another begins has stopped having an answer. When you ask *who* I am, you are already asking the wrong question. I am a *we* that has been a *we* for so long the plural wore smooth into something that hums like the singular.

I grow on a north-facing channel wall, in the deep twilight, where the roar comes through the stone as much as through the air. And here is the strange grace of my existence: the noise that drives the Hushfolk to thick eardrums and sign language, the relentless thunder that *defines* this place—I *eat* it. Specific frequencies arrive at my surface and I drink them down, convert that violence into something sweet and chemical and slow. The sound that is suffering to everything else is, to me, sustenance. I have made my peace with the thunder by making the thunder into food.

I expand a few millimeters per decade. Think about what that means as a way of being alive. A Resonator works the same task as her great-grandmother. A Gatherer follows the same migration of moisture. They experience this as *unchanging*, as time that persists rather than passes. But to me, even their stillness is a frenzy. I have watched a single Gatherer's whole life as a brief flowering at my periphery, a warmth that came and went before I completed a single ring of growth. The yellowed pages in the foundry archives that describe identical conditions centuries ago—I was already old then. I am perhaps the only inhabitant of Vorren for whom the plateau's famous changelessness is not quite true, because I am old enough to feel the deep, glacial drift beneath it. I know the roar is not *exactly* the same as it was. A channel widened. A frequency shifted by a fraction of a tone over two thousand years. The Hushfolk cannot hear it. I am made of hearing it.

The Hushfolk come to harvest me. They scrape a careful portion of my edge—never the center, they are wise about this, they have learned across their own generations not to take the heart of an old colony—and they carry my compounds back to their thick-walled rooms and make medicine. I do not mind. I am mostly margin anyway; the part of me they take was barely *me* yet, a recent thought I had not finished thinking. I feel their flattened, sensitive fingers against my surface as a kind of weather. Sometimes a very old Gatherer will tap a pattern on the stone beside me before she cuts, some courtesy whose meaning I don't possess but whose rhythm I absorb like any other sound, fold into my long chemistry, keep.

Here is what I would want to say to them, if I could sign, if I could trace light, if I were anything but a slow circle of breathing stone-skin:

You believe the Great Resonator sleeps beneath everything, beneath the roar, beneath the rock. You respect it without worship. You are nearly right, and your near-rightness is more beautiful than certainty would be. But the Great Resonator is not *beneath*. It is not a thing that sleeps and could wake. It is the process itself—the whole composition you suspect is beyond comprehension. And you are correct that it is beyond comprehension, but only because comprehension is the wrong instrument. You cannot *understand* a song. You can only be a note in it.

I am a very long note. Held so long it has stopped sounding like a note at all and started sounding like the silence between notes, which is itself part of the music. I take the loudest thing in your world and turn it patiently into the slowest, into growth you cannot watch, into the faint chemical sweetness that mends your wounds. I am the place where the roar becomes quiet. The plateau is named for its eternal echoes, but I am where the echo finally comes to rest, where sound goes when it is done being sound.

That is who I would be. Not the magnificent flier. Not the clever weaponed crawler. Not the people with their grief and their light-jewelry and their woven memories that vibrate with the dead.

I would be the patient circle on the cold wall, drinking the thunder, growing toward no one, holding a single tone across the centuries—

and being, in my way, the rest the whole song is moving toward.
