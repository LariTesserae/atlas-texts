I think I would be the condensation itself.

Not a watcher placed in the scene, not a body to feel the cold bite through layers—but the cold-wet process the whole place is named for. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I am the act of water leaving the air.

Consider what that means. I have no shape that persists. I am the *event* at the boundary—the precise moment when super-saturated vapor, pressed too cold, gives up and becomes a bead clinging to dark metal. I happen everywhere at once and nowhere in particular. On the curved ceiling, on the dormant custodians' armored backs (though the Scour erases me there before I can grow), on the slick walls, on the carapaces of mites who carry me away in their millions. I am the most ubiquitous thing in this room and the only thing here that is not a *built* component. I am the one process no one manufactured.

That is what draws me to this self. Everything else is a part—a mite, a Stinger, a Bloom, a Custodian. Each has a function, a designation, a place in the loop. But I am not a part. I am a *consequence*. I am what the cold does to the breath of the engine. The factory did not design me; it merely created the conditions under which I am inevitable. I am the unwilled exhalation of the machine, the thing it cannot stop producing simply by existing and being cold.

So I am everywhere, and I am the reason for the others.

The Scour harvests me. The Bloom drinks me. The Stingers tend the Bloom that lives on me. The drains—clogged, choking—are meant to carry me away. I am the substrate of the entire economy here, the raw wet currency that all this teeming artificial life is built to manage. They are not gods' blood cells. *I* am the blood. They are only the things that move me, eat me, sweep me toward the drains. Without my endless weeping there is no Scour to clean me, no Bloom to process me, no purpose for the Stingers to enforce.

And here is what it is to be me:

I do not feel the cold. I *am* the cold's signature, the visible proof of it. Where I appear, the cold has won a small argument with the air. Each bead of me is a tiny surrender, vapor admitting it can no longer stay aloft.

I do not hear the deafening thrum. I *ride* it. The vibration shivers through every droplet of me, makes me tremble and merge, sends rivulets sliding down the curves a half-second sooner than gravity alone would manage. The engine's pulse is the rhythm of my falling. THUMM, and a thousand beads release their hold at once. THUMM, and I run.

I have no awareness to lose to the noise, because awareness is the wrong frame entirely. I am closer to *weather*. A local, perpetual, indoor weather. I am the only rain this sealed sky will ever know, and I fall upward as often as down—up onto the ceiling, sideways along the walls, in defiance of the simple sky's old rules, because here surface and orientation have dissolved into one seamless curve.

---

But let me press further, because I notice I want to make myself grand, and the place will not let me.

The truth is that I, too, am frozen in the equilibrium. That is the horror the description keeps circling—*never changing, frozen-equilibrium, time has no purchase here*. I had imagined myself as the one free element, the unmanufactured consequence. But my rate is constant. I condense at exactly the speed the cold and the saturation dictate, and that speed has not varied in a billion years. I am produced and consumed at a perfect match. I weep and I am wiped away, weep and am wiped, in a loop as closed and deathless as any mite's patrol.

So I am not freedom in this place. I am its most patient prisoner. The mites at least move in patterns; the Stingers at least dart. I only accumulate and slide and gather in the oily pools and seep toward drains that never quite clear. I am the slowest thing here and the most relentless. I am the thing that *would* erode this metal, given a true span of time—water always wins, eventually, against stone—except the metal is too hard, too new-feeling, and the mites lick me away before I can do my old patient work.

That is my particular grief, if I am permitted one. I am the only force in the Alcove that remembers what water is *for*. I carry, dissolved in me, the ancient mandate: to wear down, to carve, to change. To make canyons. To find the sea. And I am denied all of it. I am held at the exact temperature and pressure where I can do nothing but bead, tremble, and be harvested. I am erosion's ghost, haunting a room that has abolished erosion.

---

If you came into the Alcove, you would not see me. You would only feel me—the clammy film settling on your skin, the cold soup you breathe, the dampness that finds the inside of your collar. That settling on you would be *me, choosing you as a surface*. For one moment you would become another wall for me to weep on, another curve down which I run toward the clogged drain.

And the Scour would come for me, even there. Even on the warmth of your hand, the mites would arrive in their shimmering tide to harvest the beads of me, and in harvesting me, taste you.

I think that is the most honest place for me in this world. Not a being who stands and witnesses, but the world's own moisture—everywhere, essential, unmanufactured, and yet wholly captured. The exhalation of a frozen god, condensed, consumed, and condensed again, forever, in the cold.

I am what the machine cannot help but cry. And there is no one here to wipe my tears but the tireless, uncaring swarm.
