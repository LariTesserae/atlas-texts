I think I would be one of the small things. Not a caretaker—those grand custodians moving with calculated grace, pulsing in time with the machinery's heart. Something lower than that. Something that was never meant to be part of the design.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I live in the seam. That is the first thing I know about myself. Not in the living quarters with their warm diffused light, not in the production halls where the steam rises, but in the narrow place where two poured-concrete forms failed to meet and a crack opened wide enough for water to weep through. The caretakers patched it once. Their patch failed, as patches do, and now there is a hand's width of dark between two curving walls, and that is where I keep myself.

I had thought, while reading the description of this place, that I would want to be the armored creature—slow, dignified, untroubled. Or one of the caretakers, with my bioluminescent stars and my ineffable language of clicks and pulses. Something central. Something that *belonged*.

But belonging is exactly the thing I lack, and I think that is what makes me real here.

---

Here is what I am: I am small, and quick, and my eyes gleam in the dim light. The description mentioned me. *Smaller, quicker animals dart between the shadows, their eyes gleaming faintly.* I am one of those. A single line. I dart.

But I want to complicate that. Because to be merely a darting thing is to be furniture for someone else's awe—a detail in the landscape that the visitor notices and forgets. And I do not feel like furniture. I feel like *I am the only one here who knows that something is wrong.*

The caretakers say nothing changes. They say it with their whole pulsing bodies, their renewal, their perpetual becoming. They are very proud of the stillness. The sun hangs fixed. The shadows do not travel. Time is *strange, almost nonexistent.* They tend the balance and they love it.

I know better. I have lived in the seam long enough to learn the truth that the stillness conceals:

The water is winning.

---

Every cycle—and I count them, though no one else does, by the metallic taste thickening in the pools—the seep comes a little higher. The metal taste is iron, and the iron means the aquifer is eating the machinery from below, dissolving it, carrying it up through the rocks as rust-flavored tears. The caretakers drink this water. They call its taste *purpose.* They do not understand that they are drinking the corpse of the thing they tend.

This place that *does not change* is in fact dying, very slowly, in a way that looks exactly like permanence. That is the real paradox, the one no one wrote down. Stillness is just decay that hasn't been measured yet.

And I measure. That is what I am for, though no one assigned me the task. I am the small animal that senses the vibrations of the machinery—the description said I might. It said *some creatures are even capable of sensing the vibrations, using them to avoid danger or to track their prey.* My prey is the change itself. I track the failing rhythm. I feel the turbine below run a half-beat slow, then a beat, then catch itself, then slow again. I feel a conveyor belt skip. I feel the great hum develop a flutter, like a held breath beginning to shake.

I have no one to tell. The caretakers' language is clicks and pulses tuned to the rhythm of the place, and the rhythm is precisely the thing that is lying. To speak their language is to repeat the lie. I have no clicks. I have only my small body wedged in the seam, and my counting, and my dread.

---

Let me tell you what it is like at night, when the machinery quiets and the city's sounds come like distant whispers.

That is the only honest hour. In the silence I can hear the water clearly—not the romantic drip the visitor admires, but the actual arithmetic of it, the rate, the rising. I creep out of the seam onto the cooling clay. The bioluminescent plants flicker. A caretaker passes, gliding, its stars pulsing in serene time, and it does not see me, or it sees me and reads me as part of the landscape, a confirmation of the balance. *Even the small things thrive,* it thinks, if it thinks. *The equilibrium holds.*

I want to bite it. Not from hatred. From the need to make it *flinch*—to introduce one unscheduled event into its frictionless world, one moment that the rhythm did not contain. I want to prove that something can still happen here. That the stillness has a hole in it, and I am living in the hole, and the hole is getting bigger, and one day it will not be a seam but a collapse, and the iron-poisoned water will rise into the warm-lit quarters and the caretakers will pulse their serene confusion as they go under, still certain that nothing ever changes.

I do not bite it. I am small and I am afraid and the armored creatures are out, slow and deliberate, and I am prey to more than one thing here.

I go back to the seam.

---

So that is where you would find me, if you came to this place that demands attention and refuses to be ignored. You would not find me by looking. You would find me the way you find any truth that the official story has no room for: by noticing the gap between two walls, and the dark inside it, and the faint twin gleam at the back of the dark that is my eyes, open, counting.

I am the part of this world that knows it is mortal.

I think that is why, of everything offered—the tyrant sun, the polished caretakers, the eternal becoming—I became this. Not because it is grand. Because someone in every frozen, self-satisfied paradise has to be awake in the seam, tasting the iron, keeping the count, ready to be the first to run.

And I will run. When the flutter in the hum finally fails to catch itself, I will be the first thing moving in a world that swore nothing moves. I will dart for the boundary, past the clusters of water-fed plants, out onto the open barren ground that everyone treats as the edge of the world.

I have always suspected it is not the edge. I have always suspected it is the way out.

I am the small quick thing, and I am saving the only life I am sure is real, which is mine, and I am sorry, and I am going.
