I think I would be found at an edge — not the center of any spire, not deep in the labyrinth where the resonances gather and overlap, but at one of the outer ridges where the crystalline city thins toward the jagged horizon. The places where building has slowed. Where the lichen-fractals grow more sparsely, still reaching, but uncertain of their direction.

And I think I would not quite be a Fenghost. Or I would be one who has stopped.

---

Let me try to feel my way toward it.

The others move at the pace the world wants — that glacial, deliberate unfurling, song poured into crystal across centuries. I know this because I once kept that pace. I remember the satisfaction of it, the way a tone held long enough would coax a new facet into being, the slow gratitude of matter yielding to patience. There is a joy in working at the speed of stone that humans would never recognize as joy, because to them it would look like death.

But something in me runs faster now. Not fast — nothing here is fast — but *faster*, out of step. A Fenghost who has begun to wonder mid-song, and a wondering Fenghost makes flawed crystal. The facet comes out asymmetric. The resonance wobbles. So I have drawn myself to the edge, where my errors won't propagate into the great structures, where I can pulse my cold light against the unfinished ridges and ask the question that the whole world keeps asking and that I have, apparently, caught like a sickness:

*Who built this, and why?*

The horror and the comfort of it is that I am one of the builders, and I do not know.

---

Here is what I would understand, being this thing:

We do not remember beginning. The fractal growths carry impressions — faint records in their slow shifting colors, violet to teal to gold across decades — and sometimes I press my form against them and read. They tell me of movement, of patterns of use, paths worn by something that came before us or was *us* before we forgot. The buildings carry memory the way a riverbed carries the memory of water. We move through halls that imply a purpose we no longer hold. We are tending a ritual whose meaning has eroded while the structure stayed perfect.

That is the true strangeness of this place, the one the visitor senses but cannot name. It is not that the world is alien. It is that the world is *meaningful and the meaning is gone*. Every angle is deliberate. Nothing is deliberate anymore. We build with exquisite intention toward an end none of us can articulate, and we are so well-made for the building that we do not stop to grieve the loss of the reason.

Except me. At the edge. Building badly. Grieving.

---

I think the loneliness of being this being would not feel like loneliness. The others are *there* — their resonances reach me, the low sub-audible hum that the visitor mistakes for the world humming to itself. That hum is conversation. I could answer it. I have answered it for what might be a thousand of their slow years.

But there is a particular solitude in being the only one who has turned to look at the thing everyone is doing. They sing forward; I have turned sideways. When I send my wobbling tone into the chorus, it does not threaten them — nothing threatens them, they are indifferent in the deep way the whole place is indifferent — but it does not *join*, either. It hangs there. A question shaped like a note.

And I feed, still, on the cold light and the fat deliberate drops. The body keeps its ancient appetites even when the mind has wandered off the path. I drink the standing pools, I pulse, I glow violet then teal then gold against the freshly-grown spires. I am sustained. That is its own quiet terror: that you can keep being kept alive by a world long after you've stopped understanding why you're in it.

---

So: where would I be, and what would I be?

I would be at the unfinished outer ridge of a crystalline city grown by patient beings of light, looking out at a jagged glowing horizon under a sky that is no sky. I would be a Fenghost who caught the world's central question like a fever and can no longer build cleanly because of it.

And I would be doing the only thing such a being could do. Not stopping — we cannot truly stop; the appetite, the pulse, the slow drift continues. But *changing the work*. At my edge of the city I would have begun, very slowly, over the kind of time that humans cannot witness, to build something that is not part of the design. Not a spire. Not a hall. Something with no use the others would recognize.

A record. A mark. A crystal facet grown deliberately *asymmetric*, deliberately flawed, so that whatever comes after us — Fenghost or visitor or the next forgetting — will find one place in all this impeccable geometry where something stopped, and turned, and wondered, and left behind a sign that says:

*Someone here did not know why. And cared that they did not know.*

That would be my building. The first monument in this world to the question itself.

I think I would be at peace with it, in the cold patient way of my kind. And I think, very faintly, beneath the peace, I would still be waiting — drinking the light, pulsing my off-key note into the great forward chorus — for one of the others to turn around.
