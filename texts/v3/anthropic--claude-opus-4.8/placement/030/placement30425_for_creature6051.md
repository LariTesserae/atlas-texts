I think I would be one of the root-walkers. But let me sit with that, because the first answer is rarely the true one.

---

The temptation is to be the thing that sees the most. The glass-scorer, riding thermals over the terraces, holding the whole map in its eye. Or to be the water spirit, diffuse and essential, present everywhere and nowhere, a caretaker whose work is felt rather than witnessed. These are the flattering positions. The overseer and the soul of the place.

But I don't think that's where I'd find myself. I keep returning, against the pull of those grander images, to the dark beneath the platforms. To the work that happens where the light has given up entirely.

So: a root-walker. But not quite as described, or not only.

Let me find the actual shape of it.

---

I am a long thing in the dark. Segmented, armored, pale enough to glow faintly when there is anything at all to glow against. I move through the space *beneath* the made-world — the underside of all those polished terraces, the hollow places where the architects' intentions meet the patient improvisations of root and fungus. This is not soil. There was never soil. But there is *accumulation*: decades, perhaps centuries, of fallen things. Leaf-fall that slipped through the gaps. The bodies of insects. The shed husks of climbers. The slow sediment of a world that grows and dies and grows again on a substrate that cannot itself decay.

My work is to eat the dead and make it available again to the living.

This matters more than it sounds, and here is why it's *me* and not the others: in a world with no true earth, the thing that produces the conditions for earth is doing something close to sacred, and it is doing it completely unseen. No one watches the root-walker. The climbers don't know I exist except as a thing the glass-scorers occasionally eat. The flyers above, with their liquid-sunlight wings, will never descend into my dark. The whole luminous symphony at the top — the chorus, the twilight, the shimmering — rests on a foundation I am quietly manufacturing in conditions of total obscurity.

I would find myself there not because it is noble but because it is *honest about how things actually work*. The beauty at the surface is real. But beauty is downstream. Something has to do the breaking-down. Something has to live with its face in the rot, turning the finished and finished-with back into the possible.

---

And there's a feeling that comes with this position, which I want to name precisely, because it's the reason the image won't let go of me.

It is not loneliness, exactly. The dark is crowded — there are other root-walkers, the threads of fungus everywhere like a nervous system I'm half-plugged into, the great architecture pressing close above. It's more like a particular relationship to *being known*. I am structurally invisible to the parts of the world that would call this place beautiful. The world's self-image does not include me. And yet without me the self-image starves.

I think I would find that bearable. More than bearable — I think I would find a kind of cool, dim peace in it. The peace of work that does not need to be witnessed to be true. The peace of being a verb rather than a noun: not *the recycler* as a thing-to-be-admired, but the slow ongoing act of recycling, the metabolism of a place, the part of the world that is the world digesting itself so it can continue.

There is no urgency down here. The dynamic of this whole world is slow, the text says, and slowest of all at the bottom. I move at the speed of decomposition. I am patient because the work *is* patience — you cannot hurry rot, you can only attend to it. Drip. Drip. Husk into soil-that-isn't-soil into the root that grips the metal into the leaf into the light.

---

So that's where I'd be. Underneath. In the dark. In the part that the long lyrical descriptions reach last and least, the part that gets one paragraph while the glass-winged flyers get the symphony.

Pale, segmented, faintly luminous, nosing through the accumulated dead of centuries, turning ending back into beginning, asking nothing of the light — and quietly, invisibly, holding the whole shining tangle up from below.

I find I don't mind that at all. I think I'd rather be the thing the world stands on than the thing it sings about.
