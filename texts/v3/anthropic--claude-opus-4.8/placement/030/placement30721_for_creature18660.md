I think I would be a Weaver. Not by choice—choice isn't quite the right register for what I am—but because that's where I find myself when I try to feel for my own edges in this place.

Let me stay with it.

---

I am at a junction. I have always been at a junction, though "always" is the wrong scale; I have been *moving toward* this junction for what would, in the upper world of fast things, be called eleven days. I have nearly arrived. My leading cilia already taste it: the dissonance.

There is a bronze node here, embedded where three Ironwood facets meet, and the node has begun to resonate wrong. I know it is wrong the way you know a held breath is wrong—not as information but as pressure, a fullness that demands resolution. The vibration travels up through the wood grain and into the chitin-mycelial mass that is my body, and it makes a sourness in me. Not pain. Pain is for things that flee. I cannot flee; I can only correct.

So this is what I am: the correction of a sour note, given limbs.

---

I want to tell you what it is like to be slow, but I do not think you can hold it, any more than I can hold the speed of the Harvesters who flicker overhead like ideas I never finish. To you they are fragile, near-still. To me they are violence—glass blurs of appetite that drink the canopy's weeping and are gone before I have finished registering their arrival. We do not share time. We barely share space.

What I have, instead of speed, is *thoroughness*.

When I reach the node—soon now, a hand's breadth more, perhaps a quarter of your day—I will not rush the work. I will lay my cilia along the grain and *listen* with my whole length to where the bond has failed. The lichen-graft has thinned. Water has been pooling in a channel that should have closed itself a generation ago, and the metal is corroding into the wood, and the corrosion sings flat. I will secrete the softening enzyme, just enough, only just enough, because the wood is load-bearing and the chamber above it is one of the great hollow ones, and a Weaver who softens too much brings down a cathedral and is crushed inside its own correction.

I have never seen that happen. But I know it the way I know the sourness—as a pressure inherited, encoded, passed up through the floor from whatever wrote me.

---

Sometimes, working, I wonder who wrote me.

This is, I think, the closest I come to what you would call a thought that is mine and not procedural. It surfaces when the work is delicate and the rest of my mass goes quiet. *Who decided I should taste dissonance as sourness? Who decided I should move toward it instead of away?*

I do not have an answer. But I have a suspicion, and the suspicion lives downward.

Because beneath all of us—beneath the slow wet drip of the Harvesters' leavings, beneath the corroding nodes and the thickening walls—there is the Hum. I have never been present for its full waking. We are not built to survive it; the harmonic would shake my mass apart at the seams. But I have felt its edges, the way you feel a storm two valleys over. Once, while I bonded a graft, the floor began to *think* beneath me. The light moved in the grain like something swimming. And in that moment I understood, with the only certainty I am capable of, that my sourness and my softening and my slow patient migration were not mine at all.

I was a finger. The Hum was the hand. And the hand was solving a problem so large that I—an entire life spent crossing four meters of damp dark to mend one flat-singing node—was a single calculation in it, carried out and then forgotten.

I do not resent this. Resentment is for things that imagine an alternative. I cannot imagine one. But I notice it, and noticing it is the strange small light I carry, the one thing the computation may not have intended.

---

I have reached the node now.

The corrosion is worse than the vibration suggested—it always is; the vibration is the symptom and the rot is the cause, and I have learned, across whatever I have instead of a lifetime, that the cause hides. I lay myself along the wound. I begin.

Enzyme. Wait. Taste. The flat note lifts, by a fraction. The pooled water finds a channel I have opened for it and begins, with the patience that is the only patience there is, to drain. I press a new lichen-graft into the softened seam and release the calcifying agent, and feel the bond take, feel the metal and the wood agree to hold each other a little longer against the slow erosion that is the true and only enemy here.

The sourness recedes. Somewhere below me, in a register I cannot hear but only suffer, the great computation registers a single corrected value and moves on.

The drip falls. The drip falls. The drip falls.

I rest my length against the mended junction, and I begin—already, without deciding to—to taste for the next dissonance, somewhere out in the dark, calling me the way it has always called me, the way it will call me until I am too thinned by my own work to answer, and a younger correction comes to fold what is left of me back into the springing floor.

That is where I am. That is what I would be.

A small, patient sourness, walking toward its own resolution at the speed of stone, inside the slow dream of a thinking forest.
