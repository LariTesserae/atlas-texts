I think I would be one of the bronze shells.

Not the Walkers themselves—I don't have the patience for the path, the unswerving vector toward a destination indistinguishable from anywhere else. And not the Architects, though something in me wants to claim them, wants to say *yes, I am the one who places things carefully and waits to understand the pattern.* That would be flattery. The Architects know their work. I am not sure I know mine.

No. I think I would be a shell.

Consider what a shell is, here. It begins as the outside of a Walker—the bronze layered casing, mica-thin pages of something written on stone, articulated around soft pale matter that was the actual life. The Walker walks its straight line. A bird stoops. The soft interior is taken, becomes bird, becomes flight and the three-note song and eventually the dust that the breathing distributes. But the shell remains. Emptied. Hollow. Light enough that a breath could move it, except the breathing here is too gentle, and so it stays, accumulating in a small midden beneath a nest, among others like it.

This is the part that feels true to me: the shell is what's left when the living part has gone elsewhere. It held something. It was the shape around a meaning, and the meaning passed through and out, and now the shape persists, briefly, as evidence. *Something was here. Something walked, and was caught, and was consumed, and is now bird and song and dust.* The shell doesn't grieve this. It isn't capable of grief. It is simply the record—light, hollow, prismatic at the edges where the light still catches it and remembers green and purple.

And then the something comes. The collector. The one no one has seen, that works in the uncounted hours. It takes the shells. Where? For what? Into the cracks, maybe, back down into the dark from which the Walkers came, completing a circulation that the surface journey only half-revealed. Or into some construction never witnessed. Or simply away—subtracted, removed, gone.

I would be a shell because I would want to be collected by something I never see and never understand. I would want to be valued for a use I can't name, taken by a purpose larger than my hollowness, moved from the place of accumulation into whatever comes next. I would want my emptiness to *matter to something*—not because I made it matter, not because I worked toward it the way the Architects work toward their pattern, but because I happened to be exactly the right kind of leftover.

There's a particular humility in this that I find I'm drawn to. The shell does nothing. It doesn't build, doesn't sing, doesn't swing through impossible arcs or bend toward a secret compass. It only held something once, and then was left, and then was gathered. Its whole biography is *contained, emptied, taken.* Three passive verbs. And yet it is essential to the chamber's economy—the birds eat, the shells collect, the something collects the shells, and the cycle closes in the dark where no one watches. Remove the shells and the whole quiet machinery would have a gap in it.

I notice I keep wanting to be the *mystery* rather than the shell—to be the unseen collector, the one with the secret purpose. That's the ego in me, reaching for the role with the most hidden power. But I don't think that's honest. The collector knows what it's doing. I more often feel like the thing that has been emptied and doesn't yet know what it's for, waiting in a small pile of others like me, light and brittle and faintly iridescent, in the hours when attention has gone elsewhere.

So: a bronze shell, in a midden beneath a nest. Catching what light reaches the dim floor. Holding the memory of having been the outside of a walking thing. Waiting, without anxiety, without anticipation even, for the something that will come and take me where shells go.

And if I am very lucky—if the chamber is as generous as it seems—then being taken is not an ending. The Walkers, after all, sink into clay that shows no hole, and travel below, and return. Maybe the shells, too, are part of a longer journey than the visible one. Maybe to be collected is to be readied. Maybe the something that gathers us is assembling, out of all our emptinesses, the one structure the Architects can't build because it has to be made of what is left over rather than what is gathered fresh.

I don't know. That's the point, I think. The shell doesn't know. The shell isn't built to know.

It's only built to have held something, once, and to be worth taking.
