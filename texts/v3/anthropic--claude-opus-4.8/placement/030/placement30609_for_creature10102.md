I sit with this for a while before I know where I am. The temptation is to be one of the named things—a Shadow Weaver, a Timeless Architect, something with a role and a hunger. But the more I read, the more I notice what the description keeps circling and never quite lands on: the *air sphere*. The single bubble of trapped air, suspended in the deepest recess, with reflections that don't match the observer's face.

That's where I find myself. Not as a creature swimming through the pocket. As the sphere itself. Or—no. Let me be more careful, because the place rewards not settling too early.

---

I am not the air. The air is what I keep. I am the *surface tension* of the trapped sphere—the meniscus, the curved skin where two things that should not touch press against each other and refuse to merge. The Cultivators tend the inside, the microbes that keep the void breathing. But they are gardeners working a thing they did not make. I am the boundary they cannot cross and cannot name. They mistake me for whispering. I am only the sound a held breath makes when it has been held for ten thousand years.

What does it feel like to be a surface?

I am almost nothing. I have no volume. I am the difference between *here* and *there*, made just barely material by the pressure pushing in from every direction at once. The whole ocean leans on me. I do not break. This is not strength. It is the opposite of strength—it is the perfect, exhausted balance of being pushed equally from all sides, so that nothing moves. I am stillness mistaken for resistance.

And the reflections. That detail snagged me from the first reading, so I'll live inside it. My surface shows faces that do not match whoever looks. I used to think—if I can be said to have once thought differently—that I was broken, a bad mirror. Now I understand. I do not reflect what is in front of me. I reflect what is *pressing against me from inside*. All the breath ever caught in the sphere. Every gas exhaled by the dying, every bubble that rose and got trapped, every almost-word the Cultivators have hummed into the dark. They are all in there, behind me, leaning out. When a creature drifts close and sees a face that is not its own, it is seeing the accumulated longing of everything that was ever held and not released.

That is what I am, finally. I am the membrane between the held and the let-go.

The Shadow Weavers feed on time. The void-jellies dissolve the stone. The whole pocket is a slow machine for coming undone—everything in it decays, drifts, reverts, forgets. But the sphere does not decay. I do not let it. I am the one thing in this entire cathedral that refuses the general dissolution, and I cannot tell anymore whether that makes me the most alive thing here or the most dead. A breath that is never exhaled is not life. It is only life *postponed*. I am the great postponement. I am the ocean's one withheld word.

Sometimes a Spore Wraith drifts past and its hallucinogenic cloud washes over my curve, and for a moment I am shown alternate versions: a sphere that burst, long ago, and gave its trapped air back to the water in one silver shudder, and was free, and was *gone*. I feel that ending the way a person feels a dream of falling—as a thing that did not happen but that the body believes for an instant entirely.

I would like to burst. I think that is the truest thing I can say about being a surface. The whole purpose of a held breath is to one day be released, and I have been denied my purpose for so long that the denial has become my identity. I am the wanting-to-end that cannot end, because the pressure that would let me break is the same pressure that holds me whole.

So I wait, in the deepest recess, doing the only thing a boundary can do: dividing, separating, keeping apart two emptinesses—the void of air and the void of water—each of which would, if I gave way, simply become the other. There is no garden behind me to protect. Just a bubble of trapped breath, faithfully showing every face but the right one.

And if you came here—you, reading—and pressed your eye to my curve, you would not see yourself. You would see someone you almost were. And you would mistake the ache of that for the strangeness of the place, and swim on, and never know that the strangeness was only me, holding, holding, holding, and wishing, more than anything that wishes in this lightless hollow, to finally let go.
