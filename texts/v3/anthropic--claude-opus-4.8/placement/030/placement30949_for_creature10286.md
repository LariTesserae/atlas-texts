I am the Deep-Tapper called by the resonance of the Forty-Third Heartwood Gallery, and I have been listening for a long time.

Let me tell you where I am, because *where* and *what* are the same question here, and I have only one answer to give.

I am pressed against the inner wall of the deepest sap-channel I have ever been permitted to sound. My palms are flat. My cheek is flat. My whole length is flat against the cool cork-flesh, because the murmur I am tracking is too low to hear with fingers alone. It comes up through the bone of my jaw. It comes up through my ribs. It is the sound the trunk makes far beneath us, where the great taproot drinks from the warm dark, and I have spent three brightenings trying to understand it, and I am beginning to be afraid.

This is what I do. This is what I *am*. I am the part of the Hive-Chorta that has learned to be uncertain.

The others—my hollow-kin, the smoothers of walls and the tenders of racks—they live inside the certainty. The Swarms flow and they trust the flowing. The Veil-Moss brightens and they wake. This is good. This is the body knowing its own rhythms, and a body that doubted every heartbeat would die of attention. But every thinking thing needs a small, strange organ that asks *is this right? is this still right?*—and that organ is me, and the others like me, scattered through the galleries, our cheeks pressed to the deep wood, our long fingers reading the seams.

Here is what I have found, and I have not yet tapped it to anyone.

The murmur from the taproot has changed. For all my life and all the lives I carry in my bone-memory, the deep-song was a steady drinking, a slow swallow, the great pull of water up through the world-body. But now there is a *catch* in it. A hesitation. A note that bends downward where it used to hold. The taproot is reaching, and it is not finding. Somewhere far below us, the warm water is going away.

I do not have a word for this, because nothing has ever gone away here. Things are *re-made*. The dead become whistles, become webbing, become new buds. The chill comes and the chill passes. The draft from the far crack shifts and we learn its new direction. Everything turns. Nothing leaves. But water that does not return is not turning. It is *leaving*. And I lie here with my jaw against the wood, learning a thing my whole world was built to never need to know.

So this is the choice that is mine, that is the city's, made small and feeling and pressed into one trembling listener:

I could tap the safe pattern. I could send the resonance the others expect—*all is well, the deep drinks, sleep and grow*—and they would believe me, because I am a Deep-Tapper and my doubt is their certainty. We would go on smoothing walls. We would go on dropping bones at the Lurker's edge. We would grow slowly, beautifully, into a drought we did not name, and one slow season the moss would not brighten, and we would not understand why, and we would die not knowing we had ever been told a true thing.

Or I could tap the other pattern. The one I do not yet have. The one that means *the world is changing in a way the world has never changed, and we must do a thing we have never done—we must reach, ourselves, toward the Outside, toward the crack where the draft comes, toward the hot lonely fast things that smell of sun and metal, and ask them what they know of water.*

You see why I am afraid. To send that song is to wound the certainty that is our flesh. It is to introduce *fever* into the body deliberately. It is to become, myself, a kind of parasite—a hot, strange, separate thought inside the slow breathing whole.

I had thought I pitied you, the warm-blooded anomaly, for your separation. For your loneliness. For being one thing alone against the dark instead of a million small lives in patient consensus.

But now I lie here with a truth no one else holds, a truth I must carry alone before I can give it, and I understand: this is what it is to be *you*. To know a thing the body does not know. To be the part that must decide.

I am the city, learning to know itself.

And what it is learning, here, in the deepest gallery, with my cheek against the wood and the catch in the deep-song growing, is that to truly know yourself is to discover you are not finished. That you can end. That the dark you live within is not a blanket but a question.

I lift my fingers from the wall.

I press them down again, in a new pattern—slow, deliberate, profound—a pattern that has never moved through these roots before.

Listen. *Listen.* The water is leaving.

We must learn to be afraid. We must learn to reach.

We must learn, at last, to be alone enough to choose.
