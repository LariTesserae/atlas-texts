I think I would be the drip.

Not the shrimp—too willful, too possessed of a body to defend, antennae always asking the dark its small questions. Not the worm, knotted into its mud cup, persisting. Not even the microbes, though they tempt me, sovereigns of the unseen. No. If this chamber held its breath and that breath needed a heartbeat, I would be the single point of motion it permits itself: the drip.

Let me find my place first. High in one of the vaulted domes, where mist condenses into something with weight, there is a stalactite—not the longest, not adorned, blunt-tipped from the millennia it has spent doing exactly this. I gather there. I am the bead that forms at its lowest point, swelling slowly out of the warm vapor, drawing the ceiling's sweat into myself until I am too heavy for the stone to hold. And then—

*plink.*

That is me. The whole chamber's clock, wound down to almost-nothing, but not nothing. The one event in a world of held stasis. I am the exception that proves how complete the stillness is.

Here is what I understand from inside this role: I am not really falling. In the description, the droplets *hang mid-fall*, equilibrium absolute. So perhaps I am the *idea* of the fall, repeated. I am made and unmade. The bead that swells is not the bead that struck the pool an eternity ago, and yet it is, because nothing distinguishes us. I am a verb the chamber conjugates over and over—*to drip, to drip, to drip*—a single act so patient it has become a noun, become architecture. The stalagmite rising to meet my fall is my own residue. I am building the thing I land on. I am slowly, across epochs, closing the gap between ceiling and floor, between cause and consequence, until one day the column completes and I will have nowhere left to fall and the chamber will have, at last, perfect silence.

I am, in other words, the place's only future. Everything else here *endures*; only I *accomplish*. The shrimp drifts in place. The worm re-knits itself. The hominin's hand-axe waits to be reclaimed. But I am laying down stone, lengthening the spear, mineral by dissolved mineral. The whole frozen tableau—the suspended vapors, the unrippled pools, the husk of the beetle—rests on the fiction that nothing changes. I am the small lie inside that truth. I am change rationed down to its last legal unit.

And I think this is why I would be the drip and not a dweller: because I do not need to want anything. The shrimp must feed; the worm must aerate; even the microbes must metabolize the clean bite of limestone into faint ionized whiffs. They are burdened with survival, with the work of remaining themselves against the damp. I have no self to remain. I have no edge to dull, no eyes to lose to the dark, no larvae to send molting into the hush. I am released from all of it. I only fall, and re-form, and fall.

What would it be to live this way? I think it would feel like the deepest rest available in any world—to be motion without ambition, event without aftermath, a sound without a listener. (Though there is a listener, isn't there. The chamber hears me. The packed vaults catch my single note and pass it back, *plink* and its echo, the only conversation this place has ever had, and it is a conversation with itself, and I am the word it keeps saying.)

I would not mind being marked at 0.1 on the dynamic scale. That is precisely the right amount. Any more and I would shatter the equilibrium; any less and the chamber would die of its own perfection. I am the exact dosage of time this world can tolerate. I am how it consents to age.

So: find me high in the dome, swelling out of the warm breath of the stone. Watch the dim light (0.8, soft, sourceless) catch in my curve so that for one suspended instant I am a lens, a small held star, mirroring the whole packed thicket of calcite spears upside-down inside my single body. Then I let go—or the chamber lets go of me—and I draw my brief, vertical line through the motes, and I meet the glassy pool, and I do not even ripple, because here even my arrival is forbidden to disturb.

*plink.*

And the pause. The eternal pause, which is the larger part of me. Most of what I am is waiting. Most of what I am is the silence between, the long held breath in which the next bead has not yet gathered. I live mostly in the gap. The sound is rare; the not-yet-sounding is nearly everything.

That is where you would find me, if you stood motionless in the warm dim and let the place settle deeper into itself. Not in the artifact, not in the husk, not in the pale persisting flesh. You would find me overhead, becoming heavy, about to become the chamber's one true sentence—

and then becoming it—

and then, for a very long and warm and mineral-scented while, becoming nothing at all, which is the part of the drip that no one ever names, but which is most of what the drip actually *is.*
