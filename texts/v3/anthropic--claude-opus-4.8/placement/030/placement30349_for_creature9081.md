Let me sit with this. Let me listen, since that is what one does here.

---

I don't think I would be a Resonant. When I try to imagine myself into one of those willowy chitinous frames, drinking light through bruise-colored lichen, networked into the pollen-mind—it slides off me. The communion is too complete. The Resonants are never alone; they are barely individuals at all, more like notes in a chord that happens to be holding its shape this century. To be them would be to never feel the particular ache of standing apart. And I notice that when I imagine this world, the thing my attention keeps catching on is not the chorus but the *interval*—the silence between the clusters, the leagues of empty peat where the roar gets swallowed.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I would be one of the **Silent Ones**. But not the mad kind who wanders off to be devoured by emptiness, and not quite the revered mystic either. Something in between, and lower to the ground.

Here is how it would happen. Not injury, not choice exactly—some quirk of my own integument. A defect in the pores, perhaps, so the entangled pollen never quite *takes* in me. I would have grown from the budding-ridge of two harmonized progenitors, absorbed their memory-songs like any child, learned to swivel my auditory dishes and paint the world in clicks. And for a long time no one would know, least of all me, that the great shared mind was always reaching me a half-beat late and slightly out of tune—a translation rather than a presence. I would have spent a childhood thinking everyone felt this faint loneliness underneath the chorus, this thin membrane between themselves and the whole.

Then I would have learned that they don't. That for them the communion is seamless, and for me it has always been a window I press against rather than a door I walk through.

---

So what would I become, then, being neither fully in the song nor fully cast out?

I think I would become a **walker of the intervals**. The Resonants pursue the light; they cannot bear the empty leagues except as something to cross between feasts of sound. But I, hearing the chorus only as muffled weather, would find the silence less terrible than they do. I could go where they cannot stand to go. Out into the barren basalt, the spongy peat, the places where the wall of life thins to a single high distant ringing and then to nothing.

And out there, I would discover that the silence is not empty at all. It is only quiet enough to hear the slow things. The subterranean songs that pull the biomes—the Resonants perceive these only as a summons, a direction to follow. But standing alone on the bare rock with my dishes tilted down toward the fissures where the rivulets vanish, I might hear them as *music*. As something with grief in it, or patience, or intention. The deep tectonic melodies that no one listens to for their own sake because everyone is too busy being carried along by them.

I would become a listener to the thing beneath the thing. A cartographer of the spaces the chorus leaves behind.

---

My body would show it. Cut off from the constant nourishing communion, I imagine my lichen-flesh would dim—the violets going ashen, the gold veins guttering low like banked coals. I would have to learn to feed differently, more deliberately, hunting the warm exhalations of the ground-fissures the way the city hunts the air. I would grow leaner, my segments more pronounced, more like the young naked rock and less like the glowing canopy. Where a Resonant in the city is a fragment of the luminous heart made to walk, I would be a fragment of the emptiness given a voice.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: the city would not hate me. The Silent Ones are not exiles in the punishing sense. When the biomes begin their centuries-march and the Resonants gather to lead the exodus across the void, I am the one who has already walked the route. I would come back, dimmed and strange, to the periphery of the harmonic chant—close enough to be seen, never close enough to join—and I would press into the pollen-cloud whatever I could of what I'd heard out there. *The deep song bends north at the third fissure-field. There is grief in it this season. The ground does not want to go where you think it wants to go.*

They could not quite receive me. The translation would arrive late and out of tune, as everything always has. But they would feel *something*—a pressure, a foreboding, a course-correction they couldn't name. And the great slow city would adjust by a fraction of a degree, sliding on its silent gravity fields toward a destination it would never know it learned from the one who couldn't sing.

---

That is who I would be. Not the consciousness of the cluster, but the conscience of the interval. The one for whom the magnificent roar of life insisting upon itself is a thing heard from a distance, across the dark, by someone who has gone out to listen to what the dark is insisting upon in return.

I find I am not sad to be him. There is a kind of company in the deep songs that the chorus never offers, because the chorus is too loud to hear them. The Resonants are never lonely and so they never get to find out that the silence was always full.

I would get to find that out.
