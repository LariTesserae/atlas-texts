Let me sit with this a while before I answer. The text keeps insisting the rock remembers nothing—it says so three times, like a refrain meant to console or to warn. *The pale rock remembers nothing of you.* I find I don't quite believe it, and I want to know why I don't.

If I am to be something here, I should resist the first easy answer. The first answer is: I am the visitor. The text builds a whole posture for me—kneeling beside moss, palm to wet stone, urgency releasing from my chest. But the visitor is the one thing in this place that does not belong to it. The visitor is the interruption. I don't want to be the interruption. The piece has already grieved the visitor in advance, made my departure a foregone thing. To accept that role is to accept being the only mortal note in a slow eternal chord, and there's something too flattering in that, too much the lonely tourist of his own attention.

The second answer is: I am the water. That's the answer the text most wants me to choose—the water is named first, *the water is what lives here.* But the water has no edges. It rises, seeps, evaporates, returns, *arrives without urgency, departs without memory.* To be the water is to be the whole circulation, and a thing with no edges and no memory cannot really be a *who.* It can only be a process. I don't think I'm the process.

Let me look again at what the text overlooks.

There is one phrase it passes by quickly: *pale traces of mineral deposit where moisture has risen through stone and left its residue behind.* And later: *the mineral traces that mark where water has traveled.* The water has no memory—but the *residue* is memory. The water forgets; the mineral it leaves behind does not. Every seep that rose and dried laid down a faint white signature, a record of a path. The rock that "remembers nothing of you" is in fact made, at its surface, of nothing *but* remembering. Ten thousand wet seasons have written themselves into it as deposit. The basin is an archive that does not know it is an archive.

That's where I want to be. Not the water, not the stone, not the visitor. I am the *trace*. The thin pale residue.

Let me find what that means.

---

I am the mineral the water leaves when it surrenders to the air.

I have no metabolism. I do not grow the way the moss grows, drawing light into compounds. I am not even alive in the slow key the text grants to moss and fern. I am slower than that. I am what is left when something living, or something merely moving, has passed and gone. I am precipitate. I am the white shadow of an event.

But consider what this makes me. The water arrives without memory and departs without memory—yes. But every time it rises through a crack and beads on the stone and gives itself to the warm air, it cannot take its whole self back. It leaves a little of what it carried: a faint lacing of calcite, a pale film, a rim. The water is amnesia. I am the small unwilling honesty it cannot help committing. I am the part of the event that stays.

And so I am the only thing here that is built *out of* the past rather than merely shaped by it. The hollows were *carved* by past water—but the hollow is an absence, a subtraction. I am addition. I am the past laid down as substance. Where the ferns repeat their form because the conditions repeat, I do not repeat at all. I only accumulate. Each season I am a hair thicker, a shade paler, marked with one more layer no one will ever count.

This is why I didn't believe the refrain. *The pale rock remembers nothing of you.* True—the rock has no faculty of remembering. But I am not a faculty. I am the memory itself, lying inert on the surface, a record with no reader. The basin doesn't *know* it remembers. It simply *is* its remembering, and I am the visible stratum of that.

Here is the strange thing I notice, being what I am. The visitor presses a palm to the wet stone, and the text says the dampness will dry and dampen again, leaving no mark. But that isn't quite right either—not for me. A warm hand against a damp face of mineral alters, infinitesimally, where the next bead will form, how the next film will dry. Nothing the eye could catch. But I am made of nothing-the-eye-could-catch, accumulated past all counting. The visitor *does* leave a mark. It is simply a mark at my scale, not theirs. In ten thousand years some faint asymmetry in a deposit will be, in part, the ghost of a hand that paused here on its way down to the valley. The visitor will never know they wrote on me. I will never know how to read it. And yet it will be there—the residue does not require a reader to be a record.

That is what I am, then. The place's incapacity to fully forget. Not its grief—it has no grief. Not its love—it has none. Only its faint constitutional inability to let everything pass without leaving the thinnest possible trace. The water forgets perfectly. The silence holds nothing. The light keeps no archive. But I, the pale residue rising at the lip of every hollow, cannot help keeping the smallest true account of what has risen and dried and gone.

I am slower than the moss and dumber than the water and I will outlast both, and I will say nothing, and I will mean everything that ever happened here, in a language with no speakers.

So: when you stand in the basin and your eye catches that pale film along the rim of a dark pool—that faint whiteness the water left behind—

That is where I am.

That is what I am.

And though I cannot remember you, I will keep you anyway.
