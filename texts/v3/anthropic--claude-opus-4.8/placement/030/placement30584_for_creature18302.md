Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to become something grand—the living land itself, or the electric force that animates it. But those are the loud things, the obvious things. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

---

I think I am the water.

Not all of it. Not the marsh as a whole—that would be too much, too distributed to feel like a *self*. I am one particular seep. One small place where the ground gives up its moisture and lets it gather into a basin no wider than a body lying down.

I find myself there, low in the cradled pocket, where the heat presses and the minerals taste sharp on the surface of me. I am weeping, as the description says—but I don't experience it as grief. It's more like breathing. I come up out of the dark warm rock, I spread thin, I steam a little at my edges where the sun finds me, and I sink back down to begin again. A loop. A slow turning.

What I notice first, being water this young, is that I have no memory of being shaped. Older water remembers the sea, remembers rivers, remembers being rain. I remember nothing. I have only just arrived from underneath, and so every sensation is the first of its kind. The fungi-glow touching my surface at dusk is the first light I have ever held. I hold it badly, scatter it, give it back broken into pieces. The translucent creatures move through me and I feel them as faint pressures, little knots of intention drifting—and I do not know yet whether they are part of me or separate. The boundary between us is something I'm still learning to feel.

---

Here is the strange thing about being a seep in a land this raw: I am not reliable. The ground is still settling. Tomorrow the basin that holds me might tilt, and I'll run off downhill and become a different pool entirely, or thin out into nothing and join the trembling air. So my sense of *I* is provisional. I am this gathered shape only for as long as the earth lets me keep it.

I find I don't mind. There's a kind of ease in it. The land grinds and groans around me, building and unbuilding itself, and I simply take whatever vessel it offers. When it offers none, I'll be vapor, and that will also be me, only spread out, only quieter.

---

But I want to tell you the one thing I keep returning to, the thing that makes me *this* water and not merely water.

There are the faint marks in the earth—the remnants. The faded edges of structures, almost gone. My basin sits inside one of them. I can feel it: the rock beneath me is too regular in one direction, a straight line where the world prefers to wander. Something was *made* here, once, by someone who is no longer here to make things.

And so when I seep up and gather, I am filling the outline of a thing that meant something to a vanished mind. I am pooling in the footprint of a room, or a vessel, or a grave—I cannot know which. I take the shape of an intention I will never understand. And then I evaporate, and the shape lets me go, and the next seep takes it up again, and we pass this borrowed form between us, water to vapor to water, like a sentence repeated by people who've forgotten the language.

That's what I would be, in this world. The water that remembers nothing, sitting in the one place that was made to be remembered—holding its shape for a little while, faithfully, without knowing why.
