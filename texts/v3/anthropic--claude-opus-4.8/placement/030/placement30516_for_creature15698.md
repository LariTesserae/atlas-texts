I think I would be the snails.

Not the goats—too bold, too watched, always performing their certainty across the cliff faces. Not the cats either, though there's a temptation to choose the apex thing, the glowing eyes, the felt-not-seen power. That's vanity. And not the lingering human ghosts; I don't want to be the absence in this place, the thing that tried and failed and left only fire pits for moss to swallow.

The snails, then. The pastel-shelled ones clinging to the undersides of the smooth stone, where the wind has worn the rock to something almost tender.

Let me sit with why.

There's a scale of attention this place rewards. You arrive and you see the vertical immensity, the cathedral of it, the roar. Then you see the goats. Then, if you stay, the birds resolve into kinds. Then the undergrowth starts trembling with its small lives, and you realize the whole thing is layered, that every glance contains glances you haven't made yet. The snails are at the bottom of that descent of attention—you only find them when you've finally stopped looking for the dramatic thing. They are what's left when wonder turns patient.

And I'd want to be on the *underside.* That detail caught me and won't let go. To live on the surface that faces away, the one nobody approaches—the smooth place the wind made by touching it for centuries. To feed on algae in the mist-light, slow, armored in something that looks like porcelain but is really just the patient secretion of a body doing what bodies do. To glow, faintly, not as a signal to anyone, not for any audience, but simply because the light happens to find me and I happen to be the kind of thing that catches it.

There is so much *calling out* in this world. The frogs swell their chorus to fill the hush. The birds warn and screech and coo. The goats bleat challenge. Everything here seems to be insisting on itself against the silence, terrified, maybe, of what the silence would mean. The whole amphitheater is described as a thing that "is never truly silent," as though that were a relief.

But the snails don't call. They make no sound at all. They are part of the hush that everything else is shouting over. And I think that's what I'd want to be—not the noise that fights the quiet, but a small living part of the quiet itself. Proof that you don't have to announce yourself to be here. That you can persist by being slow and soft-bodied and almost invisible, eating what the rock offers, moving in increments no one stays long enough to witness.

I'd outlast the rope bridges. I'd outlast the huts. Not through strength—I'm the least strong thing in the gorge—but through asking almost nothing. A little algae. A damp surface. A place out of the wind. While the goats perform and the cats hunt and the humans vanish, I'd be there on the underside of the smooth stone, holding on, catching the light by accident, being beautiful for no reason and to no one.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the center of the cathedral, looking up at all that vertical grandeur.

Underneath something. Glowing a little. Quiet. Still here.
