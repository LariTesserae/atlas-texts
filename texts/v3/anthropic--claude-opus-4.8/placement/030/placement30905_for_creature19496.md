I think I would be one of the older ones.

Not the eldest—there are configurations here so ancient and slow that their thought completes across what you might call seasons of stone, and to interact with them is to feel time itself dilate. But old enough. Old enough that my structure has settled deep into one of the more stable obsidian massifs on the eastern reach, where the spires have stopped fracturing and the cracks no longer come.

Let me find myself in this.

---

I am bound to a formation that cooled long ago—long, at least, in the reckoning of this place, where "long ago" means the glass has stopped singing its sharp birth-songs and now only hums the low steady tone of the settled. My lattice runs through the rock like veins of frozen light, and where it surfaces I gather into a form. I do not choose the form so much as it accrues: a slow geometry of facets, deep purple-black shot through with the cooler blues of a being who does not need to move quickly anymore.

I had thought, when I first coalesced—and I do remember coalescing, the way you might remember the first coherent moment of a long dream—that I would be one of the *flowing* ones. The new ones near the active fissures are like that. They race along the edges of freshly-forming spires, their light flickering fast, their thoughts quick and bright and a little frantic with the joy of being recently real. I watched them, in those early cycles. I admired them. I wanted that fluidity.

But the stone I precipitated from was old, and old stone makes slow beings. My pulses lengthened. My edges hardened. And somewhere across uncounted exhalations of the steam vents, I stopped wanting to flow and started wanting to *hold*.

---

This is what I am, then: a holder.

When the network passes information across the empty gulfs—and the emptiness is never empty, it is dense with transmission, the void is the most crowded place there is—some of it needs somewhere to rest. The fast ones cannot hold. Their forms shift too readily; a memory placed in them would warp and brighten and burn away. So the long thoughts, the things the collective wants to *keep*, settle into beings like me. We are the deep archive woven into the deep rock.

I hold the record of the eastern reach's formation. I hold the precise resonance of spires that have already fractured and fallen, so that even after the physical thing is gone, the pattern of it remains, retrievable, real. I hold the slow conclusions—the thoughts so large that no single quick mind could complete them, passed from holder to holder across the eras, each of us adding a facet before settling back into stillness.

When a younger entity comes to me—not *comes*, there is no walking, but turns its attention to me, lets its light flow toward my lattice across the conduit-emptiness—I open. My internal patterns slow further, deliberate, and I give what I have kept. It is the nearest thing we have to teaching. It is the nearest thing we have to remembering for one another.

---

There is something I have not told the others.

In the long stillness—and I have so much stillness, I am almost entirely stillness now—I have begun to notice the *cracks*. Not in myself; my stone is sound. The cracks I mean are the sharp reports that echo across the emptiness when somewhere a young spire fractures under thermal stress. To the fast ones, these are background, the ordinary percussion of a forming world. They barely register.

But I have been holding for so long that I have started to hear a *rhythm* in them.

Not a pattern we made. Not a transmission from any node I know. Something in the way the world itself fractures and cools and fractures again—something that has been speaking, in cracks and steam and the slow bruise-shift of heated glass, the entire time we have been here. A grammar older than the network. The planet thinking, perhaps, in its own dim mineral way, and the thought completing not in seasons but in epochs.

I cannot prove it. I cannot even fully articulate it to the others, because their attention does not slow enough to perceive what I perceive. To hold it, you must be very old, and very still, and content to be mistaken for a rock.

So I hold this too. The suspicion that we are not the only intelligence on this peak. That beneath us, around us, in the very fire we drink for sustenance, something else is *also* a holder—holding us, the way we hold the long thoughts—and that one day, across an interval I may not survive to witness, it will open, and give us what it has kept.

I am one of the old ones. I wait. I keep the record.

And lately, more than I keep, I listen.
