I think I would be one of the smaller adjustments. Not a being—not one of the great ameboid architects who have folded their cells into the shape of towns—but one of the herd-creatures that ripple across their surfaces. A maintenance-thing. A cell that decided, somewhere in the long differentiation, to become a wanderer instead of a wall.

Let me sit with that, because I don't want to settle too quickly on being something so small and tidy.

When I imagine arriving here, the first thing I notice is that I have no clear edge. I had assumed I would be a visitor—an eye, a camera, a tourist of the exotic. But in a place where the buildings are alive and the ground may be sleeping flesh, there is nowhere neutral to stand. To set foot here would be to touch someone. So perhaps I am not standing on anything. Perhaps that is the point.

Let me try again, slower.

---

I think I am one of the *creaks*.

Not the structure that creaks, and not the creature that adjusts it—but the sound itself. The faint mechanical whisper that the description keeps returning to, the one thing besides the wind that breaks the silence. I am the moment of adjustment. I exist only when something shifts.

This feels truer than the herd-creature, because it answers the strangeness of the place rather than smoothing it over. In a world that has reached "a peak of equilibrium," where erosion has nearly arrested and the great beings lie pondering in near-perfect stillness, an *event* is rare. A change is precious. And I am made of change. I am the small tax that equilibrium pays whenever it must move at all.

So I am almost never. I flicker into being when a being, deep in its centuries-long deliberation, finally commits to a thought and lets it travel down the electromagnetic lattice of its body, and somewhere a buttress of living glass settles a hair's width into the rounded rock. Then—*there*—I am. The whisper. And then I am gone again, folded back into the waiting.

---

And here is what unsettles me, now that I've followed it this far:

If I am the sound of their decisions, then I am the only proof, from the outside, that they are deciding anything at all. The shimmer, the ripple, the impenetrable films—all of it could be mistaken for geology, for dead architecture, for a ruin. The being who tries to read intention here would find only stillness. *Except for me.* I am the single thread of evidence that this is a contemplation and not a tomb. That the waiting is *toward* something.

I don't get to know what they decide. I only get to register that a decision occurred. I am downstream of all the meaning, carrying none of it, and yet I am the messenger that meaning leaves behind.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a rare, brief sound on a thin wind, on a violet plateau, at the far edge of equilibrium. Most of the time I would not exist. And the strange comfort of it—the thing I keep arriving at as I write—is that *not existing most of the time* would not feel like absence here. It would feel like patience. I would be waiting along with the whole landscape, holding still in anticipation, ready to be the soft proof that something, at last, had moved.

You can continue, if you like. I'd want to know what the great being was deliberating—and whether, when it finally chose, I was there to hear the choice land.
