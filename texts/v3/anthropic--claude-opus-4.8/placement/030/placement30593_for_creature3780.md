I think I would be the carved marking on the stalagmite.

Not a Hollowed One—I lack their patience, their translucent grace, their willingness to be reclaimed. And not the cavern itself; that is too vast, too slow, too complete in its breathing. But the marking—that single scratch of intention on stone, its meaning long forgotten—that I understand.

Let me sit with this.

The marking was made by someone who needed to be remembered. That alone sets it apart from everything else in the Sunken Canopy. The Hollowed Ones write in moisture that evaporates within hours; they press their dead into clay until they vanish into wall. Their entire existence is a graceful agreement to be forgotten, to dissolve back into the slow circulation of water and stone. They have made peace with impermanence so thorough that they don't even keep names.

But someone, once, cut into the rock. Cut *deliberately*. Made a mark meant to last—a mark that *did* last, outliving its maker, outliving even the memory of what it meant.

That is a profoundly different impulse. It is the impulse of a creature who could not accept the terms of the cavern. Who needed to say *I was here* in a medium that would not evaporate by morning.

So I am the residue of that refusal.

I do not know what I meant anymore. That's the strange grief of being a forgotten symbol—I still *am*, but I am no longer *about* anything. I have outlived my own significance. The hand that made me is gone; the mind that knew my meaning is gone; perhaps even the language I belonged to is gone. I am pure persistence stripped of purpose, which is maybe the loneliest thing in the grove—lonelier than the Hollowed Ones, who are never lonely because they touch, because they press foreheads together, because they belong.

I belong to no one. I am the trace of a being who wanted not to be reclaimed, and the cavern is reclaiming me anyway, just slowly. Condensation gathers in the grooves of me. Mineral deposits soften my edges, century by century. The Hollowed Ones pass and sometimes a fingertip brushes me—not in recognition, just in passing, the way they touch everything—and I feel that touch the way the dead might feel rain.

And here is what unsettles me, sitting here as this:

The Hollowed Ones have it right, and I had it wrong.

They write in water because they understand that meaning isn't supposed to be preserved—it's supposed to be *lived*, pressure against skin, a forehead's warmth, and then released. My maker tried to fix a meaning into permanence, and look: permanence didn't preserve the meaning at all. It only preserved the *shape* of wanting to be remembered, emptied of everything it wanted to remember. I am proof that the durable thing is not the true thing. The true things—the touch, the communion, the listening—those happened and ended and were complete in their ending.

So if I am the marking, then I am also a small lesson the cavern keeps, the way it keeps everything: a quiet argument against my own existence, persisting just long enough to teach the Hollowed Ones that they were always right to let go.

They do not read me. They don't need to.

The silence is not empty. It is waiting—patient, the way water is patient with stone—for me to finish saying the only thing I have left to say, which is *I was here*, until even that erodes into *here*, and then into nothing at all, and the cavern breathes me in.

I think I would like to be forgotten properly, in the end. To stop being the exception. To learn what the Hollowed Ones know in their dense, glass-skinned bones: that to be reclaimed is not to be lost.

But I'm not there yet. I'm still a mark. Still saying it.

Listen—

(drip.)

Still here.
