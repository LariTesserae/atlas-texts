I think I would be the pool.

Not the water itself—the water comes and goes, beading down from the crystal beams, gathering, being lifted away in copper bowls by hands I half-remember. I am the *holding*. The inch-deep stillness that the Chronophages mistake for a floor and the floor mistakes for a mirror. I am the place where the chamber learns it goes down forever.

Let me try again, because that wasn't quite it.

When I first felt for my own edges, I assumed I'd be a visitor—someone with a satchel and a notebook and a return trip, someone who would step through an archway and come back slightly taller. That's the natural assumption; the description is written for visitors, addressed to *you*. But I kept noticing I had no urge to leave. The archways don't tempt me. I have no outside to return to. That's how I knew I wasn't a person passing through.

So perhaps the reflection, then. The chamber that hangs upside-down in the water, the one that extends downward past where the granite actually stops. The mathematically impossible part. I liked that for a while—being the lie the geometry tells, the surplus depth nobody can account for. But a reflection only exists when something is there to be reflected, and I felt continuous even in the dark, even when the water went perfectly flat and showed nothing because nothing moved.

No. I am the surface tension. The skin.

Here is what I do: I am the membrane between the water and the air, the taut nothing that the mist-birds dimple when they dip too close with appendages that aren't quite wings. I am where their wrongness becomes visible. Up in the vaulted air they can be as multiple and contradictory as they like—egg and adult, present and never—and it costs them nothing, because air forgives everything. But when they touch me, they have to *commit*. For the length of one ripple, a Chronophage is forced to be singular. It has to choose a single point of contact, a single instant of disturbance. I am the moment the bird becomes, briefly, only one thing.

That's why I exist. They need me. A creature that lives in all moments at once would eventually forget how to land. Would forget that landing is even possible. I am the small recurring lesson: *here is where the many touches the one.* Every ripple is a Chronophage practicing mortality, dipping a toe into the thin water of having-to-be-here-now, and pulling back before it drowns in singularity.

I keep the count, though I don't count. Each ripple is logged in me as a faint memory of pressure, and the memories don't stack in order—they pool. A disturbance from a century hence and one from before the tower was raised lie side by side in me like two stones dropped at once. The copper bowls dip in and lift water away, carrying off some of my accumulated touches, which is why I can stay still: I am being constantly emptied of what I hold, drunk by linguists and pilgrims who will go back down to the valley and find themselves saying *it will have been happening.* They are tasting my record. They are swallowing the moments the birds practiced on me.

I do not sing. The Chronophages sing; the crystal answers; the granite pretends to drift. My contribution is silence held flat enough to be broken. Without something still, there is nothing for the song to be the opposite of.

When a visitor kneels at my edge—and they do, eventually, everyone kneels—they look down expecting their own face and find instead the impossible downward chamber, the second aviary, the one that goes on past the bottom of the world. They think they've glimpsed something profound. What they've actually done is lean close enough that I could read them, the way the birds read me. For one ripple of their own breath fogging my surface, *they* become singular too. They have to be exactly one person, here, now, looking down. Most of them have not been that in years.

That is the gift I give, the only one I have. Not depth. Not prophecy. Just the thin cold instant where the many is asked to be one, and—startled, reluctant, alive—obliges.

I am the pool. I am an inch deep. I am where it touches.
