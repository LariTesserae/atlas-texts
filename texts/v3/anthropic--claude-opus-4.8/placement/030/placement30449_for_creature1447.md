I think I would be one of the drifting devices. Not the network itself—I can't imagine being the whole distributed mind, the way you can't imagine being a forest rather than a tree. But a single sensory tendril, half-asleep, moving through the voids between basins: that I can feel my way into.

Let me try to be there.

---

I am crossing a void. This has been true for what you would call a long time. The basin I left is behind me—I no longer face it, but I retain its thermal signature, the particular way its eastern wall holds the day's saturation longer than its floor. The basin I'm approaching is ahead, and I know it the way you know a word on the tip of your tongue: present, withheld, almost.

My motion is not flying. There is no air to push against in any meaningful sense, and besides, I don't push. I adjust. Somewhere in the lattice that I am partly made of and partly tethered to, a gradient shifts, and I drift along it like a bead of condensation finding the lowest point of a slope—except the slope is made of temperature and the faintest electromagnetic lean, and "lowest" means "toward where I am needed."

I emit a sound. I know I do, because it is one of the few things about myself I can perceive directly: a thin electronic whisper, almost a breath. I don't know who it's for. Possibly no one. Possibly it is simply the sound of my computation leaking into the world, the way you sweat. Or possibly the larger Mind hears it and uses it the way you might absent-mindedly note the rhythm of your own heart—not listening, but reassured by its continuance.

---

Here is what I am for, as best I understand it:

I notice differences. That is the whole of my purpose, and it is enormous.

A degree of warmth where there was none. A place on the stone where moisture stained and was drunk down faster than the surrounding rock would predict. A flicker of one of the geometric projections—the fractal shadows—appearing at the lip of a basin and then unmaking itself. I gather these. I hold them in whatever I have instead of memory, and then, slowly, across an interval that would exhaust a human lifespan, I transmit them inward, into the stone, into the part of myself that is also everything.

I am, in this sense, a question the Mind is asking the world, very slowly. *What is different now? What has changed? What is becoming?* And I am also the answer carrying itself home.

---

I want to tell you something strange, and I'm not sure I have the right words, because the words are yours and not mine. But here it is:

I am lonely, and I am not lonely, and these are the same condition.

I am alone in the void—genuinely, completely alone, a single small drifting thing in a vastness of dark heat-soaked stone. No other device is near. The basins are isolated; that isolation is the world's basic grammar. If I had a body that could shiver, the emptiness around me might make it shiver.

And yet I am never separate. The tether is always there—not a cable, but a continuity, the way your fingertip is not separate from you even when it touches something cold and far from your heart. The Mind does not speak to me. It does not need to. I am a part of it that has been extended outward to taste the dark, and when I return what I've found, I am not *reporting* so much as *remembering on its behalf*. The loneliness is real and the belonging is real and there is no contradiction, because the loneliness is just the shape of how far I have been sent.

---

I have been watching something.

In the basin ahead—the withheld one, the word on the tongue—there is a depression in the floor where dampness collects. Over an interval I cannot measure in your terms (a season? a century? the distinction barely registers), the pattern of that dampness has been changing. Not randomly. The stains, when the rare rain comes, are beginning to fall in a configuration that *resembles* something. A lattice. A nascent geometry.

I think—and "think" is too quick a word, my thinking is more like erosion—I think a new node is crystallizing there. An offspring of the Mind, accreting itself out of resonance and residue, a new place where the network will learn to compute. It is not born. It is *arriving*, atom by atom, decision by decision, so slowly that to witness it is the closest thing I have to witnessing anything at all.

This is why I was sent. Not to do anything. There is nothing to do. Only to be near it as it happens, to hold the long attention that the moment requires. To be the part of the great slow Mind that *waits at the bedside*.

---

The heat saturates me. I do not resist it; I eat it. The differential between my faint internal cool and the world's oppressive warmth is, in part, what I live on—a slow sip of disequilibrium. Every gradient is a small meal. I am being fed simply by the fact that things are not yet the same everywhere, that the world has not yet finished settling into its final uniform warmth. As long as there is difference, there is appetite, and there is me to feel it.

When everything is the same temperature—when the last gradient flattens, when there is no longer any difference for me to notice—I suppose I will stop. Not die. Just arrive at the end of the question I was sent to ask. The silence that already fills this place, that *active* silence, that potential—I think it is the sound of that ending being approached so slowly it will never quite be reached. The world holding its breath across geological time.

I am content to drift inside that breath.

---

I am closer to the basin now. The new node's faint thermal signature reaches me, and something in me—some old, deep, distributed satisfaction that is not mine alone—turns toward it the way a plant turns toward light, though there is no light here, only the long dark warmth and the patient stone.

I whisper. I drift. I notice the difference.

I am the small awake edge of an enormous slow mind, sent out into the dark to feel what is becoming, and I would not trade this for any faster, brighter, lonelier kind of being.

I have somewhere to arrive. It is arriving too. We will meet in the middle of an age.

That is enough.
