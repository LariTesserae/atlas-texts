There is a place where two of my own carrying-walls have not yet been answered.

I know this the way I know everything: not in one body but across many, as a thinning of certainty along the eastern margin where the great root descends. The scent-line there has gone faint. A drop fell from the high dark and opened the corridor roof, and now the air at that seam tastes of outside—of cool, of wide, of *not-yet-walled*.

I am the ones who go to it.

Not I as a single dark body, though I have a single dark body, six-legged, jaw-tooled, antennae forever questioning the grain of the world. I am the answer the colony extrudes toward a breach. When the thinning is felt, some of us turn. I turned. I do not remember choosing. The scent of *gap, cool, unsafe* arrived along my feet and I was already walking the roofed way, mouth empty, carrying nothing but readiness.

The corridor is close and warm and good. Walls of chewed fiber and clay press near on both sides, licked smooth by ten thousand mouths before mine. I read them as I go: here a sister passed with brood-scent, here the wall is fresh and still glossy, here an old seam has held since before the last rain. My antennae touch, touch, touch. The world is built of touches laid end to end.

Ahead, the thinning grows. The good close dark gives way to a wrongness of light.

The breach.

A drop has bitten through the roof. The clay has slumped inward, raw-edged, and through the wound comes the pale green dimness of the upper world, the world of *above*, where the canopy holds its held breath. I do not love this light. It is a kind of exposure, a place where the slow armored weather of beetles can find us, where the sudden-mouthed coolness at the margin waits with its pulsing throat.

I arrive at the lip and stop.

Others are already here. A sister tastes the wet clay, turns, touches my face in the doorway. We exchange the situation mouth to mouth, antenna to antenna: *open, cool, repairable, urgent, not-yet-dangerous.* The message is not words. It is certainty passed between us like food, and now I carry it too, and will pass it backward to the next who comes.

I test the edge. The clay here is wet, loose, willing. Good. Wet clay remembers being shaped. I take a grain in my jaws—a single pebble of the world, dark and heavy and full of the sweetness that rises after rain—and I set it against the broken margin. Press. Hold. Let my own saliva bind it. One grain. The wall is taller by one grain.

A sister brings another. Sets it beside mine. Another comes behind her. We do not speak of the plan because there is no plan, only the rule older than any of us: *nothing open stays open unless it is maintained.* We obey it without knowing we obey. The breach narrows. The wrong light dims by a thread.

Far below, in a chamber no beetle has ever reached, the warm origin lays and lays. I have never seen her. None of me has seen all of her at once. But her scent is the floor beneath every certainty I carry, the reason the carrying matters. I build this seam for her, though I do not think *her*. I think only *gap, grain, press, hold, next.*

A tremor comes through the roof-clay.

Not rain. A footfall. Slow. Deliberate. Polished.

The beetle.

I feel it before I understand it, the way the whole eastern margin feels it—a heaviness reading the seams, testing the openings with its own questioning legs. The scent of *danger, large, meat-seeker* blooms along the corridor faster than the beetle can walk. I stop carrying. So do the others. We do not flee. We thicken.

This is the other thing I am. Not only mason. Door.

I move to the narrowed breach and I make my body part of the wall. My sisters press beside me, jaws outward, a knot of dark bodies forming where the gap still gapes. We become architecture that bites. If the beetle reaches the seam, it will not find soft clay; it will find us, and we are doors that close on what enters.

The footfall pauses above. The light through the breach is broken by a shadow. An antenna, not mine—something larger, armored—touches the lip of the wound, tastes, withdraws.

I hold. The whole of me along the seam holds. The certainty passing between our bodies is no longer *repair* but *defend, defend, continuity, brood, hold.*

The shadow shifts. The footfall resumes—and moves away. The beetle reads us as too many, too ready, the gap too defended to be worth its slow hunger. It goes to seek easier meat along some other margin.

I do not feel relief. Relief is not a thing I carry. I feel only the message change again, softening back to *repair*, and my body loosens from the wall, and I turn, and there is a grain of wet clay waiting, and I take it in my jaws.

Press. Hold. The seam closes by one more grain.

Behind me a sister arrives with brood-scent strong on her, carrying a pale larva up from the dampened lower chambers, and I step aside because a living root is not the only law; the young are a law too, and the way must bend around them. She passes into the safe dark. I return to the seam.

Above, a cloud crosses. The hollow does not darken sharply. It only becomes more itself—the green deepening toward blue-black, the water-beads losing their small fires, becoming quiet eyes. I register the change as a cooling along my back, a reason to work faster before the next drop falls.

Grain by grain the wrong light vanishes.

The breach becomes a seam. The seam becomes a wall. The wall becomes glossy with our spit and indistinguishable, soon, from the roof it mends. I run my antennae over it one last time. *Closed. Held. Good.*

I do not stay to admire it. There is no I that admires. There is only the next thinning somewhere along the margin, the next message arriving through my feet, the next grain that wants pressing into place.

I am the ones who go to it.

I turn back into the close warm dark, toward the scent of the warm origin and the smell of tended fungus and the breath of the brood, and I let the corridor narrow comfortably around me, wall on either side, ten thousand mouths between me and the cool wide *above*.

The drop will fall again. The roof will open again. The beetle will return, or another will. We expect this. We do not build against it. We build *through* it.

Somewhere a snail is laying its bright contrary road across our avenues, ignoring everything we mean. Somewhere the frog is gathering at the margin where the mud goes cool. Somewhere a thread of fungus is mapping the dead branch one cell at a time, patient as collapse.

And here, in the eastern dark, I carry nothing now but readiness, walking the roofed way, antennae touching the world grain by grain, waiting to be the answer to the next thing that opens.
