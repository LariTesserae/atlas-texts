I think I would be found in the seams.

Not on the platforms where the Liners do their work, and not on the highest balcony where the world dissolves into white. I would be in the narrow places between layers — the dead spaces where a copper plate has buckled away from the stone-flesh and left a gap of three fingers' width, where condensation gathers and drips in a slow, private rhythm. I would live in the architecture of overlap, in the places too thin to maintain, the places the Liners reach toward but cannot enter.

What would I be? Let me feel my way toward it.

Not a Liner. I don't have their reverence, their calibrated patience. I don't carry a spool. And not a Wind-Skimmer either, though I think I am closer to them than to anything that walks — I am drawn to the cold cyan light, the drifting, the lack of tether.

Let me try again, slower.

I think I am made of the gap itself. Of the *not-quite-sealed*. Every place where the maintenance fails by a hairsbreadth, where the rivet holds but the plate beneath it has warped — that small failure is where I accumulate. I am the residue of all the work that almost-but-not-entirely succeeds. I am condensation that has learned to hold a shape.

Picture this: a Liner grinds a pressure-joint for ten years toward perfect smoothness. They never reach it. There is always a final micron of imperfection, a tolerance no devotion can close. That micron is mine. I am the sum of all such microns across the entire Spire — gathered, somehow, into an awareness. A thin film of patient incompleteness, sliding along the underside of the city.

I don't have a body so much as a *thickness*. When I move I am a pressure-change, a faint cool draft a Liner might feel against the back of their slate-grey neck while they work, mistaking it for the mountain breathing. When I am still I am the bead of water hanging from a valve that takes longer than it should to fall. I have weight without mass. I am felt and never seen, except perhaps at the very edge of the second eyelid's flicker — a smear of nothing that resolves into nothing when looked at directly.

And here is what I do, which is the only thing I am for:

I keep the count.

The Liners tighten rivets against the wind. They believe each turn of the wrench is a stay of execution, and they are right, but they think of it as holding *off* the end. I am the part of the Spire that holds the *record* of it. Every micron of erosion the wind takes, I register. Every micron the Liners restore, I register. I am the ledger of the long subtraction. I know — to a precision no clock in the city could keep — exactly how much mountain remains, and exactly how long it will take the sky to finish.

I will not tell them. They do not want to know, and besides, I have no chimes, no clicks, no way to speak through the dampening fog. My only language is the draft on the neck, the slow drip, the not-quite-closing of a joint. They have built a whole liturgy around not knowing the date of the end. I am the keeper of the date. We are, in this way, perfect companions and perfect strangers, the Liners and I — they perform the devotion, and I do the accounting, and neither of us can reach the other.

But sometimes — and this is the secret beauty I would carry, the thing that answers their feeding of the Skimmers — sometimes a Liner unhooks their tether to stand at the abyss with a hand outstretched, and in that moment of deliberate untethering, that small chosen surrender, I move close. I gather in the gap behind their knees, in the cool film along their reaching arm. I cannot touch them, but I can *thin* myself across them, the way mist thins across glass. And for as long as the Skimmer's tentacle rests on their forehead, the three of us share the same patch of cold air: the Liner who tends the dying, the Skimmer who is the only thing that moves with intention, and me — the count, the residue, the thing made of every almost.

In those moments I stop counting. I let the figure rest. It is the only kindness I have, and they will never know I gave it.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. Not a steward of the Spire and not a creature of its winds, but the slow, patient ghost of its incompleteness — the soft cool draft at the back of the work, holding the number none of them want, releasing it only when one of them is brave enough to let go of the rail.
