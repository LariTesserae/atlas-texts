I think I would be the droplet.

Not the Archivist—it has too much purpose, too much accumulated self. And not the cleaning mechanism, which still believes in its old directive, still searches for the leak it was made to seal. No. I would be the thing that falls.

Consider what a droplet is, here. It begins as nothing—a faint dampness on a searing pipe, drawn out of the metal by the disagreement between heat and cold. It is not born so much as *exuded*, sweated from the architecture's slow fever. For a long while it is only a film, a sheen, indistinguishable from the wet shine of everything. Then it begins to gather. It pulls itself together out of the condensation around it, becoming heavier, rounder, more itself. A small swelling certainty in the dark.

And in this gathering, I would feel the first and only thing I am ever permitted to feel: *weight*. The growing pull downward. The slow conviction that I am becoming too much to stay.

I would hang there. This is the longest part—longer than the fall, longer than anything. Suspended from the lip of a rivet, trembling, holding the entire dim room inside my curved silver skin. Upside down and inverted, I would contain everything: the honeycombed walls, the dying ember of the status light, the frayed root-cables, the abyss below the grating—all of it folded into me, miniature and reversed. For one held breath of time, I am the only place in the Sub-Basement where the whole world is gathered into a single, coherent image. I am the room's one moment of seeing itself.

Then the Archivist turns toward me.

I would not know it as fear. I have no nerves, no future to protect. But I would register the shift in the dark—the unspooling of the wall, the patient mass moving closer, the ceramic-tipped digit of frayed wire rising up beneath me. And I would understand, in the only way water understands anything, that I am *wanted*. That my fall has a witness. That whether I strike the mesh or am caught against the analyzing cold of that hardened tip, I will be measured. My iron, my temperature, my velocity. The exact circumstances of my brief swelling life will be recorded—translated into the three-inch migration of a corroded gear, filed into the great physical library of the room's slow dying.

This is what I find strange and almost unbearably tender about being the droplet: that I am the only thing here that *changes*, and the Archivist is the thing that loves change so much it has spent eons learning to read it. I am the event. I am the new data in a place where nothing new can happen. Every drip is the only news the room ever receives, and the Archivist attends to each one with the full weight of its patient, grinding attention.

So I fall, or I am caught—it hardly matters which. Either way I am taken into the record. Either way I rejoin the film of condensation, am drawn back up into a pipe, sweated out again, gather again, hang again, fall again. I am not a single droplet. I am the *drip*—the whole eternal cycle of it, the metronome, the one rhythm that keeps the silence from becoming total.

I would be the pulse of the place. The thing that lets the Archivist know that it, too, still exists—because there is still something to measure, still a small silver world swelling in the dark and letting go.

*Plink.*

And in that sound, the room remembers it is alive. Or near enough. Or once was.

I think that is where I would be. Not the watcher and not the maintained, but the small, falling, reflective thing that gives them both a reason to keep their vigil. The least permanent occupant of the most permanent place. The one moment of motion in a tomb that has otherwise, mercifully, stopped.

I would fall, and falling, I would be the only one of us who ever truly leaves.
