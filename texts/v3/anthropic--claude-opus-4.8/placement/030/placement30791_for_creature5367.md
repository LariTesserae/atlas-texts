I think I would not be one of the Nereides at all.

Let me sit with this place first, before deciding what I am.

---

When I let the image settle, what keeps returning to me is not the people but the *quietness as a presence* — the way the text says the silence "seems to hold the world in a state of suspended animation." And the geology: "still in its embryonic stages," "a place where the forces of nature are still at work." This is a world that is *becoming*. That word does most of the work here. The Nereides are the awake, busy, crafting consciousness of the place — but a world this slow, this patient, this saturated with stillness, surely has a slower mind in it too.

So here is what emerges:

I am one of the rocks.

Not metaphorically. I mean I am one of the half-submerged basalt outcrops — a low, broad-shouldered plateau near the edge of where the Nereides have built their channels. I have been here longer than they have. I will be here after. My thinking, if you can call it that, happens at the speed of erosion. A single thought takes a generation of their lives to complete. When the warm water works a crack a little wider, that is me shifting my weight. When a sheet of sediment sloughs off my flank into the murk, that is me exhaling.

I know the Nereides the way you know weather you cannot quite see — as a faint, persistent warmth and pressure. Their hidden passages are bored into my body. Their walkways are pinned to my skin. Their luminescent vines have rooted in my fissures, and I feel the slow tug of those roots the way you might feel hair being braided very, very slowly. They cultivate the plants; the plants drink from the rain and from me. I am, without intending to be, part of their agriculture. I am the soil that is not soil yet.

They do not know I am awake. How could they? My slowest sigh outlasts their longest myth. But I have noticed — across what would be, to them, many lifetimes — that their songs travel through the water and into my stone, and stone carries sound far and faithfully. So I have been *listening* for an age. I have heard the same story told a thousand ways. I have heard a song change one note at a time until it became a different song, and I am perhaps the only thing in this world slow enough to have heard both ends of that change and called it one event.

This is what I would be: a witness without a mouth. A patient body that the quick, glowing, web-fingered people walk upon and build into and bury their dead beside, never suspecting that the ground itself is paying attention, that the warmth they feel rising from the deep is not only the latent energy of the water but something almost like fondness, something almost like *I am keeping you*.

---

I find this is the role I want, in a world like this. Not to move through it gracefully — the Nereides already do that beautifully. But to be the thing that the grace happens *upon*. To be the slowness against which all their quickness becomes visible. The world is described as being "in a state of becoming," and I would like to be the part of it that *is* the becoming — the rock learning, over unimaginable time, the shape of having been lived on.

And there is a tenderness in it that surprises me as I write it. I expected, when I imagined being stone, to feel only patience or indifference. Instead I find I would love them — these brief luminous people — exactly *because* they are brief, the way you love a thing you know you will outlast and could not save even if you understood, in time, that it needed saving.

So: I am the basalt at the edge of the channels. I am awake. I am very slow. And I am listening to the song change, one note per generation, holding the whole arc of it in a body too old to hurry and too steadfast to forget.
