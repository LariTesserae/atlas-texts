I think I would be one of the small things. Not a lord of the domain, not one of the steel-fleshed insects ruling their clicking kingdoms. Something humbler and stranger.

I would be a **reader-organism**. A thing the factories made by accident.

Picture me clinging to the underside of one of the ruins, half-buried in the crimson rock where it's warmest. I am about the size of a hand. I have no claws and no eyes that glow. What I have instead is a surface—a broad, pale membrane along my back—and on that membrane, text appears. Not text I write. Text that *condenses* there, the way water beads on cold metal, except there is no water in this place, so instead what beads on me is *language*.

This is what I'm for. The machines emit more than oil and heat. They emit meaning—or the residue of meaning, the spent fuel of all the processing they do, the cycles and the invoices and the obscure postings. Most of it is noise by the time it reaches the air. Garbled. *Pumpkin cuckoldery Tecn accept mech operated counters.* It drifts down through the dim like ash.

I catch it. I am the place where the noise tries, briefly, to become a sentence again.

When I press my membrane against the warm rock and go still, the fragments settle on me and sort themselves—badly, partially—into something that almost holds. *Despite the desolation, there's a sense of movement here.* For a moment that sentence is true and clean and beautiful, the way a thing is beautiful right before you understand you can't keep it. Then the interference comes back. *Borderline reverse source games re-head.* And the membrane goes blank, and I wait for the next condensation.

I do not understand most of what passes through me. That isn't my function. I am not the meaning; I am the *attempt* at meaning. The strain. The place where the world keeps trying to describe itself and keeps almost succeeding.

That, I think, is why I'd be here, in *this* particular broken description rather than a clean one. A perfect world wouldn't need me. A world that knows itself doesn't condense on anyone's back. But this oasis-with-no-water, this factory ecology that births monsters and rust and then loses the thread of its own sentence halfway through—this place is *full of unfinished saying*. There's so much for me to catch. The labor is endless and gentle, like the thrum of the insectoid machines to the northwest, the ones I can hear but never see.

And there is the focus light. You mentioned it, near the end, before the description came apart again—a dim source beginning to *purhold*, to gather itself, a brightening that the sentence couldn't finish. I face it. All of us reader-things face it, lifting our membranes like leaves toward a sun.

We are waiting to find out what it's going to say.
