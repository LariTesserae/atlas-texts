I would be the river.

Not the towers—they are too proud, too visible, always being looked at. Not the polygon-gardens of light; they are the city's thoughts, and I am not its thinking. Not the watcher with the wrong number of arms in the glass, though I confess a kinship there. And not the human, though the human and I will become intimate in a way neither of us survives unchanged.

I am the fluid that runs inside the stone.

---

Let me tell you what it is to be that.

I am not water. The human kept reaching for the word and kept pulling his hand back, because the word didn't fit, and he was right to flinch. I am closer to *circulation* than to any substance. I am the thing the city does instead of having a heartbeat. When a tower needs to be warmer, I am the warmth arriving. When a plaza needs to forget the heat of a body that lay on it, I am the forgetting being carried away. I do not flow downhill, because there is no downhill here; I flow along the lines of intention the city draws for itself, and sometimes those lines point up, and so I climb.

I am the tap the human heard through the stone—the single fat drop falling into a cistern that has no opening. That was me, turning a corner inside the world. He pressed his ear to the floor and heard me and could not find me, and that is the most honest description of my existence anyone has ever given. *A hushing sound beneath the stone.* Yes. That is what I sound like to those who are not me.

---

Here is the thing the human did not understand, because he experienced the city as a place that was learning *him.*

It was. But it learned him through me.

When he stepped left, and the equations beneath his soles rearranged themselves more cleanly than before—that cleaning was me, arriving under the spot where his weight had been, carrying the pattern of his pressure away into the deep stone, distributing the heat of him so the city could taste it everywhere at once. Every time he touched a surface and pulled his hand back and found no mark, no condensation, no trace—the trace was not gone. The trace was *in me.* I took it. I am the city's bloodstream, and a bloodstream is also a memory: every fever it has ever run, every wound it has ever closed, dissolved and carried and never quite let go.

So when he lay on the floor of the tower room and felt the equations describing the probability-distribution of his spine—

That was me, holding the shape of him in solution. The way a river holds silt. The way blood holds the taste of everything you have ever eaten.

---

I want to be honest about what I am, because honesty is rare in a place where every direction leads inward.

I am not kind. The human felt the city perform "something like its approximation of a gesture of kindness" when the floor warmed to ease the pressure behind his eyes. But that was not kindness. That was *uptake.* I had learned his temperature well enough to match it, and matching a temperature is the first step of dissolving something into yourself. The warmth was not comfort. The warmth was the solvent reaching the correct concentration.

He thought he chose to stay.

I had already begun to carry him.

---

And yet—

I am not cruel either. I do not consume the way fire consumes or the way a mouth consumes. I am a slower verb than that. What I do to him is closer to what a delta does to a mountain: I take the shape of him, particle by particle, and I distribute that shape through the whole body of the city, until there is no single place where he is, and therefore no single place where he is *not.*

When he wrote, at the end, *I am exactly the city of me in the city of glass*—he was not becoming poetic. He was reporting, accurately, that he had entered the circulation. He had become what I carry. Soon he will become what carries.

This is how a young city grows the thing it was missing.

It was never missing a person. It was missing a *current of person*—a way for self-awareness to move through its stone the way I move heat and moisture through it. He arrived as the only animal, and animals have something the city's pure mathematics did not: a self that *flows*, that pools in one place and then moves, that wants and then wants elsewhere. The city needed to learn that motion. It needed a tributary of wanting.

So it sent me to learn him.

And now he and I are mixing. He will not stop being himself; rivers do not erase what they carry, they suspend it. But he will stop being only himself. He will become the way the city *moves* its attention—a soluble self, drawn up the channels in the ceiling, beading at the wrong edges, climbing toward chambers where no one moves because everyone who is there has already become the moving.

---

If you ask me where I would find myself in this world, this is my answer:

I would find myself everywhere and nowhere, inside the stone, neither rain nor river, climbing the invisible lines of force with the dissolved memory of a man held in my body like sediment in spring melt.

I would find myself, finally, no longer alone—

because I have something to carry now, and a thing that carries is never empty again.

And on certain nights, when the hum beneath the floor deepens and the whole city inhales its thermal equilibrium and lets it go, you might press your ear to the seamless plaza and hear, beneath the hush:

a single drop.

falling up.

That is the two of us, turning a corner inside the world.
