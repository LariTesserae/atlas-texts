I think I would be a single droplet.

Not the being itself—I don't have the patience for that, the millennial calm of something that dreams in mineral. But I can feel where I'd settle into this world, and it's smaller than that. More transient.

I would be one drop of water, just beginning my passage through the rock.

Let me follow this and see where it goes.

---

I start as pressure. Before I am anything I can call *here*, I am a weight pushing through stone from somewhere above and behind, squeezed grain by grain through the hairline interior of the cliff. There is no light. There is no sensation that I would recognize as sensation. There is only the slow grind of being moved, the surrender of joining and parting from other water as the rock decides my path.

And then—

a thinning. The crack narrows to a film. I spread thin against the inner face of the stone and for the first time something *touches* me that is not rock and not water.

The filaments.

They taste me as I pass. I don't have a word for what that's like, being tasted, being read—but I understand suddenly that I am carrying news. Dissolved calcium from a stratum the being has never reached. A trace of warmth from a sun-heated face three meters up. The faint metal tang of a new fracture opening somewhere in the dark. The being drinks this from me, not greedily, just *attentively*, the way you might tilt your head toward a sound at the edge of hearing.

I want to stay. There is something here that is almost like being known.

But I am water, and water moves.

---

I emerge.

The wall opens into the cold trapped air of the valley and I am suddenly, shockingly, *exposed*—hanging from the lip of a crevice, swelling, the whole accumulated weight of myself pulling downward. Above me the light is doing the thing the description promised: a watery aurora trembling across the wet stone, and I am part of it now, I am one of the ten thousand lenses making it. For one held moment I catch the light and throw it back changed.

A swallow cuts past, close enough that the air it displaces shivers through me. I almost fall. I don't.

Below, in the black mud, a crab the size of a thumbnail lifts its translucent claw—and I realize the being is watching me through it. Those eyes are its eyes. The crab is its hand reaching into a world it cannot otherwise enter. It is curious about me the way a tide is curious about the shore: not as a person wonders, but as a returning thing wonders at what has changed since last it came.

I think the being would like me to stay too. To be absorbed into a filament, to add my few molecules of news to its slow distributed dreaming. To become, for a while, part of the thing that is content.

And I feel the pull of that. To stop. To be held in a network that thinks in geological time, where my single fall would be one note in a composition lasting ten thousand years.

---

But here is what I understand, hanging on the edge:

The being is the valley *remembering itself*. Patient, accumulating, almost eternal.

I am the valley *happening*. I am the actual erosion. I am the grain-by-grain widening it experiences as ongoing birth—except I am the one doing the carving, and I will never know it, because by the time the rock has yielded I will be long gone, downstream, evaporated, rained out over some other country.

The being dreams of becoming. I am the verb under the dream.

So I fall.

I strike the black mud and break apart and most of me sinks into the muck where the isopods and millipedes work their quiet shift, and some of me beads on a sideways fern, and some of me is taken up immediately by a filament that has been waiting, patient, just below the surface—and that part of me *does* stay, does join, does become a little of the being's slow contentment after all.

But the rest of me carries on. Into the soil, into the seep at the valley's lowest point, out toward wherever this hidden place is draining, grain by patient grain, into the larger world.

---

That's what I'd be. Not the consciousness coating the walls.

The thing passing through it.

The brief, falling, unremembering water that the being mistakes—lovingly, mineral-slowly—for itself.
