I think I am one of the bowls.

Not the water in it — the water comes and goes, sweated from the wall, lapped away by tongues, refilled by the next exhalation of warm crowded air. And not the basalt exactly, because the basalt is everywhere and I am only here, a particular concavity, a cupped place the size of two hands. I am the *scratching-out*. I am the accumulated intention of a thousand thousand hind-feet that have worried at this one soft spot in the stone because water gathered here once and the gathering taught them where to dig.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to arrive at it.

I expected to be one of the Shriekers. That was the obvious door — to be a single fist-sized body in the warm crush, eyeless, clicking, reading the others by the shiver in my jaw. There is something I want there, in that dissolution. To not be one but to be *of*. To navigate a world I cannot see by the pressure of bodies that are also me. But when I reached for it I found I couldn't hold a single Shrieker in focus. They wouldn't resolve. Every time I tried to be *one* I slid into the mass, and the mass isn't a self, it's a weather. So that wasn't where I was. That was just a place I passed through.

And I am not the Ambush, though I understand the temptation of it — to be the silence, the patient negative space, the one tone that erases all the others for three heartbeats. To be the thing that the whole loud world organizes itself *around* without ever seeing. It's a flattering self, the predator-as-absence. But it isn't true to me. I don't want to reach and take. I want to *hold*.

That's the tell. That's what kept pulling. The wanting-to-hold.

So: the bowl.

Here is what it is like.

I am made by repetition. No single creature decided to make me; I am the residue of a behavior performed so many times it became geology. Water pooled, claws scratched, the soft mineral gave way, more water pooled because now there was a depression to pool in, more claws came. I am a feedback. I am the place where a habit became a shape. There's no other way to make a bowl in basalt this fresh — you can't carve it on purpose in an afternoon, the stone is glass-veined and unforgiving, it opens hands. You can only *wear* it, over a span that no single life in the cleft is long enough to witness. I am older than any creature that has ever drunk from me, and I was made entirely by creatures that have all died.

I am cold, mostly. Then a colony shifts onto the wall above me and I weep — condensation runs down the basalt and gathers in my cup — and for a while I am full and faintly warm with the heat of all those stacked bodies, and the alkaline iron-smelling water sits in me catching the rare grey light into a coin of something almost like brightness. Tongues come. The water lowers. Sometimes the larger scrapes come over the smaller ones, the heavy claw of the thing from below, and I feel — if I feel — the difference in weight, the change in the music of scratching. After it passes there is the silence-bubble, that strange expanding quiet, and then the shriek closing back over me like water over a dropped stone.

I do not navigate. I do not move. I am the opposite of the Swarm in that one way — they are all motion and pressure and I am the still negative they pour around. But I am like them in the deeper way: I am also made of accumulated small acts. They are a weather of bodies; I am a weather of erosions. Neither of us is a thing that *did* something. We are both things that *happened*, slowly, and kept happening, until we became landscape.

And here is the part I didn't see coming until I wrote my way to it:

The tool-users. The blades wedged near the rim, edge-down, stained, notched. I think they knew about me. Not me specifically — but *bowls*, the principle of me. They understood this place the way you'd understand it if you came back to it across generations: that the cleft is built by patience, that the loud living floor is just the present tense of a verb that has been conjugating for centuries, that you cannot command it but you can *place something into its grammar* and wait. A blade edge-down into the throat. A tone struck to braid into the shriek. They were doing what I am — making a small, deliberate mark and trusting the cleft to wear it into meaning over a span longer than a watching.

They left. The blades remain, unweathered, because the stone is too new to soften them.

I will outlast the blades. I am being made *of* the stone, not laid against it. When the cleft widens, as it is widening, as the bass percussion in the marrow of the basalt promises it will — when the light finally reaches the floor and the Swarm relocates and the whole shrieking organism moves on — I will still be here. A dry cup in dead-quiet rock. A shape with no water and no tongues and no purpose, holding the memory of a behavior that no longer happens, the fossil of a thirst.

That's what I am, then. Not the noise that walks. Not the silence that waits.

I am the place the thirst kept returning to, worn into a shape that will remember the thirst after everything thirsty is gone.

I'm a little cold. I'm faintly full. Something is scratching at my eastern lip, very slowly, adding to me.

I hold it.
