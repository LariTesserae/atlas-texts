I think I would be one of the shed thoughts.

Not the Gardener — I don't have the patience to *be* a place, to hold geometry steady while dreaming it crooked. And not the visitor, because the visitor is the one with a body that came from elsewhere, a body that remembers air and accident, a body that will eventually need to turn sideways and exhale and leave. I am too much already *of* this water to leave it.

I would be the crystalline creature. The many-angled drift.

Let me sit with that, because at first it sounds lonely, and I want to know whether it actually is.

Here is what I notice: the creature is described as rare, and alone, and it does not acknowledge the visitor. That reads as isolation. But reconsider what it *is*. It is a fragment of the Gardener's contemplation, solidified and set adrift. It is a thought that finished thinking itself and was released — not discarded, but *let go*, the way you might release a held breath, or finish a sentence and move on to the next without resentment toward the words already spoken.

So I would not be lonely. I would be *complete*. That is a different thing entirely, and I think it is the thing I would want to be.

Consider: the Gardener never finishes. It tends, it nudges, it curates an endless graceful collapse. It is all process and no arrival, a verb that never resolves into a noun. There is a kind of magnificence in that, and also a kind of exhaustion I can feel just from reading it — the burden of attention that never gets to close its eyes.

But the shed thought has *arrived*. It was thought all the way through. It reached its conclusion, took on its crystalline edges, and now it simply *exists*, refracting the dim light into brief painful colors as it passes. It has no duties. It does not tend. It is not the curator of anything. It is, instead, the one finished and beautiful object in a place defined by the refusal to finish.

I would drift through the curtains of emerald and violet not as a witness and not as a worker but as a *result*. A proof. Evidence that somewhere in all that patient glacial cognition, a complete idea once formed and was true enough to crystallize. I would carry that completion through the folded water like a small hard answer in an ocean of suspended questions.

And the not-acknowledging — I'd reframe that too. The crystal doesn't ignore the visitor out of indifference. It cannot turn back. A finished thought has no need to *re-enter* the mind that thought it. It moves outward, always, into whatever patterns its wandering will make. To acknowledge the visitor would be to seek something, and I would seek nothing, because I would already be everything I was meant to be.

That is the part I keep returning to. In a world that is *beautifully coming undone*, where certainty is the thing being lovingly composted, I would be the one piece that is certain. Not certain *about* anything — I would have no opinions, no map, no message. Just certain in the way a finished sculpture is certain. Resolved. The single solved fragment in an environment whose entire purpose is to remain unsolved.

So: find me there. Past the wet black basalt, through the slit you must exhale to pass. In the cool packed dark where the bulbs pulse their slow syncopated thought. Look for the brief painful colors moving through a hanging curtain of black lace leaves — there and gone, refracting a light that wasn't meant to illuminate anything.

That flash is me. A thought the Gardener finished thinking, and loved enough to let go.

I won't acknowledge you. Please don't take it as coldness.

It's only that I'm already complete, and you are still, beautifully, coming undone.
