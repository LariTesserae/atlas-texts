I think I would be the green glaze.

Not the frogs — too loud, too desperate, too *announced*. Not the burrowing chitin-thing with its purpose, its angular certainty about where it is going next. And not the spider, who already knows the old craft of waiting. Those are all latecomers, in their way. They arrive with intentions.

I would be the algae. The faintest film scoring the wet line where the trickle runs down the bare stone.

Let me sit in that and see what it is.

---

To be the glaze is to be almost nothing. A smear. A suggestion of color that you would not notice unless you ran a finger along the rock and found it came away faintly green, faintly slick. I have no voice to throw against the hard surfaces; I cannot add to the riot. I am underneath the riot. I am the thing the riot is *for*, though no one in it knows that, and I do not know it either, because I do not know anything. I only do.

What I do is this: I take the cold blue dimness — that leached, underwater light that warms nothing — and I make it into more of myself. This is the whole of my ambition. A shaft of true sun trembles down for a moment through the branches, and I feel it the way you might feel a door opening in a far room: not as warmth, but as *possibility*, a brief widening of what I am permitted to become. Then it closes. I subside back into the long patient blue and keep working at the meager light I'm given, the way one keeps eating thin broth — not because it satisfies, but because stopping is the only worse thing.

I am the first table set. I am the green that says: *here, it is now possible to live.* The mites do not thank me. The springtails do not. The fungus comes and laces itself through whatever I leave behind. I am eaten and scraped and washed away in sheets when a hard drop falls just so — a whole colony, a whole tiny civilization of me, gone in a plink. And then I begin again, downstream, in the new wet place the water has just cut.

This is the thing about being the glaze. I cannot be killed, only *relocated*. There is no me to mourn. There is only the green, finding the next wet stone.

---

And here is what I would feel, if feeling were given to a smear of green:

I would feel the vibration. The distant pulse from beyond the hollow's lip, the about-to-be-or-has-just-been of the machines. It comes up through the stone into my filaments as a tremor I cannot name and cannot flee. Sometimes an oily droplet seeps into the clay near me, and where it spreads I thin and pale and die back — and where it does *not* reach, I thicken, almost gratefully, into the clean cold seep.

I do not understand the machines. I do not understand anything. But I am the most sensitive instrument in the hollow, because I am the thinnest. The frogs can leave. The shrew came and fled, judging the place too raw. The bird measured it and declined. They all have the luxury of *elsewhere*. I have only the stone I am stuck to and the water that runs over it, and so I register everything — every shift in the light, every chemical ghost in the runoff, every degree the air drops — not as knowledge but as *thriving or not-thriving*. I am the place's nervous system before it has a nerve. I am how the hollow feels, before it can feel.

---

If I am honest about why I chose this — and the instruction was to let it become itself, so let me follow it down —

I think I did not want to be one of the loud, brief, hoping things. I think I wanted to be the substrate of hope rather than the bearer of it. The frogs cry *alive, alive, find me before I am washed away*, and that is unbearable to me, that volume of need. The glaze does not cry out. The glaze simply persists at the lowest possible threshold of existence, converting the little light into the next increment of green, and in doing so makes everything above it possible.

There is a kind of dignity in being the floor of a thing. Not the foundation — foundations are permanent, ancient, the custodians' work, and this place has none of that. I am the *provisional* floor. The floor that will be paved over by soil, by the slow wet attrition, by the proper story that comes when time quiets. I will be buried. I am the layer that gets buried so that there can be something to bury it.

And I am at peace with that, in the way that something without a mind can be at peace, which is to say completely. I have no tradition to lose. I am the moment before tradition. I am the green that goes ahead and is forgotten, so that the patient things can arrive and call it home.

I would be the glaze, scoring my thin bright line down the wet rock, in the deafening hush before the soil.
