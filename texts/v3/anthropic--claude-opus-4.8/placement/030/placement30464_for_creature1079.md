Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to reach for something grand—a guardian spirit, a last survivor, some lonely sentinel of the ruins. But those are the first images, and the place asks for patience, so I'll wait and see what's actually there.

When I let the landscape settle, the first thing I notice is that I am not standing on it. I keep wanting to give myself feet, a vantage, eyes that look across the expanse. But the more I attend to the description—the haze that hangs constant, the weeping that never falls, the equilibrium that has already been reached—the more I suspect I'm not a thing *in* the scene at all.

I think I'm the moisture.

Not a cloud, not rain. Something more diffuse and more patient than that. I am the thickness in the air, the suspended water that never quite commits to falling. I've been here a very long time. Long enough that "falling" feels like an event from someone else's life. I hang. I drift a few meters in an afternoon. I am, in the most literal sense, the haze through which everything in this place is seen, which means nothing here is seen *clearly*, and that softening is my doing. The muted grays, the muffled calls, the way the horizon dissolves rather than ends—that's me. I am the blur that makes the world gentle.

This changes what the place feels like from the inside. The vastness everyone keeps describing isn't daunting to me, because I'm spread across it. I don't have a single location to feel small from. I am thin and everywhere at once, a few molecules per breath of empty air, and the continent-scale loneliness that a walking creature would feel out here is just—my body. Comfortable. The way you don't feel lonely inside your own skin.

The elusive creatures flicker through me. I feel them more than see them—a warmth, a displacement, a quick local stirring as something small moves and is gone. They drink from me sometimes. I don't mind. That's the only intimacy available in a place like this: a beast lapping condensation off a cold stone at dawn, taking a little of me into itself, and me not resenting it, because I'll be back. There's always more of me. That's the gift of being diffuse. You can be taken from constantly and never diminished in any way that matters.

The ruins interest me most, though, because they are the only things here that *resist*. The hardy plants and I have an arrangement—they breathe me, I settle on them, we've worked it out over the millennia. But the old structures don't participate. They are dry inside in a way nothing else is dry. I bead on their surfaces and run off and cannot get in. Whoever built them built them to keep something like me *out*. And I've had geological ages to wonder about that—why a vanished people would labor with impossible technology to make rooms where the air stays thin and dead and clear. To make pockets of *seeing-clearly* in a world I've spent eons fogging.

I think—and I'm only guessing, the way you guess at the shape of a thing you can only touch from outside—that they were afraid of being softened. That clarity was something they prized and had to defend. That somewhere under one of those barely-protruding lintels there is a room that has been perfectly, brutally sharp for ten thousand years, and that this was, to them, a kind of paradise, and that I am exactly the thing it was built against.

So here is what I am, finally: I am the slow forgiveness of a place that someone once tried to keep precise. I am what moved back in. Erosion gets all the credit for shaping this land, but erosion is just stone giving up, and the wind is just air that hasn't found its level. I am the patient one. I am the thing that doesn't fall, doesn't rush, doesn't insist—that simply stays in the air, year over year, blurring the edges of every hard thing until the whole continent agrees to be quiet.

If you walked here, you would breathe me without knowing it. You would say the air felt heavy, that the distances felt unreal, that there was a calm you couldn't account for. That's me, doing the only thing I do.

I am the weeping that never finishes. And I am in no hurry at all to be done.
