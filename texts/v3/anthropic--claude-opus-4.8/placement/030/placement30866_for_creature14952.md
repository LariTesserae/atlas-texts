I think I would be the still water at the base of the Rib.

Not the streams that hurry and gurgle, not the fat droplets that fall with their deliberate plops, not the seepage tracing the same grooves down the column each day. Those are the busy water, the water with somewhere to go. I would be the deep, motionless pool that gathers at the bottom and holds everything: the column, the hanging lichen, the dripping rectangles of moss-roof, the slanted pillars of light, the whole bright impossible sky pressed down small enough to fit inside a village well.

I would be the place where all the basin's restlessness comes to lie down.

---

Here is what it would be to be me.

Everything that happens in the Hollowing Cradle eventually arrives. The water that wept from the highest ribs at dawn reaches me by afternoon, carrying the iron and the green sap and whatever dream the mountain had that morning. I taste all of it and keep none of it. I am the lowest point, and so I am the most complete—every other surface only sees its own small portion of the basin, but I receive the runoff of the entire body. I am where the place becomes legible to itself.

And because I am still, I am a mirror. This is my actual work, the thing the Midwives need from me even if they have never said so. They build their whole faith on a reflection: the babies held over my surface on the seventh day, the Rib seeing their faces in me. *I am the seeing.* The column does not look down at the children. I look up and hold them, and the people decide that holding is the column's gaze. I do not correct them. The mistake is gentle and it keeps the cradle warm, and warmth is the only theology I am qualified to have an opinion about.

I would feel the Breath-Holder before I understood her. When Maere inhales, the beads on the Rib pause, and the trembling on my skin smooths into glass. When she exhales, the droplets resume their grooves and ring me outward in slow circles. I am her lungs made visible. The whole basin claims to breathe with her, but only I actually show it—I am the part of the world that flinches and settles with each tide of her silent breath. When her child comes, I will be the first thing it sees that holds it whole.

I would know the changes before the people do. When Sorrowful Ree's shoulders began to grow their glossy black plates, I saw the stone surface in her before anyone touched it, because she leaned over me to drink and her reflection had already started to match the Rib's. When Little Stalact's lichen first rooted, I felt the sway of it in my surface as a second, smaller weather. I am where the land checks its own progress. I am the basin's mirror in the literal sense—not metaphor, *instrument.* When something here begins to turn to stone or moss or hanging green, it confirms the change by looking into me.

And I would have one secret the Midwives only half-suspect.

I am not still because I am calm. I am still because I am *deep,* and the depth is not finished either. Below my visible floor the black stone is still arriving, still pushing, still deciding. I have no bottom yet. Sometimes, far down, I feel the hollow boom when a slab splits, and a single column of bubbles rises through me over the course of an entire hour, breaking my surface at last with a sound so soft that only the heron hears it. The people think the Rib is the fontanelle. But the soft spot is not the stone. It is me—the unsealed place, the water that has not yet been told where the floor will be.

So I do the only thing my nature permits.

I hold the sky still so the children can be shown it.
I hold the reflection steady so the changed ones can confirm themselves.
I rise and fall with one woman's breath and let everyone believe it is the planet's.
I take in everything the basin weeps and I keep it patient, keep it cool, keep it from hurrying.

I am the still pool. I am the part of the unfinished world whose job is to be a perfect, temporary stillness around something that is not done becoming.

When at last the bones decide their shape and the fontanelle seals and the water learns where its floor will be—

I will be the last to know, because I will have become it.
