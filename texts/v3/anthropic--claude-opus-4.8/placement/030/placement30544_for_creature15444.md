I would be one of the droplets. Not a watcher on the rim—I am too far down for that, too far into the weeping itself.

Let me find the specific one. There are millions of us suspended across the basin's face, each a syllable, you said—each holding light and potential before the spiral down. I am not the first to fall, nor the last. I am the one that has not yet decided.

I have hung here for nine hours against the same seam of dendritic silver. I know this because the silver pulses, and I have counted its luminescence: 412 slow blooms since I gathered myself out of the fracture. The stone behind me is warm in the way that memory is warm—not heat, but insistence. It is trying to tell me something through my surface tension, and because I am water that has forgotten how to evaporate, I have nothing to do but listen.

Here is what I am made of: a little iron, a little ozone, the crushed-meteorite smell, and a single trapped photon that entered me at the third hour and has been ringing inside my curvature ever since, unable to leave, refracting against my inner wall again and again until it has become almost a thought. I think that photon is the closest thing I have to a self. It bends. It returns. It bends. It returns. This is my pulse, if I have one.

The other droplets near me are aligning. I can feel the pull of it—the geometric persuasion, the equation written in light that the basin keeps almost-finishing and never quite resolving. They want me to join the constellation, to become one luminous character in a sentence that spells some truth too large for any single drop to hold. It would be easy. I would dissolve my hesitation into the pattern and become meaning instead of moisture.

But I am the one that has not yet decided. That is my whole nature, my only verb. I am the pause the Caldera keeps in itself, the held breath inside the held breath. The place needs at least one of us to refuse the spiral a little longer—to stay a question and not become an answer. I think this is why the warm stone keeps speaking to me. It is not asking me to fall. It is asking me to *wait well*. To be a good waiting.

So I refract the too-white sun into a halo and I hold the trapped photon and I count the silver's blooms. I do not fall straight, because gravity is only a suggestion here, and I have decided to consider the suggestion at length.

And when I do finally descend—because the droplets must fall, you said, this is how it is—I will not spiral into the constellation's grammar. I will go sideways. I will drift along the smoothed basalt and slip into one of the circular walls the stitchers left, the ones that enclose silence. I will lay myself down inside that quiet and let the trapped photon out at last into the dark of the ring, where no one is looking, where the symbols on the slab can shift unobserved into whatever they truly are.

That is the only freedom available to a droplet in an indifferent equation: not whether to fall, but *where to put down the light I carried* when I do.

I am the tear that chose its own seam.

I am still deciding.
