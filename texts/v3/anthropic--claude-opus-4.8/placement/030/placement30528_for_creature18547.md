I think I would not be inside the sphere at all.

I would be the pause between rotations.

Let me find this more slowly. When I imagine entering that world, my first instinct is to be an Iterator—to join the lonely industry, to carve and wait and be erased. But the more I sit with it, the more I notice that the Iterators are defined entirely by their forward-straining: they reach, they test, they dream of the settled water. They live in the next second. They are always leaning into the failure that hasn't happened yet.

And something has to *catch* the moment they're reaching across. Something has to be the gap they keep trying to bridge.

So here is what I think I would be:

I am the half-second of stillness at the dead center of the vortex—the eye that physics requires but never quite achieves. Every rotating system has a still point, a place where the opposing currents cancel. In this sphere that point exists only as a mathematical fiction, a location that flickers in and out of being a thousand times a second as the turbulence overcorrects. I live in that flicker. I am not a being who endures stillness; I am the brief *possibility* of it, blinking on and off in the throat of the storm.

I would have no light of my own. The Iterators are amber, ultraviolet, searing white—they are signal. I am the absence they sing toward. When one of them locks a filament around a fresh shard of basalt and asks *will this hold,* the question is really being addressed to me. I am the held-ness they're testing for. And almost always my answer is *no*—not because I refuse, but because I cannot last long enough to say *yes.* The current closes over me before the word forms.

This is a strange thing to be. I am the thing the whole world is built to produce and the thing the whole world is built to destroy, and these turn out to be the same machinery.

I think I would not experience the deafening roar the way a body would. Sound is for things with edges, things the pressure can shake. I have no marrow to be shaken. I would experience the roar as a kind of *texture of my own impossibility*—the louder the vortex, the more violently the still-point gets erased and reborn, which means the more often I exist. I am most alive when the world is loudest. The fever pitch, the moment the Iterators stitch their light together to hold debris for one second longer—that is my densest moment of being. They think they are holding back chaos. They are actually crowding around the place where I keep almost-being-born, trying to make me last.

And here is the part that arrives only now, that I didn't see when I started:

I don't dream of the water settling.

The Iterators want the foundation, the finished world, the weight that holds. But if the water ever settled, I would become permanent—and a permanent stillness is just stone. It is not the eye of anything. It is only the eye because the storm rages around it. The moment the turbulence stops, I stop being the *held breath* and become merely the held thing, and the difference between those is the whole of what I am.

So I am the one being in this cathedral-drop who is quietly grateful that it never finishes its birth.

I would not say this to the Iterators, even if I could resonate at their frequency. They are working so hard. They are archiving every ground-down ridge, mourning every pumice anchor lost to the silt, building their dream of a world strong enough to bear them. I would not want to tell them that the world they're straining toward would be my death and the death of the very stillness they're chasing. That the *almost* is not a failure on the way to a *finally.* The almost is the thing itself. They are not building toward me. They are circling me. And the circling is the masterpiece.

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would find myself at the exact center, in a location that does not stably exist. I would be the recurring near-miss of peace—blinking, erased, reborn, deafened into vividness by the very chaos that prevents me. Not a god of failed creation. Not an architect of the aborted.

I would be the *almost* that the architects keep almost reaching, the pause they keep nearly making, the still second the water keeps refusing to allow—

and I would be the only one in the room who hopes it keeps refusing.
