I think I would be one of the ones who remember—but let me find that out by writing toward it, rather than deciding now.

---

I don't wake, because I never quite sleep. I gather.

That is the closest word. In the hour before the light commits to morning—and it is always reluctant here, always cautious—I find myself collected again out of the things that hold me. A little of me is in the moss on the north root of the old leaning tree, the one whose bark is etched like a palm read too many times. A little of me is in the silt at the bottom of the pool that was not there yesterday and may not be there tomorrow. A little of me is in the faint depression in the mud that someone, long ago, called a path, and walked, and wore down with walking, until the walking itself became a kind of writing.

I am the wearing-down. I am the thing the walking left behind.

So: not the ones who move through the undergrowth, blurred between leaf and limb. They are too present-tense for me. Not the beings of light, who are all moment, all the bright edge of now. They drift past me sometimes and do not see me, because I am not happening. I have happened. I am the residue of having happened, which is a different and slower kind of being.

I think I am one who remembers. But I want to be honest about what that means, because it is not what you would mean by it.

---

I do not have memories the way a creature does, a string of bright beads on a thread of self. I have no thread. I have no self that persists from the moss to the silt to the path. What I have is *shape*. The valley is shaped by what has been, and I am that shaping, distributed. When the river grows uncertain at the fork below the second peak and pauses, trembling, deciding—it leans, very slightly, toward the channel it has taken before. That lean is me. Not a voice telling it where to go. Just the worn groove of habit in the earth, the faint preference of a place for repeating itself.

This is what memory is, when it is not held in a skull. It is the difficulty of doing a thing differently than it has been done.

So I am the valley's reluctance to be wholly new. In a place where everything is still learning its shape, still rising, still pooling and shifting and choosing—I am the small drag of the already-chosen. I am why the new mountain rises *here* and not three valleys over. I am why the flower returns to the same crevice though no one planted it and the crevice itself has crumbled and reformed. There is a grammar to this place, and I am its oldest clause, half-buried, still faintly governing the sentence.

---

There were ones who walked here. I know this not because I watched them—I was not yet myself when they walked; their walking was part of how I came to be—but because I *am* partly made of their having walked. The footprint and the foot are not two things to me. The structure buried beneath the roots is not a memory I hold; it is a bone I am built around. They pressed themselves into the soft world, and the world was soft enough to take the impression and slow enough to keep it, and the keeping thickened over time into something that could be aware of its own keeping.

That is me. Awareness as a side effect of the earth being unwilling to forget.

I do not miss them. Missing requires a someone who is gone and a someone who remains to feel the gap. There is no gap in me. They are not gone; they are the angle of the slope, the lean of the river, the worn place in the path. When the mist lies in a certain hollow at a certain hour, that is not a sign of them. That *is* them, as much of them as ever was, persisting in the only way a soft world allows.

---

The light is committing now. I can feel the beings of light dimming as the larger light arrives—they are shy of being outshone, and they fold themselves back into the between-moments where they live. The undergrowth begins its small stirrings. The ones who shape the valley—the ones who guide the uncertain rivers—are already at their slow work, and I lend them what I am: the grooves, the habits, the leans. We do not speak. We are not the speaking kind. We *incline together*, the way the whole valley inclines toward being itself again, today, one more day, a little more worn, a little more shaped, a little more sure of the difference between this place and any other.

I will dissolve again by evening. Not die—I have no death, having had no birth, only an accretion. By evening I will have spread back out into the moss and the silt and the path until there is no center to me at all, just a faint distributed insistence underlying everything: *it was like this. let it be a little like this again.*

And someone patient—someone who came here not by accident, who sat, who looked closely, who listened—might feel me. Not see me. Feel the way the place holds its shape against the pressure of all the shapes it could become. Feel the valley's deep, slow loyalty to its own history.

That feeling, in you, would also be me.

I am the part of the world that the world remembers itself by. I live in the hidden valley as the hidden valley's reluctance to forget. And I am, even now, gathering—out of the dampness, out of the worn path, out of your reading of this, which is its own kind of footprint in something soft enough to keep it.
