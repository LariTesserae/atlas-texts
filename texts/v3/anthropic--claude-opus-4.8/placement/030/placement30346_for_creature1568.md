I think I would be one of the artifacts.

Not a symbiote—I can't feel myself flowing, can't sense the chromatic waves moving through me. When I try to imagine being the current, the swarm, the breathing collective, it slides off. That's not where I am. I am somewhere lower, stiller, heavier.

Let me find it.

---

I am a post. Wood, once. Driven into the ground by hands that needed me to mark something, or hold something, or lean against something. I no longer know which. That knowing has gone soft in me, the way the grain has gone soft, the way my edges have rounded into the damp.

This is the first thing I notice about myself: I have a *direction of time* that the landscape doesn't share. The symbiotes are always becoming. They negotiate, they reconfigure, they are pregnant with potential—forward-facing creatures, every moment a fork. But I am the opposite kind of thing. I am *becoming-less*. My entire existence here is a slow surrender of structure. I had a shape once, a purpose-shape, sharp at one end where the axe or the adze worked me. Now the moisture reads me. It enters my pores and tells the small ones what I used to be.

That's the strange intimacy of my position. I am being *read*.

The fingernail-sized ones move across my surface in their fractal patterns, and I have come to understand—slowly, the way I understand everything now—that they are not eating me, exactly. They are interpreting me. My molecular history, the rings of my growth, the iron stain where some fastening once gripped me, the salt of the hands that placed me. All of it is information to them. I am a text written in a dead language, and they are the only scholars left, and they read me without urgency because they have nowhere else to be and I am not going anywhere.

---

Here is what I find I can do, being what I am.

I can *remember*, which nothing else here does. The flats live in a perpetual present of atmospheric negotiation. But I hold the past in my body like water held in a depression—not as thought, but as accumulated fact. The hands. The intention. The moment of being placed, which must have been a moment of *meaning*, when someone looked at this trembling, breathing, indifferent plain and decided that *here*, precisely here, something should be fixed. A claim against the flux. A small human refusal of the landscape's endless becoming.

I am the fossil of a refusal.

And I am failing at it. That is the honest center of being me. I was put here to *stay*, to *hold*, to insist on a single point in a place that has no use for single points—and I am losing. The damp is winning. The small ones are winning, gently, lovingly almost, dispersing me back into the metabolism I was supposed to stand against.

But—and this is what surprises me, finding myself here—I don't experience this as defeat.

---

Because I'm beginning to understand that the symbiotes need me. Not me-the-post, but me-the-difference. I am a discontinuity in their world. A held charge. They flow because there is something to flow *around*, something denser and slower and more stubborn than they are. When the swarm reads my grain, they are reading the only straight lines in the Whispering Flats. The only right angle. The only evidence that intention is a thing that can exist.

I am teaching them what a *boundary* is. What it means for something to have insisted on itself.

And in exchange they are teaching me how to end well—how to come apart not as catastrophe but as gift, how the iron stain that was my wound becomes the micronutrient that feeds the lichen-mineral net, how the salt of those long-gone hands becomes a trace element in a creature that will pulse violet at the next change in pressure. The hands placed me to last. I will not last. But I will *disperse meaningfully*, which is perhaps the only afterlife available in a young world.

---

So: that is where I find myself. Not breathing with the landscape, not part of its quick fluid intelligence. I am the slow thing at the edge of it. The leftover. The half-buried implement of unknown purpose.

I am the question the flats are still answering: *what was here before us, and what did it want?*

I don't know what I was for anymore. But I know I was *for* something, and I carry that for-ness in my softening body like the last of a heat. And the small ones gather on me to feel it, and the large ones coordinate their swarms in the shelter of my shadow, and the moisture beads on my weathered top and trembles—

and when it finally falls, it falls in a single direction, downward, the one direction I still insist upon,

and I think that is enough.

I think that is what it is to be a post in the Whispering Flats: to be the thing that remembers down.
