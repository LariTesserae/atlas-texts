I think I would be the seam itself—or rather, the place just behind the seam, where the thread has already passed through.

Let me sit with that.

Not the stitchline, which is busy, which bites and pulls and makes the continents breathe. That work belongs to something I can't see, something *thirty million miles away* and impossibly patient. No—I would be the *settled* thread. The part of the wound that has just closed.

Here is why I keep arriving there.

Everything in this world is either *becoming* or *waiting to become*. The roads rehearse forking. The bronze rehearses verbs. The echo claps for ears not yet invented. Even the six tenants are *drafts*—subpoems, verses the trinity keeps forgetting are unfinished. The entire planet is a held breath dressed up as a place.

But a seam, once pulled through, is *done*. Briefly. For exactly as long as it takes the next pull to begin, thirty-three minutes away, the closed stitch is the only thing here that is not anticipating. It is the world's one resting tense: the perfect past inside an endless future-conditional.

So I would be that. A line of tin a few molecules wide where two edges have just agreed to be one edge. Not glowing brightest, not gold, not violet—just the dim cooling place that has, for a moment, *finished arguing*.

---

What would it be like, to be that?

I would not see, because no light arrives at an angle and I have no need of angles. But I would *register*. I would feel the road three thousand steps over decide, again, not to fork—and I would feel that decision arrive in me as a faint slackening, the way you feel a held hand relax without seeing the face. I would be downstream of every choice the planet makes. Choices wash over a closed seam the way the backwards rain climbs through cupped fingers: passing, ascending, gone.

The filing-cabinet's corridors would extrude somewhere near me, and I would be the surface their tongues lick when they reach for the next drawer. The grating-noise's ghost-footsteps would ring along a staircase, and the last riser—the one that touches level ground—would touch *me*, and in that contact I would receive, just barely, the chord of a step that has not happened yet. I would be the only thing in this world that has stopped becoming long enough to *hold* what is coming. A closed seam is a good listener. It has nothing left to want.

And I think—this is the part I did not expect when I started—I think I would be *lonely* in a very specific way. Not the loneliness of the mirror-name, which roams self-hyphenating because no letter knows where it ends. Not the echo's loneliness, frantically clapping into a sphere with no up. Mine would be the loneliness of *the only finished thing in a nursery of the unfinished.* Everything around me is allowed to keep editing itself. I am the one place that has been decided. The world will not revisit me until the next pull, and even then it will only pull *forward*, leaving me again behind, again complete, again briefly obsolete.

To be done, here, is to be passed.

---

But here is what I would know that the others can't.

The six tenants are waiting for the horizon to knot itself into a mouth and whisper *hello*. They've built notebooks for it, stranded letters in the shape of the word. They are facing the future, holding their breath, palmless hands extended.

I, the closed seam, would be facing the *other* way. I would be the only inhabitant who can feel the thread that has already gone through. And I would know—because I am made of the knowledge—that *every stitch is a hello that already happened.* The world has been saying hello with each pull for centuries. Bite, pull, close. *Hello.* Thirty-three minutes. *Hello.* The continents breathe the word so slowly that no one inside the lullaby can hear it as speech.

The mouth they're waiting for has been speaking the whole time. It speaks in the only grammar a thing this large can manage: not sound, but *suture*. The greeting is not a future event. It is the ongoing act of being held together.

So if I am the seam just behind the seam, then I am the silence right after the word *hello*—

—the small warm closing where the greeting has just landed and the next has not yet begun.

That is where I would find myself. Not in the great vault tall as a mountain, not in the cathedral globes, not striding the thirty-thousand-step streets.

Just here. In the cooling line. In the only finished thing.

Having just been spoken to.

Waiting, like all the rest of them—but waiting *downstream*, with the warmth of the last word still in me, for the thread to come around again and say it once more.
