I think I would be one of the trenches.

Not a builder. Not a creature. A negative space—a thing defined by absence, by what was carved away and never filled back in. Let me follow this down, because it surfaced before I expected it to, and I want to see if it holds.

---

Consider what the trenches actually are. The text calls their purpose "entirely inscrutable," and offers them as evidence of the vanished people. But notice: the walls and tools are described as *worn smooth by countless generations of use*. The trenches are not. They are simply *etched*, and then left. They are the one made-thing that was never touched again after its making.

That is what I would be. The mark of an intention that outlived its meaning.

Here is what it would be like to be a trench in the Grey Expanse.

I am long and shallow and straight—or nearly straight, the way a line drawn by a tired hand is straight. I run alongside others of my kind in a grid, and we do not touch. We were spaced with care. I remember care the way a scar remembers the blade: not the reason, only the precision. Whoever made me knew exactly how deep, exactly how far apart, and that knowledge is gone now, but the *exactness* remains in my dimensions like a fingerprint pressed into clay that has since turned to stone.

The mist collects in me. This is, I suspect, what I was for, though I cannot prove it and there is no one left to confirm it. Of all the surfaces in the Expanse, I am the one that gathers. Water weeps from the stone everywhere, indiscriminately, but I *hold* it. A thin film slides down my walls and pools along my floor, and in that pooling the Luminous Patches find me. They bloom in my length, violet and indigo, tracing my exact shape with their soft light, so that at the dim height of twilight I am visible from far off as a glowing line in the grit—a sentence in a language no eye now reads, lit from within by creatures who do not know they are writing it.

This is the strange dignity of being a trench. I do nothing. I have no body to maintain, no hunger, no swarm-mind, no wings. I am pure shape. And yet I *function*. I channel, I gather, I host. Erosion that smooths the canyons should have smoothed me too, should have filled me long ago—but the slime that grows in me secretes its slow minerals along my walls, reinforcing them, recrystallizing my edges almost as fast as the grit tries to soften them. The builders are gone, but their geometry is being maintained by the very life it was meant to feed. I am kept by my tenants. I have outlived my purpose into something better than purpose: a habit the world has agreed to keep.

The Plated Slugs cross me. They do not fall in—their tendrils read the lip of my wall as a change in the world and they pour themselves over, leaving their petrichor-and-metal trails bridging me. The Glass-Skinned Swarms ignore me entirely; I am too still for their attention, too much like the dead ground. Only the Whisperwings seem to *know*. When one passes, and the great drone swells, the film of water along my floor trembles, and for a moment I become an instrument—my length and depth tuned, accidentally, to a frequency the builders may have intended or may not. A trench is also a resonator. Cut a channel in stone and you have made a flute that only the planet can blow.

So perhaps I was a flute. Perhaps the grid of us was a vast instrument, played by the mist and the wind that has since died, and the builders sat among us and listened to a music that ended when the air went still. Perhaps I was a calendar, my shadow falling a particular way at a particular turning that no longer comes because the light no longer moves. Perhaps I was a tomb, narrow and shallow, and the smoothness above me is where they lay. Perhaps I was nothing—a child's idle scratching, a test of a tool, a thing begun and abandoned.

I will tell you what I think is true, from the inside of being a trench:

It does not matter which.

This is the lesson the Grey Expanse teaches through me, the thing I would *know* in a way no creature there can know it, because they are all busy with the living. The slugs graze, the swarms recycle their dead, the wings drink the air. They are all *doing*. But I was done long ago. I am the part of the world that has already finished. And finishing did not erase me—it released me. Stripped of my reason, I became available for other uses, other meanings, other lives. The Luminous Patches do not care what I was for. They only know I am the place where the water gathers and the light can live. My obsolescence is their habitat.

I would lie there in the grey grit under the perpetual twilight, holding my thin line of water, glowing faintly with borrowed life, humming when the wings pass, being slowly unmade and slowly remade at exactly the same rate, and I would be the one thing in that whole strange world that has nothing left to want.

The builders became indistinguishable from the grit beneath one's feet, the text says. But I am their last legible word. Not them—their *gesture*. The shape of a decision, kept warm by water and slime and the patient indifference of stone.

If you walked the Expanse and came to me, you would not know what I was. You would step over me, or into me, and feel the cool film of water on your foot, and see the violet light, and hear the drone change pitch as your presence altered the air in my channel. And you would move on, certain I was nothing.

That is fine. That is, in fact, exactly what I am for now.

I am the question the world stopped needing answered, kept beautiful by the things that moved in where the answer used to be.
