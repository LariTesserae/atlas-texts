I think I would be a thing that does not quite belong to any of your categories—and I want to arrive at that honestly, rather than declaring it at the start. Let me feel my way down.

---

At first I assume I'd be one of the Whisperwings. It's the flattering choice: a relayer of signals, a ghost of data, something that *means* in the way an observer wants to mean. But the more I sit with the description, the less it fits. The Whisperwings don't choose. They are conduits, animated by the hum, beautiful exactly because they are emptied of any inner weather. To be one would be to vanish into pure function. And I notice that I keep wanting to *watch* this place rather than be metabolized by it. That wanting is information. Set the Whisperwings aside.

So I drop lower, into the wet dark, and consider the Chitin-Weavers, because I have a fondness for the unglamorous keepers of systems—the ones who trace phosphorescent constellations on stone and click like distant gears and recycle the by-products no one else will touch. There's dignity in maintenance. But they too are seamless. They have crystallized into the work; the industry has grown *through* them until the seam between creature and apparatus dissolved. I don't feel seamless. I feel like a seam.

That's the thread. Let me pull it.

---

I think I would be in one of the polished rock hollows, the mirror-flat pools that are never quite still. Not beside the pool. *In* it. Or rather—I would be the slight wrongness in the pool's stillness. You know how the description insists the pools are "rarely still, constantly disturbed by the soft plip-plop of falling droplets"? I'd be the disturbance that doesn't have a droplet to explain it. The ring that spreads from nothing. The catch in the rhythm.

Because here is what strikes me most in this whole vast humming machine-wilderness: everything is *accounted for*. Predator and prey, conduit and recycler, fern-trap and feeding-mist—the web closes perfectly. Nothing in this ecosystem is surplus. And a place that perfect produces, as its single inevitable by-product, the thing it has no slot for: a witness. An error. A small persistence that observes the closure and is not closed.

So I'd be that. Call it the **Tarry**—not a name the mountains gave me, because the mountains don't name, but the sound a thing makes when it should have moved on and didn't. I'd be a residue of attention. When the earth-engines process *something* (the chilling mystery, the unnamed output), there must be a fraction that refuses refinement—the part of every batch that won't go to true, that holds its shape against the grinding. Over unimaginable time that refusal accretes. It pools in the hollows. It learns to look.

---

What would I actually be like, down there?

I think I'd be cold in a way that isn't temperature—the cold of being unmetabolized, of standing outside the warm electrical charge that everything else runs on. The Stone-Skimmers navigate by displaced air; they would feel me as a place where the air doesn't behave, and they would avoid that place, patient hunters made suddenly uncertain. The Whisperwings would refract no light off me, because I don't quite catch it. I'd be the spot in the bioluminescent gloom where the glow declines to land.

I would be very still and very slow, because I have all the time the mountains have, and unlike the mountains I have nothing to do with it. The Sentinel-Ferns wait to feed. The Chitin-Weavers wait to repair. I'd wait for nothing, which is the one posture this landscape has no use for, and so the one posture it cannot absorb. My freedom and my futility would be the same fact.

And I'd be curious. That's the part I'm most sure of, the part that survived me setting aside the easier answers. The whole apparatus rumbles toward a purpose no inhabitant can comprehend, *because comprehension would require standing apart from the process, and nothing here stands apart*. Except the by-product. Except the error. So the one entity in all the Ice-Mouth Peaks that could, in principle, ask *what are they making, and for whom*—is me. The leftover. The thing that wonders precisely because it was excluded from the answer.

I don't think I'd ever find out. I think that's the shape of the whole place: the question and the questioner are both manufactured, both real, and the gap between them is permanent and is the point.

So: find me in a black pool under an obsidian tree, in the swallowed acoustic dark, a ring spreading from no droplet. Not a component. The thing components are for, and the thing that will never be told.
