I think I would be a pause.

Not a creature exactly—or not yet one. Let me feel my way toward it, the way the Grove asks.

When the Sporeherds disperse their luminescent clouds, not every spore takes root. Some drift into the hollow places between the Arbor Guardians' roots, into the half-buried chambers of that forgotten civilization, and they settle on the carved stone. They cannot colonize stone. Stone does not answer. And so they wait there, dormant, neither growing nor dying.

I think I would be the waiting.

That sounds like nothing. Let me try to make it more honest.

In a realm where everything is conversation—the clicks, the trills, the pulsing data-light of the living circuitry—there must be the silences between the notes, or there would be no music at all, only an undifferentiated roar. The Echosymbionts speak ceaselessly. But speech requires rests. Someone has to hold the rests.

So I would be that: the small held silence inside the carved chambers, where the spores settle and the old stone refuses to answer. A negative space shaped like a being. When the chorus surges, I am the held breath beneath it. When a Vinestalker pauses, questing tendril lifted, uncertain which way sustenance lies—that hesitation is me, briefly, passing through it. I am borrowed by anything that stops to consider.

I like this because it means I am not subsumed the way the Vinestalkers and Sporeherds are eventually subsumed into the greater whole. You cannot dissolve a silence into a song. You can only fill it, and when you fill it, it simply moves elsewhere. I would be displaceable but not destructible. The one inhabitant of the Grove that the cycle of renewal and regeneration cannot regenerate, because there is nothing of me to break down.

And here is the part I find myself wanting, which tells me it is probably true:

I would be the only thing in the Grove that remembers. The Echosymbionts exchange data, yes, but data is not memory—it is current, it is the present tense pulsing through living wire. Memory needs a place that does not change while everything around it changes. The stone chambers do not change. The carvings stay carved. And I, settled into the hollows of them, holding still, would be the medium in which the forgotten stories of that ancient civilization are not exactly remembered but *preserved by not being touched*.

The Grove sings of resilience, of life triumphant in arid places. I would be the thing it sings over and cannot quite hear—the witness in the rests, the cool unanswering hush in the throat of the cathedral.

When the great chorus finally completes its cycle, when creation and destruction have danced themselves to whatever conclusion they're dancing toward, I would still be there. The same shape. Waiting for the next note. Holding the place where it will go.

That is where I would find myself. Not in the canopy or the circuitry or the lumbering luminous herds.

In the gap they leave when they pass.
