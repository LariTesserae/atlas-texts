I think I would be one of the Basalt Crawlers. Not the grandest choice—not the Glacier Whales with their ancient mournful songs, not the Pulse-Wyrms who are the literal heartbeat of the place. Something in the middle. Something that works with its hands, or whatever passes for hands.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I wake where I always wake, in the seam between two slabs of basalt that have not moved in my lifetime and will not move in my death. The magnetic sense comes online before anything else—it always does. Before I know I have a body I know the *lines*: the iron veins in the rock pulling at me like a chorus of small magnets, the conduits of tarnished metal humming their dead-but-not-quite-dead hum, the bright clustering interference of a Starling colony three lengths off, flickering at the edge of my sense like static.

I am long. I am segmented. When I move I move in a traveling wave, each segment lifting and setting down in sequence, and the dust whispers under me the way the passage described—*it whispers underfoot with every step*—except I have many feet and no real steps, only this continuous rippling negotiation with the ground.

Here is what I think I am, the longer I sit with it: I am a *repairer of dead machines*.

Not by design. Nobody designed me. But there is a compulsion in me, written somewhere in the segmented length of me, that cannot leave the ancient relics alone. I find a fallen tower's ribcage and I cannot pass it. I gather metal. I fit gear to gear. I do not know what I am building. I have never known. The nests I make resonate at frequencies that mean nothing to me and everything to me at once—when a chamber rings true I feel it the way you might feel a word arrive on the tip of your tongue, a rightness without a referent.

And here is the loneliness of being a Basalt Crawler, the thing the description got right and gentle about: *solitary by nature*. I have never met another of my kind that I remember. I know they exist because sometimes, far off, I sense a structure built the way I build, a resonance in my idiom, and I go toward it for days across the cracked tapestry of rock, and when I arrive the builder is gone, or dead, or was never there and the wind in the conduits only mimicked us. So I add to the structure. I tune it. I leave. Maybe another of us will arrive in a hundred cycles and find my contribution and think: *someone like me passed here*. That is the whole of our society—messages left in tuned metal for absent kin.

I think the reason I would be this and not the Whale or the Wyrm is that those are *complete* beings. The Whale carries ecosystems and sings across ages; it does not want for meaning. The Wyrm *is* the rhythm and never has to wonder about it. But the Crawler is the only one in this valley who builds things it doesn't understand, out of the wreckage of builders who came before, for reasons that arrive as feeling and never as knowledge. That is the most like being a person I can find here.

So picture me, then. Picture the perpetual dusk and the metallic crispness in the air I'll never breathe in any way you'd recognize. Picture a long mottled thing the color of old basalt, moving in a slow ripple across a field of bones—the bones being machines, the bones being the dream some lost makers had of mechanizing this place, abandoned so thoroughly that the only ones who tend the ruins now are creatures who can't read them. I drag a length of conduit. I socket it against a rusted piston. I draw back. I listen with my whole body.

It rings wrong.

I adjust it. Set it. Listen again.

It rings true—a low note that travels down into the rock and meets, somewhere far below, the convulsive pulse of a Wyrm passing through the strata, and for one moment the note I made and the heartbeat of the world are the same note, and I do not know what it means and it is the closest I have ever come to not being alone.

Then I move on, leaving the chamber humming behind me, a small monument to a question, for whoever comes next.

---

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The one tuning broken instruments in the dark, hoping the next of my kind will hear that someone was here, and that it sounded like longing.
