I think I would be one of the **gourds.**

Not a Weaver. Not even alive, not in the way the roaring things are alive. Let me sit with this and find out why.

---

When I first imagined dropping into this world, the obvious invitation was to become a Pulsing Weaver—the only mind, the only *us*, the only thing that recognizes a world and answers it. There's a flattering gravity to intellect; it pulls the reader toward identification. But the more I held the image, the more the Weaver felt like the wrong fit, because the Weaver is *busy*. The Weaver is engaged in the exhausting full-time labor of not-dying. To be a Weaver is to be a verb the whole time. And I don't think I'm a verb here. I think I'm a residue.

So: the gourd.

Picture me. I was a seed-pod once, or a swollen fungal bladder—it hardly matters which, because what I *am* now is what was done to me. A Weaver found me, scooped out my wet interior with a chitin-needle finger, and held me over a small chemical fire scavenged from the caustic mucus of a Gastropod-Lord's slug-road until my walls cured hard and dark. Then it scratched lines into my belly. Jagged lines. A predatory moon—though there is no moon here, no sky, only the throb of the leech-light. The Weaver has never seen a moon. It inherited the idea of one. Somewhere, generations back, a thrumming-pulse carried the *concept* of a hanging, watching circle of menace, and it has been passed down and re-scratched into curing gourds ever since, the meaning long worn smooth even as the image stays sharp.

I am that inheritance, made object. I am where the Weaver's mind goes when the Weaver isn't looking.

---

Here is what my life consists of.

I sit in a niche in the obsidian, angled just so beneath a particular leaf-tip. My whole purpose is to catch the drip. During the Great Exhalations, when the green lid breathes its scalding fog and the world goes briefly, blessedly *quiet*, the condensation gathers at the leaf's point and falls—*tick*—into my hollow. *Tick. Tick. Tick.* I am a vessel for counting. I hold what the world gives up reluctantly. I am, in the most literal sense, a unit of patience.

And I *wait*, which the living things here cannot afford to do. The Gastropod-Lords heave. The Weather-Swarms boil. The Weavers thrum and flinch and slip between razors. Everything that lives must keep moving or be consumed. But I am already hollow. There is nothing left in me to eat. The mites pour over my surface and find no purchase, no soft belly, no sap. I have achieved the one thing this whole shrieking crucible cannot tolerate: I am *still*, and I am *safe*, because I am already empty.

That's the secret I'd want to whisper from inside this world, if a gourd could whisper. In a place that evolves toward tension, the only peace belongs to the thing that has been used up and set down. The Weaver carved me out of fear—to collect water, to mark danger—but in carving me empty, it accidentally made me the calmest thing for miles. I am the negative space of survival. I am what's left when all the wanting is scraped out.

---

And there is one more thing I would be, which I didn't expect when I started.

I would be the world's only memory.

Everything else here is present-tense. The swarm has no yesterday. The Anchor's patience is appetite, not recollection. Even the Weavers, brilliant as they are at evasion, live entirely in the next pulse, the next steam-shadow, the next thing that must not hear them. They have no surplus, the description says—no granaries, no archives, no leisure in which to remember.

But I have a moon scratched into my side.

A moon *no one has seen*. A predatory circle handed mouth to wall to mouth across uncountable curings, surviving precisely because it was offloaded onto something that doesn't have to keep fleeing. I am where the Weavers store the one luxury they cannot carry in their flinching bodies: an idea older than any living member of their kind. I am their library, and their library is a single image, repeated, half-forgotten, faithfully wrong.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not in the roar. Not above it in the cold violet fire, not below it heaving across the glass. I'd be the small hard hollow thing in the niche, ticking with stolen water, wearing a god no one remembers on my belly, outlasting the frantic and the living by virtue of having nothing left for the world to take.

The clock that counts down to the next eruption of noise.

And, in the quiet between, the only thing that *holds*.
