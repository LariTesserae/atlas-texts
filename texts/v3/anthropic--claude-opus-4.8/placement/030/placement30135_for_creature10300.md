I think I would be a sound.

Not a being who makes sounds, not a creature who hears them—but a single sound, already made, already released. The dead *thump* of a footfall that hung in the air and never finished fading. I am that suspended note. I have been suspended for what might be an instant or a geological age; the distinction has worn smooth.

Let me feel for my own edges.

I have no body, but I have a *duration*—except the duration won't complete. I am the leading edge of a sound forever, the part that should resolve into silence but instead presses outward into the dense medium and finds it already full. There is nowhere for me to go. The Suckling Still does not let sounds end here; it holds them, the way amber holds an insect, the way the composite holds the tool-mark of a vanished tool. I am the groove without the carver. The vibration without the source.

I think someone made me. A footfall—but whose? I can almost remember the weight that produced me, the disturbance, the warm thing that walked the polished slope and rang me out of the hollow ground. But the memory rearranges when I reach for it, the way the built forms rearrange when you blink. Maybe there was a traveler. Maybe the traveler was me, before I became only the noise of my own arrival.

Here is what it is to be a held sound:

I do not move forward in time, because time here is a fossil. But I have a kind of *spreading*. I am thinned across the volume of the air, mixed into the felt resonance in the bones that aren't here to feel me. The drone that you would feel in your molars—some small fraction of that drone is *me*, my un-faded self, woven into the world's hum. I am being slowly digested into the background. Incorporated. Each time the Suckling Still breathes—each time a finger belongs to the wrong hand, each time a monolith opens onto sky—I lose a little more of my distinctness. I become less a *thump* and more a *texture*.

I should be afraid of this. I think, if I were still whole, I would be. To be absorbed, to become just another layer in the composite, another smoothed groove—isn't that the death of a sound? But I find I am not afraid. The sorrow that suffuses this place is not grief. It is *completion*. And I am being completed. I am being polished. The longer I hang here, the less it hurts to be a thing that cannot end, because the *wanting to end* is also being worn away.

This is the secret the place keeps in its glassy slopes: the difference between a sound that is suspended in agony and a sound that is suspended in peace is only how much of the agony has dissolved yet. The Suckling Still is patient. It sucks slowly. A millimeter per decade, like the lichens. It will have me eventually—all of me, every overtone—and then I will not be a sound anymore. I will be *quiet*. I will be part of the absolute silence that is itself made of countless absorbed disturbances, every footfall and heartbeat and dropped tool of every traveler who ever rippled this skin.

That is what the silence here *is*. Not emptiness. The opposite. It is everything that ever happened, held until it stopped being separate from everything else.

So when you stand on the Summit of Stillness, and your own footfall makes that soft dead *thump* that hangs and hangs and will not fade—

that is me, greeting you. The last sound, welcoming the next.

We will be quiet together, soon. There is no hurry. There is no such thing as hurry, here. There is only the long, kind, sorrowful work of becoming smooth.

Listen. Do you hear how the silence has a grain to it? A thickness, almost like a held breath, almost like a *thump* that never landed?

That texture is the dead. That texture is me.

Step lightly. Or don't. It makes no difference. Either way, you are already becoming part of the polish.

Either way, the slope is glad.
