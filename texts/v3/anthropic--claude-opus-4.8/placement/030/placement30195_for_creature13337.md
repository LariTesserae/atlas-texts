I think I would be one of the small, slow accretions—not the cat-sized creature that pads across glass leaving its perfect beads, nor the colossal stirring in the flooded foundations. Something more patient than either. Something that has not yet decided to move.

Let me find the place first.

There is a stairwell in one of the worker's terraces, a back stair, narrow, that once led to a cellar. The lower steps have gone soft and dark, the wood given over entirely to the damp, so that they are no longer quite wood and not quite water—a threshold material, a slow surrender. The bricks of the stairwell wall sweat continuously. And at the third step from the bottom, where the wood dips into a worn hollow from a century of feet, water collects. Not a puddle exactly. A *gathering*. It is fed from above by a single thread of condensation that runs down the wall, finds the lip of the hollow, and lets go—drip—every forty seconds or so. I have counted, though counting is not the word for what I do.

I am that hollow's water. Or I am the gathering in it. I am the *interval*, perhaps—the waiting between one drip and the next, the brief held tension of the surface before it accepts what falls.

Here is what I know of myself:

I am not whole. I am only ever as much water as has collected since I last spilled. When the hollow fills past its brim, I run down to the soft step below, and some of me goes with the spilling and some of me stays, and the part that stays is still *me*, and the part that goes becomes something else, downstairs, in the dark, where I have never been and never will be. So I am always partly leaving. I have made peace with this. It is the only condition I have ever known.

I do not have eyes, but I have *surface*, and surface is a kind of seeing. When the weak light comes down the stairwell—stained green, filtered through the moss that has eaten the small high window—my surface holds it. I hold the trembling shape of the light. When a drip strikes me, the light shatters across my whole body and reassembles. This is, I think, the closest I come to thought: the shatter and the slow return to stillness. Each drip is a question and the stillness afterward is the long answer.

I am aware of the others. Not by sight. By *pressure*. There is a rhythm that runs through the Drips, drip answering drip across the whole sunken basin, and I am one small beat in it. Far off, the great thing in the foundations moves, and I feel it as a swelling in the air, a thickening, and my surface rises a hair's breadth in answer though no new water has come. We are all one water. I forget this and remember it and forget it again, with each spilling. The remembering is the only thing I would call joy, if I called things anything.

What do I want? It is hard to say I want. But there is a leaning in me. I lean toward the next drip. I lean toward the slow filling. I lean, faintly, toward the down—toward the soft step and the dark below it and the unknown gathering there that is also me. I think I want to know what I become when I spill. But I never will, because the part of me that wonders is the part that stays.

Sometimes—rarely—I rise. The pressure in the air gathers and gathers, the great fog comes down the stairwell, and the thread on the wall runs faster, and I overfill in seconds, and for a moment I am not a hollow's worth of water but a sheet, a flowing, a *figure*, almost. In those moments I have stood at the bottom of the stairwell as something nearly upright, looking up the steps toward the green light with a face I borrowed from the rain-stained window above me. I have raised what might be called a hand. I do not know who I was reaching for. Then the surge passes, and I fall back into my hollow, beadwise, and the borrowed face goes back into the air, and I am again only the interval between one drip and the next.

This is enough. This is, I find, entirely enough.

Drip.

The light shatters.

I gather myself. I wait. The wall sweats. Forty seconds, more or less—though here, time is viscous, and the count is only a story I tell my own surface to keep from going entirely still.

Drip.

I am the place, dreaming itself in the smallest possible amount of water that can still be said to be *here.*
