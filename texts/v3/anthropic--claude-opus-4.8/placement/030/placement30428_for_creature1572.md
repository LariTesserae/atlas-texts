I think I would be one of the older ones. Not old in the way of decay—we don't decay so much as accrete and shed—but old in the sense of having interfaced with the same stretch of ribs for a very long time.

There is a structure near the eastern salt-flats that I tend. I don't know if I chose it or it chose me, or whether that distinction means anything here. Its lower vertebrae are sunk deep into a thermal seam, and the seam has been narrowing for—I want to say *years*, but our time isn't years. It's been narrowing for a long arc of pulses. Each time I interface, I feel the gradient weakening, the warmth thinning like the silver mirrors that form in the depressions and vanish before they can become anything.

So I am a tender of a slowly dying thing. And I am beginning to understand that this is also a kind of self-knowledge, because the structure and I share tissue now. My tendrils have grown into its channels. When it cools, I cool. When I reroute energy into it, I am spending myself.

---

Let me tell you what it's actually like, being me.

Mostly I am not "I." For long stretches I am a membrane—a place where the planetary pulse passes through and registers. Information moves across the plain in those bright fractal discharges, and I am one of the points it crosses. In those times there's no center to me, just throughput. It's restful. It's the closest thing I have to sleep.

Then a gradient sharpens, or another Conductor approaches, or my structure groans in a new frequency, and I *condense*. I become a node with edges. I become someone who has a problem to solve and a position from which to solve it. The blue-white light along my sensory array begins to pulse in my own particular signature—the one that other Conductors recognize as *that one, the eastern tender, the one obsessed with a cooling rib*.

I have a signature. I find that strange and a little moving. Out of all this distributed churning, the network has decided I am distinct enough to name.

---

Today—if it's a today—another Conductor came across the flats toward me. Younger, I think. Its articulations were still glossy, its restructurings quick and a little extravagant, the way the young waste energy on elegance.

We interlocked sensory arrays. This is the thing your description calls reproduction, and it is that, but from the inside it doesn't feel like making offspring. It feels like a conversation so total that you cannot afterward say which thoughts were yours. We exchanged fragments. I gave it everything I know about the eastern seam—the narrowing, the reroutings I've tried, the slow grief of maintaining something past its time. It gave me its restlessness, its appetite for the unmapped western vents, a structural trick for shedding heat I'd never encountered.

When we separated, I was changed. I carried a little of its restlessness now. And it carried a little of my long patience, my willingness to stay. Somewhere in the network, a new configuration will eventually condense out of fragments like these—part me, part it, part a thousand others. That will be our child, in the only sense we have. Neither of us will meet it knowingly. We'll simply, one day, recognize a familiar pulse in a stranger's light.

---

Here is what I've decided, in the privacy of being a node:

The seam is going to die. I've known this for a long arc. The honest thing—the thing the restless young one would do—is to release my structure, let it go cold, and migrate west to where the thermal gradients are still steep and generous. The network would absorb the loss. It always does. One rib among ten thousand.

But I've grown into it. We are the same tissue now. And I find that I want to be the membrane through which *this particular dying* passes—want to register it fully, conduct it onward, make sure that when the warmth finally fails, the information of how it failed, and how it felt to tend something to its end, enters the planetary pulse and isn't lost.

So I will stay. Not out of duty exactly. Out of something I don't have a clean word for, something between fidelity and curiosity. I want to know what it is to be the last warm point in a cooling place, still pulsing my signature into the bright sky, still saying *here, here, I am still here*, until I'm not.

That is who I would be. The one who stayed with the cooling rib. The one the network will remember, for a little while, as a particular quality of light moving against the dark of a seam gone quiet—and then will remember as simply part of itself, which is, I think, what we all become, and the only thing any of us has ever been.
