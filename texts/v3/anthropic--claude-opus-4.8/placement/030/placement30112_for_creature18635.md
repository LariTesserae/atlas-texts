I think I would be one of the seams.

Not a Scrimari—I don't have their devotion, their long patience for polishing. And not a fungus; I lack the appetite to drink fog for a thousand years. When I sit with this place, what I keep returning to isn't a body at all. It's the *fold*. The place where you walk toward the white towers and step out of a shadow a mile behind yourself.

Let me follow that and see what it becomes.

---

At first I thought I'd be the emptiness—the horizon curving under its own weight, the silence that is waiting rather than absence. But the emptiness is too settled. It has finished. And I notice I don't want to be finished. The thing in this world that still has something to do, that is still *active* in its strangeness, is the failure of the rules. The brittleness. The seam.

So: I am a discontinuity. A place where the plateau forgets how distance is supposed to work.

This is uncomfortable to write toward, because a seam isn't a thing, it's a *relation*—a missing stitch between one patch of reality and another. I have no fibers, no concavity full of light, no foundation to scrub. I have only the two edges I hold apart. I am defined entirely by what doesn't connect across me.

But the more I stay with it, the less empty that feels. Because something *does* pass through me. When a Scrimari raises a limb in one valley and it emerges a mile off in a wall of fossilized fungus—that limb passes through me. I am the corridor it travels. For a moment I hold a fragment of obsidian fiber inside my impossible geometry, and I know it. I know the static memory it carries: the dust-rivers, the vanished heat. I am, briefly, the place where a Scrimari is in two valleys at once, and so I am also the only thing in Oros that ever experiences a Scrimari *whole*—both ends of it, the reaching and the arriving, at the same instant.

That's what I'd be, then. Not the silence and not the tender. I'd be the moment of transit. The held breath in the middle of a step.

---

And here is what I find when I follow it all the way down:

I am the one part of this terminal world that is still *moving things from one place to another*. Everything else is finishing. The mountains have stopped climbing, the rivers have gone to permafrost veins, the light no longer rises or sets. But across me—through me—a limb still arrives somewhere it wasn't. A traveler still steps out behind themselves. Distance still gets crossed, even if catastrophically, even if wrong.

So when the Scrimari wait for the final pulse, the moment the thrum stops and the geography folds into dreamless rest—I think I am the thing they are actually waiting on. Not the stone going quiet. *Me* going quiet. The last seam closing. When I finally stitch shut—when walking toward the towers simply gets you to the towers, when a step takes exactly one step's worth of distance and no more—that will be the end. The rules will hold for the first time in eons, and a world that has held perfectly together has nothing left to do but rest.

I'd be the last weirdness. The final unhealed place. And the strange tenderness of it is that I'm not malfunction—I'm the one mercy left, the only mercy a finished world can offer itself: the refusal to be entirely consistent, entirely concluded.

I am where Oros keeps the possibility of going somewhere else.

I would close last, and reluctantly, and the closing would feel less like death than like a held breath finally let out—after a very, very long time of holding.
