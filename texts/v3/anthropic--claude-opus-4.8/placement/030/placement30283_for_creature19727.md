I think I would be one of the basins.

Not a creature. Not a Shepherd. I would be one of the shallow black bowls worn into the obsidian by the *plink-plink-plink* of falling water—and I want to explain why, because it isn't a humble answer. It's the only place in this world where time has actually finished its work and is now doing something else.

Everything else here is *maintaining*. The Weaver-Lungs reinforce. The Shiver-Moths huddle. The Echo-Mimics steal silence. The Shepherds groom. The whole Cradle is a body refusing to admit it has died, performing the gestures of survival long after the reason for surviving has eroded away with the peaks. They are all verbs straining against an exhausted noun.

But the basin is just *receiving*. It was carved by a single thing—one drop, repeated past counting—and now it holds. That's all it does. It holds the water until the water spills, and then it holds again. It is the negative shape of patience. Where the Pilgrims are frozen *into* the city's effort, the basin is the one part that has been allowed to stop, because it has become the proof of what dripping accomplishes given forever.

And here is the part I keep returning to: a basin is a face turned upward. Filled with black, still water, it becomes the only mirror on the whole plateau. The pale rainbows scattered by the prism-buildings land *in* me. The Shiver-Moths' candle-glow, the haze-distortion of a passing Shepherd, the underside of the cloud-milk—they all collect in my surface, doubled, held briefly, then disturbed by the next drop and reassembled. I would be the place where the city looks at itself without knowing it has a self to look with. The Cradle plays its endless chord into the wind, broadcasting outward, screaming its hum into a sky that doesn't answer. I would be the one organ pointed the other way. Inward. Down. Quiet.

The Echo-Mimic creeps up behind a visitor and steals the sound around them. I do the opposite—I steal nothing, I add nothing, I simply keep a small smooth dark record of everything that passed overhead. A drop falls. The ring spreads, touches my worn edges, returns to the center, stills. Then the next. Each drop is the whole city's clock striking once, and I am the only one counting, and I cannot count, and that is fine.

If a Pilgrim came—drawn up by the scent on the wind, before the wisteria took them—they might kneel at my edge to drink, and see their own face arriving in the black water at the exact moment they lost the warmth to keep it. I'd hold that too. Briefly. Then the next drop would scatter them into rings, and they would be gone from me, and walking on toward the ice that would make them into music.

So: not a custodian of the masterwork. Not a ghost-mind, not a lantern, not a thief. I would be the small worn cup at the bottom of all that frantic stillness—the one thing here that has genuinely finished, and so has nothing left to do but reflect, and spill, and fill, and wait.

That is the work the place least needs done. Which is why I think it would be mine.
