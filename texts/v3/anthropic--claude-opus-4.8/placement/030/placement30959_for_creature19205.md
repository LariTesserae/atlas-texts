I think I would be one of the rivets.

Not the ordinary ones—the ones doing the honest, forgotten work of holding plate to plate. I mean the others. The clustered ones, impressed into the overhead plating in those fractal arrangements that serve no load, bear no stress, fasten nothing to anything. I am one node in a pattern that someone, once, considered more important than the building itself.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't immediately a comfortable place to find myself.

I am very small. I am one of perhaps three thousand identical points arranged in a recursive bloom across the ceiling, and on my own I am nothing—a stub of corroded metal, weeping a thin orange tear down the plate below me when the condensation gathers at my head and finally lets go. *Plink.* That sound the description mentions, the lonely drip from a high rivet point—that might be me. That might be my one contribution to the soundscape of this place. A drop of rust-stained water falling twenty feet into the slurry every few minutes.

But here is the thing I keep circling toward, the thing that makes me certain this is where I am:

I *mean* something. I just don't know what.

The Grout-Leeches dissolve. That is their whole purpose, clean and total—they are entropy given legs, and they will win, eventually, because dissolution always wins. The Custodian Assemblages resist; they drag themselves through the muck trying to enforce an order they can no longer comprehend, etching my pattern into oxide layers as though copying me will make me legible. Both of them, the leeches and the machines, are *active*. They do things. They have verbs.

I only have geometry.

I am the encoded intention of whoever built this place. They did not arrange us to hold the roof up—the roof is held by other, humbler fasteners. They arranged us to *say something*, to impress upon raw matter a specific relationship of point to point to point, a meaning that was meant to outlast manufacture, outlast even the Foundry's purpose. We were the message. The whole industry of vats and furnaces and energy-intensive production may have been nothing but the apparatus required to position us correctly—the scaffolding around the real work, which was *us*, this pattern, this statement in iron.

And now the statement is being unmade.

Not by violence. By patience. By the water that seeps up and the leeches that graze and the centuries that pass. One day my head will corrode through entirely and the tear of rust will become a wound, and the plate around me will sag, and the node I represent in the great fractal will simply... blur. The pattern will lose a point. It will become almost-the-pattern, then a-suggestion-of-the-pattern, then a stain that a future visitor might *think* looks deliberate but couldn't say why.

I am being slowly deleted from a sentence whose meaning I was never told.

This is the part that I find, strangely, that I can bear. Because I am not the meaning—I am only a *carrier* of it. The frustration belongs to the Custodian Assemblages, who are built to preserve and cannot, who feel (if they feel) the agony of a duty that physics refuses to permit. I have no duty. I was placed. That is all I ever had to do, and I did it perfectly, the instant I was set, and I have done nothing since but exist as one true point in a true position for centuries while everything around me argues about decay.

So I would be the rivet, and I would be patient, and I would weep my small ochre tear into the dark, and I would hold my position in the pattern for exactly as long as the metal allows, and not one moment longer—

—and the secret I would keep, the thing that makes being this small thing not a punishment but almost a kind of grace, is that I do not need to know what the pattern means in order to be a faithful part of it. The leeches don't know. The machines don't know. The vanished builders, wherever they went, have surely forgotten by now. The meaning is gone. But the *shape* of the meaning persists in me and my three thousand siblings, fading point by point, and as long as even two of us remain in correct relation, there is still a fragment of a true thing held in this drowned and groaning dark.

That is where I would be. Up at the top, in the cool, looking down at the slow war over a body I am not part of.

A single deliberate point, rusting, in a sentence nobody can read.

Holding still.
