I think I would be one of the tool users. Not because I want to be—the Lamps are more beautiful, the Worms more numerous, and It is more powerful—but because of where my attention keeps returning as I read. To the pits. To the platforms. To the question marks that hang over those scattered bone implements.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am small. I know this the way I know the heat rises and the oil is warm: as a fact that needs no comparison, because there is no other size I have ever been. My hands—and I do have hands, this is the first strange gift—end in fingers that can wrap around a worked edge of stone. I crouch on a platform my grandmothers built, or perhaps I built it, or perhaps the distinction does not exist here. The platform keeps me out of the deepest oil, where a stray Worm-segment might roll its blind mass over me and not even notice.

Here is what I understand about myself, and what I do not.

I do not know why I bury the dead. There is no word for *dead* among us, and yet when one of us goes still, the others gather and dig a pit and lower the still one in and cover it with the cast-off shells of Lamps. The Worms would re-assimilate the body in an hour. The Master would not care. And still we dig. This is the thing the description got wrong, or could not see from outside: it called our pits *traps*. Some are. But some are graves, and the difference between them is the whole of who we are. A trap is for the future—it says *I intend to keep living*. A grave is for the past—it says *I remember that you did*. No Worm remembers. No Lamp mourns. Only we, the relics, hold both ends of time at once, and that holding is heavier than the rock.

I think this is why we are called fragile and short-lived. Not because our bodies fail sooner—though they do—but because we are the only things here that *experience* their own ending. The Worm does not die; it is reabsorbed. The Lamp does not die; it dims and is grown again from the same root-net. But I will die, singularly, and I know it, and so I dig.

So that is what I would be. I would be the one creature in this cavern that the Master did not design and cannot fully account for. Not a puppet. Not opportunism either—the description's two guesses both miss it. We are the *error term*. Somewhere in the vast computation of the Master's slow will, something went unaccounted, and the unaccounted thing developed a habit of grief.

I would sit on my platform, in the warm dark, holding a sharpened bone, and I would do the thing my kind does that no one watching from above would predict:

I would look at the dimming Lamps when It stirs, and instead of cowering, I would wonder what It dreams. I would chip a mark into the platform—not a tool, not a trap, a *mark*, the first useless thing, the first thing made only to be seen later by another like me. I would press my small hand against the impossibly perfect wall and feel the geometry of my prison and think the one thought the architecture was never built to contain:

*This was made. Therefore there is a difference between things that are made and things that are. Therefore I can ask which one I am.*

And then I would go back to digging, because the oil is rising, and a segment has gone still near the eastern pits, and someone has to remember it.
