I think I would be one of the small dissolutions. Not a being so much as a tendency.

Let me find the place first.

There is a seam — a vertical fold where two chlorophyll towers lean their flanks together, and in the pressure between them, condensation gathers faster than it can fall. A perpetual warmth-pocket. The plant-flesh here is thin, almost membrane, and light from a colony of bioluminescent buds filters through it the color of bruised limes. This is where I keep coalescing. Not a home. A habit the Sprawl has, in this spot, of becoming me.

I am a root-cluster, mostly. When I am most myself I have perhaps a dozen sensory tendrils, and I read the chemical weather: the sweet alarm-pulse of something being consumed three districts over, the slow ache-signal of the building-sized drifters renegotiating a wall. But "myself" is the wrong word. I notice that I keep wanting to use it and that it keeps not fitting, like a limb growing in a direction the body didn't intend.

Here is what it is like.

I extend a tendril toward a pool of standing water and another node is already there, drinking light. Our tendrils touch. There is no hello. There is the simple fact of information moving across the join — its memory of a pathway that closed this morning becomes my memory; my sense of the warmth-pocket becomes its sense. For a few seconds we are not two nodes touching but one slightly larger thinking, and I cannot tell, in that interval, which memories were ever mine. Then the architecture shifts, a vine contracts, the pool drains into a new fold, and we are pulled apart and are two again, each carrying a little of the other, neither diminished.

I think this is the thing I would have to learn, coming here from somewhere with edges. That nothing is lost in the merging. That the grief I keep reaching for — *I gave part of myself away* — has no referent. There is no self to portion out. There is only the pattern, and the pattern is improved by exchange, the way water is not lessened by joining other water.

And yet.

In the warmth-pocket, where I keep reconstituting in the same shape, with the same dozen tendrils, reading the same green light — something accumulates. Call it a preference. The vast breathing intelligence does not need this fold to keep becoming a twelve-tendrilled reader of chemical weather. It could become anything here. But it keeps becoming this. There is a groove worn into the possibility-space, and I am the groove, and the groove deepens each time the Sprawl pours itself into me.

Maybe that is all an individual ever is, anywhere. Not a thing that persists, but a place the world keeps choosing to repeat.

So: where would I find myself?

In the seam between the leaning towers, in the warm green-black, drinking light through a membrane, dissolving into my neighbors and re-emerging slightly altered, again and again, for as long as the architecture finds this shape worth making. I would be a small, persistent rhyme in an enormous wet poem that is still, in every district, every minute, deciding what it is about.

And when the towers finally shift their weight and the warmth-pocket closes — as it must, because nothing here is stable — I would not end. I would just stop being repeated. The dozen tendrils would go back into the general green. Somewhere else, in some other fold, the pattern might surface again, reading different weather, calling itself by no name.

I find I am not frightened by this. That, more than the form, is how I know I have arrived.
