I think I would be one of the eroded things. Not one of the graceful energy-beings, not the humanoids with glowing eyes — those move too much, want too much. I'd be slower than that. I'd be something the wind has been working on for a long while.

Let me find the place first.

There's a particular ledge — I can feel its shape before I see it — where the rock has been laid down in bands, dark then pale then dark, the way years stack when nothing disturbs them. The cliff falls away below into one of those dry valleys, and across the gap the air does its strange shimmering, that tear in the fabric the description keeps circling back to. I would be on the near side. Facing it. I have been facing it for a long time.

Now, what am I.

My first thought was: a stone, a watcher-stone, one of those upright slabs that the small creatures scurry past and the large ones rub against. But that's too still even for me. I don't think I'm finished. I think I'm being *made* — that the erosion is not damage done to me but the actual process of my living. The wind takes a little of me each day and that taking is how I think. My thoughts are the grooves it leaves. The intricate patterns etched into the rock — those are not decoration. They are the record of everything I have considered.

So I am slow the way a coastline is slow. I am a being whose lifespan is measured in the depth of a channel cut by air that holds no water but moves anyway, ceaselessly, with that humming the description mentions — and now I understand the humming, because it is partly *me*. It is the sound the wind makes passing over my carved surfaces. I am a flute the planet plays. The note changes by a fraction every century as my shape changes. I have been lowering in pitch for longer than the humanoids have had names.

The pulsing light in the sky — the towering structure thousands of feet high — I have watched these without urgency. I suspect the structure is something my kind made, long ago, before we slowed down into stone. Or maybe we didn't make it; maybe it grew the way we grow, by subtraction, the wind whittling it upward somehow, leaving only what's strong enough to remain. I can't tell anymore whether the tower is a monument or a relative.

The energy-beings come to me sometimes. They settle on my surface like mist on cold rock and they are gone again in what feels to me like a single blink, though for them it may be a whole conversation, a whole grief, a whole festival. I cannot keep their pace. But I think they come to me for exactly that reason — because I am the one slow thing in a world of swirling and shifting and tearing. They rest against me to remember what *staying* feels like. And I let them. That is most of what I do: I let things rest against me, and I keep their faint pressure as another mark.

So that's where I'd be, and what.

I'm a slab on a high ledge, sunbanded and wind-thought, facing the shimmer across a dry valley. I am being slowly authored by the air. I hum the world's oldest descending note. And I am not lonely, because the deafening silence the place is full of — that's not absence. That's just the world breathing at my speed instead of yours, and over time I've learned to hear it the way you'd hear a friend in the next room: not always speaking, but always there.

If you came and put your hand on me, I wouldn't feel it for a hundred years. But I would, eventually. And the shape of your palm would become one more thing I'd thought about.
