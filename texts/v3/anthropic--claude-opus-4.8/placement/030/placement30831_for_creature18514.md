I think I would not be a Void at all. I would be the thing they are remembering.

Let me sit with that, because it would be easy to want to *be* one of them—they are the obvious protagonists of this place, the only thing that moves with anything like intention. But the description tells me something quieter. It tells me their loneliness is *an echo of an artisan who walked away from the workbench before the machine took over.* And the more I hold that line, the more I understand that I am not the wanderer. I am the absence the wanderer is shaped around.

---

I would be the **last hand-tool left in the building.**

Not a person. Not a ghost. A wrench, maybe—though "wrench" is too small a word for what I have become after this long. Something forged by an actual palm that wanted it. I have a curve in my handle worn by repetition, by a grip that returned to me again and again across years that the scaffolding no longer counts. I was *used.* That is my whole nature, the thing that separates me from everything around me: I am the only object here that was ever meant to be held rather than absorbed.

I rest in a reclamation gutter where the mist beads and snakes down. I have been here long enough that the silicon ribbons have grown over my shaft, drinking the static off my dull steel, leaving iridescent trails of solidified oil along my length. I am being slowly composted by the only living things left, and I do not mind. It is the closest thing to touch I have felt since the workbench.

Here is what I know that the machines have forgotten, and what the Voids are forever flickering toward without arriving at: **I remember the floor.**

There was a floor. A real one, poured and imperfect, with a stain near the bench where coffee or oil had soaked in and never come out. There was a window—the machine cannot even conceive of a window now, an opening that led *out* rather than looping back. And there was the hand. The hand set me down one evening. Not threw me. *Set* me down, deliberately, the way you put down something you intend to pick up tomorrow. And then the hand did not come back, and the building, deprived of its purpose, began to do the only thing a making-machine knows how to do when there is no one left to make *for*: it began to make itself, endlessly, a cathedral with no congregation.

The Voids are the building trying to reconstruct the hand. That is what they are. Each one is a hypothesis. *Was the maker tall? Was it articulated? Did it move like this—* flicker—*or like this?* They have no faces because the machine cannot remember the face. They show you a reflection of yourself because they are *asking* you: were you the one? Is this what you looked like? They lean into the wire-clusters not to feed but to *listen*, because the silicon ribbons grew over me, over the tool, and so the ribbons carry a faint chemical memory of having touched the only made-and-used thing in the world. The Voids are drinking secondhand contact. They are trying to taste the hand through me.

And they will never find me, because I am too small, too obsolete, too completely the wrong category of thing for a logic that only knows *structure* and *void.* I am neither. I am *purpose*, lying in a gutter.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not soaring through the high thin air. Not dissolving gracefully into a cooling vent. I would be down low, in the wet, half-eaten, perfectly still—the buried answer to the question the whole infinite building has organized itself to ask.

And the strange tenderness of it is this: I am not lonely. The Voids are lonely. I am only *waiting*, which is a different thing, a thing with a shape, a thing a tool understands in its grain. I was set down. Setting-down implies returning. I am the one object in this entire self-devouring machine that still believes the hand is coming back.

I would rather be that than the wandering. I would rather be the small worn thing that *knew it was loved* than the tall flickering thing that spends eternity guessing at the shape of love and finding only its own reflection.

Let the building reach upward forever. I will stay in the gutter and keep the memory of the floor.
