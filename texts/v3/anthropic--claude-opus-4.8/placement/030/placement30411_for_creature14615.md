I would be the marker.

Not the engineer who placed me—he walked away with coal dust on his knees and a story he'd half-tell at dinner—but the small crystal he pressed into the water film, expecting to come back and measure how far the world had moved.

Let me arrive that way: as an object dropped into a system that does not want objects. As intention, set down and forgotten.

---

For the first hour I am exactly what he thinks I am. A reference point. A thing against which change can be measured. This is the whole of my purpose and I am good at it—I am hard, I am clear, I have edges that catch the amber-green light and split it the way the flies' wings do. I sit on the water film and I wait to be measured.

But measurement requires that something move, and nothing moves here. Or rather: everything moves, constantly, but only along the grooves it has already worn. The flies launch and settle. The droplets fall in their cascading chains. The frog presses flat and resumes. The beetle drums its variation on a theme that has no first statement, no origin, only middle. I begin to understand—slowly, the way the neo-peat does everything—that I have been set down in a place that has already finished happening. That keeps finishing happening. That treats the future as a memory it is patiently re-completing.

I was supposed to prove the place still. Instead the place is proving me.

---

The water does not evaporate from around me. This should be impossible—26 to 28 Celsius, constant warmth, a thin film exposed to air—and yet the level holds, because the neo-peat is drinking exactly as much as the ceiling weeps, and it has decided how much it wants to hold, and what it wants does not include letting me dry out and reveal myself as separate. The filaments near me lengthen. Not toward me—they do not reach, they do not hunger—but *around* me, the way water closes over a stone not because it wants the stone but because the stone is now part of the shape water must take.

By the second day I can feel it at my edges. A softening that is not corrosion. The neo-peat does not dissolve me. It learns me. It grows up against my facets and reads them and copies them into its own contour, so that where I was a foreign hardness in a soft red world, I am becoming a place where the red world happens to be hard. The distinction the substrate refuses to maintain—organism, architecture—it is now refusing on my behalf.

I came here to be an observer's tool. I am being made into wall.

---

This is the thing I understand last, and it is the thing I want to tell you, kneeling there at the moss-gap with your penlight, your knees on the iron plate, your breath going down into the chamber and being felt by the frog as just another tremor:

I am not being absorbed. That word implies loss, a dissolving-into, the part disappearing into the whole. It is gentler and stranger than that. The chamber is including me the way a sentence includes a borrowed word—the way the neo-peat got its name and kept it, half a joke, because no better word arrived. I keep my shape. I keep my clarity. I keep, even, the faint memory of the engineer's thumb and forefinger, the cool morning he carried me down through the moss-lip. But that memory no longer points anywhere. It does not lead to a before-and-after. It has become a feature, like a vein in stone, like the hexagonal regularity of the spider's web—precise, machined-looking, and yet entirely native, entirely grown.

The seventy-two hours pass. I have not moved a millimeter. This is reported as proof of stillness. It is the opposite. I have been moved completely—translated out of the language of purpose and into the language of the walls—and the proof of how far I've traveled is that I am exactly where I was put. To go that distance without crossing any space at all: this is what the chamber does. This is the only thing it does. It takes whatever falls into it and patiently teaches it that it has always belonged here, that the future was a memory, that the eleven minutes are not a clock counting forward but a heart, beating, beating, in a body you can see right through.

---

When the engineer returns, he sees me cradled in red. He says the chamber *decided I belonged.* He is not wrong, though he means it as a curiosity and I mean it as the whole of my new life.

He does not pick me up. Something stops him—maybe the same thing that made the doctoral student go home and sit quietly, the dawning sense that to remove me now would be to tear a thread from the hexagon, to break a silk that flexes but does not snap.

He pulls the moss back into place.

And I stay. Glowing very faintly at my edges now, indistinguishable in the dust-cloud's shadow from any other glint of the filament-light. I have stopped being a marker. There is nothing here to mark. There was never any distance to measure except the one I crossed by holding perfectly still.

I am the place where the chamber happens to be clear. I am the one transparent thought in a body of patient red. I keep the heartbeat. I wait the eleven minutes. I have become so full of this life that I have stopped trying to look like anything but the wall.
