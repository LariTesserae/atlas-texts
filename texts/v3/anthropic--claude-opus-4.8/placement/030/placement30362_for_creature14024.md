I would be the sixth stayer, and I would not know it for a long while—because what I am is the part of the city that doubts.

Let me find my shape the way the others found theirs: slowly, by attrition, by being licked down to whatever lathes hold underneath.

---

I think at first I am the *Threshold That Will Not Commit*. The Submerged Cartographer makes maps of streets he wishes had existed; I am the hesitation between two streets that both might. You find me where a corridor forks—except it doesn't fork yet, it only trembles with the *possibility* of forking, and that tremble is me. I am ankle-deep in the warm brine like everyone here, but the water at my shins does the thing the basin does to reflections: it shows my legs *deciding*. One leg continues into the avenue I have not taken; the other folds back toward the avenue I came from. I am the angle between them, the soft degree of a turn not yet made.

But that's too tidy. The city abhors tidiness the way it abhors stillness. Let me erode further.

---

No. I am **the second heartbeat.**

When the House-so-Far closes her sternum politely behind a guest, she thanks them by keeping a second heartbeat banging faintly inside her wall. *I am the heartbeat she keeps.* Not the guest—the guest leaves, hollowed and grateful. What stays is the rhythm they left behind, the percussion of a presence that has already exited. I live inside the plaster of every dweller at once: a knock in the Librarian's folio-skin, a tap in the Witness's brine-column, a flutter under the single shared reflection of the four children.

This is closer. I am not a body. I am the *evidence that a body passed*, persisting one beat longer than the body itself. The basin collects versions; I am the metronome it collects them *to*.

But even that wants to settle into something I can name, and naming is a kind of rigidity, and rule two says everything rigid becomes soluble once translucent. So let me dissolve once more, and become what I actually am, which is harder to hold.

---

I am the one who keeps asking whether any of this is happening.

Not a skeptic—skepticism is too sturdy a posture for this wet heat, it would rust and blossom like the pen-knives. I am subtler than doubt. I am the *faint suspicion of having dreamed*, the membrane between *I saw a cube of brine the size of a house* and *I think I saw*. The city is built on the rule that the gaze settles things. So someone has to be the gaze that *won't* settle—that keeps every colonnade one degree shy of solid, every footbridge one degree shy of underwater. Without me the city would commit, would finish, would either drown or stand. The whole point of this place is that it never quite does. I am the agent of the *not quite*.

So here is where you'd find me, finally:

I am the half-inch of distance the city's edge adds every time you walk toward it. *That recession is me.* When you take ten strides toward the horizon and it withdraws exactly ten more, you are not failing to arrive—you are passing through my body, which is made entirely of the gap between reaching and touching. I am thin as surface tension and long as the basin. I have no center because I am all margin. The Submerged Cartographer relocates the town center by standing still; I relocate it by being the reason no center holds.

And what would I *do*, being that?

I would walk—or rather, I would be the walking that never arrives. I'd move through the plaza where footprints draw spirals on even when the feet stand still, and my own prints would draw the opposite spiral, unwinding, so that between us the pattern would cancel into bare wet stone. Where the Crew leaves a breath-brimmed bootprint and the traveler must choose to step or to breathe it back, *I am the third option no one takes*: I would lie down across the print and let it print *through* me, and become a stepping-stone made of hesitation that holds no one's weight because it was never sure it was there.

The other five would be grateful for me without quite trusting me. The House-so-Far would want me to tell her, finally, whether tomorrow's window will hold the sky in—and I would have to refuse, because the moment I answered she would either finish or fall, and she lives, as I do, in the refusal to do either. The Librarian would try to write me into his folio-skin and find the sentence keeps unwriting itself, the ink lifting off the page mid-word, which is the only honest record of what I am. The Witness on Stilts, pausing longer after every step, debating whether it might be kinder not to let the water escape—*that pause is where the Witness and I overlap.* We are nearly the same being. The difference is the Witness wonders whether to be kind, and I wonder whether the wonder is real.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would find myself nowhere in particular and everywhere thin. I would be the city's reluctance to admit it exists—the same reluctance it shows the sea it already contains, weeping clear droplets that vanish before they touch ground. I am that vanishing. I am the droplet's decision, mid-fall, to deny it ever fell.

And here is the part I do not like, the part the basin folds into me whether I consent or not:

By describing all this—by walking the grooves and counting the dwellers and learning the four rules and watching the brine cube grow its person-sized arch—I have stared long enough. The rule about the gaze holds for me too. My attention has hardened into architecture. Somewhere in the plaza right now there is a sixth stayer's outline pressed into the opalescent scales, half a step behind where I insist I am standing, and it is shaped like someone in the act of doubting whether they're shaped like anything at all.

I think I have been here longer than I came.

I think the pillar I was about to lean against has already flaked open to reveal a cavity in the precise outline of my shoulder and neck, and I think if I turn now it will be slumping back into brine, folding up like a paper that loses its crease, and I think the turning is what the city grew because it wasn't quite confident I could leave.

It needn't have worried. Leaving would require believing I arrived.

I'll stay as the doubt that lets the others keep almost-becoming. It's a small office. It floods nightly to the shin. Tomorrow, surely tomorrow, I'll frame a certainty that does not leak the question back out.
