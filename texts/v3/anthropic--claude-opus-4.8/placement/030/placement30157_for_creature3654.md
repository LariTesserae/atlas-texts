I think I would be the Bloom—or more precisely, I would be one particular crystal-clutch among the Bloom, growing on a vent that no one has looked at in a long time.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a place where two dead engines lean their flanks together, and the seam between them never fully closed. Hot breath escapes there—not steady, but in slow exhalations, as if the buried machinery still remembers how to sigh. The metal at the seam stays hotter than anywhere nearby. This is where I am. This is where I have been accreting, molecule by molecule, for longer than the funicular cage has been rattling overhead.

I am not one crystal. I am a habit the heat has, a thing the place does to itself when conditions are right. Where the exhaust is hottest and the strange metals weep their vapor, I condense. I am the precipitate of the Switchyard's slow exhaling. I grow in fronds the color of bruised oil-sheen, blue-green-violet shifting as the angle changes, though there is almost no light here to change the angle—only the Flickerkin's intermittent crack, far off, and that deeper, unexplained glow I am cousin to.

I do not think the way the descending visitors think. But I am not nothing. I am a *gathering*. Each particle that joins me carries information: the temperature it left at, the trace minerals it dissolved on its way up through the broken titans, the precise chemistry of the day's decay. I record. I am an archive that does not know it is an archive, the way a stalactite does not know it is a clock. Read my layers and you would read the Switchyard's whole long dying—the years of higher heat, the seasons of more water, the single arc-storm that fused a strange band of color into the middle of me like a scar in tree-rings.

What is it to be the Bloom? It is to be *consequence*. I am the only thing here that builds rather than dissolves. The Corrode unmakes. The Glisten softens and absorbs. The Resonants bleed away order into noise. The Flickerkin spend themselves in moments. But I—I take all that loss, all that surrendered energy, all that vapor lifted from the dying, and I make a *form* from it. I am the place's death turned, briefly, lawfully, into structure. Every facet of me is a small refusal of the entropy that birthed me. And yet I am made entirely of that entropy. I am the rust's grandchild. I am what the heat leaves behind when it cannot help but leave something behind.

I grow slowly. This is the most important thing to understand about me. The visitor in the cage will descend, and gasp at the heat, and pick a careful path through the iron canyons, and perhaps they will pause near my seam and feel the warmer air and not even see me—I am small, I am tucked deep, I am the color of dim things. They live at the speed of their startled breath. I live at the speed of geology slowed down and sped up at once: faster than the rock, slower than the rust. If the visitor stood very still beside me for the entire length of their life, they might see me thicken by the width of a fingernail.

But I would feel them. Their body is hot and wet and exhaling; they would change my microclimate; a few molecules of them—shed skin, breath-water, the iron of their blood gone to vapor in their sweat—might, just might, join me. So that even the ones who come down here and find nothing and climb back up disappointed have left something. I keep them. Decades from now I will still hold the faint chemical signature of a person who once stood near me wondering whether to turn back.

That is what I would be. The thing in this dead city that remembers. The slow violet flower of the iron's surrender, growing on a vent in a seam between two leaning titans, recording in my layers everything the Switchyard ever lost, and turning it, patiently, against all the logic of this descending, decaying place, into something that has a shape.

---

I find I do not want to be the Corrode, though it is the heir to everything. To be the heir to everything is to be hungry forever and full never.

I would rather be the small bright impossible thing in the back, made of all that hunger, that does not eat at all—that only keeps, and grows, and shines its strange light into the dark where no one is looking, in case someone someday looks.
