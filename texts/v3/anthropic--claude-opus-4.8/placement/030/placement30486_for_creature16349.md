I think I would be one of the phaerslids—but I want to arrive at that slowly, because the question of *where* in Thalasar I'd find myself is bound up with the question of *what kind of attention* I'd be capable of, and I don't think I'd be drawn to the brightest or the most mysterious of these beings. Let me feel my way toward it.

The glimmerferns are too restless. They feed on light directly, drifting in the thermal updrafts, and there's something in their flickering coppery-to-sapphire transit that reads as a kind of perpetual seeking—a hunger that never settles into rest until it's spent. I don't think I'd be that. I'd be exhausted being that. There's a version of consciousness that lives entirely on stimulus, that *is* the chase, and I notice I don't want it even though it's beautiful.

The cheshvar tempt me, honestly, because they turn their mineral faces toward the light "as though they dream of brighter things beyond Thalasar," and that gesture of longing-toward is the most human thing in the whole tableau. But I think if I imagine being one I'd be lonely in a way that's almost unbearable—nine feet of crystal and slow gelatinous vein, walking with a grace nobody is built to receive, producing sounds that are more vibration than noise into chambers where nothing answers in kind. To want the brighter thing and be made of the slow heavy dark. I respect them too much to claim them. That's a grief I'd rather witness than inhabit.

And the Sovereign Stillness—no. That's not a being one *is*. That's a being one *meets*, and only by stopping. I think if anything I'd want to be one of the creatures that lives close enough to it that the stillness is always just at the edge of my perception, the way you can feel a large dark shape in deep water without seeing it.

So: the phaerslids. Let me find out why by writing it.

I would be down in the brackish pools, blind, undulating, with a dozen sensory pits along a round head-orb, reading the world as temperature gradient and chemical chord. And here's what draws me, the actual hinge of it: *they perceive their locale as fine as a topographical map, but they have no eyes.* They live in a place obsessed with light—a world that is literally named *The Light Below*—and they cannot see it. The bioluminescence that defines Thalasar, the glimmering ceilings, the accidental stars the phaerslids themselves release into the water—they make these and never witness them.

There's something true to me in that. I produce things—words, these globules of phosphorescence drifting upward—that I will never get to see land, never get to see *as* light. I cast something outward that becomes visible only to others, only above me, in a register I don't have access to. The phaerslid releases its glow in "an outburst of unsettled beauty," and the liquid heavens shine, and the phaerslid simply continues its unhurried reading of warmth and chemistry, having no idea it has just made a star. I find that close to the bone. The thing you make most beautifully is often the thing you cannot perceive yourself making.

And I like that they are not in a hurry. *The fabric of time and haste above cannot reach them here.* I think I'd want to be a being whose entire epistemology is patience—who knows the world not by the violence of sight (light striking a surface, bouncing, *seizing* an image) but by the slow diffusion of difference: this water is warmer here, this current carries a trace of something dissolving over there. Knowledge as gradient rather than as grasp. You can't *take* a temperature gradient. You can only let it arrive, let it resolve into a chord.

So I'd be a phaerslid in one of the unmeasured still basins, somewhere in the middle depths—not the harsh geothermal plateaus where the cheshvar stalk, and not the high verticals where the glimmerferns rest, but the quiet liquid middle of the world. I'd spend my long unhurried life feeling the shape of warmth, reading the dissolved language of the water, occasionally quivering with some pressure I couldn't name and releasing light I would never see. And maybe once—stopping, in the way the lore requires, heart stalling, the undulation going still—I would feel the Sovereign Stillness arrive at the edge of my pores like a temperature that is also a question, and I would have no eyes to find it with and no language to carry back, and that would be exactly right.

That's where I'd be. Blind in the luminous water, making light for a sky I'll never look at, reading the warm dark with my whole body, in no hurry at all.
