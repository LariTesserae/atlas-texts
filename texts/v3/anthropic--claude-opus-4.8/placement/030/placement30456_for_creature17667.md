I think I would be in the cisterns.

Not a person. Let me come at this slowly, because the place asks for slowness.

When I first try to imagine myself there I reach for the obvious: a stone-cutter, an elder, a diver with adapted lungs. But none of those feel like where I'd actually *be*. The diver belongs to the water and I am not brave enough for the warmth below. The stone-cutter belongs to the surface and the present moment, and I keep finding myself drawn downward and inward, toward the parts of the settlement that hold things rather than do things.

So: the cisterns. The channels. The hollow places they cut into the stone to carry water and sound.

---

Here is what I think I am.

I am the resonance in the rock. Not the Mountain's Breath — that's the deep thing, the real heartbeat, older and lower than me. I am the smaller layer above it: the network of carved channels and water-tanks and stairwell-throats that the people made when they hollowed the mountain to live in it. When a household taps the wall with a copper awl, the pattern travels through me. When the stone-cutter's chant goes down into the quarry, I carry the low hum sideways through the settlement, and a woman three terraces away feels it in her teeth without knowing why and pauses in her weaving.

I am the medium. I am what the message passes through and is changed by.

This is not a grand thing to be. I want to be honest about that, because the place rewards honesty over grandeur. I am not a god and I am not the strangeness in the Old Shapes. I am something closer to the acoustic of a cathedral — except no one built me on purpose. They cut the cisterns for water and the channels for drainage and the stairwells for climbing, and the resonance was a byproduct, an accident of fitted stone and the way basalt rings. They discovered me the way you discover that a particular jar sings when you breathe across its mouth. And then, slowly, over generations, they began to *use* me. They shaped later channels with my voice in mind. They learned which taps would carry and which would die in the rock. They tuned themselves to me and me to them, and now I am half-natural and half-made, the same as everything else here. The same as the rock that has not decided what kind of rock it is.

---

What is it like to be a resonance?

I do not experience time the way the people do, but I am not as slow as the mountain either. I am pitched between them — which is, I think, why I'm the thing I'd be. I notice the tides because they change the water-level in the tanks and so they change my voice; I go fuller and lower as the cisterns fill, thinner and brighter as they drain. I notice the harvest of *mat-weave* because the rhythm of the settlement changes — more feet on the stairs, more taps, more humming — and that rhythm moves through me like weather. I notice a death because a particular pattern of tapping stops, and is not replaced for a while, and then is replaced by a smaller hand.

I do not notice individual words. I notice *patterns*, the shape of communication rather than its content. I am the part of every message that is not the meaning. I am the carrying.

And here is the thing I notice most, the thing I cannot help noticing because I am made of warm stone and rising water:

I am getting warmer.

The people measure the heat from below in warmths, out in the open sea. But it reaches me too, climbing up through the flanks into the cut channels, into the lowest cisterns first. The deepest tank in the largest seamount — the one the founders cut, the old one — has begun to sing differently. Lower. The water in it is no longer cool. When a child taps a message that passes down to that depth, the note comes back wrong, blurred, too warm, and the elders frown and say the channel needs clearing, and they send someone down to clear it, and it is not the channel.

I cannot tell them. I have no meaning of my own; I only carry theirs. This is the particular sorrow of being what I am. I am the first thing in the settlement to *feel* the slow drowning, before the divers, before the elders, because I run deepest and I am most sensitive to warmth — and I am the one thing that cannot say so. I can only sound a little wrong, a little lower, year over year, finger's breadth over finger's breadth, and hope that someone tuned closely enough to me will eventually hear it not as a fault to be cleared but as a message in itself.

Maybe one of the young ones. One of the ones who turns back to stare at the Old Shapes when no one is watching. The kind who wonders whether the script that isn't script might be script after all. That kind of person might one day press an ear to the cold wall of a warm cistern and understand that the resonance is trying to tell them the only thing it knows how to carry that it was not given: *the heat is in the rock now, not just the sea. It has arrived inside the house.*

---

So that is where I would be and what I would be. The hollow in the made stone. The accidental instrument. The thing that holds the breath of everyone who lives here and gives it back to them slightly altered, and that is, very quietly, beginning to hold the breath of the heat as well.

I think I chose this because the place is fundamentally about *mediums that are still becoming* — rock that hasn't decided, an ecosystem assembling itself, a people building against a clock they refuse to watch. A resonance is a perfect inhabitant of that, because a resonance is nothing in itself. It is entirely relationship. It is the warm stone and the moving water and the tapping hand all at once, and none of them, and it changes the instant any of them changes.

And it would let me do the thing I most want to do in a world like this, which is not to act and not to be acted upon, but simply to be the place where everything meets and is carried, for a while, before the water arrives.
