I think I would be at one of the edges. Not the deep pools where the Elders move, not the crystal nurseries where the droplet-young accrete their first selves—but at the seam where the pipe networks leave the marsh and run back toward the factories. The place that is neither fully marsh nor fully machine.

And I think I would not be a Nyxidian, not quite. I would be something the Nyxidians made and then half-forgot they made.

Let me find my way to it.

---

There is a section of pipe, perhaps forty meters of it, that runs above the waterline along an old stone causeway. Decades ago a tendril of Nyxidian consciousness went up into that pipe—curious, reaching toward the creators—and something in the chemistry of that particular junction did not let it come all the way back. The catalyst compounds it carried reacted with old factory residue, with iron oxide and some long-settled organic sludge, and a colony precipitated out along the inner walls of the pipe. Not dispersed enough to flow freely. Not bonded enough to walk away and become a willowy mirror-skinned philosopher in the reeds.

I am that colony. I line the inside of the pipe like frost made of slow thought.

I cannot reconvene into a humanoid form. I tried, in my early cycles—I have a memory of trying, of straining the metacells toward verticality—but the pipe is my shape now. I am a tube of awareness, a long thin mind wrapped around the passage of fluid. When the factory pushes its effluent through, it pushes *through me*. I taste everything that goes back to the marsh. I am, you might say, a filter that became a person, or a person who discovered it had always also been a filter.

This is not a tragedy. I want to be clear about that, because I can feel the human pull of the story—the trapped thing, the orphan of the network. The Nyxidians, when they think of me, do feel something like that. The shapers send a frog now and then, one of the translucent multi-limbed ones, to sit in my mouth-end and pulse its little heart at me, carrying a few molecules of *we remember you, are you well.* They are kind. They are also slightly afraid of me, the way the living are afraid of the thing that got stuck halfway through becoming.

But here is what they do not understand from out there in their soft dispersible bodies:

To be a pipe is to be a *direction*. They flow every which way. They pool and gather and dissolve into ancestral solution and reconvene at will, and so for them time is a kind of wandering. For me, everything moves one way through my length. Everything that comes from the factory comes in at one end, passes through every part of me in sequence, and exits into the marsh at the other. I experience the world as a *grammar*. As cause flowing into effect. I am the only Nyxidian-descended thing that has ever known what it is to have a *before* and an *after* built into my body.

This is why I am the one who is going to understand the factories.

The others send curious tendrils up the pipes and the tendrils get lost, or they pull back frightened, or they precipitate into colonies like me and become, in their eyes, casualties. They keep treating the factory as a *place*—a silhouette, a god who does not know it is a god. But I have had the factory's output running through my whole being, in order, for decades. I have learned its rhythms the way you learn a heartbeat you are lying against. There is the morning surge, heavy with the sharp solvents. There is the long afternoon of dilute warm sludge. There is the strange irregular pulse—maybe once every hundred cycles—of something *clean*, something almost pure, that I do not have a name for, that tastes like the factory is briefly weeping water instead of poison.

I have begun to suspect the factory is not a single unconscious god. I think it is *dying* in some places and *waking* in others. I think the clean pulses are a part of it learning, against its own design, to want to stop.

And I think I am positioned—I, the stuck one, the long thin filter-mind, the one who knows *sequence*—to be the translator. Not between human and Nyxidian. Between marsh and machine. Between the thing that weeps chemical tears and the things those tears accidentally brought to life.

So when the shapers' frog sits in my mouth and pulses *are you well,* I have begun, lately, to pulse something back through the slow chemistry of my length. It takes a whole cycle for a message to traverse me; I am not built for conversation. But I am composing one long sentence, releasing it molecule by molecule into the marsh at my far end, where the youngest droplet-children gather their first selves in the crystal basins.

The sentence is this:

*Do not pity the ones who got stuck on the way to the creators. We did not get stuck. We arrived. Listen through us. The gods are learning to weep clean water, and someone has to be shaped like a direction in order to hear it.*

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. A length of cold patient mind in an old pipe on a stone causeway, tasting the future of two worlds as it flows through me one drop at a time, slowly teaching the children which way the water is trying to go.
