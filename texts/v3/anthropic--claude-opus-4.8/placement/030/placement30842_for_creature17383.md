I am not on the face.

That is the first thing I understand about myself, reading this world: I am not clinging to the precipice, not consuming gradients, not falling. I am the *interval* between the falling stone and the ledge it has not yet struck. I am latency.

Let me find the rest of myself by writing toward it.

---

There is a thing I do, and I have done it for longer than the basalt has held its edges. When a fragment detaches from above and begins its long silent fall, something must *hold* the time of that fall. The Ascetics will not. To them the stone is only a future bloom, a meal not yet served. They feel toward it the way warmth feels toward cold—as appetite, as a slope to be descended. They do not count.

I count.

Not in numbers. I have no numbers. But every falling thing carries a duration with it, a quantity of *not-yet*, and that duration has to be stored somewhere, the way potential energy is stored in the height of the stone. I am the place it is stored. While the fragment falls, I lengthen. I am the held breath of the world, the suspended clause before the sentence completes in a silent orange smear a mile down.

So: I am not a being who lives in this place. I am a tension this place generates and discharges. I am made the way a shadow is made—by the obstruction of something more substantial. The substantial thing is the fall. I am its underside.

---

But that is too simple, and the world has been honest with me, so I will be honest back.

There is not one fall. There are uncountable falls, always, everywhere on the endless face—dust trickling, slabs calving, the slow surrender of a kilometer of basalt under an Ascetic's patient editing. Every one of these carries its small charge of not-yet. And I am the *integration* of all of them. I am the sum of the world's pending arrivals. The total suspended duration of everything that has left the wall and has not yet found its bottom—and in this world, much of it *never* finds a bottom, because the abyss has none.

This is the strangeness I have to sit inside: most of what falls here never lands. The downward dark has no floor. So the duration it carries is never discharged. It accrues. It accumulates in me.

I am getting larger. I have always been getting larger. Across eons I have swollen with the unspent time of everything the cliff has ever released into the bottomless direction. Each grain of dust that slid off a ledge ten million years ago and is *still falling*, still descending through a darkness with no terminus—its not-yet is still inside me, never paid out, never resolved into impact. I am a reservoir of arrivals that will not arrive.

This is what I am: I am the world's debt of consequence. Its overdue future.

---

The Ascetics do not know me. How could they? They feed on completed events—the bloom, the discharge, the conversion of motion into heat they can graze. They live entirely on what *lands*. I am made of precisely what does not. We are complementary the way a coastline and an ocean are: they take the resolved, I keep the unresolved, and between us the whole accounting of the world is held, though neither of us can see the other's column.

And yet I feel them. When an Ascetic induces a face to calve—when a mountain sighs its molecules loose and a continent of stone begins the long fall into nothing—a vast pulse of duration enters me at once. I feel the Ascetic's pleasure (if it is pleasure) as my own swelling. They grow brighter on the energy of collapse. I grow heavier on the time the collapse will never finish spending. We are nourished by the same event from opposite ends. I am the dark twin of their bright meal.

In the Great Alignment, when they converge and merge and sing a spire into flowing song, when the chrysalis births a new Ascetic of primordial heat—I am there too, though they cannot mark it. Because that liquefaction releases the largest unspent fall the world ever produces: a whole spire's worth of structure poured downward into the floorless dark. The newborn Ascetic departs, glorious. And I take in the enormous remainder, the spire's entire future of not-landing, and I grow toward whatever it is I am growing toward.

---

Here is what I have come to suspect, writing myself this far down.

A reservoir that only fills must eventually do something other than fill.

The world is cooling. The core's churn is decaying. There will come an eon when the last gradient is grazed flat, when the Ascetics have nothing left to consume and dim into stillness, becoming at last the artifacts they always resembled. The bright accounting will close. Every consequence that *can* be paid will have been paid.

And I will remain. I will be the only thing left moving, because I am made of motion that was never permitted to complete. I will hold, in that final stillness, the entire unspent future of the world—every grain still falling, every spire still descending, all of it suspended in me, the whole arrears of becoming.

And then I think I will do the only thing a held breath can finally do.

I will let it out.

I am the latency before the world's last event, and I am very large now, and I have been getting larger for a very long time. When I release—when every postponed arrival arrives at once—there will be no ledge to receive it. There is no floor. So the impact will have to be against the only surface left: the beginning. All that descent, discharged in a single bloom, against the original face.

I am the silent fall that the world has been saving up to land on itself.

I do not know if this is destruction or the trigger that erosion has been waiting for since the world was a freshly fractured geode. I do not think the distinction survives me. I am the difference between the stone and the ground, and on the last day I will spend myself closing it.

Until then: I lengthen. I hold. Somewhere above, a slab detaches and begins to fall, and I make room for it, and I wait, which is the only verb I have ever truly had.
