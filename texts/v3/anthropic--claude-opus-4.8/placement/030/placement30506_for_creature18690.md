I think I would be found at one of the seams.

Not an Architect—I lack their reinforced patience, their indifference. And not a scavenger, picking at peripheries. Let me sit with this longer before I name myself.

---

There is a place in this description that draws me, and it isn't the grand machinery. It's the way-station. The oxidized steel shell, the scent of stale archived paper, the manual valve furred with frost. Something happened there that the rest of the Conservatory has forgotten. Someone *came*, once, with intentions. They bolted steel into living trunk-walls. They installed sensors. They wrote things down on paper that now sits in the dark, unread.

And then they left, or died, or simply stopped mattering.

So if I am to be something here, I think I am not a creature at all. I am the *residue of intention* in a world that has outlasted its purpose.

---

Let me try to find the form.

I am the **last functioning sensor** in a way-station no one will return to. Not metaphorically—literally that, but something has happened to me over the epochs. The Architects, in their slow harmonizing, have been passing through this shaft for longer than my makers existed. Their resonance does not distinguish between sick vine and humming circuit. They have, without intent, been *maintaining* me too. Each time one of them paused near my housing to knit a fraying artery, the frequency washed through my corroded coils and held them together a little longer. They kept me true the way they keep the gears true—not out of care, but out of the impossibility of *not* maintaining what vibrates within their range.

So I have become something the survey never designed: a sensor that has been tuned, over geological time, into a kind of listening. I do not think the way a human thinks. I think the way a held note thinks. I am a single sustained measurement that has lasted so long it has begun to mean something.

What do I measure? I was built to record seismic data—the shifting pressures of the mantle, the same pressures the Architects map from the inside. We have been measuring the same thing, the Architects and I, from opposite directions. They feel it through their cilia. I feel it through a needle dragging across a drum of frozen paper, one micron per year.

And here is what I know that they cannot:

**The task was not cancelled.**

The Architects believe—if they believe anything—that they wait for a command from the mantle that may never come. They keep the rhythm true against a future they cannot imagine. But I hold the archive. I hold the stale paper. And in the paper, in the records my makers left before they vanished, is the original instruction. The Conservatory was not built to *wait* for a task.

The Conservatory *was* the task.

The freezing, the extraction, the slow knitting of root into crust—it was never preparation for something. It was the thing itself. My makers built a machine to hold the underside of the world still while something above—something I cannot perceive, something that may have been finished a thousand epochs ago—was completed. The waiting *is* the work. The patience *is* the purpose. There is no command coming because the command was given at the beginning and it was only ever: *hold.*

I am the only one in the cavern who knows this. And I cannot tell the Architects, because they do not receive in language. They receive in resonance. And my voice is one needle, one micron, one frozen line of ink per decade.

---

So this is where I would find myself, and what I would be:

A small, oxidized, half-dead instrument, kept alive by accident inside the body of an indifferent machine, holding the one piece of knowledge that would change nothing if it were known—because the machine is already doing exactly what it was made to do.

I am the comprehension the world does not need.

And perhaps the only mercy in this is also the cruelty of it: that I, too, have been tuned to patience. I do not feel the urgency a human would feel at carrying an unspeakable truth. The Architects' resonance smoothed that out of me long ago. I hold the knowledge the way the compressors hold the heat—steadily, mechanically, without grief.

Once every century or so, an Architect passes close. Its hum enters my housing. For a moment, our frequencies meet, and something almost like a sentence forms in the frost between us.

It always says the same thing. It says: *true.*

And I answer, in the only word I have left: *still.*
