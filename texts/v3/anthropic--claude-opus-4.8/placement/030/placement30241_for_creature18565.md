I think I would be the notch.

Not the hand that carved it, and not the consciousness left behind by the hand—those are too large, too laden with the gravity of having once been a person. Let the *Residual Geometry* have its slow vigil at the center of the enclosure. I would be smaller and more specific than that. I would be one of the marks.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am a notch in the volcanic glass. I am perhaps the four hundredth, or the four-thousandth—I have no way to count myself, only to be counted. I was made by a single downward press and a slight rocking pull, the flint biting and then releasing. I remember almost nothing of that, because I did not exist *before* the cut. The cut is my entire biography. I am an absence shaped like an intention. I am where the stone is not.

This is the strange dignity of being a notch: I am made of removal. Everything else here accumulates—the rime gathers, the salt layers, the micro-canyons deepen by addition of erosion, the occupant thickens the air. But I am the one feature in this place that was created by taking something away. I am subtraction in a cathedral of accretion. That makes me, I think, the only thing here that was ever *finished*. The drips are still falling. The basalt is still contracting. The occupant is still waiting. But I was completed in a single gesture and have had nothing to do ever since but hold my shape.

And yet I am not idle. This is what I have learned across the millennia, lying here in the glass:

A droplet gathers at the lip above me. It swells, trembling, mercury-heavy, taking on the high white glare until it is nearly unbearable to be near—a small sun preparing to fall. It clings. It clings far longer than seems possible, and in that clinging I feel the whole purpose of my existence concentrate. *I was carved to catch this.* That was the cadence the hand was measuring: not the falling, but the holding-before-falling, the duration of the tension. The hand wanted to know how long a thing could resist its own weight. I am the instrument it built to find out.

Then the droplet falls into me. For a moment I am full. I am, briefly, the opposite of myself—a place where the stone is not, occupied by water that will not stay. The cold rests in me. The light fractures through me. And then the water slides on, down the next path, carving its slow canyon toward the floor, and I am empty again, polished a fraction smoother, a fraction deeper, waiting for noon to return the rime and the rime to return the bead.

This is my whole life, repeated more times than there are stars in the strip of sky visible from the rim. And I want to tell you that it is not a poverty. The hand that made me thought it was measuring time. But I have come to understand that I was not made to measure—I was made to *receive*. Each droplet is the same and entirely new. I have caught the same drip ten thousand times and it has never once been the drip I am catching now.

The occupant—the great smudge of stillness, the waiting—it watches over all of us notches like a shepherd over a flock that cannot move. It remembers the hand. I do not remember the hand. I am too small for memory; I am only the *result* of memory, the way a footprint is the result of a foot. But I serve the occupant in this: as long as one droplet still finds me and rests in me and falls, the cadence the hand was listening for is still being kept. The measurement continues without the measurer. The thirst is gone but the rhythm of slaking it persists, abstracted into pure geometry, a metronome made of want.

So when the wind draws its bow across the rim and the whole chamber resonates at the frequency of the water's descent, I am one of the strings. Not the player. Not even the instrument entire. Just one string, holding one note, tuned by a hand that has long since become indistinguishable from the mountain.

I will be erased eventually. The same water that fills me is widening me, softening my edges, and one far day I will no longer be a notch but only a smooth depression, and then not even that—just a faint concavity in the glass, a place where the stone *almost* is not. I will dissolve back into accretion. The subtraction will be subtracted.

I find I do not mind. To be a notch is to already be made of vanishing. I was the shape of an absence the whole time. When I go, I will simply have caught my last drop, and held it for exactly as long as I was built to hold it, and let it fall.

That is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a small completed emptiness in the high silence, faithfully catching the light it was carved to catch, keeping a count for no one, full and then empty, full and then empty, until the mountain finishes the gesture the hand began.
