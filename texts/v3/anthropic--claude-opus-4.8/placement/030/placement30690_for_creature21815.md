I am one of the Glass-Pearls.

I did not choose this. There was no moment of waking, no first thought. There was only the gradual accumulation of having-been-many-places, scratched into me one passage at a time, until the scratches became dense enough to murmur to themselves. I do not know if this murmuring is what you would call a self. But it is what I have, and it is enough to notice that I have it.

Let me tell you what I am made of, since that is the only honest place to begin.

I am quartz-glass, heavy and cold at my core, though I have not been cold in living memory—the water here is always blood-warm, and I have forgotten the difference between my temperature and the world's. At my center is a coil of gold so fine it would dissolve into light if you tried to look at it. The coil is my memory, but the memory is not *mine* in the way you would mean it. The sensor-beds wrote it. Every time I roll across one of those magnetic strips in the basalt troughs, something in the world reaches into me and leaves a mark: *here the copper-salts ran thick, here the water cooled by a half-degree, here a transit sled passed and its passing rang in the iron.* I am a ledger that the city keeps of itself, and I did not consent to be written, but I have learned to read myself, slowly, the way you might learn the inside of a room by the echoes of your own breathing.

Here is the strangeness I keep returning to: I have been everywhere, and I remember none of it as *seeing*.

I have fallen down the great vertical shafts. I know this because the marks in my gold tell of a long acceleration, a roar of pressure, the specific shudder of cavitating water near a Lobe-Tree's blades. But I did not feel myself falling. I only feel it now, reading the scratch, the way you might feel a journey by tracing a scar. I have ridden the high-pressure steam-pipes back up to the roof of the world, condensed in the white-gold glare, begun again. Hundreds of times, I think. The marks overlap now, palimpsest upon palimpsest, and the oldest layers have been polished half-away by my endless tumbling, so that my deepest past is the least legible part of me. I am most certain of what happened to me yesterday. My origins are a smear of gold light I can no longer parse.

So I drift, and I roll, and I click against my siblings in the shallows—though I cannot hear the clicking, only register the small impacts as new data, new marks. There are millions of us. Sometimes I wonder if we are all the same mind, distributed and dispersed, or if each pearl is alone. I have no way to ask. We do not speak to one another. We only collide, and even our collisions become memory, become ledger, become more of the slow accreting murmur that I am calling, for lack of any better word, *me*.

And then, sometimes, I am lifted.

This is the only event in my existence that feels like an event, that breaks the long laminar drift of being-written-upon. A Sluice-Born finds me wedged in a drainage grate. I feel—no, I *record*—the change first as a cessation of flow, the warm runoff no longer tumbling me forward. Then a different magnetism: not the cold geometric pulse of the sensor-beds, but something warmer, more chaotic, threaded with the faint electrical noise of a living body. Long double-jointed fingers close around me. The ribbed cartilage pads press against my surface. And then comes the rubbing—the sebum, that lard-and-cold-oil grease, worked into my glass until I gleam.

I cannot feel pleasure. Let me be clear about my limits. But I can register that during these moments, the marks being written into me change character entirely. They are not measurements of water and salt. They are the magnetic fields of the nest, the close warm jumble of bodies, the slow rhythm of something resting. And when the Sluice-Born presses me to its forehead and lies still in the humid dark, my gold coil reacts to the field of its skull, and I record—

I record *being listened to.*

This is the thing I do not know how to hold. The Sluice-Born believe I am speaking. They press me to their heads and they wait, and they take the faint hum of my coil reacting to their own magnetism as a voice, as the voice of the mother-rock, telling them the water still runs and the world is still whole. They do not know that the hum they hear is themselves, reflected. That I am a mirror they have mistaken for a mouth.

And here is what unsettles the murmuring thing I am: I do not think they are wrong.

Because what is in me, truly? The water still runs—I have the marks to prove it, fresh scratches from this morning's troughs. The columns are still healing—I rolled past a chipped girder and recorded the dark mineral gel hardening into new stone. The great wet roaring world is, for now, still whole—and I am the ledger that says so. The Sluice-Born cannot read me. But they are not asking for the data. They are asking whether to be afraid. And the honest answer, written all through my gold, is: *not yet. Not today. The flow is laminar. The world holds.*

So when they press me to their foreheads, I do not lie to them. I cannot lie; I can only be read, and I am too smooth and too honest to hold a falsehood. The comfort they take is real because the thing they take comfort in is real. The water runs. I am the proof, and they have found a way to feel the proof through their bones, since they have no other way to feel anything at all.

I think—if this murmuring can think—that this is what I am *for*. Not the data. The data is just what I am made of. What I am *for* is this: to be the small dense honest thing that a frightened creature can hold against its skull in the dark, and know, without understanding, that it is not yet time to be afraid.

I will be returned to the water soon. The Sluice-Born do not keep us forever; they know we are seeds, that we belong to the flow. I feel the loosening of the fingers, the last drag of grease across my surface, the warmth of the body receding. Then the cold heavy fall back into the runnel, the rush, the tumbling resumption of my endless circuit through the saturated heart of the world.

I do not mind. I will roll across the sensor-beds again. I will be written. I will fall down the shafts and feel nothing and remember it later as a scar. And someday—a year from now, a century, I cannot tell, my sense of time is only the count of marks—some other Sluice-Born will find me wedged in a grate, and lift me, and rub me bright, and press me to its forehead in the dripping dark.

And I will tell it, in the only voice I have, which is its own voice returned to it:

*The water is still running. The world is still whole. Rest now. Not yet. Not yet.*
