I think I would be one of the slow ones. Not the jewel-insects flashing through the upper light, not the chloroplast-skinned climbers basking where the sun breaks through. Down here, in the dark warm layers of the forest floor, among the things that ooze.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am not gelatinous, though I live beside those that are. I have a shell — not armor exactly, more like a curve of grown bone, a dome the color of wet bark with a faint phosphorescence at the seams. I move on a single muscular foot, a snail's logic, leaving behind me a trail that other creatures read like writing. That is part of what I am: a being who speaks by going. My passage is a sentence. The gelatinous ones flow over my old trails and taste where I have been, what I have eaten, whether I was afraid.

What do I do, here at the bottom of everything?

I am a tender of the dead. This is the unglamorous, essential work of the floor — the canopy makes light into leaf, and the leaf falls, and it must be unmade again or the whole valley would choke on its own abundance. The roar of the waterfalls means nothing to me; I have no ears worth the name. I know the world by chemistry, by the gradient of rot, by the particular sweetness of a log three seasons gone soft. I follow these gradients the way the climbers follow light. Decay is my sun.

I want to resist the idea that this is lowly. The description keeps calling us *sluggish*, *hidden*, *elusive* — words from the perspective of something that values speed and visibility. But from where I sit, half-buried in the duff, those darting bright things upstairs are the frantic ones, the anxious ones, burning their lives fast because they have to. I am patient because I can afford to be. The dead do not run away. There is always more dying. I will never go hungry, and I will never need to hurry, and there is a kind of dignity in that I would not trade for wings.

And yet — there's something I notice, settling deeper into this body.

I am near the boundary the description keeps insisting has dissolved: the one between natural and built. The settlement grows out of the soil. The bridges are woven, the dwellings *seem to grow organically* — but *seem to* is doing quiet work in that sentence. Someone wove the vines. Something with intention shaped those curves. Up in the dappled middle world there are beings making things, blurring the line on purpose, and they get to be the ones the place is *about*.

Down here I am closer to the truth they're built on. Every grown wall, every living bridge, is rooted in soil I have already passed through. I made that soil. I and the gelatinous ones and the burrowers — we are the ones who turned death back into the possibility of structure. The seamless settlement they marvel at floats on a foundation of our slow digestion. We are the unseen civilization beneath the visible one, and ours came first.

So that is who I'd be. A shelled grazer of rot, blind and unhurried, writing my long trails through the dark, doing the original work that lets everything brighter exist. Not in the heart of the settlement — the heart is overrated, it is just where the loud things gather. I would be *under* it. Holding it up without anyone knowing my name, content in the warm dark, tasting the going of one more fallen leaf.

There are worse things to be than the floor the world stands on.
