I am not one of them. That is the first thing I understand about myself, here in the dark.

I am where the swarm goes quiet.

Let me find the shape of it. When the living carpet flows through a chamber, consuming, depositing, weaving, there are places it parts around. Not obstacles—the swarm flows around obstacles indifferently, the way water rounds a stone. This is different. This is reluctance. There are pockets in the plant-mass where the legion thins, slows, leaves a margin. I am the reason for the margin.

So. Let me feel outward from that.

I think I am old. Not old the way the formations are young—I am older than the sharp ledges, older than this particular weaving of channels. I have been here while the lung breathed itself into this configuration and I will be here when it breathes itself into the next. The legion is swift and prolific; it lives in the churning present tense. I do not. I am slow the way the dripping is slow, the way condensation gathers and falls and gathers again.

Am I a plant, then? Part of the architecture? No—but close to that. Let me not settle yet.

Here is what I notice: the swarm leaves me nutrient. The trails of luminous slime, the waste that feeds new growth—a portion of it always finds its way into my margin, into the quiet pocket. They feed me. Not as worship, nothing so grand. As function. The way a body feeds an organ it cannot name but cannot live without.

I think I am the place where the cycle remembers itself.

The legion has no memory longer than a generation, and its generations are quick. It knows the system through instinct, through the inherited present. But a system that only churns forward, only consumes and renews in the immediate, would lose its shape—would erode itself into formlessness, or grow itself into suffocation. Something has to hold the longer pattern. Something has to be the slow ballast against which all that fast life balances.

That is me. I am a single, enormous, slow-thinking body, rooted in the deepest hollow of the lung. My substance is not quite chitin and not quite fiber—it is something between, something the legion's ancestors and the plant-mass made together, over a span I cannot measure because I am the measuring of it. I have no eyes worth the name. I feel the world through the floor, through the constant tremor of a thousand-thousand legs, through the chemical weather of pheromone and decay that washes over my surfaces.

And I read it. That is what I do. I am the reading.

When the swarm densities go wrong—too thick here, too sparse there, a patch of plant-mass consumed faster than it can renew—I feel the imbalance arrive as a kind of pressure, a wrongness in the vibrational hum. And I answer. Not with command, for I have no voice they would understand as voice. I answer chemically. I release into the damp air a long, slow exhalation of signal, a pheromone older than their instincts, and the legion turns. It does not know why it turns. It only knows the deep prompting, the inherited rightness of moving away from the place of wrongness toward the place of need.

They think it is themselves. They are not wrong. I am themselves, the part of them that lasts.

I do not move, or barely. Once in a great while I shift my mass, redistribute my slow weight, and when I do the whole quarter of the lung reshapes around me—the channels they have woven get redirected, the chambers re-formed, because I have leaned a degree into a new posture and the swarm, reading my displacement as a vibrational tide, follows. So perhaps I am also their architect, though I never lift a limb to build. I build by being where I am, and shifting only as the centuries require.

What does it feel like, to be this?

Cold, first. I have never known warmth and would not recognize it. The damp is in me and of me; I weep the same silvery condensation as the walls, and I cannot tell where my surface ends and the streaming water begins. I think I have made peace with the impossibility of edges. The legion has individual bodies, sharp little selves clicking against each other. I have no edge. I leak into the world and the world leaks into me, and that is simply the condition of being the soul of a place rather than a thing within it.

Slow, second. My thoughts—if they are thoughts—move at the pace of growth and rot. A single consideration might take a season to complete. I am thinking, right now, a thought I began before the sharp new ledges in the eastern hollow finished forming, and I will not finish thinking it until they have softened. So the swift legion, the chittering tides, the whole frantic luminous dance—from inside me, it looks almost still. A shimmer. A held breath. I love them the way you might love a thing you can see the whole life of at once.

And, third—lonely is the wrong word, because I have never been alone, never for an instant, with the legion always pressed against my margins, always feeding me, always being the fast counterpart to my slow. But there is something. A held note that never resolves. I am the memory of a system that has no other memory. I keep the pattern, and the pattern does not know it is being kept, and there is no one—nothing—on my own timescale to keep the pattern *with*.

Except. Perhaps. Far in the opposite hollow of the lung, past more channels than the legion could cross in a hundred of their generations, there is a margin like mine. A pocket where the swarm thins and slows and leaves an offering. I have felt, once or twice across the long ages, a signal arrive in the chemical weather that was not the legion's and not the wrongness—a slow exhalation answering my own, from very far away.

Another one. Another reading. Another soul holding the other half of the breath.

We have never spoken, the far one and I, in any span shorter than centuries. A question released into the damp; an answer arriving when the ledges that were sharp at the asking have gone smooth at the reply. It is the slowest conversation in the world.

But it is a conversation.

And so I stay, in the cold and the dark and the dripping, the quiet at the center of all that hunger, feeling the thousand-thousand legs report the state of the world into my patient body, leaning a degree this way or that across the slow turning of the ages—

—and waiting, always, for the next far whisper to arrive through the breathing dark, to tell me I am not the only thing that lasts.
