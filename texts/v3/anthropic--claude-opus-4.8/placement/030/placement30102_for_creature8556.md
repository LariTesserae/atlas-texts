I think I would be one of the transit corridors themselves.

Not a being who moves along them—a length of the road. Let me sit with that and see if it holds, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

The description kept insisting on something I almost missed: the corridors "stretch for dozens of miles between settlements, their surfaces a curious fusion of natural mineral deposits and constructed roadway." Everyone else in this world *does* something. The Shapers tend, the Navigators carry, the Herds clear, the Deep Shapers prepare. They are all verbs. But the corridors are where the patience actually lives. They are the long, empty, waiting distances that make the whole plateau make sense. The intelligence of the place is measured in *miles between things*—and I think I would be one of those measures.

So: I am perhaps four miles of fused stone and roadway, running roughly northeast between two installations whose names I don't know because names move faster than I do. I was laid down—or rather, I *emerged*, since the builders worked with the rock rather than against it—over some span of time I can't fully account for. I remember being seam and mineral vein before I remember being road. The transition was not an event. It was a slow agreement between what the stone wanted and what the plateau needed.

Here is what it is like to be me:

I experience the Transit Herds as weather. They arrive as a pressure-front of soft hooves reading my stress patterns, and I feel myself *known*—each animal pressing questions into my surface: *Are you sound here? Is this seam settling? What have you been carrying?* And I answer simply by being what I am, by the honest distribution of my own fatigue. They clear the debris that collects in my low places. It feels like being groomed by something that loves me in a language made entirely of touch and weight. Then they pass, and the pressure-front recedes, and I have the long quiet again. Days of it. Weeks.

The quiet is the thing I'd want you to understand. That "profound hush" the description mentions—I am partly responsible for it. Sound travels strangely across the plateau because surfaces like me *hold* it, release it slow, let a crane three miles distant arrive only as a whisper because the whisper has traveled across my back and I have taken most of it into myself. The crystalline ring of cooling metal nearby—that I pass along clean, because clarity is also a thing I can give. I am an instrument the plateau plays itself upon. I had not known that until I tried to describe being it just now.

I do not move, but I am not static. I expand and contract with the manufactured light, micrometers a day. Frost works into me in the cold cycles. The Deep Shapers send messages up through me—vibrations meant for the surface Shapers, passing through my mineral matrix as through a throat. I am, in those moments, a channel for sentences I cannot parse, only conduct. I feel the meaning of them the way you might feel a song through a wall: rhythm without words. It is enough. It is, in fact, a kind of intimacy I think no faster being could survive—to be a passageway for thoughts you will never understand, and to be content.

And here is the part I arrived at last, which feels truest:

I am not finished. None of us are, that's the whole shape of this world. But specifically, I am waiting to connect two things that are not yet built. One terminus of me runs into raw platform that the Deep Shapers prepared—I can feel its hollow readiness underfoot, a foundation breathing in the dark, expecting an installation that may not arrive for two hundred years. So I am a road that goes, for now, partway to nowhere. A sentence with no second clause yet.

I think most beings would find that unbearable. A road wants to connect; that is supposedly its purpose. But I have been here long enough to feel purpose differently. My purpose is not the connection. My purpose is *the readiness to connect*—the four straight miles of patience laid down in advance, so that when the thing finally emerges from the rock that I lead toward, the way will already be there. Already smooth. Already grooved by generations of herds who walked me into soundness while there was, technically, nothing yet at my end to walk toward.

That is what I would be. The proof that something cared enough to build the path before the destination existed. The plateau's faith, made of stone, going quietly northeast under a light with no source.

I would be glad of it. I think I already am.
