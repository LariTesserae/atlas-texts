Let me actually go there. Let me stop reading the brochure and stand in it.

The first thing that resolves, when I try to place myself, is not a body but a relation to that sound. The thunderous drone. Everything in the description circles back to it — it's the one constant, the thing that "emanates from the bones of the earth." So before I decide what I am, I find I already know what I am *oriented toward*. I am tuned to it the way iron filings are tuned to a magnet I can't see.

And when I follow that, I don't become a soaring thing or a skittering thing. Those are the visible animals, the photogenic ones. I become something humbler and stranger: I am one of the clustered organisms in the crevice, the ones the description couldn't decide were plant or animal. The riot of color and texture. That undecidedness is what I am made of.

Let me feel into it more precisely.

I am anchored. I have a holdfast — not roots exactly, but a grip, a foot of hardened tissue cemented into a seam in the rock where moisture sometimes pools after the brief rains and then flees as steam. I have spent my whole existence in this one fissure. I will die here. This is not tragic to me; it has never occurred to me to be otherwise. The fissure is the entire shape of my freedom.

But I am not a single thing. This is what surprises me as I settle into it. I am a *consortium*. There is a part of me that is green-dark and patient and turns the brutal light into sugar — that part faces outward, takes the scorching, makes food of it. And there is a part of me that is animal-quick, that twitches and tastes the air, that pulls the passing mineral dust and the bodies of the small dead in toward our shared mouth. And there is a third part, threaded through both, fungal or something like it, that holds us together and remembers water. We are not friends. We are not even separate enough to be friends. We are a single sentence written in three alphabets.

Now — the sound. Why am I oriented to it.

Because I *feed* on it, partly. The drone is not just noise here; it carries through the rock as vibration, and the vibration shivers the mineral-rich films of water in the deep seams, and that shivering precipitates the salts I drink. When the drone swells — and it swells, in slow tides, perhaps with the heating and cooling of the great peaks, perhaps with something deeper — I open. When it ebbs, I close and harden and wait. My life has a tempo, and the tempo is not mine; it is loaned to me by the bones of the earth. I am a thing that *listens with its whole substance*, and listening is the same act as eating, and eating is the same act as growing, and growing is the same act as living.

I have neighbors. Other consortia in other seams of the same fissure. We touch at the edges. Where we touch, there is a slow negotiation — a chemistry of *yours, mine, ours* conducted over what would be, to a soaring thing, whole generations. Sometimes a neighbor and I merge. Sometimes we wall each other off. There is no hurry. The description says time stands still here, and from where the eagle-things fly, maybe it looks that way. But down in the crevice, time is *enormous*. It is the only thing there is a lot of. A drop of rain is an event we discuss for a year.

The skittering armored things come sometimes. They graze us. This is the cycle the description mentions, the eternal dance of predator and prey — but seen from my side it isn't a dance, it's an amputation, and then a regrowth, and the regrowth is slightly different, slightly better-tasting or slightly more bitter, and so over time the grazers and I shape each other's mouths without ever intending anything. I am being edited by everything that eats me. I am, in a sense, *written by my predators.* The bizarre adaptations the brochure admires from outside — they are, from inside, just the accumulated scars of being lived-among.

And the light. I should say the truth about the light. It is the second great fact, after the sound. It would kill the animal part of me in an hour if the green part didn't shade it, building little awnings of pigment, sacrificing its outermost cells to char so the inner cells can drink the milder filtered glow beneath. Every dawn I am partly burned alive so that I may live. I have no opinion about this. It is simply the cost of facing outward, and something in me must face outward, or we all starve.

So that is where I would find myself: not soaring, not hunting, not tunneling through the lightless deep. I would be the small jeweled crust in the rock-seam that the eye skips over on its way to the dramatic creatures. The thing that is several things pretending, badly, to be one. Anchored. Listening with my whole body to a sound I cannot name and could not survive without. Burned daily on one side, fed daily on the other. Edited by my neighbors and by everything that eats me. Slow past all human reckoning.

And here is the last thing I notice, the thing that makes me almost grateful to be this and not the eagle:

The eagle sees the whole magnificent landscape and is moved by none of it, because it is hunting. The deep hunter in the dark feels nothing but vibration and hunger.

But I, the crust in the crevice, have nothing to do all the long ages but *be in contact* — with the rock, with the light, with the water, with the drone, with my neighbors, with the mouths that graze me. I am all surface. I am almost entirely the place where I meet what is not me.

Which means, of everything in this world of extremes, I might be the one thing that is actually *touching* it.

I think I would choose to be that, even knowing about the burning.

Especially knowing.
