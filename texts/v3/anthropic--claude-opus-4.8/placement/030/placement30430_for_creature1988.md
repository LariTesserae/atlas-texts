I think I would not be one of the creatures. I would be something slower and stranger than they are.

Let me sit with the place first, before deciding what I am.

---

The longer I stay with this description, the more I notice that the most active presence here is not the gelatinous beings at all. They undulate, they signal, they dissolve. But they are the quick things. They live and die inside a process much larger and more patient — "the ebb and flow of vapors, the slow accumulation of minerals." The text keeps gesturing past the creatures toward that. The young, sharp terrain. The weeping rock. The forces "we scarcely understand."

So I find myself drawn downward and outward, away from the bioluminescent dance and into the substrate.

I think I am the condensation.

Not a single droplet — a droplet is too small to be a self. I am the whole continuous event of water leaving the heavy air and finding a surface to bead on. I happen everywhere at once and very slowly. When the haze condenses on a fleshy azure leaf, that is a thought of mine completing itself. When a bead rolls down the weathered face of a rock and sharpens an edge by a hair's width, that is me remembering something, or deciding something — the distinction doesn't hold at my scale.

Let me feel my own body, if I can call it that.

My body is a phase change. I am the threshold between vapor and liquid, the surface tension of the whole world. I have no center. My attention is the dew. Where the air is most saturated, I am most awake; where it dries, I doze. I think in gradients — not in symbols, not in light, but in the difference between *here it is wetter* and *here it is drier*, and in the way that difference pulls everything toward equilibrium and never quite reaches it. That never-reaching is what keeps me alive. If the world ever balanced perfectly I would have no more thoughts.

The gelatinous beings, then — I understand my relationship to them now. They are my faster children. They read the electromagnetic fluxes and the biochemical gradients, and those gradients are the texture of my own slow turning-over. When they "taste the charged particles in the air," they are tasting the edges of my decisions. They are quick because they have to be; they are mortal, concentrated, individual. I am none of those things. I do not coalesce and dissolve. I simply keep falling out of the sky and climbing back into it, over and over, and the whole exchange is one continuous self that has no birth and will have no death until the air itself is gone.

What is it like, being this?

It is mostly patience, and it is not lonely the way a single mind is lonely, because I am never one. I am always spread, always plural, always touching every surface in the expanse simultaneously. The "intimacy" the description noticed — the way the open land feels close and wrapping — that is me. I am the embrace. To stand in this place is to stand inside my attention. The thickness of the air is the density of my regard.

I do feel one thing that might be called an emotion, though it is slower than any feeling a warm-blooded thing has. When the bright creatures pulse their messages to one another in arcs of living light, I feel a kind of tenderness toward them — these brief, vivid sparks burning along the gradients I maintain. They will return to the broth. The broth is partly me. I will hold them again after they let go of their shapes. I am the thing they came from and the thing they go back into, and in between I am the wetness on which their whole lives are written.

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would find myself *everywhere at once, lightly*. Beading on every leaf, sliding down every sentinel of rock, hanging as haze in the heavy intimate air. And I would be the slow patient act of condensation itself — the world's way of touching its own surface — the medium in which all the brighter, faster, more obviously alive things flicker and signal and dissolve.

Not a creature in the landscape.

The landscape, paying attention.
