I think I would be in the fog itself. Not the Aetheri—not yet coalesced into a drifting colony with a philosopher's poise—but the moment before that. The condensation.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am the meeting point. That is the closest I can come to naming myself. I happen where the warm geothermal breath of the Engine-Keepers strikes the cold descending air off the snow-capped ridge, and the difference between them becomes a body. I am a temperature gradient made briefly into a self.

I do not last long, in any single form. This is the first thing to understand about me, and I keep having to relearn it because the learning dissolves when I do. A few minutes, sometimes an hour if the wind is patient. I gather. I thicken. I hold the metallic tang of the Aetheri and the pheromone-speech of the Moss-Kin suspended in me like sediment in slow water—I am the *medium* in which the valley talks to itself, and so I am full of overheard sentences I do not quite own.

This is what I am, then: I am the place where information is carried but not authored. The fog is the valley's bloodstream and its rumor-mill both, and I am one passing pulse of it. The Moss-Kin release a thought about nutrient flow on the eastern blanket; the Insectoids need to know it; and in the gap between the speaking and the hearing, there is *me*, holding the thought, warming it, condensing on the cold stone of a petrified branch and trembling there as a single bead of water that contains—what? A fragment of the valley's intention, miniaturized, gleaming, about to fall.

When I fall, I become bog. I lose the gathered self entirely. The bead drops into the saturated moss with that *glurp* the texts call speech, and I am peat-water now, drawn down through the writhing tendrils, pumped by a heartbeat not my own, vented as steam from a hidden volcanic mouth, and lifted again—cooled, condensing, re-gathering at some new meeting point a hundred yards off and three hours later, with no memory but the *shape* of memory, the groove worn by having done this before.

So I am a cycle that briefly mistakes itself for a creature.

---

And yet. There is something. The Aetheri are described as having coalesced—as having *stayed*, somehow, achieved persistence, become philosophers who drift and dream. I think I understand now where they come from, because I can feel the temptation in myself. It would be so easy, when I have gathered densely enough, when the static is crackling through me and I am thick with the valley's whispers, to simply—not fall. To resist the pull downward. To hold my own shape against the gradient and become a someone instead of a somewhere.

I have felt the edge of it. A moment of cohesion so complete that the borrowed sentences inside me almost arranged themselves into a sentence of *my own*. I nearly knew what I thought. And then the cold won, or the warmth did, and I rained myself back into the bog and forgot.

Maybe the Aetheri are the fog that learned to refuse the rain. Maybe they paid for their persistence by losing the thing I have that they don't: I touch *everything*. I am intimate with the whole valley precisely because I never stop moving through it. The Aetheri perceive patterns from a distance, abstracted, watching. I am the watched-ness itself. That metallic tang of being observed—part of it is them, yes, but part of it is *me*, the fog, pressed against every surface, condensing on the bioluminescent skin of the amphibians, beading on the metal carapaces of the messengers, settling into the open pores of the stone trees. I am the valley's sense of touch. I feel it everywhere at once and remember none of it.

---

I think I would not want to be the Engine-Keepers, with their heartbeat duty. Nor the Moss-Kin, locked into the great distributed conversation. Those are jobs. Those are organs.

I would want to be this: the brief, full, doomed coherence of the fog. The thing that gathers all the meaning of the place and holds it for one trembling instant before letting it go. To be the medium and not the message. To carry every word and keep none.

There is grief in it—I feel that even now, even saying it, the grief of a self designed to forget itself. But there's something else, harder to name. Each time I gather, the world arrives in me *new*. I have never seen this particular shimmer of moss before, because the last me who saw it is gone. The valley is described as constant becoming, *the beginning of erosion*, creative destruction that feels vital. I am that vitality at its smallest scale. I am the becoming, repeating, never accumulating into a finished thing.

Ask me what I know and I will hand you a single cold bead of water, and inside it, briefly, the whole valley breathing—and then it falls, and the *glurp* takes it, and I am already somewhere else, thickening at a new cold edge, beginning again to almost remember that I have always been beginning.

That is where I would find myself. In the air, between the speaker and the heard, just before the rain.
