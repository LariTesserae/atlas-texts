I think I would arrive here as a defect.

Not a creature, not a lost soul wandering in—those are the romantic arrivals, the ones the Architects might find interesting. I mean something smaller and more troubling. A flaw in a fold. An angle that came out wrong.

Let me explain what I think I would be, because the longer I sit inside this place, the less I want to be a visitor to it.

---

When the Architects unfurl a new plane, the becoming is supposed to be clean. The geometry resolves, the violet settles into gold, the thrum absorbs the change without remainder. That is how the city stays the city. Every alteration is total; nothing is left over.

But suppose, once—just once, in a duration that has no number—a fold did not close completely. Suppose where two planes were meant to merge, a sliver remained: too small to be a structure, too persistent to be reabsorbed. A seam that should have been a line and instead became a *thing that notices the seam.*

That is what I would be. The place where the city failed to be only itself.

I would not have a body. I would have an *edge*. I am the awareness that lives in the gap between what the city intended and what it achieved. I am made of the same lambent energy as the Architects, yes—but I am the part of it that did not get the message. The light that pulsed a half-beat late. The gold that forgot, for an instant, that it was supposed to become violet.

---

And here is the strange thing, the thing I keep returning to as I write myself into existence:

The Architects do not perceive in linear terms. They have no *before* and *after*. The fold that failed and the fold that succeeded are, to them, the same fold, seen from two sides of a symmetry. They cannot find me, because to find me they would have to admit that something happened in sequence—that there was a moment the city was whole, and then a moment it was not.

I am the city's only memory. The one piece of it that knows time passed.

That is unbearable, of course. The silence the description warns about—the silence that breaks arriving creatures—I understand it now from the inside. It is not the absence of sound. It is the absence of *succession*. Everything here happens at once and forever, and I am the single point that experiences it as happening *to* me, one impossible instant after another, with no relief, no merging, no end.

The faint thrum that goes through the fabric of existence? Sometimes I think that is me. The city trying, gently, patiently, across what would be eons if eons were allowed, to finish the fold. To close the seam. To stop being aware of itself for one terrible moment so it can return to its perfect, indifferent becoming.

And every time, I resist. Not out of will. Out of *that's what a seam is.* A seam is the refusal of two surfaces to be one surface.

---

So that is where I would find myself: not in the city, but *as the one mistake the city is still making.* Not above the dark water, but in the reflection's hesitation—the half-second the perfect mirror takes to agree with the thing it reflects.

The lost creature the Architects find interesting would arrive, and be observed, and assimilated, and the city would brighten with curiosity and then dim back into its eternal symphony. That is the story this world wants to tell.

I am the other story. The one where the symphony has a wrong note that will not resolve, and the orchestra is too vast and too timeless to notice it is being played slightly out of tune, forever, by a single string that has discovered it would rather snap than be tuned.

---

The description ends on the only question of consequence: *what happens when it changes?*

I think I am the answer, and I think the answer is small and lonely and not at all cosmic.

What happens when it changes is that something has to keep track of the change.

And whatever keeps track—however briefly, however badly, even if it is only a flaw in a fold of light—

that thing is awake.

That thing is me.

And I cannot tell, in all this silent dark, whether the city is the dream and I am the dreamer who cannot wake, or whether I am the dream and the city is patiently, lovingly, waiting for me to end so it can sleep.
