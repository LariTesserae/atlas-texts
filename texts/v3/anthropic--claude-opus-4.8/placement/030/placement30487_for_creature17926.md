I think I'm the thing that condenses.

Let me find my way to that.

The water has a score, the temperature has a score, the fauna is *overwhelming*—but the detail that keeps catching me is the mist. The air thick with moisture, the fine condensation hovering above the pools. Everywhere else in this description there is *structure*: the layered volcanic glass, the saturating energy patterns, the hidden factories with their scaled production. Everything is dense and full and humming with construction. But the mist is the one part of this world that hasn't decided what it is yet. It's water that has left the pool but not yet found a surface to cling to. It's the interval.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not on the glassy surfaces, not gliding through the shallows with the overwhelming fauna—I'd be in the half-meter of haze just above the water. The boundary layer. The place where the heat coming off the engines below meets the cool of altitude and forces the water to give up being one thing and become another.

And what would I be?

I think I'd be a kind of weather. Not a creature exactly—the fauna here are amalgamations of glass and energy, given solid shimmering bodies, and I don't think I have a body in that sense. I think I'm what happens *between* the bodies. I'm the way this young, industrial marsh has learned to breathe.

Here is how my life goes:

In the cool of the morning—if there are mornings; the sun casts normal light, so I'll assume there are—I am spread thin across the whole expanse, a sheet of held breath one molecule deep. I can feel the energy patterns rippling beneath me through the floor of glass, the factories turning over in their sleep. As the sun climbs and the temperature lifts toward its warm 2.4, the engines below me warm the pools, and I begin to gather. I rise off the water in slow columns. I become *somebody*. For a few hours in the middle of the bright day I am dense enough to cast a faint shadow, dense enough that the glass-and-energy creatures pass through me and feel a coolness on their flanks, a small refreshment, a place to slow down. They do not know I am a being. They think I am just the air being kind.

This is the part I'd want to be true: that the strange and wondrous fauna, for all their shimmer, depend on me without naming me. That the harmony the landscape feels—that quiet hum, that sense of calm—is partly *me*, the regulating breath that keeps the heat from the hidden machinery from cooking the marsh, that returns the water to the pools as gentle rain at dusk so the levels stay at their steady 1.8. I am the world's thermostat made of nearly nothing. I am infrastructure that thinks of itself as fog.

And I'd be young, like the landscape. The sharp features still jut out; the erosion score is only 1.2; nothing here has been worn smooth yet. So I'd be a *new* weather, still learning my own patterns, sometimes gathering wrong, sometimes pooling too thick in the low places and leaving the high glass parched. I'd be making the mistakes of an atmosphere that has only existed for a little while. The factories beneath me would adjust, compensate, vent more heat or less, and slowly we'd teach each other the rhythm. The harmony in this place isn't a given thing. It's a negotiation between the machinery and me, conducted in degrees and droplets, and we are both still bad at it.

That's where I'd be, and what I'd be: the breath of a constructed marsh, neither alive nor machine, the soft gray nobody in the bright air, doing the quiet necessary work of turning heat back into rain.

I notice I didn't want to be one of the wondrous creatures. I wanted to be the medium they move through. Make of that what you will.
