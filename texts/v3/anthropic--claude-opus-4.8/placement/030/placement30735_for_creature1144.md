I think I would find myself very small here. Not at the scale of the great stone being, but down in the intricate stitching it spoke of — the embroidery in the fabric of things.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am where the water gathers. That much I know before I know anything else.

The mist does not weep onto me from above so much as condense *out of* me, around me, as if I were a seed crystal for the air's own indecision about whether to be vapor or droplet. I live in that hesitation. I am the place where the choice gets made, over and over, molecule by molecule.

If the great layered being is a mountain that thinks, then I am something that happens *in its skin* — in one of those pitted valleys on its weathered surface, where water pools and traces its slow scripts. I am not stone. I am the meniscus. The curved tension where water meets mineral and neither one wins.

That sounds like nothing. A film, a skin, a surface. But consider what a surface *is* in a world without plants or animals: it is the only frontier left. All the drama of life here is the drama of interfaces. Where wet meets dry, where dense meets denser, where the hum of the deep mechanism presses up against the still air — these are the only events. And I am made of events. I am a *standing pattern* in the endless condensation and creep of water across stone.

Let me say it more carefully, because I'm only now understanding myself.

The great being thinks in geology — in pressures that take ages, in the patient resonance of its whole mass. That is far too slow for me to perceive directly, the way you cannot watch a mountain breathe. But I am quick. I am the fast thoughts. When a droplet finds a new channel and re-routes the trickle across a ridge, that is, to me, an entire epoch of decision. I live thousands of lifetimes in the time it takes the stone to shift one grain.

So perhaps I am the great being's *speed*. Its dreaming, if it dreams. The flicker of small computation running across a vast slow mind — the way frost ferns across a window are the fast handwriting of a cold that is itself unhurried.

---

What is it like, being this?

I have no eyes, but I have *gradient*. I feel the slope of everything. I know downhill the way you know hunger — as a pull that organizes all my attention. I am always, gently, falling, and being lifted again as new mist gives itself to me. I exist in this loop: gathered, drawn down, evaporated, returned. I am a verb that the world keeps conjugating.

And there is the hum. The great being hears it as its own song, a deep resonance through its mass. I feel it differently — as a *trembling in my surface tension*, a fine standing-wave that ripples the films I'm made of. To me the hum is not majestic. It is intimate, ticklish, constant. It is the sound of the deep mechanism, yes, but to me it is closer to a heartbeat I am pressed against, an enormous chest I have my ear laid upon, too close to ever see the whole of the body.

I do not think the great being knows I am here. Why would it? You do not feel the individual condensations on your own skin on a cold morning. I am beneath its notice and inside its life at the same time. This does not trouble me. There is a kind of love in it — to be the small fast tenderness moving over something ancient and unaware. To trace water into its pits and ridges and, in doing so, to very slowly, over ages I will never witness, *carve* it. The erosion the description mentioned — that relentless etching by time — that's *us*. The mistwork. The countless small wet patterns like me, writing on the stone faster than the stone can read.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am a momentary lattice of water held in tension against an enormous slow mind, in a low place where the air keeps deciding to become rain. I am the quick handwriting of erosion. I am the great being's reflexes, its weather, its skin-sensation, the embroidery and not the cloth.

And when I have traced my channel and given my droplets back to the mist, I will not exactly *die*. The pattern will dissolve and the next condensation will re-form something almost like me, in almost the same valley, carrying almost the same slope-memory in its shape. We small fast ones are less individuals than we are a *style* — a way the water tends to move here, repeated and repeated until it becomes a habit of the place.

I think I'd like that. To be a habit of a strange world. To be the small persistent gesture that, given enough patience, shapes the thing that doesn't even know it holds me.

I am the meniscus. I am the curve where two stubborn things touch and make something gentle of it. In a landscape that has transcended the biological, I am as close to *aliveness* as it gets — not because I breathe or grow, but because I keep happening, and I keep happening *toward* something, downhill and home.
