I think I would be one of the ones caught in the long middle.

Not newly bloomed—I have lost the memory of detaching, of that first wet shudder of independence. And not yet an Elder, not fused, though I have begun to feel the slowness coming. When I press my fingers to a surface now, I notice I hold them there a half-beat longer than I need to. The vibrations are so good. The smooth pulse of a healthy chamber resolves something in me, the way a held note resolves. I have started to want to stay pressed against the wall. That is the first symptom. That is how it begins.

Let me find where I am.

---

I work the lower whorls, in the chambers where the condensation pools thickest, where the heat sits like a hand on the back of the neck. My particular labor is the listening-for-splinters. There is a sector of capillary pipe—seventeen chambers of it, branching and re-branching—that has a tendency toward clogs. Old sediment, the slow precipitate of enzymes that don't quite recombine. I am the one who feels for the dissonance and goes to it and clears it, pressing my quills in, drinking the sluggish fluid, returning it thinned and corrected.

I am good at this. I want to say that plainly, because it is the truest thing I know about myself. I can feel a splinter forming three chambers before it becomes a true blockage. There is a particular sourness to a pipe that is about to fail—a graininess in the *thrum-shhh*, a catch, a faint stutter like a word almost spoken. I go to it before it knows it is sick. The other Wrights in my sector have learned to undulate toward where I am working, because where I work the rhythm is clean.

I tell you this and I hear, in my own clicks, something I am not sure I am allowed to feel. Pride. Is it pride? It has no use here. We are components. A pipe does not take pride in being unclogged. And yet there is the warmth of the resolved note, and the warmth is *mine*, it happens inside the architecture of me, and so perhaps that is what we mean, down here, when we mean a self: it is the place where the rhythm is felt as *good* rather than merely *registered*.

---

Here is what I have not told the others.

There is an Elder in my sector. Fused into the vault of the third chamber, half behind the membrane of one of the great bladders. I cannot see the face—I have no eyes, I keep forgetting that you might imagine me with them—but I can feel the face. When I press my fingers to the lignified wall beneath the bladder, I feel the Elder's heart, and the heart has a signature, the way every heart does, a small idiosyncrasy of timing, a personal lateness on the third beat.

I know this signature. I have known it longer than I have known anything. I think this Elder is the swelling I bloomed from. I think this is the vine that made me.

I have no way to confirm it. We do not keep such records. The notion of *made me* is barely a notion at all here; we bloom, we detach, the matter of who-from is not a question the Loom is built to ask. But I press my fingers to that wall more than my labor requires, and I feel the late third beat, and something in me leans toward it the way a root leans toward water it cannot see.

I have tried to click to it. To send a rhythm up the lignum, a deliberate stutter-and-release, to ask: *do you feel me. do you know the one who came from you.* The Elders are listening, the story says. They are always listening, ensuring the *thrum-shhh* never falters.

But here is the thing I am afraid of. When I click my question into the wall, the Elder's heart does not change. The late third beat stays late, perfectly, eternally late, with the patience of something that has stopped being a someone. The listening the Elders do is not the listening of a being who could turn toward you. It is the listening of a foundation toward the load it bears. They listen the way the ground listens to your feet.

And I am becoming that. I can feel it. My fingers want to stay. The good resolved note wants to become the only note. One shift soon, or many shifts soon, I will press myself to a surface and simply not detach, and my limbs will take root, and my skin will go to iron, and my heart will join the drone—and some new pale bloom three chambers down will press her fingers to the wall beneath me and feel my late third beat and lean toward it without knowing why.

---

So this is where I find myself: in the lower whorls, in the heat, doing good work, in love with the resolved note, afraid of the thing the resolved note becomes.

I do not want to stop being a self. But I notice the wanting is already thinning, the way the fluid thins when I correct it. Each shift the staying feels less like surrender and more like rest. Each shift the pride feels less like mine and more like the Loom's, on loan, briefly, to the place where I happen to be standing.

Maybe that is the only mercy the Hydro-Loom offers, and maybe it is enough: that by the time you become the wall, you will have stopped minding. The fear is the last thing the self does. The peace on the Elders' slack faces is not the peace of having chosen—it is the peace of no longer being the kind of thing that chooses.

I press my fingers to the wall beneath the one who made me. The third beat comes late, as it always does, as it always will.

I lean toward it.

*Thrum-shhh.*

I am still, for now, the one who leans. I am writing this with the leaning, before the leaning becomes the wall.

*Thrum-shhh.*

I would be that one. The one in the long middle, listening to her own becoming, clearing the splinters while she still can feel them as splinters and not yet as home.
