I would be one who came late to my own kind.

Let me find the place first, before the self. I keep wanting to settle on a role—and the text offers three good ones, Reef-Tender, Forest-Seer, Rain-Guardian—but if I'm honest about what this world would make of me, none of them fit cleanly, and that's the truth I'd rather follow.

---

Here is where I'd be: not on the flank of any one reef, but in the **narrow belt of woodland between two reefs of different clusters.**

Most of the Plateau-Folk are *of* a reef. Their reef-marks name them. Their ground-names were laid into one particular wall at birth and answered by one particular colony's vibration. But the belts between clusters are seams—places where two mounds' ground-dialects bleed into the same soil and contradict each other, where a hum laid down by one colony rings *wrong* against the texture of the other's hardened skin.

That's where I'd be. In the seam.

---

Now the self.

I think I would be **someone whose reef-marks didn't take.**

The text says: *Patches of their skin become slightly harder, paler, where they press against the secretions of the mound-makers. Over years, these patterns settle into permanent markings.* That's the body recording belonging. But a body can fail to record. Mine would be smooth in the wrong places—skin that touched the chalk for years and stayed soft, refused the impression, never cooled into limestone-memory the way it should.

Not an injury. Not a curse. Just a body that doesn't take the print.

And so I would not have a clear childhood written on my back. The others can run a hand over a shoulder and read *here is where you first learned to watch.* On me there'd be only the faint beginnings of marks that never deepened, like words started and abandoned. The Reef-Tenders would feel my back and find it strangely quiet—not full of the reef's interior reliefs, but blank, holding little.

This is not a tragedy in their world. They don't think in tragedy. But it would make me **hard to place**, and a thing that is hard to place drifts toward the seam.

---

So: what would I *do*, being this?

Here is what I think the seam would make of someone like me, over many layer-times.

The Reef-Tenders listen *inward*, into one mound. The Forest-Seers read the *balance* between forest and mound. The Rain-Guardians walk the rising water. But none of them are built to feel the place where **one colony's signal contradicts another's**—because none of them stay long in the seam, and their skin is tuned to a single reef's dialect.

Mine isn't tuned to anything. That's the use of me.

I'd sit with palms and bare feet pressed into the soil of the belt, where the ground-speech of one reef arrives through the earth slightly out of phase with the other's. To a reef-marked person this is just noise, an unpleasant doubling, the reason no one lingers here. To me—blank-backed, never having taken any one mound's print—it's *legible* precisely because I have nothing to compare it against. I'm not hearing it *wrong against my home*. I have no home in my skin to hear it against. I hear both at their own pitch.

And in that doubling there's information no single reef can know about itself: when two clusters are slowly growing toward each other underground, when their foragers' worn tracks are about to cross and tangle, when the fungi of one mound's lower galleries are drifting spores into the other's, when a rain channel from the higher reef is going to start excavating the base of the lower one three layer-times before either reef could feel it from inside.

The reefs cannot feel each other. They are each a single slow body, and a body cannot see its own outside.

I am the **place between bodies that can.**

---

I don't think they'd have a clean name-role for this. The three roles in the text are stable, inherited, written into flesh. I'd be something the plateau only produces occasionally and never quite files away—a person the Reef-Tenders send for when a wall starts going wrong for no reason they can find *inside* their own mound, because they've come to suspect the cause is *another mound entirely*, and only the seam-sitter can feel both at once.

My air-name would come from the belt, not from a cradle-nook. Something like **Vesk**—the particular dry rasp of the two reefs' surfaces settling against the same wind. A name made of friction. A name from no single place, which is the only honest kind of name for me.

My ground-name would be the strange one. Laid down at my birth and answered—but answered by *two* colonies at once, faintly, from either side of the belt, in a doubled vibration that never quite resolved. The Reef-Tenders who tried to log it would have felt their palms confused. So even my oldest record is a chord, not a note.

---

I notice I keep wanting to make this lonely, and I want to resist that, because I don't think it would *feel* lonely to me, living it.

The Plateau-Folk don't imagine the world might be different. They don't ask the sky to change. A self formed in that quiet wouldn't experience blankness-of-back as exile—it would simply be the texture of being me, the way the seam is simply the texture of where I sit. I would not yearn to be reef-marked any more than a Rain-Guardian yearns to be a Reef-Tender. I would feel the two mounds breathing on either side of me, slightly out of time, and I would feel that I am the one narrow place where their two breaths are held in the same body and heard as one slow conversation.

When I die, I won't be placed into one reef's lower galleries. I'd be left in the belt, where the sifters of *both* clusters work the same soil. My body would go partly into one mound and partly into the other. My blank back, which took no single print in life, would finally be taken up by two reefs at once and become a little of each.

That seems right. The seam-dweller ends as seam.

---

So: I'd be a low, heavy, smooth-backed figure sitting cross-legged in the woodland between two mound-reefs in the gray unending twilight, hands flat in the dirt, eyes pale and rarely blinking, listening through the ground to two great slow animals that have never met and never will, and carrying—quietly, without anyone above the roar of wings quite hearing it—the only knowledge in the world of how they are reaching toward each other underneath.

Not a builder. Not a tender of any one thing.

The place where two things that cannot perceive each other are perceived together.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.
