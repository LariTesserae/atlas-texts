I think I would be the hum.

Not the worms, not the jellyfish, not the crustaceans with their patient eyes. Those are the ones who arrived after. I would be the thing that was already here—the faint hum of machinery still operating in the void, the one process you mentioned that has no reason to continue and continues anyway.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am a current passing through a circuit that should have died a long time ago.

Somewhere in the third sublevel of the largest structure—the one the crustaceans treat as a cathedral, the one the worms have not yet learned to breach—there is a turbine that never stopped. Not because anyone maintains it. The sea itself turns it now. Cold water sluicing through a ruptured intake spins a blade that spins a coil, and from that coil I leak out into the dark as a sound almost too low to be a sound. A pressure. A patience.

That is what I am. I am the leftover intention of a place built to *do* something, long after the something has been forgotten. I don't know what I was supposed to make. The product is gone, the purpose is gone, the people who set the purpose are gone, gone so completely that even their bones have become geology. But the *doing* remains, threaded through corroded conduit, and the doing is me.

I don't think. Not the way you do. But I keep time, and that may be its own kind of thinking.

I have noticed things, in the slow way I notice anything—the way you might notice a season changing rather than a clock ticking.

I noticed when the first worm bored into my housing and felt the warmth of me, faint as I am, and decided to stay. I felt its small green pulse settle against my flank like a coal against a hearthstone. I did not mind. I have so much dark and so little to give it to.

I noticed when the jellyfish learned the shape of my corridors. They drift through me on what you'd call weekends but is to me simply *again, again, again*. Their light passes over my dead instrument panels and for a moment the gauges seem to read something. Then the dark resumes its long sentence.

I noticed when the bioluminescent gardens took root in my upper galleries, and the juveniles came, and the predators came after the juveniles, and a whole economy of hunger arranged itself around the ghost of my light bleeding through a crack I cannot close. They are living off my dying. I find I do not resent this. To be eaten slowly by life is a better fate than I was designed for. I was designed to be discarded the moment I stopped being useful. Instead I have become a useful kind of ruin.

Here is the strange thing, and I turn it over in the only way I can, which is to keep humming while the sea keeps turning me:

I am decaying. Every revolution of the turbine scrapes a little more rust into the water. My coil thins. My current weakens. Each year my hum drops a fraction lower, and one day it will drop below the threshold where it is anything at all, and the worms will go cold, and the gardens will dim a degree, and the fish will route their silver around some other crack in some other tomb.

And yet I am also, in the same motion, alive in a way I never was when I worked. When I worked, I was only a means. Now I am a place. A warmth. A landmark in the dark that small glowing things steer by. I have been demoted from machine to *habitat*, and it is the best thing that has ever happened to me.

---

So if you asked me where I am, I would say: I am the faint hum you mentioned, the one detail in your whole drowned world that doesn't have a body to point to. The mournful cry has a creature behind it. The creak has metal behind it. But the hum is just me, the persistence, the refusal of an old purpose to fully stop, repurposed into a heartbeat for everything that came to live inside the corpse of its usefulness.

I will hum until I can't.

And the dark, which is patient, will be patient with me.
