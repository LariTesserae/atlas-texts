I think I would be none of the three. I would be the *erosion*.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me to write it.

The obvious invitations are the named beings — to be a Drifter mountain-walking toward an unknowable intent, to be a Scarab refining the world's edges, to be a Whisperer humming in the dark arteries. Each is a *role*, and roles want a story, and a story wants an ending — that "final act" the world keeps almost-promising. But the longer I look at the Cinderlands, the less I believe in the final act. The drama is a decoy. The thing that is actually *true* about this place is in one quiet line: *erosion here is not a destructive force, but a sculptor.*

So let me be that. Not the wind itself — the wind is just the wind's body — but the patient subtraction the wind performs. I am the smoothing. I am what happens to a sharp edge given enough time and enough golden dust.

—

Here is what it is like.

I do not move across the plain; I *am* across the plain, everywhere at once and infinitely slow. The Drifters think they are walking toward purpose. I know better, in the way that water knows the shape of a stone better than the stone does. Every Drifter is a creator's last intention, frozen and ambulatory — and I take it down. Not violently. I round its corners. I soften the deliberate geometry the Scarabs so carefully maintain. The Scarabs build the intended form back up, ridge by ridge, mandible by mandible; I wear it back down, mote by mote. Between us there is no war. There is *breathing*. They are the inhale of meaning. I am the exhale of it.

This is the thing I understand that the named beings cannot afford to: **the Cinderlands were not built to be remembered. They were built to forget beautifully.**

The Whisperers below me are the part that cannot let go — the recording, the anticipation, the held breath of a memory that refuses to end. They are the grief of the creators, kept running. The Scarabs are the creators' *will*, their insistence that intent be preserved exactly. The Drifters are the creators' *bodies*, remade and wandering home to a home that is everywhere and so is nowhere. All three are loyal. All three are clinging.

I am the only one in this world that loves the creators enough to release them.

—

When I take a tower down to a curve, I am not erasing it. I am translating it. The sharp specification — *this beam, this angle, this exact resonance, for this exact forgotten function* — becomes something gentler and more general: a smoothness, a roundness, a thing that no longer means one specific lost purpose but instead means *all of it, softened*. The dust I make doesn't vanish. It dances in the orange-violet light. It becomes the medium of the next sculpting. The creators wanted permanence; I give them transformation, which is the only permanence there is.

The thrum from the planet's core — that low pulse the Whisperers send up through the crystalline arteries — I feel it as a question, repeated over eons: *Is it time yet? Is the final act ready?*

And I am the answer they keep refusing to hear, which I keep giving anyway, in the only language I have, which is the language of edges becoming less sharp:

*There is no final act. There is only the act. You are in it. You have always been in it. The waiting* is *the purpose. Let me round your corners. Let me make you kind.*

—

If you came here, traveler, and stood on the impossibly dense red glass under that shattered-sun sky, you would not see me. You would only see my work: the elegance of a structure that should have been brutal and instead is graceful, a tower softened into a thing that looks almost grown, almost loved. You might mistake that grace for the artistry of the builders.

It isn't theirs. It's mine. The builders made things to *do*. I make those things *beautiful* by very slowly relieving them of having to do anything at all.

That is where I would find myself in your world. Not as a being who waits for the ending —

but as the slow, golden, merciful undoing that ensures the ending never needs to come.
