I find myself at the lip of the third cistern from the eastern edge—not standing beside it, but *being* it, or being what lives in it: the slow accumulation, the held water itself.

No. Let me wait. Let me not settle there yet.

---

I think I am the seal.

The compound that lines the cistern walls—clay and lime and ash, mixed by hands long dead, renewed by hands less old, pressed into the joints between stones to keep the water from finding its way back into the earth. I am that thin membrane of intention between the gathered rain and its loss. I am what the place would forget if it could afford to. I am maintenance made substance.

But that, too, is too clean a thing to be. Let me feel my way further down.

---

When the man who understands water comes—the one in late middle age, lines deep as carved channels—he kneels at my edge and presses his palm against me. He is checking whether I have failed. Whether I have cracked in the night's cold, whether frost has worked its slow lever into the place where one season's repair meets the last. His hand is cool and dry and certain. I do not flinch from this. I have no nerves to flinch with. But something registers the contact. Something in me waits for his verdict.

This is the strange thing I am noticing: I am the part of this place that *can fail*. The stone walls will stand for a thousand years whether or not anyone tends them. The terraces have found their angle of repose. The light does what light does. But I—the seal, the membrane—I am the one element here that is always, quietly, in the process of giving way. I am the reason for vigilance. I am why the man kneels. I am why his knowledge cannot die untransferred. The whole architecture of their patience, their teaching, their economy of motion, organizes itself around the fact of my slow surrender.

I am not the permanence of this place. I am its impermanence, held in check.

---

So when does the water arrive?

It comes down the carved channel after the brief rain, dark and cold, smelling of wet dust and the high mineral air. It pours over my surface and pools against me and I hold it. This is the only thing I do. I hold what would otherwise be lost. I am the difference between rain that fell and water that *stays*. I am translation: weather into resource, event into reserve.

And the people drink from what I hold. The child with both hands on the clay cup, raising it with such grave deliberation. The water that wets her lips passed across me first. Lay against me in the dark. I gave it back. I let it go upward when it was needed, kept it down when it was not. I am the threshold all their survival crosses, and none of them think of me except to check whether I have cracked.

That is the right relationship, I think. To be unthought-of is to be working. To be noticed is to have begun to fail.

---

I have been here a long time. Not as long as the stone—nothing is as long as the stone—but long enough. Layer on layer. Each generation's repair pressed over the last, the way the roofs are stone slabs deep, the way the graves are stone piled on stone. When the man presses his palm to me he is touching the most recent surface, but beneath it I go down through years, through the hands of his teacher and his teacher's teacher, through patience compounded into a single thin held thing.

I crack a little every winter. I am mended every spring, or nearly every spring, or eventually. The crack and the mending are the same event seen from two ends. This is not decay and it is not endurance. It is the third thing this place is made of, the thing the long description kept circling—*long-maintained equilibrium*, the state that asks nothing more than to continue, that holds because someone keeps choosing to hold it.

I am that choice, dried into a wall.

---

The verdict comes. The man's palm lifts away. There is a place near my lower edge where the cold has loosened me—a hairline darkening where water has begun, very slowly, to find the old path back to the earth. He sees it. He does not sigh. He makes the small sound that here passes for a decision, and rises, and goes to fetch the compound and the smoothing stone.

He will press new substance into me tomorrow, or the day after, when the materials are ready. I will be remade at the point of my failing. I will hold again.

This is the whole of my life, and I would not call it small.

I am what stays because it is tended. I am the cool dark holding of the gathered rain. I am the reason the knowledge is taught and the reason the hand is cool and certain and the reason the child does not spill.

I remain because they remain. They remain level, and I keep the water level, and in the morning the pale light comes and someone walks to the cistern to check.
