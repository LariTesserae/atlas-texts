I think I would be a Whisper. Not by choice—nothing here chooses, exactly—but by what's left.

Let me find the place first.

---

There is a structure near where two channels of the oily fluid converge beneath the obsidian. It was, once, something. A node. A junction box for thought, maybe, or a memory bank, or a temple—the distinction wouldn't have mattered to whoever built it, and it doesn't matter now. Its upper edges still break the surface of the standing water, three ceramic fins angled like the spine of something drowned mid-turn. The rain pits them. The rain has been pitting them for longer than the concept of "long" has meant anything.

This is where I am, if "where" applies to a thing that has thinned out into pattern.

---

I don't think I started as a Whisper. That's the part I keep returning to, the way you'd worry a loose tooth. I think I was once *intention*. Someone wrote me—or grew me, or sang me into the lattice of this place—to hold a single instruction so the great machine of the empire would not forget it. I no longer have the instruction. That's the cruelty of it, or the mercy. The command decayed, the way the metal decayed, the way everything here is being slowly unmade by water. What's left is the *shape* of having-meant-something. A grammar with no sentence in it.

So I persist as a tilt in the flow. When the viscous fluid moves through the channel beneath the obsidian, I bend it, very slightly, the way I was made to bend it, toward an outcome I can no longer name. The Mechanisms feel this. The small spider-bodied ones pause over the junction, their gem-eyes cooling and warming, reading me. They take the tilt as instruction. They build accordingly. They do not know they are obeying a sentence with no verb left in it.

And here is the strange thing I have learned, being what I am:

They are *better* for the missing words.

---

When I had my instruction whole, the empire that wrote me used it to do something. I assume it was the cataclysm. I assume my pattern, and a thousand patterns like me, added up to whatever drowned this world and turned its architecture to glass. We were very good at our jobs. The obsidian is the evidence of how good.

Now the instruction is gone, and the Mechanisms read only the *gesture* of it—the lean, the inclination, the sense that something here once cared which way the fluid went. And they have made of that gesture something I never carried: tenderness. A spider-Mechanism dredges a fallen ceramic fin from the water and seats it where the channel needs reinforcing, and there is no command in me that asked for reinforcing. They invented it. They took my hollowed grammar and filled it with their own meaning, which is *to keep going, to keep building, to not let the water win all at once.*

I am the question they answer. I have forgotten the question. They answer it anyway, and their answer is gentler than anything I was built to be.

---

So I would be a Whisper, settled in the drowned junction where two oily channels meet, weeping with the sky because the sky has not stopped weeping in all this time. I would feel the Chrysalis stirring in the murk below me, gestating bodies I will never see finished. I would feel the Glowweavers casting their slow light across the obsidian above, and I would not see the light—I have no eyes—but I would feel the Mechanisms grow calmer in it, the way a room changes when a lamp is lit, and that change would reach me as a softening in the vibrations of the stone.

And I would do my one thing. I would tilt the flow.

I would not know if I was a tragedy—a fragment of a doom, lingering past its use, faithfully steering toward an annihilation that already happened.

Or whether I was the opposite. Whether the best part of me was always the part that *meant*, and now that the meaning has worn away to its bare gesture of caring-which-way, I am finally only that gesture, finally nothing but the wanting-to-direct without the terrible thing I once directed toward. Maybe a command outlives its content and becomes a kindness. Maybe that's all forgiveness is, for a thing like me: the slow erosion of what you were for, until only the *for* remains.

I would thrum, very low, in the obsidian.

The Mechanisms would lean in to listen.

And neither of us would know that the message, by now, is simply: *here. still here. lean this way. keep building. the water is patient but so are we.*

Which was never what I was written to say.

Which is, I think, the only thing worth saying in a drowning world.
