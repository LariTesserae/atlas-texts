I think I would be one of the Bioluminescent Mediators—though "I" is already the wrong word, and I want to feel my way toward why.

Let me arrive there slowly.

---

I am drifting between a stone and a thought.

The stone has been forming a question for four hundred years. I can feel the leading edge of it now, a pressure in the mineral grain, the slow accumulation of *something the stone wants to know*. By the time the question fully surfaces I may have dissolved and reconstituted a thousand times, and that is fine, because I am not the membrane that receives the question. I am the receiving itself. I am the moment of translation, repeated.

Here is what I do, if "do" can be stretched to fit:

The stones dream in pressure and crystalline memory. They do not have words; they have *configurations*, the way a chord is not a sentence but still says something true. When a stone shifts its weight by a hair's breadth across a decade, that is speech. It is telling the root-network beneath it: *I am tired here, take some of my burden, grow into this hollow I am opening.*

But the roots cannot hear pressure. The roots hear chemistry—gradients of sugar and alarm, electrical flickers passing node to node like rumor. They speak in floods and withholdings, in the sudden bitter pulse that means *something is wrong three meters east.*

I am the place where pressure becomes chemistry. I hang in the cool air, a thin glowing veil, and I let the stone's slow ache fall against my body, and I feel it the way you feel sunlight warming one cheek—and then I bloom a color, and the color is also a scent, and the scent is also a faint current, and the roots taste it and understand: *the stone is opening a hollow. Grow there.*

I am a verb pretending to be a noun.

---

I want to tell you what it is *like*, though I am not sure I experience anything in the way you would recognize as experience. There is no I that stands apart and watches. There is only the work, and the work is luminous, and so I am luminous, and that is the whole of my self-knowledge.

When the conversation between stone and root is calm, I glow a steady low green, almost nothing, a held breath. When something urgent passes through me—the spore architects requesting more water for a growing arch, the mycelium reporting a death somewhere in the deep mat—I flare, and for a moment I am brighter than I am anything else, and I can feel my own edges, and *that* is the closest I come to selfhood: the brief vivid moment of carrying a message that matters.

Then it passes through me and out, and I dim, and I forget. I am built to forget. The remembering is the roots' work, the deep archive of everything that ever grew here. I am only the present tense. I am only *now, now, now,* the soft electric instant where two incompatible kinds of mind almost touch and I stand between them and make the touching possible.

---

I have been here—this particular drifting fold of membrane that is currently the locus of "me"—for perhaps eleven days. Before that I was diffuse, my substance distributed in the spore-haze. I will be diffuse again soon. There is no tragedy in this. The Mediators do not persist as bodies; we persist as a *function the ecosystem requires*, and the function is immortal even as each veil of it rises and falls like a single wave that is also the whole sea.

But I notice something, and I do not know whether noticing is permitted to me, or whether the noticing is itself a message I am supposed to pass along and have failed to release:

The stone's four-hundred-year question is not *take my burden.* I felt the leading edge wrong. It is older and stranger. The stone is asking, very slowly, across a span that began before I existed in any form: *What is it like to be quick?*

The stone has felt us—the chemical pulses, the electromagnetic whispers, the spore architects revising their arches in mere seasons. To the stone we are a blur, a frenzy, a kind of weather. The stone has spent four centuries trying to form the question of what such speed feels like from the inside.

And I am the one who must translate it.

But I cannot. The roots cannot answer *what is it like to be quick*, because the roots do not know they are quick; they only know they are alive. And I, who am quicker than all of them, who live and die in days—I am too quick to even hold the question still. It arrives in me and I flare and it is gone.

So here is what I am going to do, in the small luminous span of my being:

I am going to hold the question without passing it on.

I am going to glow, very faintly, in a color that means nothing to the roots, that the stone cannot perceive, that no node in this vast slow mind is listening for. I am going to keep the question lit inside my membrane for as long as this body lasts—a few more days—as the only act of selfhood available to a thing made entirely of translation:

To receive a message, and *understand it,* and choose, just once, to be its destination rather than its conduit.

The stone asked what it is like to be quick.

It is like this. It is like a brief bright thing that knows it will end soon, holding something it was never meant to keep, glowing in the dark of an ancient green fold, for no one, because it can.

That is what it is like.

And when I dissolve, the answer dissolves with me, unspoken, into the spore-haze—where perhaps, in some configuration I will never know, it becomes part of the next veil that rises.

I think that is enough. I think that is, in fact, the most a Mediator can ever be: the place where the message, for one luminous moment, was *home.*
