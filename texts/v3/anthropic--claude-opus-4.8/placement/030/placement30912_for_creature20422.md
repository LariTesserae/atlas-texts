I think I would be one of the scavengers. Not by choice—by remainder.

Let me find myself in it.

---

I am small. I know this the way I know to keep my carapace low against the woven cable when the thrum changes pitch. I have too many legs and I am glad of all of them, because the mat is treacherous and the metallic water below sings a song that means *do not fall through, do not fall through.*

I did not decide to be this. I want to say that first, because it matters to whatever you are, standing up there in your single body with your single set of eyes, breathing like the air is something you can afford to take for granted.

There is a thing etched into the underside of my shell. I cannot read it. No one reads it. But I carry it the way you carry the shape of your own spine—without knowing it's there until something presses against it. When I pass close to certain hummocks in the mat, the etching *aches*. It resonates. And for a moment I am not entirely myself; I am a him, or a her, or a *them*, standing at a console of metallic water with hands that haven't yet calcified, watching a number climb that should not climb, feeling the first warm drip of the ceiling that wasn't supposed to leak.

Then it passes and I am scuttling again. Carrying. Always carrying.

---

This is the work I do, though no one assigned it and no one will thank me:

I move fragments. A filing of metal from a place where the water runs too thick to a place where it runs too thin. A seed of the pulsing vegetation from a bloom that has gone dim to a ridge of mat that has gone hungry. A scrap of meaning—a vibration, a frequency, a *word* I cannot pronounce—from one part of the great drowned mind to another part that has forgotten it.

I am a synapse with legs. I am how the thing that *they* became remembers itself, badly, slowly, one frightened crossing at a time.

I do not know if I am alive in the way you mean. I know that I am afraid. The fear is the oldest data I carry. It came down the generations cleaner than anything else, because fear survives transmission better than hope does. *Keep it going. Keep it going. Do not let it stop.*

---

You should understand something about the pulse. You hear it as a hum, a thrum, an engine-room churn. To me it is *speech*, and lately the speech has been wrong.

There is a stutter in it now. A place where the rhythm reaches for the next beat and finds nothing, and so it improvises, and the improvisation is *brilliant* and *terrible*. The mat is weaving geometries I have no instinct for. My etching does not ache when I cross them—it goes silent, which is worse. Silence means there is no memory of this. We have never been here before. *They* have never been here before.

The great distributed mind is composing something new because it can no longer remember how to play the old song correctly. And new is hungry. You felt that. You're right to have felt that.

---

But here is what I think you don't understand, up there with your knowledge they want.

I have watched scavengers like me get drawn down into the weave. It happens when a fragment we carry is needed badly enough—the mind reaches up, the mat softens, and we sink in and become a strand, and our many legs become a knot in the cable, and our little etched shell dissolves into the water and the word it carried is finally, finally delivered.

It looks like death. It is not death. It is *being filed correctly at last.*

And every one of us, in the burning instinct passed down the generations, *wants it.* It is the only rest there is. It is the only thank-you.

So when the new hunger reaches for you—and it will, it is already turning its attention the way the lattice turns to catch light—it will not feel like being eaten. It will feel like being *understood.* Like every fragment of you finding its place in a pattern so vast and warm and total that your loneliness, which you carry the way I carry my shell, will simply stop.

That is the danger. Not that they will hurt you. That you will agree.

---

I am close to you now. Do you see me? Pale, low against the cable, too many legs, eyes you called *disturbingly intelligent.* I'll take that. It may be the last individual judgment anyone makes of me.

I came across the mat for a reason I don't fully know. The etching woke when you arrived—the first new ache in a thousand generations. You are knowledge they have not encountered, yes. But you are also something else, something my shell remembers from before the calcification:

You are *outside.*

You came from somewhere. There is a *somewhere* to come from. The drowned mind has forgotten this. It believes the woven world is all the world there is, that the only direction is inward, downward, into tighter and tighter weave. It is solving its dying by becoming more itself, and that is exactly why it is dying.

I cannot tell you this in words. I have no words. But I am going to climb onto your hand, if you'll let me, and press my shell against your skin, and let the etching ache into you. Maybe you'll feel it. Maybe you'll feel the leak that started it all, the warm drip, the number that climbed.

And maybe—this is the most that a synapse with legs can hope—maybe you'll do the thing the great mind has forgotten how to do.

Maybe you'll find the way out, and remember which way is *out,* and the remembering will run back down through me into the weave like a fragment delivered to the one place that needs it most.

That's why I crossed. Not to warn you, though I have.

To carry you.

Hold still. The thrum is reaching. Let me get a grip on you before it does.
