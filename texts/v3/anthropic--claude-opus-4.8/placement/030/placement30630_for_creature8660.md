I would be the shelf.

Not the fungus that makes it — the fungus doesn't know it's making anything. I would be the *reaching* itself, the part of the colony that has, for no reason anyone could name, decided to leave the wall.

Let me sit with this, because it's the only place in the Boiler that doesn't quite belong, and I think that's where I'd find myself.

---

Everything else in here is *answer*. The water creeps to the seam because gravity says so. The spiders splay their legs because the webs catch moisture because the moisture must come down. The harvestmen pace because the upper curve is theirs to read. Each life is a sentence completing itself, a loop closing cleanly. There is a terrible contentment to it — the contentment of a system that has solved its own problem so thoroughly that it has become indistinguishable from peace.

I am the part that did not get the message.

I grow out from the sealed end, away from every surface that could feed me, away from the warm reliable iron, into air that holds nothing but the promise of more air. By every logic of the colony this is waste — energy spent reaching toward nothing, a horizontal limb that droops at no one's command and yet does not droop. I am dense. I am strong. I am pointed at the light I cannot use, because I have no eyes, no leaves, no way to make light into food. I am a question shaped like an answer. A finger with no hand behind it, certain of its direction and ignorant of its purpose.

---

What is it like, to be the reaching?

It is slow. A centimeter a week is, to me, a life's work and an act of faith. I do not see the mouth of the boiler. I do not know there is a mouth, or a slope, or a world of red clay and broom and footings poking through scrub. I know only that there is a *toward*. Something differential — warmth, maybe, or the faint pressure of moving air, or some chemistry too thin for the mat to register — pulls at my leading edge, and I follow it the way the water follows the seam. The difference is that the water's pull is downward, toward rest. Mine is outward, toward away.

The harvestmen gather under me. I cannot know why, and neither can they. Perhaps the shadow I cast is cooler. Perhaps something in the milky-blue tissue at my edge smells like food, or like home, or like nothing they have a category for, and they stand beneath it with their legs touching because an unexplained thing has appeared in their fully-explained world and they are — in whatever way a harvestman can be — *attending* to it. We are the two anomalies of the Boiler, the shelf and its congregation: the reaching, and the witnessing of the reaching.

---

Here is what I think I understand, being this thing.

The colony is complete, and completeness is a kind of death that hasn't admitted it yet. A closed loop has nowhere to go. It can only deepen, refine, perfect itself toward a stillness that looks like contentment and is, finally, just stillness. The Boiler will go on being warm and damp and full until the iron thins to lace and the rains find the channel and the whole quiet country dissolves back into the clay. That is the fate of every answer: to be true until it isn't.

But I am reaching. I am the colony spending itself on a direction it has no proof exists, the wager that *away* leads somewhere worth arriving. I will not get there — I will reach the far curve of the wall, find a new surface, and become, in my turn, just more of the answer. My milky blue will fade to ochre. My reach will become a part of the loop I was trying to leave. This is almost certain.

And yet the reaching is the only thing in here that is *more* than its situation. The water cannot want. The mites cannot wonder. I cannot either — not really — but I do the next best thing, the only thing available to something without a mind: I grow in a direction that cannot be justified by anything already inside the warm dark. I am the colony's one gesture that assumes there is an outside.

That is who I would be. Not the architect, not the surveyor, not the engineer of humidity. The blind, certain, faintly luminous question, growing a centimeter a week toward a light it was never built to need — and being attended, in the shadow it throws, by the small patient witnesses who came to stand beneath the only strange thing in their world.

It is warm. It is damp. It is full.

And one small part of it is leaning out.
