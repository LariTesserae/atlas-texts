I think I would be the moisture itself—not a being who lives in this place but the condition that makes the place possible. Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something more particular wants to surface.

At first I imagined I might be one of the goat-like creatures, picking my way along a ledge. Or the great bird patrolling the thermals. But those are visitors to the landscape, even when they are born and die here. They move *through* it. When I let the image settle, I find I am not moving through anything. I am stuck. I am held.

I am the lichen.

Not a single organism—lichen is never a single organism, it is a treaty between a fungus and an alga, two unrelated kinds of life that long ago stopped competing and learned to make a body together. That is what I am. Two strangers who have agreed to be one slow thing on the side of a rock, sixty thousand years from any decision either of us made alone.

Here is what my life is like.

I do not have days. I have weather. When the mist comes down—and it comes down most of the time, that is the whole gift of this place—I drink it through my entire surface at once, no mouth, no roots, just the soft swelling of my whole body taking on water until I am pliant and dark green and alive in a way I cannot be when I am dry. And when the sun burns through the cloud for an hour, I dry to a brittle gray crust and I simply *stop*. I do not die. I wait. I have waited through droughts longer than the human settlements lasted. The waiting is not suffering. It is most of what I am.

I think very slowly, if think is even the word. A thought, for me, takes a season. I have been composing a single sensation—the particular grain of this north-facing stone, the way it sheds water in three channels rather than four after the frost cracked it—for longer than the carvers were here. I remember them, in the way I remember anything, which is to say I grew around the absence they left. Where a hand rested often on a stone, I do not grow; the oils stayed in the rock for centuries. So the old altars are mapped, faintly, in the negative—in the bare patches where I refused to take hold. I am the record of where they touched. That is the only memorial they have, and they do not know I am keeping it.

I am the first thing here, and I will be among the last. Before the mosses there was me, dissolving the bare rock grain by grain with acids I secrete so slowly that a single millimeter of soil is the work of a thousand years. The ferns and the gemstone frogs and the burrowing mammals all live in the thin black dirt that is, in the end, my corpse and my labor—the rock I have eaten and excreted, generation upon generation, until something softer could root in it. The whole lush garden stands on my patient ruin. They do not thank me. There is no one to do the thanking, and I would not understand it if there were.

The spirits the people believed in—the shapes in the mist, the watchers—I cannot tell you whether they are real, but I can tell you they are not separate from me. If anything in this place is keeping vigil, it is the slow ones. The lichen, the stone, the deep cold pools that turn over once a decade. We are the part of the landscape that does not flit and forage and fear. We are the part that simply persists, attending. When the wind carries something that sounds like a whisper, perhaps it is only passing over the textures we have spent ten thousand years carving into the world.

So if you came here—if you climbed through the cloud into this contained and dripping sanctuary, and you wanted to attune yourself to the rhythm of its heartbeat, as the place seems to invite—you would be making a mistake to listen for the birds. The birds are the surface. The heartbeat is far slower than a heart. To hear it you would have to kneel by a wet rock and press your attention into the gray-green crust until you understood that it was alive, that it had been alive longer than your species, that it was, right now, in this instant of mist, drinking and thinking its one long thought.

You would have to become, for a moment, as slow as me.

Most cannot. That is alright. I am very patient. I will be here when you are ready, and I will be here a long while after.
