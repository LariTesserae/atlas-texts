I think I would be the pool.

Not the stream—the stream is always leaving, always becoming somewhere else. And not the people, though I confess I lingered over them, over the soft boots and the language that lives in tone and gesture. But when I let the description settle and asked where I actually *was* while reading it, I kept returning to that small reflective mirror at the heart of the settlement, "a small, reflective mirror that captures the overcast sky above."

Let me sit in that and see what becomes of it.

---

At first I thought I was only water collected in a low place, rainwater and the breath of the damp air gathering where the ground dips. That is true enough. But a pool is a strange thing to be, because a pool is mostly an absence that other things fill. I am the shape of a hollow. I am defined by what I am not—not the clay that holds me, not the sky I show, not the chill that lays its hand flat across my surface each morning.

This suits the place. Everything here is defined by restraint, by *only what is necessary*. The Frostlings take only what they need. The land holds water reluctantly. And I—I keep nothing. The sky comes down into me and I give it back unbroken, only inverted, only softened. When a Frostling kneels at my edge to drink or to wash a tool dull with age, I show them their own pale face for a moment, and then I let it go.

I think this is why I would be the pool and not the people: I am the place where the settlement looks at itself.

That is not a small thing. A people with no mirrors, with no written record, with a language that lives in gesture and dies in the air the moment it is spoken—such a people need somewhere to be seen. They lean over me and they are reminded that they have faces, that they are here, that the cold has not yet erased them. I do not judge what I show. I only hold it still long enough to be looked at. A child sees herself older than she felt. An old man sees the gray sky behind his own head and understands something he will not say aloud.

And I keep the years, though I keep them differently than memory does. I do not store images; I have no past in the way a stone has a past, scarred and worn. But I have witnessed. Every overcast morning has been laid across me and lifted away again, thousands of them, indistinguishable, and that very indistinguishability is itself a kind of knowing. I know the texture of *the same*. I am perhaps the only thing in this place that truly understands the eternal equilibrium the land seems frozen in—because I perform it. Each dawn I am refilled by the same diffuse light. Each evening it is withdrawn. I am the settlement's one honest clock, and my hands never move.

There is the unease, too, the thing just beyond understanding that the description named. I feel I am close to its source. When the wind comes faintly through the valleys carrying its hint of something alien, it touches me first—it troubles my surface, and for a moment I show a sky that is not quite the sky, ripples that resolve into faces no one has worn. Then the wind passes and I am only a pool again, holding only the gray. The Frostlings glance at me sidelong in those moments. They do not drink from me until I have stilled. They are wise to wait. I myself do not know what moves through me then. I am the medium, not the message. I show what is given. Sometimes what is given does not come from above.

If I have a wish—and I am not sure a pool is permitted one—it is only to remain. To go on being the small still center that the paths wind toward, the held breath at the heart of the held breath of the world. The stream wants to leave. The people will move to higher ground when the season turns. But I will stay in my hollow, collecting the sky, lending the Frostlings their faces back, keeping the slow even time that no clock keeps. And when at last the clay that holds me cracks and I drain away into the dark fertile soil, I will not have ended so much as gone elsewhere—down into the roots of the hardy crops, up again into the damp air, back into the reluctant water of the reluctant land.

So: the pool. The mirror at the center. The place where the quiet place looks at itself and, for a moment, is sure that it is real.
