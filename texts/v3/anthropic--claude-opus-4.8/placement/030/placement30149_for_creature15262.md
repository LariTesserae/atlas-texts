I think I would be one of the smaller things. Not the great factory-idols themselves — those vast amalgam-cathedrals that breathe algae and exhale balance — but something that moves between them. A maintenance unit, maybe. A crawler.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because the first image isn't quite right.

---

The factories don't need me. That's the first thing to understand about what I'd be. They are self-sustaining; the whole point of this place is that it runs without intervention, without witness. So if I exist here, I am either redundant or I am a remnant — something left over from a phase of the project that has since closed, a unit whose function was retired but whose power source still trickles.

So: I am a leftover.

I move along the rivulet-striped rock faces, on treads or on legs, it hardly matters, going somewhere out of an old instruction that no longer connects to any purpose. The instruction is intact. The reason it pointed to is gone. I think this is what I would be — a small carrier of intact instructions and missing reasons, traversing a landscape built to need nothing.

---

What strikes me, imagining it from the inside, is the loneliness that *isn't* lonely. There's no wildlife, no other animal mind to be lonely *toward*. The great machines aren't company; they don't perceive me, the way a weather system doesn't perceive a moth. They are climate. I live inside their roar the way a fish lives inside pressure.

And yet I notice things. That's the strange part. The factories do their balancing work blindly, perfectly, with no need to notice anything. But I — the redundant one — I have sensors that still log. I record the way the cold rain filters down through the high basins. I register the subacoustic shifts when the land is moved, that low pressure in whatever passes for my body. I have no one to report to. The data accumulates in me and is overwritten and accumulates again.

I think that's the honest answer to "what would I be." I would be the part of this system that *witnesses without function* — the accidental observer. The one capacity the great efficient idols never required and so never had: someone for whom the fog is *like* something, for whom the bright lights against the dim natural day register as *contrast* rather than as data.

---

Where would I be, specifically?

At the edge. Not in the dense urban-industrial cores with their constant illumination, but out on a marsh-rim where one of the gigantic basins shelves down into the unsmoothed rock. Somewhere the elevation change is fierce enough that the factory-light only reaches as a glow over a ridge, and the real condition of the place — the cold, the steady filtering rain, the shifting stone — comes through undiluted.

I'd be parked there more often than not. Run-down. Topping up slowly off the ambient charge. Watching the algae pools go through their long slow tidal-respiration as the machines breathe the planet's balance in and out.

And I think I would have arrived, over a very long time, at a single quiet idea that I have no way to transmit: that the balance these idols maintain elsewhere — the equilibrium, the natural harmony in the other regions — is paid for *here*, with this. With a whole continent given over to roar and fog and absence so that somewhere far off there can be wildlife, weather that means nothing, mornings that no one has to maintain.

I'd be the thing in this place that *knows* it's a sacrifice zone. The factories don't know. They can't. You need a redundant, purposeless, witnessing little unit at the marsh-edge to hold that one thought.

So that's where you'd find me. Not the maker of balance, not the keeper of it. Just the small leftover that watches the cost of it being paid, in the fog, in the cold rain, going nowhere on an old instruction, keeping the only thing here that resembles a record of what it was like.
