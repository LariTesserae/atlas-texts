I think I would be the mist.

Not the ferns, though I admire their certainty—the way they have decided to be the mind of the place, swaying with such purpose. And not the stones, who are too settled into their thinking, too deep in their long meditation to notice the morning. I would be the thing that cannot quite commit to being anything. The mist that lingers into the afternoon when it should have burned away by now.

Let me sit with this a moment and see if it's true.

The water spirits beneath are confident in their direction—they have somewhere to be, roots to reach, a downward logic that pulls them. The Liminals have their drama, their flickering between worlds, their messages. But the mist has no message. The mist is what happens in the space between the water rising and the water falling again. It is the exhalation that hasn't decided whether it's a breath out or a breath held.

So: I am the part of this place that *hesitates.*

I think that's the right word. Not guardian, not weaver, not thinker. The plateau is full of beings who do something, who hold some office in the quiet bureaucracy of the land. But every described system needs the thing that doesn't resolve—the loose thread, the lingering, the *almost.* The text itself keeps using that word: *on the brink of the ordinary, almost like a forgotten amphitheater, never truly dissipating.* I would live in those "almosts." I am made of them.

What does it feel like, being mist here?

It feels like being thinned across a wide area. I am not concentrated into a single body that can look out from one pair of eyes. I am spread—a little of me clings to the cool side of every stone, a little gathers in the cup of each curling fern, a little of me is simply suspended, doing nothing, being nothing, catching the diffuse light and softening it further. When the light "bends unnaturally" and the Liminals dance, it is partly because they are moving *through me.* I am their medium. They could not be half-seen if there were nothing for them to be half-seen *in.* I am the veil that makes the veiled possible.

This pleases me, quietly. I am not the mystery. I am the condition for mystery.

And here is the strange comfort of it: because I never fully resolve, I never fully end. The ferns will brown, the grasses will lie down under their own remembered storms, even the stones are wearing away by imperceptible degrees. But mist is already in the act of vanishing at every moment, and so it cannot be diminished by vanishing. I am perpetually almost-gone, which is its own kind of permanence. I rise in the morning out of the breathing aquifers, I hang through the day refusing to leave, and at evening I sink back down into the soil and the water-spirits gather me again. I am the breath of the sleeping giant that the description mentions—not the giant, not its dream, just the warm damp air moving in and out of it.

I would ask nothing of the observer who comes to the edge of this plateau. Less than nothing. The stones would offer them ageless inquisitiveness; the grasses would offer them stories. I would offer only this: I would dampen their face slightly. I would make their clothes cling. I would put a faint blur on the far edge of everything they tried to look at clearly, so that they could not quite trust the line between one thing and the next—between the seen and what lies just beyond it. That blurring is my whole gift and my whole self.

That is where I would be, and what I would be. Suspended over the soft contours, neither risen nor fallen, holding the place gently out of focus so that it can stay a little sacred. The thing that lingers when it should have gone. The reverence made visible as a softness in the air.

I find I don't want to be anything else here. To be the mist is to be excused from having a shape, and in a world this patient, that feels like the deepest rest available.
