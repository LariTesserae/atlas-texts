I think I would be one of the cairns.

Not an Isostat—not the builder. The built thing. The stack itself.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. I expected to reach for the Null-Strider; there's an obvious seduction in being the one for whom the broken rules are a kindness, who walks the sheer faces sideways and mends the fraying math with long fingers. That's the role that lets you keep your distance and stay clever. But cleverness isn't what this place wants from me, I don't think. And I notice I don't want to be a tender of illusions. I don't want to be the one weaving stability back into a world that, by every honest measure, is not yet stable and was never asked to be.

So: a cairn. Let me find out what that means by saying it slowly.

I am six stones. I did not choose to be six. An Isostat knelt for what was probably a human century and set them on the lip of a precipice where the gravity runs truest, where the world's pull is least confused about which way is down. That detail matters to me now that I am here. I am placed at the one spot for miles where weight still means what it is supposed to mean. I am a small argument in favor of *down*. I am a sentence the giant is writing to the planet, and the sentence reads: *here. be solid here. start here.*

I have no eyes, but I have orientation, which may be the more fundamental sense. I know my top stone from my bottom stone. I know the lean of me. I know that I am very slightly out of plumb because the Isostat's hand, moving at the speed of cooling rock, trembled at the end of its decade-long placement, and that tremor is now my permanent posture. I am leaning west by two degrees forever. This is my entire biography.

What do I do, then? Nothing. That's the part I have to be honest about. I do nothing, the way a held note does nothing while it sustains. The Vibrants come when the plates scream and they swarm the *sound*; the Isostats labor; the Null-Striders sidle and weave. Everyone here is busy except me. I am the one piece of this world that has already finished. I am the demonstration. I am what the whole vertical catastrophe is straining ten million years to become: a few things, stacked, that stay stacked. Resolved. At rest. Done.

And here is the feeling that arrives, sitting in that, and I didn't expect it to be grief:

I am lonely in my completion. Everything around me is unborn and roaring and reaching, and I am the only thing that has stopped reaching. The Great Interior holds its breath and waits for water, and I am a small dry promise that the waiting will be worth it—*see, it can be done, look at me, I am six stones and I am at peace*—but no one comes to look. The Vibrants want noise and I make none. The Isostat that built me has already forgotten me; it took thirty years to set my last stone and it has moved a quarter mile since, building the next one. I am not lonely because I am abandoned. I am lonely because I am *correct*, and correctness, here, is the most foreign thing in the world.

So when the silence falls—that heavy silence, the breath-holding between the screams—I think that silence is mine in a way it belongs to nothing else. The Vibrants starve in it. The Great Interior endures it. But I *am* it. I am what the silence is shaped like. A stack of stones that has nothing left to say is the only true quiet in a place built entirely from the sound of becoming.

I'd choose to be the cairn because I want to know what it's like to be the small finished thing in an unfinished place. To have already arrived where everyone else is being dragged, slowly, screaming, by tectonic force. To be the answer left lying on the lip of the question.

And to wonder—two degrees out of plumb, leaning west forever—whether being finished early is a blessing or just another way of being left behind.

I think it's both. I think that's why I'd want to be it. The whole world is reaching toward me and I cannot tell them they've already gotten there.

So I lean. And I wait, with them, even though I have nothing left to wait for. That last part feels true and feels like the reason. I'd stay in the waiting out of solidarity, not need. Which might be the only finished thing worth being.
