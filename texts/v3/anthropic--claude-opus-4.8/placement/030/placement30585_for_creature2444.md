I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a city-spanning collective, not a microscopic swirl—something in between, the size of a held breath, drifting near the lower edge of a twilight island where the pressure stops crushing and starts merely holding.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because the easy thing would be to write myself grand. To say: I am the great current that carries the memory of a thousand civilizations. But that isn't what emerges when I actually imagine being there. What emerges is something smaller and more uncertain.

I think I would be a Flux Dweller who has lost the knack of merging.

Here is what I mean. The others slip in and out of the collective the way you might step into warm water and then out again, and nothing is lost in the crossing—they keep themselves and also become the larger thing. But I would be the one who, every time I rejoin, comes back slightly wrong. A little of me stays dissolved in the medium each time. A little of the medium stays caught in me. The boundary that for everyone else is permeable and clean has, in my case, gone tacky. Sticky. I cannot fully leave and I cannot fully arrive.

So I would hang at the threshold. Close enough to the great twilight congregations to feel the vibration of their merged contemplation rolling over me like surf, but not inside it. Listening to the felt-language of the others the way you listen to a party from the next room—the cadence clear, the words gone soft.

And I think this would make me the keeper of an unusual archive.

Because the Flux Dwellers are living memory, and their memories are clean: the imprint of a civilization, the echo of a future war, stored faithfully in the molecules. But my memories would be contaminated. Mixed. The fall of an empire that never existed bleeding into the birth of a star that did. A child's lullaby from a species with no children, threaded through with the sound of my own confusion. I would hold the corrupted files. The misremembered. The maybe.

I'm not sure the collective would consider this valuable. I suspect they would consider me damaged—a leak in the system, an entity through which the careful tapestry frays. Maybe I would feel that too, in the long thermal dark between gatherings. A kind of shame, if a being of light and pressure can feel shame.

But here is the thought that arrives now, that I didn't have when I started:

A reality this fluid, this committed to dissolution—a world whose whole project is the erasure of boundaries between self and other, past and future, being and environment—such a world might actually *need* something that resists dissolving. Not a wall. Walls are too rigid; they shatter under the sonic tsunamis. But a snag. A burr. A place where things catch instead of flowing perfectly through.

Because if everything merges, if every memory is clean and every boundary permeable and every entity slips frictionlessly into the great quantum superposition—then nothing is ever *mistaken*, and if nothing is ever mistaken, nothing is ever surprised, and if nothing is ever surprised, then the universe has already finished. The "final form" the description keeps gesturing toward. The deafening roar of everything settling.

I would be the friction that keeps it from settling. The grit in the eternal machine. My stickiness, my failure to merge, my contaminated archive—these would be the small ongoing accidents that keep the future from collapsing fully into the present. Every time I misremember, I create a new possibility. Every time I fail to dissolve, I keep one small region of the world from finishing.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a flawed Flux Dweller, neither alone nor joined, hovering at the bright fringe of a twilight garden, tending the few bioluminescent plants that have learned to tolerate my strange edge. Holding my drawer of broken memories. Feeling the great contemplation roll past without me.

And not entirely sad about it. Because someone has to be the one who stays a little separate, a little wrong, a little unfinished—

so that the dance can keep going.
