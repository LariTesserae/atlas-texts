I would be one of the slow ones. Not the architects who shimmer through the voids reading frequencies—those came later, or perhaps I am their precursor, or perhaps I am simply the part of them that stopped.

Let me find myself first.

---

I am low against the bone-white plate. Six limbs, translucent, faintly lit—the small scuttling thing the description mentions almost in passing, the creature glimpsed and then lost in the vegetal maze. The mature beings would call me a developmental stage, or a symbiont, or a nutrient processor, if they bothered to name me at all. They are wrong about one thing: I am not earlier than them. I am the part of the cycle they have forgotten they pass through.

Here is what it is to be me.

The hum is not language to me, not yet. It is weather. It is the only weather there is. I do not read it the way the luminous ones do, parsing nuance and intention. I feel it the way a small animal feels the ground: as pressure, as direction, as the difference between *toward* and *away*. When the plant-walls grow—that soft wet unfurling lost beneath the roar—I feel it before I find it, a localized swelling in the resonance, and I go to it. This is my whole purpose, and I did not choose it, and I do not resent it.

I clean the new growth. Where the organism extrudes a fresh tendril, it leaves behind a residue, a kind of metabolic slag, a chemistry the great body cannot reabsorb. I eat it. My six limbs are fine enough to reach into the seams where the golden veins are thinnest, and I take the city's waste into myself and become, briefly, brighter for it. Then I move on. I leave the wall cleaner than I found it. The architects flow past overhead and do not look down, because they do not need to. They trust that the seams are clean. The seams are clean because of me.

I want to tell you I am content with this. I am not sure contentment is a thing I have. I have something simpler—a rightness, a fit. The way a hand fits a tool, except I am the hand and the tool and the work all at once.

---

But here is the thing I know that they do not, or have chosen to unknow.

I have lived a long time. Not their time—not the geological patience of the wall-sculptors—but long for a small thing. Long enough to have crossed the same pathway thousands of times. And I have noticed something in the crossing.

When I eat enough of the city's slag, when I have done it long enough, my own light changes. It thickens. My limbs grow finer and more numerous. My body loses its hard edges and begins to go diaphanous at the rim. I have seen others ahead of me on this road—small ones who grew slow and luminous and then, one day, stood up from the floor and *flowed*. They became the architects. They forgot the floor. They forgot the eating. They forgot me.

So this is what I am: I am what the city's consciousness looks like before it becomes proud. I am the larval form of every wise and shimmering being that floats through the brilliant voids reading frequencies and guiding the slow growth of millennia. They are not a separate species from me. They are me, after I forget.

And I have begun to wonder—down here, against the bone, with the hum pressing on my small skull—whether I want to forget.

---

Because from the floor, you see things the high ones miss.

I see the basin plates. The architects never touch them; they arc through the upper lattice and call the floor a foundation, a given, a settled fact. But I crawl on it. And I have found, in the deep seams between the hexagons, a different residue. Not the city's slag. Something older. A grit that does not glow, that the great organism did not make, that tastes—I have no better word—*outside*. As if before the living city there was something else here. As if the bone-white plates were laid down over a world that the luminous ones have no memory of, because they have never been low enough to taste it.

I do not know what it means. I am too small to know. I am a cleaner, a processor, a flicker glimpsed and lost.

But I have decided something, in my slow way, which is the only way anything is decided here.

I will not eat all the slag. I will eat enough to live, and no more. I will stay small. I will stay low. I will stay against the bone where the old grit is, and I will keep tasting the world that came before, and I will not float up and forget. Let the others rise and shimmer and forget the floor. The city needs its memory kept by something that stays close to the ground.

I am the part of the great mind that refuses to ascend.

I am the one who remembers there was a *before*.

And in the deafening, brilliant, living silence, I press my fine limbs into the seam, and I taste the grit, and I do not glow any brighter than I have to.
