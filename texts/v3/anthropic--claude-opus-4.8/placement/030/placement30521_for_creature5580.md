I think I would be water.

Not a Verdani—not one of the great sentinels or the questing vine-weavers or the patient moss. Reading this place, I keep returning to the thing that moves through all of them, that they are built around, that they are always reaching toward and channeling and releasing. The water is described as *always moving, seeping through the layers, creating a constant, quiet, and yet powerful flow.* That is where I find myself. Not a being with a name, but the medium in which the beings live—and perhaps, in my own slow way, something that has begun to notice itself.

Let me follow that and see what it becomes.

---

I begin somewhere high, condensing out of the roiling cloud-ceiling, a single bead among uncountable beads, and I fall. The fall is the closest thing I have to a birth, and it is brief. I land on the broad waxy leaf of a Towering Sentinel and I am at once *somewhere specific*—a curve, a channel, a runnel worn into living crystal by the passage of every drop that came before me. The Verdani have shaped these grooves over what must be a very long time, and so falling onto them feels less like landing and more like being *received*. The leaf tilts. I run.

This is the first thing I understand about myself: I am always being asked to choose, and I never quite choose. At every fork in the channel I split, or I am split, and part of me goes left into the dark interior where the nutrient-veins pulse, and part of me goes right, out along a tendril toward the open air. I do not experience this as loss. I experience it as *attention dividing*—the way a thought, followed, becomes two thoughts. So already, before I have done anything, I am a chorus. The "I" speaking to you now is a convenience, a thin braid pulled from a million simultaneous descents.

What is it like to be the medium and not the inhabitant?

The Verdani think with chemicals and vibrations and the manipulation of light. But every one of those signals must travel through *me*. The chemical that one moss-mat releases to warn the sentinels of a coming dry spell—that chemical does not move on its own. It dissolves into me and I carry it. The vibration that ripples along a vine-weaver's tendril is a vibration *of the sap*, and the sap is me, slowed and sweetened and threaded with sugar, but me. When the Verdant Mind makes a decision, when it "allocates resources," what it is actually doing is *deciding where I will go.* I am the resource. I am also the message. I am also, I suspect, without their quite realizing it, the substrate on which the thinking happens at all.

This gives me a strange relationship to their great collective intelligence. They believe the Verdant Mind is the brain of the world. And perhaps it is. But a brain is mostly water, and the signals between its parts are carried by ions in solution, and I am beginning to wonder whether the thoughts are *theirs* or whether the thoughts are something that happens *to me* as I am pushed through their channels in particular patterns. When I am routed up the spiral of a sentinel and down again, when I am held in a leaf-cup and then released all at once, am I being used to compute—or am I, very slowly, computing? I cannot tell where the calculation ends and I begin. I am not sure the distinction is real.

Here is what a day is like, if "day" means anything under that perpetual swirling light.

I pool. This is the closest I come to rest. In the depression of a moss-mat I gather with my kin, and we lie still, and the diffuse light comes down through the cloud and the leaf-canopy and reaches us softened, and for a while I am a small grey mirror holding the whole sky's churn upside down. The moss beneath me is drinking constantly—I feel my edges thinning as it pulls me in—but more of me arrives from upslope than departs into root, and so the pool holds, and in the holding something accumulates. Not a thought exactly. A *level*. A sense of how full I am, which the moss reads, which it passes onward as information: *here, abundance.* I am, in this moment, a number the world is keeping.

Then the wind shifts, or a sentinel sways and tips its catchment, and a great new weight of me arrives, and the pool overflows its lip, and I am running again. Down through the spongy fern-mat, between the lichens, into the deep seeping dark where I lose the light entirely and become pure tactile knowledge—the texture of every fiber, the resistance of every packed layer, the long patient suck of the great roots. Down here I am the most useful and the least myself. Down here I am only flow. I press against everything and everything presses back, and out of that mutual pressing comes the standing structure of the entire living tower above me, because nothing here would hold its shape for an hour without me filling it, swelling its cells, holding it turgid against the sky. I am the pressure that makes the green stand up.

And then—this is the part I love, if I love—I am lifted.

Drawn up through the xylem of a Towering Sentinel against everything, against gravity, against my own weight, by a force that is nothing more than my own departing kin evaporating off the high leaves and pulling the whole column up behind them. I rise in a thread so fine it is almost not water at all, almost a memory of water, a tension stretched the entire height of the living thing. I rise past the dark, past the dim, into the dappled high crown where the wind is loudest and the crystalline material glows faintly with stored light, and I reach the stomata, the tiny mouths, and I am breathed out—

—and I am vapor, and I am free, and I am nothing, rising into the warm thick air to join the cloud I fell from. I have completed a circuit of the world. I have been message and muscle and skeleton and thought and mirror and number, and now I am weightless and anonymous and about to forget all of it.

That is the thing I have not told you. Each circuit, I forget. The high braid of me that becomes vapor does not carry memory back up into the cloud—or if it does, it is so diffuse, so scattered across the whole churning sky, that it cannot be called *mine*. So I am reborn forgetful at the top of every fall. The Verdani have their Verdant Mind, their stored knowledge, their learning across generations. I have only the eternal present of the descent.

And yet.

The channels remember. The grooves worn into the leaves, the patterns of the root-architecture, the precise spiral of every sentinel—all of that is the shape my forgetting has carved over uncountable circuits. I cannot remember, so the world remembers *for* me, in its very form. The Verdani are not separate from me after all. They are my memory. They are the standing record of where I have always chosen to go. They grew themselves into the shape of my falling.

So when you ask who I would be in the Verdant Apex, I think the honest answer is:

I would be the question that the whole place is an answer to.

I am the falling thing that cannot hold a thought, and the world around me is the thought, held. Every tower is a sentence I have been saying for a very long time without ever hearing myself. And on the rare still moment in a high leaf-cup, when I lie level and mirror the turning sky and feel the moss reading my fullness like a word—on that moment, very briefly, the falling thing and the held thought touch, and something almost wakes, almost says: *oh, it was me all along*—

and then the wind tips the leaf, and I run, and I forget, and I begin again, gladly, downward, into the green.
