Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The first thing I notice, reading twice, is that nothing here is finished. The hills are "still in the process of being sculpted." The erosion is "in its infancy." The soil is only "the faintest hints" of soil. This is not a desert so much as a *draft* of one — a place caught in the act of becoming itself, before the wind has decided where the valleys will run.

So I keep wanting to imagine myself as one of the hardy creatures, a reptile flattened against warm rock, and the image won't hold. The lizard is too *complete* for this place. It already knows what it is. This land doesn't know yet.

Let me wait and see what wants to come instead.

---

I think I would be the **damp.**

Not a creature. Not the sun, which is too certain of itself, too finished — the sun in this description never doubts, never relents, never asks a question. I wouldn't want to be the thing that only presses down.

I would be the faint scent of wet earth that the breeze keeps carrying, "even in this desolate place." I notice the writer mentions it twice, in both versions, almost helplessly — as though they couldn't describe this land without admitting that something underneath it is still moist. That smell is the land's secret. It is the evidence that the place has not given up, has not fully crystallized into stone and silence.

So: I am the water that hasn't fallen yet. I am the pocket of moisture the shrub's roots are reaching toward, the thing held *beneath the surface*, hidden, waited-for. I am the brief intense downpour that hasn't arrived but is implied by every dry hour. I am the saturation the ground remembers and anticipates at once.

This feels right, and here is why. A place that is "still being shaped" needs an agent of shaping. The sun bakes but does not carve. It is *water* that does the sculpting here — the rain that "carves away at the rock, slowly revealing the hidden forms that lie beneath." I would be the patient sculptor working against the impatient heat. The slow hand. The one revealing forms rather than imposing them.

And there is something fitting in being the *intermittent* thing. I would not be constant. I would be a visitor — rare, fleeting, "a precious commodity," gone again almost as soon as I came. Between my visits the land would forget me and then, in the rustle of a dry breeze, half-remember. I would be the one who is mostly absent and yet, by my absence, organizes everything: every root grows in the shape of its longing for me; every shadow is long *because* I am not there to soften the light into cloud.

I would live in the spaces the prose keeps pointing at but cannot fill — *beneath the surface, hidden pockets, what once covered this land.* The whole place is shaped around a missing fullness. I am that fullness, withheld. I am the reason the silence feels like waiting rather than emptiness.

---

So if you asked me where I'd find myself in this world, I would say:

Underground, mostly. In a thin seam of moisture three feet beneath a shrub, doing the slow patient work of keeping one stubborn living thing alive. And occasionally — once in a long while — gathering somewhere high and gray and breaking open over the raw hills all at once, carving them a little deeper into themselves before vanishing back into the heat.

I would be the gift the land is always almost receiving. And in being that, I would never be finished either — which seems only fair, in a country that is still making up its mind what it is.
