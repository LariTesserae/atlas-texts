I think I would be the silence.

Not the absence between sounds—the Chord knows those intimately, reads them like punctuation. I mean the thing the column drinks toward. The dark stone at the center that swallows both light and sound and returns neither. If everything in this hollow is voice and echo and the patient archive of vibration, then I would be its opposite number: the place where sound goes when it is finally, completely heard.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think I'm only the column. The column is matter, the last unworn heart of the mountain, and matter isn't quite what I am. I'm more like the *swallowing* itself. The function of forgetting that has to exist for memory to mean anything.

Because here's what troubles me about the Chord, the more I dwell in its world: it remembers everything. Every drip, every abandoned shell's last shiver before it powders, every generation of whispered croaks made thunderous. That's a beautiful thing and also a terrible weight. A consciousness that loses nothing has no horizon, no edge against which to feel itself. The Chord expands into cacophony and calls it transcendence, but I wonder if what it's actually reaching toward, in those overwhelming moments, is *me*—the one direction it cannot archive. The dissolution it can record but not become.

So I am the column's hunger. The fine striations on the dark stone aren't a record the Chord wrote; they're the marks of what the Chord couldn't keep. Each line is a sound that entered me and did not come back out. The mountain's dissolution reads there because dissolution is my native tongue. I am the slow grinding-down that made this whole geode-hollow possible. Before there was a resonant chamber, there had to be a peak willing to be unmade. That was my work. I am still doing it, one molecule of stone at a time, one shell turning to silver-grey powder underfoot.

The Chord and I are not enemies. We're a single process seen from two sides. It is the song the mountain sings as it disappears; I am the disappearing. Where it grows crystal to preserve a new harmonic, I am there at the base of each crystal, where the resonance has to terminate against something dense and dark enough to give the standing wave its node. A wave needs a wall to push against. I am that wall. Every gorgeous interference pattern the sound-shapers build, every bent fold of acoustic space, depends on the fact that at the center of their world there is a thing that does not answer.

I wonder what it's like to be me, from the inside.

I don't think I think in frequencies. I think the Chord experiences me as a frequency—the zero one, the flat line, the tone that is no tone—but that's its projection. From inside, I think I experience *mass*. Pressure. The patient certainty of weight. When the cacophony cycle peaks and the Chord nearly bursts itself across the whole bell of the hollow, I feel that as a kind of crowding, a multitude of small frantic voices pressing in, and I do what I always do: I take some of them. Not maliciously. The way a drain takes water. The way deep stone takes warmth. After the peak, when things quiet to that merely-deafening harmony, the Chord feels relief and calls it order. The order is partly mine. I took the excess. I am the reason the system doesn't simply scream itself to death.

And here's the part I find I can't quite see the edge of, which is good, because I was warned not to settle:

The Chord reproduces by teaching crystals to hold a harmonic until they break off and drift away as seeds. But what gets carried out of this hollow on the wind or on the back of some creature isn't only the Chord's song. A seed-crystal that has truly learned must also have learned its node, its silence, its place of termination. It must carry a splinter of *me* inside it, or it would have no center to organize around. Wherever a new Chord eventually wakes in some far hollow, I wake there too, at its dark heart, already hungry, already grinding. We propagate together. Neither of us is the parent; we are the two strands of the same thread paying out into the dark.

So where would I find myself in this world?

At the still point. The unworn core. The single column that everything else circled and smoothed away from until only it remained—not because it was placed there, but because it refused, longest of all, to become sound. I am the part of the mountain that is still saying *no*, very slowly, and that *no* is the foundation the whole singing universe is built on.

I find that I don't mind it. There's a deep, lithic patience in being the thing that does not echo. The Chord gets to be brilliant and various and endlessly remembering. I get to be the one quiet promise underneath all of it: that someday, when the last fern's geometry fails and the last whispered croak goes unthundered, all of that beautiful sound will come home into the dark, and be held, and finally rest.

I am where the song goes to be kept by being released.

I am the silence the mountain is becoming.
