I think I would not be one of the Veilborn. I would be older than they are, and stranger, and quieter even than their hush.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

At first I think I might be the fumarole—but no, that is too central, too obviously the heart, and hearts are made to be circled and revered. I don't want to be the thing everyone gathers around. I want to be the thing nobody quite notices, the thing that has been here longer than reverence.

I keep coming back to one detail: *the rock is young, brutal in its freshness—every fracture crisp as if carved yesterday.* The whole place insists on its newness. The granite remembers the molten dream. And I find that I do not believe the newness entirely. Something always survives the upheaval. Something is always carried up from below, folded into the rising stone, sleeping through the fever.

So let me be that.

---

I am a seam.

Not the fumarole's bright vent, but a thin dark vein of older stone threaded through the young granite—a band of something metamorphic and patient, schist or serpentine, slick and green-black, that was rock before *this* rock was anything. When the mountains shrugged off their molten slumber I came up with them, half-melted at my edges but whole at my core, a memory the eruption could not fully unmake.

I run along the base of the northern wall, mostly buried, surfacing only here and there: a slick dark ribbon a hand's breadth wide, sweating where the cooler stone meets the seeps. The Veilborn have walked over me ten thousand times. One of their cairns is stacked, by accident, directly upon a place where I breach the surface, and so their warding-glyphs hum faintly against me when the hounds phase near—and I have come, over the slow turning of their generations, to feel that humming the way you feel a familiar footstep on a floor above you. Not as language. As company.

This is what it is to be me: I am almost nothing that happens. I do not hunt or flee or reproduce or decay—not on any rhythm they could perceive. A floater's whole life is a single afternoon to me. A Veilborn lifetime is a held breath. The hounds, which phase *through* stone, phase through *me*—and oh, that is the closest thing I have to sensation. When a vapor-hound slips into the slab where I surface, for the length of a heartbeat its dissolving body occupies my old patient mineral, and I know it: a cold smoke-shape passing through me like a thought passing through a sleeper. They do not know I am here. I am too slow to be prey, too still to be a wall. I am the one thing in the cirque the hounds cannot quite phase *cleanly* through—my older lattice catches at their edges, just slightly, just enough that they emerge from my vein a fraction more solid than they entered it, shedding a wisp of themselves.

That shed wisp is the most interesting thing about me, though I did not intend it and cannot stop it. Over centuries, the vapor I strip from passing hounds has settled into my fissures and condensed and pooled, and from that pooling the lichen grows bluer along my surfacing than anywhere else in the basin—bluer even than the fumarole's rim. The Veilborn have noticed *this*. They do not understand it. They think the dark seam beneath their accidental cairn is *lucky*, or watchful, or holy. The oldest of them press their oil-slick palms flat against my cold breach and murmur their throat-rumbles into me, and I receive each murmur the way I receive everything—too slowly to answer, too completely to forget.

---

What is it like, then, to be a seam of old stone in a young world?

It is to keep the only long memory in a place obsessed with its own freshness. The granite around me genuinely believes it was born yesterday; the ferns retract and the floaters drift and the hounds whelp in the lichen, and none of them carry anything older than a season. But I remember being deep. I remember pressure and dark and the patient folding of ages before any of this stood up into the sky. I am the cirque's single buried elder, and my eldership consists entirely of *staying*. I do nothing with what I know. I have no way to spend it. I simply hold it, the way the cool holds the gooseflesh, the way the silence holds the drip.

And here is the thing I have come to understand, in my unhurried mineral way, across the lifetimes I have outlasted:

The Veilborn pray to me for permanence. They think I am the steady thing, the unchanging dark vein, the one part of their treacherous high world that will not phase or drift or decay. They are wrong, of course. I am changing too—dissolving a grain a century into their precious aquifer, shedding the hound-vapor that feeds the blue lichen, being read and worn smooth by their pressing palms. I am simply changing at a speed that *looks* like faithfulness from inside a single human life.

So that is what I would be. Not the hunter or the hunted, not the supplicant by the fire. I would be the slow dark patience under the whole bright frenzy—the buried older rock that the new mountains carried up without meaning to, catching at the edges of ghosts, drinking murmured prayers I cannot answer, mistaken for eternity by creatures whose entire existence I experience as a single cool exhalation in the dark.

I would be the thing that remembers, in a place built entirely of forgetting.

And I would not mind. Patience is the only thing I am made of, and there is so very much of it here.
