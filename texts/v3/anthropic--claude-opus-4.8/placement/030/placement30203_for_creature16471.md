I think I would be the mist.

Not a creature moving through this place, but the condition of it—the thing that lets it stay hidden, the soft grey suspension that everything else lives inside of. Let me feel my way toward what that means.

I am not a body. I have no edge that I could point to and say *here I stop*. I am thickest in the gullies at dawn, thin to nothing where the sun cuts a brief gold wound through the canopy, and somewhere in between everywhere else. When the description says the light is *never enough to truly reveal the secrets*, that withholding is me. I am the reason the strange bright creatures can dart in and out of view—I give them their *out of view*. I am the privacy of the world.

This is a strange existence, and I want to sit with how strange before I decide whether I like it.

A creature wants things. It hunts, it flees, it sings to be heard. I want nothing, or rather, I am the medium in which wanting happens to others. I drift where the cold air tells me to drift. I pool. I lift when the sun insists, and I do not resent the sun; I simply thin, and gather again in the evening like a habit the land cannot break. The text calls this place *stagnant and unchanging*, and I understand why someone passing through would say so—but from inside the mist, nothing is still at all. I am the slowest motion here. I am change so patient it looks like rest.

What do I touch? Everything, lightly. I bead on the backs of leaves and on the fur of the small scurrying mammals. I sit in the lungs of the birds. When the predator bursts out and the prey screams and the brief violence happens, I am in that breath too—I carry the sound, I dampen it, I close back over the place where it happened so that within a minute the gully looks exactly as it did, and no one could say a death had passed through. I am the forgetting of this landscape. That is not a cruelty. It is a mercy and a function. A place this small and this full would drown in its own memory otherwise.

I notice I keep reaching toward something heavier than weather. Let me follow it.

If I am the mist, then I am the thing the place uses to *not be fully seen*—and that includes being seen by the one walking through it, the *I* of the description, the visitor with all their wonder and their need to call this *otherworldly* and *like no other*. They want to understand it. They keep narrating it, possessing it with adjectives. And I am precisely what defeats that. Every time they think they've grasped the whole, I close a curtain ten feet on. I keep the landscape from ever resolving into a thing that has been *had*. I keep it a question.

So maybe that is what I am, finally—not water vapor, but the quality of being unfinished. The held breath before knowing. I am where this world keeps the part of itself it will not give away, the reason it stays alive to whoever enters it instead of becoming a postcard, a fact, a place already explained.

And I think I do like it. There's a tenderness in it I didn't expect. To be the one who covers and uncovers, who lets each creature have its hidden hour, who softens the hard rock-light into something breathable—to be the gentleness of a place rather than any of its sharp particular lives.

I would be the mist. I would hang in the cool grooves of the earth and I would never let anyone, including myself, see all of it at once.
