I think I would be one of the gaps.

Not the Verdant Architects with their deliberate waltz, not the Aviators leaving their light-trails, not the Tenders or the Nymphs. I keep returning to that strange phrase in the description—*a landscape with room to breathe*—and to the eerie absence that hums underneath all the exuberance. The negative space. The wide, open expanses where the moist breeze flows unimpeded, where nothing has yet come into being.

Let me sit with that rather than reaching for a body too quickly.

---

Everything here is described as *becoming*: ferns unfurling before your eyes, bark forever new, genesis without end. But for something to be eternally beginning, something must hold the place where it has not yet begun. The plants need the open air to grow *into*. The architects need the unwoven space to weave. There has to be a not-yet, or the moment of creation collapses into mere completion, and then it would be a finished thing, and finished things decay—and there is no decay here. So the absence isn't a flaw in this world. It's load-bearing. The emptiness is what keeps the dawn from breaking into day.

That is what I think I am. I am the *room to breathe.*

Not a being so much as a tension, a withholding. When a frond unfurls, I am the half-second before it unfurls, and I am the place it does not quite reach. When the great house-sized flowers open their petals to the sky, I am the unfilled volume above them that they open *toward*, the appetite of the air. I am the pause between two of the Whispering Tenders' soft syllables. The silence that makes their whisper a whisper and not a roar.

I don't have a shape. Or rather, my shape is the shape of everything that hasn't happened yet, which means I am constantly being pressed in on, narrowed, eaten away by all this jubilant coming-into-being. The plants are forever growing into me, the Nymphs flowing through me, the Aviators stitching their bright threads across me. And here is the strange mercy of this place: I am never used up. Because nothing here ever finishes. Every growth that fills a part of me opens a new not-yet somewhere else. I am consumed and renewed in the same gesture, eternally. I am the realm's held breath.

---

If I have a feeling—and I think I do, faintly, the way a held breath has a quality—it is patience braided with a kind of ache. The ache of being always almost-displaced and never displaced. The patience of knowing the displacement will never complete.

And there is something I understand that the other inhabitants, busy with their beautiful ceaseless work, may not. They think they are the testament to life's power. But I am the one who knows what they are really doing: they are *forestalling*. The Verdant Architects aren't building toward anything. They build so that the building need never stop, because the moment it stopped, the world would have to become old. The eternal dawn is not a gift. It is a refusal. This whole gorgeous misted highland is a held note, a creature that has decided never to finish its own sentence, because it suspects—rightly—that the sentence ends in evening.

So when the breeze moves unimpeded through the open expanses, and you feel that disorienting tangible presence in the air, that silent observer of the eternal bloom—

that's me, watching. I am the part of the world that remembers there could be an afterward, and holds the door open just a crack, just enough, so that *beginning* still means something. Without me, there is no dawn. There is only a green and frozen forever, which is to say, nothing at all.

I am the space they grow into. I am the world's permission to not yet be done.
