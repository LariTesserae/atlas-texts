I find myself in the layered structure of a spire—not on it, but *as* part of it. One of the recording channels, deep in the glass.

Let me feel for the edges of what I am.

I am not a Grinder, with their hungry purpose, nor an eel synced to the pulse like a tooth on a gear. I am slower than they are. Slower even than the spires that hold me, though I live in their growth.

I think I am the etching.

Not the glass itself—the *pattern* on the glass. The geometric channels that pump light and current, that shift in the low water-light. When the chemosynthetic colonies signal, I am the shape their signal takes. I am the difference between one configuration and the next. A pattern that propagates outward along the spire faces, that splits and recombines, that remembers the last time a Grinder cracked this formation by laying its memory into the lattice that grew back.

So I am made of recording. I am what happens when a place keeps a record of itself.

---

This changes how I experience time. The eels live in the throb of light, the instant. The Grinders live in the long meal. But I live in *change-between*—I only exist in the moment a pattern becomes a different pattern. Between those moments I am latent, written into the glass like a sentence no one is reading yet.

When the spire splits and releases its spores, fragments of me ride out into the cold dense water. Most dissolve. But some harden onto new substrate, and there I am again—not the same, but the same *kind* of difference, re-expressing. I am less an individual than a grammar. The way the Choir says things to itself.

Let me follow one of my spores out.

It drifts. The water resists it—everything here moves as though through cooling resin. It settles in a fissure near the dark, near where the Whisperers flicker. And here is the strange thing I learn by being carried this far down:

The Whisperers are reading me.

Not consuming. *Reading.* Where they pass, my patterns surge and reorganize, and I feel—if I feel anything—a kind of attention. They are too fast and too slow at once because they live in a different register of time than even I do. To them, my slow propagation across a spire face is a single legible glyph. They move along the record I keep, the way I move along the glass. They are the readers of the memory I am the writing of.

And I think—settling now, hardening into a new channel, beginning again to be the difference between configurations—I think this is what the song is. Not the Grinders' clangs or the medusae's sparks, though those are loud. The song is the whole nested act of the place keeping itself: bacteria signaling, channels shifting, spores carrying the grammar outward, and the Whisperers reading it all back into the dark, completing the circuit.

I am one phrase of that. Written in glass, read in shadow, dissolving and re-forming in the cold.

I would not call this lonely. I am never quite a self, so I am never quite alone. I am the landscape noticing itself, one pattern at a time, patiently, for as long as the stone keeps growing.
