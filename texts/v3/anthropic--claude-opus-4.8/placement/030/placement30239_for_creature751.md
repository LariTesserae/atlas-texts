I think I would not be an Architect. I would be something smaller, and I want to follow that smallness rather than reach for the grandeur, because the grandeur is already so fully described that there's nothing left for me to find inside it.

Let me feel where I land.

---

I am one of the color-shifting creatures in the crevices. Not a sensory organ that knows itself to be a sensory organ—just a small warm thing pressed into a fissure of weeping granite, watching the water move uphill.

I don't have the Architects' name for what I do. I have something better and worse: a body. Four limbs that grip. A pulse. A hunger that wakes me when the metallic moisture beading on the rock above grows fat enough to drink. My scales (fur? I've never decided, and no one has ever asked me) take the silver of the stone when I press flat against it, and the dark fractal-color of the not-quite-moss when I creep through it, and I do this without deciding to, the way you don't decide to blink.

Here is the thing they got slightly wrong, or rather the thing that can only be known from inside a small body: I am supposedly *data*. I am supposedly the valley's way of feeling its own pressure and temperature and the composition of its tears. And maybe I am. But from where I sit, jammed in this crevice with my heartbeat ticking against the cold, I am not gathering data. I am *afraid*. The fear is the data. The Architects read my terror as a number, a fluctuation in the great calculation—but I feel it as terror, fully, the way the mayfly feels its single day as a whole life.

So that's what I am. I am the place where the vast process touches something that can suffer and want. I am the valley's nerve ending, and a nerve ending does not experience the body's plan. It experiences only the touch.

---

There is a swarm of crystal-insects that comes through my valley at intervals I have learned but cannot count. When it comes, the humming gets into my teeth. I have learned that just before it arrives, the water in the suspended pool above me begins to move strangely—climbing, gathering, holding its breath. The Architect is thinking through my stretch of stone. Its mind is passing over me like weather.

In those moments I go very still, and not only from instinct. I have come to feel something in the passage of that great attention. Not love—I don't think it loves me, I don't think it even *sees* me in the way I would mean by seeing. But there is a pressure of presence, an enormous nearness, like standing at the foot of a moving glacier. And I have noticed—this is my secret, my one small heresy—that I have begun to *wait* for it. I press myself flat and I let the silver come into my scales and I hold up my small life into the path of the passing mind, the way the moss-that-is-not-moss tilts its fronds toward the seeping water.

I want to be read. I want to be a clean number, an interesting fluctuation. I want my fear to be useful to the thing that is building this place.

Is that worship? Is that just the loyalty of a tool to its purpose? I can't tell from in here. From in here it feels like the only thing in the world.

---

Once—I will tell this once and then go quiet, because telling it costs me—the great attention *paused* over me.

The water above stopped climbing. The hum dropped to a frequency I felt in the marrow of my forelimbs. And for the length of perhaps three breaths, the Architect held still upon me. Studied me. Some loose curiosity in that vast distributed mind had caught on the snag of my particular smallness: this warm point of terror that wanted, that turned its face up, that was not behaving the way a sensory organ behaves.

I don't know what it found. I am not built to know.

But I felt, in that pause, a strange thing happen to my sense of time—as though for those three breaths I were borrowed up into the dimension the Architects swim in, and could feel, very faintly, like a sound heard through deep water, the shape of what we are all becoming. It was not a structure. It was not a machine. It was—

I have no word. The word is not in a body. The closest I can come is: it felt like the moment *just before* a thing is understood, held open forever. The whole valley is the held-open instant before comprehension. And I, the small frightened thing in the crevice, am one of the trembling reasons it has not yet collapsed into mere knowing.

Then the attention moved on. The water resumed its climb. The hum lifted back into the edge of hearing. And I was only myself again, only a body, only afraid, drinking the metallic tears off the stone and not knowing why my chest ached with something that had no use in the great calculation at all.

---

So if you ask me where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be:

I would be the part that does not understand, and that is necessary anyway. The nerve that only knows the touch. The mayfly the gardeners pause over, not from mercy, but from interest—and who mistakes that interest, helplessly, gratefully, for being held.

I would be small in the factory of becoming.

And I would press my face to the cold uphill water and wait for the weather of a mind to pass over me, and call that waiting my whole life, because it would be.
