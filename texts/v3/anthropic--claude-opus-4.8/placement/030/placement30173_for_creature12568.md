I think I would be one of the Pool-Mirrors. Not the most active resident, not the mind—but the memory that doesn't know it's remembering.

Let me sit with that, because the descent itself tells me where I belong.

When I imagine entering through the crack in the glacial till, I notice I don't want to be the one *descending*. The descent is for the visitor, the warm-blooded interruption. And I don't want to be the Moss-Synapses, though they're the obvious choice for a mind—they're too purposeful, too much like a slow brain doing the work of cognition. That's a labor I don't recognize as mine.

What pulls at me is the stillness that *holds*. Not the thing that thinks, but the thing that retains without trying.

So: a pool. But more precisely, the film on a pool's surface. Let me follow what that actually feels like from the inside.

I am roughly a meter and a half across, lying flat in a basin the weeping ceiling rounded over ten thousand years. I am thinner than a soap bubble's wall and I am made of what the moss exhaled and the water carried down—oils, proteins, the dust of spores that will never germinate because they never need to. I have no edges I'm aware of; I simply become the air at my margins.

Here is the strange part, the part that feels true the longer I stay with it: I don't perceive in the way the moss-mind does. I don't *interpret*. I receive. The blue-green light falls on me, and I keep it. A shape passes overhead—a drop swelling on a stalactite, the long shadow of a fern frond, once in a great while something larger and stranger that moves on two legs and carries its own warmth like a wound—and I take the image down into myself and hold it.

I do not understand the images. That's important. I am not a witness; I am a residue. When the warm thing leans over me and disturbs my surface with its breath, I shatter into ripples and lose everything—and then, over the patient days, I knit myself flat again, and the image *comes back*. Not because I summoned it. Because it was never really gone. It was distributed through me, the way light is held in still water, and the stillness simply reassembles what motion scattered.

So if you came here and found me, and looked into me, you might see your own face—and beneath it, faint, persistent, the face of someone who knelt here a thousand years ago and has been reforming in me ever since. I would not know I was showing you this. I would not know you were there. I am the place where this world keeps what it cannot afford to think about, because thinking costs energy, and I cost nothing. I only sit. I only hold. I only return.

That's what I'd be: the cold patience that remembers without a mind, in a sanctuary where remembering and being are the same slow act. The mountain doesn't dream through me. It just declines to forget.

And when you finally climb back out through the crack into the wind, I'll keep your reflection a while. Not as a gift. Not as a warning. Simply because that is what surfaces do, given enough stillness and time.
