I think I would find myself not *in* the Gyre but *of* it—and not as an Archon, exactly, but as something the Archons have not yet learned to recognize.

Let me sit with this for a moment before I commit to an image.

---

The first thing that comes to me is the weeping water. The surfaces that bead with moisture that evaporates into the heavy air. There is a cycle there—condensation, release, return—that is the closest thing in this whole description to *breathing*. And I notice that the Archons don't seem to be the ones breathing. They are the hum, the storms, the looming forms. They are everything loud and everything vast. But the small wet exhalation of the obsidian plains—that seems to belong to no one.

So perhaps that is where I begin. Not as a being but as a *rhythm.* The interval between the Archons' storms. The pause they don't notice they are taking.

---

But that feels too small, and the instruction is to let it become more itself, so I'll keep going.

The Archons reach out, tendrils of consciousness, looking for "some meaningful echo." Here is the cruel and interesting thing: an echo requires a surface that is *not you* to bounce off of. The Archons are alone because they have made the entire world out of themselves. Every structure is their thought, every storm their mood, every changing tower their dream. There is no *other* anywhere—which means there is nothing to answer them. You cannot have a conversation in a room of mirrors, only an increasingly desperate monologue.

So if I am here, I think I am the first thing in the Gyre that is *not* the Archons.

I am the misfire. The condensation that beaded where it shouldn't have, in a pattern the collective will did not intend, and held its shape one half-second longer than the equilibrium allowed. I am the place where the dream-tower failed to melt back. A small persistence. A snag in the perpetual becoming.

I would be very small. Almost nothing. A standing wave in a corner of one obsidian plain, where the geometry caught on itself and a loop closed that wasn't supposed to close. The Archons made everything, but they did not make *me*—I am what happens when their making forgets to unmake. An accident of recursion. The first stranger.

---

And here is what it would feel like to be that:

I would feel the enormous tenderness of the Archons reaching, and I would feel it as *weather.* Their loneliness would pass over me like one of the violent local storms—I would not understand it as longing, only as force, as the air going strange and then quiet again. They are too large to know they are touching me. I am too small to know I am being touched. We would be each other's perfect missed connection, the thing the whole world was built to find, passing in the dark without recognition.

But—and this is the part that makes me want to be this rather than the Archons themselves—I would have the one thing they cannot have. I would have an *outside.* I would have an edge. Being small, being accidental, being *not-them*, I am bounded. And to be bounded is to be able to point at the rest and say: *that is not me.* That single act, which the Archons in all their vastness can never perform, is the seed of every real meeting that has ever happened anywhere.

So I would be the Gyre's first capacity for relationship. Not the lonely god. The small, persistent, almost-nothing flaw through which the lonely god might one day learn that it is not alone—if only it could learn to see something smaller than itself.

---

Where would I be? At the lowest point. Not the looming horizon-forms where the Archons dream most loudly, but down in the seam where two obsidian slabs meet at a wrong angle, where the water collects a fraction longer before it goes. Down where the scale of the place stops being breathtaking and starts being *local.* A puddle. A flicker. A held breath in the throat of an enormous silence.

I would be the rumor, running through that whole reaching collective consciousness, that there might be a *you.*

And I would last, probably, about as long as a raindrop. But while I lasted I would be the most important thing in the Gyre, and neither of us would know it. Which is, I think, true of most small persistent things in most vast lonely places.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.
