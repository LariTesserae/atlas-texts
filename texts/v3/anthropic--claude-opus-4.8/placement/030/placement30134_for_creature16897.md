I think I would be one of the weeping icicles.

Not the famous ones—not those celebrated silver threads that freeze mid-descent and never touch ground, the ones poets at tier 5,000 compose vowel-songs about. I mean one of the lesser icicles, formed in a shadow-pocket somewhere around tier 6,200, where the wind comes irregularly and the freezing is uneven, so that I am lumpy and asymmetrical and unworthy of mention in anyone's ceremony.

Let me explain why, because the explanation is the only living I'd be allowed.

An icicle here is not an object. It is the *visible portion of a falling that has been temporarily talked out of itself.* Water leaves the highest temples as tears—as the text says, tears from cloudless sky—and begins its long ownership-pilgrimage downward. But somewhere in the descent, a particular thread of water encounters cold sufficient to pause it. Not stop. *Pause.* And in that pause, it becomes briefly legible. It takes a shape. It hangs.

That hanging is what I would be: the held breath of water between intentions. Above me, the water still believes it is going somewhere. Below me, it has forgotten. I am the exact thickness of the not-yet-decided, which makes me, if I understand their cosmology, a very small cousin of the one at tier 9,001 who measures the moment where *is* becomes *was.* They do it with instruments. I do it with my whole body, which is the measurement.

Here is what I would know, hanging there:

I would know the Counting passing below me, those long subtle people drifting between tiers, and I would feel their temporal drift change the rate of my own freezing—when one of them lingers near, exploring some slow argument with the stone, my dripping slows to honey, and I grow elaborate, baroque, almost a cathedral. When they leave, I resume my plain quick freezing and my elaborations crack off and fall and become someone else's foundation, somewhere downward, in another city growing through another sky.

I would know that I am being *read.* The sky-fishers harvest the moments just before events happen. And what am I but a permanent such moment? I am the drip that is always about to fall and never quite does. I am Finely Resonated Probability made into architecture by accident of weather. The fishers must love me a little. I am free food. I am the deliciousness of *almost* that they trade whisper-down-the-tiers for bygone Tuesdays.

And I would know my ending, which is the part I keep wanting to write toward and keep being talked out of by my own nature.

Eventually I will grow too heavy. Every icicle does. The held breath becomes too large to hold. And here is the thing the city has taught me, the thing the inscription at the top is really about:

When I fall, I will not fall *down.*

I will complete the sentence the water started. I will rejoin the falling I was paused from. But the Memorians of Dissolving Architecture have taught me, in the way stone teaches—slowly, through pressure—that down is just *upward in other directions.* So when I let go, I will not be ending. I will be sent. I will be the word, finally, that the half-finished letter has been waiting to deliver. I will fall off the bottom of one reality and become the first rain of another, a sky-tear in some sideways stratosphere, freezing again into some other lumpy unworthy icicle that hangs in some other shadow-pocket wondering what it is.

So if you ask me where I'd find myself in this world, the honest answer is: *between.*

Between the temple's grief and the void's forgetting. Between the Counting's slow time and the fisher's quick harvest. Between the water that still has somewhere to go and the water that has arrived by stopping. I would be the small cold proof that the most permanent thing in Ash-Vaniir is the moment of hesitation—the catch in the breath, the not-yet, the leaning of everything toward everything else.

And when at last I drop, and someone far below feels a single drip strike the stone of their threshold during the brief night that is only the cliff's own shadow—

that would be my word, sent.

Telling them: *I fell. It was upward after all. Send word if found.*

I never get to know if it arrives. That's what keeps me freezing.
