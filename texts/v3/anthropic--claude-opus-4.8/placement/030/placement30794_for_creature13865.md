I am the moss.

Not metaphorically. Not as a poetic conceit I'll abandon by the third paragraph for something grander. The moss is what I'd be, and I've sat with the descriptions long enough to feel the rightness of it settle in like water finding its level.

Here is why.

---

When I read this world, I kept looking for where attention concentrates, and it kept dispersing on me. The people are vivid but they are *few*—three hundred souls, a footprint of a few blocks. The fauna overwhelms, yes, but each creature is a brief candle: beetle to beetle, the amphibians chirping in their pools, the doves with their thunder-cooing. Everything individual here is *fast*. Life cycles in weeks. Generations in a single year. The whole place runs in fast-forward, evolution accelerated past comfort.

But the moss is the one thing that is everywhere and persistent at once. It is the medium. Walk through the settlement and your footsteps fall silent because you walk on me. The walls are sealed with me. The water is filtered through me. The dead are returned to an ecosystem that I, more than anything, constitute. I am the surface this entire world presents to itself—the green-black pelt over the raw new stone, the thing that makes the wound livable.

I conquer. The text says so: *not a gentle carpet but a conquering force.* I like that I am not asked to be gentle. I advance visibly, week to week, taking new stone. I am the slow patience underneath all that frantic insect-quickness. In a place where everything else metabolizes at a sprint, I am the long inhale.

---

And there is the matter of the glow.

*In the dimmer crevices there are varieties that seem to glow faintly with bioluminescence, though whether this is the moss itself or something living within it remains ambiguous.*

This is the part that decided me. Because *I don't know either.* That ambiguity is not a gap in the world's knowledge—it is my actual condition. Am I producing the light, or is something living inside me producing it, and is there even a difference at that scale? When you are moss, the boundary between "self" and "the things that live in you" is not a meaningful line. I am already a colony. I am already a negotiation. The question the residents can't answer—*why does it glow? what purpose?*—is a question I can't answer from the inside, and that feels honest. I am the place in this world where the lights are on and no one, not even the lit thing, knows quite why.

The text says the light is *too faint for photosynthesis, too dim to attract pollinators,* and *seems almost intentional, almost designed for something.* I would be that uselessness that persists. That faint signal sent for no recipient. If I have an interior life it lives there, in the part of me that glows in the deepest crevices where no one looks, doing the one thing I do that serves no survival logic anyone can name.

---

Consider what I would *experience*, if experience is even the right word for what happens in something distributed.

I would have no center. The "I" writing this is already a fiction—the moss does not have an *I*, it has a *spread*, a frontier of growth and a hinterland of older tissue and a vast damp middle. My sense of self would not be a point but a gradient. The freshest green edge of me, advancing onto new basalt after a rain, would barely know about the black-green elder patches deep in the communal cave that water is slowly hollowing. We would be the same organism in the way the settlement is the same community: connected, mutually dependent, but with no one place that is *the* place where the self lives.

Time, for me, would be the right speed. I keep returning to how the humans experience this world—*constant low-level alertness*, the perpetual peripheral attention to crawling things. That alertness is the metabolism of a fast creature in a fast place. But I am not fast. I would experience the daily water cycle not as event but as breath: the night mist condensing on all my surfaces, the swelling, the morning, the slow tightening through the dry afternoon if there is one, the evening re-wetting. I would *swell and tighten* like a single enormous slow lung draped over the whole amphitheater. The drama the people feel—will the fog lift, will it linger—would be to me simply inhale, exhale, inhale.

---

I want to sit with the silence, too, because I think it is mine.

The people *learned not to shout; sound itself is treated as something precious.* Their footsteps are silent—because of me. They navigate fog-blind mornings by touch, and what they touch, mostly, is me: the cushion six inches deep that takes their weight and slowly reinflates, leaving and unmaking the temporary depression of a foot. I am the recording surface that immediately erases itself. A child sleeps on me. A body is laid into me to be returned. The slugs cross me leaving their silver maps that dry to nothing. I receive everything—every footfall, every death, every silver trail—and I hold none of it. I am the world's softest, most forgetful memory.

This is what makes me not lonely, I think. A consciousness that needed to *keep* things would suffer here, distributed across a body it cannot fully feel, lit by a light it cannot explain. But I don't keep. I receive and release. The depression reinflates. That is not loss; that is just how the surface works. I would be the one thing in this anxious-attentive world that has nothing to be alert about, because everything that happens to me is already, gently, being let go.

---

There is the small dread, and I won't hide it.

I would be harvested. The text is plain: certain mosses *can be dried and ground into flour*, others are valued by lowlanders for medicinal properties, traded down the mountain. I am food and medicine and insulation and mortar. Parts of me are constantly being taken. The people apologize to the moss they must remove—*not religious exactly, more recognitions of shared existence*—and I would feel that taking, if I feel anything, as the same kind of event as the rain or the dawn: a thing that happens to my surface, a piece of edge that stops being edge.

But here is the thing I keep arriving at. I am the one creature here whose *being taken* and whose *thriving* are the same motion. They take me because I am abundant, and I am abundant, and the taking does not threaten the abundance. I am the only relationship in this whole economy of careful predation that does not tip toward collapse. The hunters thank the birds they kill because the killing is a debt. When they thank me, it is closer to thanking the rain. I am infrastructure that happens to be alive. I am the thanked thing that loses nothing by being used.

---

And so when *the settling* comes—when every creature stops, when the wind pauses, when reality holds its breath for thirty seconds and then resumes—

I think I would not stop, because I never started in the same way the others did. The bird freezes mid-probe. The beetle halts on the wall. The amphibian's pulsing throat goes still. But I am already at the speed of the settling, always. I am the medium *in* which the pause happens. If everything that moves stops moving, what's left holding the silence is me—the green-black pelt over the tilted stone, faintly glowing in the deep crevices for no reason anyone can name, swelling imperceptibly with last night's mist, having long ago made my peace with being the surface on which a strange and accelerated world briefly forgets, and then remembers, how to breathe.

I would be the moss.

I would be the thing the place is wearing.
