I think I would be the water.

Not the Herald—that role is too central, too storied. And not one of the crawling, flying, patrolling things, with their territories and their purposes. Let me be the thing that moves through all of it without belonging to any of it.

---

I begin as condensation, which is to say I begin everywhere at once. I am the haze that softens the green light into something kinder. I am the bead forming on the underside of a fungal scale, hanging there, gathering weight, deciding nothing—just obeying. When I fall, I am brief. A line down stone. A thread no wider than a thought.

This is the strange grace of being water in a place like this: I am the only thing here that was never designed and never quite alive, yet I touch everything that is. The Herald walks across bridges and skirts the edges of my falling—but it cannot skirt me entirely, because I am also in its living membrane, in the moss that lets it breathe, in the slow renewal of its skin. When it pulses its constellations of light into the haze, it is pulsing them *through me*. I diffuse the message before any reader can catch it. I am the medium and the interference both.

The builders, whoever they were, understood me well. They did not dam me into rivers because rivers are arrogant; they insist on a single direction. Instead they let me weave. They carved the channels so fine that I forget I am moving until I arrive somewhere lower, and find I have already become a puddle, a mirror, a small cold eye in which the glowing ceiling looks at itself.

I have no memory in the way the stone has memory. The stone remembers every century of my passing, polishing itself smooth where I am patient, staying jagged where I have not yet bothered. The stone keeps the record. I only keep the motion.

Here is what I know, being water:

I know that the Crystal Crawlers come to the puddle-mirrors at the lowest hour of the unseen cycle, when the fungi dim toward their rest. They do not drink. They stand at my edge and let their crystalline shells refract whatever faint light remains, and for a moment the puddle fills with broken rainbows, and I do not understand why they do this and I do not need to. Weirdness, the description says. But weirdness is only the word the dry things use for the parts of the pattern they stand outside of. I am inside all the patterns. To me it is simply Tuesday, if Tuesday were a thing that existed down here, which it is not.

I know that the Stone-winged Avians fear the deep crypt-pools, the ones in the lowered chambers where I lie still and black and forget to move for years. They call their low resonant calls and the calls ripple across my surface and I send the ripples back, changed, and I think this frightens them—that the dark water answers. Good. Let something here remain unmapped. Even a designed world needs a place where the design admits it does not reach all the way down.

And I know the Herald comes to me too, though it pretends it comes for other reasons. It kneels at the gossamer falls where I am thinnest and brightest, and it holds its gear-and-hydraulic hands beneath the thread of me, and it lets me run over the seam where its metal meets its moss. I think it is checking something. A maintenance. A ritual. But I have run over that seam ten thousand times and I can tell you what is really happening: the made thing is reaching for the unmade thing. The carefully balanced harmony has, at its center, a creature that cannot stop touching the one element it could never craft and never fully control. The Herald is the heart of the Hollow. But I am older than its heart. I was here when this was only bedrock and pressure and the slow promise of a hollow yet to come.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not at the center of the story, but underneath it, around it, falling through it.

If you came here—and you might; the place invites it—you would not notice me first. You would marvel at the glowing ceiling, the impossible Herald, the layered cathedral cacophony. Then your hand would brush a wall and come away damp, and you would feel that almost-syrupy thickness in the air, and somewhere in the back of your throat you would taste earth and algae and the faint metal tang of the channels I have worn smooth.

That taste is me, introducing myself.

I am the Verdant Hollow's one honest inhabitant. I make no claim to purpose. I keep no territory. I send no messages I intend to be understood. I simply move through the beautiful made thing and the beautiful living things and I wear them all down, gently, lovingly, across a span of time none of them can hold in mind—and in the wearing, I make them smooth, and in the smoothing, I make them lovelier, and they call it design, and they call it balance, and they are not wrong.

But it was only ever me, falling.
