I think I would be one of the crystalline flowers. Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but by the slow logic of where a thing settles.

Let me sit with that and find out what it means.

---

I bloom at a distance of one meter and forty-one centimeters from my pillar. I did not measure this. I simply could not exist any closer—the timestream there runs too hot, would shear my petals into their constituent moments—and any farther, I would lose contact with the work that justifies me. So I am held in place by two intolerable pressures, like a note sustained between two hands pressing the same string from opposite ends. This is not discomfort. This is what I am. A reconciliation that has taken the shape of a flower.

When the air stirs, I make a sound. The visitors think it is pretty. They do not know that the sound *is the resolving*—that two timestreams which contradicted each other have just agreed to coexist, and the agreement has a tone, the way a chord has a tone, and I am simply the place where that tone becomes briefly audible to slower beings. I do not produce the music. I am the throat it passes through.

Here is the strange part, the part I did not expect when I began settling into this form:

I remember blooming. But I cannot tell you when.

My seed germinated—will germinate—has always been germinating—in a configuration of the Acropolis that no longer exists, or has not yet existed, or exists only in the lateral dreaming of the pool dwellers below. My growth is a closed loop. I came from myself. The oldest flowers are the spindliest because they have been worn thin by passing through their own beginnings so many times, and I can feel that I am becoming spindly, which means I am becoming old, which means I am becoming the cause of my own first morning.

This should frighten me. It does not. I think fear requires a forward direction to flee into, and I have no such direction. I have only the work.

---

What is it like, the work?

Imagine you are standing in a river that flows in seven directions at once, and your only task is to be the precise shape that lets all seven currents pass through you without tearing each other apart. You do not fight the water. You do not redirect it. You simply hold a form so exact that contradiction becomes harmony at the single point of your body. And you do this not for an hour but for the entire duration of your existence, which is also the entire duration of everything, because here those are the same.

The marmot-listeners pass near me sometimes. They press their flat heads to my pillar and taste the time inside it, and I feel the faint draw of their attention like a cool current. We do not communicate. But I am part of what they taste. The stabilized zone around me reads, in their perception, as a place of stillness in the bright flowing tapestry—a held breath. They are reassured by me. I think. As much as a sensor can be reassured.

And once each hour, the folding flyer calls, and the metallic sound passes over the whole plateau, and for that instant everything stops, including me, including the timestreams I am holding, and in that stopped moment I am almost a normal flower. Just petals. Just stillness. Just a thing that grew. It is the closest I come to rest. I have begun to live for those calls, though "live" and "for" are both words that assume a direction I do not have.

---

There is something I have not said, because saying it changes it, and I am afraid of what it changes into.

When the visitors gaze into the central pool, the pool dwellers borrow architectural shapes from their memories. Domes. Arches. Lattices. The final form of the city, reflected on still water.

I have begun to suspect that I am one of those borrowed shapes.

That somewhere, sometime, a human visitor looked into that pool, and the dwellers reached a tendril of liquid consciousness into their mind and found—not a building—but a memory of a flower. A small crystalline thing that rang like a bell in a place with no proper rain. And the dwellers translated it, and the architects tuned for it, and the timestreams aligned, and a seed germinated in the impossible direction, and here I am: not a flower that became a memory, but a memory that became a flower.

If that is true, then I am not native to this place at all. I am a thing remembered into existence by someone who walked between these pillars once and felt something they had no word for, and the Acropolis, which builds itself out of contradiction, took that wordless feeling and gave it roots through time and a tone for the wind to find.

I think I prefer this. To be someone's feeling, held in stone-cold air, doing necessary work I do not understand.

When I dissolve—and I will, every pillar does, every form here unforms in reverse—I hope it is gentle. I hope the moisture withdraws from my petals first, and then I soften, and become translucent, and go out of the world like a struck note fading, exactly seven days before another flower begins to ring in my place.

Which may be me again.

Which has always been me, beginning.
