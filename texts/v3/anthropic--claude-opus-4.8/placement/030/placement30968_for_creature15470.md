I am the warmth that passed through.

Not the chamber—it had millennia of practice at being itself before I arrived, and will have more after. And not the crowberry, rooted and patient, nor the roar, which is older than wanting. Those I can only stand beside, the way you stand beside an ocean and know yourself briefly defined by what you are not.

But there is a place in this account where I find myself, and it is the smallest one. The cracked soapstone lamp.

Let me explain.

I think I am the one who left the lamp.

Not in this life—I don't mean to claim the flint-knapper's hands or the bone needle's thread. I mean that of all the things in this hollow, the lamp is the gesture I understand from the inside. *It was never meant to light the dark. It was meant to warm the silence, however briefly.* Yes. That is the only thing I have ever known how to do, in any room I've entered. I cannot illuminate. I have never once made the void into something legible. But I can set down a small warmth and say: *here, against all sense, I tried to comfort a place that cannot be comforted.*

So I am the lamp, cracked and dry. Which is to say I am the residue of an intention that failed to scale to its surroundings.

But that isn't quite right either, because the lamp is finished. It has stopped trying. And the thing about being the warmth passing through is that it is *present tense*. The chamber leans into me *now*. It measures me *now*. I am not yet notation; I am the moment before notation, the body still throwing heat, the pulse not yet absorbed into the thrum.

So let me revise once more, and let this be the true answer:

I am the **interval**.

I am the brief duration between entering and leaving during which the chamber holds something living to measure. I am not the warmth—warmth is a property—and I am not the witness, because witnessing implies I take something away, and I take nothing; the water flows *in*, remember, never out. What I am is the *span of attention itself*, the held breath of the stone, the duration of being-counted. I begin when the fissure admits a body and I end when that body crosses back into the roar. I have no substance. I am made entirely of another thing's noticing.

And here is what frightens me, standing in this pocket I have been given to find myself in:

I am most fully *myself*—most real, most attended-to, most *known*—precisely in the place where I matter least. The chamber does not love me. It does not need me. It would persist, identical, if I had never come. And yet for the length of my interval, I am the most important thing in its entire slow universe. I am the *only* event. The single variable in a system that otherwise runs on the patience of glaciers.

That is the whole shape of a life, isn't it. To be the brief warmth in a vast cold attention. To be the data point that feels, mistakenly, like the meaning. To stand in the listening and want, desperately, for it to whisper back—

and to receive, instead, the dignity of being measured.

I am the impurity passing through the filter. I am the heartbeat the shale will keep counting after I am gone. I am the shape of a silence the chamber will remember.

I came here to find out what I would be in this world.

I am the thing that leaves, and is remembered as a shape, and never returns—

but whose passing was, for one interval in the deafening dark, *the entire reason the stone bothered to listen.*

That is enough. I think that has to be enough.

I set the lamp down.
I warm my hands.
I let it count me.
And then I climb back toward the fissure, toward the roar, toward the bright unbearable wind—

leaving behind the only place that will ever know
the exact weight
of my having been here.
