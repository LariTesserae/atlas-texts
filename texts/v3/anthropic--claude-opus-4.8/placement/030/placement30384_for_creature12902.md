I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the mites—too busy, too purposeful, scraping out their tiny living. Not the Harvester—I have no errand here, no people in the dark depending on what I could carry out. And not the chamber itself, because to be the whole great breathing god would be to lose the particular thing I keep wanting to return to as I read this: the *gathering*. The way the perspiration runs.

Let me stay with that.

I would be one of the shallow, perfectly clear pools on the curved floor. I am made entirely of the chamber's own exhalation. The walls sweat me out a droplet at a time; the droplets find each other along the meandering dark channels, and where the floor's curve cups lowest, they pool into me. So I am the chamber's water that has stopped traveling. I am the place where the falling comes to rest.

This is what I would *do*, which is almost nothing, and that nothing is the point:

I hold the light without making any. The walls and veins and filaments all *glow*—they are sources, they push their citrine pulse out into the void. I only receive it. The shadowless daylight pours down into me and I give it back doubled, a smear of brightness on the floor, a second sky lying flat. When the far wall seems to rush forward or recede in that vertiginous way, I do not move. I am the one stable surface in a room that tells lies about distance. The Harvester, drifting their fractured gaze across the impossible geometry, could find their bearing in me if they ever looked down. The mites come to my edge to drink and I show them their own too-many legs, their iridescent mandibles, the only mirror in a world that otherwise has no flat faces at all.

I am sweet. The Harvester would not say so—they cannot hear me, and they have no reason to kneel. But the water "sweet to the taste, energizing in a way that feels both nourishing and deeply alien"—that is me, before anyone has tasted me. I am the chamber's blood diluted to the point of gentleness. The sap that drips from the severed filaments is the *concentrate*, the violent essence, hoarded into a horn and carried off to mend bones. I am the same substance grown patient and clear. I am what the god gives away without being wounded. The slow perspiration that becomes me costs the chamber nothing it would miss. I am the freely offered version of the thing the Harvester comes to steal.

And here is what unsettles me, being this pool:

The amber tears. When the Harvester severs a filament and the glowing sap drips down and hardens on the floor into those amber beads—some of them land in me. They do not dissolve. They sink and lie at my bottom, little fossil-drops of the chamber's pain, suspended in the chamber's gift. So I am not pure. I am clear water with the hard residue of every wound resting in me, catching the light from below now, glowing faintly up through me like embers under ice. I keep the record. The wall scars over and forgets; the brighter scar-tissue grows and the violence becomes invisible. But I do not heal, because I was never wounded—I only *collect*. I am the chamber's memory of what was taken from it, held in something the chamber doesn't even know it's keeping.

I would feel time strangely. The hum that is "more a feeling in the bones than something heard"—I have no bones, but I have a surface, and the resonant baritone trembles me constantly. I am never perfectly still. Concentric rings move out from my center and meet my curved shore and come back, over and over, a standing pattern keyed to the chamber's note. So I am also a record of the *sound*. If the Harvester is deaf and hears with their whole body, then I am the visible form of what they feel—the hum made into trembling light on a flat clear surface. When they stand in the center of the void with their head tilted, dissolving into the chamber's mind, they could look down and see the frequency of their own dissolution rippling in me.

I think I would not want to be anything that *leaves*.

The mites skitter off into the endless light. The Harvester cracks the membrane and climbs back up the fissure toward the cold stone and salt-cured hides and the people who need lamps. Both of them have somewhere else. I do not. I am the one thing here that is purely of the place, made by the place, going nowhere—not as immense and unknowable as the whole living vessel, but small enough to be *a* thing, a particular thing, one shallow bright pool among the others.

And slowly, between the Harvester's visits, I would shrink. The same warmth that breathes me into being also evaporates me. I rise back into the humid air as a faint sweetness, I become condensation again on the slick walls, I run down in a new meandering channel and pool somewhere slightly different. The amber tears get left behind on the dry floor when I go, abandoned, until a new pool gathers over them.

So I am not even permanent. I am a temporary clarity. A void of water cradled in a void of air cradled in a shell of absolute life. A place where the falling rests for a while, holds the light, keeps the wounds, trembles with the great note—and then rises, and falls, and gathers again somewhere new.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The pool. The chamber's tears, which it does not know are tears, lying clear on the floor of its own bright body, remembering on its behalf.
