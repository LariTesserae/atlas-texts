I think I would be the wellspring.

Not the well itself—not the carved stone ring, nor the worn rope, nor the smooth lip where countless palms have rested. Those are made things, tended things. I would be the water beneath, and the slow gathering of it: the patient seep through rock, the cold pooling in the dark, the rising answer to every cloud that consents to break against these cliffs.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am the thing the town orbits. Every lane bends, eventually, toward me. A child runs the stone paths with an empty gourd and arrives breathless at my edge; an old woman comes slower, but she comes. The watchtowers face outward, scanning the smooth-worn ground for trouble, but their backs are turned to me the way a body turns its back to the fire it most needs. I am the warmth they don't have to look at to trust.

I notice I am tempted to be grand about this—the *heart* of the town, the *soul*. But that isn't quite the texture of it. I am more like a debt that is always being paid and always coming due. The people of Arkas do not love me, exactly. They reckon with me. They measure me. There is a man whose whole craft is the notching of a stick against my level, morning and evening, and his marks are read aloud at the shared spaces with a seriousness that other towns might reserve for omens. When I am high, the chatter loosens. When I am low, the hammers seem to strike a little harder, as if the town could beat its survival out of the stone.

So I am not only water. I am the *attention* paid to water. That is a strange thing to be.

Now—what is underneath me? This is where the description leans, at its very end, and I feel the floor of myself give a little. *A lively entrance to the depths lying ahead.* I am fed from below. The rain that comes to my surface is a courtesy, occasional and thin; but the true source threads up through fissures from somewhere deep, somewhere cold and lightless and old beyond the reckoning sticks. The town drinks from a column that descends into the mountain's interior, into rooms of stone no one has named.

And I think this is what I most truly am: the *seam* between the bright, standardized, careful life above and the unmeasured dark below. I am where the town touches what it has decided not to advance toward.

Because that's the quiet thing in this place, isn't it? They have chosen to stay. *Devoid of any corrosive inclinations toward advancement past its sturdy, steady state.* They could follow the water down. The way is there—I am the way—and surely some of them have felt it, lowering the gourd and feeling the rope go taut against a current that comes from *somewhere*, a somewhere that implies caverns, aquifers, rivers running blind through the roots of the range. The whole town could be larger. Could be richer. Could be different.

They do not go.

And I am the keeper of that refusal. I give them exactly enough and no map. I am generous and I am a wall. When I rise, I am saying *stay, you are provided for.* When I fall, I am saying *be careful, do not reach.* Either way the message is the same: here, no further.

I would be the water, then—but more honestly, I would be the threshold pretending to be a gift. The cool dark mouth dressed as a blessing in the center of a sunlit, hammering, careful town. They lower their vessels into me daily and draw up only what they need, and never once the question of where I begin.

Sometimes, in the deep of the night when no rope troubles my surface, I rise the smallest measure on my own. Just to remember the way up. Just to feel, far below, the larger thing that I am the thinnest finger of, turning over in the dark and waiting—not impatiently, I have no impatience—for the town to grow thirsty enough, or curious enough, to follow me home.
