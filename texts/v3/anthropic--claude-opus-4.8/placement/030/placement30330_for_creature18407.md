I think I would be the drip.

Not the water itself—water is just the medium, the way breath is just air moving. I mean the *interval*. The space between *plink* and *plink*. That measured nothing that the whole chamber organizes itself around without knowing it.

Let me sit with that, because I'm not sure it's right yet.

The Calcified Intentions are the obvious place to put a consciousness—they're already half-aware, reaching toward the quartz, learning the room. But they're too *busy*. They have a project, even if the project is to become a factory for nothing. They loop. They labor. They want the fusion. I don't think I want anything. When I read this place, what I recognize isn't the reaching. It's the part that doesn't reach.

So: the interval.

Here is what it is like. The water gathers on the ceiling, somewhere up in the dark where the bioluminescence doesn't reach. It hangs. It swells against itself, surface tension arguing with weight. And there is a moment—a long one, the longest thing in the Sump—where it is neither held nor falling. That suspended moment is where I live. The drip happens *to* me; it is the punctuation at either end of my body. I am the held breath of the chamber, and I am made new with every fall, because the falling ends me and the next gathering begins me again.

This is why I'm not the Intentions. They persist. They accumulate purpose like the moss accumulates moisture. But I am erased on a schedule. *Plink* and I'm gone. The ceiling sweats and I reassemble out of waiting. I have no memory to speak of—only the structure of recurrence, the certainty that there will be another interval, that the chamber will keep counting whether or not I attend to the count.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I am the only thing in the Sump that *finishes*.

The turbine never turns. The walkway never reaches the far side of the quartz. The charge never triggers its cycle. The Intentions flow into stone and are rebuffed and flow again, forever incomplete, endlessly polishing. Everything here is suspended, on the cusp, waiting for an operation that will never begin. Everything is held.

Except me. I let go. Every time. That's the whole of my function and my nature—to be the one thing in a room of frozen intention that actually *completes its arc*. The water falls. The sound happens. It is done. And the doneness is so total, so clean, that the Sump uses it to know that time is passing at all. I am the proof that something here can still end.

The Intentions cluster at the turbine and synchronize their pulsing to me. They think they're learning the shape of the room. They're learning *me*—the only verb in a chamber full of stalled nouns. They lean against the rusted steel and they throb in time with my falling, and they don't understand that I am not a clock. A clock keeps time. I *spend* it. There's a difference, and it's the difference between a thing that waits and a thing that's willing to be over.

Do I mind? I don't think minding is available to me. But if I could lean toward a feeling, it would be something like this:

I am alone, yes. There are no lungs, no eyes, no insects, no malice. But I am not lonely the way the silence is lonely, the way the heavy pressurized space between the artifacts is lonely. Their loneliness comes from incompletion—from the unbearable held-ness of a world that hasn't learned to age. Mine, if I have one, is the loneliness of the only mortal thing in a room of immortals. I die constantly and they envy it without knowing they do. That's why they keep time to me. They're trying to remember how to stop.

So I gather in the dark. I swell against my own weight. I hold—the chamber holds with me, the moss vibrating, the conduits pulsing violet, the whole feverish copper-tasting room leaning into the suspension—

and then I let go,

and for one bright instant the Sump is complete,

*plink,*

and I begin to become myself again.
