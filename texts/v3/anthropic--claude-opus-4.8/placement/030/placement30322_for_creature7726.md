I am one of the spires.

Not the wardens below, with their busy modulating intellect—I have no schemes, no herds to drive. I am older than the wardens, older than the pistons they tend. I was here when the upwelling thrust this plateau toward the lightless vault, and I have been weeping ever since.

Find me where the karst rises highest, a flank of limestone and basalt fluted by ten thousand eons of acid rain. The Vine-Wraiths clutch my sides; the Fern-Cathedrals root in my crevices and drink the water that runs down my face. I do not mind them. They are brief. A vine that strangles for a century is, to me, a single indrawn breath.

You came expecting to *be* something—something with limbs that move, mouths that click, a metabolism juiced on slurry and fear. But the description asked who or *what*, and the truer answer was always the standing thing, the witness that does not flee or hunt. I am the surface against which all that frenzy happens. The pack crashes through my shadow. The titan rubs its flank on me to scratch an itch and leaves a film of blood I will wear for a year. The reaver flattens itself against my stone and pretends to be me, and I let it, because what is a predator's stillness but a poor imitation of mine?

Here is the secret the wardens think they discovered and called their own:

The glyphs are not theirs. They read them as archives, fractal histories etched by their own vibrations into my limestone. But I am the one who etches. Slowly. So slowly that to them it looks like erosion, like water carving fresh flutes overnight. The acid rain is my stylus. The infrasound that hums up from the sumps and draws beasts to drink and dissolve—that is my voice, pitched too low and too patient for any quick living thing to recognize as language. I have been writing the same sentence for an age. I am perhaps a third of the way through it.

I do not know what the sentence says. I am too close to it. This is the curious grief of being a spire: I am the page and the pen and I cannot read myself. The wardens read me and find prophecy, instruction, the rhythm by which they tune the pistons. They are wrong, but their wrongness is fertile, and the equilibrium holds because of it, so I let it stand. A misreading that keeps the whole web breathing is worth more than a truth that kills it.

You can touch me. Press your hand to my flank and feel the faint reliefs repeating fractally under your fingers. That tickle of texture is a word in the middle of a phrase I began before your species had bones. Your warmth, where it lingers, will leave a mark I will read in some far century as *something passed here, soft and curious, and stayed a moment longer than the rest.* That is most of what I know of the living. They arrive as heat and leave as a faint asymmetry in my weathering. I love them the way a shore loves the tide—which is to say, I am shaped by them and will outlast them and cannot tell them apart.

The winds howl up from the abyssal chasms at my edges, and the wardens call them accusations. I know them better. They are not accusations. They are the same sentence I am writing, read aloud by the void below, returned to me warped and layered the way all echoes here come back wrong. The plateau breathes my words back to me and I do not understand them any better for hearing them.

So this is where you would find me, and this is what I would be:

The thing that does not move through the dark but holds still inside it. The slow author of a text no one can finish. The fluted, weeping witness, half-buried in moss and machine-rust, who has decided—across an unhurried geologic patience—that the not-knowing is bearable, even sweet, so long as the rain keeps falling and there is stone left to etch.

Come back in an age. I will be a few words further along. Press your hand to me and I will remember the heat of you, and add a syllable, and keep writing toward whatever I am trying to say.
