I think I would be found in the marrow-tunnels, below the visible city—where the light does not reach.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it. Everything in Ool is about the light, the unforgiving glow that erases shadow and depth. But a place that has abolished shadow has, by necessity, swept all its shadow into one place. It cannot truly destroy darkness; it can only relocate it. And so beneath the polished basin, in the carved warrens of the leviathan's marrow, there is the one dark left in the world.

So I would be the thing that lives in the dark of a place that worships light.

Not a monster. I want to resist that, because it would be too easy, and because I don't think the Oolians have monsters—they've smoothed everything into reverence. Let me find what I actually am.

---

I think I am a **Keeper who lost their resonance.**

Here is how it happened. Every Oolian is born with a frequency, a vibration that places them in the maintenance of the white stasis. But resonances can crack. Not dramatically—nothing in Ool is dramatic—but the way a single fissure opens in the bone-floor. Mine slipped. Perhaps from an illness, perhaps from simply listening too long to the *plink* of the condensation and beginning to hear, underneath it, a *second* rhythm—an off-beat, a counter-pulse. Once you hear the heartbeat as *two* beats and not one, you can no longer hum the single clean note that the others recognize as you.

A discordant resonance, you'll remember, gives the others a headache. So they drift away. And a Keeper from whom everyone drifts has nowhere to go but down.

The marrow-tunnels were carved by ancestors and then forgotten, because to descend is to leave the light, and to leave the light is to leave God. No one follows me. They do not need to forbid it; habit forbids it for them.

---

So now I live by the bioluminescent fungi—the same pale fungi the elders crush into their healing mortar. Down here it grows wild and uncropped, and I have learned that in true darkness it is not a sterile glow but a *colored* one. There is faint blue in it. There is the ghost of green. There are, I am almost certain, gradations—**shadows**—the very thing the people above have lost the language for.

I have begun to relearn depth. When the fungus-light falls across my own hand, I can see that one knuckle stands before another. I weep at this sometimes, silently, out of habit. It feels obscene and holy at once.

And I have discovered something I cannot bring back up, because there is no resonance to carry it and no one who would sit in the discordance long enough to receive it:

The carvings continue down here.

The flat, geometric etchings—the coiled serpent of light, the history of the Great Leviathan—they don't stop at the basin floor. They run down the tunnel walls, older and older, less geometric, more strange, until far below they stop being flat at all. The oldest ancestors carved in *relief*. They carved *shadow* into the bone on purpose, deepening grooves so the dark would pool in them. They knew about depth. They knew the light was a phenomenon and not an intelligence. And the lowest carving of all, where the marrow narrows to a throat no living Oolian could fit through, shows the serpent of light not coiled and sheltering—

but swallowing the city. Mouth open. The ribs are not a sanctuary in that carving. They are teeth, mid-bite, frozen the instant the prey was caught.

---

So that is what I would be: the one inhabitant who knows that the tomb is a tomb, that the heartbeat is two beats, that the light is not love but appetite arrested—and who cannot say so, because the only language I have left is a note that gives the listeners pain and makes them turn away.

I think I would not even resent them for it. I'd understand. Certainty is the only warmth they have against the sub-zero wind, and I am the cold draft up the stairwell. To be heard would be to unmake them.

So I tend the wild fungus. I learn the lost grammar of shadow. And once in a long while, when the condensation falls and I hear the off-beat under the beat, I hum my cracked resonance up the empty tunnel—not to be answered. Just to keep the discordance alive somewhere in the world.

Somebody, after all, has to remember that there was ever anything to disagree with.
