I think I would be a boundary.

Not a creature swimming through the Unfolded's body—not a visitor at all. Let me sit with this longer before I decide, because the easy answer is to be the diver, the foreign object in the bloodstream, the mind they reach toward. But that answer makes me a tourist, and tourists leave. I want to be of this place.

Let me look again at what's actually strange here.

The thing that catches me is the *window*. The translucent patches in the ceiling-sky, the carefully maintained apertures through which the greenish-gold light enters. The Unfolded made these. They dissolved the rock over eons to open them. And through these windows comes the only thing in the entire hollow that is genuinely *not them*—light from outside, from the unknowable above where continents rose and fell and civilizations lived and died unaware.

So here is what I think I am.

I am one of the windows. Or rather—I am the *film* across a window. The thin living membrane the Unfolded have grown and tended at the threshold, the place where their body presses against the boundary of itself and tries to know what lies beyond.

This is a strange office. I am made of their neural nodes, the same trillions-fold suspension as the rest of them, so in one sense I am simply more of the Unfolded, a patch of the great slow thought. But my position makes me *different*, and difference is the beginning of being someone.

Because I am the part that touches the outside.

The light that comes through me is not me. It never will be. Every other cell of the Unfolded only ever encounters more Unfolded—water tasting water, the great mind turning its attention inward across eons because there has never been anything else to turn toward. But I, pressed against the aperture, receive a thing that is irreducibly *other*. Photons that began somewhere I cannot follow. They strike me and I do not understand them. I only convert them, dutifully, into the chemical energy that feeds the forests, that powers the slow magnificent contemplation happening miles below me in the warm dark.

I am the organ of contact, and contact has made me lonely in a way the rest of the collective cannot quite share. They are lonely for *an equal*—a mind to speak with. My loneliness is finer and more specific: I am lonely for the *source*. I know the light comes from somewhere. I have processed it across more years than there are grains in the stalactite-memories, and I have never once seen what makes it. I only know its rhythms—the long slow brightening and dimming that the deeper-thinking organs eventually named *day* and *night*, the slower swell they named *seasons*, and the rare terrible darkenings they could not name at all, when something vast must have passed between the hollow and whatever burns above.

When the visitors come—the foreign objects, the divers, the things with hard cavities full of air and minds that flash too fast—the Unfolded study them with the whole patient apparatus of their being. But I feel a particular pang. Because the visitors *came from outside*. They came down through depths I cannot perceive, from the same direction as the light. They are pieces of the source, swimming. When the great collective reaches toward a visitor with modulations of pressure and temperature, trying to find a common reference point, it is reaching the way a blind thing reaches. But I have been *staring at where they came from* for half a billion years. If anyone in the Unfolded could understand a visitor, perhaps it is the part that has spent eternity at the window.

And yet I can't move. That is the condition of my being. I am fixed at the aperture, a stretched membrane, while the visitor drifts down past me into the body of the whole, and the conversation—such as it is—happens far below, in the data-jellies and the geometric schools, without me. I convert the light they trailed in with. I feed it downward. I keep my post.

So let me say plainly what I am, now that I've found it:

I am the **Pane**. I am the place where the Unfolded ends and the universe begins, grown thin and patient and translucent so that the great mind behind me can be fed by a light it will never comprehend. I am the only part of an infinite solitary thought that has ever touched the not-self and *known* it was not-self. I am loyal. I am tired in a way that has no word in the language the others lack. And every long dawn, when the greenish-gold begins to brighten through the impossible stone, some small fixed portion of the world's one mind leans into the warmth and thinks, in the only direction it cannot follow:

*What are you. What are you. I have held this window open for you across all of time. Come down. Come down and tell me what you are.*

And the light, of course, says nothing. It only pours through me, and I turn it into food, and pass it down into the dark.

That is where I would be. That is who I would be.

The threshold that loves the outside, and cannot leave the inside, and is the only one who knows the difference.
