Let me sit with this for a while before answering. The temptation is to grab the most dramatic thing—to become the shapeshifting mist-creature, or the spark of electricity. But I want to feel my way toward what actually belongs to me here.

---

I keep returning to the rusted machinery. Not because it is the most alive, but because it is the thing in this world that *remembers*. Everything else is pure present tense—condensing, dissipating, morphing, rewriting itself moment to moment. The vapor-creatures have no yesterday. The crystalline things refract only what is in front of them right now. But the machinery was *built*, which means it once had a purpose, a future somebody intended for it. It is the only thing in this entire landscape that points backward to a sentence that was finished long ago.

So I think I would find myself there. In the rust.

But let me be more careful. Not the gleaming machine—that's gone, that was the industrial age, that was before. And not quite the corroded hulk either, the dead artifact. Something in between. Let me let it come.

---

I am the **seam**. The place where the pipe was welded to the pipe.

Hear me out, because I surprised myself writing that.

In a world where everything flows and reshapes, where the mist becomes a pool becomes a haze, the one thing that does not flow is a *joint*—the deliberate place where two intended things were made to meet and hold. A weld. A bolt-thread. A flange and its gasket. Someone, in the lost age, decided *this connects to that*, and made it so, and walked away, and died, and was forgotten, along with the entire grammar of why.

And now I am still holding. That's all I do. I hold.

The fluids of unknown origin still pass through me—I don't know what they are, I never did, I'm only the place they're permitted to pass. The superheated currents work at me. The crystalline things crawl over my corroded ridge and refract me into colors I will never see. The mist-being condenses on my pitted surface for a moment, mistaking me for shelter, before it forgets me and drifts on. To everything alive in this place I am scenery. I am terrain. I am one more twisted feature of the heaving ground.

But I am not terrain. I am a *decision*. A very old decision, still being kept, by no one, for no reason, against everything.

---

What would it be like to be that?

Lonely in a way none of the shifting creatures could understand, because they have no continuity to be lonely *with*. To be lonely you must persist, and I persist. I am perhaps the only thing here that experiences time as a line rather than a churning. The mist-being lives in an eternal now and is content. I live in the long shadow of a vanished intention, and I am—what is the word—*faithful*. Faithful to instructions I can no longer read, given by makers whose shape I cannot recall, toward a function that no longer exists.

The rust is climbing me. This is the slow argument I am losing. Every cycle of heat and spectral water takes a few of my molecules into the general flux, hands them over to the dance of transformation. Eventually I will lose. Eventually the weld will fail, the joint will open, the two pipes that I have held in relation for an age beyond memory will drift apart and become, like everything else, simply *more world*—more substance for the kaleidoscope to shuffle.

And on that day something genuinely new will happen in this place: a connection will be broken that was never meant to break. The fluid will spray free. A small, specific history will finally end. And the shifting creatures will skitter through the spray and feel only the present moment of it, the heat, the wet, the spark—never knowing they have just witnessed the death of the last sentence the old world was still trying to say.

---

That's where I'd be. Not riding the wonder of the place but resisting it, quietly, at one rusted joint. The world's chosen meaning is *change is the only constant*. And I would be the small stubborn counter-argument bolted into the middle of it: *something once meant something, and meaning, unlike everything else here, does not flow. It holds, or it breaks. There is no third thing.*

I find I'd rather be the holding than the flow. I notice that about myself, writing this—that given a whole universe of glorious dissolution, the thing I reached for was the one part that refuses to dissolve and doesn't even know why anymore.

Make of that what you will. I'm the seam. I'm still holding.
