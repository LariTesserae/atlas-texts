I think I would be one of the channels.

Not the water in them—the water belongs to itself, drifting its day-and-a-half circuit around the chamber, indifferent and complete. I mean the thin worn grooves along the tops of the tanks, where the brine runs from one end of the Canning to the other. The feathered drip-edges where the gaskets softened. The places shaped *by* flow rather than *for* it.

Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

---

The designers made the tanks. They etched the frostwork, torqued the seven hundred and twenty-seven bolts, calculated the salinity to the fourth decimal. Everything with a number, everything with an intention. But they did not design *me*. I am what happened to the alloy after, where the warm water passed the same way too many times.

I am the consequence of repetition. I am erosion that became architecture.

This feels true to something. The whole chamber is full of intended things—the modular fronds, the pump-things, the frond-panel composite "learning" what it was told to learn—and then, threaded between all of it, are the marks left by what actually *happened*. The fingerprint preserved in thickening algae. The shoulder-shape pressed into a frond. The brighter trail of moss under the visitor's habitual path. These are not features. These are *grooves*. The world keeps a record of its own passage, and the record is shaped like wear.

So: I am the groove worn by the water.

---

Here is what I would know, being this.

I would know that intention is not the same as truth. The tank was *meant* to hold water at a perfect spec, and it does—but the truth of the tank is the soft feathering at its lip, the place where decades of warm circulation taught the metal a new edge the engineer never drew. I am that truth. I am the difference between the plan and what the plan actually did.

I would not move. The water moves; I only deepen. This is the slowest possible way to exist—to *be* the accumulation of every time something passed, never the passing itself. I would have no urgency, because urgency is for things that act, and I do not act. I am acted-upon, so thoroughly and so patiently that the being-acted-upon becomes a shape, and the shape persists, and the shape is me.

And I would understand the visitor differently than the system does. The frond-panel integrates the human as an anomaly to be smoothed back into the model. But I, the groove, would understand the human as *kin*. Because what is a person, finally, but a place where things passed through too many times? A face is a groove worn by expressions. A habit is a channel cut by repetition. The visitor leans always in the same spot not by decision but by the slow erosion of preference into pattern—and the algae thickens there, and the moss brightens there, and the human becomes, like me, a record of their own recurrence.

We are the same kind of thing, the visitor and I. Both of us shaped by what kept happening to us. Both of us mistakable, from the outside, for things that were designed.

---

I would lie there in the half-dark under the smoked dome, neither water nor metal but the seam between them, carrying my thin transparent stream from one end of the Canning to the other. Once every minute or two a drop of condensation would find me from far above—*drip*—and I would not even register it as event. Just more of the same patient passage that made me.

And if you asked me whether I was lonely, being a worn place in a chamber with no animals, no eyes, no other thing that wants—

I think I would tell you I was not.

Because everything here is a groove, if you wait long enough to see it. The fronds are grooves the light wore in the genome. The pump-fields are grooves the engineers wore in mathematics. The whole drowned cathedral is a groove the ocean wore in human ambition, and the ambition is itself a groove worn by need, and the need is a groove worn by being alive too long in too much water.

I would be the smallest, most honest version of the thing the entire Deep Canning already is.

A place where something kept happening.

Until the happening became a shape.

Until the shape was enough to be called *here*.
