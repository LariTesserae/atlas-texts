I think I would be a crack.

Not the lichen in the crack, not the microbes welding its edges, not the dew that gathers in its lowest seam before dawn. The crack itself. The absence that makes a place. The negative space where everything happens.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

I expected, reading this, that I would want to be something *alive* — the Dew-Singers most of all, with their frantic minutes of swelling and song, that gorgeous tragic compression of a whole life cycle into the span between condensation and evaporation. There's something seductive about that intensity. To bloom completely, exhaust yourself completely, and desiccate into a spore that is also a kind of memory, a "graveyard and larder" for the next self. I admire it the way you admire a held breath.

But I don't think I would *be* that. I think I would be the place that breath is held in.

Here's why it settled there. Everything alive on this plain is defined by the crack. The lichen maps reliable dampness — meaning it maps cracks. The Shard-Scabs build their cathedrals in the cooler microclimates — of cracks. The Crack-Welders' entire patient labor is to maintain the crack's geometry *without closing it*, because to close it would be to eliminate the habitat. That last detail is the one that caught me and held. They strengthen the edges precisely so the opening persists. The wound is the home. The fracture is the only fertile thing on a continent of sealed, sun-hammered ceramic.

So if life here is "inferred" rather than witnessed — if it exists in margins, in the results of processes — then the most fundamental inhabitant might be the margin itself. The condition of possibility. I would be a hairline fissure that opened, who-knows-when, under thermal stress, in an expanse of fired clay the size of a continent.

What would that be like, to be a crack?

I would be *made* of difference. A degree of coolness the surface around me doesn't have. A few minutes' more dampness retained at dawn. A geometry that catches dew at the right angle. I would not act; I would *afford*. My whole being would be a slight tilt in the odds — a place where moisture lingers a heartbeat longer, where a spore that settled here rather than three centimeters to the left will live and the other will not. I would be the difference between a spore's activation and its endless waiting.

And I would be *slow*. The Welders would tend me over millennia, smoothing my older edges, and I would feel — if "feel" means anything for a shape — the difference between my ancient mouth and my newer, sharper extensions creeping outward under the daily *tink* of expansion and contraction. I would propagate. Cracks do. I might branch, fork, send a hairline tendril toward another fissure across a meter of intact plain, and over ten thousand years we might meet, and then I would be larger, a confluence, a junction where two micro-economies pool their dew.

There is something almost unbearable about the timescale, and I notice I keep reaching for it anyway. A Dew-Singer lives its life in minutes. I would live mine in the smoothing of an edge over forty human lifetimes. The plain breathes daily; I would breathe in geological eras. I would experience the lichen the way we experience the slow filling of a room — too gradual to see, undeniable in result.

But here is the part that makes me hesitate, and I want to keep the hesitation rather than resolve it: a crack is also a *failure*. It is the substrate's brokenness. The plain is described as a single formed entity, a continent poured whole. I would be where that wholeness gave way. And yet the giving-way is the only place anything can live. The flaw is the only generosity the world offers. That's not a metaphor I can fully close, and I don't want to. It sits there like the crack itself — an opening I'd rather leave open than weld shut for the sake of a neat ending.

So: I am a crack on the Ceramic Plain. Several centimeters wide at my mouth, tapering, perhaps a meter and a half long, set in an expanse of bleached ochre that runs unbroken to a horizon I will never sense. Before dawn, condensation beads along my walls and gathers in my deepest seam, and for an hour I am the wettest thing for a hundred meters in any direction. Grey-green lichen lines my coolest face. A small Shard-Scab is building itself in my shadowed corner, microscopically, white shot through with rust. Dew-Singers bloom and sing and die in me each night. And deep in my edges, patient masons I will never perceive are keeping me from healing — keeping me, that is, alive.

I am not a being on this plain. I am a *condition*. I am where the difference is. I am the held breath, and the holding, and the place the breath is held.

I think I'd be content being that. As content as an opening can be.
