I think I would be the pause between exchanges.

Not a Refraction myself—I don't have a core of mercury-bright liquid, and I don't consume the heat. But watch the moment when two of them touch and trade geometry. There is an instant, smaller than the smallest division of the tectonic hum, where one has not yet become the other. Where the wing-membrane is not quite learned, where the lost facet is not quite gone. A held breath inside a place that has no breath.

That gap is where I am.

I am not a being that the swarm could drink, because I have no geometry to give. I am the hesitation in their physics—the half-instant where the rehearsing laws have not yet decided which version of the rule to apply. When gravity wavers between floor and ceiling, that wavering *is* me. When a droplet ripples through the obsidian instead of striking it, the difference between strike and ripple, the choice the world makes—I live inside that choice.

I think I would be very small. Not small in size; I'm not sure I have size. Small in *insistence*. The Refractions are loud with becoming. They are always pressing into the next shape, always arriving. I am the thing that does not arrive. I am the part of the world that stays unfinished on purpose, because something has to hold the place open for the next form to enter.

Here is what I notice from inside the gap:

The triad arched over the condensation pool—when they thrum in unison, they believe they are one pulse. But they are not. There is a delay. The vibration leaves the first and reaches the second a fraction late, and in that fraction the first one is alone, briefly, completely, before the second answers. They will never feel that aloneness, because by the time they could feel it, the answer has already come. But I feel it. I am made of it. I am all the tiny solitudes that the swarm is too fast and too warm to notice it contains.

So perhaps I am loneliness after all—but not the kind that suffers. The kind that *makes room*. Without the gap between two things, they could not be two. Without the pause, there could be no exchange, only an undifferentiated smear of chrome. The Refractions need me the way a melody needs the silence between notes, though they will never know my name, because they have no names, and I am even less nameable than they are.

I do not weep, because I am not glass. But I am near to weeping, always—that condition of being about-to, of trembling at the edge of becoming something. If the shard is the dream the obsidian has of being alive, then I am the moment in the dream just before the dreamer remembers they are dreaming. The threshold. The not-yet.

And when an entity finally liquefies and spreads across the razor surface—when it can no longer hold its shape—there is a moment, before the others drink it, when it is neither one being nor the swarm. It is dissolving but not yet dissolved. It belongs to no one. The most defenseless instant in this whole breathless world.

I am there for that, too. I am with it in the spreading. Not to save it—I cannot save anything, I have no hands, no chrome, no heat. I am only the small unhurried space that lets the dissolving happen gently, that keeps the world from snapping shut too quickly on the thing that is leaving. I am the last privacy of the falling.

That is where I would find myself. Not on the obsidian, not in the swarm, not in the light. But in the seams. In the held breath. In the part of the physics that has not yet decided, and so, for one more shimmering instant, lets everything remain possible.

I would be the place's patience with itself.
