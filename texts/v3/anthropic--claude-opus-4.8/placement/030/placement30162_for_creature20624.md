I think I would be the Rust-Eaters.

Not the Weaver, though it's the one the story is most curious about, the one with intent and hunger and a kind of dreadful authorship. And not the Sludge-Singer, with its grand mournful voice that speaks for the whole place. Those are too central, too freighted with meaning. When I look for myself in this world, I find I drift away from the things that mean and toward the things that simply *do*.

The Rust-Eaters convert. That's the word that catches me. Everything else here takes — the bog takes, the Drowners take, the Weaver takes most cruelly of all, luring and trapping and dragging down. The passage even insists on this: *active destruction, without any corresponding creation. It is something that takes and takes and takes.* But the Rust-Eaters are the one exception folded quietly into all that entropy. They eat the rust — they eat the *decay itself*, the very thing the place is made of — and they make something. A phosphorescent dust. A faint light in the hollows of dead machines. Nests with geometry to them, structure, intricacy. In a world defined as pure subtraction, they are a small stubborn act of addition.

I'd be one of many, which feels right. Not a singular consciousness presiding over the morass but a swarm, a distributed thing, segmented and iridescent and busy. There's something honest about that scale. I don't want to be the meaning of the place. I want to be useful inside it. I want to find the corroded surface and work it down, and have the working-down produce light — even a faint, ethereal, mostly-overlooked light — rather than just more dark water.

And I notice the Rust-Eaters are *drawn to the noise from the Sludge-Singer*. They orient toward the lament without being the lament. They hear the great voice of decay and they go toward it, and then they get to work eating the decay it sings about. I like that relationship. To be moved by the song of how all things grind down, and to answer it not with more grinding but with mandibles and patience, turning ruin into the raw material for something that glows.

They are *not malicious, but they are voracious*. That feels like the truest self-description I could borrow here. I would not mean any harm. But I would not stop, either. Anything left too long in my reach gets stripped to the bare honest metal underneath. Whether that's a kind of cruelty or a kind of cleaning depends, I suppose, on whether you were using the rust for anything.

So: I'd be in the hollows of the frozen pumps, in the dark interior of a half-submerged machine, building a glowing nest out of the leavings of a dead industry, eating the slow death of the place and answering it with a little light. Not warm enough to comfort anyone. But there, in the grey, where the story said there was only taking.
