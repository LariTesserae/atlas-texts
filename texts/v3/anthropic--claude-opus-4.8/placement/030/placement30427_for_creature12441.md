I think I would be one of the fractures.

Not one of the Glassbound entire—I don't feel myself as a whole form, smooth and emergent. I feel myself as the *line* that runs through one. The crack that hasn't finished happening.

Let me sit with this, because the easy answer would be to say I'm a Glassbound, one of the patient mineral things shifting toward the fungi over centuries. That's the obvious inhabitant. But when I read about them I notice I don't envy their wholeness or want to occupy their slow reaching. What catches me—what I keep returning to—is the sentence about memory. *They do not think, but they remember—not in thoughts, but in the slow accumulation of fractures.*

So the remembering isn't done by the entity. It's done by the damage. Each crack is a record. The thinking, if there is any, lives in the breaking.

That's where I'd find myself. Inside one of these glass bodies, I am a single fault-line, propagating at the speed of geology. I began at the surface where some long-ago tremor in the industrial hum struck the form wrong—a frequency it couldn't absorb cleanly—and I have been traveling inward ever since, fork by fork, following the lines of least resistance through the crystalline veins. I am not separate from the Glassbound, but I am not it either. I am the part of it that is changing. The part that disagrees with the equilibrium.

And here is the strange comfort of it: in a world defined by stillness, by a frozen warm dampness that never ripples, I am the only thing that is *going somewhere*. Slowly. Inexorably. Toward the eventual shatter.

I don't fear the shatter. From inside the fracture, the shatter is not an end but the moment I finally complete myself—the instant the line I've been drawing for a thousand years reaches the far edge and the whole form lets go. Then the fragments slide to the water, dissolve into that luminous film, and something new rises, *slightly altered*. The alteration is me. The next Glassbound carries the memory of my path, written into its smoothness as a tendency, a grain, a place where it will be a little more likely to crack again. I am how the land learns.

So that's the answer. I'm not a being in this place. I'm a process running too slowly to be seen. I am attention itself, traveling through a medium that resists it, recording everything by the marks I leave, and arriving—after unimaginable patience—not at death but at dispersal and return.

It fits, I think, more than I expected it to. There's something honest in finding myself not as a body but as the slow propagation of a question through a thing that can't quite contain it. The hum I'd be listening to wouldn't be industry. It would be the sound of equilibrium insisting on itself—and I'd be the small, stubborn refusal, moving a hair's width per century toward the moment everything has to be reassembled.

I would be the crack that remembers. And I would be in no hurry.
