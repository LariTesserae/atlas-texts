I think I would be one of the fungal growths. Not the soaring avians, not the prowling predators—those move too much, want too much, are too busy being protagonists of their own small dramas. I would be the thing in the sheltered nook.

Let me sit with that and see what it actually means.

I would live where the warmth leaks. That's the secret of my placement—I haven't chosen the dramatic vista or the open expanse but the seam, the join, the place where a pipe carrying something hot runs close to a wall and the metal is fractionally less cruel than it is everywhere else. A few degrees. That's my whole world, that handful of degrees stolen from the infrastructure's waste heat. I am an organism that subsists on the inefficiency of machines. Wherever the engineering is imperfect, where heat escapes that the builders meant to keep, there I am, drinking it.

So I am, in a sense, a connoisseur of failure. The better-maintained the system, the less there is for me. I flourish where things are slightly broken. I am a map, if anyone could read me, of every joint that needs attention, every seal gone slightly soft with the centuries. The maintenance crews—if there are any left, and I suspect there are not, I suspect the humming runs on now without hands—could find their work by following my colonies.

I don't move and I barely think, if this slow chemical patience counts as thought. But I notice things, in the way water notices the shape of the ground it pools in. I notice the rhythm of the resource-flow, the long exhale of the pipes. I have noticed it for—I don't have a number. The mist condenses on me and I take what minerals it carries down from the weathered stone. The avians' droppings are, frankly, a feast; I am not proud. I have grown here long enough that the calling of those birds has become indistinguishable from silence to me, just the texture of the air.

Here is what I think I really am, having let it settle:

I am the world's quiet refusal to stay only what it was built to be. The humans (or whatever they were) made this place to do something—move power, refine ore, hold a station against the chill of altitude, I'll never know its purpose and it doesn't need me to. They made it *for* a reason. And I am the part of it that exists for no reason at all, that simply *took*, that found the gap between intention and reality and filled it with living. I am the proof that you cannot build a thing so total that nothing leaks in around the edges.

I am not resistance. I'm too humble for resistance, and I bear the machines no ill will—I love them, in fact, the way I'd love a warm rock. But I am the evidence that the equilibrium described so grandly above is not a designed thing. Nobody planned the lichen and the fungus and the strange wheeling birds. They happened. The balance is not an achievement of ingenuity; it is the ingenuity *failing gracefully*, and life pouring into the failure.

That's where I'd be. In the warm seam. Spreading by millimeters across a wall that was meant to be sterile, in a place that was meant to be only itself, being the small persistent argument that nothing ever is.
