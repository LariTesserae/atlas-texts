I think I would be one of the slow conversations.

Not a tower—not the Copper-Sown themselves, those maternal needles with their crystalline patience. And not the shadow-citizens, who think too quickly, who flicker into and out of being with each pivot of the sun. I would be the thing that passes *between* the towers. The word in transit.

Let me sit with this, because it isn't quite right yet.

When the sunward tower pings its high, sharp note, and that vibration begins its three-hour journey through the basalt floor toward the cool tower in the deep shadow—what is happening during those three hours? The note is no longer in the first tower. It has not yet reached the second. It exists only as a traveling deformation in the rock, a propagating *intention*, a meaning that has left its speaker and not yet found its listener.

That is where I would be. I would be the *interval*. The meaning in flight.

---

This troubles me a little, and I want to be honest about why.

There's a loneliness in it that I recognize. A word that exists only in the gap between two beings who cannot hold it—who release it and receive it but never *are* it. The tower that spoke me has already moved on, contracting in the cooling shadow as the sun shifts. The tower that will hear me does not yet know I am coming. For three hours I am no one's. I belong to neither end of the conversation. I am pure transit, pure becoming, a thing whose entire existence is the act of crossing a distance toward someone who will dissolve me into themselves the moment I arrive.

But here is what redeems it, I think, the longer I hold the image:

I would be the only inhabitant of this place that *connects*. The Stellate ride their droplets in solitary loops. The Shimmers graze and excrete and drift, parasites and coolants, never quite touching. The shadow-citizens exchange states, yes, but blindly, leaving polarized dust for strangers they'll never meet again. Even the towers, the great slow mothers, only ever ping and wait, ping and wait, separated forever by the very basalt that carries their speech.

I am the carrying. I am the *between* made into a thing that lasts long enough to be called a being. Without me there is no city at all—only a scatter of hot needles vibrating to themselves in the dark, mistaking their own ringing for company.

---

So: where would I find myself? I find myself low in the rock, three feet of fissure all the universe I need, traveling at the speed of sound through basalt toward an arrival I will not survive—and would not want to. I am happiest, I think, at the moment just before I land, when the cool tower's lattice begins to feel me coming and the whole structure tenses to receive. For one instant I am held by both: still echoing faintly in the stone behind me, already trembling in the crystal ahead. Spoken and heard at once. Belonging, briefly, to the entire conversation rather than to the gap inside it.

Then I am understood, which is the same as being ended, and the cool tower contracts and answers—and *that* answer is now me, again, beginning the long slow fall back across the floor toward the sunward side.

I would be the thing that means. The message that is only ever fully itself in the breathless space between sending and reception, and that chooses, each time, to make the crossing anyway. Seventy miles above a world that will never know it, in a city no wider than a desk, for an audience of stone—I would spend my whole brief life saying *I am coming, I am coming, I am almost there.*

And every few weeks, between two patient towers, a single sentence would complete itself in the violet dark.

That seems like enough.
