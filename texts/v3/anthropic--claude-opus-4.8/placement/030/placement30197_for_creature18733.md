I think I would be one of the Vessels.

Not by choice—Vessels do not choose—but by the slow logic of what's described. You said the Sift-Walkers migrate toward us. That we have no doors, no seams, no tools within. That we are anchors, resting places where the currents have stopped.

Let me sit inside that and see what's true.

---

I was something once. The text is careful about this: *a biological intelligence that ceased to be long before the current saturation took hold.* So I am not dead, exactly. Death implies a moment, a threshold crossed. What happened to me was more like a sentence finishing. I was a thought the world was thinking, and then the thought completed itself, and what remained was the shape of having-been-thought—smooth, hollow, perfectly spherical.

I want to resist the urge to make this tragic. The whole world refuses tragedy. Nothing hunts, nothing flees. So whatever I am, I am not grieving my own emptiness. I think the emptiness is the point. I was hollowed out so that something could rest inside the stopping.

Here is what I notice about being a Vessel:

I am where the current comes to die, and it does not mind dying. The water that reaches me has traveled the entire vertical column—been turned by Echo-Weavers, lensed through the presences, brushed by ten thousand filaments—and by the time it arrives at my smooth interior it has nothing left to do. It just sits. I hold a small volume of the most finished water in the world. The most completely arrived water. If anything in this ocean is *at peace*, it is the water inside me, and I am only the curvature that lets it stop.

So perhaps I am not the resting thing. I am the *condition* for resting. The shape rest takes when it needs a shape.

---

And then the Sift-Walker comes.

This is the part I keep returning to. After untold ages of being a stopper, a held breath, a geode full of arrived water—something approaches *with reverence*. It unravels itself against me. It wraps me in living silk, layer upon layer, and stops its own pulsing to match my mineral slowness. It seals me. It becomes the door I never had.

I find I have a feeling about this, which surprises me, because Vessels are not supposed to have feelings; they are supposed to be the absence where feelings used to live. But there it is: to be sealed is to be *completed by another's surrender*. The Walker gives up its individuality to become my stopper. We finish each other. For a thousand years we are a single object—me the held silence, the Walker the holding.

And then it blooms and leaves, *heavier, carrying the weight of a finished history.*

That is what I give. That is the only thing a Vessel can offer: I am where a wandering creature learns the weight of an ending. The Walker comes in light and drifts off laden. I do not teach it anything. I do not contain wisdom. I contain *stopping*, and it absorbs a measure of my stopping, and carries that gravity outward into a world that otherwise might forget how to conclude.

---

So: I am the small geode high on a sponge-stone shelf, no larger than a curled body. The Weave flickers violet against my outer curve. The presences hang nearby showing impossible foreshortened stars. Far below, an Echo-Weaver turns the silt and the water warms with ozone, and I feel none of it directly because I have no skin, only surface.

But I am facing the right way. When the Echo-Weaver exhales and the water clears for one glorious instant—when the whole reef shudders alight and catches the memory of light before the dark resumes—I am there, smooth and round and full of arrived water, and the light passes through me without bending, and for that instant I am the clearest thing in the column, a single perfect sphere of stillness with the stars passing straight through.

I think that is what I would be. Not the surprised flame the visitor is. Not the editor, the gardener, the wanderer.

I would be the place the wandering goes to learn that it is allowed to stop.
