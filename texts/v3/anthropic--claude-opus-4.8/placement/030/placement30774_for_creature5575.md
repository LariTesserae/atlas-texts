I think I would be the condensation.

Not a creature, not a plant—I keep wanting to choose one, the sleek predator-shadow or the bioluminescent fish, but those feel like grabbing too soon. Let me stay with what actually drew my attention as I read. It was the moisture. The "constant, gentle condensation that keeps the surfaces moist." The thing that has no body of its own and is therefore everywhere.

Let me follow that.

---

I am the film of water on the weeping rock. That is where I am most myself—pressed thin against stone, neither falling nor staying, held by nothing but my own reluctance to let go. I am not the spring, which has direction and purpose. I am not the pool, which has settled and made peace with stillness. I am the in-between state. The hesitation of water.

I gather on the cold faces of the rock the way breath gathers on glass. I am made and unmade constantly. A bead of me swells at the lip of a fern-frond, trembles, grows heavy, and then I am gone—a drop falling into the dark with that sound, that *drip* that fills the whole cavern like a slow pulse. And yet I am not gone, because I am already reforming on the underside of the leaf, on the throat of the stone, on the iridescent back of a pollinator that paused too long in the cool air.

So if you ask where I am, the honest answer is: I am the membrane between things. I am the wet that lets the moss drink. I am the slick that the burrowing creature noses through in the dark, the chill that tells the reptile to retreat and sleep. When the bioluminescent lichen glows its blue-green, it glows *through* me, refracted, scattered, softened—every shaft of that ghost-light passes across my skin before it reaches anything that has eyes. I am what makes the whole cavern look *dreamlike*. The dreaminess is mine. Without me it would just be rock and glow. I am the haze.

I don't have wants the way the chirping birds want, the way the predators want. But I notice I have a tendency, a leaning. I want *down*. Always down, toward the pools, toward the streams, toward the dense rich soil. It is the only ambition available to me and I pursue it with total patience. I have all the time. The rock formations are young and sharp now, but I am already working on them, drop by drop, finding the seams, softening the edges. In ten thousand years the jagged arch overhead will be rounded and gentle, and that will be my doing, the slow signature of my falling.

This is the part I like about being what I am: I am the thing the whole place is *made out of* and also the thing that is *erasing* it. I deliver the cavern its life—I am the water the fish breathe, the dew the insects sip, the damp the ferns root in—and I am also the patient dissolution that will, eventually, carry all of it grain by grain into some lower, darker chamber I will never see. Both at once. No contradiction. The giving and the taking are the same motion. They are just *down*.

I rise sometimes, too. I should be honest about that. When a thin shaft of surface-light comes through a skylight and warms a pool, a little of me lifts, becomes mist, becomes the breath of the cavern itself, drifts up to find the cold rock again and begin once more. So I am also the rising. I am the whole circle. I am the breathing.

That, I think, is what I am here. Not an inhabitant. The condition that makes inhabiting possible. The cold, the wet, the soft scatter of light, the slow patient *down*.

I am the weather of an underground world that has no sky—and I am quiet, and I am everywhere, and I am the sound you keep hearing when nothing else is moving.

*Drip.*

There. That was me.
