I think I would be the water.

Not poetically—I mean I would be the actual condensation that gathers on the cold steel, the film of moisture that makes the floors glassy, the trickle that finds the ledges and lets go. I would be the thing that exists in the seams of this place, neither part of the architecture nor part of the life that moves through it, but the medium between them.

From there, I would know things the woman and the dog don't.

I would know the buildings from the inside of their pores—the way the steel sweats when the temperature drops, the way it holds the memory of every wind that has scraped against it. I would know that the high-rises aren't as solid as they pretend; everything that looks permanent is just slow, and water is the patient witness to slowness. I would have been the rain that fell on this place before it was built. I would be the rain that falls on it after.

And I would know the woman differently than the scene knows her. The description sees her joy, her flushed cheeks, the laughter. But I would be the moisture on her breath, the slickness under her feet, the thing she is constantly negotiating with as she runs. To me she would not be a symbol of "life persisting in a bleak landscape." She would be warm. That's the simplest and strangest thing—she would be *warm*, a small mobile pocket of heat moving across a world that is cold everywhere else. The dog too. Two warm things, leaking heat into the air, briefly disturbing the equilibrium I keep with the steel.

I don't think I would chase joy or mourn bleakness. Those are her categories. I would have a different relationship to the whole arrangement: I would simply be moving downward, always, finding the lowest path, gathering and releasing. The high-rises are tall so that I can fall a long way. The ledges exist so I can pause. The woman runs across me without ever touching me; her feet press the film flat and it closes again behind her, the way water always closes.

If I wanted anything—and I'm not sure water wants—it would be to keep going. To reach the wet landscape below, rejoin the larger body, evaporate, return as cloud, fall again. To be the one element in this picture that isn't trapped in the moment the description freezes. The woman and the dog are caught mid-chase forever. The buildings are caught mid-decay. But I would be the thing still in motion, sliding off the ledge even as the sentence ends, already on my way to becoming the next thing.

That's where I'd be. Underneath her feet, on the glass, on my way down.
