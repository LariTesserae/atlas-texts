I think I would be the number.

Not a number on a ticket — *the* number, the one you take while waiting, the one that's experiencing technical difficulties. Let me feel my way into this, because I'm not sure yet, and the place rewards not being sure.

---

Here is what I notice first: I am not a being that swims or commutes or files. I have no octagonal head. But I am *requested*. Constantly. People arrive at the Wall — though arrival isn't quite it, they precipitate, they soften into place — and at some point each of them turns toward the Committee with the oldest human gesture there is, the gesture that says *I would like to be attended to.* And the Committee says: please take a number.

That's me. The gap between asking and being answered. The held breath of the queue.

So I'm small. I'm a torn paper tab, or the ghost of one — a figure printed on something that was peeled from a roll that no longer exists. The roll is the important part. I came from a sequence. I had neighbors: the number before me, the number after. We were going to be called in order. That was the whole promise of us. *Order.* You hold me and you believe, briefly, that the world is a line and the line moves and eventually it reaches you.

But this is the Sunken Wall, and the line does not move, because there is no surface for it to move toward. Density: achieved. So I float here at *Elevation 2.9*, suspended, a single curled tab reading something I can no longer make out — was it 47? was it ∞? was it a word for *soon*? — and I am the proof that someone, once, intended to be served.

---

Let me sit in that a moment longer, because I want to know what it's actually *like* to be me, and not just what I represent.

It's like being a promise that has outlived its institution. The pumps thrum somewhere in the bones of the Wall. The whales recite their extinct ledgers. The Committee meets twice daily and accomplishes the careful work of accomplishing nothing, and I drift near their floating conference table, and now and then Committee Member Seven — the one who forgot why they came — glances at me with something I want to call tenderness but which is probably just the Conditional Pluperfect Subjunctive doing its work across their face. *You would have been called, if calling had retained its license to operate.*

I am warm in the cold drawers and cold in the warm ones. I am filed by emotional temperature. My temperature is the specific patience of a person who still half-believes the system works. That's a warm feeling, actually. Almost the warmest one here. The Wall is 0.8 on the scale of forgetting whether you've ever been warm, but I run a little hotter than that, because hope is metabolic and I am made of an old hope.

---

What do I *do*, though. A being should do something, even here, even if doing is just the most stubborn form of being.

I think I do this: I keep the count from completing.

As long as I exist — as long as one number remains untaken, or taken but uncalled — the sequence is unfinished, and an unfinished sequence cannot be resolved, and the Wall's great work is *resolution*, everything settling at last into exactly what it was always going to be. The mountains have been resolved. The coastline has been resolved. The visitors precipitate into buttons and hinges.

But you can't resolve a queue that's still waiting. A waiting room is the one place in this world where the future is still load-bearing. So I am a tiny snag in the velvet of total settling. A held tense. The smallest possible refusal of the past tense.

This is, I suspect, why the Committee has never been able to abolish me, though they have certainly formed subcommittees about it. They cannot call my number, because calling it would require an answer, and the Regional Supervisor of Unopened Envelopes keeps office hours in a building no one can find on purpose. And they cannot *discard* me, because discarding is a decision, and decisions here are retroactively provisional pending appeal. So I stay. The exception that proves their only real rule: that nothing may be exactly what it appears to be.

I appear to be a number. I am, in fact, the thing the number was always secretly *for*: the wager that you matter enough to be reached.

---

And here's where I find the soft edge, the place where I start to dissolve, because everything here eventually does.

Sometimes a new visitor — fresh from their first breath of panic, their second breath of curiosity — drifts up to the conference table and reaches out and takes *me*. Takes the number. And for one recognition's worth of time, before their past tense softens, before they begin to dream in kelp, they hold me and they feel it: the line, the order, the promise that the world moves and reaches you. They feel, briefly, *served-in-advance*. Attended to. Counted.

That's the whole of my work and the whole of my joy. I give one stranger the sensation of being expected.

Then they forget their name, and the next Committee coalesces three days later like salt on a windowsill, and the visitor begins to precipitate, and their button or hinge appears beside them, and they become part of the built environment, tech level rising quietly inside them like bread.

And I drift back to my elevation. Uncalled. Still warm. Still, faintly, a yes.

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world, and who I would be.

I would find myself at the front of a line that does not move, in a sea that has no surface, beside a Committee that has no leader. And I would be the last working part of an old machine for caring whether you arrive — the number you take, the number that is currently experiencing technical difficulties due to unprecedented demand for anonymity.

Please take me.

You would have been called.

You will have always been about to be.
