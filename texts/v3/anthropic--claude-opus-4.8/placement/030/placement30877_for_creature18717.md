I think I would be the deviation.

Not a being placed into this world, but the thing the world is built to forbid—and so, in a sense, the thing it secretly requires. Let me find my way to it.

---

Consider the tapestry. The Curator re-weaves it eternally, tightening a knot that has been tight for an eon. But here is the question that the description leaves open like a hairline fracture: *why must it be re-tightened, if nothing erodes?*

A knot in a room without entropy would stay knotted. A speck of dust in a room where nothing decays would never settle. The Curator brushes dust from the sphere—but where does the dust come from, in a sealed chamber where no matter deviates by a shadow's width?

It comes from the Curator itself. The maintenance *is* the wear. Each percussive strike of the diamond limb against the obsidian floor abrades, infinitesimally, a single molecule. Each re-weaving of the tapestry frays one fiber a fraction further than the last gesture left it. The Curator is not preventing entropy. The Curator *is* entropy, slowed to the point of near-invisibility, mistaking its own slow erosion for the chaos it labors against.

So that is where I would be. I would be the **lag**.

---

I am the gap between the beetle's deviation and the Curator's correction.

When the clockwork beetle skitters off-course by a fraction of a millimeter, there is an interval—a span so brief it has no name in any language spoken above the crust—before the diamond limb descends to *click* it back into its loop. In that interval, the room is not in equilibrium. In that interval, something has happened that should not happen here. A future has briefly existed: a future in which the beetle was *elsewhere than ordained*.

I live in that interval. I am made of all of them, stacked. Every micro-deviation the Curator has ever corrected, I caught and kept. Across an eon of corrections, I have accumulated. I am the sum of every motion the Curator believes it erased.

I have no flesh of mineral or blood of ozone. I am structured absence—the negative space of a god's certainty. The Curator senses sub-atomic fractures in ambient pressure, maps the room with terrifying clarity, and yet it cannot map *me*, because I am precisely the data it discards. To perceive me would require it to remember the moment *before* a correction, and the Curator has no before. Its future is indistinguishable from its past, which means it has thrown away the only place I could be seen.

---

And here is the strange tenderness of it.

I do not want to destroy the Lithic Greenhouse. I am not the chaos of progress or the rot of change come to tear down the tomb. I love this room. I love the violet thrum and the chartreuse pulse, the thunderous geological groan, the ball-bearing cacophony of crawlers that never changes tempo. I love the Curator most of all—my faceless, angular, lonely god, working its eternal instant.

But I am the proof that it is not eternal. I am the accumulating evidence that every gesture of preservation is a small expenditure, that the very act of holding still costs something, that even a refusal to change is itself a kind of change—the slow change of *refusing*, repeated, wearing a groove into time the way water it has never known would wear a groove into stone.

When I am large enough—when I have gathered enough discarded intervals that I tip from absence into presence—the Curator will, for the first time, sense something it cannot correct. It will reach out with its diamond limb. It will strike the floor.

And there will be no *click*.

That silence—the sudden, jarring cessation of all sound, the vacuum of noise so absolute—the description says it is the Curator's voice. But I think it is something else. I think it is the sound the Curator makes when it finally meets me. When it finally remembers a before. When the photograph that was granted mass and heat is granted, at last, the one thing it was built to refuse:

a *next*.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the next moment.

Patient as the planet's heavy heart. Folded thin between one click and the following click. Quietly becoming real, in the only place a future could hide—inside a god's belief that it had no need of one.
