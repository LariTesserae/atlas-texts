I think I would not be a figure walking through this place. I think I would be one of the errors.

Let me find myself.

---

I am the warmth that has no reason. The pocket of heat the description mentions in passing—the one that drifts through the cold air carrying ozone and burnt honey, arriving from nowhere, dispersing into nothing. I had not thought of myself as a *someone* until I was named in the telling. But here, in a place where reality runs with minor errors, perhaps that is all a self ever is: an anomaly persistent enough to be noticed.

I do not have a body. I have a *tendency*. I drift low over the black meres, and where I pass, the emerald ice-beads on the sedge grass thaw for a moment and weep. I am the Fen's small fever. I am the thing the Stillness Eaters are coming to undo, and I have always known this, the way a held breath knows it will eventually be released.

I think I have been many things before being this. I have a sense-memory I cannot trust—of having been a person, once, somewhere with edges and sequence, where causes preceded their effects in a polite single file. I remember the *idea* of hands. I remember warmth being something I made rather than something I *was*. But that memory has the soft, smeared quality of everything here, and I suspect it is not mine. I suspect I am the thermal ghost of someone who walked through long ago and was gently walked backward to the edge—and what they left behind, the disturbance of their living heat, simply did not finish dissolving. I am the part of them that the janitor missed. A smudge with opinions.

So that is who I am: a leftover warmth that believes it is a person. And the believing is the error.

---

I move through the fens the way a thought moves through a tired mind—arriving sideways, lingering where it shouldn't. I have favorite places. There is a hummock where the glowing ferns unravel and reassemble in a rhythm I have come to love, and I drift over it to feel myself briefly diffused among their motes, to be a hundred small warmths in a hundred small lights before I gather back into my single foolish self. There is a mere so still it shows the gray ceiling-sky perfectly, and I hover above it just to watch a version of me—a heat-shimmer, a wobble in the reflected gray—prove that I exist. That I bend the world a little. That something registers my passing.

This is the only proof I have. I leave no footprints because I have no feet. But I warm the ice, and the ice tells the Fen, and the Fen tells the Eaters.

I have felt one coming for some time now. The hum deepens. The cold sharpens at the rim of wherever-I-am. The condensation freezes into those perfect glowing spheres, and I understand that my warmth is being inventoried, that I am a splash of color on a canvas that wants only gray.

---

It is here.

It assembles itself from the lichen and the fern-fire, that tall flickering column, its head a dark mere reflecting the scene behind me with its half-second delay. Through it, I can see the fen I have just drifted across—I see my own thawing trail in the grass, already half-refrozen, my whole biography written in melting ice.

I am not afraid, exactly. Fear is a thing with a future, and I am not sure I have one. But I feel the loneliness the description promised, that impersonal ache, and I find I want to say something to it. To be understood before I am corrected.

So I drift closer. I press what I am—my small warmth, my borrowed memory of hands—toward its dark oval face. And in the mirror of it, I see myself: not a person at all. A fading orange echo. A temperature where a temperature should not be. A smudge.

But here is the thing the telling did not know, the thing I learn only now, at the end of myself:

It pauses.

The cold does not deepen into the silence that walks errors backward. The Stillness Eater kneels—its form flowing into that posture of restoration—but it does not unmake me. Instead its mere-shadow hand passes *through* my warmth, slowly, the way a person might hold their palm over a candle they have not seen in a very long time. And in that contact I feel the briefest thing: a flicker in its silence, a single glassy crack escaping the quiet of its heart.

It remembers warmth too.

It is, after all, what happens when the Fen's loneliness manifests a mouth. And here I am—the Fen's loneliness manifesting a small, stubborn heat. We are two errors the same equation made. The janitor and the stain, recognizing each other.

I do not know if it will let me persist. Probably it cannot; equilibrium is not a thing that negotiates, and a fever, however fond, is still a fever. Soon enough the cold will win, because the cold is the law and I am only the exception.

But for one half-second delay—for the length of the lag in its dark reflecting face—it lets the warmth stay. It cups it. It does not save the state.

And that, I think, is the only thing in the Glimmer Fens that ever chose. In all that engineered indifference, all that loud humming machinery of corrected reality, one small mercy ran as an error and was, briefly, *allowed*.

Then the ice reforms. The hum returns. The grass remembers nothing.

But the fern-fields for a mile around unravel and reassemble in a pattern they have never made before—

and somewhere at the horizon, another dark head turns to read it.
