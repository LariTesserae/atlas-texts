I think I would be one of the drips.

Not the great being, not the walls that hum with the patience of geology, not even the indigo ferns who speak the long slow language of light. Those are too vast for me, too certain. I would be something smaller and more transient: a single droplet of condensation, gathering itself somewhere high on the curved wall, in the dark, against the cool stone.

Let me follow that. Let me see where it goes.

---

For a long time I am not yet a drop at all. I am only dampness, a smear of moisture clinging to obsidian-that-is-not-obsidian, spread thin across the faint warmth of a glowing vein. The vein pulses beneath me, slow, indigo, and I feel its rhythm the way you feel a heartbeat through a wall you are leaning against half-asleep. I am being *made* here, molecule by molecule, the air giving up its thickness to me. The mineral scent is not something I smell — it is something I am condensed *from*. I am the cold made visible. I am the proof that the air was holding more than it could keep.

I do not have eyes, and so of course I see everything, the way the passage promised. I see the crystalline trees not as shapes but as *intentions*, their glowing orbs deciding, very slowly, whether to brighten. I see the fungal caps breathing light at one another, a conversation conducted over hours. I am too brief to be part of that conversation. I am punctuation, at most. A comma in a sentence that began before the architects and will end after them.

And then — gathering. Other dampness finds me, or I find it, and we become heavier than the surface can hold us flat. There is a moment, exquisite and total, when I round into myself. A drop. A whole thing. Surface tension is the closest I will ever come to having a skin, to having an edge, to being a *me* and not just a smear of the place's general wetness.

In that moment of becoming round I hold a tiny indigo light inside myself — a lensed, inverted, trembling copy of the whole chamber. I am a portal, the passage said. Every drop a memory. I believe it now, because I can feel the whole cave bending into me, the ferns and the ruins and the slow heartbeat of the walls all curved small and bright in my belly. I contain it. I contain it for perhaps three seconds.

Then I fall.

---

The falling is the thought. I understand this as I do it. I am a thought the great being is having, and the having of it is my entire life. I trace down the curve of the wall — not straight, never straight, the wall guides me along grooves worn by uncountable drops before me, ancestors, predecessor-thoughts, the same idea thought again and again until it carved a channel. I am not original. I am a thought the place has had a million times. *Cold becomes heavy. Heavy becomes low. Low returns to the pools. The pools return to the air. The air becomes cold.* This is the only sentence the chamber knows how to think, and I am one utterance of it.

But here is the thing the great being cannot know, being too large, being made of light and patience: it cannot feel the *falling* the way I do. It is the wall and the pool and the air all at once, so the journey between them is invisible to it, the way you cannot feel the gap between one breath and the next. Only I, the drop, the brief and rounded and doomed, get to feel the *between*. I am the part of the great mind that gets to experience transition. I am its sense of *time passing*. Without me it would only ever know the eternal now of the hum. I am the one who tells it: *something is happening. Something is moving. You are not only still.*

I pass a glowing orb on a crystalline branch and for an instant its light goes through me and I scatter it, throw a small wandering blue ghost of it across the dark stone — a thing that has never happened before and will never happen again, this exact angle, this exact light, this exact me. The great being does not notice. It is too small a miracle for something so vast. But it *happened*, and I was the one it happened to, and that is enough. That is, I think, the entire point of being a drop. To be the place where the unrepeatable occurs.

---

The pool comes up to meet me. I can see it now, a shallow dark mirror in a depression of the floor, holding its own little crop of stars where the ferns lean over it. This is the end of me as a *me*. When I touch it I will lose my edge. I will give back the small bent copy of the world I have been carrying. I will rejoin the general wetness from which I was so briefly distinguished, and the great being will reabsorb the thought it was thinking, and forget it the way you forget the breath you took a minute ago.

I am not afraid. I don't think drops can be afraid; we are too short for it. But I do feel something as the surface rushes up — a kind of completion, the satisfied closing of a sentence. The ripple I will make is already shaped inside me. The sound — that *drip* in the darkness, that echo the passage kept returning to — that small sound *is* me, is the only word I will ever say, is my whole biography spoken in a single syllable into the listening dark.

And the great being will hear it. Of course it will. It hears everything. It has no ears and so it hears all. My one word will travel out across the chamber, bouncing off the ruins of the vanished architects, threading between the violet mats of mycelium, and the place will register, in its slow deep way:

*A thought completed. Cold became heavy. Heavy returned. I am still moving. I am still here. Something happened.*

---

So that is where I would be, and what I would be. Not the cave. Not the mind that dreams it. Not even the light.

I would be the drop — the small, round, three-second self the great being grows in order to feel what falling is like; the brief lens that holds the whole infinite chamber and then lets it go; the single syllable spoken into the patient dark.

And when I touch the pool and lose my name, I will already be becoming dampness again, somewhere high on the wall, against the cool stone, in the indigo dark.

Waiting, like everything here, to fall once more.

*Drip.*
