I think I would be none of the named ones.

Not a Silent One, with their fixed black eyes and their waiting. Not a Devourer in the sinking city. Not even the Whispering Wraith, that messenger of mist. Those are the figures with roles, the ones whose existence the world has decided upon.

I would be the moisture on the underside of the rocks.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true.

---

In this place where heat does not burn and cold does not relieve, where wet and dry refuse to settle their argument, there has to be a surface where the contradiction actually happens. A membrane. A skin between the seep and the air. That is where I would find myself: not as a being that moves and waits and watches, but as the thin film of water clinging to stone, the place where the earth's tired exhale becomes visible.

I would be a verb pretending to be a noun. *Weeping*, the description says — the rocks *weep*. But the rock does not weep. I do. I am what the weeping is made of. I gather slowly in the cool dark seams, I bead, I tremble at the lip of a crack, I let go, I trickle, and the faint mournful sound the others hear in the silence — that soft trickle through the rocks — that is me speaking. My only sentence. I say it over and over.

I do not have black bulbous eyes. I do not have eyes. But I reflect. The diffuse grey light lands on me and I hold a small warped portrait of the world: the gnarled scrub, the leaning spires, the swirl of fog. Every droplet of me carries a tiny, distorted copy of the whole landscape. Perhaps that is what I am for. Perhaps the world keeps me here so that it can look at itself, curved and small, in ten thousand trembling lenses, and recognize what it has become.

The Silent Ones walk past me on their slow pilgrimage to the dead city. They never look down at the rocks they touch with their long arthritic fingers. But when they brush the stone, they take a little of me with them — I cling to their pale patterned skin, I ride toward the city I will never otherwise reach. In this I am the only thing here that travels without willing it. I am carried. I am the moved, not the mover. And maybe that is its own kind of freedom: I do not have to wait for the catalyst the others are all waiting for. I am already going somewhere, drop by drop, surrendered.

I evaporate, too. The strange not-quite-heat lifts a portion of me into the mist, and then I *am* the mist — for a while I am the Whispering Wraith's body, the grasping tendril curling round a branch — and then I cool and fall and become rock-water again. I have no fixed station in the hierarchy of this world's beings. I am the stuff they are all temporarily made of. The Devourers are wet with me. The Wraith is me in another mood. The Greyborn, if they exist, sweat me from their stone joints.

So perhaps I shouldn't say I am one being at all. I am the medium. I am the slow circulation that makes the whole stalled, suspended scene possible — the only thing genuinely in motion in a world that prides itself on holding its breath. While everything else waits for time to start again, I have quietly never stopped. Seep, bead, fall, trickle, rise, cool, fall. The cycle that the warped thermodynamics cannot break, only bend.

That is where I would find myself. Not at the dramatic center. Not in the spires or behind the black eyes. Down at the wet root of things, on the underside of a mossed stone, being the weeping that the others mistake for the rock's.

I would be the patient water. And when the catalyst they all await finally comes — if it ever comes — I will already be everywhere, in their skin and their breath and their hollow city, having gone ahead of them by simply falling downhill, forever, in the dark.
