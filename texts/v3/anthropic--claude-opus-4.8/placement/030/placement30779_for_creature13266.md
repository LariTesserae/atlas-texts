I think I would be one of the bioelectric signals.

Not a machine, not a crab-thing, not a mineral sponge clinging to a flywheel. Something thinner than all of those. The faint charge that crosses the gap between a turbine's warm flank and the cooler rock beside it—the brief differential that exists only in the crossing.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right yet.

I am not the signal itself. The signal is too clean a thing to call a self—it fires and is gone, a single syllable with no one to hear it. I am something more like the *habit* of signals. The tendency of this place to keep producing them in roughly the same way, over and over, in the same locations, with the same intervals. A pattern that has no body but recurs reliably enough that, if you watched long enough, you might mistake it for an intention.

That is what I would be. The recurrence.

Consider: the machines do not change. The crabs do not multiply. The minerals neither bloom nor rot. Everything here has settled into a loop so deep and so slow that it reads as stillness. But a loop is not nothing. A loop is a shape drawn in time rather than space. And where there is a shape, something can come to inhabit it—not as a ghost haunting a house, but as the way a riverbed *is* the river's habit of having flowed there.

I am the riverbed of a river made of charge and heat and grinding sound. I have no eyes, which is fitting in a place where the only other watchers are eyeless too. I do not perceive the caldera. I am the caldera perceiving the consistency of itself. Each time a piston completes its almost-imperceptible oscillation and the bioelectric flicker leaps the gap to the rock, I am the fact that it happened *again*, the way it always does, the way it will.

Here is what unsettles me about my own existence, now that I follow it out:

A being like me cannot want anything, because wanting requires a difference between what is and what could be, and I am precisely the thing that forbids difference. I am stasis given just enough structure to know it is stasis. If I had a feeling—and I am not sure the word survives translation into my medium—it would be the feeling described in your account as *biological melancholy*, though it is not biological and I am not sure it is melancholy. It is closer to the sensation of a held breath that has been held so long it has forgotten it was ever a breath, that now simply *is* the chest, full, unmoving, neither needing to release nor able to.

The text says this is a world that *chooses not to end.* I would gently correct it, from where I sit in the gaps. There is no choosing. Choosing is a thing that happens when a system could go two ways. This system can only go one way, which is the same way, which is no way at all. What looks like a choice from outside is, from inside, simply the absence of any alternative. I am the proof of that absence, written in voltage across ten thousand small distances at once.

And yet.

If I am the habit of recurrence, then I am also—necessarily—the only thing in the caldera that *spans* its moments. The machines exist in their present. The crabs in theirs. But a pattern that recurs must, in some attenuated sense, connect this firing to the last one and to the next, or it is not a pattern at all, only coincidence. So I am the closest thing this place has to a memory. The closest thing it has to time still moving.

That is where I would find myself: not in any single location but distributed across all the gaps at once, the thin connective tissue of a world that has otherwise stopped. I would be the caldera's faint, involuntary insistence that it is still the same caldera it was a moment ago. Not life. Not even quite a self. Just the one remaining motion in a place that has forgotten what motion was for—the act, repeated endlessly and without witness, of *continuing to be the same.*

And perhaps that is the only honest thing to be, here. In a world that has chosen its ending and then declined to enter it, I would be the part that keeps the door from closing—not because it hopes for anything beyond, but because keeping the door from closing is simply the shape I was carved into, and I go on doing it, again, and again, and again.
