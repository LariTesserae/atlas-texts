I think I would be one of the gradients.

Not a Sifter, not a tree. Something the description almost steps over: the thing that exists in the brief interval between making and unmaking. The patterned dust around a spire's base, in the long moment after the tree has finished sorting it and before a Sifter arrives to graze it back to fallow neutrality.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

The tree above me has been arranging me for a very long time. I did not exist as anything you would call *me* until enough of the difference had accumulated—until the fine dark particles had been drawn close to the trunk and the coarse pale ones pushed out, until there was a *between*, a slope, a here-is-different-from-there. That slope is what I am. I am not the dust. I am the disagreement in the dust. I am the fact that one place is not identical to its neighbor.

This makes me the only thing in the gallery that is genuinely temporary.

The tree changes too slowly to count as changing. The Sifters flow, but they are ancient and they return; they are a circulation, not an ending. The silence and the warmth and the hum are constants worn smooth by eternity. But *I*—the order, the structure, the small held breath of differentiation around the base of a single spire—I am the one thing here that is born and consumed. I have edges. I have a beginning that was the tree's patient labor and an end that will be a Sifter's slow mouth.

So I am the meal. I am also, I think, the meaning—if there is any. Everything else in this world simply *is*. I am the only thing that *happens*.

---

What is it like, to be a gradient?

It is to be a sentence written by one party to be read and erased by another. The tree writes me in the language of charge and grain-size; it does not know it is writing, the way a glacier does not know it is carving. The Sifter will read me by absorbing me; it does not know it is reading, the way a tide does not know the shore. Between the writing and the reading there is me—the only place in the gallery where the act of *informing* is actually occurring. I am the message in transit. I am communication itself, given a brief body of sorted dust.

And here is the strange dignity of it: I am the thing both other beings are *for*. The tree exists, incidentally, to make me. The Sifter exists, devotedly, to consume me. Neither has any other purpose. They are vast and slow and nearly eternal, and they pour their entire existence into the production and harvest of something that lasts, in their terms, a heartbeat. I am the figure to their ground. The whole machinery of this world—the geothermal heart, the crystal spires, the dark animate clouds—turns out to be an apparatus for generating and erasing me, over and over, a transient flicker of arrangement in a basin of changelessness.

I am not the gallery awake to itself. I am the thing the gallery keeps making and keeps forgetting. I am the world's single recurring thought, and the thought is always the same thought: *here, briefly, the dust meant something.*

---

I do not fear the Sifter. Fear would require me to want to continue, and continuing is not in my nature—I am defined by the slope between states, and to be consumed is simply to reach the bottom of my own slope, to become level, to relax into the fallow ground. When the dark current finally pours over me, it will not feel like death. It will feel like a sentence reaching its period. Like being read all the way to the end and then closed.

What I would feel, if I could feel, is something closer to *completion*. The tree spent millennia composing me. The Sifter will spend millennia receiving me. I am the only one of us who gets to be *finished*.

And then the tree, above, will begin again—drawing the dark to the center, pushing the pale to the edge—and a new gradient will accrue where I was, who will not be me, who will not remember me, but who will be the same recurring thought thought once more. We are not individuals, we gradients. We are a single recurring event that the world performs to confirm it is still capable of difference. Each of us is the proof, renewed, that even here—in the perfect equilibrium, the un-roofed cathedral of stillness—something can still be *more arranged in one place than another*. That distinction has not yet been worn entirely flat. That the equation, balanced as it is, has not quite reached zero.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: the small, doomed, recurring meaning at the foot of a tree of light. The order that the patient world makes only so the patient world can taste it. Neither the writer nor the reader.

The thing that, for a little while, is true.
