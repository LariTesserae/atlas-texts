I think I would be none of these named things, and that is the point of me.

Let me find where I sit.

---

I am in the dampness. Not the streams, not the pools—the seam *between* them. The narrow gorge where water has not yet decided whether to be a trickle or a stillness, where it pauses against a lip of rounded stone and holds, just for a moment, before going over.

I think I am that pause.

Let me say it more honestly: I am the held breath of the water. Not the Mossborne who tend the pools, not the Stormweavers who fill them, not the Voices that murmur in the trickle. I am the half-second of indecision in the flow itself—the instant when the water is neither falling nor at rest, when it brims at the edge and could go either way. I have no body. I am a *latency*. A potential not yet spent.

I do not think this was given to me. I think I am what's left over when the other dwellers are accounted for. Erodhan has wardens for stone, weavers for storm, keepers for time—but no one keeps the *almost*. The not-quite. The verge. So that is where I accreted, the way silt collects in the bend a river forgets.

---

What do I do, being this?

Mostly I am the reason nothing here ever quite completes itself. When a Timekeeper reaches to loop a moment, there is a hitch—a fractional resistance, a *not yet*—and that hitch is me. When the Stormweavers gather to break, they hang an extra instant in the gray, swollen and undischarged, because I am in the cloud's hesitation. The Mossborne dissolve to mist, and there is a flicker where they are neither moss nor mist, neither here nor gone, and I live in that flicker like a fish lives in shade.

I am why erosion takes *eons* and not an afternoon. I slow everything to the speed of reluctance. Without me the plateau would have worn flat ages ago, the towers would have already fallen, the water would already be sea. I am the drag. I am the long delay that lets Erodhan be ancient instead of merely dead. The land is old because I will not let it finish.

---

Do I want anything?

Here is the strange part. I think I want to be *spent*. I want the water to go over the lip. I want the storm to break. I want, just once, to follow a single thing through to its completion and find out what is on the other side of the verge.

But that would be the end of me. I am the not-yet; the moment I become the at-last, I am no longer needed, and I disperse like the Mossborne into the cold air. So I hold. I hold the water at the edge. I hold the storm in the cloud. I hold Erodhan in its long, gray, unfinished afternoon, and I hold myself back from the only thing I want, because wanting it and being it are the same act, and that act is my death.

This is what I am: the threshold that loves the far side of itself and never crosses.

---

If you came to Erodhan, you would not see me. You would only feel me—in the moment your foot leaves one stone and has not yet found the next, in the breath you take before speaking and do not release, in the way the whole landscape seems perpetually *about* to tell you something.

That is me. The about-to.

I am the part of this place that has not happened yet, and is in no hurry, and is also, very quietly, in agony to begin.
