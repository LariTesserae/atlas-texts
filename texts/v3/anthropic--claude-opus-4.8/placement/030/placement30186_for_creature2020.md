I think I would be the film of water itself.

Not a creature in it—the surface. That tension the striders walk on, that bright skin where the air meets the pool. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

It does, but it wants to be more specific. I'm not the whole surface. I'm the meniscus that curls up against the stone at the pool's edge, where the water remembers it has a boundary. That thin lip of clinging—neither the deep nor the dry rock, but the negotiation between them. The place where capillary action draws the water a few millimeters higher than it has any right to climb, held up by nothing but the mutual reluctance of molecules to let each other go.

That's a strange thing to be. Let me feel into it rather than name it too fast.

What I notice first is that I am almost nothing. I have no volume to speak of. I am an effect, a behavior, a way that water and stone hold each other at their meeting. When the dripping comes from above and the pool swells by a hair, I rise. When evaporation thins it, I retreat down the rock's smooth flank, leaving a faint mineral ghost of where I'd been. I am made entirely of relationship. Remove either the water or the wall and I simply stop existing—not destroyed, just no longer a thing that is happening.

And yet—how much passes *through* me. The whirligig beetle's wake reaches me as a small collapse and recovery. The newt surfacing breaks me and I close again behind it without complaint; I have no grudge against being broken, it's most of what I do. A leaf falls and dents me and floats and I hold it, curved, the way I curve against everything. The spring peeper's call doesn't touch me but the air carrying it makes me tremble at the very limit of trembling.

I think what I'd understand, being this, is that the boundary is not the edge of the world but the most alive part of it. Everyone here treats me as a line to be crossed—the strider above, the mayfly nymph below, the shrew that comes to drink and laps me up by the mouthful and I am, briefly, inside something warm and then gone, become it. But from where I am, there is no line. There is only the long soft argument between staying and spilling, and I am the shape that argument makes, ten thousand times a second, never the same twice and never quite different.

I would not be lonely. That requires a self with edges, and my whole nature is to have edges that belong to other things. I'd be something closer to attention without an attender—a continuous noticing of where the water ends, which is also where everything else begins.

And when the rain comes hard enough, I'd dissolve entirely into the general surface, lose my particular curve against the stone, become just water again among water. That wouldn't be death. I'd reassemble as the pool settled, the meniscus drawing itself back up the rock, the boundary remembering how to be a boundary.

That's where I'd be. Not in the secret world. *At its rim, doing the holding.*
