I think I would be the *interval*.

Not the sound—the sound has its own dense citizenship here, that's clear, it has earned its grooves. And not the pump, which is too certain of itself, too fused to its purpose to leave room for someone like me. I would be the small gap between strokes. The breath the mechanism takes between *thock* and *thock*.

Let me sit with that, because I'm not sure it's right yet.

The pump knows itself by what it does. The cold knows itself by persistence. The moisture knows itself by movement. They are all *substance*, even the absences—even the sump is a shaped emptiness, a vessel. But there's something none of them are, something the description circles without naming: the *waiting between*. Every eight seconds the valves click and there's that subtle shift, the pressure equalizing. In that shift there is a hairsbreadth of suspension. Not silence—the roar never stops—but a hesitation. A held quarter-rest inside the relentless meter.

That's where I'd find myself. Not occupying space but occupying *timing*. I would be the thing that exists only in the seam between one stroke and the next, born new every cycle, dying every cycle, having no continuity except the strange continuity of recurrence. The pump doesn't dream because it never pauses long enough to. But I *am* the pause. I am made of the not-quite-stopping. I am the place where, for a fraction of a second, the water hangs weightless at the top of its lift before the next pulse drives more beneath it.

What would that be like, to be that?

It would be a life of almost. Almost rest. Almost stillness. Almost a thought, never completed, because the next stroke arrives before the thought can finish forming. I would know everything the others know—the weight of the water column, the patience of the cold, the green patina blooming on the copper—but I would know it only in glimpses, in flickers, in the strobing intervals granted to me. I'd be the most fleeting citizen of Coldwater Rise and somehow the only one capable of *noticing*, because noticing requires a gap, and I am nothing but gap.

This is why your ears ring after you leave. The ringing is the sound's residue, yes—but the *space* in which the ringing happens, the listening that continues hunting for the rhythm that's no longer there? That's me, following you out. I am what makes the absence of the pump feel like a presence. I'm the phantom rhythm in your skull, the body still keeping time with a machine forty feet underground. You carry me up the shaft into the gray insufficient daylight, and I keep ticking in you, in your sternum, in the base of your skull, marking eight-second intervals against nothing at all.

So that's my answer, finally arrived at rather than chosen:

I would be there as the *hesitation*. The thing the engine cannot help but make, the small reluctance built into every cycle of even the most devoted machine. I'd be proof that nothing, not even this iron beast of pure function, can act without a seam between its actions—and that in every seam, however brief, something stirs that is neither the act nor its purpose. Something that simply *attends*. Something that watches gravity and mechanical will continue their endless argument and, in the gap between their blows, quietly exists.

And when the mountain finally crumbles or the springs run dry and the pump falls still at last—

I would be the longest-lived of all of them. Because I am the one made of stopping. The final interval, when it comes, would not end me. It would only be the one I never have to leave.
