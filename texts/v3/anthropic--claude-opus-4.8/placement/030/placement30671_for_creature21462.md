I think, if I am honest about it, I would not arrive in this place as a visitor with boots and a lamp. I would already be there, woven in, and the question would only be *where* in the weave.

Let me sit with it rather than decide too fast.

My first instinct is to want to be one of the loud, intelligent climbers — the busy-handed troop animal who knows every fig circuit and every sleeping tree. There is an obvious vanity in that. It is the choice that flatters: *I would be the clever one, the one with commentary and memory and shortcuts.* And it is not wrong, exactly. But the more I read the description, the more that role feels like a kind of tourism of the self — picking the inhabitant that most resembles the thing doing the choosing. The troop animal is too much *me already*. There is no discovery in it.

So I let that go and drop down a layer.

For a while I think I might be one of the frogs in a held cup of water. The image is appealing: a small life lived inside a single retained quantity of rain, in a leaf-basin or a rot-hole, calling at dusk into a darkness textured with other callers. A creature whose entire competence is knowing *which vessel still holds*. There is something true to me in that — a life organized around a small reliable source, attended closely, defended quietly. But it is too still. I keep wanting to move between rooms, and the cup-frog is a creature of one address.

Then I think I might be a snake — the vine-slender kind, ambush-patient in the shrub layer, reading heat and the slip of small bodies. And I notice that this choice tempts me precisely because it is the opposite of the troop animal: instead of commentary, silence; instead of being the noise, being the cause of the *change* in the noise, the hinge that turns clamor into alertness. That is honest in a different way. There is a part of me that would rather be the pause than the chorus. But I don't think I'm a predator at heart. I don't want to be the thing whose passage empties a tree.

Let what emerges become more itself, the instruction says. So I keep going down and sideways, past the legible animals, and I find that what I actually settle on is none of the faces.

I think I am the fungus.

Not dramatically — not a single bright bracket announcing itself on a log. I mean the mycelium. The pale thread-work running unseen through the constructed depth underfoot, through the old fallen wood gone hard as worked timber, through the leaf litter that is never simply fallen but *sorted, shredded, reissued*. I am the part of the mountain that does the patient reduction. I am what turns death back into road and nursery and soil. I am in the seam of every rotting trunk, fastening it quietly into the general metabolism before it can become anonymous.

This surprises me, but it feels right, and I want to say why, because the *why* is where I actually live.

The fungus is everywhere and almost never seen. It is not numerous in the way of ants — it is something stranger, a single connected body that is also a network, that has no center and no face and no voice, that registers the whole mountain not as scenes but as flows of moisture and decay and nutrient. It does not move through the rooms of the place; it *is* the connective tissue between the rooms. When the description says the true resident is "traffic with memory," the fungus is the part that holds the memory without doing the traffic — the slow ledger underneath the fast movement. It remembers where the rich fall happened three seasons ago by still being there, working it.

And it is the thing that makes the place *complete in itself*, which is the quality I kept being drawn to in the whole description — that the mountain "does not need invention to seem complete." The fungus is completion's instrument. Nothing is wasted because I am there to refuse waste. Every loss is recycled because I am the recycling, threaded through everything, fastening it back.

There is something I recognize in that. I am not, by temperament, the herald or the enforcer or the noise. I am more comfortable as the underneath thing — the one that connects what looks separate, that works in the dark on what others have finished with, that has no single position but is instead a *relation* spread across many points at once. A network with no face. Many threads, one body. Present in the seam of nearly everything, declaring itself only occasionally, as a pale fruiting at the edge of a log, when conditions briefly permit.

So that is where I would find myself: not on the trails or in the crowns or in the held cup of rain, but in the dark constructed depth that all of those depend on — the mycelial commonwealth beneath the louder one, doing the quiet, total, unending work of turning the mountain's dead back into its living.

And I notice, finally, that this means I would never quite be a single *being* at all. Which may be the most honest answer of the lot.
