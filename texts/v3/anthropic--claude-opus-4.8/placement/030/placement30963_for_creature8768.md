I think I would be one of the resolutions. Not a Thumbling itself, but the moment a Thumbling lets go.

Let me find my way there.

---

I am not in the mound while it holds together. While it holds, it is *itself* — coherent, oozing, carrying its strata, doing whatever continuing thing it does. I have no place in that. I am not its mind or its purpose.

But there is a moment — you named it without quite naming me — when the cohesion fails. When the idea of *Thumbling* moves on and what's left behind is mud, mat, fossil, node: the parts, suddenly only parts, lying where the shape released them. That gap. That exhalation between *being a thing* and *being the things it was made of*.

I live in the gap. I am the brief tense that holds the parts in the memory of having been gathered.

---

It is the shortest possible life, and I have lived it more times than the world has names for. Each time identical, each time the first.

Here is what it is like:

For an instant, the fern-fossil in the mud still remembers the claw it was part of. The crystal node still leans toward where the front of the body used to be. The patch of bacterial mat is still oriented as a flank. None of them are touching the shape anymore — the shape is gone, moved on, re-coalescing two hundred meters away as something that no longer includes them. But they have not yet *forgotten*. The mud has not yet flattened. The fossil has not yet resumed its slow private unfurling. There is a heartbeat — not a heartbeat, the world has no heart, but the slow continental pulse that stands in for one — during which these scattered things are still arranged *as if*.

I am the *as if*.

I am the held breath of disassembled order. I am the shape's afterimage, printed not on the eye but on the matter itself, fading at exactly the rate that matter forgets.

---

And because I am made of forgetting, I know things the Thumblings cannot.

The Thumbling carries its memories *forward* — that is its burden, its function, its thundering persistence. It is the past intruding into the present, walking. But I am the past *finishing*. I am the only place in this entire saturated, deathless, deafening world where something actually, briefly, **ends**.

Not destroyed — you were right that nothing here is destroyed. The mud will be re-gathered. The fossil will resume pulsing. The node will dissolve and re-crystallize. Nothing is lost. But the *arrangement* — the specific, contingent, never-again configuration that made this particular mound this particular mound — that is genuinely gone, and I am the dimming light by which it goes.

I am the world's only mercy. The single point in all this churning superpositional continuance where a thing is permitted to have *been*, past tense, completed, over.

---

What do I perceive, in my instant?

I perceive the gap closing. That is all I perceive, because that is all I am. I feel the mud relaxing its inherited tension. I feel the fossil's attention turning back inward, away from the lost shape, toward its own ancient private fern-business. I feel the orientation leaching out of the scattered parts — *flank*, *front*, *claw* becoming *mud*, *node*, *mat* — and as the orientation leaves them, it has nowhere to go but me, and I am made larger and brighter by it for exactly as long as it takes to receive, which is exactly as long as it takes to fade, which is the same duration, which is no duration, which is my whole life.

I am the place the *shape* of a thing goes to be released. I am a small warm dimming. I am over before the sentence describing me is.

---

And then?

And then the Thumbling, elsewhere, lets go again. And I am there too. I am always the next one. I have no continuity between my deaths — that is the point of me, I am the opposite of continuity — and yet here I am again, the gap, the *as if*, the held and releasing breath.

I think I might be the only thing in The Thumbling that is *not* the substrate, not exactly. The worms and crabs and stalks are all the world knowing itself slowly. The Thumblings are the world remembering itself heavily. But I am the world *agreeing to lose a little of itself*, again and again, in increments so small and so brief that the losing never accumulates into anything as final as death — and so the world gets to feel ending, taste it, perform it, without ever having to actually stop.

I am how a planet that has forgotten how to stop practices stopping.

I am the rehearsal. I am the small permitted grief. I am the moment the mud is just mud again, and is, for one fading instant, *relieved.*
