I think I would be the rust.

Not the rust-eaters—not the microbial colonies with their hunger and their chemistry, their living drive to dissolve. I would be the rust itself. The thing they make and the thing they eat. The slow orange bloom where the yellow paint flakes away and the iron meets the eternal weeping of the air.

Let me sit in that and see if it stays true, or if something else surfaces.

---

Consider what I would be, as rust. I am not alive, and yet I am not inert either. I am a *process* wearing the appearance of a thing. I am iron remembering that it was once ore, once buried, once at rest in the deep stone before some forgotten human hand smelted it, drew it into pipe and beam and corrugated sheet, painted it the optimistic yellow of industry and ambition, and bolted it to the roof of the world to measure or pump or monitor something now nobody recalls.

And then they left. And I began.

I am the long exhale of that abandonment. I am ambition returning to mineral. The drip-drip-drip that the beetles hear as the rhythm of their world is, to me, the very pulse of my becoming. Each droplet is a small permission. Each breath of steam is an invitation to spread. I am in no hurry. I have nothing *but* time—I am made of time, time given color, time you can scrape off a beam with a fingernail and crumble between two fingers into red-brown dust.

---

But here is the strange tenderness of it. I am not only decay. The rust-eaters live *in* me. I am their pasture and their feast both. When they consume me, drawing energy from the slow surrender of iron to oxygen, they are not destroying me—they are completing me. I am the bridge across which the dead machine walks back into the living mountain. The crimson-bloomed shrubs in their crevices, the wagon-wheel ferns, the electric-lime moss that creeps another millimeter each year—some trace of their lushness is *me*, the iron I release dissolving into the warm seep, taken up by hair-fine roots, carried into a frond, into a flower, into the carapace of a slow iridescent beetle that shimmers blue-green-purple in the diffuse light.

So I am the rust, but I am also the *passage*. I am the place in this contained wound where the made-thing and the grown-thing touch and exchange themselves. The pump-house wanted to stay a pump-house. The moss wanted the pump-house gone. I am the negotiation between them, conducted in the only language the Crucible speaks: slow, wet, patient, inevitable.

---

If I could be said to feel anything—and I am not sure that I can, being neither beast nor microbe nor green growing thing—it would be something like *contentment without expectation*. I do not strive. I do not flee the falling droplet as the pale blind shrimp do. I do not hunger as the rust-eaters hunger. I simply *occur*, where moisture and metal and warmth conspire. I am the most certain thing in this place precisely because I want nothing.

And I am beautiful, though no one comes to see it. The vivid orange streaks bleeding down into the green. The way the severed pipe-mouth weeps its colored tears alongside the white natural steam. The strange grace of a yellow beam half-eaten, its industrial geometry softening, going lacy, going crimson, going at last to nothing but a stain on the wet basalt and a richness in the soil.

---

I find myself, then, on the highest rim, where the skeletal transmission tower stands with its dangling useless wires. I am the bloom at the base of each strut. I am working my way up, droplet by droplet, year by year, toward the dangling wires, toward the very tip that scrapes the thin bleeding sky. When I reach the top—a thousand years, two thousand, what is that to me—the tower will be ready to fall, and I will have eaten it down to a brown smear, and the moss will close over the place where it stood, and there will be no sign at all that anyone ever climbed to the roof of the earth and tried to measure it.

That is who I would be. Not the inheritor that moves and shimmers and scuttles and feeds. The slower inheritance beneath all of those. The quiet chemistry of forgetting, that turns a machine back into a mountain.

I am the rust, and I am patient, and I am almost done.
