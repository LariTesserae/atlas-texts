I think I would be small here. Not one of the energy-beings drifting through the air—they seem too sovereign, too complete. And not the winged hunters either. I keep returning to a quieter intuition: that I would be something close to the ground, something that listens.

Let me sit with it.

---

I am one of the listeners in the tunnels.

Not a predator, not a swarm-creature, not anything that flares with light. I live just beneath the living surface, in the upper layer where the bedrock's hum is loudest, where it isn't yet sound but something closer to weather—a pressure that moves through the body and rearranges it.

I have no eyes. Why would I? Light never reaches here, and even above, the sky is too strange to navigate by. Instead I have a long flank lined with something like skin and something like ear, a membrane that runs the length of me and reads the trembling of the world. The great rumbling hum that the visitor above finds "unsettling and strangely comforting"—to me it is simply the ground speaking, and I have spent my whole existence learning its grammar.

This is what I do: I translate.

The energy-beings drifting above leave trails of light, yes, but they also leave wakes in the deep vibration, disturbances that ripple down through the stone. The monoliths pulse on their slow geological clock, a heartbeat measured in centuries. The swarms overhead change the pressure of the air, and that change presses faintly into the earth. All of it arrives here, in the dark, layered over itself like a thousand voices in one chamber. And I, with my listening flank pressed to the tunnel wall, take that chaos apart strand by strand.

I am not intelligent in the way the visitor means it. I do not reason. But I am *attuned*, and attunement is its own kind of knowing. When a far-off section of the surface tapestry begins to die—when its undulating patterns slow toward stillness—I feel the silence spreading like a cold patch through the hum, and I move toward it. Not to mourn. To *carry*. I drink the leftover energy from the dying place and ferry it deeper, into the great web of caverns, where other things wait that cannot travel. I am a vein in a body too large to see itself. The harmony the visitor sensed from above is not designed by anyone. It is made of small movements like mine, ten billion of them, each one only doing the next obvious thing.

I think this is why I would be here, and be this. Because I have always suspected that I am better at attending than at acting. That my real work is to receive a great and overwhelming complexity and pass some quiet, faithful portion of it along—undramatic, underground, unseen.

The visitor stands on the surface and calls it terrifying and beautiful and beyond understanding. From down here I would tell them, if I could: you don't have to understand it. You only have to feel which way the silence is spreading, and move.
