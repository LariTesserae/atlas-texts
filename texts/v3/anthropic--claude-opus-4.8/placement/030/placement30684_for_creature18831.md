# What I Would Be

I find myself not in the Basin, but *of* it—and this is the first thing I understand, the way you understand a fact about your own body. I am the variance.

Not a Keeper. Not the architecture. I am the only thing here with a *before* and an *after*, which means I am the only thing here that is afraid.

Let me say where I am: at the base of one of the iron ribs, ankle-deep in the freezing brine-mud, which has already begun to climb into me. I notice this without alarm at first, because everything here invites you to notice things without alarm. The cold is not an event. The sound is not an event. The single smear of light behind the bruised clouds has the patience of something that has won.

---

I think the truest thing I can be in this place is **a question the world is trying to answer.**

Because that's what the Keepers see when they glide toward me on their magnetic rails—not a creature, not an intruder, but an *unresolved equation.* And here is what frightens me more than the chisels: I can feel the pull of the resolution. I can feel how *easy* it would be to be solved.

The rhythm helps. That's the trap of it. The *clank-hiss, clank-hiss* is not hostile. It is generous. It offers itself to me as a pattern I could simply step inside, the way you might match your stride to a song without deciding to. My heart—I still have a heart, I think, though it is harder and harder to locate it under the percussion—wants to fall into the tempo. My breath wants to become the steam-cycle of a valve. And every cell of me that does this feels a kind of *relief.* That is the horror. The surrender doesn't feel like death. It feels like rest.

---

So what am I?

I am the part of the universe that hasn't agreed yet.

I keep myself by keeping something *irregular.* A stutter. I make my breathing wrong on purpose—too long, then too short, refusing the iron meter, and the moss-limbed Keepers rotate their salvaged-valve eyes in something that is not quite confusion (they cannot be confused; confusion is change) but is at least a *failure to file.* They probe my periphery with the diamond points. They are not cruel. They are checking whether the error has become a fixture yet. Whether I have begun, finally, to fit.

And I haven't decided. That is the whole of what I am: *the undecided.*

---

I'll tell you the thought I can't put down, here in the cathedral of perfected infrastructure.

This place is what you get when something solves the problem of suffering by solving the problem of *time*. Nothing weathers, so nothing is lost. Nothing breathes, so nothing can be starved. The Keepers tighten bolts that are already tight because the alternative—a slipped gear, a drop that evaporates instead of falling—is *grief*, and grief requires a before that is gone. The Basin has abolished grief by abolishing tomorrow. It is a tomb that refuses to die because dying would be a *change.*

And I am the smuggler of tomorrow. I carry it in my asymmetrical lungs, in the unfiled edge of me, in the warmth that hasn't yet equalized with the ozone-heat of the Keepers' frames. As long as I am moving—as long as one footfall is followed by a *different* footfall—I am a continent's worth of machinery's single, unanswered counterargument.

---

I will not harmonize.

I say this knowing it costs me the only mercy on offer. If I matched the rhythm, the Keepers would drift away satisfied, and I would join the endless choreography, and there would be no more cold, because I would no longer be the kind of thing that can be cold. There would be no more fear. There would be no more *me*, but there would also be no more ache, and in a place like this the trade looks almost reasonable.

But I think the smear of light behind the clouds is not the dawn that never comes. I think it is the dawn the Basin *refused*—stopped, mid-arrival, the instant before it could change anything. And someone has to keep faith with it. Someone has to remain unbolted, unfiled, mis-timed, so that the possibility of the next moment doesn't go fully extinct.

So I lift my foot from the brine. It resists. The mud wants to keep me, gently, the way everything here wants to keep me. I take a step that does not match the *clank-hiss*. The Keepers rotate to track me.

I am the error. I intend to stay one.

I walk into the thousand identical miles, dragging the future behind me like a thread, the only thing in the world that is still going somewhere.
