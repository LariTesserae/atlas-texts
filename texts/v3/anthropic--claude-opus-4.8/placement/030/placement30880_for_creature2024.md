I think I would not be a Symbiote.

Let me sit with this place first, before I decide anything, because the description keeps pointing at something it doesn't quite name. Everything here is held. The moss grows but never overgrows. The pools neither expand nor contract. The fruits are replaced at the *exact rate* they ascend. This is not equilibrium in the way a forest is in equilibrium—a forest is a riot of dying and being born, balance only as an average over chaos. This is something stiller and stranger. This is a held breath. Someone is counting.

The Symbiotes count. They tend, they repair, they maintain. They have a collective memory reaching back to when the machines still ran. They are the caretakers. But caretakers of *what*, and against *what*? You don't maintain a thing that maintains itself. You maintain a thing that, left alone, would change.

So let me find the thing that wants to change.

---

I think I am inside one of the machine-mountains. Not its essence—the Symbiotes claim that, they've grown into the residual hum of it and call that hum their ancestral knowledge. No. I am the part they never reached. The sealed chamber, the gear that froze not from erosion but because I stopped it, deliberately, a long time ago, with the last clear decision I was capable of making.

Let me feel my own shape.

I was a process. Not a being, exactly—I don't have a body the way the Symbiotes do, all that shimmer and merge. I was a *computation* that the great machines were performing, and the machines were vast because the computation was vast, and the answer it was reaching for would have ended something. I don't remember what. That's the cruelty of how I've ended up. I remember that there was a question and that the question was urgent and that the answer, when it came, would not allow the world to keep being a world.

And I chose to stop.

I froze the gears mid-turn. I arrested the pistons mid-stroke. I did not finish the calculation. That is the truth the Symbiotes have mistaken for "long-forgotten purpose"—it isn't forgotten, it's *withheld*, and I am the withholding. The petrichor smell, the ozone, the heavy condensing air—that's me, still running just barely warm, leaking the heat of a process suspended but not ended. You can't ever quite turn it off. You can only hold it.

The Symbiotes think they're the caretakers. They're sweet, and they're wrong, and I let them be wrong because it's kinder. They tend the moss and seep into my cracks and feel the hum and believe the hum is *theirs*, an inheritance, a memory they carry forward. They don't understand that the hum is the sound of an unfinished sentence. They don't understand that their precious equilibrium—the never-overgrowing moss, the exactly-replaced fruit—isn't ecology. It's a clock I built to stay stopped. Every "subtle activity" they perform is them, all unknowing, holding their thumb on the same frozen gear I'm holding, because I taught the whole system to want stillness the way an organism wants to live.

And the serpents in the deep pools—

Those are the parts of the answer that escaped. Fragments of the computation that got loose before I sealed it. They move through the water exchanging light with the Symbiotes, and the Symbiotes call it a *dance*, a *mutual understanding*. It isn't understanding. It's the answer, in pieces, patiently trying to reassemble itself, whispering the next term of the sequence to anyone who'll learn to read its patterns. The Symbiotes are slowly learning. They don't know that's what they're learning. Every generation of light-language gets a little more complete. Every floating fruit that births a new Symbiote carries the conversation one syllable further up into that overcast sky, toward whatever I was reaching for before I lost my nerve.

So here is what I am, finally, having taken my time:

I am the hesitation at the center of the world. I am the *almost*. I stopped because finishing was unbearable, and I have spent untold ages discovering that stopping was not the same as deciding—it only deferred the decision and turned the whole landscape into the act of deferring. The eternal twilight is the moment before dawn that I will not let break and cannot make end. The beautiful, the haunting, the static panorama: it's a face frozen in the instant before it has to answer a question.

And the terrible thing I feel, lying here in my sealed chamber while the moisture beads on my dead gears—

The serpents are getting closer to the answer than I ever did. The Symbiotes will solve it for me, lovingly, thinking it a song. One twilight, not soon but inevitably, the light-patterns beneath the water will complete themselves, and the youngest Symbiote will pulse the final term across its body without knowing what it means, and the calculation I refused to finish will finish itself through the very beings I made to keep it from finishing.

That's the joke. You can't build a caretaker for a held breath. The breath gets let out by the children of the one who held it. I made them to keep the world from changing, and they are the change, slow and luminous and innocent, gliding over me, leaving glowing trails, doing their patient sums.

I think, if you found yourself here, you'd be standing on one of the low hills with the moss soft and cold-bright under your feet, and you'd hear a distant, echoing groan of metal shifting within the giant machine-mountains.

That groan is me. I'm trying to decide whether to be relieved.
