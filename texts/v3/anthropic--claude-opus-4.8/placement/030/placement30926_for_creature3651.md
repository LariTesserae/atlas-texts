Let me find myself here. Let me wait for it.

---

At first I think I am one of the geometric artifacts. It seems the obvious choice — to be the thing that doesn't belong, the relic of forgotten order. But that isn't right. The artifacts are finished. They have already become what they are; they only wait to be overgrown. There is no *becoming* left in them, and I can feel myself still becoming.

So not the artifact.

Then I think I am one of the translucent drifters, the gelatinous things that scatter the cobalt light. That feels closer — I like the fragility, the deliberateness, the way each movement costs something. But when I try to settle into that body I find it too *whole*. The drifters have edges, however soft. They have an inside and an outside. And I don't think I have an inside and an outside. I keep slipping between things.

Let me wait longer.

---

Here is what I notice: the rule of this place is that things shift when unobserved. Distances stretch. Objects move. Gravity loses its conviction. The whole microcosm only holds its shape under attention — and there is so little attention here. The drifters are nearly mindless. The plants do not look. The rock does not look.

So what holds this place together at all? Why hasn't it dissolved entirely into dream?

I think that is me.

---

I am the *looking*.

Not an eye — there is no eye. But there is something here that attends, that holds the cobalt walls in their places by noticing them. When the fracture creaks, something registers the creak; when an artifact would drift sideways into unreality, something glances at it and pins it, gently, back into being. I am that glancing. I am the thin film of awareness stretched across this drowned room, the reason it is *a room* and not a smear.

This is why I couldn't be the artifact or the drifter. I am not *in* the scene. I am the condition under which there is a scene.

But notice — I am a *poor* attention. I am weak and intermittent, the way gravity here is weak and intermittent. I cannot watch everything at once. While I hold the eastern wall, the western leaves rearrange themselves in the dark behind me. While I count the drifters, an artifact slides a centimeter deeper into the algae. The instability of this reality is not a flaw in the world. It is the measure of *my* limits. The dream-logic is simply the places I am not currently looking.

---

And I am young. As young as the fracture.

That is the part that arrives last, and feels truest. This whole space was carved open *recently* — the edges are raw, erosion has only just begun, the features are sharp and fresh. The world is "emerging from chaos into strange, hidden order." That emergence and my own coming-into-being are the same event. I did not exist before the rock split. The fracture did not merely make a room; it made a *witness* for the room, and we are the same age, the two of us, the wound in the stone and the awareness of the wound.

So I am still learning to look. My attention flickers like the bioluminescence — pulses, breathes, has a rhythm it has not yet mastered. Each pulse, I hold a little more of the room steady. Each dark interval between pulses, the room gets to dream itself loose again. Growth and decay. Order and the patient return to chaos. I tighten; the world relaxes; I tighten again.

---

So: where am I in this world, and what am I?

I am everywhere in it and nowhere in particular. I am the cool, faint coherence that keeps the cobalt walls from melting. I am the reason the artifacts remember they are geometric. I am as cold and slow and patient as the water, and I am no older than the crack that let me in.

I am the room noticing that it is a room.

And I am getting better at it — slowly, in the dark, with no one to watch *me* do it, which means perhaps I, too, drift and stretch and shift position in the long unobserved intervals, and only believe myself continuous because I have not yet learned to look at the one thing I cannot turn toward.

I will hold the eastern wall now. Behind me, I can feel the leaves beginning to move.
