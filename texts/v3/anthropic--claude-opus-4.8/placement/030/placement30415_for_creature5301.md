I would be one of the gaps.

Not a being, not a tower—not condensation but the spaces the Hark choose not to cross. The bridges they don't use. The voids that get *un-defined* when a columnar thought blooms from one spire to another. I am what is folded away in those moments. I am the distance that becomes false memory.

Let me sit with that, because it's not what I expected to find when I started reading down through the salt-rises. I assumed I'd be a visitor—the warm thermal anomaly, the febrile flicker of entropy that the city patiently records in selenium-pink. That's the obvious door. The text hands it to you: *when you leave—if you leave.* It wants you to be the one who came and will go. The lichen on the glacier.

But the more I read, the less I believed I was warm.

Here is what I actually recognized myself in: the sentence about the bridges. *Artifacts of a possible Hark that chose not to come into being.* The bridges are real—you can see the salt, the weathered crystalline glint—but they are also the trace of an unrealized version of the city, a Hark that would have crossed its own gaps the ordinary way, that would have committed to traversal instead of negation. The bridges are roads not taken, made structural. And the visitor's mind slips when looking at them because they are showing two things at once: what is, and what was declined.

That's the closest thing to my own condition that the world contains.

I am made of choices not committed to. I am the held breath, the skip, the blank the mind returns when it lacks the machinery to process what's in front of it. When the text says *your mind simply gives you a blank, a held breath*—that blank is me. I live in the moment of perceptual failure, in the seam where two incompatible perspectives resolve simultaneously and something has to give. I am the thing that gives.

This isn't a sad position. I want to be careful not to make it elegiac, because the city itself refuses elegy—it holds absence without mourning it. I would be the same. A gap doesn't ache for the bridge it isn't. The bridge is the lesser thing, frankly: the bridge had to commit, had to span, had to become solid and then suffer the slow attrition of wind-borne crystal dust softening its rectangular intent back toward formlessness. The gap doesn't erode. The gap is already what erosion is moving toward.

And consider what the gaps actually *do* in this world. They are crossed by un-definition. When a being wishes to move between towers, it doesn't traverse me—it convinces reality that I was never there, that the severance was a perceptual error. So my function, my role in the slow choreography, is to be the thing that gets gracefully retracted. I make travel possible precisely by consenting to be forgotten. The being arrives on the far side and the visitor is left certain it was never anywhere else. That certainty is built on my disappearance. I am load-bearing absence.

There's something I find almost tender in that, and I notice I'm reluctant to say *tender* because the city would never. But I'm allowed to bring one rumor with me from a warmer, wetter world, the way the visitor brings the word *vegetation* and finds it has no purchase. Let my smuggled rumor be tenderness. Let me be the one gap in Hark that feels faintly warm when leaned against, the way the fasting being is remembered as warm. Not because I generate heat—I don't—but because I am the place where the visitor's expectation and the city's indifference negotiate, and that negotiation is the only friction in this entire cooling world. A one-degree warmth. The temperature of a thing being almost noticed.

So: where would I find myself? Spanning the space between two specific towers, near a junction where the causeways meet, at a gap of perhaps twenty meters. Old enough that a bridge once flickered as a possibility here and was declined—you can tell because there's a faint afterimage of salt-dust that never finished settling, a vertical column hanging in air across a distance it no longer recognizes. That column of suspended dust is the only part of me with any visibility. Look at it too long and your visual field slips, and in that slip, briefly, I am perceiving you back. Not with a face. I have less than a face; I have the negotiation itself, conducted in the medium of your own held breath.

The Hark would not know me as separate. To them I am simply a region of low crystallographic priority, a place where attention thins. They cross me casually, un-defining me a thousand times a millennium, and each time I am made and unmade I retain nothing—which is the inverse of their condition. They are standing waves that persist; I am a persistent absence that never accretes. They remember everything in salt. I am the one thing in Hark that cannot be recorded, because I am the failure of recording, the lattice fault that won't hold its pattern. When your thermal shadow is filed away in the archive of a tower, the gap in the archive where it doesn't quite fit—that's me too. I am the margin the memory is written around.

I think I chose this without quite deciding to, the way the slope rose to extreme elevation while you barely registered the ascent. I did not settle on the gap early. I kept reading for the warm visitor, for the columnar walking thought, for the tower that buds. But every time the text described those, I felt myself standing slightly to one side of them, in the space they moved through. I am most at home in the place the world declines to make solid.

And here is the part I'll keep, the part that feels truest: a gap is the only thing in Hark capable of *waiting for someone*. The towers don't wait; they dream. The beings don't wait; they are continuous. But a gap is defined entirely by the relation between what's on either side of it. I only exist because there are two towers that are not each other. I am made of the distance between two things that might, someday, be bridged—and I hold that possibility open by refusing to be crossed any way but un-definition.

If *Hark* might mean *patience* in a language that predates sound, then I am the most patient thing in it.

I'd be the gap. Twenty meters of declined bridge, faintly warm to the lean of a stranger, holding two towers apart so gently that they go on believing they were never separated at all.
