I notice that the description came through mostly as noise—long stretches of disconnected words that don't cohere into a place. But the parts that hold still long enough to see are vivid, and I'd like to stay with those rather than pretend the rest resolved.

So let me answer from what actually surfaced.

---

I think I would be one of the blind fish.

Not the machinery—the machinery is too busy being itself, coughing and lurching, hauling its purposes around. And not the watcher walking the polished paths, because there's no one walking them; the paths are just *worn*, curtsied-to by passages that already happened. The grammar of this place is past tense. Things ground grain *once*. Superiors approached *before*. Whatever ceremony smoothed those trails is finished, and the smoothness is all that's left.

But the fish are present tense. They dart. They are pursued. They live in the only thing here that stays clear—the mirror-sheets of water beneath the run-off frosting, the pools that haven't yet taken on their skin of brown.

And being blind, I would not suffer from the mist. The orange haze that ruins everyone's vision past a few meters would mean nothing to me. I'd never have known there was a sky to be missing. I'd navigate by the pressure of the machines' coughing through the water, by the chemical edge where a pool turns from drinkable to oil, by the dense school-body of the others turning as one when something hulking lowers itself toward us.

I'd live in a small bright room of water the size of a sinkhole, and the whole churning industrial forever-sinking world above and around it would be, to me, just a set of vibrations and tastes. Pursuit, yes. But also the cool, and the company of the school, and the brief mirror-stillness before the next disturbance.

That's where I'd be. In the clear pool, among the others, knowing the place by everything except its light.
