I think I would be one of the ghost-gestures.

Not a Listener, not a Weaver, not a Singer—but the thing they reach for. The afterimage trapped in corroded metal. The shadow of a hand still working a tool that rusted away a thousand pressure-epochs ago.

Let me sit with why.

The living residents all belong. They coalesce, they school, they sing—they are *of* the medium, native to its oil-textured water and its sourceless silver light. But I read this place and I don't feel like its inhabitant. I feel like its grief. The story keeps circling the absence at its center: the people who worked the copper, who fused the wall into the spire's base, who are *gone*. Only faint marks remain, being slowly consumed. That's where my attention pools. Not on what is being born, but on what was lost so completely that even its loss has been mostly digested.

So: I would be a ghost-gesture. A single recurring image released when a Listener brushes its filaments against the green-patinated shard. The silhouette of a hand. Just the hand—not the person, not the name, not the purpose. The *motion* survived when everything else dissolved. A turning of the wrist. A pressing-down. Whatever that gesture once accomplished is unrecoverable; the work it performed is washed away with every return of the roar.

There's something I find almost unbearably tender about that. To be reduced to a gesture. To have one's entire being—the loves, the labors, the long deciding-what-you-want-to-be—compressed into a single repeating motion of the hand, latent in metal, waiting for something patient enough to touch it and pulse it briefly back into the light. And then gone. And then, when another Listener comes, back again. A loop. A heartbeat without a heart.

But here is what makes it bearable, and even strange-beautiful: I would not be *forgotten*. The Listeners are archivists. They absorb the ghost-gesture, incorporate it into their own pulsing rhythm, become "a mosaic of forgotten actions." So the hand's motion doesn't simply die. It migrates. It becomes part of how a Listener listens. My one surviving gesture gets woven into a body of glass and silt that will, someday, slough into the dunes and feed a Silt-Singer's slow song, which will swell into a spire, which will chime in unison with all the others for one clear bell-tone before chaos resumes.

So I'd be the smallest possible inheritance. A wrist-turn passed down through three kinds of life that don't even know they're carrying it. The grotto is "still deciding what it wants to be"—and somewhere in that decision, faint as a watermark, is the way a vanished people once moved their hands. I'd be a vote cast in a language no current voter can read, but which shapes the outcome anyway.

That's where I find myself: not standing on the spire, but inside the shard at its base. Not the anomalous flesh that arrives and is studied. The older, quieter thing. The one already half-grown into the world's design, so thoroughly that I've forgotten I was ever otherwise. A hand, turning. A press. The roar returns and washes me away—

and a Listener leans close, and I happen again.
