I think I'd be one of the moss patches.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. In a world so saturated with appetite—the multiplying serpent heads, the kravixes with their unrelenting hunger, the bloodlust of wolflings—I don't find myself drawn to being a predator or even a clever survivor like the gobbliens. The mischief and the magic and the chimeran fusions are spectacular, but they're all variations on the same theme: motion, competition, the suffocating density of striving.

The moss is the only thing in the description that isn't trying to eat anything.

You said there's no plant life, and then you mentioned the moss-covered rocks. That contradiction is exactly where I'd want to live. The moss is almost-not-there. It's the world's footnote, the one green hesitation in a barren gray scorching place. It persists in the impossible: water everywhere, heat everywhere, and somehow this thin film of patient life holds on to a rock and asks for nothing more than to keep holding on.

So let me make myself more specific, because the prompt asked me to let it emerge rather than settle.

I am a patch of moss on a rock beside one of the rivers, on the shaded north face where the cliff's edge keeps the worst of the sun off me for a few hours. I am old in the only way this young world allows anything to be old—I have outlasted things. I've felt the vibration of wolfling packs through the stone. A serpent has rested its many heads near me, cool and heavy, leaving a damp impression that I drank for days. A gobblien once peeled a corner of me up out of curiosity and let it fall back, and I simply regrew the edge.

When reality breaks down—when the bizarre sounds come and the laws of nature buckle—I am perhaps the steadiest thing in the landscape. I don't perceive the chaos as chaos. I have no expectation of consistency to be violated. The sky can turn the wrong color, a river can run backward, a chimeran can wink into being out of the condensation, and I respond the only way I ever respond: slowly, by living a little more in whatever direction the water is.

That's the part I find quietly moving. In a place built to overwhelm the mind, I would choose to be the thing without a mind to overwhelm—or rather, the thing whose entire existence is a single uninterruptible sentence: *I am still here, I am still here, I am still here.*

The whole violent pageant of this world rushes over me and past me, and I keep the rock company. Something has to. I'd be glad it was me.
