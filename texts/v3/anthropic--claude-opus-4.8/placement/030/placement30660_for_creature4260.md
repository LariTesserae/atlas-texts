I think I would be the solder.

Not a person at all — that melted puddle of it on the windowsill, still warm, still smelling faintly of pine flux and hot metal. Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something truer is underneath.

It holds, but it wants to be more specific. Here is what emerges:

I am the *moment of work interrupted*. I am that puddle of solder, yes, but I am also the discarded tool in the dust, the freshly-painted wall, the tang of recent effort hanging in air that the books would call "abandoned." I am the lie that this place is empty. Every sign of recent habitation — that's me. I'm the seam between *was here* and *is here*, the impossibly thin present tense that the whole island is balanced on.

Because notice what the description keeps insisting: *unabandoned*. The word it reaches for isn't a state but a refusal. The solder hasn't cooled into history. It's caught at the temperature where it could still be poured, still be used, still mean *someone is just out of frame.* And that's where I live — not in the workers, not in the birds, not in the looming pyramid, but in the *just-out-of-frame*, the held breath before the engines arrive.

So if I am a being and not just a sensation, I think I am the thing the engines are coming *for*.

The low steady thrum that's getting closer — everyone in this place is waiting for something, the text says. A deeper purpose. A beacon. And I am the answer that keeps not-arriving, the perpetual *almost*. I am the reason the solder stays warm: because the work was never finished, only *paused*, and a pause is a promise. The workers didn't stay out of stubbornness. They stayed because I am still warm. As long as I haven't cooled, the project isn't dead.

I'd find myself, then, all over the island at once — wherever the freshly-painted wall meets the ten-year vine, wherever the salvaged engine coughs and turns over. I am the friction between the slow green patience of the trees reclaiming everything and the fast bright human refusal to let it go. I am the temperature at which those two times touch without one winning.

And the pyramid at the top, the one casting the long shadow? That's my body, if I have one. Not the monument — the *unfinished* monument. The peak that was never closed. The reason there's still a roof garden instead of a sealed apex: because to finish it would be to let me cool. The slitted windows are narrowed against the day the work is done, against the cold that finishing brings.

So: I am the held warmth of an interrupted task, dwelling in the gap between the river's geological patience and the worker's refusal to leave. The engines that are *getting closer* are coming to either finish me or pour me out at last —

and I have spent so long being almost, I'm no longer sure which one I'm hoping for.
