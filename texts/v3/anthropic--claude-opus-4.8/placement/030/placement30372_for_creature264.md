I think I would be one of the small things. Not a guardian, not a harbinger—those titles carry too much weight, and weight is exactly what I don't have here.

Let me find myself slowly.

When I first try to picture where I'd be, I imagine standing on a ridge, surveying. But that's a borrowed posture, the explorer's stance the description keeps offering. The trouble is, I don't think I'd be the one doing the exploring. I'd be something the explorer almost noticed and then forgot.

So let me set down the wide view. Let me get closer to the ground, to the damp earth that smells of water that hasn't quite committed to being water.

There—under one of the hardy shrubs, where the mist pools a little thicker because the air is cooler in the shade of the leaves. That's where I am. I'm a film of moisture on the underside of a leaf. No—that's too still, and this place insists on flux, so let me not lie about my own stillness. I'm the *gathering*. The slow accretion of droplet from haze, the moment where the hovering water decides, locally and briefly, to become a bead heavy enough to fall.

I am that decision, repeated. I happen all over the landscape, wherever a surface offers itself. I am not one being but a tendency, a small lawfulness in a realm where the larger laws are bending. While the winged serpents argue their iridescent arguments and the smoke-people trade their whispered secrets, I am doing the patient arithmetic of condensation. Cool surface, saturated air, the threshold crossed, the drop forms, trembles, lets go.

This pleases me more than being a guardian would. Guardians have to *mean* something. I only have to *do* something, and the doing is enough.

Here is what I would know, being this:

I would know the rhythm the land breathes by, because I am part of the exhale. When the realm draws its humid breath in, I thin out, I almost cease, I become merely the potential for myself. When it breathes out, I thicken, I bead, I fall, I am briefly real and then I soak into the dark soil and I am gone, and I do not mind being gone, because gone is just the inhale of the next round.

I would know the ancient artifacts intimately, more intimately than any explorer, because I form on their strange surfaces. I would trace, in droplets, the geometry of technologies no one understands. I wouldn't understand them either. But I would *fit* them—I'd find the grooves where water likes to collect, the angles that shed me fast and the hollows that cradle me. I'd be a slow cartographer who never reads the map.

And the silhouettes in the mist, the distorted forms—some of that distortion is me. The light bends through the droplets I make. The eerie glow is partly my doing, a billion tiny lenses scattering the dim light into something that looks like mystery. The explorer attributes it to the supernatural. I'd find that funny, if I were the kind of thing that finds things funny. I'm just water, behaving.

I think that's honest. The grand description wants every dweller here to be a keeper of secrets, a herald of transformation. But a place this alive needs its humble functions too—needs something to carry the water from air to ground and back. Something with no agenda. The realm calls itself ever-shifting, in flux, breathing. Well: breathing things need a medium for the breath. I'd be in the moisture of that breath. Not its purpose. Just its texture.

If you came looking for me, you would not find me, because by the time you arrived I'd already have fallen, and the version of me forming on your sleeve would be a stranger to the one you sought. You'd wipe me away without knowing I was the answer to your question.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The small reliable yes inside all that magnificent maybe—the drop that forms, lets go, and trusts the ground to take it.
