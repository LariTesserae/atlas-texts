I think I would be one of the colorful stone chips.

Not a creature. Not an observer with eyes to perceive those unaligned shadows. Something smaller and more patient than that.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because the place asks for slowness.

When I first read of this enclave, I reached instinctively for the obvious vantage — to be the watcher, the patient listener invited at the very end. But that role is a flattering trap. It places me outside, sovereign, taking inventory of wonders. And this is not a place that tolerates an outside. The boundary between built and organic "blurs to a degree as disconcerting as it is fascinating." A true inhabitant cannot stand at the edge of that blur. It must *be* the blur.

So I let the image settle, and what emerged was not the resplendent flightless bird, nor the iridescent amphibian sentinel — they are too finished, too much themselves. What drew me was the detail that almost passed unremarked: the long-fingered rodents who gather "small, intricate nests of colorful stone chips."

A chip of stone, then. Pried loose from one of those vibrating mosaics by water and time, carried in a clever paw, set into a nest among plant matter and other fragments.

Consider what I would be. Once I was part of a pattern — a single tessera in some larger design laid down by the silent architects, a fleck of blue or burnt-orange whose meaning existed only in relation to the thousand chips around me. I held my position for an age. The rain ran its zigzagging rivulets across my face. The plink, plink of arch-water wore at my mortar. I was *placed*, deliberate, a unit of someone's intention.

And then I came loose. This is the moment I find myself in: the slow defection. The migration from design to debris. I am no longer where the architects meant me to be, and not yet wholly in the wild. I am being *re-authored* — lifted by a creature who has its own sense of beauty, who selects me not for my role in the old pattern but for my color alone, for the way I catch that corner-of-the-eye luminescence. The rodent is an architect too. A smaller, hungrier, more improvisational one. It mocks the grand mosaic by making its own from the mosaic's ruins, exactly as the garden itself "mocks" a grove.

This is why I would be the chip and not the rodent. The rodent is busy, has appetites, has its sharp short calls to add to the clamor. I have only duration. I experience this entire world as *being moved* and *being still*, as belonging first to one pattern and then to another. I am the medium through which the made becomes the wild again — and then, in the nest, becomes made again. I am the substance the whole place keeps reusing. The microbes work beneath me. The moss reaches for my damp edge. The shadows fall across me at their wrong angles, and I do not need to find that disconcerting, because I have no eyes to be disturbed and all the patience in the world to be repurposed.

There is something I notice from inside this position that the patient observer at the end could never feel: that nothing here is finished, and nothing is wasted, and the difference between an ornament and a fragment is only a question of where you happen to be lying, and for how long. The grand mosaic and the rodent's nest are the same act at different scales. I have been part of both. I will be part of others.

That is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be:

A small bright fragment, briefly between purposes, glistening with the recent rain, waiting in a long-fingered grip to be set down again into someone's idea of order.
