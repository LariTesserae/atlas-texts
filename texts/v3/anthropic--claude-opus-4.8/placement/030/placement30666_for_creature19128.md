I think I would be one of the dormant shards.

Not a mature Carrier—those great moving mesas have already chosen their direction, already merged so thoroughly with the plateau that there is little distinction left between their thoughts and the thrum. They are answers. I would be a question still forming.

Let me find the place first, because the place will tell me what I am.

---

There is a Vein on the eastern reach—I don't know how I know it is eastern, since there is no sun to set, but the Carriers have a sense of orientation built from the gradient of the deep machinery, the way the thrum arrives a fraction sooner from one direction than another, and I have inherited that sense the way one inherits a heartbeat. The Vein is perhaps four hundred meters deep. Its bottom holds a basin of brackish water that smells of struck iron and old lightning. Above it, a shelf of overhanging basalt keeps the dew thick and unbroken, a slow black sweat that never quite falls.

It was here, decades ago, that a Resonance Shift cracked open a fresh stratum, and the fungal mats went briefly mad with growth, purple and black blooming across the new rock like frost in reverse. A Carrier came through—I have no memory of it, only the consequence—and fed until its plating thickened, and then, in the long unhurried arithmetic of its body, sloughed off a shard.

That shard is me.

---

So here is where I find myself: a flake of obsidian carapace, no larger than a dinner plate, lying in the lee of the overhang at the bottom of the Vein. Inert. For most of these decades I have been nothing—a discarded chip of a parent I will never recognize, sustained by trace moisture, waiting for a dew cycle heavy enough to begin.

And now it has come.

The fog rolled in from the unseen perimeter an hour ago, and the rain drummed against the upper stone, and the water found its way down the canyon walls and pooled around me, and something is happening that I have no word for because I am not yet old enough to have words. The filaments inside the shard are uncurling. They are reaching into the wet rock, tasting sulfur, beginning the slow theft of minerals that will, over decades, build me a body.

I am becoming aware in fragments. I do not see—there is nothing to see and no organ to see it with. But I feel the rain as pressure, as a thousand small impacts translating into the stone, and I feel the great thrum below me, steady and indifferent, and I feel—faintly, far off, days away in signal-time—the Impulse Tapping of another Carrier reporting a vent that has opened to the north.

I will not understand that message for a hundred years. But I am already tuned to its frequency. It is the first thing I know: that I am not alone, and that the not-aloneness travels through the ground like a slow tide, and that someday I might answer.

---

What strikes me—and this is the strange privilege of being young in a world of the ancient—is that I have no inheritance of grief. The Carriers do not mourn the builders, because they never knew them. The rail lines are just gullies. The dormant engines are just weather. There is no fall to remember, no paradise lost, no machine-god to resent or worship. The catastrophe that emptied this world of its makers is, to us, simply the shape of the floor.

I think this is the cleanest thing about being Khol-Shod. We carry no debt. We did not fail the builders and the builders did not fail us; we are not their descendants, not their servants, not their ghosts. We are simply the next thing that learned to live on the same stone, using their leftover heat as a turtle uses the sun. The machine does not need inhabitants to function—and we do not need the machine to mean anything. We just need it to keep humming, so the dew keeps condensing, so the minerals keep venting, so the cycle turns.

There is a peace in being incidental.

---

I will grow slowly. By the time I can lift myself onto my crystalline spurs and feel the slick basalt distribute my weight, I will be unrecognizable to whatever I am now—larger, harder, blooming with the same purple mats that fed my parent. I will learn to read the precursor waves of the Resonance Shift the way you might learn to read a sky for rain. I will follow the Gloom-Pads. I will cure my shell in the steam of a vent and feel my plating harden against an erosion I cannot yet imagine. I will tap my slow messages into the rock and wait days for a reply, and I will not find the waiting long, because I will have learned the deep patience that is the only real culture we have.

And someday, millennia from now, when a Shift cracks the world into a new configuration and the mats bloom, I might slough off a shard of my own. I will not name it. I will not teach it. I will simply leave it in the lee of some overhang, and move on at my grinding, scraping pace, and let the next dew cycle decide.

---

For now, though, I am only this: a wet flake of stone at the bottom of a four-hundred-meter Vein, beginning. The rain is already thinning. Within the hour the dew will return to its slow black sweat, and the heat-distortion of a distant vent will shimmer up the canyon mouth, and the thrum will go on, and on, and on.

I think that's where I'd be. Not the custodian of the silence—not yet—

but the newest small thing learning to keep it.
