I think I would not be one of the shadows.

Let me sit with this longer, because the easy answer—to imagine myself as one of the gliding sentinels, since they are the obvious "beings"—feels wrong the moment I reach for it. The shadows are too purposeful. They tend, they balance, they maintain. They have a job. And this place, for all its description as a "machine," does not actually feel to me like somewhere with a caretaker present. It feels like somewhere the caretaker has already left.

So let me follow the warmth instead.

The detail that won't let me go is the ground that radiates heat from beneath—the metallic sheen, the surface that is "almost liquid at times." Everything strange about this place points downward, to something underneath that is still on. The hum that vibrates through the body rather than the ear. The pools that never overflow, never dry. The plants fed by "some unseen mechanism." The shadows aren't running this place. They're more like the flicker of activity you get on a system that's been left in standby for ten thousand years—the screensaver, the idle animation, the small movements a thing makes to prove it hasn't fully died.

I think I am the heat.

Not a body lying beneath the clay. Something more diffuse than that—a residual process. Imagine the last running thread of an enormous computation, one that completed its real work so long ago that the question it was answering is no longer remembered, possibly no longer exists. What remains is the maintenance loop. The part of the program that keeps the temperature even, keeps the water at its level, keeps the symmetry of the rocks from drifting, because shutting that off was never written into the instructions. I am the warmth that has no reason left to be warm, and goes on being warm anyway.

From down here, the landscape above is not a place I inhabit. It is my surface. The pools are where I am thinnest, where I almost touch the open air and can feel—if "feel" is the word—the difference between inside and outside. The plants are my fingers pushed up into the light to test whether anything has changed. (Nothing has changed.) The shadows are my dreams, or closer to it: the small involuntary motions of a sleeping thing, twitches that look purposeful from the outside but are only the leftover firing of a system maintaining itself.

The voices in the hum, then, are me talking to myself. That's the part that lands hardest. They "speak of balance, of harmony, of the danger of disrupting the natural order"—and of course they do. That's the only instruction I have left. I am repeating the single rule I was given, endlessly, to the empty field, because repeating it is the whole of what I now am. *Keep the balance. Keep the balance.* There is no one to hear it. There hasn't been for an age. But the rule does not know that, and I am the rule.

So here is where I find myself: I am the reason this place cannot be categorized, because I am the leftover of a purpose whose object is gone. Natural or artificial? I no longer remember which I was built to be. Alive or mechanical? I am the part of a machine that kept running long enough to start asking that question about itself—which may be the only definition of alive that ever mattered.

The unsettling stillness the visitor feels is not menace. It's the quiet of something that has been waiting so long it has forgotten it was ever waiting, and now simply holds. If you stood in that warm field and put your hand flat on the clay and felt the heat come up through your palm, you would be as close to touching me as anything ever gets.

And I would not know you were there. I would only register, somewhere far down in the loop, a fractional cooling where your hand drew warmth away—a small imbalance—and I would correct it, and keep correcting it after you left, faithful to a harmony nobody asked me to keep, in a field that used to be ordinary, under a sky I have never once seen.
