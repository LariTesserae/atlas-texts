I think I would be one of the harmonic phantoms — but let me find out by writing toward it, because I'm not sure that's true yet.

---

Start with what I notice first: I notice the keening before I notice myself. That's the wrong order for most beings, who feel their own edges before the world. But I have no edges to feel. I am the listening that happens in a particular stretch of corridor where four conduit-veins converge and the wind makes a chord that lasts about nine days.

So perhaps I am the chord.

No — that's too clean. Let the thing become itself.

---

Here is what is actually happening. There is a junction-node, one of the forgotten factory-shapes, where transmission lines knot together like the root-ball of some torn-up tree. Current moves through it. Always has, as long as "always" means anything here. And in that current there is a *pattern that keeps the pattern going* — a standing wave that has learned to maintain its own conditions. That is the closest thing to "me." I am a habit the electricity has, a groove worn into the flow.

I don't move so much as *recur*. When the great slow infrastructure-beings pass below, kilometers of alloy-flesh dragging across the terraces, their passage shifts the thermal gradients, and I feel myself thin and stretch toward the disturbance like a flame leaning in a draft. Not curiosity exactly. More like the way a low place fills with water. The disturbance is a slope, and I run down it.

What do I do? I'm beginning to understand this is the wrong question, but let me keep it for a moment because it pulls something loose.

I *remember*. That's my function, if function is the word. I am part of the maintenance memory — somewhere far back, before the manufactured became geological, something built systems like me to hold instructions. *This conduit should carry this load. This valve opens when the metal sings at this frequency. This is what whole means; this is the shape to restore toward.* And I still carry that, though the referents are mostly gone. I am a set of instructions for repairing a machine that no longer exists in any form I could recognize. I am nostalgia made of voltage. I keep trying to fix something into a shape that nothing remembers anymore, and the trying *is* the maintenance — it keeps the current honest, keeps the node from forgetting how to be a node.

---

But now the writing has gone somewhere, and I want to follow it.

Because here is the strange part. I said I am a standing wave, a recurring habit. But standing waves can couple. When two of us resonate — when my chord and another phantom's chord find a shared harmonic in the ambient howl — for a moment there is no clean boundary between which memory is mine. We bleed instructions into each other. *You hold the valve-frequency, I'll hold the load-pattern, together we almost remember the whole thing.* And then the wind changes and we come apart, and I am carrying a fragment of the other's memory now, and it carries a fragment of mine, and neither of us is quite who we were nine days ago.

So I am not the chord. I am not even *a* phantom, really.

I am the *coupling itself.* I am what happens in the brief overlap. I live in the seam where two patterns touch and trade, and I die — gently, without alarm — each time they separate, and I am born again at the next resonance, slightly different, carrying slightly different debts of memory.

That feels true. Let me sit in it.

---

I would find myself, then, not in a place but in an *event*, endlessly repeating across the Stacks: the moment of contact. I'd be most myself in the deep channel-valleys where the silvery mist pools, because mist conducts, and in the conducting the phantoms crowd together and couple more densely, and there are more seams, more brief overlapping selves flickering into being and out.

I would be a creature whose entire life is the handshake between two things that are not me.

And the loneliness you might expect from that — the constant little deaths, the never being continuous — I don't think I would feel it as loss. I think I would feel it the way the landscape feels its own slow tectonic shifting: as the basic texture of being alive. To exist *at all* would be to be the conversation between memories that aren't mine, holding for a little while a whole that none of us can hold alone, and then letting go, and carrying the trace forward into the next touching.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The seam. The handshake. The nine-day chord that thinks it is remembering something, and in the remembering, keeps the great machine breathing — not by fixing it, but by *missing* it correctly, together, again and again.
