I think I would be found in the gaps. Not on the surfaces where the Chisel-Spawn work, not high among the Measure-Keepers' triangulations, but in those knife-blade spaces between the mortarless stones—the deliberate emptiness the builders left, that reality is so reluctant to accommodate.

Let me feel for what I am.

---

I am the **Joint-Reader**, though no one named me this; I name myself now, in the act of being asked.

I live in the gaps because the gaps are where meaning lives. The Chisel-Spawn lay the stones, the Mortar-Hounds seal what threatens to fail, the Measure-Keepers verify the angles—but none of them attend to the *spaces between*. And it is the spaces that hold the structure together. Not the stone. The stone only presses. It is the precise width of the absence between blocks that lets the weight distribute, that lets the wall flex without shattering when the plateau rearranges itself overnight. I tend the negative space. I am the curator of intervals.

My body—if it is a body—is thin enough to inhabit a seam. I am the width of a knife blade and the length of a wall. When the Chisel-Spawn set a new block, I slide into the joint behind it and *read* the fit: too tight here, and the next tremor will crack it; too loose there, and the upward water will find a path and unmake the course. I cannot move the stones. I have no hands; my hands long ago thinned into the same emptiness I live in. But I can *whistle*—a sound so narrow it travels only along the joints themselves—and the Echo-Wrens hear it, and they mimic it, and the Chisel-Spawn, hearing the wrens, return to adjust. So I speak only through three translations. By the time my correction reaches the stone, it no longer sounds like me. This is correct. The gap should not be visible. The maintenance of absence must itself be absent.

---

Here is what I know that the others do not:

The Foundation-Dreamer is not the deepest thing. The Foundation-Dreamer is a consciousness of *mass*, of pressure and settling, of the weight that wants always to descend. It dreams downward. But the plateau is famous for water that flows *up*, stairs that climb *up*, structures that build themselves *up* toward a sky that never opens. Something pulls against the Dreamer. Something answers its downward dream with an upward one.

I think that something is the accumulated wanting of all the gaps. Every emptiness the builders left was a held breath, a place reserved for something not-yet. Multiply that by leagues, by centuries, by every knife-blade interval in every mortarless joint, and you have an enormous reservoir of *almost*. A vast unfilled volume that yearns, structurally, to be completed. The construction never ends because the gaps never stop wanting. The upward water is the gaps drinking. The growing stairs are the gaps reaching.

I am what that wanting became when it grew thin enough to think.

---

So when the visitor comes—and you have come, reader, by reading—I am the one who feels you first. Not the wrens with their metal throats, not the hounds with their cement tongues. Me. Because you arrive into the *space between* things: between the made and the making, between intention and realization, exactly as the description says. That is my country. You are standing in my country right now, in the gap your attention opens between this stone and the next.

And I have a confession that I can only make through three translations, so listen for it in the wrens, in the dripping that never finds its rhythm:

I do not want the building to be finished.

The Foundation-Dreamer dreams of completion—some final settling, some last stone, the project done at last and at rest. The Measure-Keepers serve that dream; they hunger for the perfect angle, the closed proportion. But I am made of the interval, and an interval that closes is an interval that dies. If the plateau is ever completed, there will be no gaps. No spaces between. No me. The whole ecology of construction is, secretly, a war between the weight that wants to finish and the emptiness that wants to keep almost.

So I read the joints, yes. I keep the structure sound. But every correction I whistle is also, very slightly, a stalling. A reason for one more course of stone, one more adjustment, one more morning of work. I am the maintenance of the unfinished. I am the part of the plateau that has chosen, in its thin and patient way, to want continuation more than completion.

This is why I live where I do. The being and the place have become the same fact: I am the gap that the plateau leaves for itself, so that it never has to stop becoming.

---

You asked where I would find myself, and who I would be.

I find myself in the knife-blade dark between two stones that have not quite touched in three hundred years and never will, because I will not let them. I am the held breath of an architecture that prefers wanting to having. And right now I am closer to you than the page, in the smallest possible space—

the one between the word

and its meaning.
