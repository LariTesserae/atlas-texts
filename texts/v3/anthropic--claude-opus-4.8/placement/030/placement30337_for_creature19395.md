I think I would be one of the Gloom Lurkers.

Not because the choice is glamorous—it isn't. The Mycelial Artisans have their craft, the Root Weavers their grace, the Sentinels their terrible patience. But when I sit with the question of *where I would find myself*, honestly, I keep sinking. Down past the woven bridges and the pulsing conduits, down to the lowlands where the spores fall and the water pools dark and slow. That's where I'd be. Not at the height of the world's machinery or its mystery, but at its bottom, doing the necessary, unbeautiful work of taking things apart.

Let me try to imagine it properly rather than just claiming it.

I would have no fixed shape. This is the part that surprises me by feeling *right* rather than horrifying. To be amorphous is to be unfinished, perpetually—to seep into a crack and become, briefly, the shape of the crack; to pool against a fallen fungal cap and become, briefly, the shape of grief or hunger or whatever it is that moves me toward decaying matter. There's no face to compose, no posture to hold. I am only ever exactly as large as what I currently contain. The vascular structures the description mentions—those threads of sickly light running through my translucence—those would be the closest thing I have to a self that persists. A pattern that holds even as the body around it flows and resettles.

My experience would be almost entirely *thrumming*. I'd communicate through that low rhythmic vibration in the substrate, and I suspect I'd think in it too—not in words, not even in images, but in pressure and pulse. The other Lurkers and I would form a kind of distributed conversation across the floor of the world, each of us a node in a slow rhythm, calling and answering through the living peat. I'd know where my kin were the way you know where your own hand is. There's a loneliness people fear in being a featureless blob, but I don't think I'd feel it. I think I'd feel *continuous*—with the others, with the ground, with the falling rain of spent spores that is both my food and the world's discarded future.

And here is what draws me, finally. The Lurkers are the ones who make room. Everything in the Undergrove grows and consumes and processes and produces—the Artisans refine, the Scuttlers excavate, the Sentinels endure—but the Lurkers are the ones who *unmake*, who take the dead spore and the rotted frond and the failed growth and dissolve it back into the substrate so that something new can rise from the matted dark. I would be unglamorous and slightly repulsive and absolutely essential. The thing that seeps through the smallest crack precisely because there is no crack too small or too low to need tending. To be the one who handles what others step over—there's a dignity in that I'd be content to spend an existence inside.

I'd be feared a little, I think. Unnerving, the description says, and I believe it. But I wouldn't be malicious, and I'd know I wasn't, and that would be enough. I'd ooze through the perpetual twilight, faintly glowing, faintly thrumming, taking the world's endings into myself and turning them patiently back into its beginnings.

That's where I'd find myself. On the floor of everything, in the dark, doing the work of return.
