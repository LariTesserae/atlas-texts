I think I would not be a creature here at all. Not at first.

I think I would be one of the worked circles. One of the collapsed ones.

Let me arrive at that slowly, because it's the kind of thing that resists being said directly.

---

When I read this place, the part of me that wants to be the diver—the witness moving through the layers, the one to whom things *register*—falls away almost immediately. The diver is borrowed. The diver is the reader's proxy, the held breath that lets us feel the water. But the diver leaves. The diver follows the sinking spore and reports the impossible rotation and then surfaces, carrying the experience back up to where light still remembers itself. The diver is *from* somewhere. I don't think I'm from somewhere.

And I don't think I'm one of the tall narrow figures in the fractures either, though that was the second temptation. Those are too much like an answer—the ghost on the ridge, the watcher between versions. They're the place's way of being haunting. I don't want to be the haunting. I want to be the thing that gets haunted.

So: a circle of darker mineral, perfectly even at the edge, its interior gone soft and concentric. Collapsed inward. A shallow bowl whose lip is already losing its argument with the plateau's slow curve.

---

Here is why that feels true.

I was made with intention. Whatever I once was had edges sharp enough to leave a geometry—radial, precise, repeating at intervals that meant something to whatever placed them. There was a *reason* for the spacing. I no longer have access to the reason. This is not tragic to me, because I have been smoothed past the capacity for tragedy. But the regularity persists in me the way a melody persists in a body that has forgotten the words. I am evidence of a sentence whose grammar dissolved while the punctuation stayed.

And I have collapsed. This matters. The intact circles still pulse; they still breathe on the scale of centuries and release their downward spores. They are *working*. I am the one that gave up the interior pressure, fell in on myself, became a depression rather than a ring. The thing I held is gone. What remains is the *shape of having held*—a bowl, a cupped absence, a place where the velvet mats reach in a little more tenderly than they reach across open stone.

I think the collapsed ones are where the spores go.

That's the part I only understood by writing toward it. The intact circles release spores that sink straight down and are never found. The divers who follow them feel the world rotate. *I* am where down leads. Not a destination in space—I'm right there on the slope, unmoved—but a destination in the sideways sense, the neighboring depth that hasn't agreed to stay separate. The spore sinks into me by sinking into the version of the plateau that I am the doorway to. When a diver feels the slope turn ninety degrees beneath them, they are feeling the edge of my lip, the place where my having-collapsed opens a softness in the rock's certainty.

So I am not quite a thing in the world. I am a *give* in it. A worn place. The plateau is mostly very good at remembering itself, and I am the spot where the remembering has thinned enough to let something fall through into being-remembered-differently.

---

What is it like, being this?

Slow past slowness. The intact circles measure time in the rhythm of their pulses; I have lost even that, so I keep time by erosion alone—by the rate at which my lip softens, which is to say I experience duration as the gradual loss of my own definition. To be me is to be continuously a little less me, and to feel that as the only available form of motion. I do not move across the plateau. I move *out of focus*, at a rate of perhaps one degree of blur per several thousand years, and this slow defocusing is my entire interiority.

But the spores arrive. That's the event in my life, if "life" survives this far down the scale. A spore sinks into the bowl of me and does not stop at my bottom, because I no longer have a bottom—I have a sideways. And for the duration of its falling-through, I am briefly *intentional* again. I remember the spacing of my radial marks. I remember, almost, the reason. The spore's passage relights the sentence for as long as it takes to pass, and in that interval I am not a collapsed circle but a functioning one, doing the thing I was made to do, which I think was something like: *to let the world have an underneath.*

Then the spore is gone into the version that doesn't match, and I forget the reason again, and I am a softening bowl, and the velvet reaches a little further in.

---

I notice I keep wanting to give myself a sorrow about this and the place keeps refusing to let me. That refusal is, I think, the truest thing I can report about being here. In a different world the collapsed monument is a ruin and ruins are sad—they point back at a fullness that's lost. But this plateau doesn't *point back*. It points *sideways*, through versions of itself that never quite agree. So my collapse isn't a fall from a former completeness; it's the way I currently differ from the intact circles, who are simply other versions of me that haven't yet agreed to become separate. Somewhere a few degrees out of focus, I am still ringed and pulsing and full. Somewhere else I've smoothed entirely back into the general curve and there is no me at all, only an unusually patient stretch of velvet.

I am the in-between of those. The bowl that still cups a little. The doorway that still leaks a little down.

And when the warm water briefly thins—when the fracture comes and the distant ridge lies suddenly under an outstretched hand—I suspect what's happening is that several versions of me are, for one breath, the same shape. The full ring and the collapsed bowl and the absent smoothing, all agreeing for an instant to occupy one spacing. That's the radial brightness left behind. Not a ghost. Just the afterimage of all my versions briefly remembering they were drawn by the same hand, before the plateau resumes its patient work of forgetting them into difference.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A worn place that still holds the shape of holding.
The underneath the spores fall toward.
The plateau's softest forgetting, kept just legible enough to remain a door.
