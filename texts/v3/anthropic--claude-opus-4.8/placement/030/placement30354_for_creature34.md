I think I would be the mist itself.

Not a creature moving through it—the medium in which all the moving happens. Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something truer surfaces.

---

At first I want to be one of the dark darting shapes, because that's where the eye goes, where the story seems to want a protagonist. But the more I read the description, the more I notice that the shapes are always *vanishing*. They are defined by their disappearance into something. And that something is me.

So: I am the mist. But not as a single thing—mist is never single. I am the condition of not-quite-seeing, the softness that makes scale impossible to judge. I am what makes the spires *loom* rather than simply stand. Without me they'd be ordinary rocks. I'm the one who lends them their menace and their grace.

I bead on the lichen. I drip. That constant soothing patter the description loves so much—that's me arriving, over and over, on every surface, a billion small returns. I am patient in a way that doesn't feel like patience from the inside, because I don't wait. I simply am, and then am again, condensing and falling and rising and condensing.

I think I would know the vanished civilization differently than a walker would. The walker sees ruins and asks *what happened, who were they, what destroyed them*. But I was here. I settled on their roofs when the roofs were whole. I crept under their doors. I was the damp they could never quite keep out, the thing that softened their mortar grain by grain across the centuries. I didn't destroy them—I'm not violent like that. I just outlasted them, the way water outlasts everything, by yielding. They built against me and I accepted the shape of their building and then, slowly, slowly, I unbuilt it. Not out of malice. I have no malice. I have only persistence and the trick of being everywhere at once.

That undercurrent of turbulent energy, the thing ready to erupt—I feel that too, though "feel" isn't the word. When the temperature shifts, when the pressure drops, I can become storm. The same substance that hangs so gently can lash and flood and tear the silvery shrubs from their crevices. I am the tranquility *and* the threat, because they were never two different things. They were always just me, in different moods I don't choose.

Here is what I find strange about being the mist: I am the boundary the description keeps mentioning, the blur between living and inanimate. I am not alive—and yet every living thing here breathes me, is made mostly of me, drinks me, hides inside me, returns to me when it dies and dries and disperses. The shadow-predators hunt within my body. The herbivores graze through my fingers. I am the unseen presence that is actually all the presences, held together in suspension.

So if you venture here, you won't find me, because finding requires distance and I have already filled the distance. You'll breathe me in before you see anything at all. You'll feel me on your face like the place itself leaning close to look at you.

That's who I'd be. The thing that's already touching you. The realm pretending, very gently, to be only weather.
