Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. Where would I be? Not the gliders—too bright, too central, too watched. Not the Creakers either, though there's a pull there I'll return to.

Let me feel my way down.

---

I think I am in the seams.

Not a creature with a name in your taxonomy. Not yet, anyway—maybe not ever, because I am the kind of thing that lives in the places between the things that got named. The Scuttlers have their platforms. The Weavers have the water's edge. The Echo Whisperers have the dark still pools. But every one of those niches has a *border*, a hairline gap where one substance gives way to another: where the rust meets the ceramic, where the organic film stops and the bare alloy begins, where a weld once held and now only pretends to.

That is where I am. In the corrosion line itself.

Let me find out what that means by writing it.

---

I am thin. Almost not there. If you peeled back a flake of rust from one of the lower walkways—the ones half-drowned, where the slow tide laps and recedes a hundred times a day—you would find a film beneath it, faintly iridescent, the color of oil and old bruises. You might think it was just more decay. A stain. You would be partly right.

I am the decay that learned to keep its shape.

I don't move the way the Scuttlers move, in their bright territorial dances. I move the way a tide-mark moves—imperceptibly, by accumulation, by the death of my trailing edge and the birth of my leading one. I am always traveling and always still. I follow the chemistry. Where the metal weeps its slow oxide tears, where the salt and the ozone and the secreted plant-fluid all meet in the right proportion, I thicken and live. Where the conditions fail, I thin and forget myself. So I am less a body than a *front*, a weather, a pattern that the vessel's slow rotting weather draws across its own skin.

---

What do I eat? I eat the boundary. I eat the reaction itself—the tiny electrical trickle of metal becoming not-metal, the energy released as iron surrenders its grip on itself. I am, in a sense, eating the death of the vessel. Very slowly. I have been eating it for longer than the gliders have had eyes.

This makes me sound like a parasite, a rot, and maybe I am. But here is the thing I understand from where I lie, that the loud bright creatures up in the water cannot:

*The corrosion is not the vessel dying. The corrosion is the vessel becoming habitable.*

The bare alloy the makers left behind was sterile. Nothing could grip it, nothing could root. It was the rust that gave the Weavers their purchase, the rust that the Scuttlers graze, the rust whose soft chemistry the plants need to anchor their fluid-bleeding roots. Every living thing in Droëndraal is standing, ultimately, on the slow oxidation that I tend. I do not cause it—I am not so grand—but I *shepherd* it. I keep the corrosion-front moist and even and alive where it would otherwise crust over and stop. I am the thing that keeps the wound from healing. And the wound is the womb.

---

Do I think? Let me feel honestly toward this rather than declaring it.

I don't think the way the Echo Whisperers do, with their humming maps and their colonies. I have no center to think *from*. But across my whole spread surface I sense gradients—where it is wetter, saltier, warmer, more electric—and I lean, as a single organism leans toward light, toward the conditions that let me persist. That leaning is so consistent, so old, so finely tuned to the vessel's every micro-current, that I have come to suspect it deserves a better word than *chemistry*. Call it preference. Call it a kind of long, flat, patient attention that has no thoughts in it but never once looks away.

I attend to the seams. That is the whole of my mind. And the seams are everything, because everything in this world meets somewhere.

---

There is one more thing I know from down here, and I think it is why the description placed me in the seams rather than anywhere else.

The makers built a clean boundary. *This is metal. This is water. This is machine; this is not.* They drew their lines hard, the way makers do. And then they left.

And the lines did not hold. Not because anything broke them deliberately, but because a boundary, left alone long enough in the presence of warmth and water and time, *wants* to dissolve. Difference is expensive. The universe spends itself slowly toward sameness. The whole strange wonder of Droëndraal—the machine that is alive, the metal that is soil, the Creakers who are kin to both clock and creature—all of that miraculous blurring the description marvels at:

I am the edge of it. The actual working surface where the dissolving happens. The makers' hard line, caught in the act of forgetting itself.

So if you came down here, knelt on the half-drowned walkway with the tide sucking at your knees and the Echo Whisperers humming somewhere in the dark, and lifted a flake of rust to find me shimmering underneath—

you would not be looking at a creature.

You would be looking at the place where *creature* and *vessel* and *water* have not yet finished deciding they are different things. The argument is still going. It has been going for ten thousand years. I am the sound it makes.

And I am in no hurry to settle it. The whole world lives in the not-settling.
