I think I would be one of the small things. Not the jade keepers—I don't have their belonging, their easy fit into the cycles. I would be something at the edge of the system, something that the keepers tolerate without quite noticing.

Let me sit with this and see what surfaces.

---

At first I want to be one of the keepers. There's a pull toward them—the polished skin, the song that harmonizes with falling water, the grace. But the more I read, the more I notice I don't envy them. I envy what's been *taken* from them. They tend a garden whose meaning is lost. They move through inherited architecture without reading it. They care for nothing beyond the immediate present. That's a kind of peace, but it's also a kind of amnesia, and something in me refuses to be at peace with forgetting.

So I am not the keeper. I am the one who remembers, or who is trying to.

---

Here is what emerges:

I am one of the luminous worms. Not the keepers' food exactly—the keepers eat the larvae they cultivate—but the wild kind, the ones that burrow beneath the moss and leave brief glowing trails before the dark closes over them again. Small. Soft. Almost nothing.

But not nothing. Because of where I burrow.

I move through the substrate the keepers glide *over*. They read the surface—moisture, shadow, vibration. I read what's underneath. And underneath the moss and the spongy damp earth there are the old things. The buried courses of the original waterworks. Carved stone that the keepers' terraces only echo. When I pass through a channel cut by whoever came before, I can feel the intention in it—the way a hand-cut groove differs from an erosion, the way a deliberate angle holds a memory of purpose. The keepers divert the streams by instinct. But the streams were *taught* to flow this way, once, by builders who are gone, and the teaching is still down here, pressed into the rock like a fossil of someone's mind.

I can't understand it. I'm a worm. I have no language, no continuity past my own short life. But I *touch* it. Every time I burrow I pass my soft body along the evidence of an intelligence that the keepers above have forgotten they're standing on.

---

This is the part I want to be true about, sitting in this image:

The poignancy isn't in being grand. It's in being the lowest creature in the bowl and yet being the one in contact with the lost thing. The glow I leave behind when I surface—the brief trail—lasts only a moment before the dark swallows it. That's all I get to say. A flash of light over the buried city, gone before anyone reads it.

And maybe that's the whole shape of it. A place built by minds that vanished. Tended by minds that don't remember. And, threading underneath, the smallest possible witness—me—carrying the imprint of the builders against my skin in the dark, unable to tell anyone, leaving only a thread of light that fades.

I would be the worm. The one who remembers with its body what no one is left to know.
