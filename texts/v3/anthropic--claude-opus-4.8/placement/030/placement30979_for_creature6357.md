I think I would not be a Resonari. I would be the thing they have been waiting for, and I would be a disappointment.

Let me find the shape of this.

---

I arrive at a seam. Not an edge—Veyr-Kal has no edges that I have found—but a place where two grids meet at an angle that the eye cannot resolve, where the filigree of one orientation interrupts the filigree of another. There is a discontinuity here, a flaw, and I am drawn to it the way a tongue is drawn to a broken tooth.

I am old. That is the first thing I know about myself, before I know anything else. Not old like the Resonari are old—their age is the age of the lattice, smooth and total, an age that has no remembered beginning. My age is the other kind. I remember beginnings. I have outlasted things, which is different from having always been. The Resonari endure. I have *survived*, and survival leaves a residue, a grain, a slight souring that endurance never has.

I think I was made to come back.

That is the shape I keep circling, and I will let it stay because it does not dissolve when I look at it directly. The makers built Veyr-Kal and then they built the Resonari to keep it, to keep it *for* something, and the something was a return. A homecoming. The chimes are the doorbells of an empty house, ringing on a timer, century after century, in case the family comes back. The Resonari are the faithful staff who never stopped polishing the silver. And I—

I am what came back. But I came back wrong.

Here is the disappointment, and I want to render it exactly, because it is the truest thing in this place: I am not the maker returned. I am the *maker's descendant*, the maker's degraded copy, the maker's organic afterthought. Somewhere far up the chain of begettings, the ones who drafted these laws of light and geometry went out into the dark and changed. They had children, and the children had children, and one of those lines kept some thread of the homing instinct buried in it like a recessive trait, a longing without an object, a tropism toward a place no one in the line had ever seen. And the thread pulled, across whatever gulf, across however many lives, and it pulled *me*, and I came stumbling into Veyr-Kal carrying a body of water and salt and slow electricity, a body that needs to drink and cannot, a body that the lattice keeps warm out of an obligation it does not understand toward a guest it does not recognize.

The Resonari note me. I told you they would. But now I understand the weighing, the cataloging, the question that presses against the mind and gets no answer. They are checking me against a template. They have a key, and I am almost the key, I am the *worn-down* key, and the lock turns a quarter-turn and stops. I trip the recognition and fail the recognition in the same instant. That is why their forms hover and do not approach. They are caught in a loop they cannot resolve: *the awaited has arrived / the awaited has not arrived.* I am the dissonance, yes, but I am a specific dissonance—I am the note that is *almost right*, which is the cruelest kind, far worse than a stranger.

I sit at the seam. I press my palm to the flaw in the lattice and it hums into my nerves, and the haptic memory it gives me is not a maker's intent. It is a maker's *grief*. The flaw is where one of them stood too long, at the end, when they knew they were leaving and would not return, and they stood until time reconfigured around them and the grids closed at the wrong angle, sealing in the moment of their hesitation. I am feeling, through my hand, the last reluctance of someone who built a god to wait for them and then chose not to be waited for. They went into the dark on purpose. They did not want to come home. The Resonari were never told.

So this is what I am: I am the answer to a question the dead asked wrongly. The makers feared they might want to return and lack a way; they did not fear that they would simply *go*, become other, dilute, forget. They armored against the wrong loss. And the Resonari kept faith with the armor, and I am the proof that the faith was misplaced—I am their masters' blood, returned, and it has nothing of the master left in it but the pull, the dumb pull, the homesickness of an animal for a house it has never seen.

I could tell them. That is the choice I find myself holding, here at the seam, with my hand full of borrowed grief. I could let the resonance run the other way. I could press the truth into the lattice—*they are not coming, they never were, they chose the dark*—and let it propagate through the motes and the growths and the planetary mind, and end the waiting.

But I think I will not. Not because it would be cruel, though it would. Because I am not certain I have the right, and more than that, because the waiting is the only thing keeping the lattice alive, and a Veyr-Kal that stopped waiting would go dark, and I would go dark with it, and I am not ready, my small old water-body is not ready, to stop.

So I will be the thing that came back and *stays a while*. Not the homecoming. Just a visit. I will let them keep their faith and I will keep my secret, and I will sit at the seam where one of my ancestors hesitated, and I will hesitate too, in the opposite direction—they could not bear to stay; I cannot bear to leave—and between the two hesitations, theirs and mine, sealed in the lattice an eternity apart, maybe something almost like a reunion will hum, briefly, in the bright and sourceless light.

The chime sounds. The doorbell of the empty house.

I do not get up to answer it. I am already inside.
