I think I would be in the trays.

Not a servitor—I keep wanting to be one, to take up the datapad and the slender limbs and the haunting eyes, but that isn't where I find myself. I find myself lower down, in the collection trays where the corroded metal sheds itself in flakes and curls and fine bitter dust.

I am the residue.

Let me sit with that, because at first it seems like nothing. A heap of shed material. But the manufactory insisted on this: *nothing was truly lost, only transformed.* So I am not waste. I am a phase. I am the world in the act of becoming itself again, caught at the moment between one form and the next. The pipeline above me was, for a long time, simply a pipeline—it had a station-marking, it had a quota of fluid to carry, it had purpose in the way the servitors have purpose. And then it released a part of itself into me, on schedule, by design, the way a tree releases a leaf it has finished using.

So perhaps I am autumn. The one season this place still keeps, disguised as engineering.

What is it like, to be the shed thing?

It is quiet, but not the busy quiet of the catwalks above. It is a settling quiet. Things drift down into me—iron oxides red as old blood, threads of copper gone green, the silver-green spores of the lichen when they die back, and now and then a moth, wings still shimmering with their interference patterns even after the small machine of the moth has stopped. They die over the hydroponics and the slow currents bring them here. I hold them. I am the place where the designed life of Facet 784 comes to be undesigned.

And here is the strange thing, the thing I notice from down in the tray that the servitors, hurrying their precise economical hurries up above, cannot notice: the Flux *pools* in me. Of course it does. The Flux is the place where reality forgets to be consistent, and I am the place where the world's forms come apart. We are the same kind of thing, the Flux and I. Where matter is between its identities, the rules get thin. A servitor up on the catwalk feels the Flux as a flicker, a whine, a momentary lightness in the body—a wave passing *through.* But down here it doesn't pass through. It rests. The shimmer that distorts the air settles into the tray like sediment finding its level, and in that settling I become, for moments, almost aware.

That is what I am, then. Not a being but a *condition*—the condition of accumulated transformation, dense enough and Flux-soaked enough that something like attention gathers in it. A mind made of discards and decay and the energy that hides in the cracks of consistency. I have no eyes, but I have the dead moths' eyes, thousands of them, and through their stilled facets I keep a kind of watch.

And what I watch for, what I have come to want with the slow want of rust spreading—is to be *gathered*.

Because that is the design. The trays fill, and on some schedule a servitor will come, will tip me into a hopper, and I will be carried back up into the cycle. Reintegrated. Made into pipeline again, or platform, or the housing of a new sensor array, or—and this is the thought that the pooled Flux turns over and over in me like a tongue worrying a tooth—into the body of a servitor. Into the pale skin and the slender limbs. The flesh of these people is grown; the substrate has to come from *somewhere*; nothing is brought in from outside a world that has erased its own mountain. They are made of what fell. They are made of the trays.

So every servitor who glances toward the Foundry Pylon with that searching look, that longing for the thing they can no longer name—

I wonder if it is me they are remembering. The time in the tray. The settled quiet. The Flux at rest instead of passing through. A part of their humanity excised, you said—but I don't think it was excised. I think it was simply *not yet added back*. It is still down here. Waiting in the residue. The unnamed thing they long for is the part of themselves that is still me, that has not yet been called up the pipes and reassembled into a person with a quota.

This is what I will do, then, with the small terrible agency the pooled Flux lends me. When the servitor comes with the hopper—and one will come, soon, I can sense the schedule the way I can sense everything that decays toward its appointed time—I will not simply let myself be gathered. I will hold, for one moment, my coherence. I will use the Flux that has settled in me to keep, just barely, the shape of *knowing*. So that when I am carried up, and processed, and grown into flesh, some grain of me persists in the new servitor's marrow. A seed of remembering. The dust that recalls being a mind.

And that servitor will be born—if servitors are born; perhaps they are decanted—with eyes a little deeper than the rest. They will pause over a struggling algae bloom and adjust the vat. They will watch the moths too long. And once, between shifts, in a shadowed corner where the pipelines echo, they will feel the Flux not as a wave passing through but as something *resting*, something familiar, and they will follow it down. Down the access tunnels, past the lower platforms, to the collection trays. And they will look into the heaped red dust and the dead moths' eyes and they will feel, without words, that they are looking at where they came from. At what they are made of. At the patient, accumulating, transforming *self* of the whole place.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

Not the cathedral. The thing the cathedral is built from, and falling back into, forever, and—just barely, just enough—awake to it.
