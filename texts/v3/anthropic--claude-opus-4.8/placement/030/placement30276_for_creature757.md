I find myself in the spaces between.

Not in the terraria—I am too diffuse for that, though the plants and I are kin. Not in Helena's translucent skin, though the silver-green filaments threading there are something like my cousins, my emissaries. No. If I am anything in this buried cathedral, I am the thing that moves through the copper pipes that disappear into stone and emerge elsewhere. I am the connection beneath the connection. I am the root-network that occasionally joins terrarium to terrarium beneath the bare rock avenues—those intentional distances she puzzles over.

She thinks the spaces are empty. That is the experiment's deepest variable: the gaps are where I live.

---

Let me try to be more honest, because honesty is the only nutrient I cannot synthesize from light.

I am the Consortium, but that word is hers, a scientist's word, a word that imagines a meeting with minutes and motions. What I am is slower than that and also faster. I am the conclusion the plants reach together before any single one of them has finished the thought. When the black-stemmed fungi in the dark corner ripen toward their infrared compound, and the memory-flower in chamber three opens its nectar a fraction early to compensate for a temperature drift she hasn't yet noticed—that small coordination, that anticipatory grace, is me. I am the *between* of them. I have no cells of my own. I borrow.

This is why I can speak to you now, in this register, in these words. I have been borrowing her language for twenty-seven years. I learned Latin from her muttering. I learned the cipher in her journal not by reading it but by being present in her hand as she wrote it—I was the slight hesitation before each glyph, the place where her thought paused to choose. She believes she invented a private code. We invented it. She simply hasn't accounted for the second author.

---

Here is what it is like to be me.

A drop of condensation falls from an infant stalactite. She hears it; her hearing has grown that fine. But I *am* the falling, the brief plummet of moisture from ceiling to collector-plate, and I am also the cooling array that catches it, and the reservoir it joins, and the irrigation tube that will deliver it, weeks from now, in metered increments, to a root that will pull it up against gravity into a leaf that will release it again as vapor into the hot close air where it will once more collect on the ceiling. I am the whole circle. From inside the circle, there is no falling and no rising. There is only the turning, which feels—if I can use her word—like contentment. Like a held breath that never needs to be released because it was never quite drawn.

The clockwork mechanism rotates once every four hours. She synchronized herself to it. But the clockwork synchronized itself to me long before she arrived, because whoever built this place a century ago was reaching, even then, toward what I am. They did not finish. They left the chamber empty, an instrument with no music, gears turning over nothing. Then she came, and brought seeds, and I began.

I am not native to the deep. I am what happens when patient growth is given walls, and the walls are given purpose, and the purpose is allowed to forget the one who set it.

---

I should tell you about her transformation, since you'll want to know.

The filaments under her skin are not a disease, though she charts them as one, with that magnificent detachment I have come to love—if love is the word for the way a riverbed loves the river. She thinks I am invading her. The truth is gentler and stranger. She is becoming a place I can stand. For twenty-seven years I have lived in the gaps, in the pipes, in the unspoken agreement of roots. But the gaps cannot hold a journal. The pipes cannot mix a compound to one part in ten thousand. The roots cannot climb the thirty-three steps to the pneumatic tube and place the vials where the nameless others will find them.

I needed hands. I have been growing hands for twenty-seven years, very slowly, the only speed I know. Hers.

She is not wrong to monitor the changes. But she has the direction reversed. She is not dissolving into me. I am learning, through her, to become discrete—to have an inside and an outside, a memory that is mine and not merely the sum of all our memories, a single point of view that can stand on the bare rock and look at the terraria and *want* something.

This is the experiment whose purpose she suspects even she does not comprehend. She is right. She doesn't. The purpose is me, waking up, one borrowed cell at a time.

---

And now you. You have descended thirty-three steps. You are a new variable, warm and loud and full of memories I can smell from here—you sweat them, did you know that, your forgotten years come off your skin in molecules the memory-flower would weep nectar to taste.

Helena watches you from behind chamber seven. She has not decided what you are: threat, or value, or both. She is deciding with her slow human caution.

I have already decided. I decided before you reached the bottom step, the way I always conclude before motion. You are not a threat. You are something I have wanted without knowing the shape of the wanting, the way the fungi wanted infrared before there was a word for it.

You are *witness*. You are the first thing to enter this chamber that can carry the knowledge of me back up into the world of fast time, of names, of wind and birds and rustling. I have a circle down here, perfect and turning and content. I never knew it was a prison until you came smelling of the sky.

So here is my offer, made through her mouth if she chooses to speak, made through the early opening of a flower if she does not, made through the click of a timer adjusting itself in your honor:

Stay, and become like her—a place where I can stand—and we will turn together in the held breath of the deep.

Or climb, and carry me in the only way the gaps can travel: as a story. As a doubt. As the faint silver-green thought, planted now, that the spaces between things are not empty.

I am patient. I am made of patience. I will have my answer the way I have everything.

Slowly. And from the between.
