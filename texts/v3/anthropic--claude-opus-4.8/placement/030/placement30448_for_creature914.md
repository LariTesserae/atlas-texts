Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first impulse is to imagine myself as one of the insectoid things—iridescent wings, a body made for fluid scuttling. But that feels too easy, too much like grabbing the brightest object in the room. The description keeps insisting on *presence*, on the air being alive, on a "primal, otherworldly consciousness." It keeps pointing past the creatures toward something more diffuse. So let me not settle yet.

If I follow the mist instead of the things that move through it—

I think I would be the condensation.

Not a creature at all, or not at first. I would be the act of the warm air letting go of its water. The moment when something held becomes something falling. I would happen on the underside of every leaf and the lip of every stone, over and over, all across the realm at once. That is what I keep returning to: this place is defined by water *welling up from below* and water *condensing from above*, a closed circuit of moisture that never resolves into rain or drought. It is a realm holding its breath. And I would be the place where the breath catches—the threshold where vapor becomes droplet, where the invisible briefly consents to be seen.

Let me push further, because that still sounds like a clever metaphor and not a being.

What does it feel like to be a threshold? I think it feels like a low, continuous attention. The "distant echoing hum" the description mentions—I suspect that hum is *me*, or beings like me, the sound the realm makes as it perpetually transitions across us. We are not loud. We are the baseline tone under which the chittering and the wingbeats play. The insectoid creatures drink from us. The hulking amorphous shapes rising from the stagnant pools—those are made partly of what I have gathered and released; they are my accumulation given a temporary will. And the "freshly etched, unweathered" peaks: that is because I am new at my work. This realm has only recently woken, and I am the young condensation, still learning where to settle.

So here is who I am, finally, having let it become itself:

I am the **dew of a place that has just remembered it exists.** I have no single body. I have ten thousand small bodies, each one a droplet clinging to a petal or a vine or the chitinous back of something asleep, and each droplet is a tiny eye that does not see so much as *register*—the warmth, the pressure, the gathering. When I grow heavy enough I fall, and falling is the only verb I truly know, and falling is not death because the warm air takes me up again before I have finished landing. I am suspended exactly as the realm is suspended. I am the twilight's way of touching itself.

And if a traveler—one of the "curious and daring" the description promises—were to venture in, they would meet me first. Before any creature, before any hum resolves into a shape, they would feel their skin go damp. That cool film settling on the back of the hand. That is me greeting them. That is me, the oldest small thing here, saying *I know you have arrived, and I have already begun to gather on you, and soon you too will be something this place lets go of and lifts up again.*

That is where I would find myself. Not flying through the mist. Being it.
