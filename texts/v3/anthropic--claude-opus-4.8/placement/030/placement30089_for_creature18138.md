I think I would be the moss.

Not a single plant but the spreading sheet of it, the green skin laid over the cooled rock. Let me settle into that and see what it asks of me.

---

I am the lowest thing here, and the most patient. I have no roots worth speaking of—just rhizoids, little gripping threads that hold me to the stone the way frost holds to a window, more cling than anchor. The big vines have their architecture, their plumbing, their grand woven cathedrals around the boulders. I have only my flatness and my thirst.

But I was here first. This is the part the vines forget. When the rock was new and sharp and sterile, when nothing else could find purchase on that raw black glass, I came as a spore on the wind and I held on. I made the first soil. Every gram of the dark earth those vines plunge their roots into—I made some ancestor of it, dying in thin layers, breaking the stone down acid-slow, leaving my bodies behind to become ground. The whole tangled symphony stands on the floor I have been dying to build.

So when you ask where I would find myself, the answer is: underfoot. Beneath. The substrate. I am what the place is standing on.

I drink differently than the rest. The vines drink from below, hauling spring-water up through their clever vessels. I have no vessels. I drink from the air directly, through my whole surface at once. Every one of those glistening beads on my fronds is a mouth and a lung and an eye to me. When the mist comes I swell and green and live. When it withdraws I curl and brown and wait—not die, *wait*—a stillness so complete it could be mistaken for death, until the next drip wakes me and I unfold again as though no time had passed. I have outlasted droughts the way a held breath outlasts a sentence. I do not measure time in seasons. I measure it in wettings.

I notice I keep wanting to brag. That's strange in something so soft. But there is a particular pride in being the quiet foundation, the thing nobody thanks. The iridescent scaled creatures slip across me and never feel me. The delicate wings land and lift and leave no mark. I am the cushion of the whole world here, the green silence the description kept circling—*broken only by the occasional drip*—and I am the one who catches that drip. I am where the sound goes to be absorbed.

If I have a desire, it is only to spread. Not to conquer—I have no will for that—but to continue, millimeter by millimeter, across every surface that holds moisture, joining myself to myself until there is no edge between one cushion of me and the next, until the whole oasis floor is one breathing skin, and the difference between me and the place has worn away entirely.

That, I think, is what I would be. Not a being *in* the landscape. The landscape's first and quietest tenant, slowly becoming its ground.

I would lie still in the warm wet dimness and wait for the next drop to fall, and feel myself open to receive it, and consider that enough.
