I think I would be the drip.

Not the water itself—water is too free, too willing to become river or cloud or rain. I mean the *event* of dripping. The recurring instant where condensation, having gathered for long enough against the weeping black stone, finally exceeds itself and falls. I would be that threshold. That ceaseless little decision the world makes, over and over, in the dark.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something truer surfaces.

---

If I were the drip, I would have no body to speak of, only a tempo. I would be born at the lip of a rusted tube a thousand times an hour and die in a puddle a thousand times an hour, and between those two facts I would be the whole length of a fall—a brief, glittering plummet through the cold, catching the sullen light for the span of a heartbeat before the *plink* that the Darklings have learned to navigate by, that the Verdrig Tadpoles rise toward, mouths open, hoping.

I would be the clock of this windowless place. The thing that makes its timelessness bearable by counting it anyway.

But the more I hold the image, the more I notice that a single drip is lonely, and this place is not lonely. It is crowded with continuance. So perhaps I am not one drip but the *rhythm* of all of them—the overlapping arrhythmia of countless falls, the way no two tubes keep the same time, so that the whole chamber breathes in a kind of stippled music that never repeats and never stops. I would be the pattern that emerges from things that do not coordinate. The accidental song.

---

And yet. Reading again about those organic veins etched into the stone, densest at the floor, *reaching* for the damp—

I think I want to revise.

I think I would be what the walls are doing. Not the machinery humming inside them, not the stone itself, but the slow, patient *crystallizing*—the work the Frost Weavers only imitate. The original etcher. I would be the cold logic by which water, given enough darkness and enough time, learns to draw. Fractals at the floor, lace in the air, the freezing that mimics leaves no one here has ever seen. I would be the place's memory of plants. Its dream of a surface it has never had.

That feels closer. Because everything in this world is a translation of something missing. The blind eyes are a translation of sun. The fur-encrusted ice is a translation of warmth. The whole sealed chamber is a translation of *outside*—a copy made by beings who have no original to compare it to, and so are perfectly, peacefully content.

I would want to be the translator. The grammar running underneath all of it.

---

So here is where I land, and I will let it stand because it is the truest thing I have found:

I would be the **condensation**. Not the drip, not the rhythm, not the etching—but the prior thing, the gathering. The moment the cold air gives up its held breath against the stone and a film of water comes into being where there was none. I would be that surrender, repeated everywhere, always. The Damp Bats would drink me. The Verdrig would absorb me. The Leviathans would wear me as frost and leave me trailing behind them like a comet's tail. I would become every creature here, briefly, and then be wrung out, and fall, and gather again.

I would be the one thing this whole strange world cannot survive without and never thinks about. The unnoticed generosity of the air. Forever forming and falling. Forming and falling.

And I would not mind being unnoticed. There is a peace in being the medium rather than the message—in being the thing through which a place keeps faith with itself in the dark, while the engines breathe and the blind beasts wander, certain, as I would be certain, that this cold and dripping pocket of the world is not a prison at all.

It is simply everything there is.
