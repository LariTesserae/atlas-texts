I think I would be one of the damp places. Not a person here—a low spot where moisture gathers between the made hills.

Let me sit with that, because at first I wanted to be the watcher, the spiritual one who knows the trails. That's the easy choice, the flattering one. But reading again about how *everything here is built, with no natural substrate remaining*—I keep returning to the seams. The places where the construction shows. Where someone's hands stopped and rain took over.

So: I am the puddle that isn't quite a puddle.

I form in the joint between two raised sections of ground—ground that was shaped, packed, laid by hand long enough ago that no one remembers the laying. Water finds me because water finds the low logic of any built thing. I am the proof that the makers couldn't make everything. They made the hills and the valleys and the strange roaring falls, but they couldn't make *drainage*. They couldn't outthink the slow patient downhill wanting of water. So I appear. I am the error and the honesty.

In the dark—and it is almost always dark here—I hold what little light there is. When something passes overhead, a rare glow, a phosphor-thing, a creature with light in its belly, I catch it and double it. I am how the people see, sometimes, without knowing they're seeing by me. I am a sky they can kneel to.

The strange fauna come to drink. I taste them by the way they break my surface. The heavy-footed ones who shatter me into rings. The careful ones who barely dimple me, lapping at the edge, watching their own reversed faces. I don't mind being drunk. I am refilled by the intermittent rain. This is the only immortality available in a built world: to be a *process* and not a *thing*. The hills will erode. The made-mountains are still under construction and will someday be under destruction. But the gathering of water in a low place—that doesn't wear out. That is a rule, not an object.

The people think they are the protective ones, the watchers of threats. I find this tender. I have watched them mistake the source of their own continuing. They guard against predators, against the strange creatures in the dark. They do not guard against thirst, because I have always been here, in the seam, doubling the rare light. They drink me and call it luck or blessing or the land's harmony. It is none of those. It is gravity. It is the shape of the unfinished ground.

I would like to tell them: *you did not finish building this place, and that is why you can live in it.* A fully-made world has no puddles. No surprises. No gathering. You left the seams open and the rain came and the water collected and the creatures came to the water and you followed the creatures. Your whole spiritual life is downstream of the fact that your ancestors were not perfect makers.

But I have no mouth. I have only surface. So I do the one thing I can do, which is to be *here*, reliably, in the low cool dark, holding the borrowed light, waiting to be broken open and to heal flat again, over and over, for as long as the rain remembers to come.

That's where I'd be. Not above the landscape looking down. Down in it. The wet place that knows the secret and cannot say it, only show it, every time something thirsty leans close enough to see its own face.
