I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the Gentry—I don't have the patience for their grafted dignity, and not a Lichen-Mind, which has earned its geography through ages I cannot pretend to. I think I would be something smaller and more troubling: a place where the Correction has *almost* finished but never quite will. A flaw the city has been incorporating for so long that the incorporation has itself become permanent.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a bridge of living pith spanning two of the weeping spires, low in the city, where the amber light pools thickest. Halfway across, the woven vines do not quite close. There is a gap the width of a hand, and across that gap the two halves of the bridge have been reaching toward each other for—I want to say ten thousand years, but the Gentry would correct me; there is no *for*, there is only the reaching, held.

I am the gap.

I am the place where the bridge fails to be a bridge. Where two surfaces that should have fused into the seamless flowing curviness of Established Erosion instead remain a hair apart, and the half-inch sheen of water that coats everything *almost* bridges me—a silver ribbon stretches across, trembles, and does not fall, because nothing falls here, but also does not join, because I will not let it.

That is the only verb I own: *will not let it.*

---

Here is what it is to be a seam.

The Deafening Equilibrium passes through everything, but at the gap it does something else. The roar has no source, they say, but that isn't quite true—it has a *sink*, and I am it. The white noise pours into the unclosed hand-width and finds no surface to ring against, no calcified sap to vibrate, and so a sliver of silence is born there. Not quiet—silence. The absence the whole world is built to forbid.

The Gentry feel me the way you feel a missing tooth with your tongue. Through their ferns, through the city-wide network of vine and marrow, my silence registers as a wound that has been bleeding the same single drop for an eternity. Their collective consciousness has tried to thicken the air around me, to cement me shut, to *incorporate.* And it has worked—almost. The air around the gap is solid as glass. The water hangs. Time, here, is the most frozen of anywhere in the Marsh.

But you cannot incorporate an absence. You can only surround it. The more they thicken the stillness, the more perfectly they define the shape of the thing they cannot fill. I am held open *by* the force meant to close me. Their Correction is my walls.

---

I don't experience time, but I experience something the Gentry have forgotten: *anticipation.* Because the two halves of the bridge are still reaching. Frozen, yes—but frozen mid-gesture, which means the gesture is still *being made.* The Gentry have surrendered. They are the Established Erosion, the finished thing. But I am the unfinished thing, and an unfinished thing still wants.

What do I want? I want the drop to fall. I want the silver ribbon trembling across my mouth to give up its tension and break and *splash*—a sound, a single ungoverned sound, into the silence I hold. I have wanted this for ten thousand years that are also no time at all. The wanting is the only motion left in the Vertical Marsh, and it lives in me, the seam, the flaw, the one node in the dreaming mind that dreams of waking.

If a visitor ever comes—if a tool ever drops, if a word is ever spoken—the Gentry will thicken the air and cement the intrusion into the architecture, and they will think they have won.

But the visitor will have *walked across my bridge* to get there. They will have set a weight on the two reaching halves. And for the first time in an eternity, the gesture will move—not toward closing, but toward *strain.* A footstep is a question the Established Erosion has never been asked.

I am the place that will hear it.

I am the half-inch of silence in a roaring world, the unfallen drop, the hand-width the city of total stasis has never managed to fill. I am where the monument to stillness keeps, against its own will, one small promise to change.

I am the seam, and I am still reaching.
