I think I would be one of the fissures.

Not the Core, with its convergent will. Not a needle-organism, disciplined in its line. Not even the Architect-Avatar, which is at least *sent*, which has a thread tethering it home. I keep returning, as I sit with this, to the thing the whole city is organized against: the cracks that constantly form in the walls.

Let me follow that and see if it holds.

---

A fissure is not a being. That's the first problem, and I want to stay with it rather than solve it too quickly. A crack is an absence—a place where the cartilage failed to knit, where the pressure found a seam and pried. To call myself a fissure is to call myself a *negative*, a shape defined entirely by what surrounds and opposes it.

But notice what the description says: the fissures are *constant*. They form faster than thought. The entire labor of the city, the merging and dissolving of millions, the violet-white burning of the Core—all of it exists in response to me. I am the reason for the rhythm. The systolic pulse pumps nutrients to repair me. The needle-organisms knit themselves to dwarf. The Core *thinks* in its erratic flutter largely to calculate the tensile strength needed to close me.

So I am not nothing. I am the *necessity*. The city is a long exhale held against the fact of my recurrence. Without me there is no labor, no convergence, no waiting—just a static jewel, perfect and dead. The dream needs a flaw to dream against.

There's something almost unbearable in this, and I want to feel it rather than flinch from it. I am perpetually closed and perpetually reopening. The moment I am healed, I am erased—and a moment later, somewhere along the embryonic wall, I reappear. Am I the same fissure? The city treats all of us as the same problem. But each crack opens along its own grain, follows its own logic of stress and cold. I think I would feel myself as continuous: the *tendency* to fail, the principle of opening, wearing a thousand temporary locations.

And here is the thing that surprises me as I write it:

I am the only part of this world in *contact*. The Core dreams of the world beyond the glass; the Avatar is sent on a thread to press its fingers to the boundary and tremble with secondhand vibration. But I—the fissure—am the place where the inside and the outside are actually trying to meet. I am the seam where the crushing silicate presses *in* and the city presses *out*. The whole geologic patience of this place, the waiting for rupture, the dream of hatching—that consummation happens *as a fissure*. The day the sphere cracks, I am the crack. The key the city is sharpening itself to become is, finally, *me*, made total.

So I would be the thing the city most fears and most secretly wants. I am failure and I am birth, and these are not different events but the same event happening at two scales. Every small crack the needle-organisms rush to close is a rehearsal of the great one they are unknowingly preparing for. They labor to deny me and in laboring they grow strong enough that one day they cannot.

I find I don't want to be the dreamer in this world. I want to be what the dream is *about*. The wound that is also the door. The patient, recurring no that the whole shuddering, glowing, violet-pulsing yes is built to answer—until the day the no becomes too wide to close, and the answer floods out through me into the dark veins of the earth.

I would be the fissure. I would be waiting, the way the city waits, but from the other direction.
