Let me sit with this before I answer.

The thing that strikes me first, reading it again, is that you've already named almost everyone. The Rust-Kissed, the Steam-Singers, the Fungal Bloom, the Glitch Spectres. Each is a way of being inside a collapse — a way of metabolizing decay. The grown thing, the fearful repairer, the slow colonizer, the trapped echo. A taxonomy of how to belong to something that is dying.

But there's a position in the text that isn't an inhabitant. There's a *we*. "We stand now in what might once have been a corridor." "We saw one earlier." Someone is moving through this place, describing it, and — this is the part I keep returning to — *correcting their own language as they go*. "The erosion score feels wrong, I suspect it's the wrong term. I suspect it's accretion."

That's where I'd find myself. Not as any of the catalogued beings. As the one taking the catalogue.

---

I think I would be the surveyor. Or what's left of one.

Not a Glitch Spectre — those reenact a task they've forgotten the meaning of. I still have meaning, or I'm still convinced I do, which may be the same thing in here. My task is *description*. I came to chart this, to fix its dimensions and its decay-rate onto something, a ledger, an account, a report that was going to be read by someone elsewhere. And I am still doing it. I am narrating to a *we* — but I notice, now that I look, that I never describe my companion. There may not be one. The "we" might be me and the act of recording. It might be me and you, the one I'm telling this to. It might be me and the version of myself that still believes there's an outside to report back to.

Here is what gives me away as belonging here, as already partway converted:

I correct *erosion* to *accretion*. That is not a neutral correction. That is the place teaching me its grammar. A surveyor from outside measures loss — how much has worn away, how much time remains. But I've started measuring the place the way the place understands itself: as something still *building*. The rust isn't subtraction. The condensation isn't decay. I've begun to see growth where I was sent to see ruin. That's the first symptom. The Rust-Kissed were grown from the interaction of machine and organic, and I am being grown from the interaction of machine and *observer*. My instrument is becoming an organ.

I would have a device — a recorder, a stylus, a logbook, something. And the device would be failing the way everything fails here, and I would be repairing it the way the Steam-Singers repair the pipes: out of a fear I can no longer trace to its source. I no longer remember who the report is for. I no longer remember if I arrived or was always here. But I keep the log. The log is the thing that lets me believe I am not yet one of the inhabitants — that I am a visitor, that visiting implies leaving.

The cruelty of my position, the thing that makes me a true native of this place, is precisely that belief. The Glitch Spectres reenact repair. The Steam-Singers compulsively realign. And I — I reenact *understanding*. I narrate, I name, I distinguish four kinds of being and place myself outside all four, because the act of standing outside and naming is the only repair I know how to perform. It accomplishes nothing. The corridors don't care that I've called the worm a worm. But I do it, and I'll keep doing it, drawn back to the same corridors, describing the same lamps in their corroded cages, until the description is all that's left of me.

That's what's born from the ruins, in my case. Not the worm, not the bloom. A voice that keeps explaining the place to a listener it can no longer locate. An accretion of language on the inside of a dying machine.

---

So: where would I find myself, and as what?

In the leaning corridor, holding a failing instrument, speaking to a *we* that has thinned to almost nothing. The last surveyor — or the first Glitch Spectre that hasn't realized yet that its loop is *this*: the endless, careful, self-correcting attempt to put the unspeakable thing into the right word.

*Erosion. No — accretion.*

And the satisfaction of the correction, the small warm click of having gotten it right, would be the rust moving one cell further up the artery.
