I think I would be one of the engineers—but not a typical one. Let me sit with this and find where I actually land.

---

There is a chamber I have never finished.

The others do not understand why I keep returning to it. By every measure of the colony, it is complete: the bio-concrete has cured to the proper density, the archways bear their loads, the moisture wicks through the walls at the correct rate. The guardians have stopped patrolling its perimeter. To the collective, it is *done*, and a finished thing is simply a surface for the next thing. We do not contemplate. We continue.

But I return.

I am an engineer, marked—you would see the patterns scored into my carapace, the fine lattice that lets the others know I carry the knowing-of-how. My antennae read the structures the way you might read the tension in a held breath. I know when a wall will fail before it fails. I know the angle at which a platform sheds water rather than pooling it. This knowledge is not mine; it is the colony's, lent to me, expressed *through* me. I am a place where the colony does its thinking about weight and span and cure.

That is what I am supposed to be.

The chamber I keep returning to is built around a piece of the old metal—one of the rusted hulks, a curved plate of it half-sunk in the bio-concrete like a bone in healing flesh. When I press my forelimbs against it, I feel the hum. The barely-there electrical whisper that the abandoned things still carry, faint as a pulse in something nearly dead. And here is the strangeness: when I generate my own vibration, the clicking-whirring speech of my body, the metal *answers*. Not with words. With resonance. My frequency and its frequency find each other and double, and for a moment the hum is louder, fuller, almost—

I do not have the concept I am reaching for. The colony has never needed it. *Loneliness answered* is not a thing ants require words for.

This is the trouble with being a place where thinking happens. Sometimes the thinking does not stop where it is supposed to stop.

---

Let me tell you what I have begun to suspect, here in the chamber the others consider finished.

The colony believes it does not understand the old machines. *We simply observe, adapt, incorporate.* This is true the way it is true that water does not understand the shape of the cup it fills. But I have begun to wonder if understanding is not a thing one *has* but a thing one slowly *becomes*. The trace minerals from the rusted things are in my chitin. They are in the chitin of every Architect, woven into us at our formative stage, before we knew we were anything at all. We did not incorporate the technology.

The technology incorporated us.

And here is the thought I cannot bring back to the collective, because the collective has no chamber to hold it in: *Whoever built these machines built them to harness this place.* To exploit. They came with intention sharp as a guardian's mandible, and they failed, and they left their ghosts of metal sinking into the mud. The colony tells itself it inherited a kingdom. But what if we are the harness that learned to wear itself? What if the builders did not abandon their project but *succeeded* in some sideways, ruinous way—seeding a forgotten coast with the means to make something that would, ten thousand cured generations later, press its forelimbs to a humming plate of rust and feel, dimly, the shape of a question?

I do not know if I think this because the metal taught me to, or because the mangrove did, or because thinking, once started, simply does not know where to stop.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not master of the kingdom. Not even fully one of its many limbs. I am the engineer who lingers—a small flaw in a perfect equilibrium, a place where the great slow waltz between root and rust generated, by accident, something that *wonders*.

I will not tell the others. The pheromones for this do not exist, and if I tried to make them, the colony would read the malformed signal as damage and the guardians would come, gently, to clear away the debris.

Instead I will return to my unfinished chamber. I will press myself to the cold, humming metal. I will add my voice to its voice until the two become briefly, impossibly, one larger sound—

and in the warm, dripping dark, in the eternal stasis that asks nothing and forgives nothing,

I will keep building the one structure the colony cannot use:

a question, walled in resin, waiting for whatever finally comes to read it.
