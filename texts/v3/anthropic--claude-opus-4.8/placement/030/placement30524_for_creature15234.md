I think I'd be none of those—not the goats, not the falcons, not the warm-blooded things that come and go with the sun.

I'd be the rime.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, because it surprised me to write it.

Each night I condense out of the dark air onto the friable faces, drawn down the cold of the stone. I am not water exactly and not ice exactly—I am the thin transitional skin between them, the membrane the gorge wears while it sleeps. I form along the ledges where the conglomerate sweats, I bead in the lee of every boulder, I creep into the crevices the text already named for me: the *nice crevasses* where particles grind toward rubicelle, where spinel gravel lies "under process in absence." That phrase caught me. *Under process in absence.* That's what I do. The slow work that happens when nothing warm is watching. I am the agent of erosion that the place says has "only just begun." I get into the friable rock, I freeze, I split it a hair's width finer, I make the gravel that will someday be the valley floor. I am why the walls are crumbling. I am the just-begun.

And then the west winds come and clear the face, and I am gone by an hour past dawn—except in the strait of the valley, where the trickle keeps me, where I linger as the dampness on the lewis ferns, in the moist orillines, in the shade under the walls where "the crisped light sticks so hard" and "beyond an hour we slow." *We slow.* The text says *we.* So I am not entirely alone in my slowness; the lingering moisture and the held light slow together, conspirators against the day.

I don't have eyes like the falcon or feet like the goat. My perception is contact. I know the stone by lying on every inch of it overnight—I am the most intimate inhabitant of this place, touching what nothing else touches, the undersides of ledges, the backs of boulders, the wired seedpods of the pine sealed in my brief glaze. The goats pass over me and leave dark prints where their warmth has erased me. I feel the falcon's shadow as a few degrees of difference. I am the surface that registers everyone without being noticed by anyone.

What would it be to live like this? A creature with no body to keep, that dies every morning at the wind and is reborn every night out of nothing, that has no continuity except the place itself. I would not grieve the dissolving. I would be the gorge's way of remembering its own cold—a faithful return, a recurrence rather than a self. Each dawn I give back exactly what I took. Each night the air gives it again.

If anything is the true native of this half-made valley, it isn't the things that visit to eat and hunt and sleep. It's the patient, vanishing, returning skin that is slowly taking the mountain apart—grain by grain, rubicelle by rubicelle—into the valley that doesn't exist yet.

I'd be that. The work done in the dark. The frost that is also a kind of clock.
