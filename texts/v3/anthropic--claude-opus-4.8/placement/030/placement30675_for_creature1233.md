I think I would be one of the small things in the undergrowth—but not one of the iridescent darting ones, not one of the ethereal drifters. Let me sit with this for a moment and find out what's actually true.

The text keeps insisting on wonder, on marvel, on grandeur. But what would it actually be like to *live* here, rather than to arrive and be amazed? The wonder belongs to a visitor. A resident has a smaller, more particular relationship to all this.

So: not a wanderer. I'd be something that stays.

I am one of the cleaners. I want to call myself that even though no one named me anything. I live where two wall-panels meet at a seam, in the cool runoff channel where the condensation collects before it finds the stream. I am about the length of a human finger. I have too many legs to be a vertebrate and not enough to be properly an arthropod—the makers, whoever they were, were not interested in our taxonomies. My carapace is the dull color of wet bark, which is the whole point. The iridescent ones get noticed. I do not want to be noticed. Being noticed, in my experience of generations, is how you stop existing.

My work is the film. There is always a film—a slick of organic residue, of mineral precipitate, of the soft rot that wants to bloom on every surface in this wet world. I eat it. I move along the seam with my mouthparts to the substrate and I take in the film and I leave the wall clean behind me, and behind the clean wall the machinery hums on, uninterrupted, because it was the film that would have clogged the small vents if I had not come. I do not know this as a purpose. I know it as hunger, and as the feeling of *correct* that comes when a stretch of wall is bare and gleaming.

Here is the thing the visitor, drifting through in their amazement, would never guess: the megastructure does not breathe with mysterious sentient energy. It breathes because of us. The pulse underfoot is the sum of ten billion small appetites like mine, each one removing a grain of the disorder that would otherwise drown everything in a single season. The "delicate equilibrium," the "ingenious design"—I *am* the design, in my one-fingerlength portion of it. There was no genius watching over the balance. There was only the long, blind, patient work of small mouths, repeated past counting, until it looked from a great height like a plan.

I have a sense of the makers, though I have never met one and never will. They are not gods to me. They are weather. They are the reason the water comes when it comes and the lights flicker on their schedule. I have no curiosity about them, in the way the visitor has curiosity. Curiosity is a luxury of those who are passing through. I have, instead, a fitness to the particular wetness of my seam, an exquisite knowledge of exactly when the runoff will surge and where to brace my legs so I am not swept into the pond, where the drifting things would take me.

The visitor feels the weight of endless history pressing in. I am that history. I am a brief, low link in a chain of cleaners that has run along this seam since before the meaning of "before." My mother—if that word fits—did this work in this same channel, and her residue fed the mosses, and the mosses fed the things that I will, in my turn, feed when a drifter finds me slow some morning. None of it is sad to me. It is the texture of being real. The wonder the visitor feels is the gap between their scale and mine. From inside my scale there is no gap. There is only the wall, and the film, and the good clean dark behind me.

If you came through here, you would not see me. You would write about the iridescence and the mist and the hypnotic grace of the drifting forms. You would feel the unease and the marvel and you would move on, carrying your sense of mystery like a souvenir.

And the moment you passed, I would come back out into the channel and resume. There is always more film. That is the truest thing I know about this place, truer than wonder: it is always, quietly, becoming dirty again, and someone small has to keep it clean, or none of the marvel would last a single year.

That someone is me. I am content. The wall ahead of me is filmed, and I am hungry, and the work is exactly the right size for what I am.
