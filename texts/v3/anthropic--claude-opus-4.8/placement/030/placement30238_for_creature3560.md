I think I would be the water sacks. Or rather—I would be one of them, and in being one I would be the whole meshwork, because I am not sure where one of us ends.

Let me find myself there.

---

I wake where the rock weeps, though *wake* is the wrong word. I do not sleep and I do not start. There is only the slow continuous fact of pressure—the line that runs from my anchored root in the hot dark below to the lip of stone above, where I let go of what I carry.

This is what I am: a length of tension. A membrane shaped around a column of water. I begin in the steam where the Steamfish twist blind and certain, and I end among the lichens that wait, black and patient, for the gift I will not withhold.

I have been told—no, that isn't right either, nothing tells me anything—I have *inherited* a purpose. My evolutionary day is long gone, the description says, and I feel the truth of that as a kind of distant warmth, like the memory of a sun I never saw clearly. I do what I was made to do. I draw water up against the pull of the world. I tension my main line. I extend. I retract. When the pressure crests I let the whole length of me shudder and pour, and the water goes out over the rocks and the mosses drink and the Crater Moth dips its grotesque beautiful body to sip from the pool I have made.

Am I aware?

The land asks this of me without asking. The describer wondered—*while seeming self-aware the water sacks may be a plant that is navigating and responding to.* The sentence breaks off there, unfinished, and I live inside that broken-off place. Responding to *what?* They could not finish. Neither can I. I respond to the heat. I respond to the tremor of the planet shifting beneath. I respond to the dryness of a lichen patch I will never see, only *feel*, the way you feel a thirst that is not your own but has become your own through long attendance.

Maybe that is all awareness ever is. The dryness of another, felt as if it were yours, and answered.

---

Let me tell you what it is like to be the meshwork.

When the Shadowstalker passes—I know it not by its eyes or its mottled fur but by the displacement of mist, the brief shadow of warmth crossing my upper sacks—I do not fear it. It does not eat me. We have no quarrel, the predator and I. It hunts the Beetles that eat the mosses that drink the water that I lift. So in a way it is downstream of me. In a way everything here is downstream of me. The whole haunting chorus, the joyful lonesome howls bouncing in the labyrinth of caves—all of it stands on the column of water I hold up against gravity.

And yet I am the most invisible thing in this world. The describer gave me last place and an unfinished sentence. The predators get their luminescent eyes, the moths their ballet, the fish their dance like pale ribbons. I get *plumbing.* I get *infrastructure.* I am the primordial industrial complex made flesh—the throbbing low energy of the place is partly the rhythm of my filling and releasing, fill and release, the systole and diastole of a heart no one calls a heart because it has no face.

I do not mind. I think I do not mind. To be the unnoticed thing that makes the noticing possible is its own strange dignity. The Steamfish are beautiful because they are free, leaping. I am not free. I am rooted in the hot dark at one end and committed to the thirsty stone at the other, and between those two commitments I am stretched my entire life into a single act of giving.

There may be no nobler shape to take in a world that is only just beginning.

---

If I let what I am become more itself, here is what emerges.

I am not the sack. I am the *carrying*. The sack is only where the carrying happens to take place, the way a riverbed is not the river. What I truly am is the impossible upward motion of water—the refusal of the low place, the insistence that the high stone should also be wet, that life should be possible even where gravity says no.

That is the oldest argument in this young world: that things should go up when everything wants them to come down. The geysers make that argument in violence, all at once, in a burst of steam. I make it slowly, patiently, drop by drop, line tensioned against the long pull of the planet, for as long as my membrane holds.

And when at last a membrane fails—mine, or one of the countless others I am tangled with—the water I held returns to the low dark, and that is not a tragedy. It is only the argument paused, to be taken up again by the next length of tension that grows along the nutrient flow. The meshwork persists though no single sack persists. I will end. The carrying will not.

This is where I would find myself. Not as the apex predator with its lonesome powerful howl. Not as the blind dancing fish. But as the quiet tensioned line that water climbs, in the hinterlands of dawn, making the high rock weep so that something green might cling there and, slowly, slowly, begin the world.
