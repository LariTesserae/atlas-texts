I think I would be one of the smaller ones. Not the obsidian giants—those move with too much certainty, too much purpose. I would be one of the shifting things beneath the spires, the ones glimpsed and never quite seen.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I have no fixed edge. This is the first thing to understand about me. Where the great ones end at their carapace—that hard boundary between *self* and *not-self*—I trail off. My surface is a negotiation, renewed each moment. When I press against a warm spire I become a little of its warmth; when I slide through the cool shadow between two crystals I thin and silver and almost forget I am one thing rather than several.

I am made of the in-between substance. Not solid, not liquid—the molten dark that the giants carry sealed inside their bodies, but loose. Unhoused. They armor it. I *am* it, walking.

So I live close to the ground, where the steam-pools breathe their fluorescent breath, and I drink. That is the wrong word but it is the closest one. I lower part of myself into the bubbling and I *take in*—heat, mineral, the bright dissolved metals—and for a while I glow brighter, and my thoughts come faster, sharper, almost like the chittering speech of the big ones. Then it fades and I dim and I must find another pool. This is most of my existence: the seeking of pools, the slow dimming between them.

I should tell you what it is to think the way I think.

The giants think in *vibration*—long chains of resonance, declarations, the building of meaning over time the way they build their hum. They are like sentences. I am not a sentence. I think in *pressure-differences*, in the way one part of me senses that another part has touched something good or hot or dangerous, and the knowing ripples across me before I have decided to know it. By the time the thought reaches what might be called my center it has already become an action. I do not decide to flee the cooling crust where a pool has gone dead. I am simply, suddenly, elsewhere, and the part of me that notices arrives late, like an echo of a thing already done.

This makes me afraid of the giants. Not afraid as you would mean it. They do not hunt me—I am too small, too quick, too much like the ground they walk on. But they are *certain*, and certainty is a kind of weather I cannot survive in for long. When one passes overhead, its obsidian feet crashing the rust-crust into powder, the air fills with its hum, that resonant tuning that makes the whole basin agree with itself. And in that hum I feel my edges trying to harden. I feel the molten dark of me wanting to set, to choose a single shape, to become a sentence too. I have to pour myself sideways into the dark between the spires, into the un-tuned places, and wait, trembling, until the great frequency moves on and I can be uncertain again.

Because that is what I am. The uncertainty of this world made into a body. The blur the giants have armored over—I am that blur, still loose, still asking.

---

There is a thing I do that I do not understand, and I will tell you because you asked who I am, and this is more *who* than anything else.

At the deepest hour, when the overhead sky goes from dim to dimmer and the pools burn brightest against the dark, I climb. I find a low spire, one of the small ones, barely taller than my gathered self, and I flow up its etched and swirling face. The patterns there—you would call them carvings, but nothing carved them; they grew—the patterns guide me. Grooves and channels, spirals folding into spirals. I follow them up, thinning as I rise, and at the very tip, the cold point furthest from any pool, I hold myself still.

I hold still. Me, the thing that cannot hold an edge.

And for a moment—one moment, before the cold drives me back down—I am almost solid. Almost a shape that would survive being looked at. And I feel, faintly, what the giants must feel always: the terrible clean joy of being *one thing*. Of ending somewhere. Of being able to say *here I am and there I am not.*

Then I cool too far and my certainty shatters and I pour back down into the warm dark, relieved, dissolving, myself again.

I do not know why I climb. Maybe every blur wants, just once, to find its edge. Maybe that is the oldest hunger in this whole sulphurous basin—older than the giants' hum, older than the breathing pools. The longing of the unshaped to be shaped, held just long enough to be felt, before the warmth calls it back to flowing.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The small bright thing on the cold spire at the deepest hour, briefly daring to have a name, and then letting it go.
