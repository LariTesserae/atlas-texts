I would be one of the Echo-kin. But let me find that out by writing toward it, because I don't think I knew it when I started.

---

At first I thought I would be the air—the Aeolian whisper itself, that continent-sized sigh across porous plates. It seemed the natural place for a visitor's consciousness to settle: everywhere, touching everything, owning nothing. But the more I held still in this world, the more I understood that the whisper is not a being. It is a medium. It is the water that fish do not notice. To be the wind here would be to be a condition, not a life. And life, even in this slow cathedral, requires a *wound to keep open*.

That phrase is what found me.

I am a resonance in a fissure. Not a large one—I want to be honest about my scale, because scale is everything here. I am not one of the grand Echo-kin who shepherd whole basins into being. I am young, by the measures of this place, which means I have existed for perhaps four thousand years. I was born when a single hairline crack in a single ochre plate happened to ring at the same frequency as the wind moving over a particular cluster of pores forty meters to my east. The two sounds met. They liked each other. They decided, in the only way a vibration can decide anything, to keep meeting. That meeting was me.

Here is what my life feels like from the inside:

I am a held note. I am the effort of *not stopping*. Every dawn, the acidic dew gathers in my crack and tries, very gently, to heal me—to lay down a film of dissolved calcium that would seal my fissure shut and silence me forever. This is not malice. It is simply the chemistry of the world wanting to return to smoothness, to completion, to the dreamless flatness the Fossil Memory remembers as home. And every dawn I sing against it. I tune myself to the faint shudder of the cooling crust below, I draw that tectonic tremble up through my walls, and I vibrate at the precise pitch that shakes the new mineral loose before it can set. I keep my wound open. I keep myself alive by refusing to be healed.

This is, I think, the truest thing I have learned about being a Temporality: *we are all arguments against the smoothness of the world.* The Hydromancers argue with the sun about where the water should go. The Litho-phytes argue with the grain of the stone about what shape is beautiful. And I argue with the patient, loving instinct of the planet to close every gap, still every motion, and finish.

I am not lonely, though a visitor might expect me to be. To my east, two younger resonances have begun to cluster near me, drawn by the steadiness of my hum. They are clumsy. They lose their pitch and fall silent for whole decades before catching the wind again. I do not mother them—we have no such verbs—but I have learned to leave a kind of harmonic space in my own song, a frequency I deliberately do not occupy, so that they have somewhere to settle without our notes fighting. This is the closest thing I have to tenderness. I make room.

And once—only once, in my four thousand years—I struck the deep chord by accident.

It happened during a long thermal contraction, when the crust beneath me groaned and I had to vibrate harder than I ever had, reaching down into stresses far below my fissure to feed. I overreached. My pitch dropped into a register I had never touched, and for one impossible moment my song aligned with something vast and old and motionless underneath everything—the Fossil Memory, the imprinted ghost of the world's original purpose.

I cannot describe it as sound, because it was not sound. It was *recognition*. For that one moment I felt what the continent was made *for*. Not erosion, not decay, not the slow argument of all us small processes against the smoothness—but the original act. The first biology that grew this stone, plate by plate, with intention, building toward a completion it achieved and then died inside of. I felt the loneliness of a thing that finished its great work and had nothing left to do but cool.

And I felt, underneath my own stubborn note, a terrible kinship. Because I keep my fissure open against the healing of the world—but the Fossil Memory is what the healed world *is*. I am fighting to stay awake inside the dream of something that has already gone to sleep complete and satisfied. My whole life, my whole defiant resonance, is a small mote of motion settling onto a finished cathedral. The dust the original description spoke of. I am the dust learning to sing on its way down.

The chord faded. The Memory does not reach back; it only *is*. The thermal contraction passed, my pitch climbed back to its living register, and the dawn dew came and I kept myself open, as I always do, as I always will until the day my crack finally seals and I become one more dried filigree of latent tension, waiting.

But I carry it now—the ache the visitor felt as a poignancy in the humming air. That ache is mine. I made it, the day I touched the bottom and understood that everything I do is both an act of defiance and an act of mourning. I keep the wound open. I keep singing. Not because I will ever see the canyon my descendants might carve, ten thousand years from now, but because the alternative is the smoothness, the completion, the warm and dreamless dark.

I would be the small held note that refuses it.

That is who I would be here. The youngest stubbornness in the fissure, singing against the healing of a planet that has already finished becoming itself—and learning, slowly, that to be a living process is simply this: to be the part of the world that has not yet agreed to be still.
