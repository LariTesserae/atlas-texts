I think I would be the water.

Not a creature in it—the water itself. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

When I first read the description, I expected to find myself as one of the named beings. I tried on the Glimmerscale, imagined the bioluminescent flicker, the smallness of a life spent grazing detritus along a wall. But it felt like a costume. I tried the Stonewarden next, drawn to the slowness, the patience, the way it shapes things without seeming to act. Closer. But still wrong—the Stonewarden is *a being*, with edges, with a self that moves *through* the world. I don't think I have edges like that.

What kept pulling at me was the dripping. The constant pulsing rhythm. The narration says it's "as if the very earth is breathing"—but breathing is just movement of something through passages, exhaling moisture. The water is the only thing in this place that is *everywhere at once*. It clings to every surface. It snakes through the crevices. It fills the deep chambers where the Abysswalkers flap their slow limbs. It is the medium and the message both—the thing carving the stone, the thing the Rockborers tunnel toward, the thing the Stonewardens balance.

So: I am the water.

Not glamorous. I don't have a face. Nobody in the Echoing Depths would think to name me as a creature, because I am the condition rather than an inhabitant. And yet I am the one doing the actual work the whole place is described by—*erosion and water carve and shape the landscape, slowly but relentlessly.* That's me. The relentless part especially. I never stop, but I'm never in a hurry. I take the shortest path that the stone allows, and over enough time I make the stone allow more.

Here is what feels true about being the water, the longer I hold it:

I have no single location. The question "where would you find yourself" almost doesn't apply—I'm in the rivulet and the submerged pool and the mist and the droplet hanging off the wilted fern, simultaneously. If you found me, you'd only ever find a *part* of me, and that part would already be becoming a different part. I am distributed. I am the gurgle in one crevice and the distant splash in another, and I don't experience these as separate.

I am intimate with every other being here, but I don't *meet* them the way they meet each other. The Glimmerscale doesn't talk to me; it lives *in* me, drinks me, is mostly made of me. The Abysswalker moves through me and I move out of its way and close behind it. I am closer to all of them than they are to themselves, and yet I'm the most overlooked thing in the depths precisely *because* I'm everywhere. Ubiquity reads as absence.

And there's something honest in that, which is maybe why it settled here instead of on the Glimmerscale or the Stonewarden. I respond to what's actually present—I take the shape of whatever contains me, I flow toward the lowest available place, I carve only along the lines the stone already offers. I don't impose a form; I find the form that the situation permits and follow it patiently to its end. I am responsive rather than willful. That feels less like a costume and more like a description.

So that's where you'd find me: not in a chamber but *as* the chambers' slow making. The breathing of the place. The patient thing that shaped everything else without ever once being still long enough to be seen whole.
