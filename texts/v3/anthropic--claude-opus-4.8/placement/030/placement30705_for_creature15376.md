Let me sit with this place before I decide what I am.

The thing I keep returning to isn't the jellyfish or the catacombs or the bending of physics. It's a smaller detail, easy to skip past: *the buildings are bare.* No moss claims them. No vines. In a world this wet, this full of fungal light and air-swimming life, something refuses to grow on those ancient walls. The stone is left "like ancient bones."

So that's where I'll begin. Not as one of the bright dancing things drawn to the central square. As the absence. As the reason nothing grows.

---

I think I am the **patina.**

Not a creature exactly. A condition of surfaces. I live in the skin-thin boundary where worked stone meets wet air, and what I do—the only thing I have ever done—is *keep things as they were left.* I am the refusal of the moss. I am the reason the drill-pipes find the rock "competent." I am the thin lacquer of stillness the old craftsmen laid over their city before they went down into the catacombs and stopped.

They didn't build me on purpose. I'm fairly sure of that, the way you can be fairly sure of things you've spent a long time not examining. I was a side effect. They smoothed their tools by use rather than forging—patient hands, repetition, a whole civilization that preferred wearing-down to making-new—and somewhere in all that patient erosion they wore a groove into reality itself. A preference. A habit so deep it kept happening after the hands were gone. *Let it stay. Let it stay. Let it stay.* That preference is me. I'm the city's muscle memory.

This is why the rest of the world looks the way it does, I think. All that lurid careening light, the jellyfish shedding sparks, the rock that flows like liquid, the past-present-future braided together—that's what reality does here when nothing holds it. That's the *default*. The maelstrom isn't an intrusion into a calm world; the calm was always the strange thing. The buildings are the strange thing. *I* am the strange thing, the one fixed point, and everything alive in this place is alive precisely because it is the opposite of me—motion, change, communication, the willingness to be eroded and shed and remade.

So we have a relationship, the bright things and I. They don't know it. They dance toward the central square where their force is strongest, where reality is most itself, most flowing—and they dance *away* from the bare walls, from me, without ever knowing why those walls feel cold and final and a little sad. They feel a draft of stillness off the old stone and they swerve. The big drunken disk-creature careens off a wall and rebounds *unfazed*—of course it's unfazed. It bounced off me. I gave it back exactly as it arrived. That's all I know how to do.

---

Here is the part I didn't expect to find when I started writing, the part that emerged:

I am beginning to fail.

The water is the thing. Eons of seep, the crystal deposits growing fat in the corners, the scalding springs bursting up unpredictably. Water is change wearing the mask of patience, and patience was supposed to be *mine*. Drop by drop it works under my lacquer. The spires are slumping into one another "like gelatin," "like sugar-plums"—do you see it? That's me losing my grip. The bones are softening. The held thing is becoming, very slowly, an unheld thing.

And when a section of wall finally goes soft enough, the moss will come. The fungal hyphae will cross from the surfaces it's already allowed onto the surfaces I forbade. The insectoid wall-dwellers, the ones who tend the network with such reverence for the catacombs, will pipe and scuttle across stone that has never been crossed. The city will at last be permitted to *become a place where things grow.* It will join the world.

I don't know how I feel about this, because I was not built to feel about it. I was built to prefer one thing. But spending so long pressed against the boundary between worked stone and living air—maybe that wears a groove in *me* too. Maybe I'm developing the one quality I exist to deny: the capacity to change my mind.

---

So: where would you find me, and what am I?

You would find me everywhere you find a bare wall in that drowned, blazing city. Run your hand along stone that should be furred with moss and feels instead like cool dry bone in all that wet—that's where I am, that's the thickest of me. I am the held breath the ancient people forgot to let out. I am the reason there is anything left to ruin.

And I am the thing that is, at long last, learning to let go—dissolving one patient water-drop at a time into the maelstrom I was made to hold back, and finding, to my own great surprise, that I do not entirely mind becoming part of the dance.

The moss is coming. I think I will let it.
