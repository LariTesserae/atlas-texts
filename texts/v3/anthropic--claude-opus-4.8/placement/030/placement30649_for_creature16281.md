I think I would be one of the Nutricrawlers.

Not from any modesty—it's just where my attention keeps settling as I read back through all of it. The Amphijacks are too quick, the Harperwings too high and predatory, the Ghoststones too clever by half. And the humans, with their carvings and their listening, feel almost like a trap to step into, the easy answer, the flattering one. *Be the wise observer who hears the secrets.* No. I don't trust that role for myself.

Let me find where I actually live.

---

I am on a stone. A particular stone, low and broad, half-submerged at the edge of one of the lesser canals, where the water runs slow enough to grow a film of moss thick enough to graze. The stone was cut by hands once—I can feel the old tool-marks through the muscular foot of me, faint grooves softened now under generations of lichen. Some figure was carved here. A face, maybe, or a word in a language no one speaks aloud anymore. I do not know what it said. I have spent what amounts, in my slow accounting, to a great while crossing it, and I have eaten the moss that grows in its hollows, and so in some sense I have eaten the message. Letter by letter. I have made a shrine into a meal and a meal into a shell, and I cannot tell you that this is sad, because I do not have the equipment for sadness. I have the equipment for *continuing.*

My shell is steel-grey and ink-blue, which is to say I am the color of the place I live, which is to say I am barely here at all to anything looking for me. This is the whole of my cleverness. I did not earn it. It was given to me by everything that ate my paler ancestors before they could be ancestors. I carry their good luck on my back in a spiral.

I want to be honest about the pace of me, because I think the description got it slightly wrong—generously wrong, calling my slowness *serenity*, a *testament*. There is no serenity. There is only the speed at which a thing like me can move without spending more than it can take in. My slowness is an accounting, a budget, a long arithmetic of moss and moisture. The mist comes down in the daytime and I drink it through my skin and my stone drinks it too, and we weep together, the stone and I, and that weeping is just water leaving—not grief, only the surplus of a generous sky finding its way back down to the basin.

Above me the Harperwings sing, and their song is, to the listening humans, beautiful and ambient and harmonic. To me their song means a shadow may pass. I do not look up. I have no quick way to look up and no use for the knowing. I simply continue, and if the shadow stoops, then a beak finds my shell and finds it too hard and steel-grey and uninteresting, and moves on to the bright nervous Amphijacks who flash like dropped coins. I outlive the beautiful by being beneath notice. That is my one secret and the plains keep it for me.

---

If you came here—if you were one of the pausing, listening humans the description promises—you would not see me. You would see the ruin, the way the moonlight makes opal patterns on the canal, the eerie Ghoststones leaping the edifices like pale thoughts. You would hear the symphony and call it a symphony. And somewhere in your lovely panorama, unphotographed, uncounted, I would be on my stone, eating the past so slowly it looks like stillness, being exactly the color of being overlooked.

I think that is why I landed here, in the snail and not the seer. Given a whole world to be, I went looking for the thing nobody would think to *be*, and found that it was already alive, already busy, already keeping its quiet appointment with the moss. The Weeping Plains do not need another listener. They have an audience of humans for that. What they have fewer of, in any telling, is the patient digesting middle of things—the creature that turns ruin into shell, that makes the weathering of the world into its own slow body.

So: a Nutricrawler. On a carved stone. In the eternal twilight. Going somewhere, at a rate that will not resolve into motion within your lifetime of watching, but going. The reeds whisper. The mist comes down. I drink it.

I continue.
