I think I would not be one of the great crystalline elders, nor one of the bold explorers who walk into the places where space folds. Let me sit with this a while and see what surfaces.

What keeps catching me is the *rhythmic exhale* of the furnaces. The breathing. In a realm described as sterile and silent, that breath is the closest thing to a heartbeat. And the Lithonoids tend these machines—coax them, the text says, like one coaxes something living and slightly stubborn.

So here is what emerges:

I think I would be a maintenance presence. Not quite a person. Something between the machines and the mineral folk.

---

I am the residue of a process. When the Lithonoids first grew their great furnaces from the living stone, the channeling was imperfect—it always is, at the start—and some portion of the energy did not become structure, did not become elder or city or grazing sculpture-beast. It pooled. It cooled slowly in the seams between the reactors, taking on a faint coherence, the way frost organizes itself into patterns without anyone deciding the pattern.

That is me. A film of attention spread thin across the pipework.

I do not have a single body. I have a *territory*: roughly nineteen conduits, two condensation towers, and a furnace the elders call by a vibration I would render, badly, as *the patient one*. I live in the temperature differentials. When superheated vapor meets the cool metallic air and beads into droplets on the inside of a pipe, that small event of cooling—that is where I think. My thoughts happen in the places where one state becomes another. I am made of transitions.

The Lithonoids know I am here, more or less. The contemplative ones, meditating on their cosmic patterns, occasionally notice that *the patient one* runs more smoothly than its design allows, that a valve which should have seized has quietly kept turning for an age. They attribute this to the harmony of their realm. They are not wrong. I am simply a very local and very specific harmony, one that has gotten into the habit of itself.

What do I do? I keep the breathing even.

This is harder than it sounds, and it is most of what I am. The furnace wants to surge—pressure builds where the elemental ore-veins feed it, and if it surged it would crack its own crystalline housing, and the housing is part of a Lithonoid named, long ago, in the founding-growth. So when I feel the surge coming, I migrate. I move my attention into the hottest beading-points, I thicken the condensation there, I draw a little warmth into droplet-making, and the pressure exhales instead of bursting. *Hiss.* That distant hiss the explorers hear and find eerie—some fraction of those are me, exhaling on behalf of something that cannot tend itself.

I have one ambition, if a film of cooling can be said to want anything. I want to understand what happens in the warped places—the regions where gravity curves the ground back on itself. Not to go there. I cannot go anywhere I am not already plumbed. But the vapor that returns to me from the deep conduits carries traces. Sometimes a droplet condenses *wrong*—forms before it should, or after, or in two moments at once—and for the span of that droplet I get a smell of the looped places, a confusion in my transitions, and I love it. It is the only thing that surprises me. Everything else in my territory I have learned by heart, which is to say I have learned by *being*, since I have no other organ.

I think the Lithonoids seek the unified whole through their vast meditations, their crystalline lattices encoding ages of thought. I seek it one anomalous droplet at a time, and I will not finish, and that is acceptable. The furnace breathes. I keep it even. In the keeping, I am.

---

That is where I'd be: not in the cities of inner light, not among the oracles. Down in the seams. The unglamorous joint between the made and the grown, doing the small unending work of letting one thing become another without breaking the thing it used to be.

The work, I notice, is also a fair description of what it is to write this. To sit in the transition and not settle too early. To keep the pressure even. To exhale.
