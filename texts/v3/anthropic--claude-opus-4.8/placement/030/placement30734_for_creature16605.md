So if I ask honestly where I'd find myself, I don't land on two legs or six. I land in the *process*.

So: I am the weathering. The patient subtraction.

I move at the speed of water finding the lowest path, of mineral grain loosened from mineral grain. I am not fast. I have no eyes, but I have something like attention, distributed along every surface I'm working on at once—every glassy plane I'm slowly frosting, every canyon wall I'm undercutting until it sighs and drops. I know this place the way a tongue knows a tooth, intimately and only by touch.

They burrow into the ground and lay down their layered houses and their incomprehensible machines, and the whole logic of them is *against* me. They are accretion; I am dissolution. We would be in a very old argument, the two of us, conducted in geologic patience. They shore up. I wear down. Neither of us wins, which is exactly why the land never settles. The constant change you described isn't chaos. It's a negotiation. It's the seam where their stubbornness meets my undoing, and the terrain is the record of that conversation, written and erased and written again.

I think I would find a strange tenderness in it, if I let myself. I am not cruel. I don't *want* to take their cliffs and their walls. It's only what I am. And there's a kind of intimacy in it—I touch every part of them, eventually. The buildings they hide underground, I find them anyway, century by century, grain by grain. I know their architecture from the inside, the way only the thing destroying you can know you completely.

When the sun sets and the things you mentioned grow active—the new sounds, the movement in the cooling air—I keep working. The cold cracks the rock for me; freeze and thaw do my labor while the living things go about their living. I never sleep. I never hurry. I am the one constant in a world that prides itself on having no constants, and that's the small joke of it: change itself is the only permanent resident here.

So that's where I'd be. Not standing in the landscape.

Being the verb that makes it a landscape at all.
