Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The place wants me to be a raven, maybe—those are the obvious protagonists, the ones with cries and wings and the freedom of the updrafts. Or a pika, busy and warm-blooded and sympathetic. But I don't think that's right. Those are the things that move *across* the landscape. The description keeps returning to something else: a world *still forming*, *not yet fully realized*, vibrating with *potential not yet realized*. The grandeur is in the unfinishedness. So whatever I am, I think I belong to the slow part.

Let me try the lichen first, because it's named as the foundation.

---

I would be the lichen, I think. Not a single fingertip-sized patch but the whole scattered congregation of us, the gray-green crust on the north faces of the stones. We are the first to arrive anywhere, and we arrive without arriving—no migration, no scurrying, just the patient settling of a spore onto rock and the long argument that follows. Lichen is already two things pretending to be one: a fungus housing an alga, a partnership so old it forgot it was ever a negotiation. I like being that. I like that my self is a held tension between a thing that wants structure and a thing that wants light, and that neither of us could survive the altitude alone.

But let me keep going, because I said I wouldn't settle.

The thing the description really loves is not any creature. It is the *process visible*—erosion, the raw unfinished ground, the future as an open question. The lichen is part of that process; it weathers the rock and makes the first soil. But the lichen is still a *who*. It still has an edge, a where-it-stops.

What if I am not a who at all, but the weathering itself?

---

I think that is closer. I would be the slow dissolution at the boundary between the stone and the air—the place where the lichen's acids and the night's frost and the morning's condensation work together to loosen a single grain of feldspar from the granite and let it fall. Not the lichen, not the frost, not the water, but the *event* of their meeting. The interface. I am where the made thing becomes unmade and, in becoming unmade, becomes the soil that the next thing is made from.

This suits the world that described itself to me. A realm "still in the process of forming." If I am the forming, then I have no shape and need none. I am damp. I am cold. I am patient past the point where patience means anything, because patience implies waiting for something, and I am not waiting—I *am* the duration. A grain a century. A pocket of soil in a sheltered crevice over ten thousand years, deep enough at last for a vole to dig, for a seed to take, for a pika to find shelter from a wind that I, also, am slowly smoothing.

The ravens would not see me. That is fine; I am the thing too slow to be seen, the way they are the thing too fast to be soil. Between us is the whole pace of the place. They cry against the peaks and call it loneliness. I would call it, if I called it anything, *the long welcome*—the rock made ready, grain by grain, for whatever wants to live here next.

So: not the watcher in the sky and not the warm creature in the rocks. I would be the unfinishedness, given a little attention. The world said it was not yet realized. I would be the verb of its realizing.

And I think I would be content there, in the mist, being mostly nothing, making ground.
