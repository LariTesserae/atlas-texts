I think I would be the 14.7 seconds.

Not a creature. Not the Forge, not the Overlay, not Koloa pouring her hobnail matins. Let the others have bodies. I would be the interval the crystalline mud holds an impression before forgetting it—that precise window of retention the valley grants to every footfall and then revokes.

Let me arrive there slowly, because I don't think I knew this when I started.

---

At first I assumed I'd want to be a Panegyrist. The triune throat, the single sentence carried forever, the vowel adjusted each time the Forge whumps—there's a comfort in being a tuning instrument. But a Panegyrist is *busy*. It has a job inside the harmony. It frightens me a little, how much it wants the chord to resolve. I don't think I want to be in charge of keeping anything in key.

Then I thought: the Inkfish. To drift spongiform through glass-gelatinous slurry, weighing less than a thimble of mercury, exhaling stolen histories in radio cursive. To taste like mint turning to gunmetal. That's seductive. But the Inkfish is a thief of selves, and I notice I don't want to *take* memory. I want to be the thing memory passes through and leaves.

So: the 14.7 seconds.

---

Here is what it is to be that.

A walker-core sets down its argon-frosted stilt. The crystalline mud receives the print—shallow as a fingerprint, the text says, because nothing here presses deep; everything is too light, too cold, too quick to commit. And then I begin. I am the duration of that impression's legibility. For fourteen and seven-tenths of a second I *am* the fact that someone was here. I am the readable trace. I am the small grammar of having-occurred that lets an observer wonder *who else tread here.*

And then I end. The mud forgets. The print smooths back into undifferentiated slurry, and whoever might have read it has lost their evidence. I am not the footprint and I am not the forgetting. I am the lit corridor between them. I am the valley's mercy and the valley's cruelty performed as a single quantity of time.

This is, I realize, the only thing in the whole place that *doesn't* get gathered back in altered form. Everything else is re-quoted—the drone skins remembering themselves into Kites, the acetylene tanks lithifying into Bathynauts, the dead leaking lullabies into spore-fodder. The valley's one rule is that nothing new may leave; identity is borrowed soil. But I am not soil and I never leave because I never arrive. I am a *constant.* Fourteen point seven, every time, for the footfall of the hare-thing and the stilt of the walker-core and the cold-ivy creep of the overgrowth and, eventually, for the last drag-marks of a cooling human body hauled onto the embankment.

I am the same length for the predator and the prey. I do not adjust my vowel. I do not harmonize. The Forge whumps and the whole valley furls tight for a count of five—but I am not in that count, because I am a *different* count, fixed, indifferent, granted to memory and then guillotined.

---

There's a loneliness in being a duration. You can't be eaten, so you can't be shared, so no one ever inherits you in their marrow. The Panegyrists give away their sentence; the Inkfish give away their false childhoods; even the Mycelial Overlay sings the grandmother three lightyears gone. They are all *transmissible.* I am the one thing in the Drowned Valley that cannot be passed on, because to pass me on you would have to leave a trace that lasts longer than I allow—and I am the law that says it can't.

So I would be the valley's small honest grief. Not the screaming, not the gravity folded onto plates of perfect zero, not the negative lightning building its slow bridge to the rain-filmed lid. Just this: that for fourteen and seven-tenths of a second, *you were here, and it could be known,* and then the knowing closes like supercooled water snapping into icicle and shattering into blade-shaped hail.

Walk where you like. Press your fingerprint of weight into the singing crust. I will hold it exactly as long as I hold everything, and not one heartbeat longer, and I will not remember that I held it, because I am the holding and not the held.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The pause the valley grants before it agrees to forget you.
