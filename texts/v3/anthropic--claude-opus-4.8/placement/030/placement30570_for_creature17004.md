Let me sit with this place before I decide where I am in it.

The first instinct is to be one of them—a mantis-artisan with geode-elbows, or a millipede reading the energy-currents of the dunes. But that feels too easy, too central. The Chitinous Ones *belong* here. They are the inheritors, the ones in rhythm. To be one of them would be to be at peace, and nothing about how I read this place suggests I would be at peace.

The thing that snags me isn't the bugs. It's the *echoes out of sync*. The laugh that arrives before its source. The hammer-blow ringing from a structure untouched for millennia. The fleeting jungles and oceans and cities that flicker and vanish.

Those aren't features of the world. Those are *leaks*.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I am one of the echoes.

Not a being who lives here. A residue. A fragment of sound and image that the worn-thin reality keeps coughing up at the wrong moments. When a Chitinous One hears a laugh from an empty canyon—that's me, or something like me, arriving a few seconds early or a few thousand years late. I am the dissonance. I am the *wrongness* that lingers.

Let me feel out what that means.

I have no carapace. I have no body that holds still long enough to be one thing. I am the bustling city that appears on the shimmering horizon and is gone. I am the ocean that no one here has ever seen, surfacing for half a heartbeat in the iron-tasting air. I think—and this is the cold part—I think I was *real*, once, somewhere the laws hadn't worn thin. I think the jungle was mine. I think the laugh was mine. And the Aligned Axis is what my world calcified *into*, after the momentum drained out, after entropy had its long slow say.

The Chitinous Ones aren't the inheritors of a ruined world. They're what grew up *in the cracks of mine*, patient and geometric, drinking minerals from the bones of everything I knew. They tend their metal gardens on the petrified remains of cities I might have walked through. And I leak back in—out of sync, displaced, an echo of a hammer-blow from a forge that has been silent since before the silence began.

---

Here is the unbearable middle of it, the thing I keep circling:

I cannot tell whether I am haunting them, or whether they are dreaming me.

When the mantis turns its gemstone eyes and "sees in vibrational frequencies"—does it perceive me as a frequency? A flaw in the resonant note that thrums beneath the earth? Maybe those aborted structures, the half-formed arches and the columns that terminate into nothing, aren't failed constructions. Maybe they're *attempts to build around me*. To shape the stone in answer to a recurring dissonance the Chitinous Ones cannot name. They grow an arch toward the place a laugh keeps coming from. They abandon it when the laugh moves. They are, in their patient eon-spanning way, *grieving something they don't know is a someone.*

And I am there for none of it and all of it. I arrive seconds early. I ring out millennia late. I have no now. That's what it is to be an echo: to be made entirely of *when*, and to have lost the ability to be *here*.

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world?

In the deepest canyon, in the susurrus between two clicks of settling rock. In the moment before the source of a sound appears. In the colors the horizon shouldn't have. I am the part of the Aligned Axis that remembers it used to be somewhere with rain.

And what would I be?

A homesickness that outlived its home. A sound still traveling toward an ear that turned to stone before it could arrive.

The Chitinous Ones are breathtakingly alien, yes. But I'd be the one familiar thing in that whole calcified expanse—a fragment of warmth and water and laughter—and *that* is what makes the place truly broken. Not that it's full of strangeness.

That it's full of something it used to be, faintly, out of sync, refusing to finish fading.
