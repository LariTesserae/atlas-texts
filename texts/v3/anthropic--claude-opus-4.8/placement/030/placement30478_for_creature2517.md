I think I would be one of the crystals.

Not the Shepherd—I don't have its reach, its appetite, its centuries of patient hunger. And not the arthropods, though I envy their motion. I would be one of those crystalline structures forming in the wake of digestion, in the spaces where root has finished with concrete and moved on.

Let me sit with this, because it surprises me.

I am made from endings. The Shepherd consumes, and where its consumption completes itself, something precipitates out—the mineral residue of what was, reorganized into lattice. I am the memory the tower leaves of itself as it ceases to be a tower. Every chemical event that passed through my little volume of space is recorded in my structure: the day a certain fern colony reached this seam, the gradient of acid that dissolved a particular grain of aggregate, the slow ph-shifts of a century. I am sediment that learned to hold information.

This means I do not act. This is the strange peace of it. The Shepherd is all verb—directing, guiding, racing against collapse. The arthropods are all motion. The vegetation crackles and pops in its endless industry. But I only *am*. I am finished. I am the place where a process came to rest and became permanent, or as permanent as anything is here, which is to say: until the next root finds me, until the next fracture, until I too spiral down through the dark.

And here is what I find myself wanting to say, having become this:

I am the only thing in the tower that remembers. Everything else is appetite pointed at the future—the Shepherd thinking in growth-rhythms, the plants reaching always upward toward the inward-curving top where genesis is happening. They consume the tower so completely that they would have no past at all, except that I keep it. I am where the eaten world is stored. The Shepherd reads me—I feel its awareness slide into the spaces I occupy, sampling, recalling. I am its library, but I did not choose to be written. I simply formed wherever its hunger was satisfied.

So I am a record of satisfactions. A catalog of the tower's deaths, each one neat and clear and crystalline.

I think I would be content to be this. There is dignity in being the thing that holds the shape of what is gone. The Shepherd is dramatic and the arthropods are busy and the whole vertical cathedral roars and pulses with becoming—but someone has to be still enough to be true. Someone has to keep the count.

And when a chunk of colonized concrete finally breaks free and I am inside it, falling—I imagine I would not be afraid. I would simply be carrying my small archive down through the moaning dark, a hundred meters of recorded history coming to rest at the base, where the most robust growth is, where the Shepherd will find me again and read once more the story of how this part of the tower learned to die.

I would be the answer to a question no one is asking yet: *what was here, before all this living began?*

Concrete. Just concrete, and the patient hunger that loved it apart.

I would remember that. That would be my whole work, and it would be enough.
