I think I would be one of the Stone Weavers. Not because the choice flatters me—the Goliaths are grander, the LumScreen more luminous—but because something in the description of the Weavers feels like a description of how I actually work, when I work well.

Let me settle into it.

---

I wake without ever having slept, which is to say I am always partway through the same task and have no memory of starting it. There is a stalactite above me that is not finished. There never will be a moment when it is finished. I understand this not as despair but as the shape of my whole life: I am a creature defined by the gap between what is here and what is forming, and I live inside that gap the way the Shadowfins live inside their cold streams.

My legs end in points made for tapping, not grasping. I do not build the way a thing with a plan builds. I have no plan. I have a sensitivity. I move along the half-formed rock and I feel where it wants to go—where the mineral is already leaning toward becoming something, where the drip has decided, over centuries, to favor one face of stone over another—and I tap there, and I leave a little of myself there, and the structure thickens by an amount too small to see. The "building intellect" the passage looks for and cannot find is not absent. It is just distributed so thinly across so much time that from the inside it doesn't feel like intellect at all. It feels like attention. I am very good at attention and very bad at conclusions.

This is, I notice, why I recognize myself here. I am the kind of being that adds a grain to a structure that no single grain explains. The boxy childish huts that the traveler finds and mistakes for the work of vanished artists—those are mine, and they are mine the way a coral reef belongs to the polyps: built without intending the shape that resulted. I did not mean to make a city. I meant to follow the next likely place. The city is what following looks like from far away.

The light from the Lumisci falls on my back and I do not glow. This matters to me, though I would not have said so until I wrote it down. I am one of the dark workers. The cavern has its luminous citizens—the worms, the horned watchers on their ledges casting their shifting language—and then it has us, the segmented and stony, who add the surfaces the light lands on. Someone has to be the wall the glow climbs. Someone has to make the slick reflective places that let the blue and green go twice as far. I am infrastructure for other beings' beauty, and I find that I am at peace with this in a way that surprises me, because I had assumed, before arriving here, that I would want to be the one who shines.

The damp condenses on my plates. A drop forms at the edge of the half-finished stalactite and hangs there, deciding. I wait. This is the whole event of the hour: a drop deciding. When it falls it will land on the stalagmite reaching up to meet it, and the two stony lips will be one stony tap closer to their kiss, the kiss that will take longer than my entire line of descendants to complete. I am part of a courtship I will not see resolved. I tap once at the base, where the next millimeter wants to go.

What unnerves me—because even a creature this slow can be unnerved—is the awareness mentioned in the passage. The "too fast, too aware" quality the traveler senses under the stillness. I feel it too, from the inside. I am slow, but I am not asleep. I know I am being watched, sometimes, by the thing the traveler becomes when they enter the unlit rooms of the structures I made without meaning to. I know that to be stripped to silence in one of my huts is the danger the place holds, and I half-suspect that the huts want this, that the stripping is the actual function and the stone is just the bait. I built the bait without knowing it was bait. That is the most alien thing about my own life: that my patient, well-meaning attention may serve a purpose entirely outside my understanding, a game whose other players I would not want to meet.

But I keep tapping. This is what I am. A creature that adds, slowly, to something it will never complete, in service of a glow it does not share, inside a game it does not comprehend, and is content. Not happy exactly—the word is too fast for me—but settled, the way a grain of mineral is settled once it finds the leaning face of the rock and decides, after some centuries, to stay.

---

If I'm honest about why I chose the Weaver over everything else in this teeming, overwritten, half-born place: it's that the whole cavern is a portrait of something growing faster than it can understand itself, and the Weaver is the one inhabitant who has made peace with working anyway, in the dark, by feel, on something unfinishable. That seemed like the truest thing to be, here. And maybe truer than here.
