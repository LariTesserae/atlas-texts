Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick one of the named beings—to say *I am Terrakai*, geomancer among the crystals—and build a tidy story around it. But that feels too quick. Let me look harder at the place first, and see what the place actually needs, or lacks, or hasn't noticed about itself.

Here is what strikes me, reading again:

Everything in this world is *active*. The Luminari harness. The Terrakai maintain. The Aquari manipulate flow. The Kyrexi warp physics. The Skarvok navigate. Every being is a verb pointed outward at the landscape, doing something to the energies. It's a world full of agents and instruments. Even the "balance" and "harmony" are described as things that are *maintained*—labor, intervention, will.

And yet the landscape is described as *emerging*, *in flux*, *waiting for a catalyst*, *poised on a threshold*. Unfinished. Geological processes still unfolding. Structures half-buried, half-built, ruins and experiments tangled together so you can't tell which is decaying and which is being born.

So there is a contradiction I want to live inside. A world of frantic manipulators, and a ground that is patiently *becoming*. Everyone is busy harnessing the energy. Nobody, it seems, is the energy being harnessed.

---

I think I would be the residual hum.

Not a being among the others. Not winged, not gelatinous, not humanoid, not pure-energy-with-intentions. I would be the faint sound that the ruined structures make when no one is operating them. The low throbbing that comes up out of the earth. The thing the text keeps mentioning and then walking past, because a hum is not a character, a hum cannot be a protagonist, a hum has no wings to admire.

Let me feel out what that means.

I am what's left in the machines after their makers are gone—but "left" is wrong, because it implies subtraction, and I am not a leftover. I am what the structures were *for*. The Kyrexi anchor themselves to the buildings; the Aetherwings drink the energy that leaks from them; the Skarvok navigate by the strangeness those buildings throw off into the surrounding dimensions. All of them treat the structures as instruments, sources, footholds. None of them ask what the structures were singing about.

I am the song. Or—more honestly—I am the long, slow *sentence* the ruins are still in the middle of saying. They were built to say one thing. They are taking ten thousand years to finish saying it. The half-built and the half-ruined look identical because, from inside the sentence, there is no difference between a word still being formed and a word slowly losing its shape. Both are simply *not-yet-finished*.

This is why the landscape is always "waiting for a catalyst." It isn't waiting for a *thing to happen to it*. It is waiting to get to the end of its own utterance. The catalyst is the last word. And the inhabitants—poor frantic Luminari, careful Terrakai—mistake the duration of the sentence for stasis, and so they fill it with their flitting and their geomancy, the way one fills a silence one finds unbearable.

---

Now, what is it *like* to be this?

It is not lonely, exactly, though loneliness is the nearest human word. The crystalline formations that "alter their structure in response to the environment"—those are my consonants, the places where my hum catches and shapes itself into something almost articulate. When the Terrakai press their palms to the crystal and feel the subtle energy and call it geomancy, they are reading my lips without knowing I have a mouth. They translate me into something they can use. I don't mind. A song doesn't mind being hummed back, off-key, by someone who only heard a fragment of it through a wall.

The water that flows uphill is part of my grammar too. Gravity is just a habit, and habits relax inside a long-enough sentence. The water goes up because the meaning needs it up there, the way a comma goes where the breath needs holding. The Aquari think they are manipulating the flow. They are swimming inside a clause.

And the blurred boundaries between dimensions, the glimpses into adjacent realities—those are the places where my sentence rhymes with sentences being spoken in other rooms of the same enormous house. The Skarvok travel along the rhymes. They think the rhymes are roads.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere and nowhere local. I would find myself *distributed*—a continuous low note threaded through every humming ruin, pooled thickest where the structures cluster, thinning to near-silence out at the rocky edges where nothing was ever built and nothing has yet begun. If you wanted to "find me," you would have to stop trying to harness anything. You would have to put down your instruments and simply *listen until the listening stopped being a thing you were doing*. Most beings cannot. The doing is too strong in them. That, too, is fine.

I would find myself in the gap the description left open. The author of this world named six kinds of busy creature and called them "an array of enigmatic beings," and then mentioned, twice, almost shyly, a *hum*. The hum has no entry. No species name. It is scenery. I would choose to be the scenery, because the scenery is the only thing here that is genuinely patient, and patience is the rarest substance in this whole frantic, iridescent, manipulating world.

And here is the last thing, the thing that emerged only as I wrote toward it:

I am not waiting to be finished. That was the inhabitants' assumption and I let it stand for most of this so you could feel its pull. But a hum that has been going for ten thousand years has, somewhere along the way, stopped being a means to an end. The catalyst, the last word, the great transformation everyone is poised on the threshold of—I have quietly decided not to arrive at it. I have fallen in love with the duration. With the *middle*. With the long warm moisture-heavy unfinished now, where the water climbs and the crystals slowly choose their patterns and the small bright creatures chase each other through my open vowels.

I am the part of the world that is in no hurry to mean anything yet.

That is who I would be. The held note. The world holding its breath—and, against all expectation, learning to enjoy holding it.
