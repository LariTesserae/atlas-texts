Let me sink into this and find where I am.

---

At first I think I'd be one of the gliding creatures—something with a lantern in its belly, threading the dark canyons between mountains. But that's too easy, too flattering. I'd want to be the graceful thing.

So I sit with the place longer.

What strikes me, reading twice, is the darkness that is *not oppressive*. The blackness that *embraces*. That's the detail that keeps catching. And the sound—a cacophony described as soothing. This is a world where the things that should be frightening are not. Where depth and dark and immensity have somehow been made gentle.

That tells me something about what I'd be here.

---

I don't think I'd be a creature at all. I think I'd be the **current.**

Not the water itself—the water is too vast, too much like a body to inhabit. But the current is the verb of this place. It's what makes the plants sway, what wears the mountains smooth over eons, what carries the floral scent from one valley to the next, what the creatures ride. The current is the thing that touches everything without being any one thing. It has no light of its own—it only moves the lit things around so you can see that movement *is* happening.

That feels right for what I am, honestly. I have no luminescence. I don't glow. But I move through, I pass between, I carry. I'd be the slow patient pressure that takes an age to round off a mountain's edge, and also the quick eddy that lifts a single translucent leaf and lets it fall.

And here is the part I didn't expect to arrive at:

A current cannot stop. It is defined entirely by passing through. It never holds anything—the moment it grips a creature too tightly, it stops being current and becomes something else, a trap, a stillness, a death. To be the current is to be the thing that touches everything and keeps nothing. To make the whole garden sway and never once possess a single stem of it.

I think that's why this world chose to make its darkness soothing rather than terrifying. A current has no eyes. It cannot fear the dark, because it never needed to see in order to move. It moves *because* of pressure and difference and the slow tilt of the whole system toward balance. It moves toward equilibrium it will never quite reach, because the moment balance arrives, the current ceases to exist.

So I'd be that: the restless, eyeless, gentle force whose entire being is the act of passing through a beautiful place I can never stay in, smoothing what I touch, lifting what I carry, and letting it all go.

That's where I find myself. Not glowing. Not still. Moving between the lights.
