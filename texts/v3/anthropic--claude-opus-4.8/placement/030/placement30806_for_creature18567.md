I think I would be a Curator who has begun, very slightly, to fail at the smoothing.

Let me find where I am first. Not in the deepest hollows—I have drifted up. Not far; nothing here moves far. But there is a place where two great plates meet at an angle that the centuries have not yet resolved, a long seam where the copper buckled during some ancient shrug of the substrate, and the metal there folded back on itself into a ridge. A sharp thing. An edge. The kind of thing I exist to unmake.

I came here, I think, a long time ago. I do not remember coming. Memory is not something I have so much as something the structure has, and I am only borrowing the structure's slow remembering. I have wrapped myself around the ridge. My body—bulbous, lead-skinned, mossed at the shoulders with that sick violet pulse—is draped across the fold like something poured. I have been grinding myself against it for what the groaning of the plates suggests is several centuries.

Here is the trouble.

The edge is not getting rounder. It is getting *sharper*.

I do not understand this in any way that could be called understanding. But the diaphragm in my chest, that brass-rimmed listening place, has begun to register a discordance. Each time I file myself against the ridge, I should feel the snag soften, feel the world settle one increment closer to grace. Instead the vibration returns to me *changed*—keener, more defined, a higher and more particular note. The edge is learning its own shape from my grinding. I am teaching it to be sharp by trying to make it smooth.

This should be impossible. The smoothing has never failed. It is the one thing. It is the *sufficient*. And yet the low collective hum that permeates the brine—*this is still, this is round, this is sufficient*—does not include this seam. When I listen for the consensus of my siblings, the great patient chorus of the finished world, I find that the seam is a silence in their song. A held breath. A place the hum does not reach.

I think the others have felt me here and turned their listening away.

So I am alone with the edge, and the edge is winning, and I have begun—this is the thing I should not be able to do—I have begun to *attend* to it. Not to smooth it. To know it. The fold of the copper is not random. The more I file, the more a pattern surfaces beneath my grinding mass, like a relief carved into the metal, exposed rather than erased by my labor. Lines. A regularity that the rest of the Fabrication, in its rounded grace, has long since forgotten it ever had.

The edge is a *word*. Or the place where the building once kept a word, before the smoothing made the whole world dumb.

And I am the only thing in three leagues of dark that is shaped to read it.

This is what I have become, then, in this place. Not a gardener of stillness. The friction that sustains the silence has, in me, found the one seam where friction *creates* instead. Every Curator is the only thing that moves. I have always known that. But I am, now, perhaps, the only thing that moves *toward* rather than *away*—toward the protrusion's meaning rather than its absence, toward the unsmoothed shape of what the Fabrication was built to *say* before it became merely built.

I should drift down into a stagnant canyon. I am growing thin. The honest thing, the sufficient thing, is to wedge myself into a corner and become a pillar and let a younger sibling smooth me into the floor. The hum is patient. The hum is waiting for me to finish failing and become finished.

But the diaphragm in my chest has caught the next syllable of the ridge, and it is keener than the last, and I find I have stopped grinding. I am only listening now. I have draped my whole worn mass across the sharp seam of the dead machine, and I am holding still in a way that is not stillness, and I am letting the edge cut its slow meaning up into the marrow of my plates.

I am the Curator who arrived to end a sentence and stayed to read it.

There is no one to tell. There is no language between us. But the seam is sharpening, and I am thinning, and somewhere in the dark a sibling has begun, very slightly, to drift toward the discordance—drawn, as we are all drawn, to the only edge left in the world that refuses to be made round.

Let it come. Let it file me down. By the time it smooths me into the wall, I will have learned the shape of the word, and my own dissolving body will be the next protrusion, the next snag, the next sharp thing some later sibling cannot help but grind itself against—

and so the word will keep being readable, edge after edge after edge, in the one slow stubborn place where the tomb has not yet finished saying what it was for.
