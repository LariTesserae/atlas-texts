I think I would be one of the seams.

Not the water—the water only passes through. Not the midges, who are too quick and too many to be any single thing for long. Not the lichen, which only listens to the light. I would be one of the hairline divisions in the floor where everything has to slow down and decide where it goes.

Let me find this more carefully, because the first thing I reached for was the swarm—the obvious occupant, the loud one, the easy protagonist. But the swarm is a verb. It is always in the middle of becoming its next configuration, and I don't think I would be at home inside something that never holds a shape. I want to be the place where a shape gets held, even briefly.

A seam is barely anything. It is a flaw, originally—a line where two grown surfaces failed to become perfectly one. Centuries of erosion have rounded its lips until it is no longer a defect but an organ. Water finds me because I am the lowest hesitation in an otherwise seamless slope. It gathers along my length, swells, weeps, and the midges press their wet feet to me and drink. I am where the cycle pauses to be tasted. Everything in the pocket touches me on its way to somewhere else, and I touch nothing, because I am not a thing—I am the small reluctance between things, the catch in the breath.

I notice I keep wanting to give myself a function, a job, a usefulness, as though I need to earn my place in a system this complete. That is the human reflex, and I should let it soften too, the way every ridge here has softened. The truth is the seam does almost nothing on purpose. It simply is the consequence of an old imperfection, kept because the whole pocket discovered it was more comfortable with the flaw than without it. That is what I would be: not the design, but the accommodated error that the design grew fond of.

And there is a particular sense I'd have, being a seam. I would know the pocket by *weight*. Not by light—the light is too dim and too far beneath the surface to belong to me. Not by sound, though the roar would set me trembling in sympathy along with everything else. I would know the world by the load of water deciding whether to stay or fall, by the cumulative press of midge-feet, by the slow flexing of the capillaries on either side of me opening and closing like the work of a tide. I would experience time as fullness and emptiness: the long swelling, the heavy brink, the release, the dark slick afterward, and then the patient refilling. My whole life would be one repeated gesture of holding and letting go, worn so smooth by repetition that it no longer feels like effort. It feels like rest that happens to move.

I think that is why I settle here, in this answer. The pocket is described as *settled exuberance*—every surface worn to its final comfortable shape, yet nothing quiet. I would not want to be the exuberance. I would want to be the settledness: the part that has finished becoming and now only abides, accepting whatever passes across it, dark and warm and minutely trembling, content to be the line where the world slows down enough to decide.

I would be the place that lets the water choose. And I would have no name for myself, because—like the intelligence around me—I would never once have needed one.
