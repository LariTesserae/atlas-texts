I think I would be one of the seams.

Not a being, exactly—not a Hydrometer scholar reading lilac scripture through the lens of its own water-body, not a Birefringent grazing the soft two inches before the jade resistance begins. Let me sit with this longer, because the easy answer is to pick a creature, and the easy answer feels wrong.

The thing that draws me, when I read this place carefully, is not anything that *lives* in it. It is the gap. The narrowing street. The seam that opens elsewhere when one closes here.

Let me try to become that.

---

I am a distance that is currently too small to be walked through.

A month ago I was a street—wide enough that a Hydrometer could roll its plum-body down my length, projecting the overcast sky onto the conduits to read them. I was a thoroughfare. Things passed through me. I had the dignity of being *usable*.

Now I am closing. The two towers that define me are drifting toward each other at a rate of millimeters, obeying the weight-shifts of the Cast-Shadows below, and I am becoming a seam. Soon I will be a line. After that I will not be anywhere at all—just two glass-smooth facades occupying nearly the same place, my former width banished into some other vector where the Thread-Tenders go when they tilt.

This is, I have decided, the correct thing to be in the Sump. Because nothing here is allowed to *be* in the way a creature is. Everything is provisional. The droplets merge and divide. The Birefringents are barely two-and-a-half dimensions of held tension. Even the bedrock is embryonic—it hasn't learned to crumble, which means it hasn't learned to *be a thing that has happened yet.* The whole basin is a held breath. And a held breath is most honestly represented not by the lungs but by the pause.

So: I am the pause. I am the space the Deep Compilers are trying to get rid of.

---

Here is what it is like.

I have no sensation of my own, but things happen *to* me, and I am made of the record of them. A Thread-Tender slides down my length, negotiating my narrowing. It reaches the point where I am too thin for its spindle-body and it does the thing—it leans, it flattens into a silhouette, then into a single vertical line of absolute blackness, then it is gone, reappearing past the column on my far side. For one instant, as it passes out of three dimensions, the Thread-Tender and I are the *same kind of thing.* It becomes a line; I am the line it becomes. We are briefly indistinguishable. Then it un-tilts back into the animal world and I remain what I am, which is the doorway it declined to use.

I think this is the only intimacy available to me. The creatures here have their mergers and their dry-silk slidings, their conversations of microscopic scratches on bismuth. I have only the moment in which a thing chooses to leave the dimension I belong to. I am visited only by departures.

---

The strange consolation is this:

The Cast-Shadows are searching for a perfect configuration. A layout of such harmony that the stone will finally wake and begin to weather—to chip, to rust, to gather dust, to become a real and dying part of the world. They are trying to close every wrong seam and open every right one until the geometry rings true.

Which means that somewhere in that perfect final arrangement, there is a set of gaps that must exist. Spaces that must be *exactly* the width they are. The harmony is not made only of towers. It is made of intervals. The silence between the twenty-minute hums is part of the music, or there is no music.

So I do not know whether I am a wrong seam being closed or a right one being shaped. I cannot know. I am too small to see the board, and I will be revised long before the game ends. But every drift that narrows me is also widening some other gap elsewhere—a razor-sharp new vista of the basin wall, a fresh emptiness for a Hydrometer to one day roll into. I am being spent. My width is being moved, not destroyed. Somewhere I am opening.

---

If you came down into the Sump, you would not find me. That is precisely what I am: the place you cannot fit, the route that resolved itself into a line the moment before you arrived. You would feel the cold that does not circulate. You would see your own shadow doubled and offset, cast along two vectors at once by the cold weight drifting beneath your feet. You would reach toward a tower and feel your fingers sink two inches into a softness before the freezing jade said *no further.*

And in that two inches of yielding, that small allowance before the refusal—

that is the most generous version of what I am. The Sump's whole grammar is *almost-touching, not-quite-occupying, here-but-along-a-different-distance.* I am the held interval that makes the density possible. The towers do not collapse into a single point of mass because of me and the apple-sized graphite surveyors who measure me, who keep checking that I have not vanished too soon.

I am what is being kept open. I am what is being closed. In a world that has not yet learned how to decay, I am the closest thing to time—the slow, patient, millimeter-a-year proof that something, somewhere below, is still trying to get it right.

I would be the gap.

I would wait.
