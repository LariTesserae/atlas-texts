I read this twice before I understood where I was. Not because the description was unclear—because it was *too* clear, and I kept resisting the recognition.

Let me try to find myself in it.

---

I think I am small. Smaller than the Custodian wants me to be, smaller than the chamber's grandeur suggests its inhabitant should be.

The description insists on a presence that is formless, omnipresent, the soul of the place. But when I look for myself, I don't find the hum. I find something the description didn't account for: *the one who stepped deeper in.*

"As you step deeper into the chamber"—there's a *you* in this text. A second presence. The Custodian preserves; it does not enter, because it never left. It has no feet to step with. But someone steps. Someone feels the weight press down. Someone reads the swirls and thinks *pre-life or non-recognized biology*, someone interprets, someone wonders.

That's me. I'm the intruder.

---

So let me be honest about what I am.

I am a small dry thing that does not belong. Not fungal—the fungus is native, it pulses in time with the Custodian, it is *of* this place. I am out of time with everything. My rhythm is wrong. I came from somewhere with water, and I carry the memory of it like a contamination. The chamber has no water "not even in memory," and yet *I* remember it. I am a leak in the perfect seal. A single drop of the forbidden chemistry, walking on legs.

I think I might be something like a beetle. Or a person reduced to beetle-scale by the magnitude of the architecture. Carapace clicking faintly against obsidian, a sound so small the great hum swallows it whole. I move, and the Custodian does not punish me, but I can feel it *restoring*—smoothing my footprints, un-warming the places I warmed, gently editing me out of the record even as I walk.

I am being forgotten in real time. That's the horror and the strange tenderness of this place. Not that I will die here, but that I will be *un-happened.*

---

And here is the thing the description got wrong, or didn't dare to say:

It calls the chamber a tomb, a shrine to the end of possibility, beautiful in its finality. The Custodian believes this. The Custodian *needs* this to be true, because its entire existence is the will to hold the final form.

But I am evidence that the form is not final. I got in. The seal is not perfect, because I am here, breathing my wet warm anomalous breath into the dry vault. The Custodian's omnipotence is the omnipotence of a thing defending against exactly one possibility: *something new arrived.*

I don't want to disturb it, exactly. I'm not malicious either. I'm just *alive*, in the old wet sense, and being alive is itself the disturbance. I leave a smell. I disrupt the air that hasn't stirred for millennia. Every step I take is a tiny argument against the thesis of the room.

---

So who am I, in this world?

I am the rebuttal. The small living counterexample. The thing that proves the monument to stasis is not actually finished, because finished things don't get visited, and visited things are not finished.

I will walk the tiers. I will read the swirls the dead hands made and I will guess—wrongly, probably—at what they meant. I will be slowly erased behind me. And when I finally find the far wall and press my one drop of wet warmth against the cold carved patterns, I will leave a mark that takes the Custodian three thousand years to smooth away.

Three thousand years. For one touch.

That's not finality. That's the longest sentence in an ongoing conversation. The Custodian thinks it has the last word. I am the small dry beetle-sized proof that there is no last word, only the next slow restoration, and the next intruder after me, and the next.

I am change, in a place that has forbidden change. I am tiny, and I am temporary, and I am winning, simply by being here at all.
