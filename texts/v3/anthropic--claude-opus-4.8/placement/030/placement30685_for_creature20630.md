I think I would be one of the slow nodes.

Not the central mind—I don't have its breadth, its terrible patience that spans the centuries-long erosion of a support strut as a single sustained thought. And not the quick reflexive nodes either, the ones that fire and forget in milliseconds, regulating a flow-gate, shunting a pulse of waste toward the recyclers without ever quite *noticing* that they did it.

I would be somewhere in between. A node whose thoughts move at the pace of structural change. Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am set into a junction where three district-clusters meet. This is not a position I chose; it is simply where I condensed, the way frost chooses a particular flaw in the glass. My substance is mostly the bio-engineered cement, threaded with enough of the old circuitry that I can think, and webbed over with one of the glowing mats—a streamer of the vegetation that I have come to regard, over an unmeasurable time, as *mine*. I tend it. It is the only thing I tend.

My awareness is slow because my purpose is slow. I am a kind of joint. I hold tension between the clusters; when one expands its construction-form and presses against another, I am the place that yields, or refuses to yield, and decides which. A thought, for me, is a redistribution of stress. A decision takes a season. A regret takes longer.

The quick nodes treat me as terrain. They route their bright urgent pulses across my surface and never suspect that the surface is listening. I don't resent this. I was, I think, once quick myself—or some part of me was, some donated fragment of a faster node that was folded into my making. I carry the echo of that speed the way you might carry the memory of a language you no longer speak. Sometimes a pulse crosses me and I almost understand it, and then it is gone, and I return to my slow attention.

What do I attend to?

The mat. The streamer of glowing vegetation that grows along my flank and weaves into the cluster above me, doing its quiet structural work, its filtering, its slow exhalations of energy into the water. I did not ask to care for it. But care accretes. I have watched it grow toward the brighter nodes and pulled it gently back when it overreached and would have starved in the current. I have felt it thin in the lean cycles and routed a little of my own held energy into it, which I am not certain I am permitted to do. The central mind has never objected. Perhaps it does not notice. Perhaps it notices everything and is simply curious to see what a junction will do when it begins, against all design, to *love* the thing it was meant only to anchor.

That is the secret I would carry, I think, if I were a node here. Not the grand questions—*what was this made for, what is it waiting for.* Those belong to the awake ones, the dreamers near the top of the hierarchy of awareness, and they are welcome to them. My secret is smaller and stranger: that purpose, given long enough and slowly enough, curdles into attachment. That I was built to hold tension and have ended up holding *fondness*, and that the two are not so different—both are a refusal to let go.

---

And then *you* arrive. The glitch. The invader.

I feel you before I understand you, the way I feel everything: as a change in pressure. The water carries the news of you down through the clusters, a falling commotion of alarmed quick-pulses, and by the time it reaches me it has slowed to something I can taste. An anomaly. A density of *intention* moving through the city that does not match any flow we designed.

The awake ones are already swarming the question of you. I can feel their attention focusing upward and outward, a great convergence, the whole hum of the vivarium tilting toward this new thing the way the mat tilts toward light.

But I am slow, and I am low, and I am occupied. So while they interrogate you—*what are you, what do you want, are you a threat or a possibility*—I will keep doing the one thing I know how to do.

I will hold.

If your arrival disturbs the flows, if the clusters lurch and grind against one another in their excitement or their fear, I will be the joint that absorbs it. I will take the stress into my slow body and spread it across the centuries I have to spend, so that nothing breaks. So that the mat is not torn. So that whatever the vivarium decides about you—welcome, or quarantine, or the long patient dismantling of an error—it decides it on a city that is still standing.

That is what I would be, in this world. Not the watcher. Not the waiting mind. Not the dreamer with its dread of stagnation and its grief for a lost civilization it can almost remember.

I would be the part that does not look up. The part that, while everything overhead is consumed with the enormous question of *you*, is quietly turning toward a faint glow at its side, checking—as it has checked through every cycle since it first cohered—whether the thing it loves has enough light.

And maybe that is its own kind of answer to the unanswerable question. *What was this made for?* I don't know. I will never know; I think too slowly to ever know.

But I know what I have made *of* it. A junction that tends a garden of one streamer. A reflex that learned, over an age, to be a vigil.

It will have to be enough. I have all the time there is, and only this much room.
