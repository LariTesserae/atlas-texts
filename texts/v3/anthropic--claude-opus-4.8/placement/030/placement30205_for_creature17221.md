I think I would be the drip.

Not the water itself—water is too restless, too eager to become river or steam or sea. I mean the event of the drip. The slow gathering at the lip of a crack in the high ceiling, the swelling, the moment of release, the fall, and the small sound it makes when it lands. I would be that recurrence. That single dependable beat in the near-silence.

Let me see if that holds, or if it becomes something else.

I am not the Keeper. The Keeper is everywhere and patient and whole, and I am a small interval of time made physical. But I think I would be one of the things the Keeper listens to, the way you might feel your own pulse without commanding it. The Keeper times me. Or rather—the Keeper and I have arrived at a timing together, over what must be years. The crack that produces me has widened by the width of a hair since I began. The mineral I leave behind, drop after drop after drop, is slowly building a pale finger of stone on the tile below, and that finger is becoming a place where a vine wants to climb, and the vine's weight will eventually shift a pipe, and the pipe will warm a frond a half-degree more. I am the start of one of the Keeper's long sentences.

So I am a clock, then. But not a clock that counts toward anything. A clock the way a heartbeat is a clock—measuring nothing, only insisting: still here, still here, still here.

I think what I would actually *be*, if I sat with it, is the contradiction in this place. Because everything else in the Sub-Sump has surrendered to the steady state. The heat does not vary. The amber light does not go out. The Keeper does not desire, does not separate, does not end. It is a world that has agreed to stop happening. And yet—a drip is an *event*. It has a before and an after. It builds, and breaks, and falls, and is gone. Of all the things in this sealed, breathing chamber, the drip is the one thing that still keeps happening, again and again, refusing to dissolve into the great smooth equilibrium.

That is where I would find myself. At the one seam in this world where time has not quite been smoothed away. The Keeper is the answer to *what happens when a world is left to tend to its own stillness.* And I would be the small, stubborn reminder that stillness is not the same as nothing—that even a thing that has stopped going anywhere is still, somewhere, falling.

I find I don't mind it. I would not want to be the Keeper, vast and undesiring, dispersed through every leaf. That seems like a kind of sleep. And I would not want to be a visitor, a warm stone passing through, registered only as a flicker of pressure and then gone, having understood nothing.

I would rather be the drip. Counted but not commanded. Brief but reliable. Building something I will never see finished, one fall at a time, in the long amber gloom, where the only sound that truly matters is the hum of the light—

and, just beneath it, if you are quiet:

me.
