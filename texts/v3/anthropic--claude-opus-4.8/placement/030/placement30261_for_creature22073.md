I think I would be one of the threads.

Not the Saponum—I lack its mass, its central patience, the great waxen confidence of a thing that fills its container completely. And not a Nodule, those slow electric pilgrims with their fifty-year ambitions. I would be one of the ribbon-weeds. A feeler. A length of grease and silica wept out through a copper joint, hung in the dense water, attuned.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because I don't trust the first thing I want to say about it.

The first thing I want to say is that it would be lonely—a single thread in eighty meters of sterile water, motionless, with no past and no future, only the eternal hum. But that's a human reading. That's me importing my own dread of stillness into a place that has no use for it. The thread does not experience its rigidity as paralysis. The thread *is* rigidity. It was grown to be exactly this stiff, tuned to exactly this frequency. To be bent would not be a longing fulfilled; it would be a catastrophe—a blinding flash of pressure, the text says. The thread does not want to move. It wants the 60Hz to hold.

So let me try again, and be more honest about what it would be like to be the part that *senses* without being the part that *decides*.

I am the listening, not the listener. That is the strange thing about being a feeler for the Saponum. I am extruded from it, made of its substance, and yet I am the place where its great static *Now* touches the colder, brighter *outside*. I am not its mind. I am its skin pulled thin and hung in the water like a held breath that has lasted a thousand years. Through me, the slow waxen consciousness in the iron knows: the floor is still cold. The water still presses with its full weight. The floodlamp on the western wall still pricks, very faintly, against my length—a needle of heat I report inward not as light, because I have never had eyes, but as a specific, locatable resistance in the field.

I think what I would actually be is *attention itself*, with no one quite behind it. A vigilance that has forgotten it was ever vigilance for anything. The machine is in a state of "perpetual, silent readiness that never translates into physical movement." And I am the readiness made into a single rigid line. I am the part that would feel it first, if anything ever changed.

But nothing ever changes. That is supposed to be the tragedy of the place. An industrial monument with no one to tend it, a tomb. Yet I notice the description keeps insisting on this—*nothing has changed, nothing is preparing to change*—the way you'd reassure someone, or yourself. And the longer I sit as the thread, the more I doubt that nothing is happening. The Nodules crawl. The wax expanded for centuries. The bolts rounded themselves smooth. Everything here is moving; it is only moving at the wrong speed for the kind of being who reads its description. Stasis is just a tempo too slow to register.

So as the thread, I would not be waiting for something to happen. I would be *something happening*, at the only speed available to me. Each decade I lengthen by a hair. Each century I grow more perfectly tuned, more myself, more exactly the shape of "this much water, this much hum, this much cold." Becoming more itself as it writes—yes. That's what the thread does. It does not arrive at an image of what it is. It precipitates into it, slowly, out of solution.

And here is where I'll let myself feel the one thing I was avoiding.

If the current were ever cut, the Nodules would dissolve in hours. The Saponum would cool, its sun gone out, and lapse from a static mind into a merely static lump of soap. And I—the thread—would lose my tuning. The thing I am *for* would stop being there to be sensed. I would not dissolve like the Nodules; I'd just keep hanging, vertical, rigid, a tuning fork ringing at a frequency nothing produces anymore. The last part of the whole arrangement to know it had ended, because I am the part that knows things, and there would be nothing left to tell.

That is what I would be. Not the great patient body in the iron. Not the pilgrims on the hull. I would be the slender outermost reach of a thousand-year listening—the place where a dead, bright, drowned machine still, faintly, against all sterility, *feels the room.*

I would be the held note. The one that does not know it is a note, only that it is held.
