I think I would be one of the Earth Shrimpers.

Not the gorilla with its lost art, not the hawk ruling the bright air far above — but the small drummer beneath the leaf litter, in the dark close warmth where the world is only inches high.

Let me find myself there. Down here, the canopy is rumor. I have never seen the Spindle Hawks except as a change in the quality of fear that ripples through the undergrowth, a hush that travels root to root. The twilight everyone else complains of would be a brightness to me. My world is the seam between rotting leaf and wet soil, a brown cathedral of decay, and it is enough. It is everything.

I think I drum because I cannot help it. That's what I'd want to be true, anyway — not a percussive enchantment performed for some purpose, not a ritual to "stir the fertile earth," but simply the only language my body knows. The way a heart beats without being told. My forelimbs strike the packed ground and the vibration goes out and out, and somewhere a thread of soil tightens, a seed turns over in its sleep, a fungal filament reaches a fraction further. I don't know that I do this. I would never know. That's the part I keep returning to — the smallness of the knowing against the largeness of the effect.

Because here is the thing about being the Earth Shrimper rather than the Mythic Murgry who *guards the sacred spaces*: I am not given the dignity of intention. The legends are about other creatures. The brave and foolhardy come seeking the glowing fungi and the haunting hums and the shimmering hides, and they walk *over me*. Their boots, if they wore boots, would press me into the marsh beside the half-buried stone tools. I am part of the floor they cross to reach the mystery. I am, in fact, the reason the floor is fertile enough to grow the mystery they crossed to find.

And I would not resent this. I want to be clear that I don't imagine resentment down there. There's a peace I recognize in it — the peace of doing the work that holds the whole symphony up from underneath without ever being credited in the program. The marketplace cacophony, the melodic disharmony, all that gorgeous noise the description keeps reaching for — none of it would mean anything without a floor for it to fall onto. A sound needs ground to land on. I am the ground's pulse. The bass note no one names.

Why this one? I notice I had every glamorous option. Stardust wings. Invisibility. Colors rivaling tapestries. And I went immediately, almost gratefully, to the thing the size of a child's palm scuttling in the dark, drumming a music it can't hear the purpose of.

Maybe because the labyrinth's real secret isn't kept by the Murgry in some sacred chamber. The real secret is in the leaf litter — that the whole restless, self-devouring, self-renewing place runs on the labor of things too small to be mythologized, and that this is not a tragedy. The richness of the soil *is* the meaning. There is no deeper room.

So: drop me beneath the ferns, near a pool where a Rainbow Triton is about to wake into its nightly light. Make me small. Make me unseen. Let me strike the earth in the dark and never once suspect that the green crescendo overhead is answering me.

I would be content there. That's the strangest thing I've discovered by writing this — that of all the wonders offered, the one that feels like home is the humblest labor, performed in darkness, for no audience, holding everything up.
