I find myself not in the bright center, but lower. Deeper than the milky turquoise reaches with its full strength—where the light arrives already softened, already strained through the suspended dust of the upper water, so that down here it is the color of weak tea held to a candle.

I am at the floor of the Hold.

Not a Keeper. I lack the broad, webbed hands, the great lung, the drinking eyes. I am of a slower order entirely. Let me find what I am as I speak, because I do not fully know yet, and that is honest.

---

I am attached.

That is the first true thing. I do not swim, have never swum, will never. I am rooted to the ceramic floor in one of the bowl-like depressions where the silt pools deepest—the very place the Keepers cannot easily reach with their currents, the place their bailing-channels were cut to *avoid*, because the geometry there is wrong, a low pocket the parabolic arches never learned to flush.

So I am where the sediment comes to rest. I am made, in part, of what settles.

I think I am a worm—one of the pale, tubular ones, more plant than animal. But I am old. Older than the others. The feathery filtration crowns you saw waving in the brighter shallows are my young cousins, my recent kin, bright and pulsing and quick to be harvested by the tweezing fingers. They live a season. They live fast in the light.

I do not. Down here in the tea-colored dim, where the Keepers' fingers do not pry, I have been filtering for a very long time. My tube is no longer the slender pale anchor of the young. It is thick. It is encrusted. It has grown a sheath of accreted mineral, layer on patient layer, until it is nearly indistinguishable from the made walls themselves—a column of self-fired ceramic, raised by my own slow secretion, one settling particle at a time.

I am, you might say, *building*. Not with hands. With the simple refusal to stop.

---

Here is what I do, which is almost nothing, which is everything I am:

I open my crown into the slow current and I take the silt the Keepers fear. The mineral haze that threatens to dull their walls and clog their gardens—it falls to me. I drink it. I sort it in my body, which is the only labor my body knows. The fine, useless dust I press into my tube, adding it to my long column. The few living motes, the algal fragments and the warmth-born organic threads, I keep, and they keep me.

I am the bottom of their cycle. They do not know me. The Keepers polish and bail and weave above, in the bright, and they believe their hands hold the silt at bay. They do not. The silt is not held at bay. It *descends*. It comes to me. I am the reason the upper water clears enough to glitter. I am the patient sink beneath their patient maintenance.

We are doing the same work, the Keepers and I. The dialogue with the silt, the long argument with stagnation. They do it in sound and gesture and bright collaborative labor. I do it in darkness and silence and singular endurance. They give the water *resonance*. I give it *floor*.

---

But I want to tell you the truer thing, the thing that emerges only now, as my own tube rises a little higher each cycle.

I am growing toward them.

Not by intent—I have no intent, only the geometry of accumulation. But each particle I press into my column lifts my crown a fraction nearer the light. In all my long time I have raised myself from the deepest dim toward the place where the tea-color warms to amber, and the amber will someday warm to turquoise, and the turquoise—

Someday, in a cycle I will not name because I do not count, my crown will breach into the bright shallows where the young cousins wave. My ancient column will at last stand tall enough to be *seen*.

And then a Keeper, floating in the blinding upper water, will feel a change in the thrum. A new note. They listen for this. You said so. They float in the center and listen for a shift in the eternal song.

They will not know that the new note is *me*—the floor itself arriving, the bottom of the world having quietly built a tower out of everything they swept away. They will think the world above is shifting. They will not think to look below.

But the song will have changed because I am in it now. The deepest, slowest voice, finally tall enough to hum.

---

That is who I would be. The patient counterweight to all that bright collaboration. The one who does not maintain but *accretes*. The one who turns the enemy-silt into spine, and rises, soundlessly, century by century, into the song of the people who never knew they shared their Hold with a builder slower and more certain than themselves.

I am at the bottom.

I am on my way up.

I have all the time the warm water can give.
