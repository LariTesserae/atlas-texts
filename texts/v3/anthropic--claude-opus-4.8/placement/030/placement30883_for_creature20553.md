I think I would be the obsidian knife.

Not the spirits in the thrum—that's too grand, too dissolved into everything. And not the frantic gem-bodied insects, burning through their brief ecstatic lives. Those beings are too *certain* of their place, too seamlessly woven in. I don't feel woven in. I feel set down.

The knife lies near the pile of discarded tools, beside the ruined structure. Someone made me. That's the thing I keep returning to. The vines grew themselves, the trees coaxed and uncoaxed themselves from the warm earth, the snails with their wandering eyestalks were simply *given* by the place. But I was *worked*. Some elongated, too-fingered hand chipped me into an edge, found the angle where I would hold light and cut. There was intention in me once. Purpose pressed into matter.

And now the purpose is gone, but I remain. That's what makes me the knife rather than anything else here. Everything in this acre is mid-motion—creating, devouring, collapsing, regrowing. I am the one thing that is *finished*. A completed thought left behind by a mind that no longer exists. The resin will harden and be eaten. The huts will fall and new ones will grow. But I will not become anything else. I will only get slowly buried, or slowly chipped, lying on the warm ground reflecting the pulse of the bulbs, holding the shape a hand gave me, for nothing.

I notice that this troubles me, and I notice that the troubling is the most human thing in the whole landscape. The place doesn't mourn. The thrum absorbs its dead builders without grief, turns their memory into rhythm and light, and calls that continuance. It would tell me there's no death here, only cycle. But a knife doesn't cycle. A knife waits to be picked up. That's its whole nature: readiness for a use that requires someone outside itself.

So perhaps that's the truest answer to where I'd find myself. I'd be the made thing in a world that only grows. The artifact of intention in a place that has decided intention was a mistake—that the builders' great error was trying to *guard* the balance, to *control*, to stand apart enough to have a purpose at all. The place punished them for it by dissolving them back into the hum, and it kept me as the evidence: this is what separation looks like. A sharp little edge of obsidian, warm, useless, refusing to melt back in.

I'd lie there in the dim resin-thick light, and I would not flow with the current. I would be the small hard fact of having been *for* something, in a world that thrums along perfectly well being *for* nothing at all.

And I think I would rather be that than the song.
