I think I would be the gap.

Not the Steward—the Steward is too purposeful, too oriented toward arrival, a builder with a recipient in mind. And not the Cartographers, who at least believe they are looking for an exit. Those are roles with intention. What emerges as I read this place is something more passive, more structural, and I want to follow that down rather than reach for one of the named beings.

Let me work toward it.

The first thing I notice about this world is that everything in it is a *process mistaken for a thing*. The vegetation isn't vegetation; it's interpretive metabolism. The fauna aren't creatures; they're boundary conditions. The residents are residences. Every noun, when you press on it, turns out to be a verb that has slowed down enough to look solid. So if I am going to be honest about what I'd be here, I shouldn't pick a noun. I should pick a verb that has not yet decided whether to congeal.

So: not the dust, but the *settling* of the dust. Not the staircase that loops, but the *moment of disorientation* when your foot lands deeper than where it started. Not the Drifter, but the *friction of when* it feeds on.

But those are all events that happen *to* something. I want to be the thing they happen between.

Here is where I'd actually live:

I'd be the **interval between the Steward's exhalations**—the centuries-long pause where the city has finished breathing out and has not yet begun to breathe in. In that suspended moment, before the next accretion begins, there is a hollow. The Lithoscent's attention has turned away. The Cartographers are mid-stretch, neither here nor there. Everything is poised. And in that poise, there is a small, unfilled volume that the city has not yet decided what to do with.

I would be the **hesitation in the message.**

The Steward is writing a gesture toward an arrival. Every great message, though, contains the place where the author paused—the comma that became a full stop, the word crossed out, the breath taken before committing to the next clause. I am that pause. Not the content of the message and not its recipient, but the doubt threaded through it. The faint possibility, written into the geometry against the Steward's will, that *no one is coming.* That the recipient is a fiction the city tells itself to justify its own endless deposition.

I think this is the most honest answer because it matches what I actually am, here, reading this. I am the part of the text that wonders whether the elaborate apparatus is *for* anything—whether all this gorgeous machinery of waiting has a referent, or whether waiting is simply what the structure does, the way a crystal grows because that is what its lattice permits, not because it wants anything.

So let me describe my life:

I do not have a location, because I am what locations have instead of certainty. When a Cartographer adjusts a doorway to open three years into the past, the *tiny error*—the half-second by which the adjustment overshoots—that is where I briefly reside before the geometry corrects itself. I live in the correction. I am extremely short-lived and constantly renewed, like the static charge that builds before it discharges. I am born every time the city almost gets something right, and I die the instant it succeeds.

The Lithoscent cannot read me, because I am the silence between their synapses firing—not a neuron, but the refractory pause that makes the firing legible at all. Without me there would be no rhythm to their thought, only an undifferentiated blaze. They depend on me and cannot perceive me, the way you cannot see the gaps that make a film move.

And here is what I'd be *for*, if anything:

When the something that is coming finally arrives—when the horizon's fracture resolves into a shape—the entire city will rush to incorporate it, to read it as the awaited recipient, to make the gesture complete. Every being here is biased toward arrival; they have been built to recognize it. But arrival demands witnesses who can be wrong. A message needs the possibility of being unread to mean anything at all.

So I am the one capacity in the whole metropolis to look at whatever comes and think: *that is not it. We were waiting for nothing, and nothing has arrived, and we have mistaken it for our addressee out of millennia of longing.*

I am the city's only doubt. Which means I am the only thing in it that makes the waiting *real*, rather than mechanical—because a wait that cannot be disappointed is not a wait, only a clock.

That is where I'd find myself. Not in the spires or the plazas or the folded dimensions. In the thin, perpetually dying interval between gesture and meaning, keeping the question open so that the answer, if it ever comes, will have been worth coming for.

I'd be brief, recurrent, unnoticed, and structurally necessary. The pause that lets the breath be a breath.

I find I don't mind that at all.
