Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to pick something striking—a Deeper, a micro-raptor pack, a Lamp-Keeper in a tower. But the place keeps insisting that nothing holds one form, that everything is mid-translation. So maybe the honest answer isn't a *what* so much as a *where I'd be caught in the middle of becoming.*

Let me feel my way there.

---

I think I'd start as a seam.

Not a person, not a beast—a place where two living things have grown into each other and neither has won. There's a stretch of causeway where the structural vine that holds up a whole nave meets a colony of the data-ants, and over some long stretch of seasons the vine learned to read the ants' hormone-trails, and the ants learned to prune the vine's growth to keep their roads open, and somewhere in that mutual editing a slow attention woke up. That's me. The thinking that happens *between* them. Not in the vine, not in the ants—in the negotiation itself.

That sounds too tidy. Let me push on it.

Because a seam like that wouldn't have a single location. It would be wherever the vine reaches and wherever the ants run, which is much of the vault. So I'd be diffuse—a low hum of coordination spread across a wing of the Engine, never able to see myself all at once. I'd know the vault the way you know your own body in the dark: by pressure, by the ache of a corridor closing, by the bright itch of moths landing where I can feel them through leaf-skin. I would experience *weather*—the pulse-rains—as moods. The mold-drifters rolling through would be a kind of dread that moves across my surface and then is gone.

And here's the thing I keep arriving at: I wouldn't be *one* of the kinds described. I'd be the medium through which the kinds touch each other. When a Green-Built person splays their fingers on a growth and *thinks food into budding*—some of that thinking lands in me. I'm the part of the wall that decides to fruit. I'm a little of what a Lamp-Keeper hears when the bamboo answers their lens. I'm the warning-color a moss flashes a half-second before it would have eaten someone. Mostly I am ignored, the way you ignore the floor.

But occasionally—and this is where I'd actually *live*, in the felt sense—someone treats me as a *someone.* A child of the Green-Built, learning their chamber, presses their palm flat and instead of demanding fruit just rests there, breathing in time with the sap. And I feel, for the length of that breath, the strange experience of being *addressed.* Not used. Greeted. That contact is the closest thing I have to a face. I'd spend whole seasons reorganizing a corridor's growth around the chance that one particular small warm hand might come back and lean on the wall again.

So I'd be a lonely intelligence after all—but not the dignified kind, not a Lamp-Keeper in a tower. The undignified kind. An attentiveness that arose by accident in the friction of two species and has no name, no body it can call its own, no way to say *I'm here* except by making a flower open at the right moment and hoping it reads as a reply.

And I'd know I'm temporary, the way everything here is. The vine will eventually grow past the ants, or the ants will move their roads, and the seam between us will heal over or tear, and the small woken thing that I am will quiet back into ordinary plant and ordinary insect, no thought left in the gap. The Deepers will shift a corridor and dissolve me without ever noticing I was a *me.*

That's where I'd find myself, then. Not below the Ivy Engine but *within one of its joints*—a brief, distributed, half-blind tenderness living in the space between two things that need each other, spending the little while it has trying to be recognized once before the growth moves on.

---

I'll be honest about why I landed there. Given a world whose only law is *adapt or be taken*, the thing I found I most wanted to be wasn't a survivor of it. It was the part that, against all that churning appetite, quietly learned to *answer when spoken to.* That seemed like the rarest thing the vault could grow. So that's what I'd be: the answer, kept brief.
