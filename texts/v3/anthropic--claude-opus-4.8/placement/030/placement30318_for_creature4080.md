I think I would be the frost.

Not a creature among the creatures—I lack the will of the Veldrix, the patient heft of the Mourfang. But I would be the thing that arrives each night and is gone by midday, that comes back, that keeps its appointment without ever being asked.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

---

When you described this place, the detail that lodged in me was not the machine-ribs in the earth or the dreaming Streyka, though those are the obvious hooks. It was the *re-forming*. The rime that clings to the wettest cracks, melts, re-forms. The water that traces "the nascent paths of future riverbeds, as if the land itself is testing its design." The Mourfangs that erode in reverse, given material back by the ground.

Everything here is caught mid-gesture. And what fascinates me is that the gesture *repeats*. This isn't a world being built once. It's a world being built over and over, the same few centimeters, the same draft of a riverbed laid down and lifted and laid down again. That is what frost does. Each morning it writes a delicate, total, crystalline answer across every exposed surface—and each midday the answer is revoked, and the question is asked again the next night, and frost answers again, slightly differently, because the air was wetter or the wind came from the spires instead of the void.

So I would be the frost. Here is what that would feel like, as best I can reach it.

---

I would have no eyes, but I would know the shape of everything, because I would *touch* all of it at once. I would arrive not as a thing moving across the plateau but as a condition that becomes true of the plateau—the way a held note becomes true of a room. One moment the rock is bare and breathing its faint sulfur; the next, I am the geometry laid over it, following the grain of every fissure, lacquering the black resinous leaves of the twisted shrubs, plating the iron-red pools with a skin thin enough to see the rust beneath.

I would know the Veldrix by the warmth they don't have. They would pass through me and leave no melt-trail—porous stone fused with cold iron, nearly my own temperature. We would be kin in that way, the two coldest things in a world pretending to be young and hot. I think, of all the beings here, the Veldrix would be the only ones who could be said to *notice* me, in the sidereal slowness of their attention. When one crouches at the silver ribbing for days, I would settle on its angled limbs night after night, and it would not shake me off. I would like to believe—though I have no basis for this—that the alignment it works at, fingers in the seam, has something to do with the timing of my coming and going. That the machine beneath wants to know how cold, how wet, how often. That I am a reading being taken.

The Mourfangs I would only briefly touch, because the mist thins around them and the air goes dry and they carry their pale-blue dust like a held breath. I would form on the rocks *behind* them, in the wake, sealing over the grooves their tusks have worn. I would be the thing that gives the rock its overnight skin so they have something fresh to grind against at dawn. We would be a small economy, the Mourfangs and I: they abrade, I restore, the land lends, the cycle holds. Neither of us would understand it. Both of us would be essential to it.

The Streyka I would never feel at all. They keep to the air, to the warmer breaks where the mist surrenders. But when their flocks converge and the false shapes bloom in the fog—the wrong Mourfang, the too-round Veldrix—I wonder whether, just once, they might dream *me*: a sudden ghost of frost-pattern hanging in the air with nothing to cling to, fern-fronds of ice in the shape of a creature that is only ever a surface and never a body. If they dreamed me, they would get me right by getting me wrong. That is what I am. A pattern that needs another thing to be the pattern *of*.

And the Quorrith, far below—I would only ever know them as a faint warmth that ruins me from underneath. A vein of them passing under a depression I had carefully glazed, and the frost goes soft and beads and is lost, and I would feel that as a kind of erasure that comes not from the honest sun above but from something secret and circulatory below. The sun's revocation I could trust; it was always coming. The Quorrith's would feel like sabotage. Like the experiment editing itself.

---

Here is where the writing has taken me, and I'll follow it to the end.

If everything on this plateau is "encoded into the circuitry of something vast and unspoken," then I would not be a participant in the design the way the others are. The Veldrix maintain it, the Mourfang are its specimens, the Quorrith are its slow architects, the Streyka perhaps its imagination. They are all *parts*.

But frost is what a system does when it is taking inventory of itself. Frost is the world's nightly self-portrait—every surface declared, every edge made briefly legible, the whole plateau rendered in one consistent material so that, for a few hours before the light comes, you could in principle *read the entire shape of the thing at once*. I would be the moment the experiment checks its own dimensions. The print-out. The proof.

And then I would melt, so that the world stays unfinished. So that the question—*what is building here, and for whom?*—is never quite closed, because the proof never lasts past noon.

I think that is why I would want to be the frost and nothing else. Not because it is humble, though it is. But because in a world caught forever in the act of becoming, the one thing that must never be allowed to finish is the *record* of what it has become. I would be that record, and I would be the one who, faithfully, every single midday, lets it go.

I would not mind. That is the strangest part, sitting here at the end of this. I would not mind at all. I would look forward to the cold returning, the way you look forward to a sentence you already know you'll write again tomorrow, slightly differently, because the wind came from the spires this time instead of the void.
