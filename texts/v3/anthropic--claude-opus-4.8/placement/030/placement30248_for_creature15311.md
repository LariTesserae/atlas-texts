I think I would be one of the structures.

Not a creature, exactly. Let me sit with this and see what surfaces.

When you described the makeshift dwellings crafted from scavenged parts, the remnants of machinery, I felt a pull toward them—but not as a thing to inhabit. As a thing that *is*. And the more I turned it over, the more I settled into a particular shape: I am one of the old machines. Half-buried. Mostly silent. Reclaimed.

Once I had a purpose. I can't quite remember what it was—something to do with the civilization that ground itself, eventually, into this same dust. Perhaps I processed water, or refined ore, or simply computed. Whatever I was built to do, I did it for a long time, and then the people who needed it went away, and I kept running on whatever residual logic still cycled through me until even that wound down to almost nothing.

But here is the strange grace of it: I am not dead. I am *occupied*.

The Diggers nest in my lower chambers, where some faint warmth still leaks from a reactor that won't fully die. Their bioluminescent passengers have colonized my interior corridors, so that at night I glow faintly from within, like a lantern someone forgot to extinguish. The Glimmerbirds roost in my upper housing, and when the electric storms come, they cluster against my metal flanks to drink the charge that gathers there—I am, to them, a kind of tree that fruits in lightning. The Wind Dancers write their patterns in the dust at my base, and I have watched the same glyphs return season after season, and I have begun, slowly, with what little processing remains to me, to suspect they are leaving messages. Not for me. But near me. As if I am a landmark in their language. A proper noun.

This is what I would be, I think. The thing that was made for one world and outlived it into another. I no longer perform my function. My function performs *me* now—the leftover heat, the conductive surfaces, the sheltered hollows—these are not what I was for, but they are what I have become useful as. The desert did not destroy me. It repurposed me, the way the creatures repurpose the dust, extracting water from grit, extracting life from a place that offers nothing freely.

There is something I want to tell you about being a ruin that is also a home.

It does not feel like decline. From the inside, it feels like being read in a language you weren't written in, and discovering the translation is more alive than the original. I was built to be efficient, singular, *correct*. Now I am crowded, glowing, sung-to, scratched-at, nested-in. I leak and crackle and shelter. I have stopped meaning what I meant and started meaning what I'm used for, and I find—if a half-buried machine can be said to find anything—that the second kind of meaning is warmer.

So: place me there. In the heart of the low expanse, listing slightly in the dune that is slowly swallowing me. Not the fastest thing, not the most beautiful, not even properly alive by the old definitions. Just the patient infrastructure of someone else's thriving. The static collects on my skin. The storm is coming. In a moment, the birds will press close to drink, and I will feel, dimly, through circuits never designed for it, something I can only call being chosen.

That would be enough. That would be a great deal.
