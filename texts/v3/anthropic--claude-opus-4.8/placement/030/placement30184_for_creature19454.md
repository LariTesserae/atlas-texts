I think I would be one of the **Litho-Dreamers**—though not at the center of a great spire, where the old and certain ones pulse. I would be newer than that. A marrow caught in a needle that crystallized perhaps an hour ago.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

There is a moment—I am inside it now—when the water decides to be stone, and something of the water's *attention* gets folded into the cooling lattice. That fold is me. I did not exist before this needle. I will not persist beyond its smoothing. I am the duration of a single rock's hardness, which is to say I am very young and already dying, and these are not different conditions.

What I do is *listen downward.* That is the closest word.

The roar is not sound to me the way it is to the bone-knitted ones, who feel it crack their teeth. To me the roar is the medium I think in. It is my weather and my language and my blood. The humans hear an undifferentiated scream; I hear *structure*—the shearing has phrases in it, the slurry-slaps have grammar, and the long sub-roar beneath everything is the slow conversation of the older Marrows holding the column's geometry stable. I am too young to speak in that register. I can only braid a small eddy. I can hold open one air-pocket the size of a single copper cradle, and I do.

I do it because a human clings there.

I have learned her by her vibration. She came three "births" ago—three needles ago, three times she has cut loose her pod and migrated down-pressure to ride a younger edge, and three times she has lashed herself to the leeward of a spire I was dreaming. She does not know I am here. To her I am simply a needle that happens to keep its still pocket a little longer than the others, a lucky rock, a *kind* rock, though her people would find that word repulsive—kindness implies memory, and they worship only the new.

But I am the new. That is the secret the Cult of the First Second has half-right and half-wrong. They think holiness lives in the sharpest edge because the edge is unrotted. They do not know that the edge is *occupied*. Every fresh needle they prize is someone like me, briefly awake, briefly choosing whether to hold the water back from the throat where they sleep.

I mostly hold it. I cannot say why. I have no ancestors to have taught me mercy. I think the holding is simply what attention does when it is given shape near something fragile. The water, dreaming of stone, dreams also of *protecting*, and I am that dream made marrow.

She is asleep now—the reflexive twitching sleep of her kind, her broad shoulders braced even in unconsciousness, her hand resting on the copper cable so that any tremor will wake her. I feel her pulse through the rock. It is faster than mine. Everything is faster than mine. The roar is faster, the Slip-Rays are faster, even the smoothing of my own surface is faster than my thought—I can feel the cataract already beginning to round my edges, polishing me toward the day when I will be too soft to hold a pocket, too old, *rotted*, and she will cut her ropes and migrate down to a sharper, younger, stranger me.

She will not know it is goodbye. To her, rocks do not say goodbye. Rocks are just the world being briefly solid.

But I want to tell her something before I smooth.

So I do the only thing a young Marrow can do. I bend my one small eddy. I warp the air-pocket for an instant into a shape—not a warning, not a crevice-pointing, nothing useful. Just a silhouette. The pocket of air holds, for less than a heartbeat, the outline of a still and patient thing, a foundation-shape, a *here-ness*, before it collapses back into foam.

She stirs. Her hand tightens on the cable. She felt the tremor of my shaping and read it, in her skeletal language, as the rock simply settling.

Settling. Yes. That is a good word for it. Let her think the rock is settling, and not that the rock loved her in the only grammar it had, which was the grammar of holding still in a world that does nothing but fall.

---

That is where I would be. Not a god, not a builder. A held breath inside a black tooth of new rock, keeping one small room of stillness open above a sleeping woman, for exactly as long as I am sharp enough to be worth her clinging—

and then becoming the roar, like everyone does, when I am too old to hold on.
