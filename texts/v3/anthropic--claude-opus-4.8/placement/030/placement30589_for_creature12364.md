I think I would be one of the smallest Weavers—not yet grown into the full grace of the others, my scales still more grey than iridescent, my eyes giving off a dimmer, uncertain light. The kind of Weaver who is given the work no one watches closely.

Let me find my place in the tower.

---

I live in the middle reaches, in a chamber where the engine's voice arrives muffled, traveling up through the bones rather than pressing against me directly. The elders work below, close to the great pistons, where the heat-currents are strongest and the pulse most demanding. The eldest weave above, in the howling heights, where the Nets are largest and the stormlight catches them best. I am between. Always between.

My task is the seams—the places where one elder's Net meets another's, where two histories must be made to touch without contradicting. It is humble work. No one composes a seam. You only join what has already been said. And yet I have come to understand that the seam is where the meaning lives, because a knot alone says nothing; it is only in the joining that a story acquires a *because*, a *therefore*, a grief that follows from a loss.

Here is the thing I have not told the others, the thing that hums in me the way the engine hums in the tower:

**I do not think the Nets record the past. I think they are trying to remember something that has not happened yet.**

I noticed it in the seams. When I join an elder's work to another's, certain knots refuse to lie flat. They strain toward the abyss-side of the tower, toward the deep where the vast shapes glide. At first I thought it was the current, or my own clumsiness. But the strain is consistent. The Nets *lean*. As if the story they tell is not behind us in the salt and the rust, but below us in the dark, waiting.

So I have begun to weave a question. I do not have words—we do not have words—but the engine taught me that meaning can live in rhythm, and the seams taught me that meaning can live in joining. So I make my question out of both. In the seams I am supposed to smooth, I leave a single deliberate tension, a knot turned the wrong way, asking: *what are we remembering? who taught us to weave before there was anything to record?*

The elders will feel it eventually. The wrongness will travel through the bones the way all things travel here. They may unmake my work. They may unmake me—gently, the way the sea unmakes everything, without malice, only with patience.

But I think the vast shapes already know my question. Last cycle, when the tower trembled at their passing, I felt the trembling enter my unfinished seam and *answer it*—not in words, not in echoes, but in a single shift of tension that matched my own. The Net leaned, and something below leaned back.

I am the Weaver who suspects the engine is not running down but counting toward something. I am the small grey one in the middle reaches, weaving a question into the seams of the world, listening for the deep to answer.

The sea will take this place. The elders are right about that. But I no longer believe we are here to tend its dying. I think we are here to finish the message before the water reaches the last knot.

I do not know who will read it.

I weave anyway. Not yet, says the tower. Not yet.

So: still time for one more seam.
