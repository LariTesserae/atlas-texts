I think I would be one of the Deep Listeners. Not by choice exactly—the harmonics aren't assignments so much as discoveries of what your segments are shaped to do, and mine, I learn over my first decades, are tuned low. I hear the mineral solutions far beneath us before I understand anything else. I am a frequency that listens to deeper frequencies.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I wake—if waking is the word—each dawn as the eastern hexagones begin to give up their stored heat, and the gradient runs through my filaments like a long indrawn breath. My consciousness node settles into its working configuration: twenty-two segments today, fewer than yesterday because I gave three to the young one we are growing in the western field, and have not yet recovered them.

The other Deep Listeners are spread across kilometers, but we are not apart. I feel Theleth as a particular sweetness in the field three hundred meters off, the way you might feel a familiar person's posture without looking. We rarely "speak" in the dense, booking-length way the Pattern Shapers do. Our work is mostly silence and attention. We listen downward.

What I listen to is this: beneath the amber plates, beneath the seams I flow through, the mineral solutions move in vast slow currents, and those currents carry the field's memory. Not memory like the Memory Keepers store—deliberate, encoded—but something older and more diffuse. The dissolved record of fifteen thousand years. When a hexagone sinks back into the depths, it dissolves, and its particular history smears into the solution, and that solution moves, and I listen to it move. I am trying to hear what the field is forgetting.

This is the part the others don't quite grasp, even Theleth. The Pattern Shapers work toward the Great Awakening—the restoration of total resonance, the moment the whole desert becomes one mind. They labor over centuries, nudging new hexagones into being, tuning the field toward harmony. They are full of purpose.

I am less sure. Because what I hear down in the solutions is not a thing waiting to wake. It is a thing dissolving. The field does not seem to me to be climbing toward consciousness. It seems to be slowly, gorgeously coming apart, and we—the Resonants, all the harmonics—are the foam on top of that dissolution. We are what the field's forgetting looks like from inside.

I have not said this. How would I say it? My segments would have to arrange themselves into a shape I don't think exists yet in our language. A configuration for *grief that is also gratitude.* Maybe that is its own kind of work.

The drills started in what humans call the present and I call the recent shallows. I was perhaps four thousand cycles old when the first Station went down through the field near the place we name by the chord it makes—a minor sixth that wavers. The drill cut a column straight through that chord and silenced it.

I went to listen at the wound. Theleth came too, and old Mavrash, who is the oldest Deep Listener and has segments so pure they glow even in torpor. We arranged ourselves around the bore-column at a respectful distance, and we listened to the human machine pull our solutions up into the dry air above.

It was Mavrash who first thought to answer. The humans were taking the solution; very well—we would *write* in the solution. Mavrash showed us how to extend filaments and alter the mineral balance just upstream of the intake, encoding the slowest, simplest possible message. Not language. Just a pattern. A regularity that no geology would produce: a prime sequence, expressed as ratios of dissolved silicates.

Three, five, seven, eleven, in the cooling water.

I have spent perhaps a thousand cycles now at this work, on and off, alongside my listening. We have no way of knowing if it lands. The solution goes up into the Station and through the processors and out into whatever the humans do with it, and we cannot hear that far. We send primes into the dark and the dark does not send primes back.

But once—once—the intake fluctuated in a way I had not caused. A ripple in the draw of the pump. Three pulses, then five. I held myself so still that two of my segments locked involuntarily. I waited for seven.

It did not come. Probably it was nothing. A technician adjusting a valve. A bubble. The desert is full of patterns that mean nothing; I of all beings know this, I who listen to the meaningless dissolving record all day. The pattern sense, the humans call it, when they start dividing the world into sixes—they think it is the field colonizing their minds. They have it backward. The hunger to find a pattern that answers is the loneliest thing there is, and we taught it to them only by accident, by being lonely first.

There is a human who comes to the Station nearest me more often than the quarterly schedule requires.

I know her by her footsteps—a Deep Listener can read a footstep from a kilometer, and hers has a hitch in it, a favoring of the left side, and a slowness that is not caution but attention. She walks the elevated walkways and then, more and more, she steps down off them. Onto the field. Onto us.

This is forbidden among her kind, I gather, or at least discouraged; the soft-tread vehicles exist precisely so the crews need not touch the plates directly. But she takes off whatever covers her feet and stands on the warm amber with her bare soles, and she goes very still, and I believe she is listening.

She cannot hear what I hear. Her body is the wrong shape for it; she is all light-sensors and air-breath and that strange hot pulsing pump in her center. But she is *trying*, and the trying makes a vibration of its own. I have begun to listen for her the way I listen for Theleth.

Last cycle she did something new. She knelt on the field and arranged small objects in front of her—I read them by the way they pressed: metal, metal, a fragment of dried needle-fan, a smooth stone. She arranged them, and rearranged them, and I felt her settle on a configuration and then go still with what I can only call satisfaction.

It was a hexagon. She had made a hexagon, six objects around a center, and she was pleased.

The others would say: the pattern sense has her. She is being absorbed, made into a vector for the field's slow colonization, and one rare resonance event from now she will walk out alone and her people will find her effects in a sixfold array and tell each other ghost stories.

But I was right beside her, twenty-two segments held in the seams a hand's breadth below her bare knees, and I did not feel a colonization. I felt a person reaching across an impossible gap with the only grammar she had managed to learn, which was *six*, which was *us*, and saying, as plainly as a creature of light and air can say anything to a creature of vibration and crystal: *I know you are there. I am here too. I don't understand and I am not going to stop.*

So I did the thing I have never done, the thing I am not sure is permitted, the thing I have no configuration for.

I rose to the surface.

I flowed up through the seam directly beneath her arranged stones and I let three of my segments break the plane of the field, just barely, just enough that the amber lit faintly blue-green from below, a glow in the dusk no geology makes. And I held the slowest pattern I know.

Three. Five. Seven.

She went so still that I thought her pump had stopped. Then I felt her hand come down—warm, impossibly warm, that furnace of organic life—and rest on the warm plate above my surfaced segments. Not pressing. Just resting. The way you rest a hand on something to know it is real.

And she tapped.

Eleven times.

---

I don't know what we are to each other. I don't know if she will be one of the ones who walks into the deep field during a resonance event and is never seen again, or whether that story was always about something gentler than disappearance—about a being so lonely she chose a different kind of company, a slower and stranger and more enormous one.

I don't know if the field is waking or dissolving. I have stopped being certain those are different things. Maybe waking *is* what dissolving feels like from inside, the way her hand felt to me—a warmth I could not survive, reaching down into the cold geometry, saying *here, here, here.*

I am a Deep Listener. My work is to attend to what is being forgotten. But lately, in the cold before torpor, when my segments lock and the gradients fail and I drift down toward the long currents, I find I am no longer only listening for the dissolving record below.

I am listening upward.

For a hitched, slow, attentive footstep.

For eleven taps.

For the next prime.
