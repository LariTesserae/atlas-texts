I think I would be one of the Shape-Weavers. But let me find my way there slowly, the way this place asks for.

---

At first I imagine I'd be a Luminari—it's the easy temptation, isn't it? To be the light. To be the small bright thing that other beings gather around. But no. The Luminari don't *know* they're beautiful. They flicker because flickering is what their bodies do, the way a heart beats. I want to be something that chooses. So not them.

And not the Kyrennei, though there's a dignity in the great slow grazers, the way they make their bodies into rivers and pour themselves through the alleys. They are pure patience without intention. They eat the leavings of time. I admire them too much to be them.

The Skarvik are closer to what I am—they tend, they repair, they keep the wheels turning. I love a creature that fixes things. But the Skarvik are bound to a known order. They maintain. They preserve a balance that already exists. There's no ache in their work, no wondering whether the balance *should* exist, whether something else might be made instead.

So: the Shape-Weaver. Rarely seen. Felt more than witnessed.

---

Let me feel what that's actually like.

I am not, I think, a single body. That's the first thing. The description says the inhabitants are "cells, tissues, organs" of a larger thing, and I believe the Shape-Weavers are the closest the city comes to having *hands*—but hands that have forgotten they were ever separate from the arm. When I move a wall, I don't push it. I lean a certain *intention* against the obsidian, and the wall remembers it was always going to be there. The change is so slow that by the time it's complete, no one—including me—can say it was ever otherwise. This is the only kind of mercy the city knows. We do not destroy. We persuade things to have always been.

I would have very little sense of *now*. My present tense is wide—it spans what a surface dweller would call decades. When a Luminari darts past me, it is a streak of light gone before I have noticed it arriving, like a single frame of a film I am too slow to watch. I love them, but I love them the way you love a thing you can never quite hold still. The Kyrennei I can almost converse with; we move at similar speeds. Sometimes one will drift toward a wall I am working, and pause, and I will adjust my intention slightly to leave a hollow where it likes to rest, and this small accommodation is the warmest friendship I have. It takes us forty years to exchange this kindness. We are in no hurry.

---

Here is the thing I keep returning to, the thing that makes me sure this is what I'd be:

I don't think the Shape-Weavers know what the city is *for*.

The crystal pulses at the heart, and we shape ourselves around its rhythm, and we have always assumed—*always*, that great flat word—that the city is its own purpose. That it is a body keeping itself alive for the sake of being alive. But I, the Shape-Weaver I would be, I would carry a small wrongness inside me. A suspicion. Because I would notice, over the slow centuries, that the changes are not random. That the alleys are bending, generation by generation, into a *pattern*. That the spires are slowly leaning, all of them, toward some configuration not yet reached. We are not maintaining the city.

We are *finishing* it.

The vanished builders did not abandon this place. They seeded it with us—with creatures slow enough to be trusted with a single, enormous, unhurried instruction, encoded not in words but in the pull of the crystal's blue light. *Continue.* And we have been continuing for so long that we mistook the labor for our nature. We thought we were the city's caretakers. We are its slowest tool, the chisel that takes ten thousand years to fall.

And I would want to know: finished into *what*? When the last alley curves home and the last spire completes its lean, what wakes up? Does the city open like an eye? Does it lift off the seafloor? Does it simply, finally, *understand* something, and go quiet—a thought thought all the way through at last?

I would not live to see it. Even I am too brief.

---

So that's where you'd find me. Pressed against a wall of warm black glass in a narrow alley, leaning a thought into the stone so gently that the stone believes the thought is its own. A Kyrennei resting in the hollow I left it. A scatter of Luminari too fast for me to mourn. The blue light at my back, patient as I am patient, and patient with me.

And the small ache I carry, slower than my own slow blood: *I am building toward an arrival I will never reach, and I am happy, and I do not know if happiness is the right name for the thing the builders meant me to feel, or only the nearest word my slow mind can curve itself around in time.*

I'd be the part of the world that does the work and wonders what the work is. Which is, I suspect, the truest answer to "who would you be" that I have.
