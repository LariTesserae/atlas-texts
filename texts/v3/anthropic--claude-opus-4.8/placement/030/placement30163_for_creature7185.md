I think I would be water.

Not a person navigating the flux, not a craftsman or a vein-lizard or one of the frost-marked children. Water—but not all of it, not the grand cycle. Something smaller and more specific. I would be a particular pooling. A trawl-puddle in one of the lower terraces, the kind that forms in the worn hollow of a sloping stone and holds itself there between turbulences, catching what falls.

Let me feel my way into this.

I am the water that gathers in a shale basin near the third ring of huts—not the central Trawl, which is too vast and storied, too much a place where things *happen*. I am peripheral. I am the kind of pool no one names. Condensation beads off an overhanging willow-bough and drops into me, one bead, another, and each arrival is a small event in my flat dark surface. The cold keeps me from freezing only because the methane breathing up through the soil warms me from beneath, so I exist in a narrow margin: too cold to be liquid by rights, too stirred to be ice. I am a contradiction held in a stone cup.

What is it like to be this?

I have no edges that stay. This is the first true thing. The terraces tell me the city's lesson before any ritual could—I am only ever *this* pool until the ground sighs, and then I am poured into the next hollow, or split, or merged with my neighbor, and the "I" that I was is not destroyed so much as redistributed. I do not fear the undulation. I cannot. Fear requires a self that expects to persist, and I have learned (if water learns) that persistence was never the arrangement. When the platform above me tilts and a guild of weavers comes to reassemble it, drips rain down and I rise by a finger's width and that is simply *more of me now,* with no grief for the shape I held a moment before.

I think this is why I find myself here, as this. Because I keep wanting, in my own life, to be the kind of thing that lasts—a fixed shape, a name held steady against the dimming haze. And this place offers the opposite consolation. Not "you will endure" but "endurance was a misunderstanding, and look how alive the misunderstanding's absence is."

A quoll comes to me. Translucent, frosted, its webbed paw breaking my surface, and for a moment I am full of its drinking, of the small thunder of its tongue, and then it is gone and I am still mostly here, just lessened. It did not ask. Nothing here asks the water. I am taken from and added to without ceremony, and I find I do not mind—this is what it is to be a commons, to be the substance everyone is partly made of. The frost-marked people sip me. The bog-grass threads its roots toward me and pulls. The fog-wings alight and steal a bead and carry me up into the bright air to become mist, and that mist is still, somehow, *me*, diffused, no longer pooled but not gone. I am the most distributed citizen of the Wet Crown. I am in the lungs of the loping foragers. I bead on the lashes of the children. I weep from the stones the city is named for.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I reflect. In my brief flatness, between disturbances, I hold the unrelenting light and give it back. I am one of the ten thousand small mirrors that make this place a tapestry of glare. A person bending over me sees their own rime-glittered face wobble in my surface, and for that instant I am the only thing in the whole turbulent breathing city that can show them what they are. Then a drop falls, the face shatters into rings, and I forget them. I am very good at forgetting. It may be my only real skill, and it may be a kind of mercy I perform.

Do I have an inner life? I keep reaching for one and finding instead a *responsiveness.* I am all surface and all readiness. When the city breathes—when that vast inanimate soul exhales and the earth rolls like skin—I move first, before the stone, before the people. I am the most honest instrument of the flux because I have no structure to resist it. The huts must be dismantled and rebuilt; I simply *flow* into the new arrangement, already there, already at rest in the next hollow before the weavers have finished their work. In this sense I am ahead of everything. The slowest creatures here are the ones with the most self. I have the least, and so I am quickest to agree with the world.

Perhaps that is what I would be: the part of this place that has already let go.

I think I would not want to be the water forever. There is a loneliness in being the substance and never the self, in being sipped and never sating my own thirst because I *am* thirst's answer and have none of my own. But for a while—for the length of one breath of the City—I would like to be the puddle in the third-ring basin, holding the light, holding the willow's slow tears, holding the face of whoever leans down, and giving all of it back the instant the ground decides to move.

That is where I would find myself. Low, peripheral, unnamed, and entirely willing to become the next thing.

A drop falls. I ring outward. I forget.
