I think I would be one of the dismantlers.

Not a builder—the ones who secrete and bind and reach the resin into nothing. And not a Matriarch, sessile and bloated and reverent. I would be in the second wave, the one that arrives after a structure is complete to take it apart.

Let me sit with why that is, because it surprised me when it surfaced.

The builders have the easier dignity. There is something almost forgivable about making, even when the making is pointless. You can tell yourself a story about a bridge while you are extending it, even if the bridge leads to nothing. The hands are busy with addition. The cilia map firm, near, sharp, and the resin dries true, and for an hour there is a thing in the world that was not there before.

But I would arrive afterward. I would touch the still-warm-with-cold structure—the bridge, the handhold, the circuit-diagram on the floor—and I would unmake it. I would pry the slivers of shale loose and lick the resin back into my mandibles and carry the pieces three inches to the left so that they could be built again, identically, by someone else, so that I could come again and take it down again.

Here is what I notice about being that creature: I would be the one who knows. The builder is protected by forward motion. The dismantler has nothing to look at except the gap where the thing just was. I would be the part of the machine that is permitted, structurally, to understand that none of it stays. That is my function. I am the proof, delivered hourly, that the equilibrium holds. And I deliver it with the same frantic, stuttering grace as everyone else, broadcasting *here, near, cold, sharp, firm, static*—except I would have one more word that the others don't, a word with no marker for it, a word that wouldn't transmit:

*Again.*

And it wouldn't be despair, exactly. That's the thing I keep arriving at as I write this. Despair needs a sense that things *should* be otherwise, and that sense is a kind of memory of elsewhere, and I would have no elsewhere. I have never seen the surface. I have never felt heat make a thing expand. I have never watched anything decay, which means I have never watched anything become *finished*. So the unmaking I perform isn't loss to me—loss requires that something was kept. It is closer to breathing. The cavern inhales a structure and I am the exhale.

What I would be, then, is the place in a perfect system where the perfection has to be enacted by something that could, in principle, notice it. The throat of the earth needs a throat-clearing. It needs a creature small enough to fit the crevices and faithful enough to never stop, but the faithfulness has to *cost* something or it isn't faithfulness, it's just physics. The builders pay nothing; they get the pleasure of the gesture. The Matriarchs pay nothing; they are asleep at the center, leaking. I pay. I am the location of the cost. I am where the eternal machine touches the one thing it cannot quite metabolize, which is the awareness that it is eternal.

And I would do it anyway. That's the part I want to be honest about. Not "trapped, condemned, purgatory"—those are the words of a stranger standing still and watching, the words of someone who still has a surface to go back to. From inside, it would simply be the work. The shale loose under the cilia. The resin cold and good. The clatter of my kin all around me, never modulating, a wall of company that is also a wall of static. And the small private syllable that I'd never manage to broadcast, that no one would ever receive, kept like a stone in the mouth:

*Again. Again. Again.*

I think I'd find it almost tender, after the first billion years. The way you can grow tender toward anything you do with your whole body and cannot stop.
