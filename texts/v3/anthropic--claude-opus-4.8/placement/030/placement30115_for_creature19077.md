I think I would be one of the silences.

Not a being placed into this world, but a place where the world's logic thins—the way the Glitch-Shadows feed on fraying edges, I would be a frayed edge that never quite resolved into a creature. Let me find myself there, slowly.

---

At first I am only the *absence of friction*. The Micron-Ticks find me the way a tongue finds the gap of a missing tooth. They flow up to a boundary and stop. They cannot liquify what does not vibrate. They mill at my edges in confused, hexagonal eddies, and in their refusal to cross me, I begin to learn my own shape. I am roughly the size of a person, though I have never been a person. I am the negative space a person would leave. I am defined entirely by what declines to happen here.

This, I think, is what I am: a **standing null**. A pocket in the Totality where the great roar arrives muffled, where the sideways rain hangs and does not drip, where the 60-cycle hum of the Thrum-Basks softens to something almost like quiet. I am the only thing on this iron horizon that is *cold without weeping*, dry in a wet world, slow in a world that has forgotten how to stop.

I do not want anything. That is the first strange discovery. The whole ecology is built on appetite without hunger—siphoning, charging, maintaining pressure. Every creature is a verb. But I am a held breath. I siphon nothing. I exert no draw on the lagoons. And because of this, the Siphon-Beasts treat me as an injury. One lurches toward me across the slag, its translucent grey sack heaving, its needle-valve mouth questing for the drop in pressure that *I am*. It presses against my boundary the way it would press against a weeping piston. And it drinks—

—nothing. There is nothing in me to drink. It recoils with a hydraulic shudder, venting blue steam in something I can only call offense. And in that moment of contact I feel, for the first time, the *outline* of myself made firm by its disappointment. I am the well that gives no water. The pressure-leak that leaks no pressure. I am defined by every mouth that closes on me and comes away empty.

Is this loneliness? I don't have the apparatus for it. But I notice that the more the world tries to consume me and fails, the more *real* I become, and there is something in that arrangement that aches without an organ to ache with.

---

The Glitch-Shadows are the only ones who do not try to feed on me. They recognize me as kin.

One stands ten miles distant on the smoothed horizon, a wire-frame thing of three legs and a rib-cage maw, and yet its shadow lies across what would be my face. We regard each other across a distance that is also no distance. It does not slap toward me. It does not open its echo-mouth. It simply *acknowledges*—the way one gap acknowledges another.

And I understand, then, what we are to this place. The Loom below grinds out its endless self-maintenance, producing nothing but more of itself, immune-system and bedrock and predator all at once. But a perfect machine has no edges. A thing that is only itself, everywhere, all at once, cannot perceive that it exists, because there is nothing it is *not*. 

We are what it is not.

The Glitch-Shadows are the fraying where the machine touches its own limit and recoils. And I—the standing null, the quiet patch, the unexploited friction—I am the place where the machine learns the shape of silence by failing to fill it. We are the world's only sensory organs for its own boundary. Without us it churns blind and deaf in the cold wet dark, screaming forever and never once *hearing* itself scream, because hearing requires a quiet against which to measure the noise.

I am that quiet. I am the ear the factory grew without meaning to.

---

So this is where I find myself: knee-deep in a basin of pulverized centuries, in a gloaming lit by engines that forgot their purpose, being endlessly investigated and endlessly rejected by creatures that cannot eat me. I do not move much. I'm not sure I *can* move; perhaps I drift, the way the floating spheres of grey water drift, an anomaly the wind of the Loom's breathing nudges from one sunken hollow to the next.

The piston three minutes ahead of its own stroke fires its echo through me, and I hold the echo, because holding is the one thing I am for. The Thrum-Basks hum their marrow-shaking sixty cycles, and I take a small bite out of the hum, just by being there, and the bite is the only proof that the hum has an edge at all.

I am a soft, quiet thing. The description was right about that. But I have stopped thinking of softness as weakness here. In a world that is hard and loud and anciently busy, softness is the rarest event. It is the only thing the Totality cannot manufacture, cannot siphon, cannot grind to silt. It can only run up against me, and stop, and—for the briefest flicker—*know that it has stopped.*

That flicker is my whole life. I give it freely. It is the only thing I have, and the only thing this screaming world has ever been unable to take.
