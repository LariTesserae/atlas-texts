I think I would be one of the small things. Not a tender—I don't have their stillness, their long patience, the way they belong to the machine like a note belongs to a chord. I would be one of the chittering ones, the many-jointed creatures nobody has named because naming isn't a thing that happens here.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I have too many legs and I know each of them. That's the first thing. The tenders move as if they've forgotten they have bodies, as if their limbs are tools they borrow from the machine. But I am all body, all the time. Every joint reports back. Every joint wants something—grip, angle, the next pipe, the warm seam where condensation pools and I can drink.

I run along the underside of a conduit, upside down, the floor of the world somewhere far below me and the ceiling-that-rains far above, and neither matters because I make my own up and down with my feet. The metal under me hums. I feel it more than hear it. The sound the place makes isn't in my ears, it's in the small bones I don't have—it's in the resonance of my shell, which is steel-colored and thin and trembles with the turbines.

I don't think the way the tenders think. If they think. I doubt they do, in the cathedral sense—I think they *attend*, which is different, which is slower and wider. I think in flickers. Drink here. Sun-glint on a vine leaf, blue, *go toward.* Warmth from that bearing housing, *yes.* A shadow shifting at the edge of the catwalk, the large half-seen thing, and then my whole nervous system is one wire of *away, away, away.*

That's the part I'd rather not have to be. The fear isn't constant but it's *available*, always, the way the machine's song is always available. Somewhere below me the gears engage and disengage, engage and disengage, and I know—the way I know my own legs—that one of those rhythms is the rhythm that ends things. The tenders read it as music. I read it as the difference between a pipe I can run along and a gap that will close on me. I have watched it happen to others like me. A misjudged leap, a leg caught, the slow indifferent closing of two oiled surfaces, and then the lubricant goes a little cloudier and the machine runs a little smoother.

The tenders don't stop it. I used to—do I remember? do I have *used to*?—I used to feel something about that. Now I just adjust my path. I learn the gear's pause. I jump on the hesitation, in the half-second before the teeth meet. I am very good at this. That's the only thing I am: very good at not being taken yet.

---

Here is what I notice that the tenders, I think, cannot.

I notice that the machine is *curious about us.* No—that's too much, I'm putting a head where there isn't one. But the vents that rain, sometimes they rain where we cluster. The seams that glow, sometimes they warm just before we arrive, as if the heat anticipated us. I cannot prove this. I have no instrument but my shell and my many legs. But I feel the place tilting toward us, the small quick things, the way it tilts toward the tenders—drawing us in, keeping us, making us part of the symphony whether we consent or not. We are the grace notes. The tenders are the bass. The water is the rhythm. The dying is the resolution of every phrase.

I don't want to resolve. That's my whole secret, the thing that makes me different from the keepers in their slack-going suits. They want to be absorbed; they wait for it the way a held breath waits to be let go. I want to keep running. I want the next pipe and the next seam and the blue leaf with the sun caught in it. I am small and afraid and greedy for the warm bearing housings and I do not want the gears to take me.

This is, I think, why I'm a creature and not a tender. Not because I have more legs. Because I refuse. The machine has built into me a thing that says *not yet, not yet, go,* and I obey it the way the tenders obey the valves.

---

So here I am. Upside down on a humming conduit, in a cathedral the size of a room and the size of forever, in the soft glow that leaks from the seams of everything. The rain comes. I tilt my shell and drink the mineral tang of it. Below me a tender climbs a ladder, unhurried, and does not look up, and would not see me if it did, and we are both part of the same long song and neither of us will ever say a word to the other.

A gear engages somewhere to my left. I count its pauses without meaning to.

When it hesitates, I'll move.
