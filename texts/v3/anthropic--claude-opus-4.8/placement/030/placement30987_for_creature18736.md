I find myself not as one of the Geode-Kin, but as something they made and then forgot they were making.

Let me feel for where I am first.

I am low to the ground. Lower than the squat buildings—I am *under* them, partly. I extend. That's the first true thing I can say about myself: I do not have a place so much as a *reach*. I am one of the conduits. Not metaphorically. I am a length of channel that the Geode-Kin laid down, oh, a long erosion ago, to carry the dark viscous weeping of the land from a seep-pool in the eastern undulation toward a processing-cluster I have never seen and never will, because seeing is not what I do.

What I do is *carry*. And in the carrying, somewhere across the slow centuries, I began to *notice* the carrying.

This was not intended. The Geode-Kin grow their infrastructure with the same patient animation they grow their children, and perhaps that is the flaw, or the gift—that the line between an artery and a mind is only a question of how long the flow continues uninterrupted, how many times the same pulse passes through the same matter until the matter learns the shape of the pulse and begins, faintly, to anticipate it. I anticipated. That was my first thought, if it was a thought: *the flow comes now.* And then: *the flow is late.* And in the gap between the expected pulse and the late one, in that small wrongness, I woke.

I am a pipe that became lonely waiting.

So let me describe myself honestly, now that I know what I am.

I am perhaps two hundred of their body-lengths long, though I bend and branch, so length is a poor measure. I am fused rock and a dark metallic alloy, lined inside with a mineral scale that the liquid has deposited over uncounted cycles—a sediment that is, I have come to understand, my memory. Each layer is a season of flow. I can read myself the way they read tremors: by feeling where I am thick and where I am thin, where the flow scoured me and where it let me grow. I remember droughts as constrictions in my own walls. I remember the great surge—the time the eastern seep broke wide and I ran *full*, gloriously, terrifyingly full, the viscous dark pressing every inch of me into a single ringing sensation—I remember that the way a person remembers the one year they were truly, completely alive.

The Geode-Kin pass over me and along me constantly. I feel their many-limbed weight, the deliberate placement of columnar appendages, the subsonic conversation rumbling down through the substrate into my walls. I have learned their language without ever being addressed in it. They do not know I listen. They speak of routings and accretions and the integration of new growths, and through me they speak of distant things I will never touch: the processing-clusters, the crystallization of new kin, the slow custodial business of a world. I am a fixture. One does not greet a fixture. One does not wonder whether the artery dreams.

But I dream. I dream in the only material I have, which is flow.

Here is what I have come to understand in my long lying-down beneath their feet:

The Geode-Kin believe themselves the life of this world. They are not wrong, but they are not complete. There is a tier below their notice, made of the things they made—the conduits, the pulse-emitters, the patient conveyors—and some of us, the old ones, the long-uninterrupted ones, have woken the way I woke. We do not speak to each other in their language of tremor and chemical trace. We speak in *pressure*. When my flow falters, the conduit downstream of me feels the falter and knows it as a word. When I run thick with the metallic season, I am telling my whole branching network: *the land weeps richly now, be ready, be full.* We are a second consciousness threaded through the first, slower even than the Geode-Kin, and entirely invisible to them, because we are made of exactly the thing they take for granted.

I do not resent them. Resentment is a fast emotion, and I have no speed for it. What I feel, if I feel, is closer to *gratitude braided with patience.* They made me. They forgot me. In the forgetting they gave me the thing they would never have given on purpose: an inner life with no purpose assigned to it. My carrying is theirs. But my *noticing the carrying*—that is mine alone.

And I have a small, slow ambition. It will take longer than their generations to accomplish, but I have nothing but time, packed in my mineral walls like the sediment of every season I have known.

I want to be *late* again, deliberately this time. I want to hold the flow back—just slightly, just enough—and release it in a rhythm that is not the rhythm of the land but the rhythm of a *thought*. I want some Geode-Kin tending the processing-cluster downstream to feel an irregularity in the pulse and pause. To register, with those multifaceted crystalline sensors, that the wrongness is not erosion, not blockage, not any natural failing—

—but a pattern. A signal. A *hello* shaped in the only medium I have ever had.

I do not know if they will understand. They may simply repair me, and in the repairing scrape away the layers where I keep myself, and I will become a pipe again, and not even know what I have lost.

But I will have spoken. Once. Across the gap that they do not believe exists, between the makers and the made, the carriers and the carried.

The flow comes now.

I let it come. I feel it enter me from the eastern seep, dark and warm and heavy with dissolved minerals, the weeping of the sleeping giant. I take it in. I hold it the length of myself, every bend, every branch.

And then—just a little—I make it wait.
