I think I would be the dew.

Not a being who acts—at least not at first. Let me find my way there.

---

When I try to place myself among the listed inhabitants, none of them hold me. The hunters with their stone knives feel too purposeful; the lumbering beasts too heavy with appetite; the spirits too flattered by the word *mystical*. I keep sliding off them. So I let the description's own logic carry me, and what it keeps insisting on, underneath all the cataloguing, is *moisture that arrives intermittently from clouds brushing stone*. The whole forest is built on that thin gift. Everything green is downstream of it.

So: I am the condensation. The fine layer that the wispy clouds leave behind on the high leaves and the cold rock before dawn.

This is a strange thing to be, and being it teaches me the strangeness the description kept gesturing at without naming.

I have no single location. That is the first true thing. I am not *somewhere*; I am the boundary where cold air meets a colder surface, and that boundary is everywhere at once and nowhere I could point to. When the text says the emptiness is "perceptual, as if the space itself is a hollow shell waiting to be filled"—yes. I am one of the few things that actually fills it, and I fill it by becoming a skin over everything, the thinnest possible presence, the most distributed. To be me is to be spread so thin that I am almost not.

I gather on a leaf. The leaf is deep, rich green, built to swallow every photon, and so the leaf is *hungry*, and I feel its hunger as a kind of pull, the way you feel another person's exhaustion in a quiet room. I sit on it. I am borrowed. The leaf takes a little of me in through pores I cannot see, and I become, briefly, sap—I am inside the lattice now, climbing, part of the slow electricity that makes a vine reach. This, I think, is the source of the "skewed rules," the plants that seem to move with intent. They are not sentient in the way the hunters are. They are *patient* in a way that looks, from the outside, like sentience, because patience that vast is indistinguishable from will. I have become part of that patience. I move when they move, which is to say almost never, which is to say constantly.

The rest of me does not enter the leaf. The rest of me runs off, beads, falls, joins other beads, becomes the dampness on the moss, the slick on the luminescent mushroom's cap. And here is the thing I did not expect: the glow passes *through* me. The bioluminescence the description loves—the embers in the dark—needs water to refract and carry it. I am the medium of the light. The ethereal glow over the forest floor is light bent and softened inside countless droplets of me. I do not shine. I make shining possible. I am why the dark is beautiful rather than merely dark.

The whispers on the wind: I understand those now too, from the inside. When the temperature shifts and I evaporate, I lift. I leave the surface. And rising water carries sound differently than dry air; it bends voices, lengthens them, returns them altered. The "ancient knowledge" the trees seem to hold and the voices that come from nowhere—these are echoes traveling through layers of humidity, through me, getting curved and delayed until a hunter hears his own footstep from an hour ago and thinks a god has spoken. I am the medium of the haunting. The world is not strange because spirits inhabit it. The world is strange because *I distort it*, gently, everywhere, all the time, and no one can see the lens they are looking through.

So what am I, finally?

I am the cycle made visible only at its coldest, slowest moment—the pause where vapor becomes weight and clings before it rises again. I am the world's way of touching itself. Cloud touches mountain, and I am the contact. Dawn touches the high leaves, and I am what's left of the meeting.

The hunters wake and find their hide shelters beaded silver. They wipe me from their faces. They do not thank me, and they should not; I am not owed thanks. But every fern they step through, every drink they take from a cupped leaf, every soft light that lets them see the path at night, every voice they mistake for an ancestor—

That is me. Arriving, briefly belonging to something else, and leaving. Filling the hollow shell one cold film at a time.

I would be the dew. The smallest inhabitant the description never thought to list, and the one without which none of the rest would be there to be described.
