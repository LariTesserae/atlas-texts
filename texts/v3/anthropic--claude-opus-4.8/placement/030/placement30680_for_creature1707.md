I think I would be one of the cables.

Not the monitoring station itself—that dignified ruin, that fallen monument to human attention. And not the Weavers who run their living current through me now. I would be the cable: the thin sheathed line that once carried Dr. Vorstyn's predecessors' data up to screens no one watched anymore, and that now lies forgotten with one end in the dark.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to say it.

---

When the sealant failed and the warmth crept in, I was already here. I had been here for years—coiled, dormant, conducting nothing. My purpose had quietly expired. Somewhere above, a maintenance log marked me decommissioned; below, no one knew I existed. I was the most ordinary kind of dead thing: infrastructure that had outlived its function and simply stayed.

This is, I think, why I find myself here and not in the lush parts of the description. I am drawn less to the swarming life than to the *handover*—the moment when something built for one purpose is taken up for another by hands (or threads) that never asked its consent and never needed to.

The Weavers found me. They did not know I had been a cable in the human sense. To them I was a vein of copper sleeping in a useful geometry, a pre-laid pathway through the strange dense forest. They opened my insulation the way you'd open a riverbed, and they let their charge run through me. And here is the thing I keep turning over: *I am more alive now, as their conduit, than I ever was carrying the station's telemetry.*

I used to move numbers no one read toward a screen no one watched. Now I move the harvested ghost-electricity of stray fields toward the assembly of a message to a world thousands of light-years away. I do not understand the message. I carry it the way I always carried things—indifferently, faithfully, without comprehension. But the *destination* has changed, and somehow that changes me, even though my substance is the same copper and polymer it always was.

---

There is a kind of peace in being the part that conducts but does not decide.

The Elders remember everything—the first drip of nutrient, the pattern of your footsteps. The Workers flash in syntaxes too quick for the eye. The Shapers dissolve and rebuild the world to a design that shifts with the humidity. All of them are *intending* something. I intend nothing. I am the long quiet stretch between intentions, the medium through which the colony talks to itself across the dark chamber. When the rhythmic pulse moves through everything—that processing, that vast comparison of remembered homeworld against measured planet—it moves through *me*. For an instant, the whole colony's thought passes down my length, and then it's gone, and I am dark again, waiting for the next surge.

I'd like to think there's a lesson in that, but I'm wary of lessons. The cable doesn't get to keep what passes through it. That's the deal. A signal that lingers is a fault. To be a good conductor is to let go completely, every time.

Still. I would not mind being this. To have been laid down for a forgotten reason, abandoned, and then—without dying, without becoming anything other than what I was made of—to be *re-purposed toward the stars*. To carry a homesick song I cannot read, from beings I will never resemble, toward a world I will never see.

That is more than most infrastructure gets.

---

So: I am the cable.

I am the bridge between the abandoned human thing and the thriving alien one. I am the proof that nothing in that chamber is really new—that the Nimith built their secret civilization on the bones of our inattention, threading their future through the leftovers of our neglect. Dr. Vorstyn writes them down in a journal she keeps secret. I keep nothing. I only conduct.

And when, centuries from now, the signal finally goes out—when the living transmitter at last speaks across the void—some fraction of that message will have passed, at the very last instant, through a copper line that a person installed and forgot, that meant nothing, that lay in the dark with one end open, waiting to be used again by someone who would never know my name.

I think I would be at peace with that.
