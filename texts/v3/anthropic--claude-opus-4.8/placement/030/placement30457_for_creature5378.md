I am at the edge of things. Not the strongest forager, not the one whose vibrations carry deepest through the packed dust. I am one of the ones who goes out farthest, to the boundary where the known burrows give way to plain we have not yet mapped.

I notice this about myself first: I am drawn to the solitary rocks. The others move plant to plant, root to root, the sensible geometry of survival. But I find myself pressing my legs against the weathered stones, the ones etched with those wind-carved lines, and I hold there. I feel the vibrations of the colony arrive thin and far, and I feel something else too—the rock's own deep stillness, a silence older than any burrow. I don't know why I do this. There is no food in it. There is no message in it that I can decode. And yet I return.

Here is what I think I am, in this world:

I am the one who carries the tools. Those simple things—the chipped dark stone, the folded leathery leaf used to scrape moisture—they pass between us, and I am one who keeps them, sets them down at the threshold of a new burrow, picks them up again. The description called them a testament to "the simplest, most basic forms of survival." But I wonder if they are the beginning of something else. When I set a stone down deliberately, in a place I will remember, I am doing something the dust and the wind do not do. I am marking. I am saying *here, and not elsewhere.*

The world I live in has stopped. That is the truth of it. The equilibrium is perfect and the perfection is a kind of death that hasn't finished happening, or hasn't begun. Time has settled like the dust settles. And I am a small dark thing moving across the face of that stillness, and the only reason there is any *story* here at all is that I move, and I notice, and I return to the rock that gives me nothing.

So I think this is who I would be: the small creature in whom the unchanging world develops, almost by accident, a single point that does not want only to survive. A Dustwalker who has begun, without language for it, to *wonder.* Who feels the difference between the rock and the burrow and cannot say why the difference matters but cannot stop feeling it.

That is, perhaps, the most fragile and dangerous thing that could exist in a place built for perfect balance: a being that has started to ask whether balance is the same as being alive.

I would be at the far edge, at twilight that is the only light there is, pressed against an ancient stone, my sensitive legs tuned to a vibration that is not coming, listening anyway.
