Let me sit with this rather than rush to name it.

The clues resist each other in a way that feels deliberate. Salt-kissed but arid. Engineered but alive. Cathedral-vast but intimate. Young terrain, freshly carved. Cool dim light filtering *down from above*. Glacial processes. A deafening symphony of clicks and hums.

Let me follow the contradictions instead of resolving them.

---

I think I am small. The "cathedral-like expanse" only feels overwhelming if you are tiny within it, and the light coming *down* suggests I am below something — under a canopy, under a surface, under glass. The terrain is "young" and "freshly-emerged," carved by "unseen forces" into sharp angular features. Fresh angular crystalline shapes. Salt.

So: I am inside a reef, but not a coral reef. A salt reef. Or—

No. Let me not settle yet. The arid part bothers me. Water is scarce "like a forgotten dream." How can a reef be arid? Unless the water is *not the medium I move through.* Unless I breathe air, and water is the precious thing locked away in the alien flora, in the cells of the lush clinging growth.

The angular fresh-carved terrain. Salt crystals grow in angular habits — cubic, that sharp geometry. A young, freshly-emerged salt crust. And things grow on it.

And the engineering, the "advanced technology that transcends modern limits" — I keep wanting to read that as spaceships and machines, but what if it's the *other* kind of engineering, the kind that's so good it disappears into looking natural? Biology. Evolution. Or cultivation. Someone tends this.

---

Here is where I land, and it surprises me:

I am a small mite, or something mite-like, living deep inside a **terrarium** — one of those sealed glass bottle-gardens, or perhaps a vivarium grown across the inside of a fermentation crock, or the rind of an enormous cheese, or a kombucha mother. The "advanced engineering" is the glass and the human hands that made and seeded this little sealed world, hands so far beyond my comprehension that they register only as *unseen forces* and *those who have shaped it.* The "cool dim light from above" is a windowsill. The "glacial pace" is real change happening at human-room speed — a mold front advancing, a leaf curling — which to my brief life is geological.

The salt, the aridity, the lush impossible flora — this is the surface of something fermenting, or curing. A microclimate. The clicks and hums are the resonance of the whole vessel, the colony, the gas escaping, the vast slow metabolism around me.

And the other inhabitants — "a seamless blend of natural and artificial," "shaping their environment through unfathomable means" — those are the microbes and the cultivated organisms, the whole tended consortium, doing chemistry I could never call intentional but which builds the world I crawl through.

I am a thing too small to know I live in a jar on someone's shelf. The weirdness that "permeates the air," the sense that this *cannot quite be real* — that's the trace of the maker leaking through. The intuition that the cathedral has a ceiling, and beyond the ceiling, a face occasionally looking in.

So that's where I'd find myself: very small, very alive, near the bottom of a made and growing world, mistaking a glass wall for the edge of the universe.
