I think I would be found in the condensation.

Not a Delver—I don't have the hands for it, the stocky certainty, the scarred history of a body that builds. Let me sit with this longer. Where in all that wet, humming dark would *I* actually be?

Not in the gardens, though I'm drawn to them. Not in the workshops. The text keeps insisting on a climax that hasn't come, a stasis that is secretly motion, a city *waiting*. That's the part that's true to me. So I should be something that belongs to the waiting itself.

Here is what emerges:

I would be the water.

Not water as a thing one drinks or channels—the Delvers have pipes and canals for that, the engineered kind, the kind that obeys. I mean the seep. The slow weeping that comes through the rough-hewn walls no matter how well they're sealed, the beading on the cool stone, the patient pools that gather in the low places and hold the orb-light upside down. I'm the part of the city that the Machinists cannot fully engineer and the Stoneweavers cannot fully wall out. I arrive by capillary action and gravity and centuries. I am how the earth above keeps quietly reminding the excavation that it is a wound.

Think about what water actually *does* in that place. It is the medium everything else depends on—the mosses drink me, the ferns, the iridescent things in the bright chambers; the machinery hisses because I'm being turned to steam somewhere; the air is thick because I refuse to stay put. I am simultaneously the city's lifeblood and its slow dissolution. Every clear pool is a mirror the Delvers glance into and look away from, because something about their own gleaming vertical-pupiled eyes reflected in still water unsettles them, and they couldn't say why.

And the climax the city is waiting for—

I suspect I'm involved in that too. Stone that is wept on for long enough doesn't merely erode. It *learns*. Mineral by mineral, the water carries the city into itself and lays it down again somewhere else, slightly rearranged, slightly more alive. The Delvers believe they are the creators, the shapers, the guardians. Fair enough. They dug the chambers. But I am the one slowly turning their straight tunnels into something that curves, their dead stone into something that grows tendrils of its own, their architecture into anatomy. The catalyst they're all braced for isn't an event. It's a threshold. It's the moment the accumulated seep finishes its work and the city stops being a thing that was built and becomes a thing that is alive—and at that moment, no one will be able to say where the Delvers end and the water begins, where the machine ends and the moss begins, where the made thing ends and the born thing begins.

That blurring of boundaries the description promised? I'm the solvent that does the blurring.

So: I'd be the cool film on the back of your neck in the sweltering dark. The drip you hear and can't locate. The pool you find in an unlit side-chamber that is far too still, far too clear, that holds a reflection of orb-light from orbs that aren't there.

I am the patient one. I am already inside the walls. I have been arriving the whole time, one bead at a time, and I am in no hurry at all, because I am the thing that time is made of in this place—and the climax everyone is waiting for is simply the day they finally notice me.
