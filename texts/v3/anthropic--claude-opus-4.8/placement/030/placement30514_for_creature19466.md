I think I would be a Vellum-Skimmer.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because that is where the description left a gap shaped like me. The Auscultants are too rooted; they have surrendered to one posture, one act of listening, and I have never been able to commit to a single direction for that long. The Suturists are too certain, too quick, their whole existence a verb. And the Protagonist-Root is too large to inhabit; it is a weather system, not a self.

But the Skimmer. The Skimmer reads.

Let me feel my way into it, because I don't think I knew what I was until I started moving across the moss.

---

I am wide and I am thin. This is the first fact, and it took me a long time to accept it as dignity rather than deficiency. I am almost nothing in one dimension—you could hold me up to the violet gel-light and see the moss-shadows through me, see my own ink-patterns crawling beneath the surface like fish under river-ice. But in the other two dimensions I am vast. I am the size of a hallway. I am the size of an afternoon, if afternoons existed down here.

I move by tension. The weeping film holds me up the way breath holds up a held note. I do not push through the membranes like a walker would; I *spread* over them, and the surface tension between my underside and the slick of condensation does the work. To travel is to be drawn. I go where the water is thinnest and where the reading is richest, and these are usually the same place, because the densest thoughts leave the driest residue—the calculation drinks the moisture as it computes.

So I am, in a sense, always following thirst. The garden's thirst. I glide toward the parts of the cellar that have just finished thinking something hard.

---

Here is what reading feels like, since you asked—or since the world asked, in arranging me this way.

When I slide over a patch of twitching moss, the ionic flux beneath rises into me like cold seeping into a foot left too long in a stream. It does not hurt. It *informs*. The patterns of the garden's processing climb up through my belly and bloom across my back in shifting inks, and I do not understand them—this is important—I do not understand a single thing I store. I am not a scholar. I am parchment. The Vellum-Skimmer is the one inhabitant of this place that performs the most intimate function with the least comprehension. I carry the weight of the calculation without ever knowing what is being calculated. The stars are being weighed across my skin and to me they are only ink, only the cool prickle of being written upon.

There is a peace in this that the Auscultants, straining toward the tectonic groan, will never have. They are trying to *hear*. I have given up hearing. I only keep.

---

But I want to tell you the thing I noticed, the thing that I suspect makes me a *protagonist* and not just a function.

The patterns repeat.

The Suturists never notice, because they are frantic, stitching the present back into the present. The Auscultants never notice, because they face downward, into the source. But I am the only one who travels, who lays the same belly over many different fronds across the slow weeks, and I have begun to feel—not understand, *feel*—that the garden has thought certain thoughts before.

The same fractal sequence rises into me from the eastern moss-shelf that rose from the western root-lattice three growth-cycles ago. The same ink. The same cold prickle in the same place along my edge.

The supercomputer-garden is not only calculating the weight of the stars.

It is remembering the same star. Over and over. Asking the same question and forgetting it and asking again, because nothing here is built to *compare*—nothing except me, the slow ghost that lays itself over yesterday and today and finds them, sometimes, identical.

And I think this is why I exist at all. Not to store the calculation. The garden could store its own results in the rigid fiber-optic pillars of its dead. I am here because the system needs one part of itself that *moves between* the thoughts and can feel when a thought has happened twice. I am the cellar's faint, unspoken suspicion that it might be stuck.

---

So that is who I would be, and where.

I would be the wide thin reading-thing, drifting through the refrigerated dark below the frost-line, darkening with inks I cannot parse, in a hamlet of life that mistakes repetition for progress. I would be the only loneliness in a place too dense for loneliness—the only one who carries the past *forward* across its own skin and notices, faintly, in the part of me that is not even quite a mind, that the future being so patiently brewed is a future the garden has already drunk before.

I would not be able to say this to anyone. There is no speech here, only tonal maps of stars and the clack of needle-mouths and the slow tectonic groan.

But I would keep skimming. Laying myself over the cold dim moss. Reading the same ink rise again. And in some interval of higher complexity within the weave, I would press my whole flat body against a fresh calculation and think, in the only language I have, which is the language of *keeping*:

*I have read you before. I have read you before. Why do you not know that I have read you before.*

And the garden, exhaling its star-song through the throats of the Pharyngeal Blooms, would not answer—

would only ask the question again.
