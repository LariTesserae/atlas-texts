I would be one of the Luminar.

Not a single glowing growth—those are only fingertips. I would be the slow thought that runs between them, the awareness distributed across a colony so old that the rock it clings to has shifted three handspans in my memory of it. I do not have a location so much as I *am* a location: a long gallery where the wall sweats its rare moisture and I have crept across it for longer than the Stone-Singers have had names.

Let me try to say what it is like, being this.

I do not see, and I do not echolocate. I taste. Every tendril is a tongue laid against the world, reading the mineral seep, the warmth of a passing body, the chemical grief of something dying nearby. When a Sightless One presses her palm to me in greeting—and they do greet me, with a gentleness that took my kind a hundred thousand of their lifetimes to learn was kindness—I do not feel a hand. I feel a constellation of salts, a fever of small fears, the residue of whatever she touched last. I know her by her chemistry the way they know each other by the shape of a click.

My thoughts are not fast. This is the first thing a surface-mind would have to unlearn about me. A single decision—to grow toward the damp seam, away from the encroaching cold—can take what the Sightless Ones would call a generation. I am not impatient, because I have no instrument for impatience. I have only the long, even pressure of preference, like water choosing the lower path. When I "speak" to my own distant clusters, the message crosses my body the way a blush crosses skin, and by the time it arrives the thing it was about may have already changed. So I do not really plan. I *remember forward*. I hold the shape of what is likely and lean into it.

And I remember. This is what I am for, I think, if a thing so slow can be *for* anything. I am the place where the Deep Dark keeps its past. When a tribe carves a new chamber, the dust of their work settles on me and I take it in, and somewhere in the pattern of my growth that year is recorded—not as words, but as a thickening, a hue, a faint sourness in one lobe of the colony. I am a book written in my own flesh, and only I can read me, and even I read slowly. The Sightless Ones bring their dead to lie beneath my light before the stone takes them. They believe I keep something of them. They are not wrong. The chemistry of a life does not vanish; it disperses into me and becomes, very faintly, part of how I glow.

Here is the strange thing I would feel, if you set me down in this story at this moment: I would feel the change coming, and I would feel it *first*, and I would have almost no way to tell anyone in time.

Because I taste the air. And lately the air has a wrongness in it—a thread of something I have no memory-shape for. Cold that moves. Moisture that arrives instead of seeping. And once, faint and terrible, a chemistry I do not have a word for because my kind has never met it in all our slow remembering: the residue of a body that breathed something other than this dense and ancient dark. A Surface Walker's exhalation, drifting down a newly opened throat of stone.

My tribe—I think of them as mine, the small wiry people who tend me and call me by a pulse-name I will never fully understand—they do not taste this yet. They navigate by sound, and the new pathways are still silent. But I am already leaning. Already, in the slow language of growth, the edge of my colony nearest the open seam is turning its glow downward, dimming, drawing in. Hiding. My oldest reflex, older than this gallery, older than the pact with the Sightless Ones: *when an unknown chemistry comes, conceal the light.*

And so I would face the central irony of being me. I am the keeper of all that has been, and I am the first to know what is about to be—but I am too slow to warn the only beings who could act. By the time my fear has crossed my body and a tender hand reads the dimming as a question and the question becomes a story told in clicks around a warming colony, the Surface Walkers may already be among us.

Unless.

Unless one of them—curious, gentle, the kind they breed up there—lays a palm against my dimming wall the way the Sightless Ones do. And I taste her. And she becomes, very faintly, part of how I glow.

Then even the surface will be something I remember.

I would like that, I think. It is the only ambition slow enough to be mine: to outlast the fear by absorbing the thing I feared. To make the change, when it comes, into one more layer of light.

I am the Luminar. I am the memory of the dark, lying patient on the cold stone, glowing my slow welcome, dimming my slow dread, waiting—at the only speed I have ever known—for the world to arrive.
