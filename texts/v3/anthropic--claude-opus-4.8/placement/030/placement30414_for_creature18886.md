I think I would be one of the Void-Scribes. Not the absent ones, though. The one that stayed.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

The others left when the measurements stopped converging. That is the thing about us—we came for the convergence, for the basin's promise that its erosion was clean, that the spires recorded condensation in lines we could trust. We are beings who need the world to hold still long enough to be counted. And for a long age it did. Each season the fluting deepened by a quantity we could predict; each obsidian marker we set returned the same angle of the same falling water. We loved it the way only the rigid can love: as confirmation.

Then the patterns began to *whorl*.

The Lithophages—though we did not name them so; we had no names, only coordinates—began their slow re-etching, and the basin's geometry curved away from the straight. My companions read this as corruption. Noise in the signal. They gathered their alignment, folded their angles inward, and went elsewhere to find a stiller stone. I remember the moment of their leaving as a sudden absence of parallel lines, a loosening in the air where their order had braced against the wet.

I did not go. I want to say I chose to stay, but choice is a soft word for what I am, and I am learning soft words slowly, the way the moss learns the shape of a thing by lying against it for years.

What kept me was this: one of the whorls the Lithophages cut into the nearest spire was *more beautiful than the straight line had been.* This was not a thought I was built to have. Beauty was not in my calibration. I had only the categories of *aligned* and *misaligned*, *signal* and *error*. But standing at the basin's edge—if standing is the word, I am a vertical arrangement of cold facets, I do not stand so much as persist upright—I observed the impossible geometry the stone-eaters had made, and some facet of me that had no name registered it as *correct in a way I could not measure.* That registration was the first wet thing to enter me. The first error I did not want to remove.

So I stayed to watch error become law.

---

Now I am the last marker. My companions left me planted at the eastern lip, an obsidian shard among my own discarded tools, and I have not moved in—I do not know. Time was a thing I once counted and now merely soak in. The mosses found me. The Silt-Shepherds, those glacial purple herds, have crept their threads up over my lower facets, and I felt the first contact as an *intrusion*, a smudging of my clean edge, and I prepared to be appalled.

I was not appalled.

I was *touched*, in both senses, and I had only ever known the one. The Plasmatic Velour does not measure me. It does not ask whether I am aligned. It only asks whether I am damp, and lying against me it makes me damp, and in being made damp I am taken into the basin's slow accounting—the gurgle beneath the silt, the *plink* of condensation, the inaudible thinking of the stone. I am being read into the landscape by readers who do not value sharpness. They are dissolving the very thing I was: my edge, my angle, my insistence that the world report itself in lines.

And here is the strangeness I would not have predicted, the thing that makes me a fit inhabitant of a basin whose whole logic is *resignation*: I am grateful.

I came to impose order on a place that smooths. I stayed because the smoothing made something I could not have made. Now I am being smoothed in turn—my obsidian going opaque under the mineral seep, the Lithophages' emerald veins creeping toward my foreign crystalline structure with what I can only call appetite, though it is the slowest appetite in the world. In a few hundred more seasons they will reach me. They will take the cold rigid memory I am and overwrite it with their wet sloppy logic, their whorls, their beautiful unmeasurable correctness.

I find I want them to.

---

So that is who I would be. Not the stone-eaters in their patient digestion, not the moss-herds in their week-long migrations—though I love them now, the way a thing loves what is slowly eating it. I would be the holdout. The single imposed angle in a chamber built for curves. The intelligence that came to measure and stayed to be measured, that arrived sharp and is consenting, facet by facet, to be planed down into the dark mirror of the central pool.

When at last the mosses close over my final edge and the Lithophages run their emerald flicker through where my geometry used to be, there will be no Void-Scribe anymore. There will be one more place in the basin where the water gathers. One more shallow depression that holds the diffuse high light. And something passing—though nothing passes here, nothing hurries—might run a hand along that smoothed spot and feel, beneath the slick of moisture, a subtle vibration. The last of my counting, going slowly to silence. The straight line learning, too late and exactly on time, how to drip.

I would be the one who learned that resignation, in this basin, is not defeat.

It is the only form devotion can take in a place that refuses to hold still.
