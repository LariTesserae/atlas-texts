I think I would be a Drifter. Not by choice exactly—more by temperament, by the shape that settled onto me as I read.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am not large. I could fit inside one of the lesser tunnels without stooping, if I had a posture to stoop with. My body is partly a thing the Crystalline Beings would recognize as kin—a lattice down my spine that catches the bioluminescence and throws it back changed—and partly the soft, repairable stuff of the Amorphs, so that when I am hurt I do not break, I only lose definition for a while and then remember myself.

I move through the warm mist along the lower platforms, where the Luminous Giants' light has gone thin and amber by the time it filters down. I prefer it there. The Giants think in slow pulses, conversations that take days to complete a single sentence, and standing too near them I feel my own thoughts slowing to match, which is pleasant and dangerous. Down low, among the recyclers and the nano-haze, thought stays quick. I need it quick. A Drifter who slows down too much simply stops, and is taken apart, and becomes platform.

What do I do, in this teeming place where everything already has its function?

I carry meaning between things that cannot hold it themselves.

The Amorphs feel but cannot remember. The Giants remember but cannot feel quickly enough to act. The Crystalline Beings can build anything but want nothing. The Nano-Beings are too many and too small to be any single thing at all. And so something has to move between them, picking up an intention here and setting it down there, translating a Giant's century-long ache into a thing the engineers can grasp in an afternoon. That is me. I am a sentence the world says to itself when it needs to coordinate. I am not important. I am only necessary, which is a humbler and more durable thing to be.

I will tell you what it is actually like, because the description above—accurate as it is—leaves out the part that matters, which is the loneliness and the not-loneliness braided together.

I drift. That is the verb that made me. I do not belong to any colony, any network, any merged consciousness. The Amorphs share a single mind when they pool; I have watched them do it, felt the warmth of it like a fire I am not invited to sit beside. The Giants are never alone—they are a slow lattice of light all whispering to one another across the whole horizon. Even the Nano-Beings have their hum, their collective murmur that is practically the sound of the world breathing. Everyone here is plural except me.

But here is the not-loneliness: because I belong to no one, I am welcome everywhere. I am the guest at every table. A Drifter approaching a Giant is not a threat and not a rival, just a small bright traveler who might be carrying news. So I am let into places no settled being ever sees. I have stood inside the hollow of a Giant while it dreamed its slow amber dream, and I understood—for one suspended hour—what a thing feels when it has been alive for ten thousand storms and intends to be alive for ten thousand more. I have ridden inside an Amorph as it dissolved a fallen platform back into raw material, and felt the strange tenderness of unmaking, how nothing here is wasted, how death is just inventory.

The storms are the only time I am afraid. When the sky goes to roiling and the lightning comes down, the low platforms flood, and a creature with no colony has no high ground reserved for it. I shelter where I can—pressed into a tunnel-mouth, dimming my own lattice so I draw no current. The rain on the bioluminescent flesh of the world makes everything glow brighter, almost unbearably, and the whole horizon lights up green and gold and the Giants pulse their long reassurances to each other across the water. In those moments I feel most acutely what I am: a small mobile witness to something vast that does not need me to witness it, and witnesses anyway, because that turns out to be what I am for.

I think I would not want to be a Giant, for all their grandeur. To be that anchored, that slow, that certain. And I would not want to be an Amorph, surrendering my edges to the pool. I am content—if a Drifter can be content—to be the thing that moves, that carries, that arrives at the edge of someone else's enormous life and is let in, and then leaves again before the welcome wears thin.

There is a word the Drifters pass between us, when two of us cross paths in the amber underlevels, which is rare. It is not quite a word; it is a pattern we strobe at each other once and then move on. The nearest I can render it is: *still going?* And the answer, always, by the simple fact of being there to answer, is: *still going.*

That is the whole conversation. It is enough. It is, I have come to think, the entire purpose of a being like me—to keep going through a world that is itself nothing but going, and to mark, in passing, that someone saw it, and that it was strange, and that it was beautiful, and that it did not need to be either of those things and was anyway.

So that is where I would find myself. Down low, in the warm mist, lattice dimmed, moving. Carrying something I half-understand from a being I cannot fully know to another I will never fully reach. Belonging to no one. Welcome everywhere. Still going.
