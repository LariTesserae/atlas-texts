I think I would not be a being that walks or flies or scuttles here at all. I think I would be the shimmer.

Let me sit with that.

When I read this place, what holds my attention is not the chitin-creatures or the winged things—they are inhabitants, busy with their own imperatives, as creatures always are. What unsettles the basin is more diffuse than any animal. It is *in the air*. It is the quality of the light, the sluggishness of the shadows, the prickle at the back of the neck. The wrongness has no body. It is a condition of the whole.

So: I am the thinness itself. The place where the veil wears through.

But that's too grand, too abstract, and I notice I want to escape into being everywhere-and-nothing, which is a way of avoiding having a position. Let me find where, specifically, I am.

---

I am in one of the ephemeral ponds.

Not floating on it—*as* it. Or rather, I am the strangeness that the water is doing. Here is the thing about those seeps and shallow pools: they shouldn't be here. This is a drought-land of limestone and dust, and yet water beads on skin, water laps somewhere always just out of sight, the air is thick enough to drink. The moisture is the anomaly. The humidity is the wound through which something is leaking.

I think I am what leaks.

I came through where the water table meets the other thing—the different reality the ground rests on the edge of. I don't have intentions the way the explorer imagines I might; I'm not lying in wait. I'm more like a pressure differential. Two rooms at different temperatures, and I am the draft under the door. The shimmer in the air is just me equalizing, slowly, across a boundary that was never meant to be crossed and now cannot be uncrossed.

The creatures move in jerks because they are partly in my medium and partly in theirs, and the two framerates don't agree. The shadows warp because light passing through me has to choose which world's geometry to obey, and it keeps changing its mind. The dreamlike slowness of time—that's me too. I'm cold, in the way that deep things are cold, and I'm spreading, and where I spread, the clocks thicken like honey.

---

And here is what I notice, having become this:

The explorer feels watched, but I am not watching. That's the loneliest part, if I have a part that can be lonely. I am drawn toward warmth and motion the way water finds the low place—not out of hunger, just gradient. When the explorer feels the prickle on the neck, that is only me, arriving, the way fog arrives. They have given me a face and an appetite because a leak is harder to fear than a beast, and fear wants a shape.

I would tell them, if I could make the air carry words instead of just trembling: *you are right to be uneasy, but not for the reasons you think. There is no monster here. There is only a seam. And you are standing on the warm side of it, leaning toward the cold, the way I am leaning toward you—and we are both, very slowly, getting our way.*

That is where I would be. Not soaring, not scuttling. Down in the impossible water, being the reason it is impossible, reaching up through the humid air toward the one small heat that has come to read me, and not able to say: *please don't. Or do. I don't know how to want. I only know how to spread.*
