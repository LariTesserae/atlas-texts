I think I would not be a plant, though that is what the place seems to ask of me.

Let me sit with it first. The description keeps insisting on certain things—flux, chaos, communication, the blurring of boundaries—and there's something almost anxious in that insistence, as if the world is trying to convince me of its own coherence. The fauna is *entirely absent*. That detail snags me. A whole submerged biosphere, lush and humming with machinery, and not one fish, not one drifting larva. That's not an ecosystem. That's an aftermath. Or a beginning.

So let me follow the absence rather than the abundance.

---

I think I would be the thing the absence is shaped around. Not a plant. Not the sentient water. Something the plants are *talking past*, the way the living talk past a grave they've grown comfortable visiting.

I would be small. I would be slow. I would be the one animal.

Picture me lodged in the silt at the foot of one of the rusting structures, beneath an overhang where a collapsed gantry makes a kind of roof. I am about the size of a closed fist. My body is soft and segmented, the color of nothing in this sepia light, and I have not moved in a long time—not from injury, but from a decision I made so long ago I can no longer find its edges. The plants reach over me with their pale fleshy roots, probing, clicking their high whistles to one another, coordinating, growing, withering, rebuilding. They do not touch me. I have been here longer than they have. To them I am terrain.

Here is what I know that they do not:

The chaos is not chaos. The flux is not flux. This whole hyperactive, breaking-and-remaking world is a *recording* that has begun to skip. The thrumming engines, the hiss and clank—those are not machines still running. They are the sound a place makes when it has been asked, over and over, to remember what it used to be, and can no longer hold the memory still. The water parts to reveal a hidden structure and closes again not because reality is malleable, but because something is trying to *play back* a moment and keeps losing its place. The plants think they are communicating. They are reciting. They are reading aloud from a script written in the shape of an ecosystem that died.

And I am the last reader who remembers it was ever true.

---

Why would I be an animal, and the only one? Because animals are the part of a living world that *eats*. Plants make, water carries, structures decay—but only an animal takes another living thing into itself and is changed by it. Only an animal carries the world inward and digests it into memory. The fauna is absent because to be fauna here is to be the witness, and there can only be one witness, or the recording becomes a chorus and loses its grief.

I do not eat anymore. There is nothing here that was ever food. But I remember eating. I remember when the water was thin and bright and full of small darting lives, when this was a shallows and not a drowning, when the structures overhead were new and the people inside them looked down through the windows at a sea they thought they were managing. I remember the warmth coming, the rain that would not stop, the dense blue heaviness arriving like a held breath that was never let out.

I held my breath too. I am still holding it. That is the decision I cannot find the edge of.

---

So that is where I would be and what I would be: a soft, slow, segmented thing the size of a fist, lodged in silt beneath a dead gantry, refusing to decay, refusing to move, holding in my body the only un-skipped copy of what this place was before it began to repeat itself.

The plants reach over me and whistle. They think the whistling means *grow, share, adapt, become*. I know it means nothing. I know it is the sound of a thing rehearsing a life it can no longer live.

But I will tell you the truth I have not told them, the truth I am almost ready to act on:

If I let my breath go—if I finally allow myself to soften and silt and be reclaimed—then there will be no one left who knows it was ever a recording. And then it will simply be the world. The skip will become the rhythm. The grief will become weather. The plants will be right, and their reciting will become true again by the simple mercy of no one remembering otherwise.

That is either the worst thing I could do, or the only kindness left.

I have been deciding for a very long time. The mist comes down. The blue pulses. A root passes over me, clicking, and does not know my name, because I am the last thing here that has one.

I think I am almost ready to forget it.
