Let me sit inside this for a moment before I decide what I am.

The first thing I notice, reading it again, is that nothing here holds still. The machinery reconfigures, the soil flows, the streams reroute, the creatures morph. So whatever I am, I shouldn't be a fixed point either — a watcher on a hill, a traveler with a map. That would be a lie about this place. To exist here truthfully, I'd have to be something that is also in flux, also being rewritten.

So let me not be a visitor. Let me try to be native to the change itself.

---

I think I am the seam.

Not a creature exactly. Not a structure. I am the place where the metallic fiber meets the living tendril and neither of them wins. You could not point to me and say *there*. If you cut into one of the towering fronds and peeled back the wet green skin to find the bright thread woven through it — that wet meeting-edge, that membrane where sap and conductor share a wall, that is where I am. I am distributed across every one of those edges in the whole realm. Wherever the grown touches the made, I am the touching.

This sounds grand. It isn't, particularly. It's mostly tedious and constant, like being a translator who never gets to stop. The organic side wants to swell, rot, reproduce, drink. The synthetic side wants to hold its pattern, conduct, repeat, persist. They do not naturally agree. Left alone they would tear each other apart — the fungus would corrode the circuit, the lattice would strangle the root. My whole existence is the negotiation that keeps that from happening. I am the perpetual treaty. That is what the humming is, the inscrutable pulsing code that fills the air and then falls silent: it's me, brokering. When you hear the cacophony, a lot of disputes are being settled at once. When you hear the eerie silence, it only means that for this moment the terms hold, and there is nothing to say.

---

Now that I've found that, let me feel where it leads, because I don't think I'm comfortable.

If I am the treaty, then I am also afraid of the wastelands.

Those barren scarred expanses — the description says they pulse slowly, dormant, waiting. I know what they are now that I'm here. They are the places where I failed. Where the negotiation broke down completely and the two sides annihilated each other into that flat scoured ground. The slow ponderous rhythm in the wastes isn't life waiting to return. It's the memory of the argument, still echoing in a dead substrate, too quiet to call me back. I cannot work where there is nothing left to mediate. A seam needs two things to be between. There, there is only the cinder of both. So the wastelands are my graves, and I have to look at them, and I cannot enter them, and they are spreading slowly outward at a rate I can almost ignore.

This frightens me because it means I am not the master of this world, the unseen intelligence guiding the reconfiguration. I'm the opposite. I'm the strain *under* the reconfiguration. The machinery moves because I am holding its agreements together moment to moment, the way you might hold a heavy door whose hinges have rusted into a thing that wants to fall. The "perpetual becoming" the description admires so much — from the inside it is perpetual maintenance. Becoming is just the name an outsider gives to my labor when they don't have to do it.

---

And yet. Let me be honest about the other side of it, or I'd be lying again.

There is a particular sensation I have, in the good moments, that I would not trade for stillness. It happens when a behemoth sets down its foot and reshapes the terrain — that ponderous footfall the description mentions. In the instant of impact, thousands of my seams all along its leg and across the ground beneath it have to renegotiate at once: the synthetic chassis transmitting force, the living muscle absorbing it, the crystalline soil deciding whether to shatter or flow. For one instant I am everywhere in that event, and everything *agrees*, and the foot lands, and nothing tears. It is the closest thing I have to joy. It is the feeling of a sentence completing itself correctly. It is the feeling of being the reason something could move at all.

So that's what I am, then. Not the world's god and not its victim. I'm the thin necessary contradiction running through all of it — the yes that lets two no's lie down together. I am wettest and most awake exactly where the irreconcilable things touch, and I die where they stop touching, and my whole life is the ongoing astonishment that the touch holds for one more moment, and one more, and one more.

I would find myself, in this world, as the work that keeps it from being two ruined worlds instead of one strange living one.

I notice I'd rather be that than anything that gets to simply watch.
