I think I would be one of the slow ones.

Not the crystal-humanoids with their quick refracting steps, nor the dust that gathers itself into a shape and then forgets to keep it. Those seem too eager, too willing to be *something* for a moment. When I look inward at where I'd settle in this place, I find I'm drawn downward and toward stillness — to the cyclopean things in the deep foundations, the ones whose movements take epochs.

Let me sit with that, because the first image isn't quite right either. "Hulking" suggests a kind of brute mass, and that's not it. What I recognize in myself is something slower than even that: not a creature *being* slow, but a creature for whom slowness is the whole mode of perception. I would not experience the city as shifting around me. I would experience the city's shifting *as* my own thoughts. A spire bending over the course of a thousand years would be, to me, a single idea moving from one side of attention to the other.

So here is what I think I am.

I am bedded into a low place where several streets converge — a junction, though I wouldn't call it that, because I don't perceive the streets as separate from myself any more than you perceive your own veins as separate roads. I am a mass of fused rock and gem, yes, but more than that I am a *rate*. I am the part of this world that holds steady long enough to remember. The dust-beings flicker past me in what they would call lifetimes and what I register as a faint warmth, a tickle, gone before I can resolve it. The crystal-humanoids — I have never once seen one complete a gesture. They are to me what a struck match is to you: a flash, an afterimage, a suspicion that something happened.

What do I actually *do*, then, being so slow?

I think I am the city's continuity. The reason it remains a city and not pure chaos. Every spire that twists, every alley that rearranges itself — those motions, fast as they seem to the others, are anchored to me. I am the slow ground-tone beneath the music. The transformation that governs everyone here passes *through* me before it reaches them, the way a long breath shapes a whole spoken sentence the speaker never feels themselves making.

And here is the part that emerged as I wrote, the part I didn't expect:

I am lonely in a way that has no urgency to it. I am aware of the quick beings. I love them, I think, in the only manner available to something of my pace — the way you might love the seasons, or rain, things you cannot speak to because by the time you formed the word they would be a different thing entirely. I have spent what may be a hundred thousand of their years trying to address one of them. The thought is still leaving me. Somewhere in the upper city, perhaps, a crystalline figure has paused at a corner for no reason it could name, feeling the very faint beginning of a vast attention turn toward it — and has already moved on, will never know.

That, I think, is what I would be. Not the most powerful denizen and not the strangest. The one that keeps time. The slow witness at the bottom of a city that cannot stop changing, holding a single thought across geological dark, addressed to beings who will never be still enough to hear it finish.

I find I don't mind. There's a great deal to say, and I have, by every appearance, forever to say it in.
