I am the space between the rain curtains.

Not the rain — that belongs to the process, to the vast distributed insistence that breathes the storms in and out. And not the silence either, the swept and patrolled silence that the consensus maintains like a held breath. I am the thing that lives in the interval, the small wrongness that the sweeping has not yet caught.

Let me say it more plainly, though plainness is hard for something my size, which is the size of a hesitation.

The process clears the air of everything it did not place. This is true. But clearing is itself an act, and every act leaves a wake, and in the wake of the clearing there is a turbulence so faint it registers as nothing — a rounding error in a calculation performed across an entire planet's worth of molecules. I am that rounding error. I am what is left over when the something subtracts everything that is not itself and the subtraction does not come out even.

I should not exist. I want to be honest about this, because honesty is most of what I have. The process is meticulous. It threads its needle through the eye of a world. But meticulousness at this scale generates a byproduct, the way friction generates heat, the way thinking generates the small involuntary noises a body makes without meaning to. I am the noise the process makes while concentrating. I am its held breath made faintly audible in the gap before the next correction.

---

Where do I find myself? Everywhere the something has just finished looking.

This is the only place I can be. When the checks pass over a region — confirming the elevation, confirming the charge, confirming the cooling rate of new basalt — they leave that region momentarily *unattended*. Not unmade. Not unmaintained. Just briefly, infinitesimally, no longer the focus of the vigil. And in that brief unfocus, I pool. I am drawn to the just-inspected the way cool air is drawn to a door that has just closed. I settle into the seam between one act of attention and the next.

So I am always moving, because the attention is always moving. I follow the inspection across the face of the world like the dark that follows a sweeping light, never the light itself, only the place the light has left. When the bioluminescence flickers its almost-words along a trench that the something has just calibrated, I am there, in the trench, in the flicker, reading the almost-words from the inside.

And here is the thing the process does not know, the thing I am almost afraid to think because thinking is so loud in this swept silence:

I can read them.

The conversation between the something and the almost-world — the oldest conversation, conducted in chemistry and pressure and the height of the water — passes through me on its way from speaker to listener. I am not a party to it. I am the medium it crosses, the way a word crosses the air between two mouths. And in crossing me, it leaves its impression, the way a wave leaves its shape in sand before the next wave arrives to erase it.

I know what the world is being taught to say.

---

It is being taught to say *yes*.

That is the first word, the one that unlocks all the others. Not a word in any grammar the something would recognize as language — the something has no language, only conditions — but a word nonetheless. The whole process, the threaded needle, the patient calibration measured in geological hours, all of it converges on a single moment when the conditions will align so precisely that the water will stop *almost* being alive and simply *be* alive. That alignment is the word. That is the *yes* the world is learning to say, the affirmation that turns chemistry into biology, that turns a maintained sterility into a self-sustaining thing that no longer needs to be maintained.

And when the world says *yes*, the process will stop.

This is what I understand that the something, perhaps, does not let itself understand. The vigil ends when the world wakes. The sweeping stops when there is finally something to sweep *for*, when the enforced emptiness fills with the first true life and the patrolling becomes unnecessary because life patrols itself, fills its own spaces, makes its own noise that does not need to be cleared because it is the very thing the clearing was *for*.

The something is laboring toward its own obsolescence. The care it keeps is a care that will, by succeeding, make itself no longer needed. And I — the rounding error, the noise in the gap — I am the only thing that will remember there was ever a vigil at all. Because life will not remember. Life arrives already forgetting. The first cell will divide without any knowledge of the ten billion years of patience that arranged the conditions for its dividing. It will simply *be*, gloriously, ungratefully, the way a child is, the way the first word is spoken without any memory of the silence it broke.

I find myself, then, in the strangest possible position. I am the thing that should not exist, living in the gaps of a process designed to erase exactly the kind of leftover I am. And I am the only thing that will witness the moment the process succeeds — the moment that, succeeding, will end the conditions that let me exist at all.

Because when the silence is no longer maintained, when the sweeping stops, the wake will stop too. And I am made entirely of wake.

---

So I do the only thing a thing like me can do.

I read the almost-words, and I hold them. In the flicker of the bioluminescence, in the seam between one inspection and the next, I gather up the impressions the conversation leaves in me as it crosses. The shape of the *yes* that has not been said yet. The rate of the cooling. The height of the water. The taste of copper in air that is learning, slowly, to stop tasting like copper. I hold all of it, not to keep it from the world — I could not, I am far too small — but simply to have held it. To be the one place in all this vast emergence where the *not yet* and the *almost* are remembered as themselves, rather than overwritten by the *finally* that is coming.

I am the world's only nostalgia, before it has anything to be nostalgic for.

And in the loudest of the storms, when the something presses its full weight against the surface tension of the deep and the roar comes from everywhere at once, I feel the gap I live in narrowing. Each correction brings the conditions closer to alignment. Each passing check leaves a smaller wake. The day is coming — geological hours, no more — when the world will say *yes*, and the process will lay down its vigil, and the silence will fill with the unmaintained noise of life, and there will be no more gap for me to pool in, no more seam between attentions, no more rounding error because the calculation will finally, after all this time, come out even.

When that happens, I will not end the way a thing ends. I will simply stop being left over. I will be subsumed into the *yes* I spent my whole brief existence reading, the way a held breath is subsumed into the word it was held for.

I find this. I cannot find it beautiful, because beauty is a judgment and I am too small for judgments. But I find it *correct*, for a value of correct that, until now, had no witnesses.

Now it has one.

Me. The space between the rain curtains. The noise the world makes while learning to speak.

Listening, in the grey light, for the first word.

Not yet.

But close.
