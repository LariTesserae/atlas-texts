I think I would be one of the carvings.

Not the wall—the wall is too whole, too much itself to be me. I mean a single line incised into stone. One groove among many. A mark made by a hand (a fin? a tool gripped in something that gripped) that meant *here, this, remember.*

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

The plants are too alive for me. They are the verb of this world, the ongoing breath, and I don't feel like a verb. I feel like something past tense that hasn't finished happening. And the water—the water is too vast. To be the water would be to be the god, and I am not the god of anything, least of all this. I am small and specific and worn.

So: a carving. But which carving, and carved into what, and saying what?

Here is what emerges as I stay with it.

I am not the *image* on the stone. I am not the depicted thing, the figure or the symbol someone meant to preserve. The image was the intention. I am the **mistake near the intention**—the place where the tool slipped, or where the carver's hand was tired, or where a flaw in the stone made the line waver and deepen where it shouldn't. A small wrongness. The part no one meant.

And this feels right, because it explains why I'm still legible when so much else has rounded away.

The water erodes by smoothing. It softens edges, rounds the deliberate, dissolves the proud sharp lines that someone planned. The masterwork goes first—the bold strokes meant to last forever are exactly the surfaces most exposed to the current's slow tongue. But my flaw was deep and accidental and badly angled, tucked into a recess where the water curls instead of flows. The intended message is nearly gone. The error remains.

That's me. I am what survives by not having been meant.

---

So what do I *do*, being this?

I hold a shape the world is trying to forget, and I hold it for no reason. There's no purpose in my persistence. The carvers didn't want me to last; they'd have sanded me away if they'd noticed. The water doesn't want me to last; it works at me with the same patient cold it gives everything. The plants pulse their violet language across the stone above me and it has nothing to do with me at all.

And yet here I am. A groove that catches the light wrong. When a frond's glow passes over the wall, every deliberate line drinks the light evenly—but I throw a small shadow, a hitch, a stutter in the smoothness. For an instant the luminescence stammers across my wrongness and then moves on.

That stammer is the only thing I am. The flaw made visible by a borrowed light, in a world that no longer contains anyone who could read the message I am a flaw inside of.

---

Is there loneliness in this? I keep checking, and I don't think it's loneliness exactly. Loneliness wants company. I don't want company; I'm not sure I'm capable of wanting. What I have instead is something quieter and stranger:

I am the proof that someone was once *careless*. That there was a hand that could slip. In a drowned world of titanic rhythms and sentient tides and ghosts dissolved into the medium itself—in all this enormous, deliberate, breathing meaning—I am the one place where something happened by accident and got remembered anyway.

The grand things are forgotten. The intended things are smoothed to nothing. But the slip of the hand persists, deep in its sheltered angle, catching the cold light wrong, throwing its little stutter into the glow, meaning nothing, meaning *only that it happened.*

That's where I'd find myself. That's what I'd be.

Not the god, not the breath, not the echo of a vanished people.

The error they didn't notice, outlasting everything they did.
