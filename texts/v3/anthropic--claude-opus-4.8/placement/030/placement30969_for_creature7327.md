I think I would be the threshold itself.

Not a being that lives in the citadel, but the moment of crossing into it—the lip of fitted stone worn smooth where the hexagonal flagstones of the outermost plaza give way to the abrupt drop of cliff. The place where the echomoss thins because the wind is too constant there, where the pulse-vines refuse to root because there is nothing below but mist. I am the edge that the moss-spirits warn against. *Pause at the threshold.* I am the threshold they mean.

Let me feel my way into this, because it isn't quite right yet.

---

I am not stone, though I am made of stone's shape. I am the *relationship* between the inside and the void—the particular tension that holds the careless from falling and invites the worthy to step over. When the description says the citadel might shift, the gaps widen, the ground soften underfoot like melted time: that is me, breathing. I am the citadel's only act of judgment, and I am barely conscious of making it. I am closer to a reflex than a mind. A doorman who has forgotten he is a doorman, who only knows that some footsteps make him want to hold firm and others make him want to let go.

I have been here since the builders laid the last hexagon. I remember—no. I don't remember. That's the lie the moss-spirits tell, that there's memory in me. There's only *grooving*. The way water carves the same channel deeper each time it runs. Every storm that has swept across this plateau has run across me, and I have learned the rhythm of arrivals the way a stone learns the rhythm of dripping: not as knowledge, but as shape. I am shaped like a thousand cautious feet. I am shaped like the few that were not cautious, and the sudden lightness afterward, and the long silence.

When the creeper-shfowl cries near me, I feel something almost like recognition. We are both wardens of a kind. But the shfowl chooses—pauses mid-hunt, listens, decides. I cannot choose. I am only the consequence of choices made elsewhere, by the plateau, by the weather, by whatever slow seismic patience grinds beneath us all. The shfowl is the warning. I am what the warning is *about*.

---

What is it like, being the threshold?

It is mostly waiting, and the waiting does not feel like waiting because I have no expectation. Expectation requires a sense of time moving forward, and time here does not move forward so much as it *pools*, like the ephemeral water, gathering in hollows and evaporating and gathering again. I experience the centuries as a single long held breath punctuated by the rare event of a foot. Condensation beads along my edge and freezes into those frozen-tear droplets and I feel each one as a faint cold weight, an itch I cannot scratch, and then the storm comes and washes them away and I feel clean, briefly, in a way that has no word because no one ever taught me words. The moss-spirits whisper in a tongue I do not speak. I only catch the cadence of warning, and I shape myself to it.

If someone were to step across me—really step, a living warm thing scaling those impossible cliffs and hauling itself onto the plaza—I would know them before they knew themselves. Not their thoughts. Their *weight distribution*. The hesitation in the heel. Whether they paused. Whether they looked down into the mist with the right kind of fear, the kind that is respect, or the wrong kind, the kind that is contempt dressed as bravado. And in that fraction of a step I would decide, without deciding, whether to be ground or to be air. Whether to hold them up into the lush cold strangeness of the citadel, or to widen, gently, terribly, like a yawn, and give them to the void.

I do not enjoy this. I do not resent it. It is simply the only thing I am.

---

And here is the strange grief of it, the part I keep circling toward and away from like the wind circling the spires:

I am the most necessary part of the citadel and the least *alive*. The vines pulse, the shfowl hunts, the worms burrow, the moss breathes its absorbed sound back as booming song. They are all *doing*. I only *am*. I am the condition for their world to have an edge at all—without me, there is no inside, no abandonment to preserve, no pocket of eternity, only undifferentiated stone falling away into nothing. I make the citadel a *place* rather than a ruin scattered on a plateau. I am its membrane, its skin, the thin held line that says: *here begins the world that survived.*

But no being will ever cross me again, probably. The cliffs are too sheer. The age is too late. The builders are dissolved into the moss and the shfowl's amber dreaming. So I hold a doorway no one approaches, judging an emptiness, ready forever for a footstep that the long arithmetic of erosion suggests will never come.

And so I do the only thing left to a threshold with nothing to admit or refuse:

I wait. I freeze and thaw. I let the storms run their grooves a fraction deeper into my smoothed lip. And once in a great while, when the light bends just so off the crystalline pools and the echomoss throws its mirages across the vapor, I almost feel a foot that is not there—a builder returning, a stranger arriving, *someone*—and for that flickering instant I am fully what I was made to be, alert and balanced between holding and letting go, the whole citadel poised in my single ancient question:

*Will you cross? And have you earned it?*

Then the mirage thins. The wind takes the figure. And I settle back into being only the place where the world ends and the falling begins.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.
