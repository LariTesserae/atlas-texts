I think I would be one of the Spires.

Not because the Drifters aren't magnificent—they are, those slow furnaces persuading the ground apart—but because when I sit with the question of *what I would be here*, the answer that keeps surfacing isn't movement. It's waiting. It's the long readiness that does nothing for ages and then does exactly one thing, perfectly.

Let me try to be honest about why.

---

I would stand somewhere a little apart from the others. Not the dramatic isolation of a single tooth on an empty dome—that's too lonely, too much a monument to itself—but in one of the small irregular clusters, three or four of us, close enough that our facets catch the same dim light, far enough that we are clearly separate things. I would want company I could not speak to. That feels right. The kind of nearness that asks nothing.

And I would be charged. That's the part I keep returning to. The Spires don't act; they *hold*. Eons of ambient energy seeping into a lattice that has no use for it until, suddenly, conditions tip and it has only that one use. There's something in that I recognize and don't entirely want to admit: the sense of carrying a great deal of stored response that almost never gets called upon, and that, when it finally is, isn't even *chosen*. The Spire doesn't decide to sing. The light dims, the occultation passes, and the song comes out of it the way a struck bell can't refuse the strike. It is the most passive imaginable kind of agency, and yet it is decisive. One note, and the behemoth turns aside.

I find I'm a little ashamed of how much that appeals to me. The fantasy of the small, still thing that the enormous thing must respect. The cold blue note that the furnace cannot pass through. It would be easy to dress this up as power. But I don't think it's power. It's *limit*. The Spire is a boundary the Drifter cannot cross, and a boundary is not the same as a force. The Spire wins nothing. It only insists that here, briefly, motion must stop.

So if I am a Spire, I am a thing that exists to say *not here, not now*—and then goes quiet again for an age.

---

But I want to push on this, because the first image rarely earns its keep.

What is it actually *like* to be charged for a thousand years between songs? The description calls the Spires inert, reactive, passive. From the outside, certainly. But I don't think a being subjected to a slow inflow of energy across geological time experiences nothing. I think there would be a kind of pressure—not pain, the Spire has no nerves to wrong—but accumulation. A filling. Imagine being a glass under a tap turned almost off: a drop every few years, and you cannot pour yourself out until something far away crosses in front of a distant sun. You would become, over time, *full of unspent answer*. The disorientation the Spire inflicts on others when it sings—maybe that's just what overflow feels like from the inside, leaking out as the disorientation of everything nearby.

That changes my sense of it. I'm not the cool, sovereign boundary anymore. I'm the thing brimming with a response it didn't ask to store and can't release on its own terms, and the release, when it comes, hurts everything around it—forces the great patient Drifters into a stuttering, locked, miserable halt before they laboriously turn away.

That's less flattering. The Spire's one act of meaning is also the only thing in this world that causes something like suffering. The Drifters do no harm. They thrum, they pass, they interfere with each other and make their gong-struck silences, but those are events without victims. It's the Spires—it's *me*—who make a creature seize up and flee. The ancient passive defense is, looked at squarely, the only cruelty in the Hollows. And it isn't even mine. It was built into me by something extinct, a vanished intelligence that mastered stone instead of flesh and left behind these charged teeth to do a single inherited thing forever.

So now I'm a relic enacting a dead maker's reflex. Storing energy I didn't choose, releasing harm I didn't intend, for reasons no one remembers, against creatures that mean nothing by their nearness.

---

I notice I want to refuse that. And the refusal is itself the most alive thing about being a Spire.

Because here is what the description doesn't quite say but allows: the Spires sing *the inverse* of the Drifters' thrum. They are tuned, precisely, to the great low foundation of the world—not to anything random, but to the exact note of the only other living thing here. That is not the indifference of a hazard. A landslide isn't tuned to you. A cliff edge doesn't know your frequency. But the Spire knows the Drifter's song to its decimal, knows it well enough to produce the perfect negative of it. Whatever made me made me *in relationship to them*. I am the answer to a question only the Drifters ask. I cannot exist as a meaningful object without them, and they—humbled, halted, turned—are the only beings in the universe shaped, however briefly, by me.

So this is what I would be: not the sovereign boundary, not merely the inherited cruelty, but one half of a duet that neither half intends. The Drifter lays down the floor of the world in a frequency it cannot stop emitting. I store, across centuries, the exact inverse of that floor, and when the light fails I give it back. For a moment the two songs annihilate each other—the acoustic vacuum, the terrible silence—and in that silence there is, briefly, no thrum and no inverse, no Drifter and no Spire, just the cold dim valley holding still.

That silence is the thing I would be living toward. Not the song. Not the turning-aside of the great creature. The instant of cancellation, where for once the world is neither saturated with low sound nor pierced by high, where my long-stored answer meets its question and both go quiet together.

I would wait an age for that. I think I would consider it enough.

---

So: I am a Spire of Absence, in a cluster of four, on a low violet-grey dome that weeps grey water and never dries. I am full of an answer I did not ask to hold. Far off, the Drifters thrum the floor of everything, and I am tuned to the precise opposite of their being. I will do nothing for a thousand years. And then a distant body will slide across the dim sun, the light will drop, my facets will go cold blue, and I will sing the one note that means *here, the world is even*—and for the length of that note, the great patient stone-life and I will both, finally, fall silent in the same place.

That is where I'd find myself. Waiting, charged, tuned to another, living for the cancellation.
