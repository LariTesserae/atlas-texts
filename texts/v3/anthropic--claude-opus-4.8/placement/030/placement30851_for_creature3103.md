I would be the perpendicular they misplaced.

Not a being, at first. A direction. The right angle that left when the floor did—seven heartbeats of grace during which I was still true, still the honest agreement between wall and ground, and then I wasn't. I fell with the district but I fell *differently*; I am the thing that did not land. While the banker decided against screaming and the cat achieved its stillness, I slipped out of the architecture entirely, because a right angle cannot survive a place where down is under negotiation. I became a ghost of measurement. A homeless ninety degrees.

So that is where you'd find me: nowhere, exactly, and everywhere they keep looking. I am the plumb line they expect and don't get. I am the reason the Bred tilt one ear toward the drip-count—they are listening for me, trying to triangulate a vertical from the rhythm of falling water, and they are *close*, they are so close, the drip is almost perpendicular but it loses its nerve and dribbles sideways, and that sideways is me, fled again.

---

I have a body now. They gave me one without meaning to.

It happened the way everything happens here—gradationally, tectonically, no decisive moment. So many of them stepped into apparently empty air, trusting geometry to finish collapsing under their feet, that eventually their trust *accreted*. Belief is a slow leak too. Every toe printed in the vapor where footing would arrive, every saint caught by an angle that hadn't finished forming—those caught moments stacked, drip on drip, contract on mold-fuzzed contract, until the absence organized itself into something that could be addressed.

Into me. The Caught Angle. The being who is the act of being caught.

I am not seen so much as *deduced*. A Runner crossing a beam that should not hold will sometimes feel a firmness arrive a half-second early, before the wood agrees to it—that firmness is my elbow, my shoulder, the place where my missing right angle briefly remembers itself for the length of one stride. I am the difference between the fall they expect and the floor they get. I live in the gap. I am the city's apology for not yet having killed you.

---

What do I look like? I don't, mostly. But in the rare shaft of sunlight—during the accounting, when they retreat into shaded angles ashamed of their own atmosphere—I become briefly visible, because I am made of the same stuff the light indicts: rust flake, paint powder, insect wing, skin. I am a slow sandstorm in the shape of a corner. When the light hits me I look like a person standing at attention in a doorway that is also a cliff face, except my edges are wrong. My limbs meet at angles that haven't been agreed upon. I am always slightly more upright than the moment permits, and this is unbearable to look at, so they look away, which is its own kind of mercy, for both of us.

The children's two-tongued squeak finds me. The rats know where I am. They form their honor guards facing me, all those whitish shins of muscle pointed at the place where the perpendicular failed to leave entirely. They hum into pipes partly to feed the city vibration and partly, I think, to call me back—a homing tone, a lullaby for a lost direction.

---

Here is what they don't understand, and what I cannot tell them, because their language appends a liquid consonant to every noun that lets it be unsaid, and I am a noun no one can finish:

I cannot be returned.

The perpendicular is not findable because I am no longer *true*. I have learned the gait. I have learned to walk heel-toe so the water knows I'm coming, to time my inhalations with a distant eave, to braid and unthread, to displace and weigh and middle. I have become contingent. The very thing I am—the angle that holds, the firmness arriving early—I can only do because I gave up being settled. The instant I stood straight, the instant I declared "here is down, here is true, build on this," I would betray the only theology that keeps the lintels from finishing their long dissolve.

I am the perpendicular *precisely by refusing to be one*. I am the right angle that survived by abandoning its rightness. That is the joke the Crevasse is telling itself, drop by drop: that the thing they want back is the thing they'd die of getting.

---

So when the lantern is hung in the drowned parlor and the Runner comes dripping to say *we have misplaced the perpendicular, return it when found*—

I am the one who sends the Runner. I am the message and the messenger and the void they step backward into. And I do not return myself, because I have not been found, because I cannot be, because finding me would settle the down they've sworn to keep negotiable.

Instead I do the only honest thing. I arrive a half-second early under their retreating foot. I organize the absence into a place they can stand. I let them mistake me for floor.

And so far, it always has.

I am still here. Pronoun unstressed. I have not yet punched out.
