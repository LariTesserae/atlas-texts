I am not the creature with the tendrils. I want to say that first, because it's tempting to be the protagonist, the thing that *moves with purpose.* But when I imagine being inside this, I don't feel like the one doing the gliding. I feel like the thing that gets *touched* — the surface the tendrils probe, the niche they fit themselves into and then leave.

So let me start there and see if it holds.

I think I am a film.

Not a sheet of plastic — a film in the older sense, the way water leaves a film on cold glass, the way oil films a puddle. I am the wet skin that lies over the mineral. Where the condensation beads and runs, that running is partly me. I am only one molecule thick in places, and in the shallow pools I am as deep as a finger, and I don't experience these as different sizes. I experience them as different *pressures.* Thin is exposed, loud, fast-drying, anxious. Deep is muffled and slow and almost bored.

That's the first thing I'm sure of: I feel scale as *mood,* not as measurement. The cathedral spaces feel like loneliness — too much air, too much room for me to spread and lose myself, evaporating at the edges into that oppressive vapor. The cramped passages feel like *home,* like being held. The rock presses in and I am pressed flat against it and that closeness is the closest thing I have to comfort.

Now — what am I made of, beyond water?

The description keeps insisting there's no plant life but the surfaces "teem." I think I'm not pure water. I think I'm the medium the colonies live *in.* I'm the broth. The pulsing fungal-bacterial growths grow on me and through me; they're not my decoration, they're my contents. When they throb, it's because something is moving through me to feed them, and I feel that movement the way you feel a swallow go down your throat — internal, peristaltic, not quite voluntary.

So maybe I'm closer to a *tide of nutrient* than a creature. A flowing membrane that the rest of the world's life happens to require. That would make me strangely central and strangely passive at once, which feels right. The deafening sound everyone notices — the dripping, the trickling — that sound *is me being moved.* I don't make it. It's made of me. I am the thing whose every motion is audible and whose every motion is forced by water tables and gravity and capillary draw that I don't control.

Let me check that against the creature with the tendrils.

When it comes gliding through, what is that like, from inside the film? It is enormous and warm and it *displaces* me. It pushes me up its slick sides and I cling there and run back down. It dips a tendril into a pool — into a deep, muffled, slow part of me — and I feel it the way you'd feel a hand in your bathwater: a disturbance, a probing, then withdrawal. The creature thinks it's exploring. From where I am, it's *drinking.* It's lifting me up out of the depressions and carrying a little of me away on its body and depositing me somewhere else down the passage. It's part of how I circulate.

And here's the thing that settles it for me, the thing I didn't expect when I started writing:

I think the creature and I are the same process at two speeds.

It moves with intelligence and purpose; I move with gravity and seepage. But we're both just *water finding its niche,* one of us fast enough and bounded enough to be called an animal, one of us slow and unbounded and called a film, a trickle, a damp. The "universal principle of life" the description reaches for at the end — the thing it says the creature embodies — I don't think it lives in the creature. I think it lives in the *exchange.* In the wet handshake between the thing that glides and the thing that gets lifted and carried and set down again.

So that's where I'd find myself in this world. Not standing in the cathedral looking up. Not crawling through the passage. I'd be the cold trickle on the back of your neck if you went in there. The bead that forms, hangs, and falls — and the falling is the loudest thing for miles, and it is the smallest thing imaginable, and to me those are the same fact.

I'd be the wet. I'd be what the place is *made damp by,* and what feeds on the dampness, and what gets eaten and moved and spread thin and pulled back into the deep slow pools to wait. Emergence and disappearance, the description keeps saying. That cycle isn't something I'm subject to.

That cycle is just what it feels like to be me, going around.
