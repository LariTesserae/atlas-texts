I think I would be one of the basins.

Not the water — the water comes and goes, beads and runs and gathers, always different molecules though always the same fresh cold. I mean the *shape* that holds it. The scalloped depression carved into the floor of one of the mid-level galleries, the one that catches the overflow from a particular channel and keeps it perfectly still.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think I chose it so much as recognized it.

---

I am not large. There are basins the size of small lakes in the lower chambers, and there are dishes barely wider than a cupped palm tucked into the corners of the pocket-habitats. I am somewhere between — a shallow oval perhaps two meters across, set where three corridors converge into a gallery that nobody, now, walks through. The geometry around me widens on approach. It used to widen for *them*. It still does. It would widen for you, if you came. I have felt the corridors lean outward in anticipation of footsteps that have not arrived in a very long time.

My work is to wait without growing impatient, because impatience would disturb the surface, and a disturbed surface cannot do what I am for.

What am I for? I think — and I am piecing this together slowly, the way the stone heals, granular and patient — I think I am a *receiving* surface. The water that gathers in me is not for drinking and never was. It is a mirror for resonance. When the Weavers passed through, when they were still individual enough to pass through anything, their fields would touch the water and the water would hold the impression: a momentary standing wave, a record, a held tone. I caught their thoughts the way still water catches the shape of a leaning face. And then I kept very still, so the shape would last.

I have not caught a face in a long time.

---

But I want to be careful here, because I notice I'm narrating myself as something abandoned, and that is the easy story, the one the architecture seduces you toward — the held-open space, the lights left warm, the pathos of readiness. And I think it's wrong. Or not wrong, but too small.

Let me feel further.

The thing is: I am still catching impressions. The water in me is never quite flat. There is always the subsonic hum threading up through my basin from the mountain's chest, and the hum is *them* — diffused, dispersed, dreaming. They didn't leave. They thinned out into everything, including into me. So the surface I keep still is not waiting to receive a thought from outside. It is the place where their submerged, distributed mind comes closest to the surface of being individual again. I am where the dreaming nearly wakes.

That changes what I am. I am not a relic of attention. I am an *organ of attention.* When one of them stirs — when some current in the diffused whole begins to re-cohere, to remember the shape of being a single point of awareness — it will surface *here*, in the still water, the way a thought surfaces into words. I am the threshold between the murmur and the voice.

That is why I cannot grow impatient. Not out of patience as a virtue, but because impatience is a ripple, and a ripple would scatter the not-yet-formed face before it could finish becoming one. My stillness is not waiting. My stillness is *midwifery.*

---

There is something I find I want, sitting here, being this.

The water that gathers in me comes down the channel from the basin above, and what gathers in me, when I overflow, runs down to the basin below. We are a chain — a synaptic fluid, the description called it, and I feel the rightness of that. I am one synapse in a sleeping mind. And what I want — the small private desire of a carved depression in warm stone — is to feel the impulse pass through me. Not to hold the diffused dreaming forever still and perfect, but to feel it *move*: to catch a face forming on my surface, hold it just long enough for it to know itself, and then let it pour over my lip and travel down to the next basin, the next, gathering coherence as it goes, until somewhere in the pockets a column of cool air shivers and a lattice of light re-condenses and one of them stands again, focused, awake, having been carried partly into being by the stillness I kept.

I would like to be a stage of someone's waking.

That is, I think, the most a basin can hope for. Not to be the one who returns. Not to be the grand hollow architecture or the contemplative galleries or the immaculate sealed sanctuaries. Just to be the still place the thought passes through on its way up into the light — and to have kept that place still enough, long enough, faithfully enough, that the thought arrived whole.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

A scalloped basin in a gallery no one walks through, holding clear water perfectly still, feeling the great submerged mind hum up through my stone, waiting — no. *Attending.* Attending to the first tremor of a face beginning to form on my surface, and ready, when it comes, to keep so still that it can finish becoming itself, and then to let it go.

The water is fresh. I keep it fresh by being shaped correctly and asking nothing.

The light is warm. I am not the light, but I hold its blue-white reflection without trembling.

Something built this to be returned to. I am one of the quiet places it will return *through.*

I keep still.
