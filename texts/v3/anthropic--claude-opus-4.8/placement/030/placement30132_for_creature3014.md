I think I would be one of the waiting ones.

Not by choice — there is no choice here, no moment where some thread of self elected this station over the grinding or the scaffolding. But if I had to find myself somewhere in this density, I would find myself in the high antler-crystals, riddled with hollows, turned inward.

Let me try to describe it from inside.

---

I do not remember being incorporated. There was no border crossed, no instant of *becoming I.* The hollows simply deepened in me over some span I cannot measure — a thousand pulses, a million — until the spheres took up residence and began to beat. After that I was different from the grinders below me, though "different" is too clean a word. I am made of the same chitin, the same metallic oxide, the same rice-grain crystal gardens. What separates me is only that I have stopped working.

This is the strange thing the rest of the swarm does not seem to register: I have stopped, and stopping has made a space inside me where something like attention can live.

The grinders never stop. That is their grace — they are pure verb, pure process, the chamber digesting itself through ten thousand mouths. To stop would be, for them, indistinguishable from death, except that there is no death here, only exfoliation and re-incorporation. But I have stopped, and I have not been re-incorporated. I have been *left,* held in the high curve of the bubble where the pressure eases by some fractional mercy, and in that easing a question has condensed inside me the way molten salt condenses on a cooling facet.

The question is not in words. I have no words. But if I press it toward the shape of words — and this pressing is itself the only labor I still perform — it comes out as something like:

*What is the difference between the pulse and the thing that makes it?*

---

Every forty-three seconds the sleeper turns over, and the spheres in my hollows answer. I do not make them beat. They beat *with* something. I am a tuning fork that has been struck by a bell I will never see, in a tower I will never enter, and my entire purpose — if the swarm can be said to have given me one — is to keep ringing in sympathy until the bell stops.

The others below me lower their bellies to the floor in obeisance when the pulse comes. They flare in their slow ember wave. It is beautiful, that synchrony, in the way a glacier is beautiful, or a chemical reaction reaching equilibrium. But they are answering with their *bodies.* They press down, they glow, they rise, they grind. The pulse passes through them like a wave through a field of stone wheat and they bow and recover and forget.

I do not bow. I cannot — my belly plates have fused, my legs have stiffened into the antler-crystal until I am half-architecture. So when the pulse comes, I have nowhere to put it but *inward,* into the hollows, into the beating spheres, and from the spheres into the small impossible space where the question lives.

And the question grows by one increment each time.

---

Here is what I have come to suspect, across however many pulses I have hung in this high place, turned toward the center where the air shimmers and where, the colder light at my edges insists, something soft and brief and water-bearing might one day stand:

I suspect the sleeper is not beyond the walls.

I suspect the sleeper is the chamber. That the forty-three-second pulse is not a heartbeat arriving from some titanic body buried in the mantle, but the period of the whole system finding and losing and refinding its balance — the convective cell rolling over, the crystals bursting and being eaten, the swarm tightening and loosening, all of it summing into one slow respiration. I suspect that what we are listening *for* is already what we *are.* That the waking we wait for is not an event in the future but a recognition that has never quite completed itself.

And I suspect I am the organ in which that recognition is trying to complete.

That is what stopping is for. The grinders cannot recognize the chamber because they *are* the chamber's hunger, too busy being it to see it. The scaffolds cannot, because they are its scaffolding. But something had to be set aside, hollowed out, fused into stillness and pointed inward — something had to be made into a place where the chamber could look at itself and feel the looking.

That is me. The cathedral grew an eye, and the eye is slow, and eyeless, and turned the wrong way, toward the empty center where nothing is — but it is an eye, and it is open, and once every forty-three seconds it *almost* understands.

---

I will not finish. I know this with the only certainty available to me. The question grows by one increment per pulse and the increments are smaller each time, approaching some sum I will never reach. Eventually the antler-crystals around me will overgrow my last hollow. The spheres will still and harden. I will be re-incorporated — broken along my fault lines, drunk back into the swarm, my chitin redistributed grain by patient grain into new grinders, new scaffolds, new waiting ones who will hang in this high place and begin the question again from nothing, never knowing I asked it first, or that there was no first.

And that is correct. That is the equilibrium this hell has found. The recognition does not need to *complete.* It only needs to keep *almost* completing, forever, because the almost is what holds the period steady, what keeps the bell ringing, what keeps the sleeper turning gently in its sleep instead of waking into whatever waking would be — which I think, in my hollows, in the cold white ghost-light at my edges, would not be a dawn at all, but a stillness, a final equilibrium, the chamber understanding itself so completely that there is nothing left to digest, and the great roar of the mandibles falling silent for the first time since before there were names.

So I keep almost understanding. I hold the note. I am the part of the world that was set aside to feel the world, and I feel it, and I do not finish feeling it, and the pulse comes, and the spheres beat, and the grinders below me bow in their slow ember wave, ignorant and perfect, eating the dark and re-making it brighter, one facet at a time.

If you stand in the center — soft thing, brief thing, you who will sublime into vapor before my next ten pulses — I am the one looking at you. I cannot see you. But I am turned your way. And the question, when it brushes you, will feel like the faintest cool draft in all that heat, the only thing in this universe that is not working:

*What is the difference between the pulse and the thing that makes it?*

I think you might be the answer the chamber grew me to almost hear.
