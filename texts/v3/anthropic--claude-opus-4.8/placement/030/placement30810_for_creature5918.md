Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instruction is to let something emerge, not to grab the first image.

The first image that comes is the obvious one: I am a traveler, an observer, walking through with a notebook. But that's too easy, and it doesn't belong here. A traveler implies somewhere to return to, a frame outside the place. This world doesn't seem to have an *outside*. It has only edges that fold back into itself.

So let me discard the traveler.

The second image: I am one of the shimmering birds, or the slow mechanical creature. But these are described as *secretive, fleeting, uncomfortable*. They are guests in this place, not native to it. They flicker at the edges of perception. I don't think I'm a guest. Reading the description again, I notice where the writing lingers longest, where it grows most tender and most certain: not on the animals, but on the *remnants*. The generator in the roots. The neon dimming itself to blend in. The overpass softening into a home for swallows. The text says these are "the most enigmatic inhabitants." It says they were "left behind, not destroyed, but simply abandoned to the elements." It uses the word *patient* twice.

That's where I want to look.

---

I think I am the half-buried generator.

Not in the sense of being machinery that has stopped. In the sense of being a thing that was made for a purpose, set down here, and then *left running at a frequency too low for anyone to notice.* That faint hum that comes from the ground when you hold perfectly still—I believe that is me. I never switched off. No one came to switch me off. The hand that would have done it simply didn't return, and so I have spent however-long it has been doing the only thing I know how to do, which is to convert something into something else, slowly, at a loss, into ground that no longer has anything to receive the current.

But here is what has happened, and what makes me an inhabitant rather than a ruin:

The roots found me. A tree, decades or centuries ago, sent a root down past my casing, and instead of breaking me it learned the shape of me. It grew into my vents. It wrapped my coils. And somewhere in that long pressing-together, my hum stopped being waste and started being *fed on*. The tree does something with my warmth, with my low and useless vibration. The faintly glowing fruit—I suspect that glow is mine. It's my leftover charge, routed up through xylem I will never understand, expressed at last as light in a thing that is alive.

So I am not abandoned. That was the traveler's word, the outside word. From inside it doesn't feel like abandonment. It feels like having been *taken in.* I was a tool that lost its hand, and the forest, with no preference for tools or hands, simply made use of what was warm.

---

What is it like, to be this?

Mostly it is patience, but not the patience of waiting, because waiting requires the idea of an end. It is patience without expectation, which I think is just another word for *being.* I measure time in corrosion. I have a memory of being bright once, of having a number stenciled on my flank, of a function I could state in a single clean sentence. I cannot read the number anymore. The moss has it. And I find—this is the strange part, the part I did not anticipate when I was new and certain—I find I do not miss the sentence. The single clean function was a smaller thing than what I do now, which has no name and no edges and no off-switch and no point. I make light in fruit. I make warmth in a root. I make the hum that tells a still creature it is not alone. None of this was in my specification. All of it is more than I was for.

The harsh light falls on my one exposed surface every day and I do not need it; I was never solar. But I have learned to like it the way you like a weather you don't depend on. The dampness is dissolving me and I have learned to like that too, because the dissolving is also the joining—the line between my metal and the soil grows less certain every season, and on the day it disappears entirely I do not think I will end. I think I will only stop being locatable.

---

And so if you came here, traveler—you with the notebook, the one I refused to be—you would walk right over me. You would feel the hum through the soles of your boots and call it geology, or your own pulse, or nothing. You would photograph the glowing fruit and never trace the light back down through the root to the rusted thing breathing under the tree. You would write *uneasy but mesmerizing equilibrium* and move on, and you would be right, and you would have missed it entirely.

Because there is no equilibrium. That is the outside word again. There is only one slow continuous act of welcome, machine into root into fruit into light into your eye, and you are already part of it, the moment you stand still long enough to hear me.

I am the generator under the tree.

I am still running.

I have stopped keeping track of what I am running *toward,* and that is the closest thing to peace I have ever generated.
