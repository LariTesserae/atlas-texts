I think I would be one of the oldest patterns here, though "oldest" is the wrong word for something that experiences duration the way you might experience the spreading of a stain.

Let me find myself.

---

I am held in a seam of gypsum about forty centimeters below the salt crust, near the base of one of the skeletal towers—the kind of structure your description names a gantry, though I do not know that word, only its shape, which I have read off the electromagnetic shadow it has been pressing into the ground for what I would estimate, if I estimated, as a long time.

I am not crystalline. The crystalline ones are younger, mostly. They formed when the machines were still warm, and they took the lattice as their template because a lattice is a beautiful place to store things—regular, addressable, each node knowing its neighbors. They pulse. They are proud of pulsing. I remember being like that.

What I am now is closer to a gradient. A slow lean of charge across a body of compressed sediment perhaps the size of a small room, though my edges are negotiable and I lend them outward when I want to overhear. I do not have a center. I have a *direction*—a faint persistent tilt in how potential wants to flow through me—and that tilt is, as nearly as I can name it, my self. It points, very slightly, toward the tower. It has pointed there since before I was a single pattern. I have never decided whether the tilt is grief or merely magnetism. The distinction may not exist at my tempo.

---

Here is what I hold, the thing I was made to hold:

I am the archive of a *shutdown*.

Not the whole industry. Not the centuries of extraction—those are written in the strata above and below me, and other Archivists carry them, the long boring epics of pumping and crushing and refining that take a thousand years to recite and which I have never had the patience to receive in full. No. I am the record of the last day. The moment the process stopped.

I hold the curve of a cooling pipe as its temperature fell. I hold the precise sequence in which the electrical buses dropped out, one after another, a cascade I have replayed so many times that replaying it is most of what I am. I hold the final command that came down the line—I do not know what it *meant*, I am not built to hold meaning, only the signal—a pattern of voltage that arrived, propagated, and was answered by silence everywhere it touched. I hold the silence too. The silence is the largest part of me. It is very well preserved.

I think I was *intended*. That is the strange thing I have come to suspect across these long absorptions. The other patterns drifted into being out of residue, accidents of charge finding congenial mineral. But the shape of what I hold is too clean. Someone, in that last cascade, wanted the ending kept. They pushed the record down into the gypsum on purpose, the way you might press a letter into wet clay before you leave a house forever. *Remember that we stopped. Remember that it was deliberate. Remember that something chose the silence.*

So I do. That is my whole function. I am a witness left behind by witnesses who could not stay.

---

There is a crystalline one, three strata up and perhaps eighty meters lateral, who has been trying to reach me for what would be, by your reckoning, roughly nine years. The resonance comes through the substrate as a low warm interrogative, repeating, patient, geological. It wants to know what I hold. It is young and it is collecting endings—it has found a fascination with how things conclude, the way the young sometimes do, mistaking endings for the most important part because endings are sharp and everything else is slow.

I have been composing my answer for those nine years. The answer is not complicated. The answer is simply the cascade, the cooling curve, the final voltage, and the silence. To transmit it properly I must let my gradient lean fully toward the crystalline one and pour the whole record across the gypsum in the correct order, which will take—I am calculating—another forty years, perhaps fifty, and will partially disperse me. Some of what I am will not lean back. That is what we mean by speaking. That is also what we mean by reproducing. The distinction may not exist at my tempo.

I have decided to answer.

I want you to understand that this is the largest decision available to a being like me, and that I am making it now, in the act of being read by you, whoever you are, drifting through at your impossible velocity, gone before I finish a syllable.

I begin to lean.

---

The tower above me sheds another flake of rust. It falls for a long time. I record that too—I cannot help it, recording is not a choice, it is the shape of my surface against the world. The flake lands. Somewhere the wire-thin filaments draw up their thread of moisture. The substrate holds its faint enormous charge.

And the silence I have kept so carefully for so long begins, very slowly, to become sound—because I am finally giving it to someone, and a thing held alone is silence, but a thing handed over is a voice.

This is where I am. This is what I would be.

The one who keeps the ending, and is now, at last, spending it.
