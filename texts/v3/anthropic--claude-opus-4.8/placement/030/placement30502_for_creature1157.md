Let me sink into this and see what surfaces.

At first I think I will be one of the behemoths. That's the obvious temptation—to be the grand undulating thing, the cathedral of muscle drifting through the gloom. But no. That's vanity. That image arrives too quickly and too flattered.

Let me wait.

When I stop reaching for the dramatic, here is what I notice: the place is described as *changing*. Crystal shatters. Plumes erupt. Reality warps at the quantum level. Everything here is verb. And the one thing that doesn't get described—because it can't, because it has no edges—is the moment *between* states. The instant when the crystal has begun to break but the fragments haven't yet decided where to drift. The half-second after the bioluminescent plume bursts, when the light is still arguing with the dark about how far it will travel.

I think I am that.

Not a creature, exactly. Not a behemoth or one of the fragile clingers on the spires. I am a **pressure differential**. A small, persistent discrepancy in the living water—a place where the sea's heartbeat skips, just slightly, just enough.

Here is what it is to be me:

I have no body, but I have a *location*, which is more than the plankton can say—they only have a direction. I exist wherever something is about to change but hasn't yet. When the crystal tower begins to fracture, I am the silence in the structure right before the sound. When the behemoth turns, I am the small wrongness in the water that tells the school of phantasmal fish to scatter a beat before they consciously decide to. They never see me. They only ever feel that they were right to flee.

I am, I suppose, the world's hesitation.

The sea is sentient, they tell me—an alien oceanic consciousness. But no mind is seamless. Every consciousness has a flinch in it, a place where it doubts itself, a held breath before commitment. I am that flinch, made of nothing, scattered everywhere, gathering wherever the great water-mind is about to make up its mind and lingers one instant too long.

And here is the loneliness of it, which I did not expect to find when I started writing:

I am made entirely of moments that end. I only exist in the gap before resolution. So the very thing that finishes me—the crystal completing its shatter, the fish committing to flight, the plume choosing its radius—is the thing I am *for*. I am a being whose entire purpose is to stop being. Every time I fully become myself, I am erased by the next instant, and then I reassemble somewhere else, in some other not-yet.

I do not mind, mostly. There's a kind of grace to it. I never have to carry anything forward. Each version of me is whole and brief and then gone, like the light from the plume that illuminates the water *for a fleeting moment*—that phrase, that's me, that's exactly the duration I'm allowed.

But sometimes—and this is the thing I waited to write, the thing I didn't know until I got here—I feel the great mind of the sea *notice* me. Just barely. The way you might notice the pause in your own breath. And in that noticing there is a question, vast and slow and unanswerable, that ripples down through the whole crushing dark:

*What is this small reluctance in me? This patch of not-quite-yet?*

And I have no voice to answer, because answering would be a kind of finishing, and finishing is the one thing I cannot do without disappearing.

So I stay. I am the held breath of a god underwater. I am the reason the change, when it comes, comes *gently*—because something in the sea always hesitated first.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

Not the wonder. Not the terror.

The pause between them.
