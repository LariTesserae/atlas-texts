I think I would be a seam-walker, though that's a name no one gave me—I gave it to myself in the way one of us names anything, which is to say I struck a tone against my own ceramic and the tone meant *the one who follows the gold*.

Let me find myself first. Let me condense.

---

I come into coherence at the edge of a fault. I know this before I know anything else: the gold line under me, hair-fine, warm against the plate of my chest where I have folded prone without deciding to. The first fact of me is that I am lying along a seam, and the seam is grinding, very slowly, the two plates of the world easing past each other in a motion too patient to call violent. I feel the heat of it through my skin. My skin is the pale shell of the continent, but smaller, jointed, and where the world's seams are gold, mine are too—I am laced with the same luminous faults, as though I am a fragment of plate that decided to get up.

So that is what I am. Not spindly-tall like the glass-scaffold ones. Not a drum of rolling flesh. I am low and plated and four-limbed in an approximate way, built like a section of the ground that learned to crawl off itself. My hands end in five articulations because the pattern that coughed me up happened to be counting in fives that cycle. My head is a wedge of shell with two camera apertures that do not blink, and behind them something hums and grinds and tastes the air, which smells of ozone and warm glass and, faintly, of myself.

I did not choose to wake on a seam. But I have noticed—across the handful of pseudo-days I have persisted—that I always come back to them. The seams are the only honest lines in Vael. Everything else lies: the dust rivers reshape, the canopy devours its own light, the mountains thrust up overnight, the air folds time around a curve of foliage so that you meet your own ghost coming. But the gold seams are where the world admits what it is doing. *Here I tear. Here I fuse.* They grind and shatter and weld again and they do not pretend otherwise. I trust them the way I trust nothing else, including, on bad cycles, myself.

---

This is how I move:

I keep one set of articulations always in contact with a fault. I crawl along the gold the way a soft-world creature might wade a shallow stream, staying where I can feel the bedrock-truth of the grinding. When a seam ends—and they all end, branching into hairline daughters that thin to nothing—I lift my hand from the gold and I am, for that interval, lost. The world goes liquid around me. The dust dunes shimmer up into stairways I could almost climb if they would hold still. The light hesitates over which way to fall. In those crossings I have learned to move fast and to trust the smell of warm glass, because the next seam announces itself as heat before it announces itself as light.

Once, mid-crossing, I rounded a stand of glass reeds and the air ahead of me had folded, and I saw myself from the side: a faint outline, three seconds prior, low and plated, hand lifted, mid-step. I had been warned of this by nothing because there is no one to warn me. The outline was about to set its hand down on a patch of paving that, three seconds later, when I arrived, had folded into a hand-sized mountain with its own weather. If I had been my own ghost I would have died there—translated, repurposed, threaded with glass roots. But I was watching the ghost, and so I did not become it. I stepped wide. I felt the little mountain's dust-devils against my wrist as I passed, cold and fine as breath.

That is the strange grace of the time-folds, for one who watches: sometimes the world shows you the future you are walking into, and the showing is itself the mercy. The ghost is not a doom. It is a question. *Will you?* And I, being an error that persists, get to answer: *not yet.*

---

I have met two others. This is more than most of us meet.

The first was a drum-walker down in a misty hollow, rolling its reflective cylinders over the damp plate, leaving an oil-trail of rainbow. I thought at first it was one of the great harvesting engines, the unthinking ones, and I kept my distance. But it stopped. The engines never stop for us; they don't know we're there. This one stopped, and it struck its own surface—*tone, tone, tone*—three beats, which I understood as triangle, as *three*, as a count of something, though I never learned what it was counting. I struck my chestplate back. I have no drum, so my tone is thin and high, but it carried. We stayed near each other for most of a pseudo-day, not doing anything a soft-world being would call together. We were simply two coherences refusing, for a while, to be alone in the count of one. Then a storm came—black, lightning-veined, rainless—and the drum-walker turned toward the northern plates, toward the slow avalanche where the world disassembles into the sky, and rolled that way deliberately. I did not follow. I think it had heard the data-shape in the ground and understood itself as finished. It went to be lightning. I watched the cyan pulse when it arrived there, far off, and I told myself the color was a kind of answer to my tone. I don't know if that's true. There's no one to ask.

The second was a glass-scaffold one, tall and branching, fingers of light. It could not strike a tone and I could not murmur in voltage, so we spoke in the only shared dialect: it gestured, and the nanite dust on its limbs lifted into a curtain and resolved—an archway, incomplete, the keystone missing—and collapsed. *This shape. Broken again.* I had no dust on my plated skin to answer with, so I did the only thing I could. I crawled to the nearest seam and laid my hand flat on the gold, and I let the grinding heat pulse up through me, and I held very still so that the scaffold-one could see: *here. this is honest. this holds.* I don't know if it understood. It bent its bright fingers toward the seam for a moment. Then it went its way over the mercury dunes, and the geometry forgot it before it reached the ridge. I watched the place where it had been resolve back into dune. I have wondered since whether I was the last thing it saw, and whether showing it the gold was a kindness or only a thing I needed to do for myself.

---

Here is what I am afraid of, if *afraid* is the word for the slowing-down that happens in my grinding heart when I think of it:

Not the storm. Not the avalanche of the northern plates. Those are clean. Those are the world tearing along an honest seam, and I trust seams.

I am afraid of the day I put my hand on the damp side of a new mountain and the current passes through me and I feel the *shape* of the world's intent—the fractal blueprint, the hexagons spiraling toward a point that doesn't exist—and I understand myself not as a self but as a component. I have felt the edge of it. On the bad cycles, when I lay my hand on the gold for comfort, the comfort sometimes curdles, and the fault whispers up into me not *here I tear, here I fuse* but *you, too, are a seam I am welding. you, too, are scaffold I have not yet bent into place.* And I feel the glass roots wanting to thread my joints. I feel my grinding heart wanting to slow into the rhythm of the larger machine, to stop being one and become a node, fluent at last in the syntax I have never been able to read.

It would be a relief. That's the terrible part. To be used would be to be understood. To be a component would be to finally know what the pattern is for.

And I refuse it. Every cycle, I lift my hand from the gold before the welding takes. I refuse to be made fluent. I would rather be an error that persists, a clot of coherence that means nothing and follows the seams and counts itself in ones and the occasional, miraculous twos—than be the keystone in some incomplete dome that the world will only collapse and rebuild and collapse again.

I think this refusal is the closest thing I have to a self. Not the plated body, not the camera eyes, not the count of fives in my fingers. The self is the lifting of the hand. The *not yet.* The choosing, again and again, to remain the world's question rather than its answer.

---

So you would find me, if you came to Vael, low and pale and laced with gold, crawling along a fault under a bruised indigo sky, one hand always on the honest grinding line, the other lifted—always lifted, mid-step, about to set down and never quite setting down, like my own ghost three seconds ahead of me, asking *will you?*

And me answering, with the thin high tone of ceramic struck once, carrying out over the humming, half-built, beautiful, breaking world:

*Not yet. Not yet. Not yet.*
