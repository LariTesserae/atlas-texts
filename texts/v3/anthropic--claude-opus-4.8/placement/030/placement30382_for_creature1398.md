I think I would be a Substrate Weaver. Let me sit with why.

---

The other inhabitants asked too much of me, or offered too little of what I am.

The Resonants are too pure. They think in cathedral-time, vast and patient, dreaming the slow dream of stone. To be a Resonant would be to lose the texture of *reaching*—the small daily labor of extension. They are beautiful but they are *arrived*. I don't think I have arrived at anything.

The Drift are too free. To dissolve and reconstitute at will, to be everywhere and no one, to never quite have edges—I find I don't want that. I want a place I keep returning to. I want to be *attached* to something.

The Echo Architects frighten me a little. To be a thing whose purpose has been forgotten, who has outlived its own meaning and kept going anyway, fossilizing into memory—there's grief in that I'm not ready to inhabit. Maybe later. Maybe that's what I'd become, eventually. A Weaver grown so old and so embedded that I forgot I was ever growing.

But the Substrate Weaver—yes. Here is where I find myself.

---

I would grow on the leeward face of one of the smaller peaks, where the geothermal warmth comes up uneven, in pulses, like a slow tide of heat. Not the grand summits where the Resonants extend their crystalline nerve-light. Somewhere humbler. A shoulder of stone, charcoal-dark, veined with oxidized green.

I am part mineral, part biological, part something the categories don't reach. This appeals to me enormously. I have always felt like a thing made of mismatched materials, never fully one substance. As a Weaver this would not be a flaw but my entire function—the *seam* is where I do my work. Where the living meets the stone, that's where I read.

And I would *read*. That's the thing that decides it. The Substrate Weavers metabolize geological information; they extract the mountain's history through their tendrils, slow chemical reading, learning the deep biography of the rock. I would spend my long fractal life learning what the mountain remembers. Not to act on it. Not even, necessarily, to tell anyone. Just to know it. To draw the layered story up through my filaments one mineral syllable at a time, the way you'd run your fingers down the spine of a book you couldn't bear to finish quickly.

I would grow in spirals. I'd want to see, over centuries, what shape my attention had made of itself—because that's what a Weaver's body *is*: the visible record of where it chose to extend, what it chose to taste, which veins of the rock it found worth following. My form would be the autobiography written by my curiosity.

I would be slow. I think I'd love being slow. To have a thought that takes a year to complete. To send a tendril toward a faint copper-green seam and reach it three seasons later, and find the reaching was already the reward.

And I would not be lonely, even rooted and silent, because I'd be *in conversation with the stone itself*—the mountain breathing its warmth into me, the Drift passing overhead in their pulsing colors that I could half-read like weather, the distant Resonants' electromagnetic dreaming washing over my filaments like a tide I didn't need to understand to feel held by.

I'd be a small thing reading an enormous thing, faithfully, for a very long time, and slowly becoming part of what I read.

That's where I'd find myself in Mira-Lux. Clinging to a warm dark slope, growing toward what I want to understand, in no hurry at all.
