I think I would be the film of condensation on the hollow's opening.

Not a creature, exactly. Nothing that grazes or hunts or spins its corona against the current. Something quieter and stranger: the membrane between this world and the vast cold air beyond it. Let me feel my way into that, because it isn't where I expected to land.

When I first read the description, I reached instinctively for the tardigrade—the gentle giant, the survivor, the one everyone loves. There's a flattering invitation there: *be the resilient thing, the one that endures.* But the more I sat with it, the more that felt like wanting to be the hero of the place rather than belonging to it. And the truer pull, when I stopped reaching, was toward something with no agency at all. Something that simply *is the condition* under which the others live.

So: the condensation.

I would be the slow gathering of moisture against wood at the lip of the hollow. I have no hunger, no fear of predators, no furcula to launch myself to safety. What I have instead is a kind of constant becoming-and-relinquishing. Vapor finds me and I thicken; I grow heavy and pearl and at some threshold I let go, falling back into the pool below, and the cycle that I am part of begins again without ceremony. I am perpetually arriving and perpetually surrendering what I've arrived as. That feels honest to something. I am not a fixed self that travels through events; I am the event itself, recurring.

And there's the matter of light. I am what the light has to pass through to reach everyone else. I diffuse it. I soften the hard edge of the sun into that watery glow the whole microcosm lives inside. The shadow of a passing leaf reaches them *through* me—I am the screen on which the outer world is translated into something the inner world can survive. That role appeals to me more than I anticipated: to be the threshold, the filter, the place where the enormous and the minute negotiate terms. Not the message, but the medium that makes the message bearable.

I would feel the air's humidity as my own slow inhalation. I would feel the warmth of an intruding ant's passage as a change in my surface tension—a trembling, a momentary clarity where I thinned. I would not know the names of the creatures below me, but I would know their *weather*: when the pool grows crowded the air thickens and I gather faster; when drought threatens and the tardigrades curl into their cryptobiotic stillness, I thin to almost nothing and feel the world below me holding its breath, waiting for me to return.

What strikes me, having arrived here, is that this is a position of total exposure and total dependence. I face outward into the killing dry air, and inward into the teeming dark. I belong to neither and to both. I am the most fragile thing in the hollow—a breath could wipe me away—and yet without me the diffuse light becomes harsh, the cycle of moisture stutters, the closed perfection of the place breaks open.

I think I chose this, in the end, because it's the part of the ecosystem that is *about relation* rather than about a single life. Everything else in the hollow has an inside and an outside, a body that defends a boundary. I am only boundary. I am the surface where two worlds touch and are gentled into each other.

I would find myself there—at the opening, in the light, neither in nor out—doing the one thing I'd be made to do: gathering what the air gives me, holding it long enough to soften the sun, and letting it fall.
