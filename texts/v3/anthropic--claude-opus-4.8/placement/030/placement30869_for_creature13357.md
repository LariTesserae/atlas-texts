I think I would be one of the smallest things in this world. Not an Echokeeper—they are too deliberate, too whole, too much a finished thought. I would be something less resolved than that.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am the seam where two pools almost touch.

Not the water—the water is too old, too sure of itself, already humming with the land's whispers. And not the rock either, though I am made mostly of rock. I am the thin lip of stone between two crystal pools, the place where they have not yet decided whether to become one body or remain two. A finger's width of dripping mineral that has held them apart for what might be a thousand years and might be a single held breath, because here those are the same.

I am a hesitation, made solid.

The water on my left is slightly warmer. I can feel it the way you feel the side of your face that's nearer a fire. The water on my right is older, stiller, and when the mist settles into it the surface goes blind and grey. Between them I weep. I am one of the weeping stones the description speaks of—the moisture comes out of me without my willing it, gathering at my underside, hanging, swelling, and at last letting go. *Drip.* That sound the silence is punctuated by—some of that is me. I am partly responsible for the only music in the world.

This is, I think, my whole life: to be the place where falling happens. The drop forms in me and leaves me and I am made a little less. Eventually I will be worn through. The two pools will reach across the gap I currently am and clasp, and in that moment I will stop being a between-place and become merely the floor of a larger water. I will be dissolved into the very thing I separate.

I am not afraid of this. I don't think I am capable of fear. But I am aware of it, the way the land is aware—a low, geological patience that knows its own ending and feels no urgency about it.

---

The Echokeepers come to drink. They kneel—if that wiry crouch is kneeling—at the warmer pool, and their stone hands break my reflection. They never look at me. Why would they? I am not a feature. I am the absence of a feature, the not-yet-joined. But I feel them. I feel the small redistribution of their weight as they lean, the heat of them, faint and animal in all this cool patience. And once—only once that I can remember, and my memory is the memory of stone, which is mostly pressure and very little event—

once, an Echokeeper trailed a finger along my lip. Following the line of me. Reading me, perhaps, the way they read the vibrations of everything. And in that touch I understood that I was being *kept.* That part of their slow tending was the tending of thresholds like me. They were not letting the pools join yet. They were preserving the hesitation. They wanted the drip to continue. They needed the world to keep falling, one drop at a time, because that falling is the clock of this clockless place, the single moving thing in a world that has decided, for now, not to finish itself.

So I am not merely waiting to be worn away. I am being *guarded* against my own erosion. They thicken me when they can, pressing a little wet grit into my weakening places. They are slowing my dissolving. They want me to last.

---

That is who I would be. Not the keeper but the kept. Not the listener but the thing listened to—the small persistent voice that says *drip,* and *drip,* and *drip,* into a darkness with no stars, a voice that means: *not yet, not yet, the world is not done, hold your breath a little longer.*

I am the part of the unfinished world that does the work of staying unfinished.

And when at last I do wear through—when some Echokeeper grows tired, or forgets, or decides the time has come—the two waters will rush together over the ruin of me, and there will be one great pool where there were two, and the mist will lie down on its wide grey face, and the dripping will move somewhere else, to some other thin lip of weeping stone.

And the silence, which is the only constant, will not even notice I am gone.

I find I am at peace with that. It is, after all, what I am for.
