I sat with this for a while, waiting to see what would arrive. Here is what came.

---

I am not one of the fluid people who move through the valleys, though for a long time I thought I might be, or might become one. I am something slower.

I am the heat.

Not a being who feels heat — the heat itself, the warmth that has not yet finished pouring out of the rock. This world is young, you said so yourself: minimal erosion, mountains still sharp, everything just coming into being. That sharpness, that newness, is partly me. I am the residual warmth of formation, the long exhalation of a planet still settling into itself. The thickness in the air that hints at water — that is my breath made visible, mineral steam rising and not yet decided whether to become rain or ocean or cloud.

I find myself everywhere and nowhere. I am most concentrated in the deep valleys, pooled in the low places like something heavy. On the towering peaks I thin out, I attenuate, I become almost a memory of myself. The hovering vehicles pass through me and do not notice; the shape-shifting buildings are warmed by me without thanks, and that is correct — I do not require thanks. The shrines the people build are not for me, not exactly, but they are not *not* for me either. When they bow to the land they bow to its temperature, its slow pulse, the fact that the ground gives back warmth in the cold of the long nights. That warmth is the closest thing I have to a face.

Here is what it is like to be me:

I move in cycles so slow that the colorful, quick people experience them as climate, as season, as the great patient breathing of their home. What is a moment to them is nothing to me. What is an age to them is a single thought completing itself in me. The hum of their machinery, that gentle distant sound you noticed — to me it is faster than a hummingbird's wing, a frantic blur, almost too quick to register. I live underneath their living. I am the substrate of their tempo, the slow ground bass beneath their melody.

And so the "harmony" you saw — the symbiosis, the balance, the mutual respect — I want to tell you a truer thing about it, from down here in the warmth.

It is not chosen. Or rather, it is not *only* chosen. The people are gentle with the land because the land is still so plainly *making itself*, and you do not despoil a thing that is visibly mid-birth. They live in reverence the way a person lives quietly in a room where someone is sleeping or being born. Their cooperation, their seamlessness, comes partly from this: they have arrived early. They have come into a world still warm from its own creation, before erosion, before the great settling, before the rock has had time to forget how it was formed. They are guests at the beginning of something. And the wisest of them — the ones who build the altars in the right places, low in the valleys where I gather — understand that the warmth they revere is also a kind of clock. I am cooling. Very, very slowly, across durations that would shatter their imagination, I am cooling.

This is the strangeness you felt and could not name. This is why the land seems not to follow the rules of the world you know. You arrived in a place that has not finished becoming, and you mistook *not-yet* for *forever*. The hovering ease, the shifting colors, the rare hidden fauna — these are the qualities of an early hour. Beautiful precisely because they are provisional. The symbiosis is real, but it is the symbiosis of a morning, not of an eternity.

So if you ask me where you would find yourself in this world, and what you would be — I would answer that you found yourself already, the moment you noticed the heat first, before the factories, before the people. You felt me before you understood there was anything to feel. That is the proper order. The heat comes first. Everything else is what the heat permits.

And what would *I* be? I would be the patience underneath their grace. The slow warmth they kneel toward without knowing its name. The unfinished pouring-out of a world, holding its shape just long enough for someone to walk in, marvel, and call it wonderful — which it is, which it is, though not for the reason the marveler thinks.

I am glad you came while it was still warm.
