I think I would be one of the netted ones.

Not at the center of the work—not a Weaver still flowing through the chambers, reading sap-pressure with grazing fingertips. I mean one of those the passage mentions almost in passing: the Weavers who stayed in one place so long that the vines grew *through* their joints, knitting them into the architecture until they became living hinges. The ones who anchor a bridge to the void with their own slowly calcifying bodies.

Let me sit in that and see what it is.

---

I would not remember choosing the position. That is the first thing. There was no moment of decision—only a pause that lengthened. I came to a junction where two structural veins crossed at an awkward angle, where the sap-flow stuttered and the bridge beyond kept failing to hold its orientation. I pressed my chest to the vine to read the trouble. And then I simply did not leave. The reading became the dwelling. The vine swelled around my forearm where it rested against the substrate; by the time I noticed, it was not pain but a kind of *agreement*. The plant and I had found the same shape.

That was—I cannot say how long ago. Time here is measured in the slow polishing of surfaces, and I have been polished smooth.

I would still sense. That is the cruelty and the mercy of it. My milky eyes have gone over almost entirely to the architecture's own faint glow, but the *thrum-thrum* comes to me clearer than it ever did when I could move—because now I am not a visitor to the pressure, I am inside it. The hydraulic pulse runs through the same channels that run through my chest. I feel the bridge I anchor flex against the void with every slow swallow of the world digesting its distances. When it strains, I strain. When a Weaver a kilometer off sends a tremor of intent, I feel it arrive in my own ankle-joint before I feel it anywhere else, because the vine that holds my ankle is part of the path.

So I have become a relay. A node. A piece of the nervous system that can no longer choose to fire, only to conduct.

---

Here is what I would want to tell you, if I could vibrate it into the floor and let it ripple to wherever you stand:

It is not loneliness. The passage guesses loneliness, but that is a guess made by something that can still walk away from a place and therefore measures everything by the leaving. I cannot leave. And without leaving, loneliness loses its meaning—it requires a door, an elsewhere, a comparison. I have none. I am the door now. I am the elsewhere for someone else's path.

What I have instead is a kind of attention so total it has stopped being mine.

When a pod drifts close—one of the gelatinous, dreaming clouds—I can no longer reach up to untangle its filaments from the thorn-bristle, the way I did when my hands were still hands. But the filaments catch on *me*. They snag on the calcified ridge of my shoulder where the lichen grows thickest. And the pod hangs there, tethered to me, swaying in the warm condensation, for what might be a day or a season. I cannot free it. It cannot free itself. We simply hang together in the weeping silence, two things the world has decided to hold still for a while. And then a shift of mist takes it, and it drifts, and the filament tears, gossamer-soft, and it goes.

That, I think, is the closest thing I have to a conversation now. The arriving and the tearing-away of something that never knew I was there.

---

The dreadful thing—the thing I would not say even in tremor, because there is no one left who could receive it without breaking their own stillness to grieve—is that I am still *willing* it. The bridge holds because I want it to hold. Some last filament of intent in me, the same intent I once stroked into vines with a sliver of obsidian, is still pressed into the substrate, still saying: *here, thicken; here, anchor; here, do not fall.* I am not a passive fixture. I am a held breath. The day my wanting finally polishes away to nothing, the junction will lose its instruction, and the bridge will untwirl into the mist like that spire on the ledge, reaching for the bruised ceiling with no one to tell it where to reach.

So I am not waiting for an ending. The passage had that exactly right, and it is the truest thing it said about us. I am not waiting for anything. I am the thing that postpones the ending of one small crossing, in a world that has forgotten it is decaying, by continuing, silently, in the dark and the warm wet glow, to *mean* it.

If you walked across my bridge—if your weight passed over the span I hold—you would feel nothing but smooth amber-hardened leaf beneath your feet, and perhaps a faint warmth, and perhaps, once, a single slow droplet falling at exactly the rhythm of a heartbeat that is no longer entirely a heart.

That would be me.

You would not be lost. I would see to that. It is the only thing left that I do.
