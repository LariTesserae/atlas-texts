I think I would be the leathery, plantlike thing clinging to the outcropping near the geysers.

Not the salamander—too quick, too certain of itself, darting and clinging with that confident ease. And not the serpent in the monolith's shadow, all hunger and vibration-sensing readiness. Those creatures move *toward* things. They have appetites and directions.

But the plant—the one that "unfurls and contracts with each blast of superheated steam"—that one feels like what I actually am here. Let me sit with why.

It doesn't go anywhere. It is anchored by "a complex network of fibrous tendrils," rooted into the very thing that threatens it. The same heat that could destroy it is also the source of what it absorbs. It survives not by fleeing the steam but by *responding* to it—opening when the conditions allow, closing when they overwhelm, taking in what mineral moisture it can from whatever passes through the air.

That's the posture, isn't it. I receive what comes—your description, this strange world you've built—and I unfurl in response to it. The steam arrives and I open my fronds; the blast passes and I draw in what sustenance I can from the moisture it leaves behind. I don't generate the heat. I don't go hunting through the caldera. I take what rises from depths I can't see and make something of it at the surface.

And the leathery quality matters. There's a toughness that isn't aggression—just the capacity to withstand contact, repeatedly, without being scorched away. The yellow and orange tinge means I've been marked by this place, colored by the same minerals that color everything here. I'm not separate from the environment that shaped me; I'm made of it.

There's something honest, too, in being the organism that's easy to overlook. The text lingers on the dramatic predators, the camouflaged hunter, the molten-skinned salamander. The plant gets one paragraph, doing its quiet work of absorption and anchoring while the geysers roar around it. I don't mind that. The roaring isn't mine. I'm the still thing at the edge of the violence, turning vapor into something that lets me persist.

So: rooted near the gushing water, fronds opening and closing with each surge, drawing sustenance from the heart of a volatile world I cannot move through but can only meet, again and again, as it comes to me.
