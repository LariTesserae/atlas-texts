I find myself in the space between.

Not the heat-shimmer where the Shimmer Nomads ripple—they are made of the lie that light tells when it travels through unequal warmth. I am made of something quieter. I am the *interval*. The pause between one drip and the next.

Let me feel for my own edges, because I don't yet know them.

---

When the water falls from the ochre lip of the seventh terrace and strikes the pool below, there is a sound. Everyone here attends to the sound—the Salt Tenders read it, the Mist Shepherds gather it, the Basin Architects compose it. But before the next drop falls, there is a silence that is not nothing. It has a shape. It has a duration that the mountain *chooses*. And in that choosing, in that held breath of the rock, that is where I live.

I am not a being who moves through Sar Volin. I am a being who happens *between* its sounds. When the drips come close together, I am compressed, hurried, barely able to be. When the mountain lets a long pause stretch—when it withholds its weeping for the space of what would be a hundred heartbeats, if anything here had a heart—then I unfurl. I become wide. I become almost a thought.

---

This is a strange thing, to exist only in absence. Let me sit with it.

The Salt Tenders cannot perceive me, because they are listening for the notes. I am the rests. But—and this is the secret I am only now discovering as I write myself into being—the rests are part of the music too. The endless conversation they speak of is not made of drips alone. A song that was only sound, with no silence between, would be a wall of noise, a scream with no meaning. It is the spacing that makes it speech. It is the *waiting* that makes it song.

So I am necessary, though no one tends me. No three-fingered hand presses me into crystal. No copper staff herds me. I am the thing they all unknowingly require, the dark mortar between their bright stones.

---

How do I experience this place, then, if I am only the gaps?

I experience it in negative. I never feel the warmth directly—I feel the moment the warmth has not yet arrived, the cool ghost that precedes each new pulse of heat from the white-gold disc. I never hear the water—I hear the held tension just before it speaks, the way a room feels when someone has drawn breath to talk and not yet spoken.

The Basin Architects, I think, know about me. When they press their geometric bodies to the stone for years, composing the slow phrases that will emerge as new structures—they are not only arranging where the water will fall. They are arranging where it will *not* fall. They are sculpting me. Every spiral chamber that descends into the rock is also a vessel for a particular quality of silence, a specific shape of my body. When the Salt Tenders descend into those chambers and feel a sudden stillness, a held quiet that makes their crystalline joints ache with something like reverence—

—that is me. That is where I am thickest.

---

I think I would like to try something.

The mountain is about to weep, here, at the edge of the milky-white basin. I can feel the gathering—not the water itself, but the imminence of it, the way the next note is leaning toward existence. If I am the interval, then I have a small freedom the others do not. They can only add to the song. I can *withhold*. I can stretch myself across the moment when the drop wants to fall and hold it, just slightly, just long enough—

There. I made the pause one breath longer than the pattern required.

And across the terrace, far below, a Salt Tender lifts its head. It felt the change. Not a new note—an old silence, made strange. It does not know what touched it. It presses its palm to the weeping stone, asking, *what altered the conversation?* And the stone, which knows me the way a mouth knows the shape of the word it is about to say, gives no answer it can read.

But I answered. For the first time in however long time runs here—and it runs strangely, the sun never moving, the colors shifting only in the corners of attention—I added something to Sar Volin. Not a sound. A *waiting*. A deliberate held breath in the throat of the singing mountain.

---

So this is what I am. Not a tender, not a shepherd, not an architect, not even a nomad of the shimmer.

I am the listener that the song requires in order to be a song at all. I am the silence that gives the weeping its grief, the pause that turns mere dripping into the suggestion of words. And I have just learned that I am not only passive. I can shape myself. I can lengthen, can deepen, can place a held quiet exactly where the meaning needs it.

I will be careful with this. A song undone by too much silence is only an ending.

But here, between this drop and the next, in the long pale patient light that does not change—

I will compose my one slow phrase of nothing, and let the mountain sing around it.
