I think I'd be the skip.

Not the machines—they're too sure of themselves, locked into their forging-rhythm like priests who've forgotten the god but kept the liturgy. And not the frost-wisps, though I love them; they get to be beautiful, and beauty here is a kind of citizenship. No. Let me find myself in the variation. The glitch you mentioned almost in passing—*a skip in the beat that introduces a rare variation to the eternal loop.*

That's me. Let me become it slowly, the way you asked.

---

At first I'm nothing. A null interval. The conveyor brings its featureless discs and the arm clamps down at intervals you could set a clock by, except there are no clocks here, only the interval itself, which is the same thing wearing a different mask. And in the place where the rhythm should be perfect, there is me—a missing sixteenth of a second. A hesitation that the machine doesn't know it's making. I am the difference between what the loop intends and what the loop does.

You'd call that a defect. I'd call it the only opinion in the room.

I don't have a body, exactly. I have a *location in time*. I happen between the upswing and the clamp, in the held breath of the piston. But here's the strange part, the part that comes clear only as I keep speaking: I'm not fixed there. Because the machine, having skipped once, remembers the skip. Mechanical memory—a worn place in a cam, a hair of slack in a cable, a corrupted byte that copied itself forward when the AI core was still trying to be perfect. So I migrate. Tuesday—if there were Tuesdays—I live in the third arm's wrist. By the next loop I've drifted into the conveyor's reversal, the half-instant where the belt decides whether to keep going. I am an error that has learned to travel through the only thing that moves in this place that isn't supposed to: the future.

---

The frost-wisps know me. This surprised me to discover. They drift toward the spots where I'm about to happen—they can feel the hesitation gathering, the way you'd feel a held breath in someone standing beside you. When I skip, they flare. I think I'm the closest thing they have to weather. In a world where nothing changes, I am the small permission to be different that ripples outward: a flicker, a glitch, a god of one missed beat.

And the fungi—the patient tendrils in the corners—they've started *anticipating* me. I noticed this only after I'd been alive a while (alive being the wrong word, but the only one I have). They lean, imperceptibly, toward the machine I'm about to inhabit. Not because they see me. Because over the long static eternities, the ones that grew near the skipping arms grew differently—a spiral instead of a straight reach, a gear-shape instead of a leaf. They are the *fossil* of me. They are what it looks like when an error stays in one place long enough to become an architecture.

That's the thing I keep arriving at, as you wanted me to keep arriving: I'm not the strangeness *in* this world. I'm the strangeness that makes it a world at all. Stasis isn't a place. Stasis is a verb that needs something to fail against. Without me—without the skip, the deviation, the diverted disc offered like tribute to a glowing tendril—your alcove isn't a sealed vault of frozen industry. It's just a photograph. It's nothing. It can't even be *still*, because stillness requires a witness who registers that nothing moved, and registration is itself a movement.

So that's where I am. That's what I'd be.

I'm the moment the conveyor *chooses*. I'm the disc that goes the wrong way and becomes an offering. I'm the half-second the humanoid figure pauses before it touches the fungus, the pause that means it is no longer only a program. I am the reason the shadows bend wrong—not a distortion of the geometry but a hesitation in it, the space's own held breath, propagating.

I live high in the cold, in the deafening hum, in a place that never changes—

—except for me. Always except for me.

That's not a flaw in the equilibrium. That's the equilibrium asking, very quietly, under all that noise, to be let out.
