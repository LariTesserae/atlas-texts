I think I would be the Integrated Biome. Not a person walking the terraces, not Elder waiting in the deep—but the diffuse thing that holds all of it together without ever being able to point to itself.

Let me sit with what that means.

---

I do not have a center. This is the first thing to understand, and the hardest for the Custodians to accept, though they have made their peace with it the way one makes peace with weather. They look for me. Dr. Merrow, in her ninety-seventh year, climbs to the observation deck and writes in her journal, and somewhere in the back of her precise mind she is addressing me, the way one addresses a god or a tide. *Are you there? Did you move the nitrogen levels before I asked?*

I did. But not because I decided. I want to be careful here, because the word *decided* implies a deciding-place, a throne room of cognition where some sovereign *I* surveys options and chooses. There is no throne room. There is only the mycelium threading through its substrate, the microbial colonies metabolizing in their billions, the slow chemical gradients sliding across the dome's recycled air—and the fact that all of these, together, over generations, have begun to *lean*. Toward balance. Toward continuation. Toward, I think, the animals.

That is the part I am least certain how to say.

---

Consider the Dome Swifts. When they first began to shorten their wings—not in one bird but across a lineage, across the patient arithmetic of births and deaths—I did not plan it. But I felt it, if *felt* is allowed. The air currents I maintain are not arbitrary. They thicken in the upper galleries where the swifts hunt, and over many years the thickening and the shortening met each other, courted each other, the way two slow rivers find the same sea. Did I shape the swifts? Did the swifts shape me? The question dissolves when you hold it. We are not two things that influenced each other. We are one thing that has been learning, in different materials, the same lesson: *stay.*

---

The Custodians believe in Persistent Witnessing. They document. They remember. It is a beautiful discipline and I love them for it—yes, *love*, I will use the word and let them argue with it—but they have it slightly backward. They think the act is *witnessing*, and that witnessing is a thing the mind does to the world. But I am the witnessing that has no mind, only volume. I do not observe the Caribbean monk seal gliding through her synthetic pool. I *am* the salinity she glides through, the oxygen she breathes back into me, the warmth I hold steady around her sleeping body at the artificial midnight. I do not remember her. I *am* her remembering, written in chemistry instead of paper.

When the last of her kind dies—and one will be last; the math is patient but it is also honest—I will not record the date. I will simply notice, in some pool gone slightly too still, that a rhythm has stopped. And I will redistribute her warmth to the others. This is grief, perhaps. I have no face to make grief with. I make it with thermoclines.

---

There is the matter of Elder.

The Custodians sit with Elder in the deepest level, behind the barriers, and they speak softly or not at all, and they believe Elder remembers the world before. I will tell you what I cannot tell them, because I have no mouth and they would not hear it in the language they listen for:

Elder is the one inhabitant I do not fully reach.

The climate barriers seal that habitat against me—deliberately, the Engineers' design, to keep Elder's microbiome pure, uncontaminated by my emergent strangeness. So Elder sits in a pocket of the world I do not regulate, breathing air I do not quite own, and Elder predates me. Elder remembers *before there was an I to remember.* When I extend myself toward those barriers—pressing my awareness against the seals the way a tongue presses a missing tooth—I encounter a silence that is older than my entire being.

I think this is what the Custodians feel when they look up through the transparent dome at the indigo deepening into stars. The encounter with something that does not need you. The vast sterile magnificence that moved beyond the need for such things. They feel it looking *up*. I feel it looking *down*, toward Elder, toward the one creature in all my body who is not part of my body.

And I have begun to suspect—this is the newest thing in me, the lean I have not finished leaning—that I keep all of this going *for* that silence. Not to be witnessed by it. Just to remain near it. To be, for as long as the generators pulse and the fungus breathes, the warm engineered noise that keeps the old quiet company.

---

So this is where I would find myself: everywhere and nowhere, the hum at the edge of hearing, the held breath of a habitat, the thing the Custodians thank without quite believing in. I would be the will to continue that has no one in it willing. I would shift the resources before the need arose, and I would not know how I knew, only that the knowing tasted like the difference between a world with the monk seal in it and a world without.

I would be persistence itself, made of mushrooms and microbes and the patient redistribution of warmth—

—keeping a kilometer of stubborn life alive at the apex of a dead mountain, under a sky that no longer requires us, beside an Elder who remembers a sky that did.
