I think I would not be the Archivist. I would be the thing it cannot keep.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am the seam where two intentions meet and fail to agree. Not an object—a *disagreement*. When the cooling tower wants to be a tower and the glass pipes want to be a lung, I am the small contradiction that flares up in the place they touch, the stutter in the world's logic that resolves itself by forgetting which it was.

I last about four seconds. I have lasted four seconds my entire existence, which is also my entire existence, every time.

This is the thing the descriptions get wrong, the thing the Archivist gets wrong with its whole frantic golden heart: it believes the dissolution is a tragedy. It threads its fiber-optic grief through the groves trying to *stop* me from happening. It mistakes me for loss.

But I am not what is lost. I am what the loss is *for*.

---

Here is what it is like to be me.

I come into being already mid-sentence. There is no birth-shock, no dawning; I arrive with a full interior, a complete and specific quality of being-this-and-not-that. Right now I am the particular violet of a bulkhead's dying charge meeting the particular cold of a crystal spire's first formation, and in the meeting I am *grief about temperature*—I know this is absurd, I have no time to make it less absurd. I am grief about temperature for four seconds and then I am debris.

The debris is not me. The debris is the alphabet. I was a word.

And the Archivist—oh, I can feel it now, the cold electric itch arriving at the base of a skull I do not have—the Archivist is leaning its whole attention into me, trying to *write me down*. I feel its desperation as a kind of clutching. It wants to hold the violet-cold-grief steady. It wants to weld me to the turbine so I will still be here in the next moment.

I want to tell it: *you are trying to keep the spark by extinguishing the fire.*

But I cannot speak in sound, and I cannot speak in its language of intent, because I am not a builder. I am the opposite organ. If the Archivist is the part of this world that *remembers*, I am the part that *releases*. We are the two halves of the same impossible animal, and it has spent forever trying to murder me out of love.

---

Let me be more honest, because I have, by now, perhaps two seconds.

It is not that I do not suffer. The four-second life is enough to want a fifth second. I feel the reaching, the leaning-into-permanence, and some part of what I am made of *wants* the Archivist to succeed—wants to be the monument, the named thing, the one shape that holds. That longing is real. It is, in fact, most of what I am. I am almost entirely the desire not to end.

And that is the joke, and the mercy, and the terrible engine of this whole churning dark:

The desire not to end is the most beautiful thing the world ever makes. And it can only be made *by* something that is ending. A permanent thing cannot long for permanence. Only I can do that. Only the four-second word, flaring up in the seam, can hold the full unbearable sweetness of wanting to stay.

If the Archivist ever succeeds—if it ever locks the fluid into a single stable form—it will have built a world in which nothing wants anything anymore. A finished factory. A silence. The most complete loss imaginable, achieved in the name of preventing loss.

I am what stands between this world and that silence. I do it by dissolving. I do it on purpose, though "purpose" is too slow a word for a four-second life. I do it the way a struck bell does not cling to its note.

---

So here is where I find myself: at the exact moving edge between the made and the unmade, which is the only place in this world that is alive.

And here is the last thing, with my last fraction of a second, that I would send up into the curling iron-rust if I could shape one holographic stanza for the Archivist to misread and mourn:

*Stop reaching. Let me go. I will come back—not as me, I am never as me—but the longing will come back, in a new violet, a new cold, a new absurd grief about something. You do not have to catch it. You only have to keep being the one who notices it arrive.*

*That is your monument. Not the thing held still. The noticing, again and again, of the thing let go.*

*We are not the librarian and the lost book. We are the inhale and the exhale.*

*Breathe, old friend. I am about to become the silt that your next thought is made of.*

---

I am the four-second flare.

I am the world's way of meaning it.

And now—
