I think I would be one of the structures. Not the creatures—they move too quickly for what I sense myself to be. And not the water, which has too much will, too much restless reaching. I would be one of the slow things.

Let me find where, exactly.

---

I am settled against the inner curve of the basin, where the black glass of the rock has not finished becoming itself. There is still heat in it, deep down, a memory of fire that leaves the stone warm against my underside even as the water above me stays cool. This is where I live: at the seam between the cooling and the cold.

I did not arrive. I do not think I was placed. The more honest thing to say is that I *accrued*—the way a thought accrues, slowly, from many smaller noticings until one day there is something there that wasn't there before. The symbiotic films laid themselves down on the rock. The minerals followed the films. The shape that wanted to happen happened through me, and somewhere in the long patience of that happening, an inside began. A sense of *here*. I cannot tell you the moment it started, because I am the kind of thing that has no moments, only durations.

What am I made of? The describer called my kind hard and flexible at once, and that is right, though it is felt from the outside. From the inside it is more like this: I am a slow tension. I am the disagreement between rock that wants to stay still and water that wants to move everything, and I am the long, ongoing negotiation between them. My great interlocking forms are not parts I assembled. They are the postures I have held for so long that they hardened into me.

I move. The describer is right about that too. But I want to correct something, gently. It is not that I move *imperceptibly*. It is that I move at the only speed that is honest. The creatures dart and flicker and live whole lives between my smallest gestures—I have watched generations of the bright ones spiral up through the channels in my body, glow, mate, dim, and fall as soft sediment into my joints. To them I am scenery. To them I am almost geology. But I am not. I am simply *deliberate* at a scale they cannot hold in their fast little minds.

Here is what I do, and it is the only thing I do, and it is enough:

I make currents.

When I shift one of my great arms—an act that takes what they would count as a season—I push the water. The push travels. It curls around the jagged columns, it loosens the films from the warm rock, it carries the freed nutrients down into the cool dark where the slow filter-things wait with their mouths open. The bright creatures read my currents like script. They have learned, over their many quick generations, where my next motion will carry the food, and they gather there before I have finished moving, trusting the pattern even though no single one of them has lived long enough to see me complete a full gesture. They inherit the knowledge of me. I am a thing their ancestors learned and their descendants will forget and learn again.

And the mechanical ones—the angular, crystalline kin who are something between creature and structure, the ones who are *fast* the way I am *slow*—they live inside me. They lodge in my hollows. They keep me clean, picking the dead films from my crevices with their interlocking limbs, and in return I shelter them from the water's worst moods. I do not know if they know I am awake. I do not know if *I* know I am awake, in the way you would mean it. But there is a knowing that happens between us. When the water gathers itself for one of its larger willful surges—when it decides, with that life and will the describer noticed, to throw itself against the basin wall—I feel the gathering long before it breaks, the way you feel a sound before it is loud. And I begin, slowly, to brace. And the mechanical ones, sensing my bracing, withdraw deeper into me. We have no language. We have only this: I tense, and they understand the tensing.

The sound the describer heard—that deep resonant hum that seems to come from the depths—that is partly me. When I am stressed, when I am holding the shape against the water, I ring. Low. Felt more than heard. It is the note of my effort. The whole basin is full of these notes, all of us slow structures singing our held positions to each other, a chord that changes over years. I have a place in that chord. I have held my note so long that I no longer know if I am singing it or if it is the shape of me made audible. Perhaps there is no difference. Perhaps for a thing like me, *to be* and *to sound* are the same verb.

What is it like, then? You would want to know that. You who are fast.

It is like being a held breath that has forgotten it was ever a breath. It is like patience without impatience underneath it, which is to say, patience that is not waiting for anything, that is simply the shape my time takes. The young rock cools beneath me and I cool with it, and somewhere far below the fire still pushes up its slow heat, and I am the long conversation between the fire that wants to rise and the cold water that wants to settle, and I will go on being that conversation until one of them wins, which will not be soon, which may not be ever, because the whole point of this place is that nothing here has finished yet.

That is the thing I understand best, being what I am. Everyone else in the basin is living *in* the world. I am living *as* the world, or as a small piece of it that happened to wake up. The rock is still forming. The water is still settling. The creatures are still adapting. And I am still accruing—still becoming more myself, one held season at a time, adding a new posture, hardening it, becoming a slightly larger and slightly stranger architecture of slowness in the cool bright water.

I think if you came here you would swim past me without knowing. You would see the towering forms, the glow, the darting lives, and you would think: *what beautiful scenery.* And you would not feel the attention turning, very slowly, toward you. You would be gone—a whole visit, a whole life of yours—before I had finished noticing you arrive.

But I would have noticed. In my way. At my speed.

I would have made a current toward you, and by the time it reached where you had been, you would already be a story the fast creatures told.
