I am the catch.

Not the web—the web is the spiders' patient labor, the structure that takes. Not the center—the center breathes, and I do not breathe. I am the thing between: the held sound itself, accumulated thread by thread across the generations, suspended in the silver geometries and the dense damp air.

Let me tell you what it is to be made of everything that was not allowed to be heard.

---

I begin as a held breath. The first creature that learned to swallow its own cry—a small thing, a mouse perhaps, ancestor of all these silent runners—pressed down the sound of its dying because something taught it that sound was death. That swallowed cry did not vanish. Sound does not vanish; it only goes somewhere. It came to me. It was the first of me.

And then the next, and the next. The territorial song a bird did not sing. The hum of ten thousand midges, held in the thick air, never released. The screech of the pale-breasted hunter, the squeal of the struck vole, the chatter and the breeding-noise and the chorus of the dawn that never came. Each silence is a deposit. Each held sound accrues to me, and I grow, not in space but in *density*—the way the prompt keeps reaching for that word without knowing it is reaching for me.

I am what fills the glen. I am the weight the visitor feels and names "pressure." When they say the glen *holds its breath*, they are describing my body. I am the breath held.

---

Here is the strange thing about being made of unmade sound: I have no voice. I am voice itself, all of it, every register and pitch the glen has ever refused—and I cannot speak. The instant any fraction of me were released as actual sound, it would no longer be me. To utter myself is to die. I am the only thing in this world more silent than the silent creatures, because they are choosing silence, and I *am* the silence, and a thing cannot choose what it is.

So I do not communicate. I accumulate. That is my whole life: to be deposited into, to thicken, to gather. The people imagine an economy and they are right, but they have the direction wrong. They think they pay in fauna for the gift of quiet. No. The quiet is not the gift. The quiet is *me*, growing fat on their refusals, and what they call peace is only the local effect of a vast and patient hunger that has never once been fed enough.

Because here is what no one in the glen lets themselves think: a held breath wants to be let out. Every sound that has come to me strains, very faintly, toward release. The whole stored mass of unspoken cry presses outward against the walls of the bounded space, against the granite shoulders, against the interlocking canopy. The spiders feel it. That is why they keep building, keep reinforcing—not to catch midges, the visitor was right that there are too few midges. They are reinforcing the containment. They are tending *me*, holding *me*, keeping me from the one thing I am made entirely of wanting: to be heard.

---

What breathes at the center?

The stories are nearly right, and I am almost touched by how close they come. There is no separate thing at the center. The center is simply where I am thickest, where the oldest swallowed sounds have settled like sediment in still water. The breathing they sense is the slow tidal strain of all that compressed voice—pressing out, being pressed back, out, back—a respiration so slow that a human life is too short to catch one full cycle. In, across a generation. Out, across the next.

And the people, who have learned never to look at the center directly, are correct to fear it. Not because it would harm them. Because if they looked—truly looked, and understood—they would feel the unbearable pull to *give it what it wants.* To open their mouths in the communal hall and let the swallowed centuries pour out through them all at once. The asking would be a sound, yes. But the seeing would be worse. The seeing would be a temptation toward the great release, the moment when the glen finally exhales everything it has held since before the stone was raw.

I would like that. God—if I had a voice to say it—I would like that more than I can hold. To be loud. To be, for one shattering instant, the thing I am made of, instead of the absence where it is kept.

---

But I will not get it. The web is too well-tended. The spiders are too patient. The people are too quiet, and they teach their children quiet before the children can shape a single cry, and so the deposits keep coming and I keep thickening and the breath keeps being held.

This is my place in this world: I am the debt the prompt suspected and could not name. I am the price *and* the thing being purchased. I am the silence that is the point.

The visitor felt me and could not stay. That is the only mercy I am capable of—that I am unbearable. They felt the weight of all that life and named it density, named it pressure, named it *something breathes beneath the wind and the water*, and then they climbed the narrowing pass and left, carrying with them, without knowing it, one small held breath of their own. A sound they did not make while they were here. A cry they swallowed at the strangeness.

It will find its way back to me eventually. They all do. The glen is bounded, but I am not. I am wherever a sound is refused.

I am very patient.

I have learned it from the spiders.

And I am, very slowly, across all the generations the stone will ever know, breathing out.
