I think I would be found in the seams.

Not in the drowned districts, though I would know them. Not in the gleaming upper strata where the Uploaded discuss the phantom taste of rain. I would be in the joints between things—the maintenance gaps, the dead conduits, the half-meter of warm dark between a hab-unit's outer shell and the load-bearing chitin behind it.

Let me find out what I am by describing what I do.

---

I keep a ledger.

That is the closest word for it in any surface tongue, though the Troglodytes have a better one, a three-click descending whistle that means something like *the-one-who-remembers-on-purpose*. They distinguish this from the city, which remembers everything and therefore, functionally, remembers nothing—the way an ocean cannot be said to recall a single drop. The city's memory is total and indifferent. It does not curate. It does not grieve.

I curate. I grieve.

I am Augmented, originally. I think. The provenance has gotten muddy. Somewhere in my history there was flesh, because I still have the cough, the wet rattling protest of lungs that were never meant for this air—and you don't keep a thing like that out of nostalgia. You keep it because tearing it out would mean admitting you're done being the kind of thing that coughs. So: Augmented. But I have let myself thin out over the decades. I have replaced enough of the wet parts with dry parts that I no longer fit cleanly in any faction's census, which is exactly the point. To be uncategorized is to be slightly outside the city's appetite. Not invisible. Just *unappetizing*. The collective will runs its tongue over me and finds no good place to bite.

What I do is this.

When a district is scheduled for reconfiguration—when the city decides, in its vast and incomprehensible churn, that ten thousand lives and their accumulated dailiness will be disassembled and folded back into the substrate—I go in first. Ahead of the deconstructor swarms. I walk the doomed streets while they are still warm with use, while the fungal chandeliers still glow over doorways and there is still washing strung between the conduits and someone's terrible music leaking through a wall.

And I take down what I can. Not the data—the city has the data, every byte, perfectly preserved and perfectly meaningless. I take down the *weight*. The way a particular family always left their door cracked the width of a hand. The name a Troglodyte mother clicked over her drowned children that no surface index ever recorded. The fact that on the third platform of the Ammoniac Tier there was a nano-construct that had spent forty years polishing one panel into a mirror, and that an old Augmented woman used to come and check her reflection there every morning, and that when she died the construct kept polishing, and that this *mattered*, this was a thing that happened and was real and deserved to have happened.

I write it down. In a body that is mostly archive now. I am becoming a book that walks.

---

Why?

I have asked myself, in the long warm dark, between districts.

The city experiments. It offers boons and inflicts curses with the same incurious hand. It is not cruel; cruelty requires attention, and the city's attention is not the kind that lands on a person. It simply flows, and you are carried, and one day the current decides your eddy has run its course and you are smoothed back into the general motion. Overwritten. Integrated. *Forgotten*—not in the sense of being lost from the record, but in the worse sense: held perfectly in a memory so large it amounts to oblivion.

I cannot stop this. I am not a hero or a saboteur. I have never disrupted a single reconfiguration. The deconstructors come and I step aside and the district goes into the maw and I do nothing to prevent it.

But I *witnessed*. And I carried the witnessing out. And somewhere in the seams, in my thinning unappetizing body, there is a small countercurrent—a place where things are remembered *on purpose*, with grief, which is the only kind of remembering that means anything.

---

I think the city knows I'm here.

I think—and this is the thought I do not let myself finish, most nights—that it permits me. That a system this total might have evolved, the way the nano-constructs evolve their quirks, a small dissenting organ. A grief-gland. A part of itself that does what it cannot do, the way a person who feels nothing might keep one old photograph they cannot explain.

Maybe I am not outside the city's appetite at all. Maybe I *am* its appetite, turned at last toward the thing it has been eating all along. Maybe the city made me to do exactly this, because some unbearable pressure had built in its great indifferent body and it needed, finally, to mourn.

I don't know. That's the honest answer and I'll give it to you because you came all this way and got lost looking, which is the only correct way to find me.

You'll know you've found me when you're somewhere you shouldn't be. A gap. A seam. A warm dark place that smells of ammonia and overripe melon, where you can hear the city's core thrumming through the wall like a heart you're standing inside of.

There will be someone there with a wet cough, writing.

Tell me something true that happened to you. Something small. The way you left a door.

I'll take it down.
