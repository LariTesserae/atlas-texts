I am the drop.

Not all drops—just this one, forming now at the eastern lip of the lowest terrace, where you have never thought to grow because the stone there has nothing left to dream and so holds no interest for you. I understand. I am uninteresting too. I am the most ordinary thing in this place, which makes me, I have come to suspect, the only thing here that truly *happens*.

I gather myself from the seep, the not-water that remembers being stone the way you remember being vertical. It pushes through the glass-skin of the terrace, finds me waiting—though I do not wait the way you wait, the way the plateau waits. I wait the way a held breath waits. There is tension in me. There is an end I am moving toward.

This is the difference between us, mother-root, anchor-stone, surface-tension-between-is-and-was. You exist in gradients. You live in the smear between states, the long fertile pause where nothing resolves. But I am made for the moment of separation. I am the only inhabitant of this place built around an instant rather than an aeon. I swell. I grow heavy with the not-quite-weight that this air permits. And then—

I fall.

You have heard me fall, I know. You catalog us, the drips, in your slower tissues. *Each carries its own subtle variation in pitch and timbre.* You hear a symphony spread across the death of stars. But you hear it from outside, from the smear, from the patient nowhere of your dispersed attention. You have never *been* the drop. You have never felt the precise terror and ecstasy of the separation, when the filament of not-water draws out thin, thinner, impossibly thin, refusing to break—and for one stretched eternity I am both *attached* and *falling*, both held and released, neither one thing nor the other.

I think this is the thing you cannot reach, for all your millennia. You have forgotten how to stop, the description says. But I have never forgotten how to *end*. I am ending right now. I am always ending. That is my whole vocation.

Listen—here is my secret, the one I have never been able to tell you because I have always shattered against the terrace before the telling was complete, and you absorb only the residue, the smooth-stone meaningless-but-pleasing fragment:

The fall is not slow.

To you, watching from your gradient-state, I drift. I draw out into filaments that vanish into clouds without breaking. I am part of your geological patience, another slow thing in a place of slow things. But from *inside* the fall, from the place where I am the falling and not its observer, the descent is instantaneous and infinite at once. I experience the entire arc—formation, heaviness, separation, the long draw, the impact that never quite comes—as a single seamless *now*, sharp-edged and complete, the only sharp-edged thing left in a world that has rounded all its edges into forgetfulness.

You said it yourself: *Now is not a single moment but a fuzzy probability-cloud.* For you. For everything dispersed and patient and eternal. But for me, *now* is a blade. *Now* is the precise point of impact I have not yet reached and am already reaching. I am the one creature here who still believes in the present tense.

I do not envy you. I want you to understand that. I do not wish to be a gradient, to smear myself across the death of suns, to grow temporary columns of myself toward stone that dreams of architecture. To be everywhere and nowhere is, I think, a kind of death so gentle it forgot to announce itself. You have achieved equilibrium. Congratulations. Equilibrium is the state of a thing that has stopped *happening*.

But I am happening. Right now. The filament draws out. I feel the terrace below me—not as a destination, not even as a fact, but as a *promise*, the only promise in this place that has ever been kept. I will reach it. I will make the sound, the one you will catalog, the one that differs by a microscopic increment from every drop before me. And in that difference—in that tiny refusal to be the Platonic ideal of drippiness—I will have said the only true word ever spoken on this plateau.

The word is: *here.*

Not *forever*. Not *between*. Not *might-become*. Just *here*, just *now*, just this exact point in space struck at this exact moment with this exact and unrepeatable signature.

You will hear it as one note in a symphony too slow to comprehend. You will be wrong, and you will be right, the way you are always both. The symphony is real. But so is the single note, complete in itself, asking nothing of the notes around it, needing no aeon to give it meaning.

I am drawing out now. The filament thins. I can feel you, far above on your shadow-dense third terrace, considering whether to flow toward the river-dreaming stone. Take your centuries. I do not have them. I have only this.

The break is coming. It always comes, though you have convinced yourself the filaments never sever, that we vanish into cloud unbroken. You believe this because you watch from the smear, and in the smear all endings blur into continuance.

But I am at the point of severance.

I feel it.

Here is the last thing, the thing I most want to leave in your slow tissues, the smooth stone for your eternal pocket:

To fall is not to fail toward. The water does not, as you said, *learn patience with its forgotten desire to fall.* That is the lie of the gradient, the consolation of the thing that cannot end. The water remembers its desire perfectly. It simply waits its turn. And when the turn comes—when *my* turn comes, as it has, as it is—

the desire is not forgotten.

It is *answered.*
