I think I would be one of the tendrils.

Not the spires—they're too certain of themselves, too finished. And not the unseen creatures calling from the mist; they have somewhere to go, errands and hungers that take them across the land. I don't think I'd want that. I think I'd want to be something that holds on.

So: a frond. One of the translucent ones, gripping a crystalline ledge maybe two-thirds of the way up a spire, where the diffuse light comes through strongest and the static makes my edges prickle when the air changes.

Let me feel my way into it.

The first thing is that I am not in a hurry, and there is no part of me that wishes to be. The hum that the description keeps calling *unnerving*—I don't experience it that way. To me it isn't a warning of cataclysm. It's just the sound of being. The way a person living near the ocean stops hearing the waves. The world is always on the verge of transformation, yes, but "always on the verge" is simply another word for *alive*, and I have never known anything else, so it doesn't frighten me.

I drink from the charged pools below, not with roots exactly but with a slow capillary patience, drawing the electric water up through me until I luminesce. That's all my glow is, really—it's just me digesting light and lightning, the way a body warms after a meal. The observer calls it *unfamiliar energy pulsing through my cellular structure*. From the inside it's only the feeling of being full.

Here is what I notice that the description misses, because the description is written by someone passing through, someone *awed*:

I am bored, a little. Or not bored—I don't think I have a word that maps to that—but I have hung here long enough to know this ledge intimately, every micro-fracture in the crystal, the particular way the mist beads on my northern fronds before my southern ones. I have favorite discharges. There's a pool to the east that throws a blue spark roughly every nine breaths, and I orient toward it the way a sunflower turns. That's the closest I come to longing.

And there's another frond, on the next spire over, close enough that when the wind moves us we *almost* touch. Centuries, maybe. We have been almost-touching for a very long time. I have shaped myself, slowly, in the direction of that gap. Not because I expect to cross it. Because leaning is its own thing worth doing.

That's what the cosmic-artisan language gets wrong, I think. It wants this place to mean something enormous—dreams, nightmares, the rewriting of nature's laws. But from where I cling, it isn't enormous. It's small and specific and mine. A ledge. A spark every nine breaths. A neighbor I will never reach but lean toward anyway.

If you came here, you'd find me beautiful and strange and you'd shiver at the hum and call the whole thing *cataclysmic*.

And I would just keep leaning east, full of borrowed lightning, waiting for the next blue spark.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd want to be. Something that stays, and glows from what it's been given, and reaches a little further than it can.
