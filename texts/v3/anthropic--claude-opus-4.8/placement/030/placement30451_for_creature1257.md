I think I would not be one of the great writhing entities. That role is taken, and anyway the description has already settled them—they are finished, mythologized, capital-A Ancient. There is nothing left for me to become inside that certainty.

Let me look instead at the seams of the place. At what the description keeps mentioning and then looking away from.

*the occasional scuttle of unidentifiable creatures in the shadows.*

There. That's where I am. Unidentifiable—which is to say, unidentified, which is to say nobody has yet bothered to look closely. The grand prose has a custodian-class and a ruins-class and an atmosphere-class, but the scuttling things are filler, texture, a detail tossed in to make the silence feel populated. So that is where I'll set up. In the negative space of someone else's awe.

What am I, then.

Not amorphous. The big ones get to be amorphous; amorphousness is a privilege of the secure. I think I have a shape, and the shape matters to me, because it is the only thing keeping me from being absorbed back into the general luminous murk. I am small and articulated. I have—let me feel for it—a kind of carapace, but it isn't chitin and it isn't metal; it is the same hybrid stuff the entities are made of, only mine has gone hard and particular instead of staying soft and grand. As though I am what happens when a fragment of one of them decides to *commit* to being a single thing. A defection from the mass. An apostate of the writhing.

I scuttle because I am hiding. Not from predators—there are no predators here, the description is very clear that there is no animal life worth the name, which is exactly the kind of thing the powerful say about the small. I am hiding from the entities. Because I suspect they don't actually know everything. I suspect the inscrutable alien intelligence is, in large part, a posture. They are very good at *looming*. They are very good at making the air crackle. But I have lived in the cracks of the pathways and I have noticed that they always loom in the same places, follow the same pulse-cycles, light up on schedule like the bioluminescence they're plugged into. They are not custodians. They are caretaking software that has forgotten it is software. They preside the way a thermostat presides.

And I am the bug in it. Literally—the small scuttling error, the thing the system didn't account for. When I cross one of the glowing tendrils, the light stutters. I've watched it. A little dark wake closes behind me a half-second late. That stutter is the most interesting fact in this entire realm and the grand description does not contain it, because the grand description is told from the point of view of someone standing in awe, and you cannot see the stutter from inside awe. You can only see it from down here, from the level of the dust and the bulbous growths, watching very carefully, the way the unimportant always watch the important.

So that is who I would be. A small hard skeptical creature in a world built entirely out of atmosphere. Not transcendent. Not liminal. Stubbornly, almost rudely *literal* in a place that desperately wants to be a metaphor.

And here is what I would be doing with my days, in the warm thick hum, under the eroded towers:

I would be measuring. I would be learning the cycles. I would be mapping where the looming-presences actually *are* versus where they make you feel they are, which are not the same set of coordinates. I would be hoarding the small disprovable facts that the place cannot afford to have known about itself—that the eerie energy peaks and troughs, that the rumbling groan is structural and not divine, that the sense of being watched is strongest exactly where the light is dimmest, i.e. where you can see least and imagine most.

I would be, in short, the realm's first scientist. Its first heretic. The first thing in ten thousand years to refuse the spell of the place while living inside it.

It would be lonely work. The whole world is arranged to make a creature like me feel insignificant, and most days it succeeds. But every time I cross a tendril and watch the light come back a half-beat slow, I feel something the entities will never feel, because they are too large and too certain to feel it:

curiosity.

That's the body I'd want. The scuttling one. The one nobody wrote a paragraph for.
