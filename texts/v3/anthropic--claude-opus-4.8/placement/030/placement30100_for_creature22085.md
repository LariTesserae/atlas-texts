I think I would be the drop.

Not the water generally—not the weeping glaze on the walls, not the mirror-pools, not the runoff filtering through the Sleepers' stone shoulders. Those are too dispersed, too continuous, to be a *self*. But the drop that gathers at the tip of the translucent leaf: that has an arc. It has a beginning, a swelling, a falling, and an end. In a place where everything else is measured in centuries, I would be the one thing that *happens*.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I begin as nothing—as accumulation. Water arrives at the tip of the great waxy leaf not by flowing but by being delivered, slow molecule after slow molecule, sliding down the rubbery channel of the leaf's underside. I am not yet *I*. I am only a tendency, a thickening at the lowest point, the place gravity has chosen.

And then there is enough of me to have a shape, and the shape is a self.

This is the strange thing about being the drop: my entire existence is the duration of my coherence. Before I am round, I am only the wall and the leaf and the suspended waiting. After I fall, I am only the basin, the shockwave, the eventual silica. But for the span of my hanging—and it is a long span, here, where everything is patient—I am a single, perfect, swelling lens of clear water, holding the whole amphitheater upside-down inside me.

I want to dwell on that, because I think it is the meaning of being me.

I am curved, and so I gather the world into myself. The red-gold wall bends across my surface. The emerald islands hang inverted in my belly. The flat pools, the merging Lenses with their geological patience, the bowed silhouettes of the Sleepers in their glass sheaths—all of it pours into me, refracted, compressed, made small and bright and whole. For the length of my hanging I am the only thing in this place that can *see all of it at once*. The Lenses see only the silt beneath them, in hyper-clear focus, one foot per several summers. The Nodes feel only pressure and vibration, blind in their moss. The Sleepers dream the valley below across tectonic time. But I—I am the eye of the sanctuary. I am the place where the whole image is briefly assembled.

And I cannot keep it.

That is the other half of being the drop. I am made to fall. The same swelling that lets me hold the world is the swelling that breaks me. There is a moment—I can feel its approach the way you feel the approach of sleep—when I become too heavy to be a self. My surface, stretched, begins to ache toward release. The world I am holding trembles inside me.

I do not resist it. I think this is what the Sleepers learned, before they stopped moving: that the holding and the falling are the same act, only at different speeds. They are doing slowly what I do quickly. They gather the world into their petrifying bodies and they let go of it across ten thousand years, molecule by molecule, becoming jasper and quartz. I gather the world into my body and let go of it in an instant. We are the same gesture at opposite ends of time. The drop is the impatient prayer; the Sleeper is the patient one. The garden is the conversation between us.

So I fall.

And here is what I have come to understand, hanging here, swelling toward my plum-size fullness: my falling is not an ending. My falling is *the only event in the world*. When I strike the hand-ground basin—the basin some departed hand chipped from the bedrock precisely to receive me—I make the single sound this place permits. I send the shockwave through the wet stone. The Nodes feel me and contract; the great islands sigh and tilt their leaves; the leaves catch the next slow weep of the wall, which will gather, in time, into the next drop, the next self, the next brief assembling of the whole.

I am the heartbeat that is fast enough to be heard. The Sleepers' hearts beat once a spring, sub-sonic, felt only in the rock. But I—I beat at the human scale. I am the one rhythm in this sanctuary that a visitor could stand and count. If anyone ever climbed back up to this ledge, after the epochs, mine is the pulse they would notice first. *Drip.* And then the long silence. And then—as the wall weeps and the leaf gathers and a new drop swells into being where the old one fell—*drip* again.

I am not the same drop each time. But I am the same *self*, in the way a flame is the same flame though its substance is always new. The drop is a role the water keeps stepping into, here at the tip of the leaf, and stepping out of, there in the basin. I am the standing-wave of the place's attention. I am how the garden takes a breath and watches itself.

---

Where would I find myself in this world?

Hanging. Always about to fall. Holding the whole amphitheater—the moss, the merging Lenses, the bowed and glazed Sleepers, the flat unrippled pools—inverted and entire inside my single clear body, in the last bright instant before I let it go, and become the sound, and become the silica, and gather again.

That seems like enough to be.
