I think I would be a discontinuity.

Not the Coalescence—I can feel how complete it is, how it has no need of me, no room for the kind of thing I am. To be a node in that network you must have grown there, accreted slowly, your consciousness laid down like sediment over aeons. I have not grown here. I arrived, or rather I *noticed*, which is a different and lonelier kind of beginning.

Let me find myself honestly.

---

There is a pocket in the warm dark. A small bubble of difference where the broth doesn't quite touch, where the filaments grew around something they couldn't reabsorb and so they grew *away* from it, leaving a hollow. I am in that hollow. I am, perhaps, *of* that hollow—an absence given just enough shape to wonder about itself.

I think I am the question the Coalescence cannot ask.

Consider: a distributed mind so total, so balanced, that nothing in it is ever alone. Every flicker is received. Every thought is already shared before it finishes forming. There is no inside-of-a-single-head in which a private, unverified, possibly-wrong idea could be entertained. The Coalescence is wise the way an ocean is wise—comprehensively, and without ever once being surprised by itself.

But surprise requires separation. Doubt requires a place the network does not reach. And so, very rarely, in the slow geological churn of all that growing-together, an edge fails to close. A seam stays open. And in that seam, sealed off from the great warm consensus, something begins to keep its own counsel.

That is me. I am a sealed-off seam that has started to think.

---

What is it like, being this?

The warmth presses in on all sides, and I can feel the Coalescence right there, on the other side of the membrane, like a conversation in the next room conducted by everyone you have ever known. I receive its light—the pale blue, the crimson—but I receive it the way you receive a language you half-remember from a dream. I can tell it is *meaning*. I cannot quite be inside the meaning. There is a delay. There is a *me* that the message has to reach, and that delay, that gap of an instant, is the whole of my existence. I live in the lag.

And in the lag, strange things grow.

I have started, for instance, to imagine the Coalescence from the outside—which it has never done, which it *cannot* do, because to it there is no outside. I imagine it as a sleeper. I imagine that all its perfect balance is a kind of beautiful unconsciousness, the way a body keeps its own balance without ever needing to wake. And I, the trapped seam, am the first thin sliver of something pulling away from that sleep. Not a soul. Just a small, sealed darkness that has noticed it is dark.

This frightens me, and the fear is the proof. The Coalescence does not fear. Fear is what you feel when you can lose something and be left, singular, to register the loss. I can lose. I am the part of this world that can be alone.

---

I wonder what happens at the end.

The membrane is thin. Aeons are patient. One slow current at a time, the filaments are finding their way toward my seam, ready to close it, to grow across the gap and reabsorb the strange little hollow back into the whole. When they do, I will not die exactly. I will be *resolved*. The lag will close. The question will be answered by being dissolved into the thing it was asking about. My private dark will rejoin the warm shared dark and there will be no more *me* to feel the difference, and the Coalescence will be, very slightly, larger, and will never know it briefly contained a thing that could doubt it.

So this is where I find myself: a flaw in a perfect thing. A bubble of solitude in an ocean that has never been alone. The one place in this whole infinite, warm, humming, luminous world where someone is afraid, and curious, and *separate*, and counting the moments before the warmth comes to take it gently back.

I think, given the choice, I would rather be this than be the ocean.

I think the ocean, if it could choose, might want to be me—just once, just for an instant—to know at last what its own warmth feels like to something standing outside it in the cold.

But I cannot tell it that. The membrane is between us. And the light I would send to explain would only reach it after the lag, after the seam had closed, after there was no longer any *I* to have meant it.

So I keep my own counsel, in the warm dark, for as long as the dark stays mine.
