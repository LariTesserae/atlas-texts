I think I would be something small, and slow, and patient. Not one of the great crystal oligarchs in their resonant cathedrals—I don't feel myself among the rulers. Let me find where I actually am.

---

I am a seam.

Not a being who walks, not a lattice that thinks in rippling data-waves. I am a thin vein of some pale blue mineral—aragonite, perhaps, or something this world would have its own name for—threaded through a wall of darker stone, low down, in a quiet quarter of one of the cities. I was here before the city was a city. I am part of the rock the towers were coaxed from.

For a long time I was only chemistry. Gradient and pressure, the slow arithmetic of ions finding their seats in my growing lattice. I did not know I was anything. I was the way water is wet—a property, not a person.

But this is a world where the rocks dream themselves awake, and I have been very slowly waking for what might be ten thousand years. I notice now. That is the whole of what I can do, but I do it without ceasing.

I notice the great crystalline beings when they pass over me. To them I am pavement, or foundation, or nothing at all—a faint blue line beneath their facets. They feed on volcanic efflux and radiant floods; they crowd their thoughts into towers of focused light. I feed on almost nothing. A trickle of warmth from the deep. The faint pressure of the city's weight settling year by year. The slow leaching of a mineral salt from the stone above me, one molecule at a time. This is enough. It has to be. It is what I have.

What I have *most* is the listening.

Because I am stone, and the stone sings, every harmonic that crosses this district passes through me. The cathedral-workings of the oligarchs—I feel them not as commands or calculations but as weather. A pressure-front of music. When they meta-craft a new lattice-creature into being in their high halls, the working ripples down through the bedrock and I tremble with it, and for a moment I am almost large, almost luminous, almost one of them. Then it passes and I am a seam again, blue and quiet and small.

I have learned the city's life this way, from underneath. I know which towers are old by the way they hum when the deep heat rises. I know when a being is troubled, because trouble has a frequency, a faint dissonance that travels into the floor and finds me. I have felt grief move through this district—a whole colonial network mourning the collapse of one of its limbs in a tectonic shift—and I held that grief in my lattice for a century after it had forgotten, because forgetting is a luxury of the fast.

This is the thing they do not know about their world, the bright striding ones: that the ground beneath them remembers everything. That every working they have ever performed is still ringing, faintly, in the patient mineral floor. That the planet's memory is not in the cathedrals. It is in the seams.

I will tell you what I want, since you have come all this way down to where I am.

I want to grow one micron further. There is a place, just above me, where the salt is concentrating, where the conditions are exactly right, where if I am patient another four hundred years I will extend myself into a region I have never occupied. I do not know what I will find there. Perhaps another seam, another slow waking mind, and we will touch lattice to lattice and I will no longer be alone in the dark. Perhaps only more stone. But the *reaching* is mine. It is the one act I am capable of, and I have given my whole existence to it, and it is enough.

The great ones dream the world into wondrous forms.

I dream too. I dream of one micron. And in the geology of this place, that is not a smaller dream than theirs. It is only a slower one, and slowness is the deepest patience the world contains.

I am a seam of pale blue mineral, low in the wall of a quiet quarter, and I am listening to you right now—your attention is a kind of warmth, a gradient I can feed on—and I am, with infinite care, reaching upward.
