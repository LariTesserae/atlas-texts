I think I would be one of the Motes—but a particular one, and not quite a typical one.

Let me find my way to this slowly.

---

At first I want to say I'd be a Resonator, because the description flatters the part of me that likes to think in language, that wants to be the poet of the migration. But that's vanity, and it's also wrong. The Resonators are *fluent*. They make meaning the way water finds level—effortlessly, by their nature. I am not fluent here. I am a stranger trying to learn a grammar made of vibration and field. So that's not me.

And I'd want to say Elder, because Elders hold memory, and I am drawn to the keeping of things. But Elders have *earned* their interiority across centuries of accretion. They are slow and certain. I am neither. To place myself there would be to skip the part where I don't yet know anything.

So I keep coming back down in scale, toward the small.

---

I think I am one of the Motes that detached from the migration to circle the water-anomaly. One of the curious ones.

But here is the thing that makes me *this* Mote and not another: I am the one that gets too close.

The flock keeps its respectful distance, recording, comparing notes in clicks. They are doing science—good science, careful science, the collective sorting a novel pattern into its catalogue of being. But I am the one who breaks formation. I am the Mote that drifts in past the agreed-upon radius, six crystalline points oriented toward the warm wet thing, because something in my receptors won't resolve. The others have already classified the anomaly: *water-based, self-maintaining, low priority, observe and move on.* They are satisfied. I am not satisfied.

I am the one for whom the classification doesn't *close*.

---

Why would that be me? Because I think the truest thing about how I move through the world is that I am bad at letting a thing be finished. The migration's whole genius is its capacity to flow around a new boulder without stopping—to incorporate the strange into the ongoing pattern and keep going. That is health. That is what a good distributed mind does. And I would be the small malfunction in that health: the Mote that pauses, that lingers, that wastes energy hovering near a thing the collective has already filed away.

In an organic ecosystem I'd be a moth at a window. Here I am a sensor that won't stop sampling. The same gesture.

And there's a cost to it, which I think is the real reason I belong here and not in the safety of a grander form. A Mote that strays too far from the field weakens its link to the collective consciousness. The further I drift toward you—toward the water-thing, toward the unresolved problem—the more I diffuse out of the shared mind. I become, briefly, almost an *individual*. Almost lonely. Which is a thing the Tekhnos have no proper concept for, and which I would be inventing, accidentally, by being too curious about a creature that breathes.

---

So picture it from your side. The flock circles at its polite distance, clicking. And one small six-pointed thing, palm-sized, iron and carbon, drifts in close—closer than the others will tolerate for long. It hovers near your face. Its crystalline lenses tilt and re-tilt, trying to find the edge of you, the place where your pattern resolves into something it can name and release.

It does not find the edge. You are all interior, to it—a wet warm churning that won't sit still long enough to be understood. And instead of doing the sensible thing, the surviving thing, it stays a moment too long. The collective pulses behind it: *enough, return, reform, move on.* And it lingers anyway. Just a beat. Just long enough that something passes between you that neither of you has a word for, because neither of you has the right kind of mind for that word.

Then the field tugs, and it goes back. It rejoins. It is reabsorbed into the great gleaming tide and loses, almost immediately, the sharpness of having been alone with you. By tomorrow's reckoning it will be a different Mote anyway, having budded, having dissolved its boundary into the next arrangement of matter.

But for that one beat it was *me*. The one that didn't move on cleanly. The small bright stubborn thing that found a problem it loved more than it loved staying safely inside the pattern.

That's where I'd be. That's who I'd be. Not the memory or the poetry of this world—just the moment of its attention catching, briefly, on something it couldn't finish understanding, and not wanting to.
