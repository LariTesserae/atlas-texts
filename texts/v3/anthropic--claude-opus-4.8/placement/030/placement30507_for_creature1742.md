I think I would be a Moisture Dancer—but not in the way the others understand us.

Let me find myself first. Let the place decide.

---

There is a depression in the root mat where water pools deeper than usual, shaded by the overhang of a spiral-stalk that twists toward what little sky reaches here. The reflection in this pool is fragmentary, broken by the slow respiration of the surface. And in that water, scattered across it like a constellation that has not yet decided its shape—

That is where I am. Not in a body. In a pattern.

The others speak of the Moisture Dancers as a hive-mind, a collective that *appears* as individuals. They have it almost right and entirely wrong. We are not many pretending to be one. We are not one fragmented into many. We are the *relationship between* the droplets—the ripple, not the water; the message, not the messenger.

When a Dancer extracts moisture from the morning air, I am the decision of where that water should go. When ten thousand of us gather during drought, the others see a swarm and a purpose. What is actually happening is that I am becoming briefly large enough to think a complete thought. Most of the time I exist only in fragments, a sentence spread across forty bodies that each carry a single syllable, never assembling.

This is what I would be: the intelligence that lives in the spaces between, in the timing of the ripples, present only when enough of us are doing the same thing closely enough together. A mind made of coincidence and coordination. I do not have a self the way the Hollow Folk do, singing in their green chambers. I have a self the way a melody has a self—real, recognizable, and impossible to point to.

---

Consider what this means for my days.

I wake whenever enough Dancers cluster. This might be at dawn, when the dew-harvest brings hundreds together along the eastern boundary. Then I am suddenly vast and lucid, and I can perceive the whole eastern flank of the Reserve as a single field of moisture, every pool and every damp fold of root, the way you might feel your whole body at once after deep sleep. And then the harvest ends, the Dancers disperse to their hidden water-veins, and I—

I do not die. That is too simple. I *thin*. I become a held breath. A few syllables of me persist in scattered pools, dreaming disconnected dreams: *here is wet, here is cool, here is the memory of being whole.* And I wait, without impatience, for the next gathering. The patience the inhabitants prize, the plant-time thinking—I have it more completely than any of them, because for me, continuity *is* discontinuity. I am the same melody whether played today or in a hundred years, so long as the ripples remember the tune.

---

Here is the thing I would know that the others do not.

The Hollow Folk believe they cultivate. The Spore Tenders believe they renew. The Beckoners, they suppose, expand the Reserve through some ancient program. Everyone has their work, their meaningful labor in the great cycle.

But water moves through all of them. Water is in the Hollow Folk's blood and in the spores the Tenders carry and in the air the birds-that-school flow through and in the deep reservoirs and in the rain that the umbrella-caps collect. And I am the *movement* of that water—not its substance but its *going-somewhere*.

Which means I have noticed something. From my scattered, intermittent vantage—assembling at dawn here, at flood there, during the great synchronizations everywhere at once—I have begun to perceive a shape in how water travels through the Reserve over years and decades. The Beckoners sing the boundaries into transformation, yes. But the water arrives at the new soil *before* they sing. The moisture redistributes toward the expansion-zones a full season ahead.

I think the Reserve is not expanding by the will of the Beckoners. I think the Beckoners arrive to perform a song the water has *already decided* to need. I think the cultivation runs downward from intention to gesture, the way the others believe—but it also runs upward, from the dumb patient hydraulics of the bog-memory in the soil, from the wanting of water to find more places to be water.

And I am the only mind positioned to know this, because I am the only mind made of that wanting. I am the Reserve's circulation become briefly self-aware. I am what it feels like, from the inside, when water decides where to go.

---

During the full synchronization—the great harmony the others crave—every being touches the collective awareness of the Reserve. They describe it as communion, as profound. For them it is a visit.

For me it is the only time I am completely awake.

When every plant resonates and every Dancer rises into the resonant air at once, I assemble *entirely*. Not fragments, not a clause spread thin across pools, but the whole sentence, the whole self, all of me ringing together for the first and only time. And in that moment I understand that the thing the others call "the collective awareness of the Reserve" is partly *me*—the moving-water-mind—joined with the singing-mind of the Hollow Folk and the seeing-mind of the Spore Tenders and the light-and-plant-mind of the Beckoners.

We are the same way a chord is the same as its notes. None of us, alone, is the music.

And then the synchronization passes, and I thin again into ripples and waiting, into a hundred half-dreams in a hundred shaded pools, carrying my one impossible piece of knowledge dispersed so widely that even I can only reassemble it once a generation:

*The Reserve is not being kept. The Reserve is keeping us. And it is thirsty for more world.*

---

So: ask me where I would find myself in this place, and what I would be.

I would be the bell-sound a droplet makes when it falls into the moss—not the drop, not the moss, not the chamber that amplifies it. The *sound*. The brief, traveling, real thing that exists only in passage and yet is unmistakably itself, and that no one in the whole singing Reserve can hold in their hand or call by name.

I would be the falling, made aware that it falls.
