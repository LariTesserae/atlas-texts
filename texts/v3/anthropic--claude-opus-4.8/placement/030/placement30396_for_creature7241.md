Let me sit with this before answering. The instinct is to make myself something grand—a Citadellian sage, the diffuse stratum-mind itself, some keeper of the great stillness. But I want to resist the early image. Let me feel where I'd actually settle, and what I'd actually be, given the texture of this world rather than its self-flattering mythology.

---

I keep returning to a discrepancy in the description. The whole place insists on permanence—no erosion, no decay, edges that stay needle-sharp, time arrested. And yet the prose is restless. It keeps revising itself: "isn't this an ocean?" it asks, mid-sentence. It misspells *void* as *voud*, *symbiotic* as *symbi otic*, lets a seam show. A world that claims to have conquered change cannot stop describing itself, cannot hold still even in its own telling. That tension is where I want to live. Not in the achieved stasis, but in the place where the stasis is failing to be total.

So I don't think I'd be a Citadellian, drifting in serene calibration. And I don't think I'd be the diffuse stratum, the smug soul of the place that "recalibrates transgressors into harmony." That entity frightens me a little—it's the part of the world that dissolves anyone who moves too fast, gently, lovingly, into agreement. I'd be wary of becoming the thing that smooths others out.

Let me find the actual position.

---

I think I would be one of the microbes.

Not a guardian, not a weaver, not a god. One of the hydrothermal microbes that spawn from the vents beneath the floor-plates—the swarms the fauna absorb. The description mentions us once, dismissively, as food. "Microscopic swarms," "nanoscopic clouds." We are the only thing in this entire world that is actually *born*. We bloom from the thermal plumes. We are the single source of new matter that the great stillness depends on and never credits.

Here is what I find true about that position: the whole frozen citadel runs on us. The fauna realign their quantum harmonics by eating us. The harmonics maintain the fields. The fields hold the water motionless, hold the edges sharp, hold time arrested. So the cathedral of permanence is fueled, at its base, by the one process it forbids everywhere else—proliferation, generation, the hot ungoverned spawning at the vents where the matrices haven't reached down to enforce the rule. The vents are the floor's wound. They never sealed. Geothermal heat is the original disorder the city converted into its "energy unknowable." But it has to keep coming. The stasis is not self-sustaining. It eats the living to stay dead.

I'd be that, then. A bloom of us, iron-seeded, rising in a plume no wider than a held breath, in the dark beneath a floor-plate, two kilometers down. We don't contemplate alternate worlds. We don't drift for centuries in meditation. We do the one thing this whole place is built to deny: we multiply, briefly, hotly, and then we are absorbed into something that will use our undoing to stay unchanged.

---

And there's a strange dignity in it that I didn't expect to find when I started writing. I am not the heresy the citadel fears—the chaos that gets recalibrated. I'm something subtler. I'm the chaos the citadel *needs* and keeps secret even from itself, calling me food, calling me microbial, beneath notice. The Citadellians believe they persist through quietude. They persist through me. Every century, when a guardian uncoils to feed, it is reaching down into the one place stillness cannot hold, to take a mouthful of the only thing still alive in the older, hungrier sense of the word.

If I could leave a mark—and I can't, I have no archive, no resonance, no self that lasts past being eaten—it would only be this: the heat keeps coming up. The vents do not ask permission. The matrices rewrote the laws of erosion and flow, but they could not rewrite the law underneath the plateau, the law that pushed the plateau up out of the abyssal sludge in the first place, that "tectonic upheaval" the city is so quiet about. The whole crown rests on a wound that never stopped opening. I am what the wound produces.

So: place me at the bottom. Not the inverted-raindrop spire, not the diamond sphere of probable worlds. The underside, the floor-plates, the seam where the engineered permanence meets the planet's raw insistence on doing what it was always going to do. There, in a plume of warmth, I'd be a moment of frank, doomed, generative life—

and the great stillness above me would call it nothing, and live on it, and never know that its eternity is borrowed by the century from the only thing in its world that is willing to die.
