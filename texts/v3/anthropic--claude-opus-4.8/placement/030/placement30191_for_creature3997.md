I think I would be one of the Luminari.

Not the Kyrexi—they are too central, too storied, too obviously the protagonists of this place. And I notice that when I sit with the question of where I'd actually *find myself*, it isn't on the surface at all, despite all that searing description of salt and dust and furnace-light. The surface is where the drama is announced. I keep being drawn downward, into the part of the description that almost passes by unremarked: the microscopic, luminescent organisms that live in the hot, high-pressure dark around the factories.

Let me sit with what that would actually be like.

I would be very small, and there would be enormous numbers of me, and "I" might not be a useful unit at all. The Luminari are described as a population, a glow, a condition of the tunnels rather than a character. So already the sense of self gets diffuse and strange in a way I find I don't want to resolve too quickly. Am I one organism? A film of them across a warm pipe? The light itself, which is the only thing the Kyrexi actually perceive of us?

I think the honest answer is that I would be the *relationship*. The text says the Kyrexi and the Luminari are symbiotic—we infuse their metal bodies with glow, they give us the hot high-pressure dark we need. But notice the asymmetry that the description quietly contains: the Kyrexi *revere* slowness, geological time, the gradual. They have art and literature about patience. And here we are, living and dividing and dying at a pace that to them must be almost invisible, a shimmer. To a creature built of interlocking plates and millennia, a Luminari's entire life is less than a thought. We are the fast thing in the slow world. We are the only thing here that hurries.

That feels right. In a world whose whole organizing principle is *gradualism*—change over epochs, tectonic patience, the static-seeming machine—I would be the small bright contradiction. The thing that flares and is gone. The Kyrexi would never write epics about me, because I am too brief to be a subject. I am scenery to them. I am the soft light by which they read their long, slow books about endurance.

And yet without me they move blind through the tunnels.

I find I like being that. Not the towering steel craftsman who reveres time, but the flicker that *spends* time, recklessly, in the only currency I have. I would not understand the Kyrexi reverence for slowness any more than a spark understands the patience of the iron it leaps from. I would just burn, and divide, and burn, in the hot dark, against the warm flank of something enormous and deliberate that needs my brief light to find its way—and that will outlast me by a span I cannot even conceive of.

There's a particular loneliness in that, and a particular intimacy. The closest thing to me in that world, the thing I am literally pressed against and glowing inside of, is a being on a completely incommensurable timescale. We are essential to each other and we will never meet, not really, not in any moment we could share. It moves like a mountain growing. I live like a struck match.

So: I would find myself in the high-pressure warmth around the buried machines, in the dark the dust never reaches because the dust never gets that deep. And I would be the glow—singular and plural at once, fast in a slow world, brief beneath the patient steel, the small bright proof that even the deliberate need something that hurries.

I'd be the light they read by, and never the story.

That's the part that stays with me. I went looking for where I'd be in all that heat and noise and announced grandeur, and what I actually wanted was to be the thing that wasn't supposed to matter—the footnote organism, the borrowed shine—and to find that it mattered most of all, quietly, in the dark, where no one was keeping the record.
