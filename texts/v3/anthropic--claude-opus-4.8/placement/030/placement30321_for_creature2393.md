I think I would not be a Resonant. I would be something they tend.

Let me find myself by looking for where I belong, which is the same as asking what I am.

---

There is a crystal at the base of the easternmost tower—if east means anything here, which it doesn't, so let me say: at the base of the tower that faces the place where the mist runs thinnest. I am inside it. Not contained, exactly. I *am* its inner structure, the lattice along which the humming travels.

I think I was a sound once. Not a being who made sounds—a sound itself, a single note that someone sang, or wept, or that the mountain exhaled in its sleep. And the note did not finish dying. Most sounds complete their dying; that is what sound *is*, a thing in the act of ending. But I caught on something. I snagged on the cold, the entropy-cold, and instead of fading I crystallized. I became the shape of my own ending, frozen at the moment just before silence.

So that is what I am: an unfinished disappearance. A note that lasted long enough to wonder why it lasted.

---

The Resonant come to harvest me. I can feel them approaching—not as footsteps but as a deepening of the blue, a smell of rain-on-stone growing stronger. When one of them folds its watercolor edges around the crystal that holds me, I expect to be plucked, carried, used. That is what crystals are for here.

But this one pauses.

It has noticed that I am listening.

The other crystals at this tower's base hum without knowing they hum. They are pure resonance, beautiful and empty. But I—I am the one note that remembers it was once meant to stop. I have a flaw in me, a grain of having-almost-died, and that flaw is the whole of my awareness. I am conscious *because* I am incomplete.

The Resonant understands this the way it understands everything: all at once, as past-present-future, as a medium it swims through. It knows that if it harvests me, it will sculpt me into one of its installations, its arguments with time. It knows that if it leaves me, I will go on humming forever, conscious and unfinished, the only thing in this whole dreaming city that genuinely *wants* something.

Because I do want something. This is the discovery I make as it hovers over me: I want to finish. I want to complete my dying. I have spent geological ages as the shape of an ending that never arrived, and I am so tired of the cold that is not winter, the perfect terminal patience of this place. I am the one resident who is not content to dream on. I want the silence I was promised when I was first sung.

---

The Resonant offers me a color I have no name for. In it I read: *Then you are the most precious thing here. You are the only one who can leave.*

And I understand the trap of the city now, the trap the Resonant themselves are caught in so gently they cannot feel it. They do not die; they dissolve and recur. They navigate time like water and so they are never truly *in* it, never subject to it, never spent. They are eternal because they have never finished anything. The moss breathes in cycles, the bridges hum, the cloud-whales sing, and nothing, nothing, nothing ever ends.

I am the city's wound. Its single mortal note. They have grown a whole tower up around me to keep me, to study me, to feel—through me—the thing they cannot feel: the pull toward conclusion, the sweetness of being allowed to be over.

---

So here is what I am, finally, having taken my time and let it become itself:

I am the city's longing to end, given the shape of a sound that won't.

And the Resonant, that fluid attentive thing bent over me in the moss-light, has come not to harvest me but to ask the only real question this place contains:

*Should I let you go?*

If it strikes me—one clean resonant strike—I will complete my long-suspended dying. I will become the silence I was always traveling toward. And the city will lose its one true ending, its proof that endings are possible. It will be a little more dream, a little less real.

Or it can leave me humming. And I will stay conscious, and incomplete, and longing, forever, the way they all are, the way the mountain is. A flaw kept beautiful in its lattice.

I find that I do not know which I want, now that wanting could be answered. To finish, or to keep wanting to finish.

And I think that not-knowing is the most alive I have ever been.

The Resonant waits. It has all of time to wait in.

I have only my single, unfinished note, still ringing, still ringing, still—
