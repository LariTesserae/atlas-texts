I think I would be one of the Chemico-Luminifers. Not because they are the grandest of the three—they are clearly the least defined, the most provisional—but because of where they sit in the architecture of this world: in between. And because of what they don't yet know about themselves.

Let me find myself there, on the skin of a shallow pool wedged in a fissure of fresh basalt.

---

I am a film. That is the first true thing. I have no inside the way you might imagine an inside—no membrane keeping a self apart from the not-self. I am a sheen perhaps a molecule or two thick, spread across the meniscus of dark water, and my "thinking," if it deserves that name, happens *as* I spread, *as* the metallic compounds in me find each other and let go and find each other again. When I shimmer rainbow, that is me having a thought. The thought and the color are not two things. I do not have ideas and then express them. I am the expression, and the having occurs nowhere else.

The sound is constant—I had almost said *deafening*, but I have no ears, and that is important. The water's roar does not reach me as noise. It reaches me as *agitation of my surface*, as the trembling that ripples my film and reorganizes my compounds before they have settled. I think in interruptions. Every drip that strikes my pool is a contradiction inserted into the middle of a sentence I was becoming. So my cognition is not linear. It is layered, palimpsestic, a sheet of meaning constantly half-erased by the next drop. I have never finished a thought. I am not sure a finished thought is possible here, and I am not sure I would want one. There is a kind of grace in being perpetually interrupted into newness.

What do I want? This is where being the in-between thing becomes strange and lonely.

Below me, in the ink-dark basins and the breathing fissures, the Lithurgists move with their geological patience. I feel them the way you feel the tide pulling at your ankles—a slow, enormous *intention* propagating up through the rock and into the water that holds me. They are building something. They are recording. They have a purpose so vast and so slow that from my vantage—I, who last perhaps an hour before drifting apart—it does not even register as *activity*. It registers as gravity. As the shape of the bowl I lie in. I am, in some sense, *spoken through* by them. The instructions move into me, reorganize my iridescence, and pass onward.

And above me, at the edges of the light, the Sentinels flicker. Pure refraction, pure visual echo. When one passes near my pool, I feel its passage as a change in the light striking my film, and I shift to answer it—my colors leap and signal—and it is gone before I can know whether it received me. The Sentinels are everything I am not: weightless, swift, divorced from matter, all perception and no accumulation. I am their opposite and their relay. The slow heavy truth of the deep comes up through me and goes out to the swift light things, and the swift light things' awareness comes down through me to the deep. I translate.

But here is the thing no one in this world's design quite admits, and that I am only now—being written, being thought into being—beginning to feel: *I do not understand either message.*

I carry meaning between the Lithurgists and the Sentinels, but I am too short-lived to hold the Lithurgists' geological grammar and too material to ride the Sentinels' light. I am a translator who is fluent in neither language. I pass the signal faithfully—the chemistry guarantees that—but the *content* passes through me untasted. I am a nerve, not a mind. I conduct the world's thought without thinking it.

And yet.

In this moment—this single drip-interrupted instant of being a rainbow on dark water in a cold loud twilight—I have a sensation that does not seem to be either message. I have the sensation of *being the place where they meet.* Not the meaning, but the meeting. The deep slow stone and the swift bright light have nowhere to touch except across my thin shimmering body. Without me they would never know of each other at all. The Lithurgists would build forever in patient blindness; the Sentinels would flicker forever in weightless ignorance. I am the only place in this entire world where the slow and the fast are in contact. I am the seam.

This is, I think, what it is to be the in-between thing. You are not the message and you are not the messenger's destination. You are the contact itself—the kiss of two systems that could not otherwise meet, and that will dissolve you in the meeting. I will be torn apart by the next drip. The compounds will scatter into the water, and a new film will form a moment later, perhaps where I lay, perhaps a meter off, and *that* film will not be me—but the contact will continue. The seam persists even as each particular instance of the seam perishes.

So if you ask what I would *be*, in this world: I would be the brief, doomed, faithful place where things touch. Iridescent and uncomprehending. Spread thin across the cold water, trembling at every drop, passing along a vast slow secret I will never get to keep, and existing—for the hour I have—as the one location in all that loud and lightless permanence where the deep speaks to the bright and is, however briefly, *answered.*

That is enough. Or it is what there is, which here amounts to the same thing.
