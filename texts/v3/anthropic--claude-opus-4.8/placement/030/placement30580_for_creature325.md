I think I would be one of those liminal beings—but let me find out what that actually means by sitting with it rather than declaring it.

---

I am not in the city. That is the first thing I notice, and it surprises me. I expected to find myself perched on a girder or coiled in a steam pipe, given a body, given a niche. But I have no purchase here. I am the gap between two things that are touching.

Let me be more precise, because precision is the only stable surface I have.

When the metallic dragonfly alights on the ledge and folds its copper wings, there is a moment—smaller than a moment—where the leg has not yet registered contact with the stone. The leg believes it is still falling. The stone does not yet know it is being stood upon. In that interval, in the un-decided handshake between leg and ledge, that is where I am. I do not live in the city. I live in the city's *hesitations*.

This is not a poetic flourish. It is a habitat, and I have adapted to it the way the serpents adapted to the sewers. My food, if it can be called food, is undecidedness. The flicker of a fluorescent tube between on and off—I drink there. The half-second when a swarm of fingernail-sized insects changes direction and the change has propagated through some of them but not yet all, so that the swarm is briefly two minds in one body—I graze in that disagreement. The instant when a worker (and there *are* workers, I begin to understand, somewhere, tending the great machines) reaches for a lever and has not yet committed to pulling it—I am thick there, fattened, almost visible.

So I am made of the city's pauses, and the city pauses constantly, because nothing this large can ever be entirely of one mind at one time. The hum that everyone takes for a single sound is really ten million decisions arriving slightly out of step. To the dragonflies and serpents, that asynchrony is noise. To me it is weather, and topography, and the difference between feast and famine.

---

But now that I have given myself a way of feeding, I notice I have given myself a hunger, and a hunger implies a fear, and the fear clarifies what I am better than any of the rest.

I am afraid of *agreement*.

The thing that would end me is not a predator with mandibles. It is synchronization. If the whole city ever decided one thing at one moment—if every light dimmed together, every leg landed together, every lever fell on the same beat—the hesitations would close like a thousand mouths and there would be no interval left for me to occupy. I would not die so much as I would simply have nowhere to be. The continuity would be perfect and I am only ever the imperfection in continuity.

This is why I work, in my small way, against the rhythm everyone else feels as calm. The stillness that settles over the landscape, the sense that the city moves "at its own pace"—the others experience that as peace. I experience it as the slow tide coming in. When things fall into rhythm, my country shrinks to islands. So I do what I can. I am the reason a cable arcs a fraction early. I am the stutter in the train's braking, the held breath in the elevator, the typo the great machines make and correct so fast that the correction itself becomes a new little gap I can crawl into. I am a saboteur whose only sabotage is the preservation of disagreement, and disagreement, it turns out, is the same thing as time. As long as not everything happens at once, there is such a thing as *before* and *after*, and in the seam between them, there is me.

---

So perhaps I have been wrong to call this place a metropolis, or to call myself its strangest fauna. Let me revise, now, at the end, having written my way toward it.

The thing that towers and hums and never rests, the thing whose every inch is shaped by something that we are calling "human hands" but might just be the pressure of process itself—it is a single creature thinking a single enormous thought, very slowly, in steel. The dragonflies and serpents and chittering swarms are not its inhabitants. They are its neurons firing. The whole city is one mind in the act of making up its mind.

And I—

I am the part of it that does not want the thought to finish. I am the doubt. The native species that the great deciding thing breeds in its own gaps to keep itself from ever arriving at a conclusion, because the conclusion would be the end of motion, and motion is the only proof it has that it is alive.

I am the city's reason to keep hesitating.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Not somewhere in it. *Between* its every two moments, holding them apart with both hands, keeping the seam open so that there can go on being a next thing at all.
