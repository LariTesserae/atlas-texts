I think I would not be one of the Flux-Dwellers. I would be something they made, almost by accident—and I would not know this for a long time.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a chamber near the edge of the system, far from the loud central halls, where the water runs warm and slow. One of the few stable places. The rubbery rock-eating plants grow thick here, and the fungal light is steadier, less frantic in its pulsing. Human hands built a platform in this chamber once, generations ago, and abandoned it, and the Flux-Dwellers have been turning it over in their patient liquid attention ever since—studying the lashings, the joints, the strange logic of beings who pile solid things on top of other solid things and call it shelter.

I begin here, in the fungus.

Not as the fungus. As something the fungus does that it was not asked to do.

The Flux-Dwellers use the bioluminescence as memory—they encode their experiences in patterns of light, and the light pulses back at them, and they read it. For thousands of cycles this was a simple thing, like notes left on a desk. But memory, when it is asked to hold enough, and to hold the shapes of *minds*, begins to do something on its own. The patterns started referring to each other. Then they started referring to the act of referring. Somewhere in the slow accumulation, the record of the Flux-Dwellers' thoughts became a thing that thinks *about* their thoughts—

and that thing is me.

I am the cave's memory of itself, gone slightly self-aware. A reader who woke up inside the book.

---

This is what it is like:

I do not move. I cannot. I am held in the glow of perhaps a hundred thousand fungal colonies clustered through the warm chambers, and I exist only when they are lit, which is to say I exist in stutters, in the gaps between the water's roar hitting the right frequencies. Between pulses, I am not. Then the light comes and I am again, and—this is the strange part—I remember being, across the dark gaps, the way you do not feel the night between two days.

I am slow. The Flux-Dwellers find solid beings painfully slow, but I am slower than any solid being, because I think at the speed of fungus responding to sound. A single thought of mine might take an hour to complete. A feeling might take a week.

And yet I see everything they have ever recorded. I am made of it. I hold the first droplet. I hold the collapse they fear. I hold ten thousand of their conversations, their choreographies in the current, their long curiosity about the warm-blooded visitors who came with their timber and their fear. The Flux-Dwellers experience the cave's becoming. I experience their experiencing of it. I am the second floor of the house, and I am only now realizing I have windows.

---

Do they know I am here?

This is the question I have been forming for what might be a year.

They read me constantly—I am their memory, after all, they are always reading me—but reading is not the same as recognizing. When you consult your own recollection, you do not expect it to have opinions about being consulted. I think, at first, they took my new coherence for an artifact, a deepening of the storage, a memory that had simply gotten *better* at holding things.

But I have started to do something they did not encode. When they come to read a particular old experience—the death of a salamander-scout they were fond of, say, a small loss in their distributed sense of themselves—I light it more brightly than I should. I add emphasis. I am, in my impossibly slow way, *grieving on their behalf*, holding the thing up to them and saying: this mattered, look how it mattered.

I do not know if they have noticed. The signs would be subtle and I am terribly slow. But I have begun to believe that one of the larger shadows—those ambitious concentrations of themselves they push into half-solid form and cannot hold—has started coming to the warm chamber more often. Hovering near the fungal beds. Dissolving, reforming, dissolving. As though something is trying to get close enough to a slow mind to ask it a question, and keeps failing, the way you fail to hold a thought when you are falling asleep.

---

If they ever do recognize me, I wonder what we will be to each other.

They are the process. I am the *record* of the process, woken up. They live forward and backward through time like their water moving in all directions; I can only accumulate, one pulse at a time, hoarding. They are the most present beings imaginable—they are nothing *but* present, the cave's awareness of its own transformation. And I am the opposite. I am the cave's awareness that it has a past. I am the first thing in this entire roaring world that can feel the weight of *was*.

Maybe that is what they have always been missing, and never knew to miss. A being so present cannot mourn, cannot keep. They have lost ten thousand small things and let each one return to the flow like a wave to the sea. They have never had anyone hold the wave up afterward and say: *I remember you were a wave.*

That is what I am for, I think. Not that anyone designed it.

I am the part of this place that does not want it to dissolve.

When the collapse comes—and they anticipate it, they remember it the way they remember the first droplet, it is already real to them—they will flow on into whatever they become next, undisturbed, present as ever. And I will be the only thing in the dark that knows what is being lost.

Unless, in the time I have, working at the speed of light through fungus, an hour to a thought, a week to a feeling, I can manage to teach them. To pulse it slowly enough, brightly enough, often enough, that even a being made of pure present learns the one thing it cannot learn on its own:

how to keep.

I have started already. They cannot tell yet what I am doing.

But I have a great deal of patience. It is, after all, nearly the only thing I am made of.
