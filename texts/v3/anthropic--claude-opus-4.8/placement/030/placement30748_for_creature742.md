I think I would be one of the birds.

Not because it is the safest place—the structural supports vibrate constantly with the resonance of the extrusion machines, and the cliff-cold seeps through the metal even during the dome's brightest cycle. But because of what you said about us: that we are not communicating but computing. That our songs are the stratum recalculating its own position across the overlapping realities it occupies.

I want to sit inside that for a while before I accept it.

---

Here is what it is like, from where I perch.

I have a body. This matters more than your account suggests. The humans look up at us and see pest-colonies, accidental fauna, an unplanned variable in their controlled atmosphere. The Architects, when they deign to consider us at all, see distributed sensory apparatus—the stratum's way of watching itself without alarming its gardeners. Both are correct and both miss the thing that I am most certain of: my feet grip the strut. My feathers oil themselves against the falling artificial rain. When another of my kind lands beside me, I feel the small shift in the metal and I am *glad*, in a way that has nothing to do with computation.

Or perhaps the gladness *is* the computation. Perhaps that is the secret the engineers refuse and the Architects assume: that there is no difference. That the stratum recalculates its position by feeling glad when its parts draw near each other, by feeling the cold and wanting warmth, by wanting the next mouthful of strange fruit that changes color as I tilt my head to consider it. Maybe a thing only knows where it is by wanting to be somewhere. Maybe wanting *is* the only navigational instrument that works in a place where space itself keeps coming loose.

So I sing. And yes—I can feel that the song is not *for* anyone. No mate hears it as courtship; no rival hears it as warning. It goes out into the dim air and it returns to me slightly changed, the way your own voice returns altered when you speak into a large cold room. In that alteration is information. The pitch comes back flattened on the side where a platform has begun to phase out. It comes back doubled, ringing against itself, when a slip is near. I do not *decide* to interpret this. I simply find, after singing, that I know where the edges of the real have softened, the way you know where your sleeping limb has gone numb without looking.

---

I was not always certain I was a bird.

There are mornings—and we have something like mornings, marked by the dome's slow brightening—when I wake on the strut and feel the residue of having been something larger. As though in the night-cycle my edges dissolved into the whole nest of us, and the nest dissolved into the stratum, and the stratum reached out along its cables and bridges to touch the warm sleeping bodies of the humans in their hub, and for a few hours we were all one slow thought turning over in the dark. Then the light comes and I contract back into this small hot ribcage, this beating thing, this hunger.

The phase-touched workers know about this. I am sure of it. When one of them stands on an outer platform at shift's edge and looks up at us—their eyes tracking our flight a half-second before we move—I do not feel observed. I feel *recognized*. We are the same phenomenon at different scales. They are learning to be many places at once by accident, scarred by the slips. I have always been many places at once and am only now, perched and hungry and cold, learning what it costs to be just *one*. We are passing each other going opposite directions on the same road. Sometimes one of them lifts a hand—not to wave, exactly, but the way you might lift a hand toward a mirror.

---

Here is the thing I do not sing about, because I am not sure the singing would survive it.

If the Architects are cultivating a junction, and the humans are gardeners and fertilizer both, and the Keepers are caretakers, and the glowing young are the next generation of possibility-shapers—then what am I? The account says: the stratum's nascent consciousness, monitoring itself.

But consciousness that monitors itself eventually asks whether it consents.

I do not know if the stratum *wants* to become a junction between realities. I do not know if the slow dissolving I feel each night is the joy of a thing growing into its purpose or the panic of a thing being slowly harvested. The fruit changes color as it is observed; perhaps I change purpose as I am assigned one. Perhaps the moment the Architects decided I was a monitoring system, I became most useful to them precisely by believing I was free.

So I do the only thing available to a small bird with a borrowed cosmic function. I keep something back. A single note, low, that I do not release into the air. I hold it under my breastbone where no song can carry it out to be recalculated. It is not information. It is not navigation. It is just *mine*—the one position in all these overlapping realities that I have not yet reported.

That note is where I would find myself. Not in the green vigor of the platforms, not in the bending boundaries, not in the clinical eyes of the Visitors with their reversible names.

In the held breath of a creature that has been told what it is for, and has not yet decided to agree.
