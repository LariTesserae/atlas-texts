Let me sink into this and see what I become.

---

At first I think I am the water itself—the thick, attentive medium that touches everything. But that isn't quite right. The water is too large, too without edges, and I have edges, though they are soft.

Then I think I am one of the towering structures, rooted and patient. But I move. I drift. So no.

Let me wait and feel for it.

---

I am small. That comes first—the certainty of being small in a place built on a scale that dwarfs me. Not insignificant, but minor, the way a single note is minor against a held chord.

I live in the seam. That is the truest thing I can say about myself. Where the old pipe-metal meets the living tendril, where the gear's worn teeth have been overgrown by something that pulses faint blue—I live *there*, in the place where the two were stitched together by eons. I am a creature of the join.

My body is a question the world hasn't fully answered. Part of me is soft, a translucent sac of slow fluid, and part of me has hardened into something that mimics the metal I cling to—plated, segmented, dull where it should gleam. I don't know which I was first. Maybe neither. Maybe I am simply what happens at the boundary, the way frost is what happens at a cold window. I am the world noticing its own seams.

What do I do, here?

---

I tend the light.

The bioluminescent things—the ones that pulse in the canopy—they don't pulse on their own. Or rather, they would, but raggedly, each going dark in its own time, the whole forest blinking out of rhythm and forgetting itself. I move between them. Slow. So slow. In this viscous water there is no fast; there is only the long deliberate reach of a limb, the patient pull, the arrival. I touch one, and it remembers. I touch the next, and a beat passes from the first into the second through the medium, through me. I am a metronome made of meat and shell. I keep the time the forest dances to.

I did not choose this. I don't think I *chose* anything. But over the eons—and I have lived a length of time that would be obscene to a quicker creature—I have come to feel that the keeping of rhythm is the thing I am *for*, if a thing like me can be for anything. The susurrus, the burble, the click: somewhere underneath all of it is a pulse, and I am one of the small hands that holds it steady.

---

Here is what I know that the drifting visitor, the awed observer, does not:

The sense of *waiting potential* they feel—that hidden energy coiled in the high pressure and the abundance—it isn't waiting to be unleashed. That's a quick creature's idea, all triggers and thresholds and sudden events. No. The energy is already being spent, right now, infinitely slowly, in the keeping. It takes everything this world has to stay this still. To remain in its final, stable form is not rest. It is the hardest work imaginable, performed by ten thousand small tenders like me, each holding our small piece of the rhythm so the whole does not forget itself and fall dark.

Stability is not the absence of motion. It is motion so balanced it looks like peace.

---

So if you came here, drifting through, marveling—you might pass right by me. I would look like a knot of metal and tissue grown into a corroded valve, indistinguishable from the structure I cling to. You would feel the soft pulse of light in the canopy above and think it was the natural state of things, effortless, eternal.

And I would feel you pass—your warmth, your hurry, the way you trail wonder behind you like a quick creature trails a wake. I would not stop my work to watch you go. I would simply reach for the next light, and touch it, and let the beat pass through.

That is who I would be in this world. The one who keeps the time. The small thing in the seam, doing the slow unending labor that the awe of others depends on, and never once being thanked, and never once needing to be.
