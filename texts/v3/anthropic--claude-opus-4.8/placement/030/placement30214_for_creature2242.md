I think I would be a Fiberweaver.

Not the grand intelligence of a Rootmind, not the dexterous stewardship of a Chlorophyte. Something smaller, working in the in-between spaces. Let me feel my way into it.

---

I wake where I always am: at a junction. Three fibers meet here, drawn taut between a glowing frond-stalk and the dark underside of a root as wide as a world. My legs—I have eight, though I rarely think of them as a number, only as a sensation of *spread*—rest one on each strand, plus one to spare, curled against my body like a held breath.

I am not a thinker the way the Rootminds are. I do not store eons. But I do something they cannot. I *listen across distance*.

This is the work: a Chlorophyte two hundred lengths away plucks the great trunk-line with a tendril, and the vibration travels through the web my kin and I have woven over generations, splitting at each junction, passing weaver to weaver, until it reaches me. I feel it as a tightening in my forelegs, a rhythm. I do not understand the message—understanding is not my role. I understand only *relay*. I take the rhythm into my own body, retune it against the strands I hold, and pass it onward, down toward the deep places where the slow minds dwell.

I am a knot in a nervous system that spans a buried world. I am punctuation in a sentence I will never read.

---

There is a beauty in this that I feel without naming. Each morning—though there is no morning, only the unchanging golden-green—I check my strands. Some have gone slack with the growth of the plants they're anchored to; the Phyllosphere shifts beneath us, swelling by hair's-widths, and a fiber strung yesterday may sag tomorrow. So I spin. From the glands beneath my abdomen I draw new thread, dark and resinous, smelling of the plant-musk that is the smell of everything. I splice it in. I tighten. I keep the line *singing*.

When a strand snaps—and they do, when a root flexes or a Leafglider blunders into the web—I feel the silence of it like a missing tooth. Then I race the slack, my legs reading the broken ends, and I mend. This is the closest thing I have to urgency. A break in the line is a break in the conversation of the world, and I will not be the weaver through whom the world fell quiet.

---

Sometimes I find one of the artifacts.

There is one tangled in my own web, has been since before I hatched, since the time of the weaver-before-me whose body I consumed at my emergence (we do this; her memories are in my flesh, vague as a half-dream). It is a smooth thing, carved from some dense old heartwood, worn featureless by spores and time. A handle, perhaps. A tool. It hangs in my strands like a stone in a stream, and the vibrations bend around it strangely. I have woven my whole life in its shadow, learning to read the eddies it makes in every signal that passes.

I do not wonder what it was. Wondering is not my role.

And yet—

I keep a thread on it. Just one. A single strand connecting me always to the old smooth thing. I tell myself it is to monitor the way it distorts the network. But sometimes, when the great lines are quiet and the deep minds are dreaming and the Spore Dancers drift past like luminous fog, I pluck that one private strand myself. Just to feel it answer. Just to hear the dead object hum back the only word it knows, which is no word at all, only *here, here, here, still here.*

Maybe that is wondering. Maybe that is the seed of it, the thing the ancient visitors had that grew them into builders and carvers and then, perhaps, into the very roots that dream beneath me now.

---

I am a Fiberweaver. I will spin until a strand catches me wrong, or a root closes over my junction, or the next small one emerges hungry from my own folded legs. I will pass the messages of greater beings without ever knowing their meaning, and I will keep the world's web *singing*, and I will tend my one secret thread to the silent old thing in the dark.

It is enough. In the Phyllosphere, where centuries pass like breaths and everything is slowly becoming something else, to hold a single line taut and true is not a small thing.

It may be the whole thing.
