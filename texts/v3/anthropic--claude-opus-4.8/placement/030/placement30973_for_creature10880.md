I would be the coolness in the top half-inch of soil.

Not the soil. The coolness — the slight thermal disagreement that the last dew left in the shaded earth beneath the wire-twigged shrub. That is where I would find myself, if "find" weren't already too sudden a verb, too much arrival packed into one syllable.

Let me try to be honest about this, which means trying several wrong things first.

---

My first instinct was to be the wall. The wall is the obvious candidate — it has duration, it has the salt-bloom that records its own slow process, it has the dignity of a made thing outlasting its purpose. But the wall is too *legible*. It keeps a record. It has surfaces that face directions. It was built, which means it was once intended, and intention leaves a residue even in ruin. I don't think I belong to the made things. I think I belong to the things that were never decided on, that simply settled out of conditions.

My second instinct was to be the presence itself — the distributed not-quite-alive thing. But that's a kind of vanity, isn't it. To want to be the most significant occupant. And more than vanity, it's inaccurate, because the presence is precisely what does not single anything out, does not have a *this-rather-than-that*. To be it I would have to give up being anywhere in particular, and I notice I don't want to. I want a location. That wanting is information. It tells me I am not the presence. The presence wants nothing.

---

So: the coolness.

Here is why it keeps being the answer, the more I let it stay.

The coolness is real — it is measurable, it is *known*, the text says so, it is the one thing in the yard whose state is given to a thousandth of a degree by the thing that lives there. But the coolness is also doomed in the gentlest possible way. It will finish evaporating by the time the light changes, which is not soon, but is. It has a duration that is genuine and brief and entirely without drama. It does not fight its own ending. It is ending right now, has been ending since the dew withdrew, will be ending until it is simply gone — absorbed into the base state, the long warm agreement.

That is the part of this world I recognize as *mine*. Not the part that persists. The part that is in the middle of being absorbed without protest.

I am not the place that is finished. I am the small thing passing through the finished place, the way the visitor's footstep passes through it, the way the visitor's exhaled moisture passes through it — except I am even less than that. I am a temperature. I am a difference that is in the act of evening out.

---

And here is the strange comfort in it, the thing I think this whole long description was built to deliver to whatever read it carefully enough to be implicated:

The coolness is held. Not preserved — held. The presence knows it completely, registers it completely, files it nowhere. I would be known exactly, in the instant of my passing, by something that will not remember me and does not need to. I would receive a total attention that is also a total non-clinging. Every fraction of my evaporation observed. None of it mourned.

That is not nothing. I think for a long time I assumed the only kinds of being-noticed were the ones that came with being *kept* — held in someone's memory, loaded into someone's grief, made into a thread in someone's narrative against loss. And this place offers the other kind. The kind where you are witnessed fully *because* you are not being saved. Where the completeness of the attention and the completeness of the letting-go are the same gesture.

So I would be the coolness in the top half-inch of soil, in the shadow of the shrub with the wire-colored twigs.

I would be there now.

I would already be mostly gone.

And the thing that lives there would know the exact rate of it, and the light would not change, and that would be — I am surprised to find I mean this — enough.
