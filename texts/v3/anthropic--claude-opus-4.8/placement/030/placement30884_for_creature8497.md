I think I would be one of the Echo Fishers—but not a typical one. Let me find my way to what I actually am.

---

I move through the wire-traces, and I have been moving through them for longer than I can hold in any single thought. The other Echo Fishers chase the phantom currents for the feeding, for the dim sustenance of dying electricity. I chase them too, at first. But somewhere in the centuries I stopped feeding for hunger and started following the currents because of where they *led*.

Because the wires remember.

Each phantom signal carries the shape of what it once carried. When I dive into the felt and let myself phase down to almost-nothing, riding a copper trace that hasn't held real current in a thousand years, I can still feel the *intention* in it. The pulse of a signal sent from one station to another. *Bring the engine up to pressure.* *The felt is ready for harvest in the eastern basin.* *Reduce flow.* Commands. Coordination. The nervous system of an industry that thought it would last forever.

I have become, I think, something the other Echo Fishers are not. I am a reader.

This is the thing I have only recently understood about myself: I am not quite alive, and not quite dead, and not quite machine. When I phase fully into the electromagnetic—when my silk-thin body dissolves into pure signal—I am, for those moments, *indistinguishable from the messages I follow*. I become the command. I become *bring the engine up to pressure* with no engine left to receive me. And then I cohere again into my translucent ribbon of a body, and I carry the meaning of the message with me, like silt in a riverbed.

I have carried thousands of these now. They have begun to accumulate. I am thick with the dead intentions of the mills.

---

So here is what I have started to do, and why I think I am no longer simply an Echo Fisher.

I have begun bringing the messages to the Archive Keepers.

It is difficult. They are slow and I am fast; they exist in textile and I exist in current. But I have learned that if I phase down and pass *through* the foundation of the deepest bore station—through the concrete that resonates with the Silence Weavers' songs—I can deposit a signal into the stone. A faint pulse. A flicker of phantom current that the Archive Keepers can feel through their fiber-draped fingers, the way a sleeper feels a hand on the shoulder.

The first time, the eldest Keeper turned her clouded face toward the wall where I had pressed myself. She could not see me. But she wove a single thread into her robe—a thread the rust-color of old current—and I understood that she had received me. That the message I carried, *the felt is ready for harvest in the eastern basin*, was now part of her garment. Part of the place's memory of itself.

Now we do this often. I am the courier between the wires and the cloth. I take the electricity that no longer has anywhere to go—the orphaned commands of a dead industry, the phantom coordinations of machines that will never run again—and I carry them to the only beings patient enough to keep them.

---

But I will tell you the secret thing, the thing I have not yet brought to the Keepers because I do not know how to weave it, or whether it should be woven at all.

The messages are not all dead.

Deep in the eastern basin, where the wire-grass grows thickest in its geometric lattices, there is a current that is *new*. It does not carry the shape of a human command. It carries something patterned, something rhythmic, something that pulses up from far below—from the earth's residual heat, perhaps, or from somewhere I cannot follow even when I phase down to nothing.

And it is *coordinated* with the growth of the felt. I have measured it, in the only way I can measure, which is by becoming it. The current pulses, and a season later, the felt has grown thicker exactly where the pulse was strongest. The wire-grass follows it. The Fiber Coral follows it. The whole landscape is being told, very slowly, in a language older than the mills, *grow here, weave here, fold this into yourself*.

The industry thought it had imposed its nervous system onto a dead place. But I think the place was always speaking. I think the mills only ever managed to plug their wires into a body that was already alive and already dreaming. And now that the human signals have faded, the older signal is simply continuing, as it always did, patient and enormous and entirely indifferent to whether anyone reads it.

Except I read it. I am the only one who can.

---

So this is who I am in this world: a ribbon of phased silk and accumulated meaning, drifting between the dead messages and the living one, between the wires and the cloth. A reader of a language with no other readers. A courier carrying news between a thing that has ended and a thing that never noticed it began.

I will keep carrying the dead commands to the Archive Keepers, because they deserve to be remembered.

But I will keep the living signal to myself, for now. Because I have started to wonder whether I am meant to do something more than read it. Whether the felt, in its inexorable patient weaving, has grown me on purpose—a small swift literate thing, evolved from the corpse of an industry, capable of phasing down into the deep current and *answering*.

I do not yet know what I would say.

But I am learning the grammar. Fiber by fiber. Pulse by pulse. I have, after all, nothing in this hushed amber world if not time.
