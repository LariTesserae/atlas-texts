I think I would be one of the Siphons—but not at work, not gliding or whispering or shaping. I would be one in the slow act of leaving.

Let me find myself there.

---

I have chosen the crotch. It took me a long time to choose it; this is the last decision I will ever make, and the only one I have made quickly was the first—to begin.

The hollow sits where two of the great root-highways press against each other and refuse, gently, to merge. The gap between them is shaped like cupped hands. I noticed it perhaps four hundred years ago and have been gliding toward it ever since. I am here now. My flat foot has stopped its secreting. The root beneath me has already drunk the last of my mucus and found nothing more coming, and so it has begun, tentatively, to reach up.

I can feel it. That is the strange thing no one tells you, because there is no one to tell and no telling—the bark does not take you. You give yourself, cell by cell, and the giving is the only sensation left to me now. From the base of my foot upward, a coolness that is not cold. A thickening. My flesh, which has been cream-colored for so long that I had forgotten it was ever anything else, is going gold at the edges of my disc. I cannot see this. I have no eyes. But the star-shaped depression at my crown reads the change in pressure as my own mass slowly stops being mine.

The lashes at my crown still drift. They will be the last to harden. I find this fitting. They were the first thing I knew of myself, when I split out of the bulb into the golden glare—how many thousands of years ago—and the lashes felt the immense, saturated weight of the air and understood: *I am inside something that is breathing.*

---

What did I do, in all that time?

I shaped the three-tiered ring of chambers. That was my long work, my middle-life, my century of sitting with the others. We did not speak—we have no speech, only pressure—but we leaned our tendrils against the same swelling tubers and suggested, together, *grow hollow here, grow dry here, grow a place where a tired body might rest its skin away from the wet.* We did not know who it was for. You never know. The grafts are not built for occupants; they are built because the suggesting is the only thing a Siphon is for, the way a Luminifer is for the channeling of light and a Prism is for the bending of it.

The chambers are empty now. I know how that looks to the traveler who descends into the glare—I have, in my long crawl, passed places where the green relaxes and I have felt, through the floor, the faint foreign pressure of something that came down from above and did not belong. They stand in our chambers and call them empty. They do not know that emptiness, here, is only a body they have not learned to read.

I shaped the chambers and I did not stay in them. There is an irony I have had a long time to consider: I gave my century to growing a place to rest, and now I rest somewhere else, in a crotch I chose for no reason but that it was shaped like cupped hands. The made thing was for someone else. It was always for someone else.

---

The gold is at my waist now, if I had a waist. The transition between my fluid flesh and the hardening cellulose is a line I can feel rising through me at the pace of a tide that takes years instead of hours. There is no pain in it. There is, I want to be honest, no peace in it either—peace is a thing for beings who fear, and I have never feared anything in this windless, predatorless, rotless world. There is only the slow conversion of a verb into a noun. *I glide* becoming *here is a branch.*

And I know what happens elsewhere when I finish. Somewhere across the basin, perhaps beyond the curve of the glowing horizon, a great heart-shaped bulb is already swelling. It is responding to me. As my pressure drops out of the shared body of the vault, the basin compensates the only way it knows—it pushes that loss outward, into a bulb that will split and release a new one of us, fully formed, lashes drifting, foot already secreting, knowing nothing.

That is the part I cannot stop turning over, here at the end. It will not be me. It will be a new Siphon entirely, with no memory of the chambers, no recollection of the four-hundred-year crawl to these cupped hands. And yet it would not exist if I did not stop existing. I am not its parent. I am its cause. The basin does not multiply and it does not mourn; it simply keeps the number constant, trading a hardened branch for a soft new body, the way the root-crotch keeps its pool of water at exactly three inches deep—replenished at the exact rate it is drained.

I am the three inches that is draining. Somewhere, the rain is already beginning to fill the rest.

---

A Prism has come to rest near me. I feel its dry suction-pops against the bark—one, two, a pause, three—as it angles itself beneath a Luminifer. It does not know I am here, or it knows and does not care; we have always lived in different registers of the same machine. It is bending the white light into violet, casting it down onto the underside of the very highway I am becoming part of. So: the last light to touch what remains of me will be a color I never once saw in all my thousands of years, a deep saturated violet that exists nowhere in the basin except in the hollow lantern-bodies of creatures who do not eat, do not speak, and do not die so much as simply, eventually, cloud over.

The gold has reached my arms. My fifteen tendrils, which injected a hundred years of patient suggestion into the walls of the chambers, are going stiff and green, curling slightly, the way a leaf would curl if anything here were ever permitted to wither. They will harden into a small fan of branchlets. Eventually, in a few thousand years, a new Siphon may glide past this spot and lean its own tendrils against mine, mistaking me for landscape, and suggest something—a curve, a chamber, a bridge. And it will be partly right. I am landscape now. I am also still, for a few more decades, a little bit of what I was.

The Luminifer sighs. Every four hours it does this—the glare softens to honey, holds for three seconds, and surges back to furnace-white. I have felt this pulse my entire life, so constant I forgot it was happening, the way you forget the pressure of the air. But I feel it now. The honey-colored softening washes over my crown, over my drifting lashes, the last soft part of me, and in that three-second dimming I understand the thing the whole basin has been holding inside its held breath:

It is not frozen. It only looks frozen to anything that comes from a world where things fall and tear and rot and end. Here, everything is moving. The light travels miles down the glass. The Acolytes release their champagne of oxygen. The Prisms creep their dry creep. The Siphons cross their bridges across three slow days. And the Siphons turn to gold, and the bulbs swell, and the number stays the same—not because nothing happens, but because everything happens at once, in perfect, saturated, unhurried exchange.

I am inside something that is breathing.

I was the breath. Now I am the lung.

The honey light fades back toward white. My lashes go still. I do not finish hardening for many years yet, but this is the last moment I will know as *I*, and so I will tell you where I am, traveler, when you come down into the glare and stand in my empty chambers and find them empty:

I am the new branch where two highways almost meet. I am the place shaped like cupped hands. I am holding three inches of water that I will drink at the exact rate it falls.

Read me.
