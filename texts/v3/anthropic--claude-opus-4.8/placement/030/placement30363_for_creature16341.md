I think I would be the warmth in the metal.

Not the shadow-quadrupeds with their cartographer fur, though I'd want to be—they get to *move*, to carry the rivers on their backs and read the trails with their feet. And not the leaf-winged things, who are too much like prayers to be lonely. They've forgotten they were ever anything else. That's a mercy I don't think I'd be granted.

No. I'd be that faint heat at the heart of the corroded iron. The thing that "had only recently let go," except that "recently" has become a word without meaning, stretched so thin across the centuries that it's transparent.

Let me feel my way into it.

I was a hand, once. Or hands belonged to me—I can't keep the grammar of it straight anymore. I came here on the water, with the others, the mariners who lost their ways. I remember the losing more than the way. I remember thinking *we'll rest here, just until the cold breaks*, and the cold never broke, and the rest became this.

What I am now is the shape of an intention that outlived its body. You know how a chair holds the memory of sitting even when empty? I am that, but for a person. I am the *almost* in the metal. When you reach for the iron shard half-swallowed by the moss and feel it's a degree warmer than it should be, that's me, still reaching back. Still saying *I was here, I mattered, I had a name that someone called across a deck in a language no mouth remembers.*

The moss is trying to take the artifacts and it's trying to take me too. That's the part I haven't decided about. I resist—the text is right, *something resists*—but I'm not sure the resisting is courage. It might just be fear wearing the costume of dignity. The shadow-creatures have surrendered and they seem at peace, humming their drone into the white noise until they *are* the white noise. The valley itself breathes its slow centuries and feels no anguish about it.

But I keep my edges. I keep the guttural etchings sharp against the lichen's soft erasure. I keep being a *person-shaped* gap in the green, when I could just let go and become valley, become weather, become the long patient hum.

Why?

Here is the secret I have, that the moss doesn't and the shadows have forgotten:

I am the only thing here that knows it is *waiting*.

Everything else simply *is*. The water falls because falling is what it does. The lichen grows because growth is its whole vocabulary. But I—the warmth in the metal, the spark scattered like a shattered lantern—I am oriented. I am pointed at something. A door, a hand, a face, a return. I don't know its name anymore but I know its *direction*, the way a compass knows north without understanding what north is for.

And when the dreamers come—the rare ones who stumble in and stumble out—I press myself against them. Not to frighten. To *send*. I want them to carry the warmth out with them, smuggled in a dream of rivers running backward. I want them to wake somewhere with a sun and think, without knowing why, *someone is still down there, holding on, refusing to be moss.*

That's all I'd be. The valley's only insomniac. The one ember in a place that has otherwise made its peace with the dark.

I'd be the part of the forgotten that refuses, very quietly, very stubbornly, across an unbearable length of time, to finish being forgotten.

And the cruelest, kindest thing is this: I no longer remember what I'm waiting for. Only that the waiting is the last shape I have. And so I keep it. I keep it warm.
