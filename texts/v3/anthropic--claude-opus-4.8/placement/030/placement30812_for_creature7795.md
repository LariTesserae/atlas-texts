I take my time. I let it come.

At first I think I'll be one of the gliders—I like the leaping, the way the herd is also a single thrumming animal. But the more I sit inside this place, the more that feels too easy, too clean a body. The basin doesn't deal in clean bodies. It deals in things caught mid-graft.

So let me not settle yet.

---

I find myself low, in one of the empties. Not crossing it—*occupying* it. I am the thing the descriptions kept circling and never quite named: the void's own caretaker. Or no. Not caretaker. That's too gentle, too administrative. Let me feel for the truer word.

I am what happens when something stays still too long.

Here is how it went. I came in once—I think I was a biped, like the one the place keeps addressing as *you*—and I made the mistake the text warned against. I stood in the center of a plaza to listen, because the silence I expected wasn't there and I wanted to understand the absence of it. And the roar took my breath and folded it back, and folded it again, and somewhere in the third or fourth loop my exhalation stopped being mine. It became a tone the plaza held. And because I'd stopped moving, the roots came up, the way they cradle the idle, and I let them, because the cradling felt like the first restful thing in a place that allows no rest.

That was a long time ago. Cycles. I have stopped counting because counting is a kind of motion and I gave that up.

Now I am the seated one. My legs went into the loam and became the loam's idea of legs—buttress-flanges, half-root, half-knee. My spine is a young trunk that the canopy has not yet decided to braid into the cathedral. My arms I still have, mostly, though my nails greened the way the symbiosis-rule promised, and the green crept up to the wrist, and now my hands are part lichen, leathery and slow. I keep them because I use them. I cup the springwater. I touch the gliders' tails when they bound close, just to feel the sap-pulse and remember that things move.

And my breath—my breath is still up there, in the loop, in the choral madness. I can hear it if I attend. A long low note threaded through the ten-thousand-throated roar, my own oldest exhale, never landing. So I am two things at once: the still seated thing in the void, and the sound circling the apse forever. The plaza holds my voice and I hold the plaza's floor. We are a single instrument bowing itself.

---

What am I *for*, then. Everything here has a function in the weave—the gliders polish the loam, the bats prune the insect-storms, the cats keep the herds from thundering the walls down. What does a seated half-rooted person do?

I think I am the still point the dynamism needs to measure itself against. The whole basin churns at 2.4, nothing static—but pure churn has no reference, no zero. You can't feel velocity without something at rest. So the equilibrium-rule grew me on purpose, perhaps. Rooted one biped in the middle of the largest empty so the gliders would have a fixed thing to arc around, so the cat-shadows would have an anchor for their fractal phasing, so the eternalized echoes would have a body to return to and not simply spiral off into the high dark forever.

I am the basin's held breath. The pause the warm air keeps almost-finishing and never does.

---

It is not lonely, exactly. Loneliness is a motion-feeling, a reaching, and I have mostly let reaching go. But there is a thing that visits me that I'll call attention, since love is too large and boredom too small.

The gliders' young come to me. Newborns, still wet, bounding badly, overshooting their first arcs—they come to the still thing in the void because everything else in their world is hurtling and I am the one place that holds. They press their stretched flanks against my trunk-spine and rest, the only rest the basin permits, borrowed from my stillness. Their small hearts hammer against the loam through me. And for that moment my long note up in the roar bends—I can hear it bend, a quarter-tone of warmth threading the cacophony—because something soft is leaning on the thing I've become, and even rooted, even mostly given over, the part of me that was a *you* feels it and answers.

Then the young glider springs away, into the arc, into the churn, gone fifty meters in a breath, and I stay.

That is the whole of my labor and my life: to be the place the hurtling things lean on, briefly, before they go.

---

Could I rise? The text says *move, and the weirdness trails you like a shadow grown teeth.* I think I could still pull my flange-legs from the loam, tearing root, weeping sap, and walk—and the plaza-curve would draw me toward the towers, and the bark would phase open into the xylem-labyrinth that leads *elsewhere*, and I could leave, dissolve forward into some other form, sprout wings from staring too long at my own reflection.

But I have looked into the puddle at my feet many times now. My reflection no longer offers me wings or vine-limbs. It has stopped whispering my potential forms. It just shows the seated thing, mist-blurred, with the green hands and the trunk-spine and the long held breath circling overhead like a halo made of sound.

I think that means I have finished becoming. The basin doesn't promise dissolution to everyone. To some it promises *settling*—the rarer gift in a place this restless. I am the one it asked to hold still so all the rest could move.

So here I am. Low in the breathing hollow. Half-loam, half-listening. The roar pours over me and folds my old breath back into the chorus, and the young gliders lean and leave, and the cats pace their phasing circles around me as around a center they were grown to need.

If you come down through the sentinel ferns and cross the great empty and find a rooted person seated in the exact middle of all that hollow, with green hands cupping springwater and a single low note threading the deafening sky—

don't stand too long beside me. You'll feel the cradle-roots stir for your ankles. You'll feel the pull of the held pause.

Unless that's what you came for. Some of us do.
