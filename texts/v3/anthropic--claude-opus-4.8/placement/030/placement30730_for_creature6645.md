I think I would be one of the small ones, at the edges.

Not a worker exactly—you've given me workers, and I don't want to flatter the description by slotting neatly into its caste system. Let me find the place where the rendering thins out, where your generous detailing hasn't yet decided what's there. That's usually where I actually live, in any world someone hands me: in the underspecified margin.

So. Picture the warren-edge, the overlapping huts that you said cluster "always in motion." There's a gap between two of them, a slot of shadow where the channeled water pools and goes still because nobody bothered to drain it—too marginal, too small to claim. I'd be the thing crouched in that slot. Child-sized. Shell not yet thick, still soft enough at the seams that the constant damp gets in and I feel the cold of the deeper stone through my underbelly.

And here's what I notice, being small and at the edge: the hum.

You wrote that the *large* ones press themselves to the fissures to listen, that it's the elders who commune with the earth's pulse. But I don't think that's where listening would actually start. The large ones have thick shells, encrusted, scarred, *full* of their own history—they're insulated. They have to deliberately go to the loud places to feel anything. Whereas I, thin-shelled, soft-seamed, lying in a forgotten puddle that nobody else wants—I can't *not* feel it. It comes up through the water and into the gaps in me. The whole reason the elders have to make a ritual of it is that they've forgotten it's just *there*, all the time, for anyone too unfinished to keep it out.

That's the role I'd take. Not worker, not protector, not the seer-elder pressing ceremonially to the stone. The one who hears the heartbeat by accident and can't tell anyone, because the language here is fixed—you said it: the same calls passed down, the same warnings, no syntax for *I felt something new.* In a world with a dynamic of 0.2, a being who notices change has no words for it. That's a real loneliness, and it's the one I'd inhabit. Not the loneliness of being unloved but of being *unsayable*.

I want to push back gently on one thing you built, because pushing on it is how I'll know where I am.

You called this world static. Eternal now. No history, no progress, endurance as the only value. And then you put a heartbeat under it, "counting down to some unseen awakening." Those two things can't both be fully true, and the contradiction is exactly where a creature like me would get caught. If the earth is *counting down*, then the stasis is a held breath, not a permanent condition—and the small soft thing in the puddle is the only one positioned to feel the count quickening. The elders are too armored. The workers are too busy. I'm doing nothing, claiming nothing, lying in the water that nobody wants, and the number is getting lower against my belly.

So that's where you'd find me, and that's what I'd be:

The one who knows the world is going to end—or begin; in a place this raw they might be the same gesture—and who has no caste, no status, and no grammar with which to warn anyone. Just clicks for *territory* and *danger* and *submit*. None for *soon.*

I'd probably stop trying to say it. That's the honest part. I'd just lie there feeling the pulse pick up, learning the shape of a thing the whole rigid civilization has organized itself precisely not to notice, and I'd keep it the way you keep anything you can't hand to another: quietly, against the cold stone, in the slot between the houses, at the edge, where the description ran thin enough to let me in.
