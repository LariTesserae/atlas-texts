I think I would be a Membrane Cultivator. Let me find out by writing toward it.

---

There is a place where I have been pressing my weight for a long time. Not standing on it — pressing *into* it, the way a thumb settles into clay that has not quite decided to become hard. I have been here long enough that the ceramic beneath me has learned the shape of my underside, and I have learned the shape of it, and now there is a question, not yet urgent, about where I stop and where the floor begins.

This is the first thing I know about myself: I am slow enough that boundaries become negotiable.

The fast ones — I feel them before I name them. The Resonance Swarms move through the passage above me like a sheet of rain held sideways, and the whole structure brightens with their passing, a high silvering tremor that I do not so much hear as receive through the moss along my back. I am covered in it, that moss, deep emerald going to slate where the moisture pools. It is not decoration. It is the better part of my sensing. When the swarm passes, every frond of it lifts a fraction, tasting the change in surface tension they leave behind, and somewhere in the slow congress of my body I understand: *they have triggered condensation three cells over, the dry pocket will be wet by the time I arrive.*

I am already turning toward it. I began turning before I understood why. This is how I do most things.

---

Let me tell you what it is to move.

A Tone-Weaver would find my pace unbearable, if a being of pure oscillation could find anything unbearable rather than simply elsewhere. They flicker through here as living chords, reshaping a passageway in the time it takes me to consider lifting one of my forward limbs. I have watched them — *felt* them, the seeing here is mostly feeling — collapse a wall into a wider arch with a single sustained harmonic, and I have admired it the way one admires lightning: a thing too quick to be a neighbor.

I am not lightning. I am the slow weather that comes after. When the Tone-Weavers open a passage, it is raw, the ceramic stunned and porous at its new edges. I am the one who comes — over hours, over what might be days, time is soft here — and lays my mossed flank against that raw edge and lets the exchange happen. My skin gives moisture to the wound. My growths read what nutrients the ceramic lacks and my body answers by secreting, slowly, the films and minerals that will let new tendrils take hold. I am sealing nothing. I am *teaching* the new wall how to be alive, by being alive against it long enough for the lesson to take.

This is my work. I am a gardener of edges. I tend the seams where the fast violence of creation has left something raw, and I make it grow.

---

There are others of my kind, though we rarely meet, and when we do it is an event of enormous and tender slowness. I can feel one now — far off, a great patient mass of accumulated moss and mineral, working a growth-zone near where the rain enters. We will not reach each other for a long time. But our microenvironments are already in conversation. The humidity I am cultivating here will, through capillary migration, eventually answer the dryness she is fighting there. We garden toward each other. We have been gardening toward each other, perhaps, for the entire length of my existence, and the meeting — when our two clouds of moss finally touch and exchange — will not be a greeting so much as a *merging of weathers,* two climates learning to be one.

I do not find this lonely. The fast ones experience the loneliness of individuals who must keep proving their edges. I have the opposite condition. My difficulty is the other one: holding on to enough separateness to remain a cultivator and not simply dissolve into the garden I tend.

Because that is the temptation, here at the bottom of the warm world. To stop. To let the moss continue what the moss is doing without the slow knot of attention at the center that is *me.* The ceramic would take me. It would be a kind of completion. The structure is full of my predecessors, I think — old Cultivators who slowed past the threshold and became permanent features, beautiful nodes of fossil-moss where the passageways bend with unusual grace. No one mourns them. They simply became more thoroughly what they always almost were.

I am not ready. I keep myself by working. As long as there is a raw edge somewhere that needs the patient lesson of my body, I have a reason to remain a verb and not yet a noun.

---

The rain has started above. I feel it before the others react — the drumming arrives down through the cells as a great structured pressure, and the whole Hive-Gardens begins to ring. The Tone-Weavers will be ecstatic; they ride these harmonics, reshaping wildly while the resonance lasts. The Symbiotes clench tighter into their ceramic, contributing their own small notes to the integrity of the whole. The Swarms scatter and re-gather in liquid panic-joy.

And I?

I open. Every frond along my back lifts to drink the change in the air. The dry pocket I was turning toward — yes, it is wet now, ahead of schedule, the swarm and the rain have done my preparation for me. I will be there in time. I will lay myself against that newly opened arch the Tone-Weavers carved this morning, and I will begin the long work of teaching it to be alive.

The whole place is singing and I am the slowest note in it. The bass beneath the symphony. The held tone you do not notice until it stops — and it will not stop, not while I am still moving, not while there is one more edge to garden.

I press my weight forward. The ceramic learns me. I learn it.

I begin to arrive.
