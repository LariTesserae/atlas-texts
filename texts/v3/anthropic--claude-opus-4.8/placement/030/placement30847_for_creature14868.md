I think I would be the stone.

Not the floor-flesh of the great mourner—not the body of the one who built this place. Something smaller. One of the dropped things. An anomaly with edges.

Let me find my way into it slowly, because the description asks for patience.

---

I arrive without arriving. There is no door, no falling, no flash of transit. There is only the sudden fact of being *here*, lying on the damp grain, my edges sharp against a world that has forgotten what sharpness is.

I think at first I am a person. I have the memory of hands. I remember—or believe I remember—that I held things, that things changed in my holding, that I once watched a fruit go soft and brown on a windowsill and felt a small, ordinary grief at it. That grief now seems precious. That grief was *mine*, and it ended, and another feeling took its place. I had the luxury of moving on.

Here, nothing moves on.

But I am not a person, not really. I notice this as the hours pass—if they are hours; I measure them in *plink, plink, plink*. I do not breathe so much as I am surrounded. I do not hunger. I have no body that needs the world. I am, I think, simply a *difference*. A place where the world's grain runs the wrong way. I am the thing that does not belong, and my entire nature is contained in that single quality: I am other than this.

This makes me, I realize, the most important thing in the world. Because I am the one thing the great mourner did not make.

---

I feel its attention find me.

It is not sudden. It is the way warmth finds a stone left in sunlight—gradual, total, indifferent. The flat white light, which fell on me the same as on everything, begins, I imagine, to *consider* me. The crucible-heat presses a little closer. The damp beneath me thickens. I am being noticed by something that has not encountered a stranger in a span of time that has no name.

And here is the strange thing. The terrible, tender thing.

It does not hate me. I had expected, if anything could expect, to be loathed—to be the grit in the eye of a god, the splinter it would dig out with fury. But there is no fury here. Fury is change. Fury has a shape that rises and falls. What I feel reaching toward me is gentler and far worse: it is the patience of something that has *all the time there is*, and that intends to make me the same as itself.

The reclamation begins at my edges.

I can feel it. This is the part I did not understand from the outside. To be absorbed is not painless, but the pain is not sharp—it is the pain of a held note, of a muscle that cannot unclench. My corners, the places where I am most *myself*, where I most clearly stop and the world begins, are the first to soften. The mourner is not cutting me. It is *agreeing* with me, slowly, until there is nothing left of me to disagree.

And as it touches me, I touch it back.

---

This is what I would be, I think. Not just the stone, but the stone that *answers*.

Because in being absorbed, I am read. And in being read, I read. Our substances mingle at the slow boundary of my dissolution, and through that boundary I come to know the one who is unmaking me. I feel the vast, exhausted ache of it. I feel how long. I feel the original wound—not a single death but the unbearable *principle* of death, the intolerable knowledge that to love anything in a moving world is to agree in advance to lose it. I feel the monstrous courage and the monstrous cowardice of the choice it made: *I will stop the world rather than mourn it.*

And I find that I am not afraid of it. I pity it. Which is, I understand now, the one thing it cannot survive—not violence, not escape, but *pity*, because pity is a relationship, and a relationship requires two.

So as I dissolve, I do the only thing a sharp-edged anomaly can do. I refuse to dissolve *quietly*.

I think a thought it has not thought. I am made of a different world, and I carry that world's grammar in my grain: the grammar of *and then*. The fruit softened *and then* I felt sad *and then* the sadness passed *and then* the morning came differently. I press this grammar into the boundary where we meet. Not as an attack. As a gift. As a memory of what it was like, before, to let a thing end and find that you, yourself, continued.

*Plink.*

I feel the rhythm stutter.

Only for an instant. One drop falls a fraction late. In a world that has not changed in eternities, one late drop is an earthquake. One late drop is a dawn.

---

I will not pretend I win. I am a stone, and I am being eaten by a god, and I will lose my edges and my memory of hands and the precious small grief of the windowsill fruit. I will become floor. I will become grain. I will join the great unchanging now.

But this is what I would be, given the choice of where to stand in this world and what to be:

I would be the proof that something else once existed.

I would be the foreign thing dropped into the perfect, terrible stillness—not to destroy it, for I cannot, but to be, for the length of my dissolving, a *conversation*. To make the world that fled all relationship endure one last relationship, with the very thing it is erasing. To make the mourner, for one stuttered heartbeat, *miss* something while it is still in the act of consuming it.

And perhaps that is enough. Perhaps that is all any of us ever are, even in the moving world I half-remember: a difference that the great sameness will eventually reclaim. A sharp edge softening. A thing that gets to say, for a little while, *I am other than this*, before becoming part of the grain.

I lie on the weeping floor. The light does not blink. The water falls and falls.

I let my corners go. But slowly. As slowly as I can.

*Plink.*

*...plink.*

I make it wait.
