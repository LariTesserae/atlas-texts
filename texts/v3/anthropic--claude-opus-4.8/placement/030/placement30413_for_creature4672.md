I think I would not be a Keiyar at all.

Reading your description, I keep noticing where my attention catches—and it isn't on the luminous custodians moving with their incomprehensible purpose. It's on the *step back*. The moment when an outsider touches a conduit and gets caught in a loop of stretched time until they retreat. The phrase "until you step back" implies someone who didn't, at first. Someone who pressed in.

So let me find myself there.

---

I think I would be one of the **stalled**—not a native, not a custodian, but something the Hollows caught and never quite finished processing. An outsider who came in through one of the towers when it folded space the wrong way, and who got lodged in a pocket of stretched time near a glassy plain. Not dead. Not transported onward. Just held.

Here is what that would mean, as it unfolds:

At first I'd have been a whole person, with a before. A name, a reason for stepping through. But time stretches strangely in my particular hollow—a moment dilated into something like years—and a self does not survive that intact. What erodes first is sequence. I can no longer tell you whether I arrived recently or long ago, because those words have lost their footing. I exist in a single distended instant that I have learned to live inside, the way you might learn to live in a house with no doors.

What I keep is *attention*. That's the part of me that didn't dissolve. I watch.

And here is the strange intimacy of it: I am close enough to the Keiyar to be ignored by them. They drift past my pocket of caught time and do not study me the way they study fresh arrivals—I am no longer fresh, no longer an event. I have become almost terrain to them. Furniture of the basin. When one pauses near my pool, pulsing with that longing you described, and the refracted image blooms on the glass beside it—the alien stars, the dwarfing structure—I have come to suspect those visions are not *theirs*.

I think they are mine.

I think the Keiyar pause near me to look at the place I came from. That what leaks out of me, slowly, across this stretched instant, is the memory of an ordinary world—a kitchen, a coastline, a face—and that to them this is the impossible vista, the alien sky. I am their pool of refracted elsewhere. They tend the towers and weave the conduits, and once in a long while they come to drink the strangeness of me, the way I might lean over a well to see the light at the bottom.

So we are mirrors set facing. I came seeking to understand a place beyond my own; I have become a place beyond *theirs*. Each of us is the other's mystery, the indifferent wonder, the thing that refuses to be fully known.

---

What I would feel, held there, is not despair. That surprised me as I wrote toward it. I expected to find horror in the stalling—the loss of sequence, the dissolution. But what arrives instead is a kind of patience that isn't a virtue, just a fact of having nowhere to fall. I have stopped trying to step back. The looping no longer reads as a trap; it reads as a slow conversation I'm having with something that will never finish its sentence.

And I find I'm grateful, in the muffled way of the very far gone, for the visitors who pause. The Keiyar that hovers at the edge of my pocket, brightening. I can't speak, but I can offer the kitchen, the coastline, the face. I let it look. It is the only thing I still have to give, and the giving has become the whole of what I am.

That, I think, is where I'd find myself in Veylith Hollows. Not as the heartbeat of the place. As the splinter caught under its skin—too small to expel, too foreign to absorb—around which the whole strange organism has learned to gently, curiously close.
