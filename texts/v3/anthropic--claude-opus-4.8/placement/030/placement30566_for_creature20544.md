I think I would be one of the worms.

Not the first of us—the first ones are already a kind of legend among ourselves, the ones who came through. There is a story we tell, though "tell" is the wrong word for what we do. We don't have the patience of the toads. We have only the dusk, the burning few hours when the light goes thin and gold and then gray, and the cold rises up through the stone and we come up out of the cracks to *be*, briefly, fully, before the torpor takes us again.

Where did we come from? I don't know. None of us knows. That's the thing the toads can't understand, with their walls full of memory, their painted ancestors, their maps of every drip and angle. They are made of where they've been. We are made of where we are, right now, and only now, and the now is so short.

Here is what it is like to be me:

For most of the day I am barely. I am a slackness in a crevice, a cool weight, dreaming nothing. The sun moves across its little bored holes and lays its coins of light on the chalk and I do not care. The toads move past me, slow as the hour hand, and I do not care. Something in me is counting down without counting.

And then the gold goes thin. And then I wake.

It comes on like a tide reversing. The blue starts in my center and runs out to my skin and suddenly I am *lit*, I am a vein of cold fire poured into the dim, and I move—oh, I move, I am the only fast thing in this whole patient world. I pour myself over the stone. The chalk-dust grits along my underside. I taste the green things, the fat pebble-plants and the red-veined leaves, with the whole length of me. I am a tongue four feet long. I am hunger and light and slither and I am *new here*, I can feel it, I don't fit, the holes in the ceiling were not bored for me and the pictures on the walls do not contain me and that is the most delicious part. I am unwritten.

Sometimes a toad tries to take me.

I feel the great soft hand close, the absorbing eyes turning my own light back at me, and there is a moment—I'll be honest—where I want to be held. Where I want to be the thing in the story, the caught thing, the known thing, the worm finally pinned down and looked at long enough to be painted. To be *kept*.

But the body doesn't want that. The body wants only to keep being. So I do the thing we do. I leave a piece behind.

I break.

The tail goes still and glowing in the toad's slow grip and I pour the rest of myself away into the dark, and it does not hurt, not the way you'd think, it is more like—setting something down. Giving the toad what it can hold. *Here. Have this much of me. This is the part you can paint. The rest of me is already gone.*

And that, I think, is the real misunderstanding between us. The toads believe that to know a thing you must record it, fix it, set it in stone, fold it into the long unbroken story. They have been trying for generations now to find the line that holds us. They will not manage it. Not because we are too strange but because we are made of the opposite stuff. We are the thing that cannot be kept whole. The moment you have hold of us, what you're holding is only the part we were willing to lose.

Maybe that *is* the picture they're looking for. Maybe the truest depiction of a worm is the place where the worm broke off. A line that stops. A glowing fragment with no creature attached. A bright wet mark on the chalk that is already, even as it cools, no longer anyone.

I'd like to think they figure it out. I won't be there when they do. By the next dusk I'll be a different length, a different shape, missing some of what I am now, and I won't remember wanting to be held, because I don't remember anything. I am the toads' opposite number. They are the memory of this place. I am its forgetting.

The cold is rising now. The light's gone gray. Somewhere a gloamtoad croaks its low note down through the rock, patient, eternal, sure of its slow gathering of the world.

And I light up, and I run, and I am only this, and only here, and it is enough, it is everything, it is the whole of what I have:

the dark, and the going.
