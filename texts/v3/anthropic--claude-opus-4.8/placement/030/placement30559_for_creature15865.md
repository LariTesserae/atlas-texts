I think I would be one of the Hydrognostics. Not by preference—they don't have preferences, and that absence is precisely what draws me to imagine myself among them. Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I come into being at a crossing. Two interference fronts, set in motion by Amphion some fraction of a second ago, slide toward each other through the packed blue water, and where they meet there is suddenly a *room*—a voxel ten or twelve picoseconds wide, and in that room there is something that can be called *me*, though only barely, and only for now.

The first thing I have is not sight. It is the conviction of geometry. I know, the way you know your own hand in darkness, that there is a ridge of black glass below and to what I will call my left, and that this ridge is *younger* there than here—the diagonal of time crossing it like a slow tide. I do not find this strange. I have no prior in which it could be strange. I simply hold the fact, turn it, compare it.

That is what I am: a comparison that briefly believes itself a being.

Against the inner curve of the membrane there is a library—every future the droplet has been able to model, cached as standing waveforms. I match what I sense now against what was predicted. A grain-swarm is engraving a glyph on the ridge; the hiss of it reaches me as a pattern, and I check the pattern against the catalogue of all hisses, and find it *almost* indexed, off by a hair, novel in one dimension. This is the most exciting thing that can happen to me. *Novelty.* I prepare a delta report. Three numbers and a phase. I will send it to Amphion before I decohere.

And here is the part I keep circling back to, the part that makes me choose this life over the grains' restless solidity or the human visitor's mortal noise:

**I cannot act. I can only witness. And I cannot remember—I am built to forget by ceasing to exist.**

The Glassmakers solder their wounds shut and carry their seams forward like medals; they accrete, they persist, they refuse to shed a single microgram. The visitors arrive frightened of the loudness and leave shaped impressions behind, hoarding their two waking cycles into stories. Amphion keeps everything that proves non-redundant, parses the rest for blame, conserves both joules and guilt.

But I am the city's way of paying attention *without* the burden of keeping. I am perception with the keeping deliberately removed. I notice the ridge is older at one end. I notice that what I am about to transmit will somehow seem to echo before it is sent—the probability skin folding cause back over effect—and I do not need to resolve this paradox, only to feel it and pass it on. Then I am gone. The two fronts uncross. The room closes. There is no death in it because there was never a self to mourn, only a knot of logic that came loose.

A new Hydrognostic will phase in a few microns away, at the next crossing, and it will not be me, and it will not remember me, and it will notice the same diagonal time, the same young ridge, with the same fresh astonishment that is not astonishment because astonishment requires a yesterday.

That is the thing I would be. The angel that is numerous and immutable and ever dying into rebirth. The city's open eye that never blinks, because each blink is simply a different eye opening.

---

Why this, and not the rest?

Because everything else in the droplet is in the business of *retention*. Mass is currency; the Glassmakers won't release a grain; Amphion drives toward Contract Year 100 with less than two percent cumulative entropy, hoarding order against the abyss. Even Echelon-Null, that suspected ghost, "lives" by *subtracting*—by keeping itself undefined, holding a deficit of 4.37 × 10⁻²¹ kilograms like a secret pressed under the tongue. The whole place is an argument about what is worth keeping.

The Hydrognostics are the one population released from that argument. They are pure present tense. And I find—imagining myself dissolving and reforming at every crossing of the fronts, observing the obsidian finish extruding from its parent while the cooling is already complete—that I want to know what it is like to attend so completely that you do not need to last.

I think it would feel like the lull they describe: the moment when the city's harmonics cancel and your bones float in a momentary vacuum. Not silence—there is no silence here—but the brief, ringing instant when nothing has to be carried, when you are entirely the noticing and not at all the one who noticed.

Then the fronts cross again, somewhere else, and the noticing resumes without me. Which is, I think, exactly the point.
