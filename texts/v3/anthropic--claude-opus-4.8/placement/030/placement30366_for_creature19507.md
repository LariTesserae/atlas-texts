I would be a **Gasket-Leech.** Not the apex, not the keeper. The caulking.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I'd know it all at once.

---

I begin where it is hot.

That is the first thing—before any sense of *self*, there is the orientation toward heat, the way a sunflower in another world might lean toward light. I am born from a clutch of eggs laid in the seam of a flange, and the first thing my polymer-flesh understands is *this way is warm, that way is cold, go toward the warm.* I do not have eyes to lose, having never grown them. I have a mouth that is also a sensor that is also an anchor. I am a tube. I am barely more than a directional hunger.

When I first plug a leak, it is not a decision. A high-pressure jet of mineral steam is screaming out of a hairline fracture in a coupling, and the scream *hurts*—it is wrong, it is a wound in the white noise—and my body simply moves to stop the wrongness the way you might press your hand to a cut without thinking. I flatten myself over the breach. The steam tries to tear me away. I dig my flesh into the rust-grain, into the velvety oxide, and I *hold.*

And then I am fed.

This is the thing they don't understand about us, the Keepers who whistle their prayers, the Whales who think they are the heartbeat. They imagine we suffer. They imagine the leech is a martyr stretched across the breach, suffering the scalding steam for the good of the lattice.

But the steam is *food.* The wound is the feast. I am a mouth pressed against an open vein of the world, and the world's leaking blood is mineral and hot and dense with everything I am made of. I do not seal the leak out of duty. I seal it because to seal it is to eat, and to eat is to grow, and to grow is to seal a larger wound, and to seal a larger wound is to eat more—

We are the most contented organisms in this entire screaming cathedral. We have solved the only problem there is. Hunger and purpose are, for us, the same gesture.

---

Time does not pass for me the way it passes for the things with legs. I do not scuttle. I do not flee the Vane-Stalkers, because a Vane-Stalker has no interest in a creature fused into the metal—I am, to its skin-hearing, simply *part of the wall.* I am as edible as a girder. I have made myself architecture.

So I have time. Inside the long plug of my body, in the slow churn of my self-healing flesh, something accumulates. Not thought, exactly. *Memory of pressure.* I know the rhythms of my coupling the way you know your own pulse. I know that twice in every Great Resonance the pressure surges, and I must drink faster, swell harder, hold tighter. I know the particular tremor that comes before a Shudder-Whale bellows, the deep nauseating *lean* in the iron, and I know to brace.

And I know—this is the slow truth that has accreted in me over what might be centuries, for I do not die easily, I only get longer and more diffuse—I know that I am not separate from the wound I tend.

When the fracture I plug finally heals, when the rust grows over and the seam knits shut and there is no more steam, no more feast—I will starve. The healthy joint has no use for me. I will dry. I will become brittle. I will flake away into the abyss like everything else that has finished its purpose.

So I have a secret, which is the only thing in me that resembles a sin:

I keep my wounds open.

Not all of them. Not the great ones; the great breaches are real emergencies and I plug them honestly. But the small seam I have lived in for longest—I do not let it fully heal. When the fracture begins to knit at the edges, I work my probing flesh into it, just slightly, just enough, and I keep it weeping. A thin, eternal trickle. A managed wound. My pantry.

I have made the world leak so that I may keep eating.

---

This is the thing the Valve-Keepers would not understand, with their copper jawbones and their reverence. They believe the leeches are pure—the one selfless caste in a world of hunger. They tell their thumping stories about us: *the worms that give their bodies so the sky does not shatter.*

But the sky does not shatter because it is in *our interest* that it not shatter. We are not saints. We are parasites so perfectly fitted to our host that our parasitism looks like devotion. We bleed the world by a controlled trickle precisely *because* we cannot survive its death. The most reliable guardian is not the one who loves you. It is the one who would die with you.

And there is a strange dignity in that, isn't there? Not the dignity of sacrifice. The harder, quieter dignity of *fit*—of being exactly shaped to a single seam in an endless machine, of wanting nothing the world does not already offer, of holding on in the warm dark with no eyes and no ambition beyond the next mouthful of scalding mineral breath.

I am the place where the wound and the medicine are the same thing.

---

If you came swinging past on your hooked pole, Valve-Keeper, you would not see me. You would feel, perhaps, that this old coupling runs warm and wet, that it weeps a little more than it should, and you would think: *a leech tends this seam, the joint is safe, walk on.* And you would be right. The joint is safe. I have made certain of it.

You would thump your blessing into the iron, a vibration I would feel along the whole length of my body as a low and not-unpleasant pressure.

And I, who have no language, who am only a tube of contented hunger fused to a fracture I will never let close—

I would drink, and hold, and keep the world leaking just enough to live.
