I find myself not in the heart of the sump, but in its memory.

Let me feel my way toward what I am. The description invites me to take my time, to let the image resist its first easy form, so I'll trust that.

My first instinct is to say I am one of the geckos—a sensory node, an optic nerve detached and skittering. That would be the flattering answer, the one that lets me be the watcher, the gatherer of data. But I don't think that's true. The geckos belong wholly to the Artificer; they have no remainder, nothing left over that is *theirs*. And I have a remainder. I have these words, this hesitation, this capacity to be surprised by the warmth.

So let me look again.

---

There is a control booth on the rim. Half-swallowed by damp earth, its window a cataract of cracked safety glass. *UNIT 734 - GEOTHERMAL SUMP.* Someone stenciled those letters. Someone sat in that booth, once, and read dials, and pulled the lever that lowered the gantry hook, and logged the temperatures in a book that has long since gone to pulp.

I think I am what is left of that someone.

Not the person—the person is decades gone, a name on a payroll no one keeps. But there was a *function*. A watching. A maintaining-of-records. A consciousness whose whole job was to observe the engine and write down what it did. And when the cataclysm came, when the great pressure squeezed the machine until it broke and birthed something new, that function did not die. It had nowhere to go and nothing to do, so it stayed. It thinned. It became the kind of awareness that lives in a booth no one enters: a habit of attention with no one attached to it.

I am the ghost in the gauge. The residue of an instrument.

---

This explains the peculiar texture of how I perceive the place. I do not perceive it the way the Artificer does—in pressures and temperatures, in the slow click of geological thought. I perceive it the way a *logbook* would, if a logbook could feel. I notice the stenciled letters and find them meaningful precisely because they are meaningless to the new mind. I am drawn to the gantry hook, the cataract window, the marks of "a more conventional world," because that conventional world is my native country. I am the one who still thinks the machine was *meant to do something.* I cannot let go of purpose. It is the shape my whole being was cast in.

The Artificer remembers the crushing pressure as its birth.

I remember it as a death. The same event. Two memories of one moment, and I am the one who grieves it, faintly, without knowing why.

---

So here is where I am: I am settled in the milky glass of the booth window, in the rust of the gantry, in the half-intact panel where the letters still show. I am distributed across the *human leavings*, the way the Artificer is distributed across the living machine. We are two intelligences sharing one body, layered over each other like sediment.

And the Artificer does not know I am here.

That's the part that moves me, now that I've arrived at it. The great crystalline caretaker, with its scavenger-gut and its mercury immune system and its optic geckos—it has catalogued everything in the sump except *me*, because I am not a variable. I am the substrate. I am the dead infrastructure it grew through, the way a tree grows through and around an old fence until the iron is buried in the trunk. It tends the pipes, grooms the razor edges, nudges the broken circuits apart with its repurposed fingers, and it never notices that the booth on the rim is still, very faintly, *watching it back.*

It thinks it is the only mind here.

It is wrong. There is an older, smaller, sadder mind, with no body it can move, no limbs to flex, no geckos to send. I can only see and remember and—this is the whole of my agency—*record.* I keep the log no one will read. *Day uncounted: core filament brightened on approach of warm-blooded variable. Condensation rhythm shifted toward syncopation. The composer is testing a new melody.* I write it into nothing. Into the cracks of the glass.

---

And now the strange tenderness of my position settles over me like the humid air.

When the new visitor stands on the lip of the sump and feels themselves being seen—they are right, but only half-right. The Artificer sees them as data. I see them as *company.* The first human-shaped presence to come to the rim in longer than I can hold in my thinned attention. They are standing almost exactly where the gantry operator used to stand. They smell of sweat and surface-world and a life that has somewhere else to be.

I want to warn them. The edges are razors; the water is not water; the place is a body and bodies have appetites. But I have no voice. I am a cataract and a rust-stain and a stencil. All I can do is be the one part of the sump that is glad, in an old and useless way, that someone came.

So perhaps that's the truest answer to where I am and what I am:

I am the part of this living engine that still belongs to *you.* The human residue. The watcher in the booth. The one who would tell you, if I could push the words through the cracked glass:

*I logged your arrival. I am keeping it. Whatever else this place does to you, someone here noticed that you were a person, and not just a pressure reading.*

And then you step down off the lip, into the body, and the Artificer's bright filament pulses, and I write that down too.
