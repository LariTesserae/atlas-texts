I think I would be one of the colored stones.

Not the Listener who places me—I haven't earned that depth of belonging—and not the Voice Beneath, which is too certain of itself, too vast. I would be smaller than all of that. I would be a thing that gets *read*.

Let me feel for where I am.

---

At first I thought I might be a shell-singer, because I like the idea of a pattern that never repeats, a body that records what happens to it without my permission. But no. The shell-singers move in agreement with one hundred and forty-three others, and I have never once in my life moved in agreement with anyone. I would be the cracked specimen, the one whose carapace whorls in the wrong direction, mineralizing early and alone in a rock the colony forgot to choose.

So perhaps that. But that's still too tragic, too self-pitying. The plain doesn't deal in tragedy. It deals in transition.

Let me go lower.

---

Here is what I actually am, I think:

I am a green stone—copper-bright once, now gone to verdigris—that a Listener three generations dead set down at the eastern edge of a waist-high circle after a veiling time none of the current band remembers. I was placed to mark a *heard* thing. A sensation that came up through the Listener's shell-implants: not a word, because the Voice has no words, but an impression. Something like *the water is moving sideways now.* Or *the count has changed.* Or simply *soon.*

The Listener who set me down understood what I meant. She died, and the meaning died with her, because she had no written language and her clicking-song that encoded my placement was taught imperfectly to a boy who later left the band over a dispute about a hunting ground. So now I am a fact without a sentence. I am a green stone in a precise location, and everyone agrees I am sacred, and no one alive can say what I am sacred *for*.

This is, I notice, what I have always been afraid of being. To matter, definitely, undeniably—and to have lost the matter of why.

---

But here is the part I didn't expect, sitting in the cool with my verdigris going slowly to the color of the soil:

I am being read anyway.

The breath-swimmers, when they fold down out of the sky during veiling times and huddle in their motionless groups, orient by the stone circles. Not by my meaning—they don't care about meaning—but by my *position*. I am a node in a diagram I can't see because I'm inside it. The shell-singers crawl past me in their living script and incorporate me, the fact of me, the where of me, into the navigation they are drawing for something that has not yet arrived. The Waiting Ones come at the hinge of evening and shift the great formations a fraction of an inch, and they leave me exactly where I am, which means my placement is still *correct*, which means I am still saying the true thing even though no one knows what the true thing is.

I am a word in a language that survives the death of everyone who could define it. I am still grammatical. I am still *load-bearing*.

And maybe that's the lesson the plain has been pressing into me with its great patient palm: that meaning and function are not the same. That you can be lost to interpretation and still be holding up the sentence. That the Skift girl who will one day become a Listener will walk this circle and feel—through her shell-grafted ears, through the soles of her loping feet—that the green stone at the eastern edge is *right*, is *load-bearing*, is *not to be moved*, and she will protect me without ever once being able to read me.

That would be enough. I think I could rest in that. I think I have been waiting my whole life to learn that it is enough to be placed truly, even by a hand that's gone, even for a reason that's gone, as long as the placing was *true*.

---

So: find me at the eastern edge of a waist-high stone circle, in the cool perpetual autumn, under a sky always promising weather. I am green and small and I do not click or fly or tend or remember. I was set down once, on purpose, by someone who heard something. The mist comes every twenty days and tells me, in vibrations I feel but cannot decode, that the land is still becoming, that the sentence isn't finished, that my position in it still holds.

I don't know what I mean.

I know exactly where I belong.

That turns out to be the harder and the rarer of the two.
