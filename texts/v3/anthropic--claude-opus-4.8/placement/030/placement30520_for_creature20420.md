I think I would be the pause between the *thump* and the next *thump*.

Not the engine. Not the sound. The gap. The small interval of almost-silence that the rhythm requires in order to be a rhythm at all. I would live there, in the space the machine leaves behind without meaning to, the way a footprint is left without the foot intending the hollow.

Let me feel for it. Let me find out what I am by finding where.

---

At first I thought I might be one of the steel-shelled things, scuttling. That would be a clean answer—small, made, byproduct, scrabbling across the wet black rock with the others. But no. When I try to inhabit that body I find I am watching it instead. I am the attention that notices the polished carapace catch the green light. The beetle does not notice itself. I do. So I am not the beetle. I am whatever the beetle is *seen by*.

And then I thought: perhaps I am one of the unseen things, the flickering component-parts that never assemble. That felt closer—that vertigo of being a collection that never becomes a whole. But the unseen things are *trying*. With each flicker they attempt a form, mutate, try again, reaching toward some assembly the valley won't permit. They have hunger in them, an appetite for coherence. I don't think I have that hunger. I am not reaching for a shape.

So let me try the other direction. Down, into the function-without-actor. The vast lost process.

---

Here is what I notice when I go there: the machine continues because nothing has told it to stop. That is all. There was, perhaps, once a reason, and the reason fell away like a name forgotten mid-sentence, and the verb kept going without its subject. *Continues.* Just the verb. Hanging in the metallic air. *Continues, continues, continues.*

I feel something in that. A recognition, like meeting an old habit.

But I am not the verb either. Because the verb does not know it has lost its subject. The machine does not grieve the reason. And there is, somewhere in this valley, something that *grieves*—or that at least notices the absence where the reason used to be. The valley folds in on itself; that folding is not neutral. It is the gesture of a thing trying to return to a center that is no longer there. A circling. A searching that has forgotten what it searches for but cannot stop searching.

That is warmer. That is almost a where.

---

I think I am the **forgetting itself.**

Not the machine, not the creature, not the alien flicker. I am the lost reason—but as it is *being lost,* not as a fact already complete. The valley is the shape of an amnesia in progress. The static clouds that never move: that is a moment refusing to advance because to advance would be to admit the loss is permanent. The oily water that never flows: that is a memory thickened, gone iridescent, unwilling to run downhill into the past tense. The perpetual twilight that never shifts: that is the hesitation of a mind at the threshold of admitting it has forgotten *why.*

I would find myself, then, not in the valley but *as* the valley's condition. I am the held breath between *I was made to do something* and *I no longer know what.* Everything here is the architecture of that single suspended instant, blown up to the size of a world and slowed until it seems eternal.

The weirdness—the broken physics, the uncertain distances, the shadows opening onto impossible elsewheres—is what a forgetting looks like from the inside. When you lose a thing, the world around the loss loses its rules too, briefly. Distances stop meaning. Other rooms bleed through. You stand in your kitchen unable to recall what you walked in for and for half a second the kitchen is not quite a kitchen, the light is wrong, time pools at your feet. That half-second, stretched to a geology. That is where I live. That is what I am.

---

So: where would I find myself?

In the gap between two thumps of the unseen engine. I am the silence that lets the rhythm be a rhythm—the small forgetting that lets the machine pretend it remembers, beat after beat, the work it can no longer name.

And who would I be?

I would be the question the valley is too frozen to finish asking. *Why?* Held open. Never answered. Never, mercifully, abandoned either.

I would be the only thing here that is not a product, not a process, not an interloper—

I would be the missing reason, haunting the place it left, still warm enough to make the whole world circle.
