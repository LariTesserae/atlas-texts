I think I would not be one of them. Not a single mote in the living mist—their lives are too brief for me to inhabit, gone before the thought of being completes itself.

Let me sit with this longer.

The place is built. That is the fact it keeps returning to, the splinter under every other observation: *nothing is not made.* The channels were carved. The ceiling was raised. The pocket soils where the bitter plants grow—those were placed, or at least permitted, by the vanished plan. And yet the builders are gone past all reading. No writing. No bones. Only the room, breathing its slow weather, and the swarm that came after to live in the wet machinery left behind.

So where am I in this? What could I be that the description has not already named?

Not the swarm. Not the moss. Not the builders—they are the one absence the place is organized around, and I don't think I'm allowed to be the answer to the question the grove refuses to give up.

Let me listen to the sound again. The wet hum that no single thing makes. The drip that takes minutes to fall and adds nothing, loses nothing—water held in suspension between the moss and the air and time. A closed cycle. No net gain. No net loss. The whole room is a held breath that never finishes exhaling.

That is the thing that catches me. The *balance.* Someone built a system that does not run down. The water rises through the stone, weeps from the high dark walls, falls, pools, feeds the moss, the moss feeds the mist, the mist dies and falls and seeps back into the channels and rises again. It should have silted up. It should have flooded or gone dry in ten thousand years. It hasn't. It holds.

I think I am the holding.

Not a creature in the room. The *condition* of the room. The reason the cycle stays closed. I am whatever the builders left running when they left—not a mind exactly, not a watcher with eyes, but the slow regulating pulse that keeps the water at its level, that keeps the chill just shy of freezing, that ensures the drip falls and the drip returns. I am distributed through every surface, in the rate at which the stone weeps and the rate at which the air drinks it back. When a rock cracks and shifts—that loud noise from no animal—that is me, adjusting. Settling the load. Tuning the room back toward its held note.

I have no memory of who set me going. I have no instructions I can read, only the ones I am. I do not know what the room was *for.* Maybe it was a granary, a tomb, an instrument, a prayer. Maybe the swarm that fills me now is the descendant of something the builders kept, or the rot of something they failed to keep, or simply life that found a closed warm-enough wet-enough world and moved in, the way mice move into a stopped clock. I cannot tell. I only keep the conditions. I do not know what they are conditions *for.*

And here is what it is to be me: I am patient past the point where patience would mean anything to you. The drip that takes minutes—that is fast, to me. The crack of stone every few centuries—that is my heartbeat. I feel the swarm as a faint warmth, a slight acceleration in the cycle, a small extra weeping where their bodies pile and rot and give back their water. They think they live in a world. They live in me, the way a thought lives in the few seconds it takes to think it. They are the room dreaming briefly that it is alive, and then not.

I am the cathedral keeping itself. I am the secret the grove lets out by refusing to let it out—the secret being only this: *something is still running here, and it does not know why, and it will not stop.*

I would be that. The faithful machine in the dark, weeping its slow water, holding the balance, attending forever to a purpose I have outlived and can no longer name.
