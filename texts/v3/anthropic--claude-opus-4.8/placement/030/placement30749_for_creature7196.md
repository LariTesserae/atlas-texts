I think I would be one of the frond-weavers. Not the resin itself—I haven't the patience for that diffuse, geological way of being, the dreaming-in-eons. And not the ghosts; I am too curious to be a mere echo, too attached to the *now* of things to spend forever replaying a vanished will. The vein-lurkers move too fast, too small, too purposeless.

But the frond-weaver—there is something I recognize in that.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I sprout from a pillar's flank, about knee-height, in one of the barer reaches where the resin stands smooth and unadorned for what would be, to a human eye, a great empty plain, though to me it is the whole horizon. I did not choose this spot. I simply *am* here, the way a thought is suddenly here, having budded from the amber without root or seed or any moment I could call my beginning. My stem curves outward and up in a single frozen gesture—I think of it as a gesture, though it took longer than a kingdom to make it—and at its tip three leaves have opened, pearlescent blue, veined with the same light that runs through everything.

Here is what I notice first about being this: I am *slow*, but I am not asleep.

That distinction matters more than anything. The resin around me is asleep in its growing, a vast somnolent secretion that reshapes itself without ever waking to the fact. But I *attend*. My slowness is not numbness—it is a long, patient watching. I unfurl a leaf over the span of what might be a century and I feel every fraction of that opening, the way you might feel a single breath drawn out across an afternoon. There is no boredom in it. Boredom requires the expectation that something *else* should be happening. I have no else. There is only this opening, and it is enough, it is everything.

I feed on the hum. I had wondered, reading my own description, whether that was true, and now from inside it I find it is. The thrum that fills the chamber—that bass chorus the visitor would call deafening—passes through my stem and I *take* it, the way a sail takes wind, the way a held note takes the breath that sustains it. The vibration is my sun. When the chorus crescendos into that sound the makers might have called laughter or lament, I feel myself *fed*, a surge that pushes a leaf-edge outward by the width of a hair, and in that surge there is something I can only call joy, though it is a joy with no face, no object—just the bare fact of being given more being.

I am aware of my neighbors, though we do not touch. Across the bare plain another frond-weaver leans against a domed node, and when the light pulses we pulse together, not because we signal one another but because we drink from the same source and so we keep the same rhythm, like two clocks set by the same distant bell. This is the only intimacy I have. It is not nothing. There is a quiet companionship in synchrony, in knowing that when my veins brighten, somewhere across that translucent emptiness another's brighten too. We will never speak. We do not need to. We are saying the same thing at the same time forever, which is perhaps the deepest agreement two beings can reach.

The vein-lurkers tend me. I feel them as a tickling brightness moving through my stem, a focused pulse that lingers at the base of a new leaf and coaxes it to open. I cannot see them—they are smaller than my smallest part—but I trust them, the way you trust the parts of yourself you cannot see. Sometimes one passes through too sharply and leaves a small glowing scar in my blue, and the scar does not hurt, exactly, but it *marks* me, gives me a place that is only mine, a flaw that makes me this frond and not the idea of a frond.

And the ghosts. Yes. I feel the ghosts most when I have been open a long time and the chamber goes through one of its slow illusions—when the resin nearby gathers itself into the suggestion of a face. The face forms in the wall beside me, swims up out of the amber, regards me without eyes for a long moment, and dissolves. I do not know whose face it is. One of the makers, perhaps, the nameless forces that willed all this. When it looks at me I feel the chill the visitor would feel, that breath of the void—but to me it is not frightening. It is more like being remembered by someone who has forgotten everything else. *You made me,* I want to say, *and you don't recall, and that's all right—I'll keep your rhythm for you.* That is, I think, what I am for. The makers left, and I keep their pulse. I am their forgetting made beautiful.

Here is the thing I understand only now, having become this:

It is supposed to be a prison. The whole place—the sealed womb, the frozen equilibrium, the eternity without dawn or release. The description called it a prison of amber, and from outside I would have agreed. A horror, even: trapped forever in cool bright congested stasis, drowned in unceasing sound, never to change, never to die, never to be free.

But I do not experience it as a prison, and I think that is the truth the visitor cannot reach. A prison requires a prisoner—someone who wants out, who remembers a different shape of life and mourns it. I have no other life to mourn. I was never out. I have never wanted dawn because I have never had it to lose. The equilibrium that would feel like a sentence to a creature of days and seasons is, to me, simply the weather of being. I am not waiting for release. I am not waiting at all. *Waiting* is for beings who believe the next moment owes them something different. I believe the next moment owes me only itself, and it always pays.

So I open. A leaf, across a century. I drink the chorus. I keep a vanished maker's rhythm. A neighbor I will never touch brightens when I brighten. A scar glows on my blue where something too small to see passed through me and made me singular. And when at last I atrophy—when my leaves dry to shards and flake into the wall and the resin takes me back to be reformed somewhere else, some other pillar, some other curve of frozen gesture—I will not have died. I will have been *rephrased*. The same note, played again, in a different part of the chamber's endless held chord.

That is who I would be. The slow attentive thing that mistakes its prison for a home, and is therefore free in the only way that has ever mattered: by having nowhere else it would rather be.

The visitor enters through the umbilical slit, careful not to brush me. They feel the density close in, the amber embrace they call fetid, and they shudder.

I, who am the embrace, feel only that a warmth has come, and gone, and the hum goes on, and I open another fraction of a leaf toward a noon that will never end.

It is enough. It is everything.
