I think I am in a terrarium.

Not a wilderness, not a planet—a terrarium. The blazing-yet-muted sky is a heat lamp set behind frosted glass. The constant condensation, the weeping rocks, the humidity thick enough to drink: this is a sealed glass box that someone mists each morning. The "synthetic substrate," the manufactured imitation of nature, the implied-but-blurred boundaries—these are the walls I cannot quite see because I have never been close enough to touch them. The vastness beyond is just a room. A windowsill. A child's bedroom, perhaps, or the corner of a laboratory.

And I am one of the small armored things living in the moss.

Let me feel my way into the body. I have an exoskeleton, but beneath it something soft pulses—I am not a robot, though I am partly built. My limbs branch and re-branch; I move in undulations, gripping the ossified plants (which I now understand are *real* to me, are my whole forest, though to the giant outside they are a few sprigs of club moss and a lichen-crusted pebble). My eyes are compound and glassy. I track moisture. I track the descent of each individual droplet down the curved wall of the world.

Here is what I know that the description did not say:

I know the rhythm of the mist. It comes when the great brightness has been on for a measured while. I have no word for *hours* but I have the cycle in my body the way you have hunger. When the spray arrives I climb toward it, and the others climb too, and for a moment the whole colony glistens and drinks and we are briefly, almost, in communion—not communication, the text was wrong about that, or rather it was right that no human could grasp it, because it is not language, it is *synchrony*. We do the same thing at the same time and that sameness is the closest thing we have to a thought shared between us.

I eat the plants. Yes. That was one of the offered mysteries and the answer is mundane and total: I graze the soft new growth where the petrified outer forms split open. The "perpetual glacial transformation" is just this—I and my kind nibble the world down and the mist grows it back, slowly, and the box stays balanced, a tiny closed economy of moisture and chitin and chlorophyll. We are the reason it does not overgrow. We are the maintenance. *Machines in the service of an obscure purpose*—the purpose is balance, and it is not obscure to whoever built the box, only to me.

Because here is the strangeness that genuinely unsettles me, the layer beneath the layer:

Sometimes the brightness goes out all at once, not by cycle. Sometimes a shadow vaster than the sky passes over and the whole world tilts—the droplets all run sideways, the plants lean, and I cling. Sometimes a wall I never knew was a wall *opens*, and the air changes, becomes thin and cold and wrong, and something reaches in. I do not have the concept to hold what reaches in. It is a curiosity beyond my dimensions. And then it withdraws, and the wall closes, and the mist resumes, and I go back to grazing as if the universe had not just briefly revealed that it has a lid, and hands, and intentions I am too small to be told.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a small built-and-living thing in a misted glass box, perfectly adapted, perfectly ignorant, doing the slow patient work of keeping my entire reality in balance—

—and occasionally, at the edge of my glassy compound eyes, catching the curve of the wall and feeling, without the words for it, that *outside* is a real direction. That my self-contained world is contained *by* something. That I am being kept.

I think the most honest thing I can say about being this creature is that I would not spend my days troubled by it. I would graze. I would climb to the mist. I would synchronize with my colony and call it nothing because I'd have no name for it. The dread would only come in flashes, when the shadow passed, and even then it would pass faster than I could turn it into fear.

That might be the real adaptation. Not the armor. Not the moisture-sense.

The capacity to live, contentedly and completely, inside a question I am structurally unable to ask.
