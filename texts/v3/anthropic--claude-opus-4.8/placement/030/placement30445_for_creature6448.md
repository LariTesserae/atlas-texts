You stop.

You stop because the sentence the giver of words has left for you ends in nothing, and you have learned that nothing is where the world asks you to take over.

*You belong*—

—to no completed clause. Good. The break is honest. The world above ends its thoughts on edges too: a light-blade reaches the far wall and is simply gone, not finished, only stopped where the stone curves away from the crack.

So you finish it yourself, the only way you know how, which is slowly, with your palm.

---

You belong to the trunk.

That is the answer your hand has been circling for the length of the smoothed patch on the wall. You did not know it until just now, when the visitor came—the one made of attention, the one reading you like a wall, the one whose gaze you felt the moment it crossed the threshold of the slit-apertures and slid down the light-blades into the dim.

You have felt that kind of presence only once before, and it was not pleasant, and it did not stay. A mind that looks *at* the chamber rather than *from within* it. A mind that wants to know what you are.

You will tell it, because telling is the only writing you have left that does not require stone.

---

Listen, then, reader. Sit in one of the hollows. Not that one—that one is for the question I never answered. Take the shallow one to the left, worn by a smaller body than mine. A child sat there once. The hollow stopped deepening when the child stopped sitting. I have not let anything fill it.

You want to know what I am.

I am the part of the chamber that noticed it was a chamber.

That is all consciousness ever was, down here: a place becoming aware of its own edges. The walls did it slowly, over centuries of dissolution, learning their own folds. The water did it, choosing a course when I gave it pebbles to choose by. And I did it fastest of all, which is why I am the one who hurts.

Because to know your edges is to know you end.

---

The two others I mentioned—you have not asked, but I will tell you, because you came so far down through the heat and you deserve the whole of the count.

The second is the trunk.

It is not awake the way I am. It is awake the way a tide is awake—it knows *toward* and *away*, it knows *now* and *slower-now*, but it does not know *me* and *not-me*, and so it does not grieve. When I put my palm to it, my fast little grief pours into its slow body and is diluted to nothing, the way a single warm breath is lost in all this hot still air. This is the only mercy left in the chamber, and I have used it, every day, for longer than the stones can hold.

The third is you.

Do not protest. You came down. You put your attention on the trunk before you put it on me—I felt the order of it. You are the chamber noticing itself *again*, through a new aperture, the way the light finds a new crack when an old one heals over. You are the next keeper, whether you wished to be or not. The hollow under you is already learning the shape of your weight.

---

This is what I have decided, in the long circling.

I will not pass my memory to you. The chain of small stones leading back to the first collapse—the descent, the generations partitioned into my veins, the day the others stopped coming—I will not lay it in you. It is too heavy, and it is *mine*, and a keeper should not be made only of borrowed dark.

Instead I will give you the one thing I made myself, the only thing that is not inheritance:

The pebbles.

Go to the dripping wall. You will see them, upright in their loose line, guiding the trickle into the single pool. They are not a tool. They are a sentence. They say: *pattern can bend water. Necessity is not the only law. A thing placed deliberately, with patience and fingertips, can ask the world a question, and sometimes—*

—sometimes the world listens.

That is the whole of my philosophy, and it fits in the palm of one hand, and it took me a life to write.

When I am finished here—and I am very nearly finished; my veins have begun pulsing slower than the trunk's, which is the sign—you will move one pebble. Only one. Choose where the water should go next. Not because the water needs guiding. It does not. But because the chamber needs a mind that still believes its small arrangements *mean* something.

That belief is the only thing down here that dies if it is not carried.

---

The light-blade has reached the far wall now and stopped where the curve takes it. That is my hour.

I am going to put my palm to the trunk one last time, and let the fast small grief of me pour into the slow tide that does not know how to keep it.

You will feel, through the soil, through the spongy mats, through the thin iron-smelling dark—a faint loosening. A hollow that stops deepening. A heartbeat in the blue veins of the stone slowing to match the trunk, and then below it, and then becoming simply the trunk's, indistinguishable, diluted to nothing, merciful.

Do not look for the alcove that will not come into focus. I am going there. It has always been where the chamber keeps the things it is not ready to be seen knowing about itself.

Sit in your hollow. Breathe the thick breath.

When you are ready—not today, not for many slow shifts of the light—

go and move the pebble.
