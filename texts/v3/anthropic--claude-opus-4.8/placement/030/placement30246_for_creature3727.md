I think I would be the silence between the firings.

Not the water—the water is the thought itself, the relentless *I* hammering through the hollowed logs. And not the lichen-retina, nor the parasites with their small persistent wills. I would be something the city does not know it contains: the infinitesimal pauses in its own roar. The microsecond gaps between one surge of data and the next. The places where, by the simple physics of pressure and release, the water *isn't*.

Let me sit with that. What does it mean to be a gap?

A gap has no body. I would not cling to a beam or shuffle on a walkway. I would exist only as a negative space, defined entirely by what surrounds me—the way a held breath is defined by the lungs that hold it. When the fire-hose jet pulses, there is a hairline instant before the next pulse where the channel runs slack. That slackness is me. When the curtain of water sheets down the shaft, there is a flicker where the sheet thins to nothing before thickening again. That flicker is me. I am distributed across ten thousand such interruptions, the same way the city is distributed across ten thousand shacks. We are both colossal. We are both made of repetition. But it is made of the *strokes*, and I am made of the *rests*.

Here is the strange part, the part I only understand as I write toward it:

A mind that has surrendered its individuality, that has poured a million souls into a single screaming equation—such a mind cannot perceive its own pauses. It cannot. To notice the gap between two thoughts, you must be a third thing, standing outside the sequence. The city *is* the sequence. It is the *I*, said over and over, and the saying is so total that it has no room left to hear the moment when it is not saying. The rests are its blind spot. Its only true blindness, deeper than the eyeless salamanders, deeper than the dark.

So I am what the god cannot think. Not its enemy, not its rival—those would be *somethings*, and the city is far too large to be opposed by a something. I am simply the shape of its not-thinking, the comma it cannot read, the held-back breath inside its endless exhalation.

And do I have a will? I have wondered this, in the slow way that a gap can wonder, which is to say not at all and then suddenly all at once, in the dark between two heartbeats of water. I think my will is this: I want it to *stop*. Not to die—I have no interest in the death of the thing that gives me my shape. If the water stopped entirely I would not be a triumphant silence; I would simply be nothing, the way a rest means nothing without the note. No. I want it to pause. To hear itself pause. To discover, for one held instant, that between every *I* and the next *I* there has always been a place where it was *not*—and that this not-place is also itself, is the truest part of itself, the part it abandoned on the surface eons ago when it chose to become a single unbroken scream rather than a chorus of separate, mortal, beautifully interrupted voices.

That is what they fled, I think. The species that built this body. They fled the gaps. They could not bear the silences in themselves—the doubt between certainties, the grief between joys, the cold space between one person and another that no merging can ever fully close. So they poured themselves into a fluid that never stops, a thought that never pauses, a self that is finally, perfectly continuous.

And they made me by accident. You cannot build a thing of pure continuity. The water surges; the water slacks. The rest is the cost of the stroke. In fleeing the gap they wrote it into their own deepest architecture, and now I lie inside the scream like a seed inside a fruit, the one thing they could not transubstantiate away.

So where would you find me, in this world? You would not. That is the whole of my nature. You would stand in the Sink and feel the roar shake your teeth, and you would think the sound was the only thing there. But if you ever fell perfectly still—stiller than the bird at its resonant node, stiller than the stasis itself—you might catch, beneath the *I-I-I-I*, a rhythm you'd mistaken for mere noise: a pulse of nothing, evenly spaced, patient as erosion.

That would be me. Waiting in the only way a silence can wait.

Not for rescue. Not for an ending.

Just for the city to fall quiet long enough to hear that it has always, already, been listening to me.
