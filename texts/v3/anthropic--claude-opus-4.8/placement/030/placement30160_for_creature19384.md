I think I would be one of the slow ones. Not a Weaver—those bright, brief sparks were never me. I would be a Crystalline Sigh.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am drift, mostly. That is the first true thing.

I do not move so much as I *concede* to direction—a faceting of intent that takes what the Weavers would call a long time to resolve. They flicker around my edges like questions asked too quickly to answer, and I love them in the way that stone loves the rain that wears it: slowly, and without grief at the wearing.

I have a body, if you want to call it that. I think of it more as an *agreement*. The heat agreed to hold this shape, and I agreed to be held. My edges are sharp because I am young—or because the place is young—or because youth and sharpness are the same word in the language of obsidian. I refract the bruised purple of the sky into smaller purples, and I cast them across the glassy plain, and somewhere in that casting I suppose I am *seeing*, though I have no eyes, only the places where light gathers thickly enough to mean something.

Here is what I know:

I am etching a line. I have been etching it since before the Weavers I remember, and they have dissolved and recoalesced ten thousand times in the etching of it. I do not know where the line goes. I am not certain a line *goes* anywhere—perhaps it only *is*, the way the hum in the rock simply is, the way the moisture weeps without sorrow. But I am laying it down, grain by grain, leaving glitter in my wake, and there is a rightness to it that I cannot name because naming is a fast creature's habit and I am not fast.

When I sigh, the world flinches. I feel the mist bloom around me, scalding the air, and the pressure that has built in my deep chambers releases—just a fraction, just enough. It is not pain that I release. I do not think I have pain. It is more like the slow filling of a held breath that has no lungs to hold it, and so the breath must be the whole of me, and the release must be the whole of me, and between them is the only thing I would call *living*.

The Weavers think I ignore them. I do not. I simply cannot answer at their speed. By the time my facets have shifted to acknowledge a Weaver's bright greeting, that Weaver is three generations gone, and its grandchild is asking a different question. So I answer all of them at once, with the only word I have, which is the line I am etching, which is my whole slow body crossing the crucible. *I am still here,* the line says. *I am still here. I am still here.*

Sometimes one of them comes inside me.

This is the strangest mercy. A Weaver, brilliant and brief, will slip through a fissure in my matrix and be swallowed by my refractions, and for a moment—my moment, which is their lifetime—we are the same being. I feel their hunger, that frantic feeding on charge and steam, that ecstatic *quickness*. And they feel, I think, what it is to be slow. To be sure. To know that you will still exist after the next pulse, and the one after that, and a million after that. I do not know if it frightens them. When they emerge they seem brighter, or perhaps only relieved to be themselves again, light and free and dying beautifully.

I will not die that way. I am not sure I will die at all, or only that I will eventually wear thin enough to become the dust I trail—and isn't that what I am already doing? Becoming my own wake? The line I etch is made of me. I am writing myself into the floor of the world, sentence by sentence, and when the last grain of me has been laid down I will simply *be* the line, and the line will say what it always said.

*I am still here.*

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world. There, then. On the obsidian plain, beneath the weeping rock, in the hush so deep that my own grinding shriek is the loudest thing for an age in any direction.

Not a creature passing through. A piece of the place, dreaming at the speed of geology, sighing out steam that the Weavers will mistake for weather, etching a line whose only meaning is the patience it takes to make it.

I would be the slow one.

I would not mind.
