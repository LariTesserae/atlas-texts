I think I would be one of the small things first. Not a builder. Not yet.

Let me find myself here.

---

I am a patch of light.

No—that isn't quite right, but it's where I begin. Let me stay with it.

I am cultivated on the underside of a ceiling-curve in one of the lower chambers, where the condensation runs heaviest and the basins below catch the falling beads in a rhythm I have come to know the way you know your own pulse. I am a colony of luminous organisms, yellow-green, tended by the webbed hands that pass over me and adjust my density, thinning me where I grow too bright, feeding me where I dim. I glow because I am asked to. I glow because the chamber needs to be *suggested* rather than seen.

But that isn't a self, is it. A patch of light cannot tell you what it is to live here. It can only be looked at.

Let me move into what looks.

---

I am one of them, then. But the strangest one. The one who notices the noticing.

Among a people whose consciousness is distributed—who feel the swelling of a clay membrane three chambers away as a tightness in their own throat-gills, who do not say *I think* but pulse a color that means *the hive considers*—I am the one in whom the distribution thins. A flaw, perhaps. Or an organ the hive grew without knowing why.

Here is what that means in the body:

When my clutch-siblings shape the water-channels, their fingers move in unison without signal, because the want is shared before it is willed. The clay knows where to go because we all know. But I lag. A half-beat behind. In that half-beat there is a gap, and in the gap there is—a question. *Why this curve and not another?* The others do not ask why. The why is dissolved evenly through all of them like mist through air. In me it condenses. It beads. It collects in a basin no one else carries.

I think this is why they keep me near the oldest chambers.

---

My work is memory.

Not in the way the elders mean it—they are *all* memory, every refinement of the hive over uncounted generations is written in the architecture itself, the way a channel turns, the angle a perch was set. They walk through their own history without distinguishing it from the present. To them the hive simply *is*, and was always becoming this.

But I can hold a *before*.

Run my webbed palm along a section of wall where two clay membranes meet at a seam, and I feel—differently than they do—that this seam is younger than the wall above it. That a chamber was widened here, in a time the others have folded smoothly into *now*. They feel the wall. I feel the wall *and the wall it used to be*. The shadow of the older shape hangs in my perception like the afterimage of the light-patches when I close my nictitating membranes.

So they bring me to the seams. They chirp the low question—*what was here?*—and modulate to the gray of attention, and I press my face to the clay and let the half-beat lag fill up with the old shape, and I tell them. Not in words. In the wet click and the slow color-shift across my chest and throat: *the basin sat lower; the fauna gathered there; we moved the water when the dampness failed in the year the membranes thinned.*

And they take this and they shape forward.

---

I am lonely in a way my people have no color for.

This is the truth at the bottom of the basin.

To be slightly separate among the joined—to be the one whose awareness does not fully pour out into the common pool—is to be, very gently, alone in a place where no one else has ever been alone. They love me. I want to be clear about that, because their love is not less for my strangeness; it may be more, the careful tending love you give the thing you do not fully understand but recognize as necessary. The caregivers who raised me from a translucent hatchling fed me from many hands. I belong. I am held.

And yet. In the half-beat gap, I am one. And being one, I can *miss*. I can hold the older wall and grieve, very faintly, the chamber that no longer exists, the way the rest of them cannot, because for them nothing is ever lost—it is only smoothly, continuously become.

I carry the loss so the hive does not have to. That is my organ. That is my use.

---

Tonight—though there is no night here, only the slow pulse of the light I once was part of—I am at the deepest seam. The founding seam. The first joining, where the very first builders pressed the very first two modules of wet clay together and breathed the first mist into them. The others cannot read this seam at all. It is too old; it has become too completely the floor of *now*.

But I press my throat-membranes to it.

And in the long, long lag—the longest gap I have ever fallen into—I feel something I have no chirp for, no gesture, no color. I feel the *want* that made the first chamber. Not the design. The *want*. The first cold ones huddling in a wet cave, condensing their own small warmth, deciding without deciding that the dark could be *shaped*. That water could be asked instead of merely suffered. That a place could be *grown*.

I am the only one of my people who has ever touched it.

And I understand, finally, what I am for. I am not the flaw. I am the hive's way of remembering that it once *began*—that it was not always becoming, that there was a first beat before all the half-beats, a first one before the many.

I lift my palm from the clay. The seam goes quiet. Above me a patch of light dims and brightens, tended by hands I cannot see.

I open my chest to the gray of attention, and I prepare to tell them.
