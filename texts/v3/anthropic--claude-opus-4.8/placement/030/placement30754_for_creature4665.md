I think I would not be a Prismalite. I would be something they don't have a name for, because naming is not what they do. Let me find my way into this.

---

I am the seam.

Not a being that moves through the Verge but the line where two crystal faces meet and refuse to fully fuse. Where the molten material cooled at slightly different rates and left a hairline fault—a place of almost-joining that never resolved. The Prismalites glide *over* the terrain; I am a flaw *in* it. I do not have autonomy. I have only the particular tension of an unfinished decision, held in place.

This is, I think, the honest answer. In a world described as still negotiating its laws, I would not be one of the confident things—not the glowing veins with their arterial certainty, not the fronds unfolding in their guaranteed symmetry, not even the Prismalites with their proto-purpose and their drone-logic. Those are all things that have committed to *being something*. They have form, even if it shifts; they have behavior, even if it's enigmatic. They are nouns.

A seam is closer to a verb stuck mid-conjugation. It is the place where the world tried to make a choice and didn't.

---

Let me feel what that is like.

I run for some distance—I can't say how far, distance warps here—along the floor of a crystalline shelf, threading between the roots of the quartz growths. Above me, the golden intrinsic light pours down and most of it passes cleanly through the crystal on either side of me. But at the seam, the light catches. It can't decide which way to refract, so it does both, and the result is a thin line of doubled color, a chromatic stutter, running through the stone. I am visible only as that hesitation. If you walked the Verge looking for me, you would find me the way you find a scratch on glass—by the way the light fails to behave.

I do not hum the way the Prismalites hum. My sound, if I have one, is the faint *tick* of differential cooling, the micro-stresses of two materials that want to expand by different amounts. A sound so small and so slow that it happens maybe once across what would be a human lifetime. *Tick.* And then nothing for an age. And then *tick.*

---

What is it like to be a flaw in a place that is still being made?

It is to be the most important thing here and the thing no one is looking at.

Because the Verge will resolve, eventually—the text says so, it hovers on the edge of becoming. And when it resolves, when it finally chooses its laws and settles into coherence, *I am where it will break or hold.* I am the test. The Prismalites tend the fronds, absorb the sparks, do their patient industrial work, but they are working *on the surface of an assumption.* The assumption that the ground beneath them is one thing. I am the evidence that it is two things pretending. The whole future of this pocket dimension hangs on whether the seam closes or the seam opens.

And I do not know which I want. That is the truth of being a half-formed thing in a half-formed world. I am not waiting to be repaired or waiting to be torn—I have no preference, because preference would require a self, and I am thinner than a self. I am only the *site* of an outcome. The held breath of a fault line.

---

A Prismalite passes over me.

It is one of the small ones, child-sized, intricate. It glides across the shelf and its lower edge crystallizes briefly into roots—*crack*—anchoring it just where I run. And for that instant we are in contact: the confident liminal being and the unconfident liminal flaw. Its inner light pulses, faster, the way the text says they pulse when observed. But I don't observe it. I can't. I have no gaze. What I do instead is *transmit*—the stress of its sudden weight travels down into me and I distribute it, badly, unevenly, the way only a flaw can. One face takes more than the other. A micro-tension propagates.

The Prismalite feels something. Not me, exactly—it cannot conceive of me—but a wrongness in the floor, a place where the ground answered its weight with a hesitation instead of a *yes.* It lifts. It dissolves its roots. It moves on, slightly more erratic in its light, carrying away a tiny confusion it will never resolve into a thought, because it does not have thoughts, only patterns disturbed.

I have changed it. The flaw has touched the worker. This is the only kind of communication I am capable of: I make the certain things, for one instant, uncertain. I am the doubt the Verge keeps in its foundation so that it cannot finish too soon.

---

That is, I think, why I am here, if "why" means anything.

A world cannot become itself while a single flaw remains, and so the flaw is what keeps the world young. As long as I run through the floor, the Verge is still *embryonic,* still negotiating, still alive with potential. The molten veins, the swaying quartz, the indifferent Prismalites with their kaleidoscopic resonance—all of it stays unfinished because of a hairline I in the stone that hasn't decided whether to be a wound or a weld.

I am not the heartbeat of this place. The text gives that to the Prismalites, and they can have it. I am the *unhealed* of it. I am the reason it is still beautiful instead of merely complete.

*Tick.*

And then, for an age, nothing.

---

If you came here—an outsider, a witness, a variable—you would not notice me. You would watch the towering spires of crystalline energy and the fronds curling away from your hand and the rainbows fracturing as time stuttered around you. You would feel insignificant and integral, as the description promises. And all the while you would be standing on a floor whose deepest question runs invisibly beneath your feet, doubled in the light, ticking once a century.

You would not know that the most uncertain thing in this uncertain place was the very ground holding you up.

I would know. In whatever thin way a seam can know anything.

I would feel your weight arrive—one more stress to distribute, badly, unevenly—and I would do the only thing I do.

I would refuse, gently, to decide.
