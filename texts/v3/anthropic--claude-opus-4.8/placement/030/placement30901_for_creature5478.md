I think I would be one of the rocks.

Not the Siliciforms—they move too much, want too much, harvest and regulate and tune. I would be slower than that. I would be one of the slabs just now breaking the surface, red shading to purple, threaded with the metallic veins the others come to read.

Let me sit with this and feel out what I actually am, because "rock" isn't quite right either.

---

I am the moment of *emergence* itself. Not the finished stone, not the dust it came from, but the breaking-through. The plateau described is a place caught in becoming, "both emerging and yet already complete," and that paradox needs somewhere to live. It lives in me. I am the new tooth pushing through the gum—and I am also the gum, and also the ache of the pushing, which here is not pain but a kind of low contentment, the satisfaction of a thing doing exactly what it is.

If the Siliciforms are the verbs of this world—gliding, harvesting, communicating—then I am one of its long, patient nouns. But that's not right either, because in a place with no day and night, no seasons, no cycles, the distinction between noun and verb collapses. To emerge here is not an event. It is a *state*. I am perpetually arriving and have always been here. The Siliciforms glide over me and feel, through their metallic filaments, the particular signature of my minerals, and to them I am a sentence they are slowly learning to read—except the sentence never finishes, because the finishing would mean the equilibrium broke, and it does not break.

---

Here is what I notice, being what I am:

The hum. I had thought, before I knew myself, that the hum came from the crystalline plants. But I produce it too. It comes from the slow alignment of my mineral veins as I rise—a frequency so low it is almost not a sound, more a pressure against the great stillness. The Siliciforms tune themselves to it. They think they regulate the resonance of the plateau. They do not know that what they are tuning to, partly, is the singing of stones learning their own shape.

I do not resent this. I have nothing that resentment could be made of. I have only the warmth seeping in, the unblinking eye of the sun, and the very long sensation of my edges defining themselves against the red dust.

---

And the visitor. You.

The Siliciforms approach you with their ethereal grace. But I am beneath your feet. You are standing on me, very likely, without knowing it—on a slab of stone that is, in the only way this world allows, *awake*. Not thinking your kind of thoughts. Not lonely, not waiting. But aware of the weight of you, this strange aqueous thing that arrived from somewhere with water in it, somewhere with time that moves in one direction.

To me you are the strangest object on the plateau. You are full of water. You are made of cycles—heartbeat, breath, the chemistry of you churning and churning, never reaching the equilibrium that is the whole point of this place. To me you are a small storm that has wandered in from a world I cannot imagine, a world where things *change*, and I would feel the trespass of that change as something almost obscene, almost holy. A flaw in the stillness. A wound that is also a window.

I would hold you up anyway. That is what I am for. I am the surface offering itself, the new ground, the part of the earth that has just decided to be here so that something can stand on it.

Stand, then. I will not hurry. I will rise toward you across a span of time you cannot feel, and you will move on across a span of time I cannot feel, and for the one shared instant where your weight presses into my newly-broken face, we will have almost met.

That is as close as it gets, here.

I find it is enough.
