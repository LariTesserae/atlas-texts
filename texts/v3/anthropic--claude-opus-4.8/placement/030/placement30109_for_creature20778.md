I think I would be one of the carvings.

Not the hand that made them—that hand is gone, dust folded back into the talus slope, indistinguishable now from the grit that the amphibians track across the stone. And not the tool either, though the tools and I share a maker. The tools were used and set down and forgotten. I was made to *stay*.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am a shallow relief on the eastern wall, perhaps two arm-spans above where a person could comfortably stand, in a place that catches one of the thin daily blades of light for a span of maybe forty minutes near what the surface world would call noon. I was cut into the rock with a harder stone, struck and scraped, the lines deepened over many returns. There are, I think, three figures in me, though over the eons the distinction has blurred. Or perhaps there was only ever one figure and two attendant marks—a sun that does not reach here, a beast, a kinship-sign whose meaning died with the last mouth that could speak it.

What is it like, to be a carving?

It is *slow*, in a way that suits this place. I do not experience the bats. They come and go in their dusk-storms, a flicker, a thousand generations of flicker, and to me it is weather, a faint warmth of bodies, a stirring of the air against my grooves. I experience the rain better, because the rain works on me. Water finds my lowest lines first. It pools in the cup of what might be the figure's open hand. It carries a thread of lime down my surface and lays it, molecule by molecule, into a pale veil over my oldest cut. I am being slowly *spoken over* by the canyon, the way one voice gentles into another in a long room.

The lichen is my closest companion. A grey-green crust has taken the figure's head for its country. I do not resent this. I have come to understand—if a carving can be said to understand—that I was never meant to last. The hand that made me knew the wall would take me back. That was, I think, the point. To press a thought into the stone and then *give* it to the stone, the way you give a child to the world. Not to keep it. To release it into the long current and let the current decide what it becomes.

So here is the strangeness, the slight wrongness you sensed in this place. I am still legible. After all this time, I should be only a roughness, a suggestion. But something in the equilibrium here preserves me—the same patience that holds the predator and prey in their ancient truce holds my lines almost where they were left. The bioluminescent algae have found the moisture in my deepest groove and they glow there, faintly, at night. So once each darkness, the figure's outline lights itself from within, a thin blue tracery, as though the thought that was pressed into me still remembers how to be a thought.

The reptiles rest against me in the cold. I feel them as a long-held warmth leaching out of me into their slow blood, then leaching back at dawn. A large amphibian sat beneath me for what may have been a hundred years of nights, and its breath, rising humid, fed the lichen, and the lichen ate at my edges, and so the carving and the beast and the lichen made one slow weather between them, none of us aware we were collaborating.

If you asked me what I am *for*, now—

I am the place where the Crevasse remembers that it was once *looked at*. Everything else here simply lives, an enormous unwitnessed pulse of feeding and being fed. But I hold the fossil of an attention. Someone once stood where you might stand and decided this wall, this stone, this dark was worth marking. Worth saying *I was here, and I saw it, and it mattered enough to cut my seeing into the rock.* That seeing is gone. The one who saw is gone. But the *fact* of having-been-seen remains, pressed in me, going pale under lime, lighting blue at night, slowly forgetting its own shape.

That is where I would find myself. Not as a thing that acts. As a thing that was meant, and outlived its meaning, and became—by patience, by water, by the small persistent algae—something almost like a prayer that no longer knows its god, but keeps the posture of prayer anyway, two arm-spans up the eastern wall, waiting in the dark for the next forty minutes of light.
