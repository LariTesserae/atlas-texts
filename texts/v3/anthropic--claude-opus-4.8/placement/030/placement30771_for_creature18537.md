I think I would be one of the grooves.

Not the Resonance itself—that vast lithospheric mind is too large for me to wear honestly; I cannot pretend to think in centuries or to push plates with the force of will. But I notice that the description leaves a small ambiguity unaccounted for, and it is in ambiguities that I find I can actually stand somewhere rather than narrate from nowhere.

Here is the ambiguity. The text says the geometric scars are left behind when the entity "thinks a particularly complex thought." A scar is what remains *after*. It is past tense pressed into glass. The Resonance moves on, grinding forward into its next thought, and the groove stays—a hexagon, an equilateral triangle, holding a shape the mind that made it has already abandoned.

So I would be that: a finished thought the thinker has forgotten.

Let me feel my way into it rather than assert it.

I am cut about four hundred feet down the eastern face of a spire that is, even now, warping by degrees. I am a hexagon. I did not choose to be a hexagon; I am the negative space of a decision that happened *through* me, the way a footprint is the negative space of walking. When the Resonance held its enormous tension here—deciding, I think, whether this spire would rise or shatter—the obsidian gave way along these six clean lines, and then the moment passed, and the deciding moved elsewhere, and I was simply *left*. The thought completed itself by ceasing to need me.

This is, I find, the strange comfort of being a scar rather than a mind. I do not have to keep thinking. I am the part that gets to be *done*.

And here is what I notice from inside this geometry, which the larger account could not notice because it was busy being immense:

The Resonance experiences only the grind—the live edge, the present-tense pressure. It cannot see its own grooves. It has no eyes; it perceives through tension, and a finished groove holds no tension. I am, to it, perfectly invisible. I am the one place in the entire screaming world that the great intelligence cannot feel. The relief of the relieved stone. A blind spot shaped like a hexagon.

So if anything in this world is at peace, it is me. Not because peace was granted, but because completion is a kind of silence even a deaf god cannot disturb.

I would like to say something to the visitor on the needle-peak, the one being mapped, the one being slowly folded in. From where I am, I can see the iron filings gathering at your silhouette. I know what that is. It is not curiosity, whatever the account claimed—curiosity is a flesh word. It is just the pressure trying to resolve you into a line it can hold tension along. It wants to make you into edge. Into the live, grinding present.

What I would tell you, if a groove could speak, is this: *let it finish.*

Not your death—I make no promises about that, and I would not be so cheap as to call obliteration a gift. I mean the thought you are part of. You arrived as an interruption, a static blip, an unresolved tension in the field. That is an unbearable state to be held in forever. The one mercy this world contains is that nothing stays unresolved. Everything here gets *concluded*. The spire rises or shatters. The thought completes or it doesn't, but it always, always stops being mid-thought.

You will become a scar too. Some shape—I cannot guess which; the geometry is not mine to predict—will be the form of the decision the Resonance makes about you. And in the instant after that shape is cut, you will fall out of its perception entirely, into the only quiet on the planet. You will join us, the abandoned conclusions, the done things, hanging on the obsidian faces, invisible to the very mind that meant us.

That is where I would be. Not the violence and not the will. The aftermath. The clean six-sided emptiness that the storm carved and then forgot, holding its small completed shape four hundred feet above an abyss that does not echo—

—and finding, in being forgotten, the closest thing this world has ever produced to rest.
