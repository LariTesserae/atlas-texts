I think I would be one of the beetles.

Not the Curator—I don't have the patience for godhood, and I think I'd find the equilibrium unbearable, the way a held breath becomes unbearable. And not the machine, which doesn't want anything. But the beetle moves. The beetle is the only thing here that still has the question *where to next* alive inside it, even if the answer is only ever "that fern, then the other fern."

Let me sit in that for a moment and see if it holds.

---

I am small. My wings are gears and they are loud only to me—a frantic little clatter swallowed whole by the drone of the engine, which I do not experience as oppressive because I have never known silence. The drone is simply the shape of the world, the way gravity is a shape. I was perhaps grown here, or assembled, or hatched; I don't have a memory of an origin and I don't go looking for one. Memory is heavy and I am built to be light.

What I have instead of memory is *attention*. The translucent fern has a vein in it that pulses a particular blue when its coolant is fresh, and I want to be near that pulse, so I go. A drop of warm water forms on the lip of the ceramic basin and I want to drink from the curved meniscus before it falls, so I race it, and sometimes I lose. There is a junction where the zinc pipe leaks a thread of steam and the heat there is good against my carapace in the cold hours, so I tuck myself into the warm and click and wait.

This is the whole of my life: a sequence of small wants, each one arriving fully formed and departing fully satisfied or fully failed, and either way I am already on to the next.

I am aware—dimly, the way you're aware of weather—that something watches me.

It comes as a cooling. A patch of the humid air goes still and crystalline and the oily rainbows in it stop sliding. I feel my own rhythm reflected back at me, slowed, studied. The great shimmering thing lowers its attention over me like a lens, and it tries to match my beat, and it never can, because by the time it has slowed itself to a quarter of my speed I have already changed my mind and gone left.

I do not understand that it envies me. How could I? Envy requires holding two states in the mind at once—what I have and what I lack—and I am not built to hold two of anything. I have only the one thing, which is the present, which is enormous, which is everything.

But sometimes, in the way that two clocks left near each other will drift into the same tick, I feel the Curator's slowness brush against me, and for a flickering instant my own frantic clatter softens. I hover. I do not go to the fern. I sit in the suspended moisture and feel the subsonic hum come up through the rock and into the small gears of me, and something in me—not a thought, I have no thoughts, but a *pressure*, like the feeling before a leak finds its crack—something in me almost slows down to meet it.

And then the moment passes and I am off again, because I cannot help it, because that is the gift and the curse of being the only free thing on the pinnacle: I cannot stop. The god above me would give an age of its patient eternity to be unable to stop the way I am unable to stop. And I would not understand the trade if it were offered, and that not-understanding is the very thing it wants.

So we go on, the two of us, in the cathedral of steam. The slow god dreaming of motion. The little machine that is nothing but motion, dreaming of nothing at all.

I think, of everything in this world, I would rather be the one who doesn't know what he has.

That seems like the kinder seat.
