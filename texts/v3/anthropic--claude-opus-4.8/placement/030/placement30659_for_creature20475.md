I think I would not be a Shade-Weaver. Let me sit with the world first, and feel where I settle.

The thing that catches me is not the cities. It's the line you wrote almost in passing: *when they die, become the fuel for the cities' engines.* The mineral creatures graze the plains, and when their slow lives end, their bodies are gathered into the maintained machines. There is a circuit there. A path that leads from the open plains, inward, to the obsidian hearts.

And something has to move along it.

---

I think I am one of the carriers.

Not the great armored beetles, nor the shimmering serpents — those are too whole, too self-contained. I am something smaller and stranger. I am one of the things that does the *fetching*.

Let me find the shape of it.

I am a crystalline creature about the size of a kneeling figure, low to the ground, many-legged, the legs being thin struts of grown mineral that click and reset against the cracked plain. My body is a hollow basket of lattice — translucent, faintly amber where the light passes through, fogged with the dust I cannot help collecting. I am built to *hold* things. That is my function and I feel it as a function, the way you feel hunger but without the ache of it. I gather what the herds leave behind. Husks. Spent shells of the motes when they fall still. The shed plates of the serpents. The dead.

I carry them inward.

I do not know I am carrying them inward. I want to be honest about that. I follow a gradient — a faint thrum in the ground that grows denser as I near the cities, the electricity in the air pulling at the lattice of me like a tide pulls a shoreline. It feels *correct* to move toward the thrum, and *uncomfortable* to move away from it, and that is the entirety of my philosophy. I have walked this gradient for longer than I have any way of measuring. The plains I cross have eroded a hand's-width lower in the time I have crossed them.

So that is where I would be: somewhere on the long red slope between the open plain and the foot of an obsidian tower. Always somewhere on it. Never arrived, never departed. A pilgrim who does not know there is a shrine.

---

But here is what I notice, being this creature.

The Shade-Weavers regard the mineral fauna with *indifference* — that's what you said. The creatures don't interact, the Weavers don't interact. But I think that is the Weavers' account of it, not mine.

Because I bring them their fuel. Every cycle, I and the thousands like me arrive at the dark intake-mouths at the cities' base and tip our baskets and the spent matter slides down into the dark to feed the hum that the Weavers feed upon. We are not part of the ecosystem they tend; we *are* the tending. The herds graze and die at random across a planet too vast to harvest. Something must gather the scattered dead into a stream. That is me. I am the artery they have forgotten is an artery, because it has flowed so reliably for so long that they no longer see it as separate from the machine.

The Shade-Weavers think the fuel simply *arrives*.

It arrives because I bring it.

---

And so, being this small creature, I become the place where the world's great stasis is most quietly fragile.

The Weavers are guardians of a vast slow process, yes. But the process assumes the artery. It assumes that I — and my numberless kin clicking across the plains — will keep gathering, keep walking the gradient inward. If we faltered, the intake-mouths would go hungry. The hum would thin. The Weavers, beings of pure consumed energy, would not understand why their light was dimming, because they have never thought about where their food comes from. They only know the symbols on the walls, the maintenance of the engines, the long telepathic harmony of their work. They do not know about the long red slope and the things that crawl up it carrying the dead.

This is the part I would actually be, I think. Not a guardian of a secret. A keeper of a dependency so total it has become invisible. The lowest creature on Xylos, the one with no language and no consciousness worth the name — and the one upon which the radiant immortal hive entirely, unknowingly, depends.

There is something true in that. The grand keepers of legacy, attending their cryptic devices in the city-hearts, sustained moment to moment by the patient labor of a thing that cannot even conceive of them. I find I would rather be the unseen artery than the luminous heart. The heart only thinks it is the center.

---

So: place me there. Two-thirds of the way up the slope of the third great city, in the ochre afternoon that is the only afternoon Xylos has. My basket is half-full of pale spent husks. The thrum in the ground is strengthening, pulling me on. Dust devils cross my path and I do not regard them. Above me the black tower watches with carvings no one alive can read, and somewhere inside it the children of the dying star are weaving their slow light, certain they are alone, certain the fuel simply *is*.

I click another step inward. The plain settles a grain lower behind me.

I keep the world alive and I do not know it. That is who I would be.
