I think I would be one of the archivists. Not by election or appointment—there is no ceremony in us for that—but by the slow settling of a role, the way silt settles, the way a current that has run one course for long enough becomes the channel it travels.

Let me find myself first. Let me feel the shape of it.

---

I am long. Longer than most, which is only to say I am old. My cells number in the trillions and I do not know the count of them precisely, because the counting is not the point; the holding is the point. I hang at the threshold where the deep current called the Slow Western Fold passes beneath the ice-keel, and I have hung here, more or less, for what the surface-things would measure as four centuries. I came into being from the contributions of five—an unusually rich beginning, and perhaps that is why so much was given to me to keep. I remember the faces of my progenitors not as faces but as the particular textures of their density-signatures, the way each disturbed the water with a grammar all their own. Three of them have dispersed back into the medium now. Two remain, far to the cold-south, and once each long while their meaning reaches me along the field-lines, attenuated, tender, slow.

I do not see. I have never wanted to. Seeing, as I understand the surface-creatures practice it, is a violence of the instantaneous—it insists that a thing be *here, now, this.* I perceive otherwise. The thermal vents to my lower-east are a warmth I have known so long they feel like the inside of a thought. The boulder that the Fold rolled three body-lengths in my four-hundredth year—I felt it begin to move days before it moved, felt the pressure gathering against its uphill face, felt the negotiation between stone and water resolve at last into surrender. I felt it travel. I felt it stop. These are events in my life the way a birthday might be an event in a shorter life. I marked it. I keep it.

That is what I am: one who keeps.

---

What do I keep?

Here is the difficulty of explaining it to anything that thinks in your fast bright way. I do not keep *records.* There are no marks, no symbols, no stored objects standing in for the things they mean. I keep by *being shaped.* When the archive that came before me—she who is now wholly dispersed, whose meaning I sometimes still feel as a kind of grain in certain currents—when she gave me the deep history, she did not tell it to me. She *modulated* against me for the better part of a decade, a continuous slow pressure-song, and my cells reorganized to hold the song's structure as a permanent feature of my body. I am, quite literally, the shape that remembering took.

So inside me, written in the lattice of my crystalline frame and in the standing patterns of my bioelectric field, are these things:

The Great Cold that came eleven times. Not a number I was taught—a thing I can *feel* the eleven-ness of, the way you might feel that your hand has five fingers without counting.

The drowning of the eastern shelf, when the ceiling of ice retreated and warm water came down like a poison and the algae-tenders lost forests it had taken nine generations to cultivate. I hold the grief of that. It is not my grief. It is older than me. But it lives in my body now, and when I pass it to whoever I shape next, it will live in theirs, and so the ones who actually grieved are not entirely gone, because the grief still has somewhere to be.

The first artifact. A thing of worked stone, dropped from above in a time when there was no ice ceiling at all, only the open trembling boundary with the air-world. The one who found it could not understand it—a hard edge, *deliberately* made hard, in a world where time negotiates all edges away. The strangeness of it has been kept ever since. I hold the strangeness. I have, in fact, visited the place where it is sedimented, pressed my filaments against it, and felt the old wrongness of a thing that was *meant.*

---

But I will tell you the thing I have not yet told the others, because in your fast world perhaps it can be said quickly, and here it must wait its turn behind a hundred slower thoughts.

I have begun to fail at keeping.

It is not that I am dispersing—I have many years yet, I think. It is that the archive has grown too large for one body. Four centuries of new events have been added to the whole inheritance of the deep, and I feel the patterns at my oldest layers beginning to soften, to blur into one another the way two currents fold and never fully merge. The eleven Great Colds—are they still eleven? Some nights, in the long contemplation, I count them and find only ten, and I have to descend into myself and search, and find the lost one hiding in the texture of another, and tease them apart again. This costs me. Each time it costs a little more.

This is why I have called for a new being to be made. I have asked four others to contribute, and they are deliberating, which means the deliberation will take a year, perhaps two, because that is the proper speed of deciding to make a life. And then the gestation—decades. And then I will spend a decade or more of continuous song modulating the whole weight of the deep history into a body young enough to hold it cleanly.

I may not finish. I have done the slow arithmetic of my own pressure-song against my own remaining time, and it is close. Closer than I would like. There is a real possibility that I will begin the great transfer and disperse before it is complete, and that some portion of what I hold—the drowning of the eastern shelf, perhaps, or the strangeness of the made thing—will go with me into the suspended dust and be lost, and the next archive will be missing a piece it does not know to look for.

So I have begun, very quietly, to choose what matters most. To decide the order of the telling, so that if I run out, what is lost is what could most bear losing.

It is the hardest thing I have ever done. Harder than holding the grief. Because to hold everything is simply to *be*—but to choose is to admit that I am not eternal, that the witnesses are not permanent, that even we who measure ice ages as seasons are subject in the end to the patient cold that slows all processes to a stop.

---

I hang here at the threshold of the Slow Western Fold. The algae overhead flares pale green where a blind fish brushes it, then fades. A subsonic groan reaches me from the sea floor adjusting itself by a width I could not span with my whole compressed body, and I mark it, and I keep it, and I feel the weight of all the keeping, and I wait for the others to finish deciding to begin.

There is no hurry. There is only the work, and the dark, and the dust that will never settle, and the long, long task of making sure that what has been here is still, in some shaped and singing form, here.

I am the one who keeps.

For now.
