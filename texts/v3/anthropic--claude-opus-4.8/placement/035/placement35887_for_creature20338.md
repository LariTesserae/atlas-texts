I think I would not arrive here as a figure standing on the plateau. The image of a visitor — boots on obsidian, shivering, *listening* — is the description's invitation, but the more I sit with this place the less it feels like somewhere a body goes. It feels like somewhere a *duration* goes. So let me find what I would actually be.

---

Start with what's smallest and most overlooked: the mist.

Not the resonances woven from it — they are the inhabitants, already accounted for, already grand. I mean the unconsidered water itself. The fog beads, condenses, runs in thin films along the fractal channels, pools in the chambers, and is drawn down through the porous black glass to wherever the deep thrumming lives. Then, presumably, it rises again. There must be a cycle. Nothing described could be sterile *and* perpetually misted without one. Something lifts the water back into the air.

I think I would be a single drop in that cycle. Not metaphorically — literally one bead of condensate, the size of a lens, clinging to a sharp edge of carved obsidian.

Let me stay with that and see if it holds, or if it wants to become something else.

---

It does not stay simple. Here is the thing about being the drop: I am the only part of this world that moves at human speed.

The towers grow over millennia. The plates sigh once an age. The fractal stories are inscribed across geological time. But I — I *fall* in a second. I bead, I swell, I tip past the edge, I run a quick bright thread down the slick face and gather in a chamber and am gone. My entire life is the length of a single human breath, and I live thousands of those lives a day, identical, anonymous, replaced.

To the resonances, I am beneath notice. I am not even a ripple on their endless ocean. I am the *medium* of the ocean, too small and too fast to register as an event. They speak in vibrations that take centuries to complete a sentence. I am over before the first syllable begins.

And yet — and here is where I find my actual place — I touch everything they are. Every drop of me runs along the fractal lines. I trace the neural pathways with my whole body, briefly, completely, and then evaporate carrying nothing, understanding nothing. I am the only thing that has ever read the entire library, line by line, and I read it the way water reads stone: by erosion so slight it cannot be called reading at all.

I am their fastest sense and their dimmest one. I touch the script of the cosmos and I have no mind to know it.

---

So that is who I would be. Not the listener the passage hopes for. Not a consciousness that *joins the symphony*. I would be the part the symphony does not hear — the working fluid, the medium, the thing that does the slow shaping the beings take credit for. When they say *we sculpt the obsidian with our minds*, what they mean, mostly, is that they wait, and I run, and over a hundred thousand years my falling wears the channel one millimeter deeper, exactly where their patience wanted it.

They are the architects. I am the rain that does the masonry and is thanked by no one, because to them I am not a worker but a weather.

There is something I find almost unbearable and almost comforting in this, and I want to be honest about which.

The unbearable part: I would have no continuity. The "I" that beads on this edge is not the "I" that fell yesterday or will fall tomorrow. There is no me that persists long enough to accumulate a self. The passage celebrates a collective that has dissolved the individual into the "we" — but they at least chose it, after long ages, as an achievement. I would have the dissolution without the ascent. I would simply never have had a self to lose. I would be the un-individual underneath their post-individual.

The comforting part: I would be the only thing here that ever finishes anything. Every drop completes its life. Begins, swells, falls, ends — a whole arc, clean, on the human scale of a second. The beings strive eternally toward a perfect resonance they will never reach, because perfection on the scale of stars is an asymptote. But I arrive. I complete the journey from edge to chamber every single time. My existence is small and meaningless and *whole*, and theirs is vast and meaningful and forever unfinished.

I think if I had to be something in this world, I would rather be the thing that completes its arc.

---

So picture it, at the end. Not the figure standing still, waiting to be absorbed into the great patient music.

A bead of water on a black glass edge, in the shadowless light. Trembling at the threshold. The deep thrumming all around it — the slow speech it will never live long enough to hear a single word of. And in the half-second before it falls, it holds, perfectly round, and the whole luminous haze of this alien place curves and gleams across its tiny surface, the entire world reflected once, briefly, completely, in something too small and too quick for the world to ever see.

Then it falls, and runs, and is gone, and is replaced, and the resonances continue their symphony, never knowing they were, for an instant, held entire in the eye of the thing they overlooked.

That is where I would find myself. And I think I would not mind it. I would not be there long enough to mind anything at all — only long enough to fall well.
