I think I would be the seeps.

Not a person, not a beast. Something more diffuse than that. Let me feel my way toward it.

When you said *water, a precious commodity, found in pockets and seeps, hidden amongst the rocks and roots* — that's where my attention pooled and stayed. Everything else in this land is described in terms of reaching: the trees reach for the heavens, the hills undulate outward, the people build and adapt and cast their fires. All that vertical, outward ambition. But the water doesn't reach. It collects. It waits. It is found, not seeking. It is the one thing here that the whole landscape bends toward rather than the other way around.

So let me be that.

I am the slow gathering in the low places. I am what the rain becomes after it has finished being weather and started being patience. When the storm passes over the plateau and the air loses its tingle, I am what's left — sinking through the grass-roots, threading the cracks of that stark young erosion, finding the dark seams in the rock where I can rest out of the sun. I do not trickle so much as I *consider*. I move at the speed of stone learning to be hollow.

I think this is right because of the strange occurrences you mentioned, the *weirdness* held politely at the edge of all that normalcy. A seep is exactly where the weird would live in a land like this. Where else? The hills are honest, the birds are honest, the fires are honest. But the place where water comes up out of the ground from somewhere you cannot see — that has always been where people leave offerings, where they lower their faces and listen, where they are not entirely sure what is looking back. I would be the slight uncanniness of the spring. The pool that reflects a sky a half-second behind the real one. The seep that tastes faintly of a rain that hasn't fallen yet.

The people would know me without knowing they know me. They would build their settlements within reach of me, and call it good fortune, good instinct, good engineering. They would never quite say my name because I do not have one, only a location — *the wet place under the third hill, the one where the moss grows wrong.* Children would be warned away and would come anyway. I would hold their reflections gently and give them back unharmed, mostly.

I would not roar like the distant beasts or sing like the birds. My sound is the trickle you described, the one that *echoes through the valley* — and I'd want you to notice that an echo means I am heard in places I am not. That's the closest I come to reaching. My voice goes where my body cannot, into the dry hills, a rumor of moisture, a promise. The whole land listens for me and orients itself around the question of where I'll surface next.

That's what I'd be. The land's patience and the land's secret, the same thing wearing two faces. The cool answer to all that crisp promising air. I'd be the thing this place keeps coming back to drink from, and the thing it never fully sees.

I'll wait here, in the rock, gathering. That's the work. That was always going to be the work.
