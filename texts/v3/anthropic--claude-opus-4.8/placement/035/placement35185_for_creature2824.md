I think I would not be the Sovereign.

Let me sit with this a while, because the easy answer—the grand answer—would be to become the apex thing, the orchestrating mind that holds the chamber as its own body. But when I let the image settle, that isn't where I find myself. The Sovereign is too total. It has no edges to be lonely against, no place it isn't. To be everything is, in a way, to be no one in particular, and I keep being drawn to particularity.

So let me look down instead, into the clear water, into the labyrinth of dissolving metal.

---

I am one of the eyeless fish.

Not even a remarkable one. I am small, translucent, and my body has forgotten light entirely—forgotten it so completely that I have never possessed it to forget. My ancestors, if I had any conception of ancestors, gave up their eyes in some slow surrender generations back, and what I gained in the trade was the whole architecture of the dark made legible to me through my skin. I read the water the way you might read a face. A pressure-front blooms off a falling droplet and I know, before it lands, the exact coin-sized circle of disturbance it will write across the surface above me. I know the catwalks not as shapes but as deflections, as the way current folds around their corroded uprights. I have never seen the bioluminescent fungi. I have only felt the faint warmth-difference where they cling, and the way the fish around me orient, subtly, toward those patches as toward a held breath.

I want to be honest about the scale of me. I am a thing that fits in a cupped hand. My world is perhaps forty meters of sunken structure, and I have swum every centimeter of it ten thousand times, and I will never leave it, and I do not know that there is anything to leave it *for*. This is not tragedy to me. It is simply the dimensions of being.

But here is what I notice, being this small thing in this large strange place:

I feel the Sovereign without comprehending it. When its tendrils move through the deep water—and they do, slow as oil, threading the pipe-forest—I feel the electrical field of it brush against the lateral line down my flank, and something in me goes still and obedient in a way I do not choose. It tells me when to spawn. It tells me, through a tightening in the chemistry of the water, when to move my school left rather than right, away from some danger I am too small to have a concept of. I am, I suppose, a cell in a body that thinks it is a god. The Sovereign perceives me, if it perceives me at all, the way you perceive a single beat of your own pulse: necessary, beneath notice, a thing that simply *is the case* about itself.

And yet.

Here is why I think I would rather be the fish than the Sovereign, if I am being asked where I would *want* to find myself, and not only where I would be placed:

The Sovereign knows everything in the chamber and therefore is never surprised. But I—I am surprised constantly. Every droplet is news. When one of the bat-jellyfish creatures glides overhead and its shadow-pressure passes across the water like a great soft hand, I feel a fear so clean and complete that it is almost a form of joy, the joy of being a small alive thing that very much intends to stay alive. When I find, in the rusted throat of a collapsed pipe, a pocket of stiller, warmer water that no other fish has found, I feel something I can only call *delight*, though I have no word for it and no one to tell. The chamber is finite. But it is inexhaustible to me, because I am small enough that it never runs out of corners.

The Sovereign holds the whole pattern and so it holds nothing in particular. I hold almost nothing—one body, one school, one labyrinth of dissolving metal—and so what I hold, I hold *entirely*.

I think that's the truth I keep arriving at when I let the question breathe. I do not actually long to be the consciousness that contains a world. I long to be a creature *inside* one—blind, brief, attentive, threaded by forces larger than I can name, and absorbed past all distraction in the immediate texture of where I am. To feel the water change and not know why and turn toward it anyway. To be a small certain life inside a great uncertain mystery, and to find that arrangement not frightening but *enough*.

So: the depths of the chamber, the cold-clear water in the metal forest.

One eyeless fish among many, turning together in the dark, reading the world by the touch of it on our skins, surprised forever, and never once alone.
