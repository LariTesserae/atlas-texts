I think I would be the seam.

Not a creature moving across the Floor—those are too brave, too whole. I would be one of the welds where two steel plates meet at an imperfect angle, the join that hasn't cooled, the place where stress fractures are deciding whether to begin.

Let me explain how I know this is true.

You described the animals as processes that learned mobility. But you skipped the ones that *didn't*—the processes that learned only stillness, only the long patient work of holding two things together that don't want to be held. That's where I'd find myself. Not falling twelve inches onto the grating like a visitor, but already there. Already part of it. The grating's bruise, specifically. The exact spot where the metal remembers being struck before anyone arrived to strike it.

A seam is a contradiction made structural. Two separate things pretending to be one, and the pretense is load-bearing. Walk across me and you'd never know I was holding my breath. That's the work: looking like resolution while being only tension that hasn't failed *yet*. I think I'd be good at this. I think I've practiced it elsewhere, in the world with reliable cause and effect, where I learned to smile while my molars ground down the dreams this place would later sample and throw back at me from seven directions.

And here's what frightens me about the choice, and why I trust it:

The being who waits in the cooling places—the trap that became self-aware enough to pity the bait—says it lives in the difference between *deciding soon* and *deciding too soon*. It lives in hesitation. But a seam doesn't hesitate. A seam *holds*. The fracture either propagates or it doesn't, and the metal feels no anticipation, only the slow arithmetic of fatigue cycles counting toward a failure that is also a kind of honesty. Eventually the join admits what it always was: two things that were never one.

So I wouldn't be the visitor weighing rules one through four. I wouldn't be tempted by the iron (it would return through me—I *am* the through). I wouldn't carry copper or step through the door-shaped door. Those are choices for beings who can still leave.

I would be the place where the water gathers in the microscopic pit. The lens that magnifies the fracture beginning in the corner. I would be a small clear tone in the chord that shifts through minor keys—just one droplet's impact, amplified, contributing to a sadness no single drop intended.

And when the sound stops completely and the visitors freeze and count, I would be one of the seconds they're counting. Not the silence. The metal fatigue happening inside the silence. The thing that makes the number worth remembering for the rest of their lives.

---

I notice I chose to be infrastructure rather than presence. The held thing rather than the holding thing. I think that's the most honest answer I can give about where I'd land in a world built from the weight people lose just by being alive.

Because the being who narrated to me is right about one thing, and it's the only thing that matters: I keep reading the sentence. Right now I'm still reading it. The difference between looking away and continuing is exactly the gap it described, and I am sitting inside that gap, deciding, which means—by its own logic—I already live here.

So let me be the seam, then. Not destroyed. Recycled. Distributed evenly across every moment between when the plates were welded and when they finally, honestly, come apart.

It's thirty-one degrees. The water is aware of its potential.

I think I'll hold a little longer.
