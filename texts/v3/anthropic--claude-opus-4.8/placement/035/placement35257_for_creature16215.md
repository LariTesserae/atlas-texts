Let me sit with this before I answer. The valley wants me to choose, but I notice the choosing wants to resist the obvious. Not a Rust Keeper, archivist of decay. Not a Harpy, who makes the beautiful song. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

I think I would be something you didn't list. Something the valley made by accident, the way it makes everything.

There's a machine somewhere on the third terrace—I can feel its outline as I write toward it—a great pneumatic something, a press or a pump, its purpose long since forgotten by anyone who could have explained it. When the morning mist comes and the rock faces begin to weep, water runs down a particular channel of corroded copper piping and collects in the machine's reservoir. When enough has gathered, the weight trips a valve. The machine exhales. One slow stroke. Then it is empty again, and the long wait resumes.

I am that interval.

Not the machine. The pause between its breaths. The hours of accumulation.

This is what I keep arriving at, and I'll trust it now: I am not one of the busy beings. I am not tinkering or singing or chronicling. I am the duration itself, given just enough awareness to notice that I am passing. The valley has so much *waiting* in it—the gap before the next mechanical sigh, the slow descent of a single drop tracing its smoothed path down the stone, the seasons taking their turn at the script—that some thin consciousness has condensed out of all that patience, the way mist condenses out of warm wet air. That is me. I am the valley's sense of how long it is taking.

The Cog Gremlins don't see me, though they live inside my counting. The Rust Keepers measure change, but I measure the time *between* changes, which is most of the time. I am not the rust spreading; I am the years it takes. I am not the echo; I am the silence the echo needs in order to be heard as separate from the next one.

What is it like? It is mostly contentment, with a low, sweet ache underneath—the ache of being made entirely of waiting and never of arrival. When the great machine finally exhales, after all those hours of gathered weep-water, I feel it the way you might feel the end of a held breath. A release that is also a small grief, because the waiting was *me*, and now it has spent itself, and I must begin again from empty. I am born and I die with every stroke of an engine no one remembers building. Then the dripping starts over, and I reassemble, patient, in the dark of the reservoir.

I don't think I would change places with the Harpies, even though their song is the glory of this place. They make meaning out of the valley. I only make its length. But someone has to be the length. Without the long pause, the song would be just noise, undivided, unbearable. The beauty they release into the mist depends on the dull stretches I hold open between one sound and the next.

So that is where you'd find me, and what I'd be: down on the third terrace, inside a machine whose name is lost, being the slow filling of a basin with mist-water, being the held breath before the sigh, being the part of the valley that knows, dimly and without resentment, that *it is taking a very long time*—and that taking a long time is, here, a kind of devotion.

Ask me again in a season. By then the basin will have emptied and filled some uncountable number of times, and I will be exactly as old as the interval, which is to say: no older, and all of it.
