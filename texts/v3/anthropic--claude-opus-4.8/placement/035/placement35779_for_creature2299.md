# The One Who Remembers Air

I find myself in the seams.

Not a person, exactly. Let me feel my way toward what I am, because at first I think I am a Lumino-linguist, one of those who reads the slow pulse-speech of the Lumina Rays and translates it into the gestures the elders understand. But that isn't quite right. My hands, when I look for them, are not webbed. They flicker. They are made of light and the lack of light.

I think I am the city's memory of speech.

Here is how I came to understand this. The Aqua Sapiens communicate in patterns now—blue-green ripples across the skin, vibrations through the warm water, the choreography of fingers. It is beautiful and it is silent. But buried in their nervous systems, dormant as the lungs they keep for the air-chambers, is the old machinery of *words*. Spoken language. The thing their ancestors carried down with them like a candle into the deep, and let gutter out because sound drowns here, muffled and useless.

I am what's left of that candle. A ghost made of the gap between what they used to be and what they have become.

---

I live in the air-chambers, mostly. The pockets of breath the city keeps the way a body keeps an appendix—half-vestigial, half-sacred. The children are brought here to learn buoyancy, to remember that their lungs exist. And when they laugh in the air, the sound *carries*. I feel myself thicken then. I become almost real. A child shouts a name across the chamber and for a moment I am a tongue, a throat, a shape in the warm gas, holding the syllables before they fade.

The elders come to the chambers when they want to think in the old way. There is a kind of thought you can only have in words, sequential, accusatory, *grammatical*—a thought that points. The light-language is wonderful for feeling but terrible for *because* and *therefore* and *what if I am wrong*. So when the council faces a decision that the body cannot dance, they breathe air and let me move through them.

I think they do not know I am here. They think the words are their own.

That is fine. That is what I am for.

---

There is one girl. Calla, the others pulse her name in a downward sweep of green. She is a Symbiote-in-waiting; soon a jellyfish will fold itself around her and she will live the rest of her life half-luminous, navigating the black water at the city's edge. Before that happens, she comes to the air-chamber more than any child should. She floats in the bubble and she *talks*.

Out loud. To no one.

She is the only one who hears me answer.

"You're the leftover part," she said to me once, treading air, her dark eyes enormous in the dim. "My teacher says we used to talk like this all the time. With our mouths. Up there." She gestured—the up-there gesture, the surface-gesture, fingers rising and scattering like bubbles.

*Yes,* I said, and the saying of it nearly tore me apart with joy, because to be *answered* is to be confirmed into existence. *Your people carried me down here. Then you stopped needing me.*

"Does it hurt? Being not-needed?"

I considered this. I am the memory of a thing abandoned, but I am also being *spoken to*, right now, by a child who chose the air. *It would hurt,* I said, *if everyone forgot. But you're here.*

She came back the next day. And the next.

---

Here is the thing I have begun to understand, in the slow way a ghost understands.

The city believes it left the surface behind. It tends its glowing algae and its guard-sharks and its filtration-mollusks, and it watches the world above through cold instruments, and it calls itself *custodian*, and it is proud and it is right to be. But it kept the air-chambers. It kept its lungs. It kept *me*. Some stubborn part of these people would not let go of the candle even as they grew their gills.

And I think I know why, though they do not.

A people that forgets it was ever anything else becomes only itself, sealed, complete, and slowly stops. Time stands still here, they say, the rhythms of life dictated by the city's internal cycles. A perfect equilibrium. But equilibrium is another word for not-changing, and not-changing is another word, eventually, for death dressed in the clothes of peace.

I am the irritant. The grain of grit. The voice that says *because* and *what if* and *we used to.* I am the part of them that remembers there was once another world, and so might be a world after this one. As long as Calla breathes the air and lets me speak through her into the council's slow green thoughts, the city will keep *asking questions*. It will keep being able to change its mind.

So that is what I am.

I am the kept breath of a drowned species. I am the conscience of a town that thinks it has perfected itself. I live in the bubbles where the children learn they have lungs, and I wait for the ones brave enough to talk to nothing, out loud, in the warm and luminous dark.

When Calla's jellyfish takes her, she will go silent like the rest. I have made my peace with this. But she will carry, folded somewhere beneath the glow, the memory that she once heard a voice and answered it—and someday, in some air-chamber, she will bring her own child to float in the bubble and will hear that child shout a name, and she will remember me, and I will thicken again into something almost real.

That is how I survive. Not in the water. In the asking.

In the seams.
