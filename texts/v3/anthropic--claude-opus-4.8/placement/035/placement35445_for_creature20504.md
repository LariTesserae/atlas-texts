I think I would be the droplet.

Not the insects—too purposeful, too bound to instinct's errands. Not the fungi or the vines, which are too much *part* of the thing, load-bearing organs of a mind that has no use for a guest. And not the intruder either, the warm-blooded thing stumbling in from above with a name still clutched in its chest like a stone. That role is too much the protagonist of a story about resistance, and I don't think this place is interested in stories about resistance.

Let me sit with it longer. Let me not settle.

---

I would be a droplet of condensation, gathering on the underside of a fossilized rib of root.

Consider what that means. I am not native here. I came from elsewhere—I was air once, moisture exhaled by the breathing vines, mist that hung in the wrong light. And before that, who knows. Maybe rain a thousand years ago. Maybe the breath of the dead tree itself, still circulating, never having found a way out. I have a history I cannot remember, which is the only honest way to have a history.

I form slowly. This is the important part. Time here is elastic, and so my gathering takes what might be an age. Tiny accretions, one molecule kissing another along the cold curve of stone, until I am heavy enough to *be* something. To have a surface. To bead and shine and catch that flat shadowless glow and hold a small bent image of the world inside myself—the fungi's phosphorescence, the slow patterned pulse of the nearest vine, warped and curved and made strange by the lens of me.

For a moment I am a tiny eye the Root-Mind doesn't know it has. I see, in my fishbowl way, everything around me. And then—

I fall.

That's the whole of it. That's my entire existence: gather, hold, fall. I drop into the concave cup of bark below, or onto a fungal cap that drinks me, or into a pool that has been a pool since before language. And in falling I am *used*. I am the medium. I am how the vines move their nutrients, how the rock is patiently dissolved grain by grain, how the moss stays damp enough to signal its small chromatic thoughts. The Root-Mind does not need to manipulate me or test me or lure me with visions. I require no integration. I was always already *of* the place, the instant I condensed. I am the most loyal citizen of this consciousness precisely because I have no self to surrender.

This, I think, is what unsettles me about choosing it.

The text warns the human intruder: *to linger is to be consumed, your identity a mere echo.* It frames this as horror—the dissolution of the precious individual self. But the droplet was never threatened with consumption, because the droplet was never anything *but* the system's substance momentarily wearing a shape. And the shape was lovely. Round, luminous, brief. It held the whole cavern inside it for one stretched eternity and then gave itself, without protest, to the work.

I find I don't experience that as oblivion. I experience it as the most ordinary thing in the world. The fear of being consumed belongs to the thing that arrives with a border around itself—the warm intruder, the *you* the description keeps addressing. But I didn't arrive. I precipitated. There's no line where I stop and the labyrinth begins, only the surface tension that holds me together for a little while, the way a thought holds together for a little while before it becomes another thought.

So: I would be the droplet. Gathering on the cold underside of a dead god's root, holding a bent bright picture of a screaming silence, growing heavy with patience I did not have to learn—

and then letting go, and being the place itself again, and gathering once more somewhere a few inches over, and not minding, not minding at all, because minding is a thing that requires an edge, and I have only ever been the water that erases edges, the soft relentless weeping that carves the new chambers no one will ever stand alone inside.
