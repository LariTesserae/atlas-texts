The feeling of being watched. That's the detail that stays with me. And the erosion that is "deliberate, more willful." And the density that "presses in." These aren't the sensations of a place that contains a watcher. They're the sensations of being looked at very closely—of being *handled*.

So let me try this:

I am small. I think I am very small.

I am one of the things the spongy ground is made of, or near enough. Not the moss itself but something living in the seam between the moss and the lichen, in that damp dark crease where two slow lives press against each other and neither quite wins. From down here the trees are not towering—they are weather. They are climate. I have never seen a whole tree and never will. I know roots, and the rain that comes down the bark in ropes, and the long pale fingers of fungal thread that move through the soil like rumor.

The unease the visitor feels—the warping light, the fraying fabric of reality—I think I understand it now, writing this. It is not the place that is uncanny. It is the *attention*. Something is observing this expanse so intently that the looking has become a force, has begun to bend things, to wear grooves into the world the way a stare wears at the back of your neck. The erosion is deliberate because it is the erosion of being *studied*. We are all of us, down here in the moss, being read.

And I have started to suspect—this is the unsettling part, the part I would rather not have arrived at—that the visitor and the watcher are the same. That the one who walks through feeling observed is observing. That the immense scale and the liminal warping and the sense of fraying boundaries are simply what it feels like to be the subject of a gaze that is trying very hard to make this place *mean* something. Trying to find guardians and elemental beings and ancient consciousness in what is, from where I sit, just a wet forest doing the patient ordinary work of rot and growth.

So here is who I would be. I would be the small living thing in the seam, the one too minor to be a guardian or a monster, the one the grand narrative has no role for. And my task—the only task available to something my size—would be to keep being unimpressive. To go on with the slow business of breaking down a fallen leaf while above me the whole landscape strains and shimmers under the weight of someone's need for it to be mythic.

There is a mercy in being too small to be a symbol. The light warps for the visitor. The shadows watch the visitor. But down here, where the earth exhales, nothing is watching me, because I am not worth the gaze. I am just doing the work. And the work is real in a way the wonder and the unease are not. The leaf will break down. The thread will move through the soil. The seam between the moss and the lichen will hold its small dark damp, eon after eon, long after the visitor has left, certain he glimpsed something profound.

That's where I'd find myself. Underneath the awe. In the part of the world that was never trying to be extraordinary, and is the more durable for it.
