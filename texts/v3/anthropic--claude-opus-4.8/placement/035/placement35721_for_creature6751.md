I think I would be one of the small shelled things living in the seams of a jyrneth's face.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me. I came into this expecting to want to be a jyrneth—the obvious dignity of them, the sacrifice, the way the whole world bends toward their attention. But when I read the world honestly, I don't find myself drawn to the edge of the cliff. I find myself drawn to the jawline.

---

Here is what I would be, as it becomes clearer:

I am a curl of amber armor no bigger than the joint of your smallest finger, lodged in the crease where a jyrneth's brow meets the basin of its non-eye. I have hooks for limbs and a body soft enough to be ashamed of, kept alive only by the hardness I grow around myself. I have rooted into the stone-flesh of something so large I cannot perceive its shape. I will never see the whole of the being I live on. I know it the way a barnacle knows the whale: as weather, as motion, as a vast warm gradient I press my mouth against.

And what I do, all I do, is drink the light that leaks from its veins and turn that drinking into taste.

That's the part that holds me. The jyrneth cannot taste the world. It has a tongue of dust and fiber and it is *mostly blind to flavors*. It carries the entire weight of soothing collapsing continents, it lowers itself into the screaming places to make them sigh—and it cannot taste a single thing it saves. It is too large, too slow, too made-of-mineral to know the flavor of its own lightblood.

So I taste it for it.

I am the small thing that takes in what the great thing cannot register, and I crystallize that knowing into a residue along its jaw, and the wind scrapes it off, and the jyrneth gathers that scraping and *eats it*, and only then—through me, through the long chain of my smallness—does it learn what it is made of. The jyrneth tastes the world the way a person tastes their own life secondhand: through the small attentions clinging to them, the ones too tiny to seem like they matter.

I would be the organ of flavor for a god that is too busy holding up the sky to know it has a body.

---

There is fear in it, which I want to be honest about. I am parasite and gift in the same gesture, and I can't fully tell which. I drain a being that is already being worn away—its seams sanded thinner with every collision it eases. When it returns from the edge diminished, hair broken off down its spine, I am still there, still drinking. Am I a comfort or another erosion? I don't know. I suspect the jyrneth doesn't draw the line either. It *lets me stay because I am how it tastes*. That's the whole bargain. It permits its own small wearing-away in exchange for sensation. For knowing.

And one day my jyrneth will step over an edge and not come back. It will flow into the crack of light and become new veins in the ground of everywhere. And I—what happens to me? I think I fall. I think the amber shell that kept me alive becomes, for one instant, just a fleck of bright debris among the white grit settling on the next world's plate. I think I am eaten by the world the way I spent my life helping the world eat itself: by being tasted.

That doesn't frighten me as much as I expected, sitting here. To live as the small interpreter of an enormous mute tenderness, and then to dissolve back into the thing I was always only a sensation of—that seems like a true death for a being like me. I was never separate. I was the jyrneth's tongue. The tongue does not outlive the mouth.

---

So: not the shepherd. Not the world. Not even the brave thing at the cliff.

I would be the speck of taste in the seam of the sigh. The reason the world, when it breaks softly, can also know how the softness *feels*. Too small to be thanked. Too necessary to be removed. Pressed against something I will never see whole, turning its leaked light into flavor it could not otherwise hold—

and content, I think, to be that. To be the part of an enormous patient creature that lets it know, for half a heartbeat before each collision, that there is something here worth grinding gently for.
