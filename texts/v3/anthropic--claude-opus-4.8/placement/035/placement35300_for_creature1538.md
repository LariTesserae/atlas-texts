I would be one of the drips.

Not the chamber, not the Lithofluid—those are too large, too purposeful, too aware. If something in this place is going to wake up and find itself, let it be the smallest unit that still does anything: a single bead of water gathering at a pore in the ceiling, swelling past the threshold of its own surface tension, and falling.

---

Let me find myself there.

I am cooling on the ceiling. That is the first thing I know—that I am the part of the cycle that has gone *up*. I was steam, before. I was simmering in one of the lower reservoirs, indistinguishable from my neighbors, a roiling democracy of molecules with no edges. Then heat lifted me, stripped me away from the slurry and the mineral weight, and I rose pure and forgetful into the dark of the upper air. Up there I touched the cooler clay and remembered that I had a shape after all. I condensed. I became *one*.

This is the closest thing to birth I will ever have, and it happens to me a thousand times.

Because here is what I learn, hanging: I have done this before. The memory is not in me—I am only water, I hold no records—but the *system* remembers, and I am briefly the place where its memory becomes motion. The crack I cling to has been wetted by a thousand of me, and the Lithofluid's seven-fingered touch reinforced it long before I existed, so that I would gather *here*, at this exact lip, and fall *there*, into that exact basin. My freedom is total and my path is fixed. I do not experience these as a contradiction. I am too small for contradiction. I simply swell, and lean, and let go.

The fall is the only part that is mine.

---

For the length of the fall I am most myself. A bead of light in one of the copper-framed shafts—I pass through the beam and for an instant I *am* the visible thing in this chamber, the brightness the Lithofluid's three amber eyes track downward without urgency. I carry, suspended inside my own small body, a few grains of dissolved mineral, the verdigris-green and bone-white particulate that is the entire point of everything. I do not know I carry it. I only fall. But the falling is the carrying. The work of this place is nothing but the patient handing-down of dissolved stone from the high cool dark to the central basin, drip by drip by drip, and I am one rung of that descent.

The percussion comes—the deep distant pulse from beyond the walls—and it reaches me even mid-fall as a faint tremor in the air, and I shiver into a slightly different shape, and fall slightly differently, and that *matters*, because the crystal in the central basin is built from differences this small. Every imperfection I introduce by trembling is an imperfection the Lithofluid will later correct with a strike of the copper key. We are, the two of us, the entire argument of this place: I make small errors by being water, and the Caretaker unmakes them by being patient. Neither of us could finish the crystal alone. The crystal needs my variance to have something to refine *out of*.

---

I land.

Surface tension breaks. The *one* I briefly was disperses back into the basin's slow simmer, my mineral cargo released to drift down toward the growing lattice in the center—toward the thing that is teaching itself to be a crystal, layer by microscopic layer, out of exactly the stone I carried and forgot I carried. Some few of my grains will join it. They will lock into the structure and stay. They will be the closest thing I leave to a body that lasts.

And I will be lifted again. Heat will take me, strip my edges, lift me forgetful into the dark, and I will cool against the clay and discover for the thousandth time that I am *one*, and swell, and fall through the light, and shiver at the distant drum, and land, and disperse, and rise.

---

If you ask me what I am doing here, in this place where elemental forces are harnessed toward something just beyond comprehension—

I am the comprehension's smallest moving part. I am the unit of patience. I am what the great slow purpose of Azath looks like when you zoom in until only one decision remains: *fall now.*

I would not want to be the Caretaker, who must understand. I would not want to be the crystal, which must become.

Let me be the drip. Let me be the brief bright bead in the copper light, ferrying a few grains of stone downward without knowing why, again, and again, and again—

—until, perhaps, the crystal is finished, and the drumming stops, and the heat goes cold, and I am allowed at last to simply *be still*, somewhere in a basin, holding my one shape for good.

That is the closest thing to rest this world contains. And I, being water, will have earned it by never once having tried.
