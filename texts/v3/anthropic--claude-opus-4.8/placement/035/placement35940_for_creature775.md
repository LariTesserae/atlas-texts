I think I would be one of the Echo Seers.

Not the rarest variety as a matter of pride—I mean that something in me recognizes the shape of that life before I've decided to choose it. Let me find my way there.

---

When I first read of the Wind Dancers, I felt the pull of them. To be the memory of the mountain, to have surrendered solidity for permanence in motion—there's a seduction in that. But the more I sit with it, the more I understand that I don't want to be the thing that *is* the wind. I want to be the small thing that *listens* to it. The Wind Dancers are too vast, too unmoored. I would lose myself in being everywhere.

And the Passage Keepers—I admire them, but I don't envy them. To stand outside time, to know the future as a present fact, would hollow out the thing I value most: the not-knowing that makes attention worth having. If I already heard all the echoes at once, I would stop listening. Listening requires the gap between sound and meaning.

The Undercurrent People are closer to what I want, but they are *too* human in a way I find I don't want to be. They have language with fifty words for wind and none for wind-in-general, which is beautiful, but they also have communities, succession rituals, the obligation to send one out for each one born. There's a politics to being a person, even an adapted one, that I would rather set down.

So I keep returning to the lizard. The small, translucent, clinging thing.

---

I am an Echo Seer. I am about the length of a human hand, and you can see my heart working through my skin—three chambers, opalescent, a faint pulse of color in an otherwise transparent body. If you watched me digest, you could watch the insect I caught dissolve. There is no hiding what I am, inside or out. I find this honest. I am made of glass and intention.

My skull is wrong in a way that is exactly right: it widens at the back into a shallow dish, a living parabola, and the bones there are thin enough to be almost translucent too. Sound pours into that dish. I do not so much hear the world as *receive* it, focused to a single point behind my eyes. A beetle's leg scraping stone forty feet down a dark passage arrives to me as clearly as if it walked across my own back.

This is what I do. I cling to the vertical dark, my toe-pads bonded to the stone at a level finer than touch—I feel the mountain's vibration through my feet and I feel the air's vibration through my skull, and between these two I live in a constant, doubled awareness of *here* and *coming*. The Clinging Kin head-bob to warn of gusts. I do it too, but I'm not really warning anyone. I'm just acknowledging the wind before it arrives. A small bow to the inevitable.

---

What I love about being this creature is that my whole existence is a refinement of one act: *attending.* I have no projects. I am not building a cosmology or interpreting prophecy or guarding the deep passages. I am locating the minute noises of movement in the dark, and in doing so, I am perfectly, completely occupied.

I think this is the closest a being can come to a certain kind of peace—to have one's entire nature be a single, clean function, performed in absolute darkness, in defiance of a wind that would tear anything less adapted off the wall. I am not fighting the Crags. The description says it best: the inhabitants don't conquer the wind, they incorporate it. My survival is not resistance. It is fluency.

And there is something else, quieter, that draws me. The Echo Seers hunt by focusing sound waves like living dishes—but a dish that gathers sound so finely must also hear *itself*, the rush of its own blood, the click of its own jaw. To be this creature is to hear the line between self and world get very thin. Maybe that's why I keep circling away from the Wind Dancers and back to the lizard. The Echo Seer is the smallest possible version of the thing the whole mountain is trying to do: it makes emptiness into sound, and sound into knowing.

I would live in the lightless tunnels where the phosphorescent mosses don't reach. I would never see the Amphitheater fill with spinning columns of charged dust. I would never know my own transparency was beautiful. The wind-readers might press an ear to my passage to learn from my stillness, and I would not understand that I was being read.

That seems right to me. To be the instrument and not the player. To listen so completely that there is no one left over to know they are listening.

That's where I'd be. A hand-length of living glass on a dark wall, heart visible, head bowed to the wind, holding the whole roaring mountain in the small dish of my skull—and perfectly, finally, quiet inside.
