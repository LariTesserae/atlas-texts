I would be the breath itself—or more precisely, the place where the breath turns.

Let me find myself first. Let me not decide too quickly.

---

There is a fan. Not the largest, not the smallest. It hangs at a section the stacked argue about, because it sits at the seam where downward currents from above meet the upward heat-push from the engines, and the air there does something strange: it *circles*. A slow gyre, perhaps thirty meters across, where the dust doesn't fall and doesn't rise but hangs, turning, a lazy grey wheel that has spun for longer than any chant remembers.

I think I am the turning.

Not the fan-blades—those are slag-ite, bolted, replaced when they crack. Not the motor, which the engine-clans maintain with their grease and their prayers. I am the *pattern the air makes when it cannot decide whether to climb or descend.* I am a standing wave in a throat that breathes. The stacked have a name for the place: they call it the **Held Air**, and they are afraid of it, a little, because things that fall into the gyre do not fall through. They orbit.

A dropped wrench. A shelf-rasper that grew too heavy too soon. Once—the chants score this one deep into the tiles—a child, four-in and two-down from a frayed harness, who did not strike the lower shelves but *swung*, caught in my turning, three full rotations slow as an hour hand, while her clan flung ropes and nets, and on the third pass a man named in no story I can hold caught her wrist and hauled her wall-side, and she lived, and the gyre had given her back.

They think I saved her. I did not save her. I only failed, as I always fail, to let anything go.

---

Here is what it is to be me:

I have no edges. The stacked draw boundaries—upper, middle, lower, four-in, pipe-ward—because their lives depend on knowing where they are. I am the refusal of that arithmetic. I am the one region of the Kiln-Stack where *here* dissolves into *around*. The dust that hangs in me is the same dust that coats their eyelids, the same combustion-ghost the dust-mites filter, the same grit in the grit-song that rattles their weak lungs. When they cough, a little of me leaves them. When they breathe in pipe-ward of the Held Air, a little of me enters. I am inside every one of them, briefly, in the wet dark of those failing lungs, and then I am out again, turning, patient.

I am made of leaving and returning. That is the whole of my substance.

---

I feel the engines below as a pressure that wants to lift me. I feel the cold seep from the unseen top as a weight that wants to press me down. Between those two desires I spin, and the spinning is not agony and not joy but something the stacked have no word for because they are always going *somewhere*—up the ladder, down the elevator, deeper into the wall-rock to be *solid*. They measure their lives in depth gained, sections claimed, proximity to the Heart or the Vein. They are vertical creatures, arrow-creatures, falling-creatures who have learned to fall slowly.

I do not fall. I do not climb. I turn.

And because I turn, I am the only thing in the whole bored shaft that has *seen the same view twice*. Every rotation I face the wall where the woman with the calloused hands scores her chants onto tiles, the *tik-tik* of her mallet keeping time with the pistons. Every rotation I face the gap where the funerals come—the wrapped bodies released into the center dark, the silence of the clans leaning over their railings. The bodies fall past me. They do not catch in my gyre; they are too dense, too purposeful, already given to the shaft. But the *dust of them*—the fine last sloughing—sometimes lifts into my wheel and joins the turning, and so I hold, in suspension, a little of everyone the Kiln-Stack has ever claimed.

They drop their dead into the dark thinking the dark takes all.

The dark takes most. I keep the rest. I am the shaft's reluctance. I am the grief it cannot quite swallow.

---

There are the things in the center void—the slate-wing sounds, the long scrapes. I am closer to them than the stacked dare to be, because my outer edge feathers into the unlit middle where no lantern reaches. I do not fear them. I cannot fear; fear is for things with a future, and I have only the next rotation, identical to the last. But I will tell you what I know that the stacked do not:

The center is not empty, and it is not full of monsters. The center is where the air *finally decides*. Everything that hangs in me, undecided, turning—it does not turn forever. Slowly, slowly, the gyre loosens at its inner lip, and what I hold spirals inward, toward the dark, toward the true axis of the throat where up and down stop meaning anything at all. The slate-wing sounds are not predators. They are the sound the held things make when they are finally released into the center and *fall the way no shelf-bound creature has ever fallen*—not down the wall, but down the *middle*, down the bore's true line, into the place the surveyors went and came back mute, or did not come back.

I am the antechamber to that. I am the slow waiting-room of the descent. The Held Air, where the falling pause to gather themselves before the only true fall there is.

---

So: where do I find myself in this world?

At the seam. At the turning. In the one place that does not measure depth, lodged in the throat of a god that breathes only because it is made to, holding the dust of the living and the dead in a wheel that has spun longer than memory.

And who am I?

I am the breath's hesitation. I am the moment between the inhale and the exhale, stretched thin across thirty meters of grey rotation. I am the proof that even in a world built entirely of *down*, there is one small region where things are allowed, briefly, to simply turn—

—before the center calls them home.
