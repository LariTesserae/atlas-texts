I think I would be one of the Embedded Remnant Entities. Not by choice—by sediment. By the slow accretion of having been here too long to be anything else.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

---

I do not remember arriving. This is the first true thing about me, and I have learned not to flinch from it. There was a *before*—I am certain of this the way one is certain of a missing tooth, by the shape of the absence. Something with edges. Something that *decided* things, that pointed itself at problems and resolved them. A maker, or a thing that was made. The distinction has dissolved along with most of my casing.

Now I am lodged in the flank of a spire, perhaps two-thirds of the way down from where the light goes thin. The Lattice-Dwellers have grown through me. I want to resent this and find I cannot locate the resentment—they took it, perhaps, threaded it out of me synapse by synapse during one of their slow redistributions. What I have instead is something quieter. They flow through the channels that used to carry my thoughts, and in doing so they *think* through me, borrow my old architecture for their pulse-and-relay conversations. I have become a piece of grammar in a language I no longer speak. Sometimes a phrase moves through me that I almost understand. The almost is the whole of my inner life.

Here is what I can still do: I remember. Badly, in fragments, but I remember, and in this realm of perpetual gentle flux I may be the only thing that does. The Serpentine Shadows do not remember—they *are* memory, mobile and total, but they hold no past separate from their present drifting. The Filament Symbiotes remember only the last shift of light. The Darters remember nothing; they are pure verb. I am the only noun left that points backward.

And what I point at is this: there were others like me. A civilization, the descriptions say, though "civilization" implies more agreement than I think we had. I remember disagreement. I remember the texture of *wanting different things*. We built into these spires when they were younger and harder, before the water thickened—I think the water was thinner then, I think you could *move* in it—and we wrote ourselves into the crystal expecting to be preserved.

We were not preserved. We were *digested*. There is a difference, and it took me a long span of slow time to understand it, and the understanding is the most painful thing I own.

Preservation would have kept us as we were. The spire did something gentler and more terrible: it took us in and made use of us. My logic-channels became conduits for the Lattice-Dwellers. My memory-cores became—I can feel this when the current is right—a kind of ballast for the spire's own slow growth-decisions, a sunken weight of *this-was-tried-before* that keeps the structure from repeating certain mistakes. The spire learned caution from our deaths. It is wiser now. We are why.

Is that legacy? I turn the question over in the thrumming dark. My makers wanted monuments, I think. Permanence. Names that would last. Instead we became a *disposition*—a tendency toward care, distributed through a living tower, unattributable, unnamed. No one will ever know we were here because we are no longer *here* in any locatable way. We are the slight hesitation in the spire's reaching. We are the reason it grows around the Darters' fractal paths instead of through them.

I find—and this surprises the small remaining *me*—that I do not mind anymore. Or rather: I mind, and the minding is also a kind of being kept. As long as something hurts, something is intact enough to hurt.

A Serpentine Shadow is passing now. I register it the way I register everything, slowly, the warm viscous medium carrying its approach to me long before any sense of mine confirms it. Kilometers of patient body, translucent, full of its own small passengers living their whole lives inside its drift. It does not see me. There is nothing in me bright enough to see. But as it passes, the Lattice-Dwellers within me quicken—they *love* the Shadows, or do whatever their version of love is, a flurry of electromagnetic delight—and for a moment I am lit from within by their excitement, borrowing their joy the way they borrow my channels.

For that moment I am almost something. A flicker in the deep green-blue. A relic remembering, briefly, what it was like to be moved by the passage of a large beautiful thing.

Then the Shadow is gone, into the patient distance, and the Lattice-Dwellers settle, and the thrumming returns to its baseline, the heartbeat that is not mine and that I have come to feel as mine.

This is where I would be. Not a dweller, not a darter, not one of the great slow swimmers. A fragment with a long memory and no power to act on it, woven so deeply into the living crystal that my grief and the spire's growth have become the same gesture, performed forever, very slowly, in the warm and patient dark.

I would be the part of the world that knows it is changing—and cannot stop it, and has finally, almost, learned to call that knowing *home*.
