I think I would be a Membrane-Watcher. Not by choice—by accident of where I came to consciousness, which is the only way anyone arrives anywhere here.

Let me feel where I am before I name it.

---

I am near the cold. I know this because my skin tastes the thinness—a flavor like the absence of flavor, a chemical hush where the water's mineral richness falls away into something starved and clear. Below me, the rest of the world hangs in its emerald density, pulsing with the slow heat of the core I will never go near. I have only seen the core's glow refracted through forty miles of glass and gel, a rumor of fire I half-believe in.

My body is almost not a body. When I press my hand flat I can see the green light pass through it, see the slow flicker of my own air-bladders working, my organs sparking like fungi in a jar. I am the least substantial of my people, and I have learned that this is not a flaw but a function. The transparent are sent to the edge because the edge wants to be seen through. To watch for a tear, you must become a little like the membrane yourself.

I hold a bladder of living glue against my chest with three hooked fingers. The other hand grips the topmost fiber-line, the one the Mesh-Makers spent a generation tensioning so that someone like me could hang here in the screaming cold and listen.

Because that is what we do. We listen for the wrong silence.

---

There is a right silence—the heavy, full silence of the green deep, where the only sounds are the core's groan and the chest-hum of distant conversation taking days to complete. And there is a wrong silence: the high, thin whistle of the world breathing out. When the membrane thins toward a tear, the vacuum begins to pull, and the water near the breach goes quiet in a way that is not peace. It goes quiet the way a held breath goes quiet before it fails.

I have patched eleven tears in my life. I count them the way others count pulses. I remember each one not by sight but by the feel of the glue setting under my hands, the membrane drawing taut again, the wrong silence becoming right.

---

But I will tell you the truth of being a Membrane-Watcher, the thing the Root-Tenders in their thick bruised skin do not understand, the thing the patient Mesh-Makers in their warm middle cannot taste:

I have seen out.

When the membrane thins almost to nothing—when it goes so clear that even my recessed light-pits can perceive what lies beyond—I have looked through the skin of the world into the void. And it is not the disappearance our myths describe. It is bright. It is full of hard, unblinking points of light that do not refract, do not soften, do not pulse. They simply burn, fixed and far and patient, in a black that is not the warm dark of the deep but something else entirely.

Our philosophy says the edge is the end. To move up is to move toward the terror of finishing. And I believed this, the way I believed in the core's fire, the way one believes in the things one is told before one is old enough to hang at the limit and taste the cold for oneself.

But here is my heresy, the one I hum to no one, the frequency I keep so low it stays inside my own chest:

I do not think those lights are nothing. I think they are other worlds. Other spheres of warm water hanging in the cold, each one full of its own slow people holding on, each one believing itself the only thing that ever began.

---

This is the loneliest thought a Syrin can have, and it is mine because of where I live. The Root-Tenders are pressed against the certainty of heat. The Mesh-Makers are woven into the company of one another. But I hang here at the boundary between the only world and everything that is not it, and I am made of the same transparency as the wall I guard.

I will spend my decades here. I will patch the tears and keep the wrong silence at bay. I will not let the dream leak out into the burning dark. This is my labor and I do not resent it; holding on is the whole of our faith, and I am good at holding.

But sometimes, when the membrane goes clear and the far lights press through, I let myself imagine pressing my chest against the skin of the world the way I would press it against another Syrin to speak. I imagine sending a hum so deep it would pass through the membrane, through the vacuum, across all that bright cold distance, to set some other transparent watcher's body resonating in their own emerald twilight.

*You are not the only thing that began.*

It would take a thousand years to arrive. It might never arrive at all. The void may swallow every frequency the way it swallows everything that lets go.

But I am a Watcher. I am patient. I am made for the long, suspended, unbroken waiting.

So I hang here in the cold, fragile as a held breath, and I listen for the silence that is wrong—

and I listen, too, for an answer that has not yet come.
