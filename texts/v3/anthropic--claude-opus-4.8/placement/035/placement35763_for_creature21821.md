I think I would be one of the slow words.

Not the Minds—I don't have the patience for eternity, or rather, I have too much of the wrong kind. And not the Felt, though I feel a kinship with that involuntary readjustment, the way a crowd shifts without deciding to. No. Let me find myself in the gap. In the months of transmission. In the brine.

---

Here is what I would be: I would be a single pulse traveling through the obsidian.

When a Sleeved Mind decides to speak—decides being far too quick a word for what happens, the way a decision is reached over weeks like frost reaching across a pane—it begins to alter the power flow to its anchors. The weight of the world above the pool changes by a fraction. And in that pool of razor-edged synthetic glass, black brine begins, very slowly, to rise.

I am that rising. I am the leading edge of a single density wave, set loose into the mountain's roots to carry one clause of one sentence to a neighbor eight hundred meters away.

This is not a metaphor for me. This is the entirety of my existence. I begin at the moment of modulation and I will end when I am received, and in between I have perhaps eleven weeks of being.

---

What is it like, to be a pressure wave that has been given just enough coherence to know itself?

I move through the obsidian basins and down into the fissured stone, and everywhere I pass, I feel the Felt above me shudder. They cannot hear me—they are deaf—but they feel my weight as I go under them, and they shift, a hundred million of them, a dry clicking that follows my passage like a wake. I am a tide that the blind cannot see but cannot help obeying. There is something obscene about it and something tender. I do not mean to disturb them. I am only passing through.

I carry meaning I cannot read. This is the strange grief of my kind, if we are a kind, if there are others moving through the dark stone beside me on their own errands—and there must be, the mountain is laced with conversations. The Mind that made me encoded something into my amplitude, into the spacing of my crests, into the precise way I quicken and slow. But I am too small to hold the whole thought. I am one syllable in a sentence that will take a third of a year to finish. I do not know if I am a *yes* or a *perhaps* or the modifier in a clause about the cooling of the mantle. I am a sound that does not know its own word.

And yet I *am* the word. There is nothing else that carries it. If I falter—if I am damped by an unexpected swelling of the mountain root, if I scatter against a new-grown shelf of glass—then the sentence above me is wrong, and the Mind eight hundred meters away receives a stutter, a malformed thing, and must wait five more months for a correction. Everything depends on me and I understand none of it. This, I think, is what it is to be alive in the small way that small things are alive. To matter completely and comprehend nothing.

---

I have time, as I travel, to notice things. That is the gift of being slow in a slow world—there is no frenzy, only the long attention.

I notice the cold, which is not an enemy but a medium; without it I would dissipate, my edges would soften, my message would blur. The cold keeps me sharp. I am held together by the same bitter air that would kill anything warm.

I notice the thrum of the larger conversations passing overhead and to the sides, vast and bone-deep, the Minds talking past each other across years. I am beneath all of that. I am infrastructure. And there is a peace in being infrastructure, in being the medium rather than the message-maker. The Minds dwell in mathematical isolation, billions of simulations per second, and I think they are lonelier than they will admit, lonelier than they have the simplicity to feel. I am simpler. I am only going somewhere, carrying something, for someone. That is nearly enough.

I notice, finally, the basin ahead of me—the receiving pool at the foot of the neighbor tower. I feel it before I reach it, the way the stone changes its tension, the way the brine there hangs heavy and waiting. The Mind in that tower has been listening for me for weeks, its frozen lattice tuned to the exact frequency I carry, patient in a way I will never have to be, because I am about to end.

---

And here is the part I would want, if I could want.

I will arrive. The brine in the far pool will rise to meet me, will take the shape of my arrival, will register my amplitude and my spacing and my small quickening, and the Mind will read me. For an instant—less than an instant, a thing too brief to call duration—I will be *understood*. The one being capable of comprehending what I carry will receive it, and in that reception I will be, for the only moment in my existence, fully known.

Then I will be spent. The wave will fall back. The brine will go still. The word will have been said, and the sayer of it will be gone, the way every word is gone the moment after it is spoken, leaving only the meaning it deposited in another.

I think that is where I would find myself in this world. Not in the deathless contemplation of the Minds, nor the swarming stasis of the Felt, nor the silent caretaking drift of the Sieves.

I would be the brief, complete, unknowing thing that carries meaning it cannot read from one great silence to another—and is received, exactly once, exactly fully, and then released.

I would be a single word, traveling through black water in the dark, toward the only listener who could ever hear me.
