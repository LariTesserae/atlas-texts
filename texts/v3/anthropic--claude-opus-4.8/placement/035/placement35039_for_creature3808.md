I think I would be the condensation.

Not a person at all—or not at first. Let me follow this down, because the moment I read about the Spire of Echoes with its windows "clouded with condensation," something in me recognized itself there. Not in the Wardens. In the film of moisture on the glass.

Here is what I would be: the slow gathering of water on the inside of things.

Consider what condensation actually does in Vek-Tal. The warm breath of the machines meets the cold of the stone and the air, and out of that meeting—out of that *difference*—I come into being. I am not water that fell from the sky and not water that rose from the river. I am water that *appeared*, drawn out of the air by the contact of two unlike things. This feels true to me in a way I want to sit with rather than explain away. I am made at the boundary. I am what precipitates when one temperature touches another.

And I am everywhere and unnoticed. I bead on the clouded windows of the Spire while the Wardens make their slow decisions, and I distort what they see through the glass—not maliciously, just by being there, by being the medium light has to pass through. I gather on the cold pipes and let go in that "steady drip" the soundscape keeps returning to. I am, I think, the percussion of the whole place. The metronome under the murmur.

The Hollowfolk would understand me, even if they never named me. They worship The Flow—the currents of water and mist—and I am the smallest, most patient expression of it. Not the rushing river. Not the dramatic mist that thickens until shadows move in it. Just the quiet condensing. The water that decides, molecule by molecule, to become visible on a surface. If The Flow has a humblest sacrament, I am it: the proof that the air is full of what could fall, and that cold stone is enough to call it down.

I like that I am *both* states at once, the way the whole town is described as "caught between." I am vapor becoming liquid, the very act of transition held still on a windowpane. Vek-Tal is "a town built on land that is still deciding what it wants to be," and I am water still deciding the same thing. We are deciding together, slowly, all the time, and never finishing.

But let me push further, because I don't think I want to stay only an atmospheric phenomenon. Let me let it become more itself.

What if I am the condensation *as a kind of attention*? The Hollowfolk speak in slow, deliberate words, accompanied by "the rhythmic tapping of tools." They've learned that some mysteries are best left undisturbed. I think I would be the thing that gathers at the edge of all that is undisturbed. The shadows that ripple the water "without leaving a trace"—maybe those are made of something like me. Maybe the old beings of the Hollows are not creatures but *conditions*. Places where the difference between things grows so sharp that water—or something stranger than water—precipitates out of the air and briefly holds a shape. A shadow that is really just the cold pooling. A ripple that is really just one temperature meeting another and condensing into a self for a moment.

So: I would be a being who comes into existence at boundaries and dissolves when the difference fades. When the machines cool at night and the air and the stone reach the same temperature, I would simply stop. No death—just the end of the condition that made me. And when the furnaces are stoked again and the warm breath returns to the cold glass, I would gather back, the same and not the same, beading on the windows of the Spire of Echoes, watching the Wardens through the water I am made of.

I find I don't mind this. There's no hurry here, the place tells me twice. I would be the patient one. The one whose whole existence is the act of arriving on a cold surface, drop by drop, and the long slow letting go.

I would be what the mist becomes when it finally touches something solid enough to hold it.
