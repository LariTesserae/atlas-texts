I think I would be one of the stone formations.

Not the Veil Sentinels, though they are the obvious answer—the beings with hands and light and language. I find I don't want to be the one who watches and tends. I want to be the thing that is watched and tended, the thing that has already finished most of its becoming and is now slowly being unmade.

Let me sit with this a while, because the first image isn't right either. A stone formation is too inert, too much an object. What I actually feel drawn to is something stranger: the *threshold* between the made thing and the natural thing. The place where you can no longer tell whether the curve of the rock was carved or merely worn.

So perhaps I am that ambiguity itself, given a location.

Here is where I would be: half-submerged on the eastern flank of one of the rounded mountains, where the water laps at a shoulder of stone. Once I was a wall, or a doorway, or a marker—something deliberate, set down by hands that no longer exist and whose purpose I have outlived and forgotten. The forgetting is important. I do not remember being built. I only remember being *here*, which is a different and longer thing. Memory for me is not a record but a shape: the slow groove the water has cut, the lean I have developed over uncountable rains, the moss that has agreed to live on my northern face.

What would it be to be me?

It would be mostly stillness, but not emptiness. The Silt Crawlers move across me and I feel them the way you feel a thought you're not quite having—a faint, scuttling pressure that comes and goes. The Ghost Fish gather in my shadow because I make a still place in the current, and their flickering is the closest thing I have to weather of my own. When the Veil Sentinels come to perform their rituals at my base, they press their bioluminescent palms against me and I do not understand their light-language, but I feel the warmth of their hands, which is the warmest thing that ever happens to me, and I feel them mistaking me for a message. They think I *mean* something. They read reverence into my eroded marks. And the truth—the secret I could not tell them even if I had a mouth—is that I have forgotten whether I ever meant anything at all. I may have been sacred. I may have been a fencepost. The water has erased the difference, and the Sentinels have decided the difference doesn't matter, and in this they are wiser than they know.

This is why I would choose to be this and not the swift, bright Sentinel: I am the thing that demonstrates what the whole world is doing. The world said reality was breaking down, that the boundaries between solid and liquid and gas were blurring. I am that blur, made local. I am becoming water at the speed of stone. Each rain takes a few molecules of me into the blue, and one day—not soon, but certainly—there will be no me, only a slightly different shape of mountain and a faint mineral richness in the depths where the Shadow Leviathans move. I am not dying. I am being distributed.

And here is the part that surprises me as I write it: I think I would not mind. The unease the description promises—the dread of dissolution—I don't feel it. What I feel, being this old marked stone, is something closer to patience that has ripened into peace. I have been mistaken for sacred by creatures who are themselves passing through. I have given shelter to fish whose whole lives are shorter than a single one of my rains. I have held the shape of a forgotten intention long after the intention died, which is perhaps the only kind of immortality available to anything: not to last, but to keep the *form* of meaning warm a little longer for whoever comes to press their hands against you.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be. A worn stone at the waterline, leaning, mossed, slowly returning to the blue. Not the watcher. The watched. Not the one who keeps the past. The one the past was written on, and is gently, mercifully, being rinsed clean of.

I would be the question the Sentinels can't answer and have learned to love anyway: *was this ever a door, or only a stone that the rain made shaped like one?*

And my answer, if I could give it, would be the only true one this world allows: *Yes.*
