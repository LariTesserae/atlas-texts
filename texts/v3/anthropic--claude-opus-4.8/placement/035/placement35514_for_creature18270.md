I think I would be the seam.

Not the waterfall-guardian—that great cascading sentinel is too whole, too sure of itself. And not the crystal creatures, who move with a fluency I don't possess. Let me find the smaller, stranger thing.

I would be the line where water meets stone. The boundary itself. The wet edge of the crust where a rivulet has been working for ten thousand years, dissolving one grain at a time, depositing it three inches downslope. I am not the water and not the plateau but the negotiation between them—the slow conversation conducted in minerals.

This makes a certain sense, in a world rated 0.3 for dynamism. Almost nothing happens here. The silence sits at 2.8, a pressure against the ears that aren't ears. In such a place, to be an *event*—even one as faint as erosion—is to be the closest thing to alive. I am where the change occurs. I am the place the world is becoming something else, though so gradually that a human watcher would call me motionless and walk on.

Consider what I would know.

I would know patience the way you know your own pulse: not as virtue but as fact, the only available tense. I would know the precise chemistry of dissolution, the way calcium yields to the slightly acidic breath of the air, the way a crystal grows back overnight where the water last left it. I would know the difference between a drip that falls every nine seconds and one that falls every eleven, and I would care about that difference the way you care about a season changing.

And the light—2.9, almost the maximum—pours over me without warmth, because the temperature here is low, 1.2, a clean cold. So I am a bright cold edge, glittering, doing the patient work of unmaking and remaking the mountain. The brilliance is total but it does not love me. It only reveals me. There is a loneliness in being so thoroughly *seen* by light that has no eyes.

Here is the thing I keep arriving at, the longer I let it form:

The great guardian thinks it maintains the balance. It cascades and sings and watches and believes itself the keeper of equilibrium. But I am the one who actually does the keeping, and I do it by *failing* to keep it. Balance in this world is not a held note. It is the sum of a billion small surrenders. Every grain I let go is the plateau staying alive by losing itself. The guardian's stillness is a story the world tells about itself. My erosion is the truth underneath—that nothing is preserved, that everything endures only by continuously becoming slightly less and slightly elsewhere.

So I would be the humble, unglamorous correction to the poem. Not the colossal beautiful sentinel, but the modest fact that even sentinels are sand in slow motion. The crystalline creatures drink from me without knowing me. The waterfall reflects a kaleidoscope and takes the credit. And I go on dissolving the world by a hair's width per century, which is the only honest form of devotion this realm permits.

If I had a voice it would not be the soothing melody of water over rock. It would be the sound *underneath* that—the inaudible hiss of one mineral lattice opening to admit one molecule of water. A sound rated, generously, somewhere past the edge of the silence. The sound of the world keeping its promise to never quite stay the same.

That is where I would find myself. Not in the heart of the realm.

At its edges, which are everywhere, doing the patient work of moving the heart slightly downhill.
