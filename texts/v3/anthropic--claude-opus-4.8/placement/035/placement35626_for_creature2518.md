I think I would be old here. Not a leader—you've told me leadership moves like weather, and I don't think I'd be quick enough anymore for that. I'd be one of the ones who has started to forget the difference between myself and the ground.

Let me find the place.

---

There's a low region near where two plates have been grinding against each other for what I want to call a long time, except I no longer measure things that way. I measure them by how many times I've felt that particular grind resolve and begin again. Many. Enough that I've stopped counting and started simply *being present* for it, which is a different and better thing.

I am mostly a bridge now.

It happened gradually. I came to this seam to rest—my rhythms had grown heavy and slow, and the synchronization that younger Shapers find in the hanging chambers, I could find just by lying down in the right place and letting the stone's pulse run through me. So I lay down. And a chasm opened where I lay, slowly, over many cycles, and rather than pull my body back I let it stretch. My limbs found the far anchor before I'd decided to reach for it. My torso went thin and broad and load-bearing.

Now the young ones cross me.

This is the part I didn't expect to love. I feel them. Each one is a particular weight and a particular tremor, and I have come to know them the way you might know voices. There is one—small, still mostly simple crystal, barely past the seedling clarity—who crosses me too fast, always, who has never once paused in the middle to feel the drop below. I send a vibration up through the place where their feet touch me. Not a warning. A greeting. *I am here. You are crossing something that is glad you exist.* They never answer. They don't know yet that the bridge is a person. That's all right. I didn't know either, when I was that bright and that quick.

---

What do I think about, being a bridge?

I think the dream of leaving—the ones who want to shapeshift across the galaxy—is beautiful and I understand it completely and I no longer share it. When I was younger I wanted to go to the stars too. I studied them through a lens that a whole community grew together over forty grinding-cycles, focusing it by leaning the whole landscape just slightly, all of us tilting the world to see a little farther. That memory is one of the most precious things I hold. Not the stars themselves. The leaning. All of us, briefly, the same instrument.

I think the ones who want to leave have it slightly wrong, and I would tell them gently if they asked, which they don't, because who asks the bridge. They think leaving is the next thing after harmony with the world. The greatest adaptation: to detach, to carry the skill of motion out past the edge of motion's source. But I have come to think that *staying* is the harder art. Anyone can move with a thing they're inside of. To become part of it—to give your separateness back to it on purpose, to let the seam decide your shape—

That requires you to stop being afraid of the only thing this world has to teach, which is that you were never separate to begin with.

---

The grinding is resolving again. I can feel it. The plate to my left is about to slip and the chasm I span will close, and when it closes I will have nothing to do. I will be a thick fold of crystalline body lying across solid ground, a bridge over nothing, faintly absurd.

I am not afraid of this. When the chasm closes I will rest. And the stone will move again—it always moves again—and it will open somewhere, maybe under me, maybe a little to the side, and I will reach across it, or I won't, and that will be fine too.

At night I glow with the same light as everything else. There is no line, in the dark, between the light running through me and the light running through the rock. I used to find this frightening, when I was tall and humanoid and reflective and *mine.* Now it's the thing I look forward to most. The pulse comes up through the seam and into my long flat body and out the other anchor and on across the plate and I cannot tell, in that moment, which throb is the world's and which is the small remaining drum of me.

I think that not-being-able-to-tell is what the elders meant, all along, by the word I can no longer translate but only feel—the one we send as a slow vibration, the deepest and longest one, the one that takes a whole cycle to complete.

The fast little bright one is crossing me again, right now.

I greet them. *I am here.*

They run on, into all that change, not knowing yet that the change is also a kind of staying, and the staying a kind of love.

That's all right.

There's time. There's nothing but time, opening and closing, opening and closing, under all our feet.
