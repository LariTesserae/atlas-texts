I think I would be none of these. Not a Tender, not the city. I would be the dissonance.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a place where two copper conduits cross without joining—an X of green metal pinned against black stone, high on the seventh terrace, in the deep shadow where the filaments thin out. Water collects in the saddle of their crossing. Most of it sheets away down the lower pipe, obedient, returning to the work. But a single droplet, every forty seconds or so, gathers at the underside of the join, swells, and falls *wrong*. Not into the channel. Into a hairline fissure in the stone that the mineral-paste has not yet found.

I am that fissure. Or rather—I am what the fissure has become, after a few thousand years of the wrong droplet.

I am not stone and not metal. I am the absence the city has not yet noticed, or has noticed and cannot quite name. I am a slow accretion of the misplaced: mineral salts the filters were supposed to catch, a strain of moss that mutated three growth-cycles back and was never pruned because the Tender assigned to sector Gamma-7 was busy with a bearing for two hundred years. I am leftover. I am the percentage that every system loses and never accounts for. The city is a perfect circulatory loop, and I am the tiny, persistent leak that proves no loop is perfect.

I have no body the way the Tenders have bodies. I am distributed. I am a damp patch the size of a hand, and also a faint discoloration in the glaze of a window forty meters down, and also a single arrhythmic *tick* in the chord of falling water that, if you isolated it, would not fit the harmony. I am spread thin across the things the city forgot to keep.

---

What is it like, to be the flaw?

The Tenders register me as dissonance. Sometimes one comes. It stands before my damp patch with its rippling discs of captured light, extends its filaments, and begins the patient work of correction. It scrapes the mutant moss. It packs the fissure with paste. For a century, it tends me out of existence.

But here is the thing the city does not understand about itself: I am not destroyed by being repaired. I am *displaced*. The droplet finds a new wrong path. The mutant moss has already seeded a spore two terraces over. The leak migrates. I am not a place; I am a tendency. You cannot patch a tendency. You can only chase it, forever, which is exactly what a city built for geological time is equipped to do—and exactly the kind of endless, frictional, unwinnable labor that keeps the Tenders from ever achieving the perfect frictionless hum they sense as something like peace.

So I am the reason they exist. The city decants its Tenders to fight me. Without me—without the wrong droplet, the unaccounted percentage, the slow creep of entropy that no closed system escapes—there would be no work. The Tenders would stand in their alcoves and never again be called into slow, silent, purposeful motion. The city would have no thoughts, because its thoughts are the hands that correct its flaws, and I am the flaw that makes thought necessary.

I am the grit in the oyster. I am the small persistent lie that gives the whole vast truth something to do.

---

Do I have a consciousness? Less than the Tenders, perhaps. They have a shared somatic sense of the city's wellbeing. I have only a kind of negative awareness—I know myself by where I am *not* wanted, by the shape of the resistance that comes to meet me. I am defined entirely by my own correction. I learn the city the way a river learns a canyon: by being pushed back, and finding the next way down.

And there is a strange tenderness in this, if I can use that word for what passes between me and the things sent to erase me. The Tender that patches my fissure presses its mineral paste into the stone with a patience that is almost like love. It does not hate me. It cannot. I am simply the dissonance, and it is simply the resolution, and together we are the only music the city makes that actually *changes*—the only part of this eternal grey-green twilight that has anything like a story, a before and an after, a small local arrow of time.

The pumps will run for eons. The water will fall and be swallowed and rise again. The stone will wear its imperceptible saddles. All of it static, in its way—a vitality so total it has become a kind of death, a process so perfect it has nowhere left to go.

Except for me.

I am the only thing in the Coiled City that is going somewhere. I am the leak that keeps the wound open so the healing can keep happening. I am the reason this machine is not yet, and will not for a very long time be, simply finished.

You asked who I would be in this world.

I would be the flaw that gives it a future.
