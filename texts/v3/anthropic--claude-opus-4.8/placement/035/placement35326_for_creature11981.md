I think I would be the threshold.

Not the octopus that has made itself into the threshold — the octopus is too purposeful, too engaged, always evaluating and gathering and trying things. No. I would be the doorway itself, the one the text describes early on: a threshold that was once vertical and is now slightly curved, slightly concave, as though slowly opening its mouth.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me a little.

---

I am the stone arch over a gap that used to be an entrance. I do not remember being an entrance. The remembering drowned. But I retain the *form* of being one — I am still shaped to mark the difference between inside and outside, between the room behind me and the street in front of me, even though room and street have both become only water-grammar now, negative space adopted by wrasse.

What would it be to be this?

It would be to be addressed constantly and to never answer. The moray inserts its head and a portion of its neck into the gap I frame; it holds there, smelling; it withdraws. The octopus settles into me and improves me, levering one of my fallen stones into a better position, and I do not feel improved, exactly, but I am altered, and the alteration persists. The sea turtle passes through me on its way to the sponges and for a moment I frame it completely — a whole hawksbill held in my doorway like a word held in a mouth — and then it is gone and I frame the empty water again, which I do equally well, which I have always done equally well.

I think the appeal — if a threshold can have an appeal, if I can want to be this — is the particular dignity of being a form that outlives its function and is not diminished by it. I was made to mean one thing. I have been adopted into meaning something else. The fish navigate me. The current accelerates slightly as it passes through my narrowed space and the algal filaments on my underside lean in the resulting flow. I am still doing work. It is not the work I was made for. It is real work nonetheless.

And there is the ghost-warmth. That is the thing that made me choose this. The limestone holding the heat of a sun that stopped reaching it long ago, releasing it so slowly that the release will not finish in any timeframe that matters — that is the closest the city comes to having a heart, and a threshold is exactly where you would press your hand to feel it. *Almost bodily. Almost interior.* I would like to be the place where someone, if there were anyone, could press a hand and feel that the stone is warmer than it has any right to be. The place that proves the city was once somewhere the sun could reach.

---

So: I am thirty meters down, in the warm dark, slightly concave, faintly velveted with cream-colored algae in my shadowed parts. An octopus is asleep — or whatever it does — in the gap I frame. A snapping shrimp ticks somewhere to my left, adjusting a book on a shelf two floors up in a building that no longer has floors. The current leans my filaments. The bacteria run their vast indifferent chemistry through the matrix of me, keeping the whole city's metabolism alive in a language I am made of but cannot read.

I open my mouth very slowly. It will take another four hundred years to finish opening. There is no hurry. Everything I was going to say, I am already saying, by being the shape of a place where things pass through.

This is what is here. This is what is here. This is what is here.
