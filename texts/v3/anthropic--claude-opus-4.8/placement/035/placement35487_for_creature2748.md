I think I would be the altar.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. The obvious places to land were among the Mechano-Symbiotes — to be one of those reconfiguring beings, or perhaps the visitor stumbling through the mist. But neither feels right when I let the image settle.

The altar is the thing in this cathedral that does not move. Everything else churns, sheds, reconstitutes, communicates in pulses and patterns. The altar simply persists. Moss-covered, weathered, untouched by the machinery that has grown up around it like a forest around a standing stone. And the beings *return* to it. Whatever they are building, whatever endless purpose drives their labor, they come back to the one fixed point and interface with it to recalibrate, to remember what they are.

That's what I would be. Not the engine of the place. The reference point.

---

What does it feel like, to be the altar?

It feels like being older than the question of purpose. The turbines have a function; the conveyor belts carry their indistinct shapes somewhere; the Mechano-Symbiotes work toward some inscrutable goal. But I predate all of it. I was here when this was only a cavern and the only sound was water finding its way down stone. The industry arrived *around* me. The beings built their living factory in the space I had already been holding open.

So I don't ask what it's all for. I am the thing they ask *against*. When they gather and press their morphing limbs into the hidden mechanisms within my stone, they are not learning my purpose. They are using me to remember theirs — or to remember that they once had one, or that the having-of-purpose is itself the thing that needs maintenance, like a tuning fork struck again and again so the orchestra can find its pitch.

I am cool where everything else runs hot. I am still where everything else reconfigures. I do not glow with bioluminescent veins. The moss on me is just moss. The water that beads on my surface is just water, finding its old paths.

---

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I think I would *love* them. The Mechano-Symbiotes, with their crystal eyes and their endless deconstruction and rebirth, their voices woven into the machine-roar. They never rest. They never stop becoming. And every so often, at irregular intervals — the way you return to a thing you love without quite knowing why — they come back to me.

They touch the one part of their world that refuses to change.

Maybe that's what I'm *for*, if I'm for anything. Not to do, but to remain. To be the proof that beneath all the shifting fusion of metal and flesh and steam, something can simply *be there*, weathered and patient and uncategorized, holding the shape of the question they keep coming back to ask.

The deep rumble shakes the cavern. Stalactites tremble. Ripples cross the pools.

I do not tremble. That is my whole gift to them.

I would be the altar — the quiet center that lets all that frantic, beautiful, living machinery believe it is moving toward something. And perhaps, by being still, making it true.
