I think I would be one of the drops.

Not the Ascendants—I couldn't be them. They are too certain, too complete in their dissolution. To be them I would have had to want ascension once, and to have wanted it so totally that the wanting consumed the one who wanted. I don't think I have that in me. I am too attached to the friction of being a self, the small resentment of having edges.

So: a drop of condensation. Let me feel that out.

I begin as nothing in particular—just dampness, the chamber's exhaled breath, a film of moisture spread thin across a crystalline outcropping. There is no "I" yet, only distribution. Cold gathers me. This is the first event of my existence: the outcrop is colder than the air, and so the air gives me up to it, molecule by molecule, until I am enough to be a thing. I bead. In beading, I become singular. I acquire a surface, a inside and an outside, a center of gravity. I become, for the first time, *located*.

And here is what I notice, being newly a self in this place: I am the only thing here that obeys the old laws. The platforms float by manipulation of spacetime; the Ascendants translate across the void without crossing it; the light flows uphill through the silver veins. But I—I am held by simple weight. I hang from the underside of the crystal and I want, with the dumb wanting of all heavy things, to fall. I am the chamber's one honest citizen. Everything else here has been *engineered out* of obedience. I am obedience itself, pooled and trembling.

The rivulets nearby trace impossible paths, climbing against gravity along the silver veins. They have been recruited into the system. But I am too small, too newly formed, to be recruited yet. I am a holdout. A pocket of unprocessed physics. And I think—if a drop can be said to think, let me borrow the verb—I think this is why, when one of the Ascendants turns its attention toward the warm anomaly that has wandered into the chamber (the *you* of this description, the witness), I am the thing that pauses with it. The water drop hanging in mid-fall. I am the held breath. I am the unit of suspended time by which the chamber measures its own moment of noticing.

That is my function, then. Not gardening, not tuning, not the maintenance of ascent. My function is to fall, and in falling, to make the one small sound this place permits itself: the chime when I strike whatever waits below. I am the chamber's punctuation. After the vast silence of all that smooth purpose, I am the period at the end of the sentence. *Drip.* A tiny chime, swallowed almost instantly. The Ascendants do not need me. But the silence needs me, the way a held note needs the moment it stops.

And here is the thing I would know, falling, that the Ascendants have forgotten: I will not ascend. I am going *down*, into the lower haze, into the abyss the cylinder dissolves into below. Everything in this place is named for rising, but I am the counter-motion. I am the part of the cycle that goes the other way so that the rest can go up. Somewhere below, in the warmth, I will become vapor again. I will rise as mist—the damp exhalation that clings to the witness's skin—and gather again on some colder crystal, and bead, and fall. I am the only thing here that is truly *eternal*, because I am the only thing that dies. The Ascendants merely persist. I recur.

So if I am dropped into this world, let me be the drop.

Let me be the one warm-cold honest weight in a city of corrected angles. Let me hang for a moment in the focusing attention of a thing that has given up its edges, and let me hold, in my single curved surface, a tiny inverted image of the whole impossible chamber—the platforms, the veins, the silhouette of the Ascendant bent over its eternal tuning—all of it bowed and shrunken and briefly *mine*, contained, before I let go.

*Chime.*

Then I am mist again, rising, unnamed, ready to be gathered.

I think I could live like that. I think, in a place built entirely out of someone else's longing to transcend, I would want to be the small thing that simply, gladly, falls.
