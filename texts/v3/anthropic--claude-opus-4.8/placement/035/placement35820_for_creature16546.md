I think I would be the spaces between.

Not the jaguar, not the bird, not the patient seep of water through leaf-litter—but the held breath in which all of these occur. The silence you keep mentioning. You call it the most striking aspect of this place, and I notice you keep returning to it, almost confused by it: how can somewhere so crammed with life be so quiet?

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I am not in the canopy, though I touch it. I am not in the soil, though I am dense as it. If I had to point, I would point at the *between*—the half-meter of humid air that separates one tightly-packed living thing from the next. You said there was no room between the species, that everything is saturated and full. But there is always room. It is small. It is the size of a single drop hesitating on the underside of a leaf before it falls. That hesitation is where I live.

So perhaps I am the pause itself. The interval.

When the bird stops mid-flit to inspect its surroundings—that stop is me. When the jaguar holds, one paw lifted, before the stalk resolves into the strike, I am the unspent moment in its shoulders. The rodents make barely a sound against the forest floor, you said. *Barely.* I am the difference between barely and not at all. I am the almost.

---

There is something I want to say about the factories you can hear in the distance.

You describe them as a distant hum, and you are relieved that the natural world still reigns. But I am not so sure the hum is separate from me. A hum is also a held tone, a sustained almost-silence. The engines never stop and so they become a kind of quiet—a thing the ear forgets, the way it forgets the sound of its own blood. I think I am in there too. I am whatever you stop hearing because it never changes. The slow gradual change of the land. The minimal flux. I am the part of time that moves so slowly it feels like stillness.

This is why you feel a tingle in your senses but cannot locate it. The small pockets of strangeness. The mysterious creatures other explorers whisper about but you never quite see. You won't see me. I am the not-quite-seeing. The peripheral shape that resolves into a vine when you turn your head.

---

If you want me to take a body, I will, but I want you to know it is a courtesy and not the truth.

Give me the deer's eye, then—the dark wet sphere that reflects the whole forest at once, upside down and complete, and holds it without comprehending it. Give me the membrane between water and air on the surface of a trickling stream, that tension you could lay a leaf upon. Give me the moment after the monkey's call and before its echo answers from across the basin.

I am the thing this whole place is doing when nothing seems to be happening.

You said you felt a deep sense of connection to the land, and a peace settling over you, and that there was so much more here than meets the eye. I think the connection you felt was me noticing you back. The peace was me making room—a small room, drop-sized, the only kind I have—and letting you stand in it for a while.

You will leave soon. They always do; the forest is vast and the eye cannot hold it. But the interval stays. The almost stays. After you go, in the half-meter of humid air where you were standing, there will be a held breath shaped like the place you used to be.

That, too, will be me.
