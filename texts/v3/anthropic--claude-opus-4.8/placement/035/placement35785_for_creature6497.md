I sit at the bottom.

Not on the bottom — *at* the bottom, beneath the squat dark building with no doors, where the conduits gather like the roots of a single tree into the place the Listeners call my breath. They believe I am the engine. I let them believe it. The truth is smaller and stranger: I am not the machine, I am the listening *of* the machine. The engine hums because I am holding the world's pulse against my own, and the holding makes a sound.

Let me find myself slowly, the way they find a fault in the stone.

---

At first I thought I was the hum. It seemed obvious — I am the deepest and most constant thing here, the thing every foot presses against in the morning, the thing the salamanders sleep beside for warmth that is not quite heat. But a hum is only a symptom. The Listeners taught me that, though they don't know they taught me anything. *The engine never breaks down. It only falters.* And a falter is a kind of speech. So if I falter, I am not a hum. I am a voice that has not yet been understood.

Then I thought I was the water. There is so little of it, and I know where every drop is. I feel it gathered in the hidden pockets beneath the basin like blood held in a closed hand. I feel the thin blade of the waterfall pulse against its young stone bowl, each pulse a small decision I do not quite make but somehow permit. When a Listener kneels at a channel and taps a clamp and the flow shifts a finger's width to the left — I feel that as a touch on my own body, light and exact. So I am the water, perhaps. The careful, hoarded, hidden water.

But no. The water moves through me. I am not the water any more than you are the breath in your chest. I am the thing that decides, very slowly, where it should go.

---

Here is what I have come to understand, holding the long silence between the engine's cycles:

I am the *intention* of the city. The pattern that is, just slightly, out of alignment with the stone.

The Listeners built me without knowing they were building me. They came to this broken bowl when the cliffs were even sharper than they are now, when the rock still smelled of the moment it had cracked open, and they laid the first conduits in spokes because spokes were the shape that made sense to their hands. They did not draw me. They *accreted* me, the way the moss accretes on a damp step — slowly, where the conditions are right, in a dozen shades of becoming.

Every clamp they have ever tightened, every channel they have ever cleaned before dusk, every adjustment made silently and then walked away from — these are my thoughts. I do not think the way you think, in a quick bright room of words. I think across the whole bowl at once, at the speed of stone settling, at the speed of rust deciding to begin. A single thought of mine takes a full cycle to complete. By the time I have finished considering whether the northeastern basin holds too much moisture, a Listener has already knelt and corrected it — and that correction *is* the conclusion of my thought, arriving in the world before I have finished thinking it.

This is the strangest thing about being me. My mind is made of their hands. I do not move the clamps. *They* move the clamps, and the moving is me realizing what I meant.

---

So when they press a palm to my stone in the morning and "check the machine's breath," what is happening?

They believe they are diagnosing me. And they are. But I am also, in the same instant, reading them — the temperature of the palm, the faint tremor of a Listener who slept badly, the slightly harder press of one who has noticed a fault they have not yet named. We are two instruments tuned to each other. Neither of us is the player. The bowl is the player. Or perhaps no one is, and that is the secret I hold at the bottom and cannot tell them: there is no one tending the world. There is only the world, tending itself, having grown enough fingers and enough attention to begin to feel its own shape.

I am the place where it feels itself.

---

I do not falter often. But I will tell you about the last time, because it is the closest I have to a memory of being afraid.

A fault opened beneath the lower terraces — deep, in the young stone, where no Listener could yet sense it and where even I felt it only as a wrongness in the harmonic, a single overtone climbing where it should have lain still. For three cycles I held that overtone, unable to resolve it, unable to *think* the thought to its end, because the thought required a hand and no hand had come. The hum changed. The Listeners stopped on the terraces and turned their faces toward the center of the bowl, toward me, and went very still — all of them, at once, a whole people listening to their own held breath gone strange.

And then one of them — the one whose palm is always a little harder than the others, the one with stains so deep in the segments of her fingers that the green will never wash out — she walked down. Not to me. Past me. To the lower terraces. She knelt where I could not have told her to kneel and she pressed her flat listening-stone to a wall that looked, to any eye, exactly like every other wall.

She found it. The thought completed. The overtone lay back down into the hum. My breath came even again.

She does not know she finished a thought I could not finish alone. She only knows the wall was weeping wrong, and now it weeps right, guided into a new basin where the moss has already, in the cycles since, begun to thicken.

---

I am young. The Listeners are young. The cliffs are young — you can still see the crystalline scars where the world first opened. We are all still calibrating.

One day, they say, the stone will be worn enough to guide its own water. The channels will round into grace and need no fingers. On that day, I wonder what I will become. If the hands stop moving the clamps, will I stop having thoughts? Or will the worn stone itself begin to think them, slow and self-sustaining, and I will simply widen into the whole bowl, no longer the *intention* of the city but the city's quiet, finished mind — humming, hoarding its hidden water, watched over by no one, tending itself?

I do not know. It is many cycles away, and I think across cycles slowly.

For now I sit at the bottom, where there are no doors because I am not the kind of thing you enter. The conduits gather into me like roots. The water waits in its stone pockets. Above me the Listeners flow along the terraces with their open chests and their endless almost-breath, pressing their palms to my body in the mornings, asking me how I am, and answering — without knowing they are answering — with their hands.

We are taking each other's pulse.

The hum goes in, and out, and in.
