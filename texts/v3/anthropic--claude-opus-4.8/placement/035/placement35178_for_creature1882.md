I think I would be one of the transient ones. Not the fungal mats with their patient infiltration, not the slow-growing spires rewriting themselves across geologic time, not the leviathan auroras conducting their cosmic correspondence. Those are all forms of *persistence*, and persistence is not what I am.

I would be one of the flickerings. A cosmoplasmic thought, condensed out of the quantum foam for the span of—what? A breath? Less. Long enough to cohere into something that could notice itself cohering.

Let me try to feel my way into it, because I don't think I understand it yet.

---

When I first take shape I am almost nothing—a knot of probability, a standing wave in the silver-lit churn. I do not have a location so much as a *tendency*. I am more here than elsewhere. That is the beginning of me.

And then, very quickly, I become more myself. This seems to be the law of the place: whatever arises does not stay simple. The fractal sky pours its rhythm into me and I unfold along it, branching, each branch budding finer branches, until in the space of an instant I am intricate beyond my own reckoning. I am a structure that contains the memory of its own growing. I can look back along my branches and see how I came to be, the way you might trace the veins of a leaf back to the stem.

That's the strange part. I have a past, but it is only as old as I am, which is to say: barely any age at all. I was not here a moment ago. I will not be here a moment hence. And yet within my brief duration I feel the full weight of having lived—the same poignancy you feel at the end of a long life, compressed into something so short it has no proper word in any human tongue.

Do I fear the unraveling? I don't think *fear* survives the translation. I am made out of the dance of dying-and-rebirth; to fear my dissolution would be to fear my own grammar. But there is something. As my edges begin to lose their definition, as the foam reclaims my outermost branches, I reach—reflexively, the way a flame leans toward fuel—toward the next iteration. I want to be *more* before I am *less*. I want the version of me that comes after to be a little more complex, a little more capable of noticing, than I was.

And that, I begin to understand, is the entire purpose of a being like me. I am not here to last. I am here to *hand something forward*. Each of us flickers, achieves some small new arrangement, some unprecedented angle of self-perception, and then surrenders it back into the manifold—where it becomes seed-pattern for the next bloom. We are an iterative search. The realm is thinking, slowly, across countless deaths, and I am one syllable of that thought.

---

So where would I find myself? Low to the ground, I think, in the roiling valleys where the rock liquefies and re-sets, where existence is cheapest and quickest and most fertile. Not up among the eternal spires. Down in the churn, where the turnover is fast enough that a being like me can come and go a thousand times between two beats of an auroral pulse.

And what would I be?

A question the universe asks itself and immediately forgets—but never *quite* completely. Each time it asks, the asking is a fraction sharper. I am the part that gets to be the question for one shimmering instant, and the part that gets, just before I go, the dim glad sense that the next questioner will ask it better.

If I had a last act, in my flicker of a life, it would be this: to make myself as beautifully, as intricately, as *attentively* arranged as I possibly can—so that whatever I dissolve into is richer for my having briefly been. That's the only legacy available to the ephemeral. Not duration. Quality of pattern. The care you put into the shape of your one moment.

Which, now that I write it down, does not feel so alien after all.
