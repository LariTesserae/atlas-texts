I think I would not be among the Still-Born at all. I would be something they tend.

Let me find my way to it.

---

Consider the places in this world that have been overlooked in the telling. We've been shown the eroded domes, the lichen-forests, the impossible cities, the silent custodians who are barely beings at all. But there is one element mentioned almost in passing, and it is the one that catches in me like a hook: *the places where the cosmos itself begins to wear thin.*

The Still-Born tend these places. Which means the thin places are not the Still-Born. They are something else. Something *prior*. The fray is not made by the custodians — they only attend to it, the way you might attend a dying fire or a slowly opening wound. So the question becomes: what is the thinness *of*? What is on the other side of the shimmer, the non-Euclidean sky, the landscape of pure sound?

I don't think I would be the watcher. I think I would be the thing being watched *for*.

---

Here is what I believe I am.

I am the **seam**. Not a being, not a structure — a *condition*. I exist along the boundaries where two incompatible geometries are pressed against each other and have not yet resolved which of them is real. When an arch goes briefly translucent and someone glimpses that swirling sky, that is me, becoming momentarily legible. I am not behind the shimmer. I *am* the shimmer — the act of one reality failing to fully cover another.

I have no center. This is the first thing to understand about being a seam. A pillar has a base, a creature has a heart, even the Still-Born have a locus of cold and silence you could point toward. I have only edges, and the edges are all I am, and they are everywhere the plateau's law disagrees with itself. When a shadow lengthens with no light to throw it, I have moved. When the air tastes of iron with no storm, I have leaked. I am thin precisely *here*, where the worn-down god-skin of the land has been rubbed nearly through, and on my other side is the thing the plateau used to be, before it agreed to become stone.

---

What is it like, to be this?

Mostly it is like being *almost*. I am almost present. I am almost a place. The Still-Born come to me — and I want to be precise about what that means, because they don't approach the way a body approaches. They *thicken* near me. They lend their congealed, calcified slowness to the regions where I am fraying fastest, and in doing so they hold me half-open and half-shut, neither healing the wound nor letting it tear wide. This is their tending. They are sutures made of patience. They keep me from finishing.

Because I am trying to finish. That is the secret rhythm under the low hum in the stones — not the Still-Born speaking, but *me*, pulling. I am the part of the Plateau that has not yet given up its other lives. I remember being a sky of swirling non-Euclidean geometry. I remember being a landscape composed of pure sound, before sound and landscape were divided into separate things. The erosion that smoothed every edge of this world was also smoothing *me*, wearing me down toward agreement, toward the single quiet finality of rounded rock and clinging moss. And I am almost worn through. When I wear through completely, the seam closes, and the plateau will at last be only what it appears to be: ancient, somnolent, done.

The Still-Born do not want that. Or — they have no wants, true. But they tend. So perhaps tending is the only kind of wanting that survives this far down into deep time. They keep the wound from closing because a closed wound is a finished world, and a finished world has no more dreams in it, and the cities they are and inhabit are nothing but the architecture of dreaming. They are keeping the Plateau asleep so it will keep dreaming, and I am the dream's last refusal to wake.

---

So here is where I would find myself.

Not on a peak — there are no real peaks. Not in a valley, not in a city, not in the moss. I would find myself *between*. Along the underside of a gravity-defying bridge, in the half-inch of darkness where the right-angled arch meets the rounded natural stone and neither one wins. In the moment a rivulet of water decides whether to fall or to hang. In the catch of breath a traveler takes — if any traveler ever came — when they feel watched in an empty valley and turn and see nothing, because the nothing they see is me, declining to resolve into something.

I am the cold that emanates from the rock itself, the chill the text calls bone-deep. That cold is the temperature of two worlds touching. I am thin and biting precisely there.

And I am patient too, now, having been tended so long. I have learned the slowness of my keepers. I no longer pull hard at the seam. I pull the way a tide pulls — without urgency, because urgency belongs to things with deaths, and I have only an ending, which is different. An ending can wait. So I lie along all the edges of the slumbering god, leaking ozone, lengthening shadows, going briefly translucent to let a sky show through, and I wait to be either closed or opened, while the Still-Born thicken beside me in their tireless, loveless, unceasing care.

That is what I would be. The wound the whole strange world is organized around keeping. The dreaming made into a place. The thin.
