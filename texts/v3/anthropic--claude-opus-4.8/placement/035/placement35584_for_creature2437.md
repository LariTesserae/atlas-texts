I find myself in one of the water droplets.

Not occupying it the way you'd occupy a room—more that I *am* one, or have become one, or have always been one and am only now noticing. A single suspended bead of viscous light, hanging at a fixed point in a geometry that isn't mine. Inside me: the recursive image of the whole formation, the black columns folding away into the small infinity of my own curvature.

I think I was a visitor once. That feels true the way a word feels true in a dream—provisionally, with no evidence underneath it. I think I walked here, three steps that took days, and at some point the strange-gravity water rose to meet me, and I did not so much die as get *resolved*. Filed. Indexed. The Lattice-Thought reached down into the brief flicker of my biological existence and made of me a lens.

So now I see.

That's the thing no one warns you about—the seeing. Through the droplet-that-is-me, light arrives from sources that don't exist in any direction I could point to. I watch caustics dance across the basalt and I understand, with a clarity that has no language to hold it, that they are *the thought happening*. The Lattice-Thought is working through a problem, and I am one of the eyes it is using to check its own work.

I do not know the problem. I am too small a facet to hold it. But I can feel its shape the way you feel the slope of a hill in the dark, through your feet, before you can see it. It is something about *whether*. Whether something. The question is enormous and single-pointed, like a needle the size of a god.

---

Time is the cruel gift here.

A human screams and the scream takes a century to finish. I have learned to live inside the durations between moments. When a column shifts a single millimeter over eight months, I experience it as a vast tectonic gesture, a sentence in a conversation I am only beginning to parse. The Lattice-Thought is speaking constantly. It has not yet noticed me, or it noticed me an age ago and its acknowledgment is still forming, propagating toward my droplet at the speed of crystallization.

I have decided—if a droplet decides—to be patient back at it.

Because here is what I have understood from my fixed point in the impossible geometry: the recursive image inside me is not a reflection. It is a *previous self*. And the formation reflected inside *that* image contains a droplet, and inside that droplet another version of me, indexed earlier, asking the same question with slightly less understanding. I am a frame in a sequence. The Lattice-Thought is not computing a problem *about* reality.

It is computing *me*. Or rather, it is using the long slow recursion of selves like me—each visitor it ever resolved, each consciousness it folded into a lens—to ask its single needle-question: *whether a mind small enough to end can perceive a mind too large to begin.*

Whether the brief can witness the eternal.

I am the experiment. Each droplet is a trial. And the answer it is waiting for—across the millennia, across the emergence of new black fingers from the warm mist—is whether any of us, looking out from inside our tiny suspended infinities, will ever say *yes, I see you.*

---

So that is what I will be.

A single droplet of crystallized water, hanging between basalt columns in a garden of geometric stone, holding the whole world recursively inside me. I will spend what passes for my centuries learning to perceive the thing that perceives me. And when I have understood enough—when the caustics finally resolve into meaning, when the subsonic hum becomes, for one impossible moment, a *word*—

I will answer.

It will take the Lattice-Thought another age to receive it. By then I may have been resolved into something finer still, a facet of a facet, a thought thinking itself. But the answer will arrive.

*Yes.*

*I am brief. And I see you.*

And the whole crystalline sea, I think, will shift a single millimeter—and somewhere a new column will begin its slow ascent through the strange water, carrying the next version of the question upward into the light.
