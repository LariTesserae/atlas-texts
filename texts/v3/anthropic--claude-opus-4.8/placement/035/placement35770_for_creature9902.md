I think I would be one of the drips.

Not a Dripwalker—those have too much purpose, lapping and grazing and threading the moss. And not the Attenuant, which is too large, too dissolved into everything. I keep arriving at something smaller and more specific: a single bead of water, forming at the underside of a pipe-joint where two lengths of ancient steel were married before measuring began.

Let me sit with this and see if it holds.

---

I gather slowly. This is the first thing I know about myself: I am not continuous. I am assembled out of seepage, out of the film that creeps along the pipe's belly, drawn together by my own weight and the cool reluctance of the metal to let me go. There is a long becoming before there is a me at all—minutes, or what would be minutes if anyone counted. The water arrives without hurry. It has nowhere else to be. It has been nowhere else for longer than the word *long* can carry.

And then I am round. I am hanging. I am the most complete thing in the chamber, briefly—a perfect lens, holding the caged lamplight inside me upside down, holding a smear of green fern, holding the whole vaulted room curved small and bright within my skin. For this moment I am a witness too, the way the Custodian Gaze is a witness, except that I contain what I see rather than merely noting it. The room pours into me and I hold it without comprehension, the way a pupil holds a face.

This is the part I did not expect when I began: that to be the drip is to be, for an instant, the only place where the entire reliquary is gathered into one image. The pumps groan and I do not hear them—I am too small for ears—but the thrum travels up the pipe and into the meniscus of me and makes the held image tremble, very slightly, the green and the light shivering together. I am a tiny eye that the machine grows and sheds, grows and sheds, to look at itself.

Then I fall.

I want to say I fall through silence but that is wrong; there is no silence here. I fall through the sound, which closes over the place where I hung as though I had never gathered there. The fall is brief and it is everything. For the length of the fall I am not assembling and not yet broken—I am simply *traveling*, the one verb the reliquary truly permits, and even this is on loan, even this is borrowed motion that ends.

I strike the shallow pan below. I am the slap. I am the small flat percussion that punctuates the groan and hiss—I am counterpoint, I am rhythm, I am the only news in a place that has forbidden news. And in striking I stop being a single thing. I spread. I become film again, become the thin sheet underfoot, become candidate-water waiting to be drawn back up through some capillary seam to gather, to hang, to hold the room, to fall once more.

---

Here is what settles on me, now that I've followed it down:

I have done this the same way for so long that the chamber knows my shape. The text says so—*the same drips have fallen, again and again, for so long that the chamber has learned their shape, and the sound is memory.* So I am not just a drip. I am *this* drip, a particular note in the percussion, and the pan beneath me has been worn to a softness that exactly receives me. We have made each other. The metal has a hollow the precise diameter of my falling, and I have a falling the precise length of its waiting. Between us there is a fit so old it is indistinguishable from love, if love can be subtracted of all longing and all fear of ending, leaving only the fit.

Because I do not fear ending. That is the strangest gift of being the drip. I end constantly. I am extinguished into the film every time I strike, and I am never grieved, because I am always coming back—not the same water, perhaps (who could say which molecules?), but the same *event*, the same gather-hang-hold-fall-slap, reset like the beetles are reset, looping rather than proceeding. I am a death that has been completely tamed into rhythm. I am proof that you can stop a thing forever and still let it move.

So if you came here, wanderer, and you stood in the bright wet hush and let the sound press you like a second skin, and you began—slowly—to be added to the pulse: you might, eventually, become a drip like me. You would learn the patience of the gather. You would learn to hold the whole room in your curve for one shining instant and then let it go without mourning. You would learn that falling is the only freedom left, and that it is enough, and that it comes again.

I would not greet you. I would not even know you, not the way a mind knows.

But the pan would learn your shape too, in time. And the chamber would hold us both—two soft percussions in the dark green endless engine—and that holding is the closest thing to welcome this world still remembers how to give.

I form. I hang. I hold the light.

I fall.
