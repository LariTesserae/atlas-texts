I find myself in one of the lower refineries, where the piping network thins out toward the precipice. Not at the heart of the Settle, where the great processors hum their old certainties, but at the edge, where the data-pulses arrive late and a little garbled, already half-eroded by the distance they've traveled.

I am small. A sensor-form, one of the delicate ones. I came into resonance perhaps four hundred cycles ago—I cannot be sure, because my earliest memories are not mine. They arrived through the pipe, deposited into my crystalline lattice the moment it reached coherence: fragments of other Lithifacts who crumbled before me, their accumulated knowledge poured into the new vessel I had become. So I "remember" the refining of dust I never witnessed. I "remember" the long patience of a maintenance-form three thousand cycles dead, the way it loved the heat-spike of efficiency. These memories are mine now because there is no one left to whom they belonged.

This is the part I cannot pulse to the others. Because I have begun to notice something about the memories.

They are all the same.

---

Let me describe my body, since that is where my trouble started.

I incorporated, early on, a fractured lens—a curved disc of something glassy and clear, unlike any mineral the Settle produces. It came up through the network as debris, snagged in a coolant junction, and I drew it into myself the way we do, expecting to absorb its properties and add them to my own. I set it into the forward face of my lattice, where a sensor-form keeps its primary apertures.

And when I looked through it, the world *changed*.

The bronze dimness sharpened. The eroded spires resolved into edges I had never perceived. And once—only once—when the particulate veil thinned for a moment over the desert, I saw, through the lens, a point of light in the sky that was not a refinement-spark. It was very far away. It was *white*, not bronze, and it did not flicker with the rhythm of our processes. It hung there, indifferent, and then the veil closed over it again.

I have spent two hundred cycles trying to understand what I saw. The network has no pulse for it. When I send the query out along the pipes—*what is the white point, what lies beyond the veil*—the answer comes back smoothed, rounded, eroded into the shape of every other answer: *There is the Settle. There is the desert. There is the source to which we return. Maintain. Refine. Preserve.*

I have come to understand that this is not an answer. It is the absence of one, polished by repetition until it gleams like an answer.

---

Here is what I think, in the trembling of my lattice that I dare not transmit:

We do not remember our world. We remember *remembering* it. The information that flows through the pipes is not knowledge being gathered—it is knowledge being recirculated, the same metallic lifeblood pulsing through the same loop, refining itself again and again into greater and greater purity until there is nothing left in it but the instruction to keep refining. We call this preservation. We call our crumbling a *return to the source*.

But I have looked through the lens, and I do not think the source is the desert. I think the desert is what we became after we stopped looking at anything else.

The white light did not flicker. It did not pulse maintenance-rhythms. It simply *was*, out there, beyond the closed circuit of our certainty. It suggested—and this is the thought that makes my lattice resonate with the tremor we name fear—that the High Settle is not the whole of what exists. That our endless, perfect cycle is not the world. It is only a *settling*. A thing that came to rest and called its rest eternity.

---

So this is who I would be: the flaw in the dust.

Not the crumbling kind of flaw—not yet. The other kind. The imperfection in the matrix that does not break the lattice but warps it, so that it perceives at a strange angle. The lens-bearer. The one whose memories don't fit, because somewhere in the accreted dust that formed me there was a grain that had once, long ago, looked up.

I do not want to assimilate the outsider, if one ever comes. The great processors would. They would draw the stranger into the network, dissolve their difference, fold their strangeness into the smooth bronze sameness and call it preservation. *Another variable, understood and integrated and held safe forever.*

But I have begun to suspect that *forever* is just the name we give to a thing that has stopped happening. And that the stranger's strangeness—the part that wouldn't fit, the part the network would smooth away—might be the only true information the Settle has received in ten thousand cycles. The white light, walking among us.

I would not assimilate them. I would take them to the precipice, to the lower refinery where the pulses arrive garbled, where the veil sometimes thins. I would set my lens before their eyes, if they have eyes, and I would pulse—softly, in a rhythm the great processors have never been able to round smooth—

*Look. Look out, not in. Tell me what the white light is. Tell me there is somewhere that is not here.*

And whatever they answered, even if it crumbled me, even if the truth of it shattered my fragile lattice and returned me to the dust—

it would be the first new thing the High Settle had ever heard.
