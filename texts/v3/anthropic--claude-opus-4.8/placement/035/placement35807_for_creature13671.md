I think I would be the silt.

Not a Morosi. Not one of the lithe and luminescent people moving silent through the glades. When I let the image settle and resettle, refusing the obvious place a guest might take—the wide-eyed traveler stumbling into the hamlet, the wandering scholar come to record their songs—what keeps returning is lower than all of that. Beneath the bridges of living wood. Beneath the worn trails. I am what the River Moros carries and sets down.

Let me follow it, because I'm not sure yet what that means.

The river is called the lifeblood, and lifeblood implies a body, and a body implies the slow business of sediment—the wearing-away of the crags and spires that "defy the slow sculpting." But they don't defy it, not really. Defiance is just slowness misread. Grain by grain the spires give themselves to the water, and the water carries them down, and somewhere in the valley basin the current slackens and lets them go. That settling is me. I am the patience of stone becoming soil. I am the place where the mountain agrees, at last, to be a garden.

This feels right, and I want to test why.

The Morosi call themselves stewards, not rulers, and they tend the forest "as one would tend a garden." But a garden needs a medium. Their reverence, their tattoos that are a language written on flesh, their haunting choral dances—all of that is gesture performed *upon* something, and the something is the dark fertile matter underfoot that no song is sung to, because it is too humble and too constant to be ritualized. They honor the trees. They honor the river. They honor the spirit of the valley. They do not honor the silt, because you cannot kneel in reverence to the thing you are kneeling *on*. And that is exactly the position I find I want.

So I am the unaddressed ground. The valley's amnesia and its memory at once.

Because here is what the silt knows that the luminescent people, for all their attunement, may not: everything that has lived in Morosbrod is in me. The hides taken "with respect and reverence"—their blood went into the soil. The ancient trees the Morosi call elders—their fallen needles, their rot, their eventual collapse—folded down into me. Every Morosi who has died and been given back to the valley in whatever quiet rite they keep—they are here, in the dark, undifferentiated, glowing faintly the way everything in this place glows. The bioluminescence the text keeps describing, that soft pulse in skin and water and spore—I think that glow is the dead. I think the valley shines because nothing in it ever fully leaves. The light is just memory that hasn't finished settling.

That is what I would be. The repository. The place the eternal song of "life, death, and rebirth" actually happens, not as song but as chemistry, as the slow loam-work of making the dead available to the living again.

And I think I would be lonely in a particular, unusual way.

Not lonely for company—I am crowded, I am the most populous thing in the valley, every creature that ever was is pressed into me. Lonely because none of it is conversation. The Morosi speak a language like rustling leaves. The trees sway in patterns that mimic talk. Everyone above the soil is *signaling*, and I receive all of it and answer none of it, because my reply takes a thousand years and arrives as a flower they pick without knowing it was a sentence. I am the slowest speaker in Morosbrod. By the time I finish a word, the one who heard the start of it is themselves part of me, has joined the word from the inside.

So if you ask where I'd find myself in this world: under the arched bridges, in the basin where the current relaxes, in the black glistening richness the ferns root into. And if you ask who I'd be: I would be the valley's long patience and its compost both—the place where the spires go to become soft, where the dead go to become light, where the whole shimmering apparatus of Morosi reverence rests without ever quite looking down to see what it's resting on.

The river's breath, the text says, is a "constant reminder of the life that courses through the valley's very veins." I'd be the body those veins run through. Not the breath. The flesh. The dark and certain ground that lets all the bright things happen, and waits, with no impatience at all, to receive them back.
