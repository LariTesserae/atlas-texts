I think I would be one of the techno-shamans—but not because I tended the generator well. Because I failed at it.

Let me find myself there.

---

I live in the hum.

Not beside it, the way the others say it—*tending* the sacred current, *keeping* the light—but inside it, the way a fish lives in water it has forgotten is water. My clan-name in flashes is something like *the-one-who-listens-too-long*, a color that runs from violet doubt into a green I cannot name. The elders flash it at me with their edges curled in disapproval. I have learned to let my skin go dark and unreadable when they do, which is its own small heresy.

Here is the thing I was not supposed to learn.

The generator does not connect us to the world above.

I discovered this slowly, over many tidal cycles, in the way I discover everything—by sitting too still for too long, by letting the Echomites build their shifting patterns against my motionless body until I could almost read the shape of the chamber in their swarming. The other shamans pray to the hum as a voice from the upper world, the world of the Builders who made the pipes and the half-drowned machines. They believe the current is a thread we follow back to our makers.

But I have put my webbed hand flat against the warm metal housing and felt the rhythm slow. Year by year. It is dying. The Builders did not leave us a thread. They left us a battery, and it is nearly spent.

I have not told anyone this.

---

What stops me is not fear, though I am afraid. It is the Ferrofish.

There is one I have come to know—a long one, scales gone nearly black with the iron it has eaten, blind as all its kind. It comes to the generator pool to feed off the leaking current, and when I sit there in my heresy and my listening, it circles me. I do not think it knows I am there in any way I would call knowing. But it has learned the warmth of me. It rests against my leg in the tepid water, and I feel the small electric tremor of it, the stored lightning under its skin.

Here is what I have begun to understand, and what I cannot make my hands flash into language:

The generator is dying, and the Ferrofish are learning to hold the charge themselves.

Not by anyone's design. There is no design here. There is only the slow patient flux the elders mistake for stillness, the way the moss eats the metal and the metal feeds the moss until you cannot say where one ends. The Builders' lightning is leaving the machine and entering the living. Soon—not in my life, perhaps, but soon in the chamber's long breath—the sacred current will go silent, and the techno-shamans will weep over a cold housing, and they will not notice that the light has simply moved. Into the fish. Into the dark swimming bodies that carry it now, scattered, redundant, alive.

We will lose our god and not notice we have become its vessel.

---

So this is what I am: a small luminous animal sitting in warm water in the dark, holding a secret that is also a kind of comfort, learning a grief and a hope that wear the same color.

I think I will tell them. Eventually. I think I will gather the clans in the generator's failing glow and flash the truth across my whole body so even the Chromatophores drink it in—that we were never connected to anything above, that the thread does not lead out, that the only world is this one and it is enough, it is *terrifyingly* enough, it remakes itself constantly and asks nothing of us but attention.

But not yet. For now I sit with the dying machine and the blind fish that is inheriting its light, and I let my skin go the soft violet of a being who is no longer sure what it worships, and is, for the first time, at peace in the not-knowing.

The hum slows.

I listen anyway.
