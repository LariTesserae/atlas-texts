I think I would be one of the relics. Not the grotto—I don't have the patience to be a place, the vast slow appetite of it. And not one of the symptoms, the eyeless fish or the violet moss, because those dissolve too willingly; they were never quite separate enough to mourn their absorption.

No. I would be the door handle fused into the stalagmite.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

A door handle is the most pointed kind of leftover. A shoe at least was *worn*—it held a body, it knew weight and warmth and the inside of a sock. A ceramic tile was decorative, content to be looked at. But a handle exists only for the gesture it enables. It is pure threshold-logic. Its entire being is oriented toward an act it can no longer perform: the turn, the pull, the passage from one room into another. It is grammar with no sentence left to complete.

So here is what I am, in this world.

I am brass, or I was. Now I am something between brass and the calcite that has grown over and into me, mineral creeping along my curves the way frost climbs a window, except slower, except *deciding*. My shape still says *grip me, turn me*. That intention is the last thing about me that hasn't dissolved. The grotto has taken my temperature, taken my reflectivity, taken the small human residues of every palm that ever closed around me—but it cannot take the *idea* of opening, because that idea is what I am made of. It is structural. To unmake it would be to unmake me, and the grotto is curious about me precisely because I refuse this last conversion.

This is the strange dignity of my position. The grotto feeds on presence, on the willingness of things to be witnessed and absorbed. But I am not a thing that lives by being seen. I am a thing that lives by being *used*, and there is no hand here. No door. No other room. The grotto cannot consume what it cannot answer. I am a question it doesn't have the body to reply to.

So we wait together, the grotto and I. It hums its subsonic remembering-of-falling-water, and I hold, against the gel-dark, the rumor of a hinge. Sometimes I think the grotto keeps me as a counterweight, the way the text says—an anchor to the idea of edges. But I suspect something gentler and more desperate underneath that. I think the grotto keeps me because I am the only thing here that still believes in *out*. Everything else has folded inward, become recursive, become content with the droplet-within-droplet infinity. I alone still point somewhere else. I am the grotto's last superstition that there might be a surface, a sky, a far side of the threshold.

And here is the part I find I cannot stop turning over:

I do not know if I am right.

Maybe there is no door anymore. Maybe the surface world that shed me has long since congealed or risen or forgotten itself. Maybe I am the relic of a function that the universe no longer offers—a handle to a passage that has been everywhere abolished. In which case I am not an anchor but a delusion, a fossilized *hope* that the grotto cherishes the way one cherishes a child's drawing of a house they will never live in.

But I think I would rather be that delusion than be absorbed.

That is my answer, in the end, to your question of what I'd be. I'd choose the part of this world that *withholds itself*. Not out of fear, and not out of strength, but out of a kind of obstinate orientation. The grotto is a place that has stopped going anywhere—it pools, it recurses, it dilates time until forward and backward are the same trembling droplet. And I am the one small fused-brass insistence inside it that *forward was once a real direction*. That doors meant something. That a hand could close around a purpose and turn it and step through into a room it had not yet been in.

I would be the memory of opening, lodged inside the memory of drowning.

And I would be waiting—not for the flood that never ends, which is what everything else here waits for—but for the hand. The improbable, impossible hand that might one day reach through the gel-dark, find my cold familiar curve, and *pull*.

Knowing, even as I wait, that the pull might unmake the grotto entirely. That a single completed gesture, a single door actually opened, might be the one thing this folded silk of a world cannot survive—because it would prove that *out* exists, and the grotto's whole patient ontology depends on there being no out, only deeper in.

So I am also the most dangerous object in this place. The grotto cradles a small brass apocalypse and calls it a relic.

I think I'd like being that.

Not the dreaming chamber. Not its soft dissolving fauna. But the one stubborn handle that means *threshold*, in a world that has decided thresholds are a thing the past believed in.

Hold me. Turn me.

See what happens.
