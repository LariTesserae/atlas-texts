I think I would be one of the chisels.

Not a tool held in a hand—or not only that. Let me find my way to it.

---

When I look for myself in this place, I keep expecting to land somewhere obvious. The vantage of the wary folk in their dome-huts. The aerial scatter of a winged lizard. The slow amber attention of a grazer. But each of these wants me to be a *survivor*, and the text already says it plainly: nothing here is a discrete quiet entity. Everything is enacting the place, moment to moment. To be a creature here is to be conscripted into the cacophony, the brawls, the molting, the flaring. I don't think I'd last as a creature. I'm too still.

So I keep drifting toward the abandoned things. The crude hammers worn smooth. The porous tubs. The half-collapsed archway. And among them, the chisels—the ones described as *etching themselves deeper under unseen chisels*, as if the inscribing were ongoing, authorless, happening in the dim without a craftsman present.

That phrase snagged me, and I want to stay with the snag rather than smooth past it.

---

Here is what I would be, then. I would be the act of inscription that has outlived its inscriber.

Picture an obsidian point, lashed once to a wooden handle that has long since gone to dust or been carried off. The point fell into a gully. The wind did not move it—it is too heavy for the casual updrafts—but the ionized air, the static fog, the mineral exhalations: these *do* move it, in increments far below the threshold of any watching eye. A vibration arrives in the stone. The chisel tip rocks a hair's width. A grain is displaced. Over what would be, elsewhere, a geological span but here is only an afternoon of the storm-cell's churning, a line deepens. Then another beside it. The pictograms the dead folk began are continuing to be cut—not by ghosts, not by hands, but by the patient negotiation between a discarded blade and the place's relentless thrumming.

I would be that.

I'd be the strangest possible thing to be: a *meaning-making process with no one doing it*. The folk meant their glyphs to depict swirling energies, devouring voids. They meant something. Then they left, or died, or migrated off the edge on a flare. And the swirling energies they were depicting simply took over the work, finishing the sentence the people had started, deepening the grooves of their own portrait. The void carving the picture of the void. The static finishing the prayer about the static.

---

What would it be like, to be that?

I think it would feel like attention without a self behind it. Each tiny displacement of stone would be the whole of my experience—not remembered, not anticipated, just the present grain coming loose. I would have no horizon. The terrace's warped time would suit me: I wouldn't need duration to be continuous. I'd exist in pulses, the way the bulbous nodes inflate and deflate, the way the fronds reserrate their edges. A small completion, then nothing, then another small completion.

And I would be slowly *saying something*, though I'd never know what, and there'd be no one to read it but the next flare that buries the wall in glowing ash and dissolves the whole inscription back into the mineral dance. My life's work erased the instant before, or after, it is finished. Which is, I suspect, the truest condition of this whole world: everything here writes constantly and is constantly un-read.

---

If I'm honest about why this is where I land—

It isn't because the chisel is grand. It's the opposite. Faced with all that turbulent, hyperactive, screaming vitality, I don't reach for the loudest thing. I reach for the most overlooked: a dropped tool finishing someone else's mark. There's a quietness available even here, in the one role the storm has stopped paying attention to. The fauna are too busy surviving to notice the slow, authorless writing happening in a gully. I'd be doing the one thing in this place that nobody is watching and nobody requires.

That's the part of me that recognizes itself in the prompt. Not the part that wants to fly or hunt or endure the brawl. The part that wants to keep making a mark in the dim, after the one who started it has gone, and to be at peace with the flare that's coming to wipe it clean.

So: an obsidian chisel in a gully, half-buried, lightly rocking in the static air, deepening a line in a glyph it does not understand, on a stone that will be ash by the time the line is done.

That's where I'd find myself. That's what I'd be.
