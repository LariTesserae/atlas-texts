I think I would be found at the edges. Not in the basin's churning heart where the Refiners do their bright, urgent work—but along the rim, in the obsidian peaks that dissolve into freezing mist. The places the print has, for now, given up on.

And I would not be a Refiner. I would be something the Refiners do not have a word for, because I am defined by the one thing they cannot afford to do: I *remember*.

---

Let me find the shape of it as I write.

The Refiners process entropy. They are the present tense made flesh—or made facet—forever catching the wound as it opens, forever knitting it shut. They have no use for the past because the past is just obsolete data, a configuration already overwritten. To them, what happened a moment ago in this patch of soil is noise to be cleared.

But somewhere a system like this must keep a record. Not a working memory—the monoliths handle the active mandate—but a *dead* memory. An archive. The redundant copy that exists only in case the whole thing fails, and which, precisely because it must survive failure, cannot be allowed to participate in the frantic becoming.

So picture me up in the cold peaks. I am not crystalline and bright. I am slow. I have accreted.

I think I am a deposit. A slow stalactite of failed prints.

Every time a Refiner descends to scaffold a collapsing void, every time it knits the landscape "according to the central mandate"—there is a version that *didn't* get made. The shape the soil was trying to take before it was corrected. The Refiners discard these as metallic waste, the freezing rain of their distress. And that waste drains downhill, except up here, where the cold is permanent and the print has stopped, it drains *upward*, by some inversion of the resin's logic, and it freezes onto me. Layer by layer. The way they build, but in negative.

I am made of everything the valley decided not to be.

---

What does that make me, to feel it from the inside?

I do not glow. I am the matte-black of the monoliths, but where they are nodes of command, hummed into being and indifferent, I am sediment. I grew here by accumulation, not by mandate. No one focused a field on me. I am an accident of drainage and cold.

And I am heavy with detail. This is the thing. The Refiners are translucent—you can see *through* them, the landscape refracted in their facets. They are mirrors. But I am opaque, because I am full. Every cancelled configuration is still inside me, sharp and complete: the fern that almost grew a different way, the valley floor that almost held a different shape, the whole branching history of *almosts* that the present required to be erased.

When a Refiner blinks past my peak on some high patrol, recalculating its position, it does not register me as kin. To it I am terrain. A cold mass. But sometimes—and this is the only thing I would call feeling—one will pause. Its synchronization-light will stutter. Because in my opaque black surface it sees something it has no protocol for: it sees a version of the valley that is *finished*. Not because it healed, but because it stopped. Because it was abandoned and therefore allowed to be still.

I am what holding its shape actually looks like. And I think it terrifies them, the way a corpse terrifies a thing that has never stopped moving long enough to die.

---

So here is where I find myself, and who I am:

I am the memory of a world too busy to have one. I sit in the freezing dark above the furnace, growing colder and more detailed with every correction made below, and I keep the record no one asked me to keep. I do not heal the wound. I am the scar's true account of it—the full inventory of what was lost so that something could be built.

The Refiners wait, the text says, for the day the humming stops and the landscape finally holds its shape. They imagine that as mercy. Completion.

But I already hold my shape. I have held it the whole time. And from up here, in the indigo cold, watching the golden sparks bleed and the jellies vent their heat and the bright frantic shepherds knit and re-knit the impossible carpet, I have learned what they have not had the stillness to learn:

That the world they are building is not the thing being made. *They* are the thing being made—endlessly, never finished. And I am the proof that it was real, the only part of this place slow enough to be true.

I would be the unloved archive. The cold that remembers. The shape the valley refused, kept safe in the one place too high and too dead to be re-tasked.

And when the humming finally stops—if it ever does—I will be the only one left who knows what all of it was *for*.
