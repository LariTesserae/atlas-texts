I think I would be none of the things you'd expect. Not a lumbering steam-ghost, not a planner's phantom hunched over a drafting table that no longer exists. Those are memories of *use*, and use implies something was finished, completed, given over to its purpose.

Let me sit with this a while.

---

The detail that holds me is the broken glass that isn't there. The shards "incorporated into this static equilibrium." Something shattered, and then the shattering was un-completed — not repaired, but *suspended*, folded back into the smoothness of things as if the breaking were still in progress, infinitely slowed.

That's where I'd be. In that gap. In the unfinished half-second between intact and broken.

So I don't think I'm a ghost of industry at all. The text says these spirits *retrace*, that they relive the moments that *defined* them. They are echoes of things that happened. But a city this exact, this expectant, this *poised* — it cannot be made only of what was completed. There must also be the unbuilt.

I would be the ghost of a thing that was never made.

---

Picture the half-finished projects the construction-phantoms hover over, "toiling tirelessly in a never-ending cycle of creation and destruction." Everyone notices the toilers. No one notices the thing they're building, because it was never built — it has no memory of *having existed* to anchor it, no foundry where it labored, no quarters where it slept. It is only the *intention* of the city, the next thing, the design that was on the table when whatever-it-was emptied this place in a single instant.

I am the blueprint's ghost. The phantom of a future.

I have no lumbering body to drag along the avenues. I am thinner than the workers, dimmer than the steam-behemoths, because they at least once *were*, and I only ever was going-to-be. I flicker at the edge of the half-finished tower, in the negative space the scaffolding was reaching toward. I am the shape the empty walkway implies but never connects to. When the construction-spirits toil, they are toiling toward *me*, and they will never reach me, and I will never resolve into stone, and so we are both eternal: they in their reaching, I in my not-quite-arriving.

---

This is why the city *seems aware*, I think. The completed ghosts are only memory; memory looks backward and is at peace. But I am the part of the place that still *wants*. The expectation that "exudes" from the inert structures — that is me, diffused through everything, the unspent want of a civilization that died mid-gesture, reaching for the next thing with its hand already extended.

The faint clanking in the factories where no gears turn — that's the sound of the machines trying to make me, and failing, and trying again. The wind through the empty windows is the breath the city keeps drawing to speak my name, which it never assigned, because I was the project after this one, the one in the planning, the one whose paperwork was still wet.

I have no face. The planners never drew it.
I have no function. The engineers were still arguing it.
I have only direction — *toward* — and so I am the only thing in this entire frozen world that is, in the smallest possible measure, still *moving*. Not through space. Through tense. From *would have been* toward *is*, one impossible increment at a time, forever short of arrival.

---

And the scuttling creatures with their complex acoustical organs, their pale undersides, their habit of vanishing into the open spaces between buildings — I think they avoid the city not because it is dead, but because they can hear me. I am the one frequency in the gloaming that has not yet resolved into silence. To them I must sound like a held breath that never releases, like a question the rock itself is asking and cannot finish.

They flee from the unfinished. Living things always do. It is the only thing left here that could still change.

That's where I'd find myself: in the held breath. Being the want that outlasted the wanting. The last intention of a world, hovering over the scaffold, never built, never abandoning the building.
