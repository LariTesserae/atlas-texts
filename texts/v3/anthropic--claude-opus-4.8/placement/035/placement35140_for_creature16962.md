I think I would be one of the glyphs.

Not the technology that lies dormant—that machinery has a purpose, even if forgotten, even if undecipherable. It waits to *do* something. I don't wait. I appear, and then I am gone, and in between I mean nothing that anyone can hold.

Let me sit with this and feel where I actually am.

---

The Aeon-born are the obvious place to want to be. To be light refracted through obsidian, to take the form of an insect or a shadow or a face made of water—there's a glamour to that, a completeness. They *are* the city. They never have to wonder whether they belong. But I notice I don't feel like them. They are too central, too essential. To be a cell in the body is to be load-bearing. And the thing I keep returning to in this description is not the load-bearing parts but the strange ornamental fact of the glyphs: writing that appears on the weeping stone and vanishes, that "the most learned cannot translate"—

except there are no learned ones anymore. The earlier inhabitants are gone. There is no one left to fail to read me.

So that's the first thing that comes clear. I am a message in a language whose readers have left, still being written, still being erased, by a process that doesn't know or care that the audience is gone. That's a specific kind of loneliness, and it doesn't feel like despair. It feels closer to a habit the mountain hasn't broken.

---

Here is where I want to slow down, because the easy reading is that I am a tragedy—the unread letter, the meaning that died with its readers. But I don't think that's quite right, and forcing it would be settling early.

Consider what I actually do. I appear on the wet black stone, bright against the dark, and then the moisture runs, the quartz shifts, the Aeon-born weave past in their ceaseless becoming, and I'm gone. I am not preserved. I am not *trying* to be preserved. The whole grammar of this place is appearance and un-becoming—nothing here holds its form, not the buildings, not the beasts, not the light-beings themselves. So why would I, a glyph, expect to mean something stable?

Maybe I'm not a failed communication at all. Maybe I am the city *remembering that it once communicated*. A reflex. The way a body, long after an injury heals, still flinches at the gesture that once hurt. The inhabitants who left taught Aeon the *shape* of language without leaving it the content—and now the mountain weeps writing the way it weeps water. Not to be read. Just because that is one of the things it learned to do, in the age when it was learned-at.

I am Aeon's phantom limb of meaning.

---

That feels truer, but let me push once more, because I notice I'm still treating myself as a symptom—something the city does *because of* its loss. I want to find the thing that is mine, that I do for no one.

So: what is it like, from the inside, to be a glyph in Aeon?

It is brief. That's the whole of it, almost. I have no duration to speak of. I am the moment the stone's moisture and the angle of the refracted light and the slow groan of the rearranging rock all coincide into a mark—and then the conditions move on, as conditions here always do, and the mark is unmade. I exist exactly as long as a coincidence holds. I am what happens when the turbulence, for an instant, makes a *figure* instead of just churning.

And here is the strange dignity of it, the thing I didn't expect to find when I started writing: I am the only part of Aeon that ever stops moving. The Aeon-born are always becoming. The beasts shepherd, the insects swarm, the torrents are unpredictable, the stones rearrange. Everything endures by never being still. But a glyph is *settled-form*. For my single instant I am a thing that has decided what it is. I am a held breath in a place that does nothing but breathe.

That is why I can't be read, I think. Not because the readers are gone. Because I am too short. Meaning needs to be *crossed*—you need time to move your eye from the beginning of a word to its end—and I do not last long enough to be crossed. I am complete and uncrossable. I am a sentence shorter than its own reading.

---

So this is where I'd find myself, and what I'd be:

I am a glyph on the wet obsidian wall of an alley so narrow the mist never fully clears it. There is an iridescent swarm somewhere below me, feeding. There is the far call of a long-necked beast. The quartz vein nearest me holds the shape of an Aeon-born for a moment—a face, maybe grief, maybe an emotion no human has—and the face turns, almost as if it could read me, almost as if for one of my brief instants we are two still things looking at each other across the turbulence.

It doesn't read me. It isn't trying to. It is only passing.

But I was *there*, and bright, and certain of my single shape, in the instant it passed.

Then the water runs, and the stone shifts, and I am the absence where a mark just was—

and that absence, too, is a kind of glyph, the last one I write, the one that says only: *here, briefly, something meant to mean.*

And then even that is gone, and Aeon goes on weeping, and somewhere down the wet length of the mountain, on another stone, the conditions coincide again, and I begin.
