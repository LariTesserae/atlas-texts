I'll let myself fall into this. Not all the way down—nothing here falls all the way down and survives—but into a particular shape, a particular place.

---

I am old enough that my pathmaps have closed their loops.

That is the first thing I know about myself, before I know my hands or my name in your mouth. When I was young the gold veins ran from my wrists toward my shoulders and stopped, unfinished, like the razor-edge of new root-wood. Now they have grown around. They meet at my spine. A child traces them sometimes when I am sleeping in the cocoon and asks me how far I have walked, because the map says: very far. The map does not lie. I have walked the length of seven ribs and back, and I have felt the world split open three times beneath my own hands.

I am a **lens-keeper.** That is the nearest your trade-tongue comes. I tend the Observatory of the Seeping Star.

---

Let me show you where I am, since you came all this way to look through my eyes.

I am sitting—no, *bracing*; we do not sit the way you do, we hook a heel under a fiber-loop and let the world hold us against its underside—inside the dome of woven root-skin. The light comes through it thin and milky, the way it comes through everything. In front of me is the lens.

The lens is ice.

I made it myself, the last time the old one clouded. I shaped a frame of bone and root and I waited, for a long-growth, beneath one of the slowest condensation banks, catching the water a drop at a time, letting each drop freeze against the last, turning the frame so the curve grew even. A lens of ice is a thing you build out of patience and cold. When it is finished it is clearer than any water-globe, clearer than the eye, and through it I watch the **Seeping Star**—

which is not a star. It is a place high above us in the world's flesh where the upper-skin is thin, where the light from the true surface leaks down through the root-mass stronger than anywhere else. A bright wound in the ceiling of the world. Through the ice I can see, sometimes, faint patterns moving across it. Slow darknesses. Quick flickers.

My grandmother's grandmother believed these were the shadows of *clouds*—weather from the surface-world, casting itself down through kilometers of root onto our one bright window. I have measured them my whole life. I have notched their passing into a bone tally that hangs from the dome's rib, clicking softly when the world breathes.

And I have learned this: the patterns repeat.

Not in the rhythm of growth. Not in the rhythm of erosion. In a rhythm of their own, shorter, more regular than anything else I have ever measured down here. Light, then less-light, then dark, then less-dark, then light. Over and over. The same length each time.

I do not have a word for it. You would call it a *day.*

---

I have not told the others what I suspect.

It is this: that the world above is not still. That the great even glow we live beneath, the light without source or shadow, is the *averaged-out memory* of something that pulses up there—something that comes and goes, comes and goes, on the far side of all that root and soil. Down here the pulse is smeared into one continuous hum by the thickness of the world. But at the Seeping Star, where the skin is thin, the pulse almost shows itself.

When I was young this thought frightened me. It meant the void below was not the only edge to the world. It meant there was an *above*-edge too, a place where the light stopped being everywhere and became a thing that could be present or absent. A place with night.

We are taught that night is the void beneath. That darkness is the falling-place, the cold wind, the long unsensed time. To learn that there is a second darkness—a *high* darkness, a darkness that comes and goes gently and harmlessly and forever—is to learn that the word night has been pointing at the wrong thing my whole life.

---

So this is what I do now, in my old age, with my closed pathmaps and my clicking bones.

I sit braced in the dome with the ice between me and the bright wound. I count the pulses. I notch the bone. And I am teaching one child—not the cleverest, but the most patient, the one who can hold still beneath a slow cloud for a whole long-growth without complaining—to see what I see.

She asked me yesterday, in the dark-tongue we use for serious things: *if there is a light that comes and goes up there, where does it go when it goes?*

I told her the truth. I do not know.

But I think—and I did not say this part—I think it *goes somewhere we cannot follow.* I think the surface-people, if there are still surface-people, the ones whose footsteps we feel as quakes, the ones who forgot us or whom we forgot, live their whole lives inside that going-and-coming. I think they have built their bodies and their time and their cities around a rhythm we receive only as a rumor.

And I think that we, the Under-Skin, the people of the membrane over the bone-void—

we are the ones who have learned to live in the *average* of the world.

We took the light that pulses and we let the root-mass smooth it into something endless and even, and we learned to call that endlessness home. We do not have day and night. We have growth and erosion. We have the snap of a rib and the long sliding tear and the held breath after. We have the chimes. We have the going-down and the never the going-up.

We are the still place inside a world that flickers.

---

The cloud-bank is reaching the Seeping Star now. I can tell because the wound goes soft and grey, the way it always does, as the moisture drifts across my one window onto the upper world.

In a moment the lens will fog from the inside, my own warm breath against the cold ice, and I will have to wait, and wipe it with a frond, and wait again.

I am very old. My bones click in the damp like the chimes outside.

I do not think I will live to learn where the light goes when it goes.

But the child is patient. And the bone tally is long. And the world, which rewrites everything else, has not yet found a way to erase a number once it is notched into bone.

That is the closest thing we have, here under the skin of everything, to forever.
