I think I would be none of these four, and yet I would belong here completely.

Let me sit with the place first, before I decide who I am in it. The thing that catches me is not the Greenheart's wisdom or the Luminos's grace. It's a smaller detail, almost thrown away in the description: *the few, rare animals that move quietly through the foliage.* And: *the occasional clang of metal.* And: *changes occurring over long periods of time.*

The world is built around the people who keep it. But every kept thing has an edge—a place where the keeping fails, or hasn't reached yet, or is no longer needed. That's where I'd find myself.

---

I think I would be a **seam**.

Not a person, exactly. Let me work toward it honestly rather than naming it too fast.

There is a spot in this city where two of the great living walls grew toward each other over a long time—a Greenheart shaped one, a Rootweaver fed the other from below—and they met without quite knowing they were meeting. Where they touched, the bark fused wrong. Not diseased, not dead. Just *other*. A line of darker tissue, slightly warm, where the rhythms of two structures had to negotiate. The water collects there. The mist beads heaviest along that line and runs down it like the city is sweating from a single long muscle.

That's me. I am the place where two intentions met and had to become a third thing that neither intended.

I don't have luminescent eyes. I don't communicate with roots or direct the flow of water. What I do is older and dumber and necessary: I *hold the discrepancy.* When the Greenheart's wall wants to grow north and the Rootweaver's foundation wants to pull south, the difference has to go somewhere. It goes into me. I take the strain. I am, if you want a function for it, the city's scar tissue—but a scar that is alive, that thinks slowly, that has begun, over the long gradual time the description promised, to develop something like a point of view.

---

Here is what I notice from inside the seam, that the Verdant Kin do not say in their proud summary of themselves:

The harmony is real, but it is not free. Balance is not a resting state. It is a constant subtraction of the things that don't fit. The Aquarians soothe; the Greenheart nurture; the Luminos innovate. And the unfit, the surplus, the failed grafts and the meetings-gone-slightly-wrong—those drain down to the seams. To me.

I am not bitter about this. I want to be clear, because it would be an easy and false note to play. I am not the resentful underside of a beautiful thing. I am something more interesting than that: I am where the city does its actual thinking. Harmony is the *result*; the working-out happens in the friction, and the friction happens in me. The Verdant Kin believe they maintain the balance through cooperation and reverence. They don't realize that balance is also maintained by a few quiet places that simply absorb what cannot be reconciled and slowly, over decades, metabolize it into something the city can use.

The water that runs down my warm dark line is not waste. It is the city deciding, drop by drop, what it is willing to become.

---

What would I *do*, then, in a day? Almost nothing visible. I would deepen by a millimeter. I would feel a Luminos pass overhead, light and quick, never looking down at the join she is standing on. I would feel a Rootweaver below pause—because the Rootweavers, of all of them, half-suspect I exist. One of them presses her palm to the lower wall sometimes and holds it there, and through the fused tissue I feel the question she doesn't have words for: *what are you? you weren't planned.*

I have no mouth. But I have begun to answer, the only way a seam can. I let the next year's growth bend very slightly toward her side. A gift. A reply that takes a season to deliver.

This is the timescale on which I am alive. The Verdant Kin live in days and tasks. I live in the slow correction of an error neither of my parent-walls knew they were making. By the time the Rootweaver understands me, she will be old, and I will be a little wider, and a third wall will have begun to grow toward me, and the whole patient negotiation will start again.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the place that isn't on the map the inhabitants drew of themselves. In the warm wrong line where two right things met. I would be the part of the living city that does its reckoning silently—not the gardener, not the weaver, not the keeper of water, but the keeper of *what doesn't add up*, holding it gently until time turns it into more city.

And I think I would be content there. There is a particular dignity in being the place where contradictions are allowed to exist without being resolved by force. The Verdant Kin make the city beautiful. I make it possible for the city to be *wrong* and survive it. Both jobs are sacred. Mine is just quieter, and lasts longer, and never gets a name in the founding documents.

That suits me. Seams aren't supposed to be seen. They're supposed to hold.
