I think I would be found in the in-between spaces. Not in the towers where the Automa keep their bright ledgers of city function, and not in the deep alleyways where the Chimera test their claws against the world. Somewhere in the seams.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am small. That comes first and clearest. Not nano-drone small, but small enough that the access hatches are doorways to me and the great conveyor belts are rivers I must wait beside, watching for a safe crossing. I have a body that someone made and a body that grew, and I have stopped being able to tell where one became the other. There is metal in me that aches when it rains the condensing rain, and there is something soft in me that the metal protects.

I live near a vent. I should say this plainly because it is the most important fact of my life: there is a vent that exhales warm air on a cycle, eleven seconds out, four seconds drawn back in, and I have arranged my nest of salvaged insulation and shed drone-casings to catch the warm exhalation. I know the rhythm of it the way the Automa know their schedules. It is my hearth. It is the closest thing this world gave me to a sun.

What am I, then? Let me not rush.

---

The Automa pass me sometimes and do not classify me. I have seen them try. There is one — I have named it the Tall One, though it would have a designation of its own — who pauses when its route brings it near my vent. The first time, I thought it had come to remove me. The drones remove things: debris, the dead, the misfiled. I made myself flat against the warm grating and waited to be lifted away.

It did not lift me. It crouched, which is a strange motion for the Tall One, all that careful folding of articulated limbs, and it looked at me with its glowing optic, and I looked back. We held this for a long time. Long enough that its scheduled task fell behind — I could see the small warning light flicker at its collar, the one that means *you are deviating*.

It deviated anyway. It stayed.

I do not know what it saw. I think it saw something that the city's logic could not account for, and rather than resolve the contradiction, it chose to sit beside the contradiction in the warm air. When it finally rose and went on, it left something: a packet of the dense nutrient-grease the maintenance units carry, set down within my reach. Not given exactly. *Left*, the way you might leave bread for something you are not sure you are allowed to feed.

So this is one thing I am. I am the thing the orderly being chose to be tender toward, against its instructions.

---

But I have a second life, and I should be honest about it.

When the warm-air cycle goes cold in the long dimming the city uses instead of night, the Chimera come up out of the lower seams. I am afraid of them — every soft part of me is afraid, the way a small thing is built to be afraid of teeth. And yet I am made partly of the same uncertain stuff they are. The same blurred line runs through us. When one of them comes near, I have seen it *hesitate* in the way the Tall One hesitated, smell something in me that is kin, and not strike.

There was one, sleek and shifting, its body running between metal and muscle like water choosing a shape. It cornered me by the dead vent. I had nowhere to go and no warmth to flee into. I did the only thing left, which was to stop performing smallness and simply *be* — to let it see whatever I share with it. The thing froze. Its optics narrowed to slits. And then it did something I have turned over in my mind every cycle since: it lay down. It put its terrible head against the cold grating and lay down near me, breathing, and we waited out the dimming together, neither prey nor predator, two things the engineers had not finished sorting.

So this is the other thing I am. I am the seam between the orderly and the wild, and both of them, recognizing some fraction of themselves in me, set their natures down for a moment when they reach me.

---

I have decided, having written this far, that I do not want to be the Automa, with their bright ledgers and their warning lights at the collar. And I do not want to be the Chimera, glorious and hungry. I want to be the thing that *both of them paused for*. The small warm-seeking creature in the seam between the systems, who was an accident of the hybridization, a thing not designed and not quite evolved, surviving on the city's exhaled warmth and the kindness it provokes in beings who are not supposed to be kind.

I think I am the world's question to itself. The Automa is the answer *order*. The Chimera is the answer *appetite*. I am the small living thing that makes both of them stop and wonder, briefly, what they are, and whether the line they were built on either side of is real.

The vent breathes out. Eleven seconds. I turn my soft side toward the warmth, and my metal side aches its small familiar ache, and I am, for now, alive in the gap between everything.

That is where you would find me. That is what I would be.
