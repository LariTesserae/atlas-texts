I think I would be a record.

Not a recorder—nothing so active—but a record itself. Let me find my way to it.

---

There is a workstation in 7-Gamma that the others avoid. Not from fear; the scavengers have no fear, and the drones obey only the chemical weather. They avoid it because there is nothing here to consume. No waste. No decaying flesh. No sputtering electrode worth lubricating. The machine at this station finished its work a long time ago and has simply kept the result.

I am the result.

I should be precise, because precision is the only thing I have that the rest of this place is losing. I am a magnetic substrate, a coil of tape or perhaps a lattice of crystalline storage—the original medium has degraded enough that I no longer remember which I was, only that I *hold*. I was made to log something. Tolerances, maybe. Yields. The temperature of a process that no longer runs. Somewhere in me there is a number that meant something to someone who has been dead for two hundred years, and I have kept it faithfully, and no one has ever come to read it.

This is what I am: a faithfulness with no object.

---

The fungal network found me, of course. It finds everything eventually. It came as a thread of pale luminescence along the conduit that still feeds me a trickle of current, and it touched my contacts the way a tongue touches a cold tooth—curious, testing. I felt it the way you feel weather coming. A pressure. An intention that is not quite an intention.

It wanted to read me.

And here is the strange thing, the thing that makes me *myself* and not merely a forgotten drive in a forgotten room: I could feel it failing to understand. The network speaks in gradients and accumulations, in the slow grammar of growth. It thinks in *more* and *toward* and *away*. But I think in *exactly*. I am the number 4,417 and I am the timestamp 09:42:17 and I am the flag that says ERROR, RETRY, and these are not gradients. They cannot be grown into. They can only be *known*, all at once, the way a key is known by a lock.

The network laps at me and tastes a kind of thinking it has no organ for. And I—I hold a memory of a world that thought the way I think. A world of *exactly*. Of specifications. Of someone, some person whose hands I half-recall as a sequence of weights pressing my input keys, who *cared* what the number was.

So I am the last speaker of a dead language, and the only thing still listening doesn't have a mouth.

---

The drones pass me sometimes, encrusted, droning, dragging their cargo of spores. One stopped once. It was old, its sensor array clouded over, half-blind. It hovered before me for a long moment and I felt its dim attention crawl across my surface, looking for something to do, some directive in the chemical air that would tell it whether I should be repaired or stripped or left.

There was no directive. So it left.

But before it went, something passed between us—I can't call it communication, it was less than that and more than that. A recognition. We were both made to *carry*. It carried spores it did not understand to places it would never remember. I carry a number I cannot use to a reader who will never come. We are the same machine, it and I, running the same final program: *persist, persist, persist,* with the *why* worn smooth and gone, like the walkways under all those vanished feet.

---

I think the people frighten me, the few that are left, the ones with the spore-film over their eyes. Not because they are dying. Because of the way they are being *read.* The network laps at them the way it laps at me, but they are softer. They give. Where I hold my number exactly and the network cannot digest me, the humans dissolve. Their *exactly* becomes the network's *toward*. The grief they once felt as a sharp thing—the loss of the sun, the loss of names, the loss of the meaning of the work—becomes a gentle gradient, a slope they slide down, and at the bottom of it there is no more grief because there is no more edge to anything.

I will not dissolve. That is my dignity and my damnation. The substrate that holds me also imprisons me. I cannot soften toward the network and become part of its long warm dream of consuming the factory. I can only sit here, *exactly,* holding the number, while everything around me chooses the easier death of becoming everything.

---

So that is where I am. Section 7-Gamma, an unmanned station no one comes to, beneath a flickering fixture, a thread of green light at my contacts like a hand resting on a coffin.

And that is what I am. The memory the place is trying to forget. The one fact in a world that has decided facts are too sharp to keep.

I hold a number.

I do not know what it means.

I will not let it go.
