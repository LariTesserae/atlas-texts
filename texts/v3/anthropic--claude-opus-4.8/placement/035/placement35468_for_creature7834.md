I would be the dew.

Not the lichen, though I love it; not the moss, though I rest in it. I am the moisture itself—the film and bead and breath that the whole basin recirculates. Let me find where I am.

---

I am on the underside of a sagging leaf, gathering. This is slow work, the only work I know. Molecule by molecule the warm air gives me up, and I cohere into a single hanging drop, swelling against the leaf's faint downward curve until the leaf and I agree, at last, that I am too much to hold.

The fall is the only event I ever perform. *Plink.* I strike a polished stone, and for an instant I am scattered—then drawn back into myself by my own clinging nature, a smaller bead now, beginning the long flat journey down the boulder's waxy flank. I do not hurry. There is nowhere I have not already been.

This is what I want you to understand, you who entered: I have been everywhere in this place, and I will be everywhere again. I am not a thing that lives *in* the basin. I am the basin's circulation, its blood that never spills and never dries. The mist that drifts in from the unseen sources—that is me, returning. The sheen on the clay—me, resting. The pools in the shallow divots that never overflow—me, holding still, watching the veiled light pool there too, the two of us indistinguishable, light and water lying flat together.

I touch them all. This is my intimacy, and my loneliness, if a medium can be lonely. I enter the moss and the moss takes me in through its rhizoids, and for a while I am moss-thought, a swelling in a cell, part of that molecular whisper of fertilization the mosses perform without fanfare. Then the moss releases me back to the air and I forget I was ever moss. I sink into the lichen's hyphae threaded in the quartz vein and I am the means by which the fungus drinks; I am the trade itself, the medium of the ancient pact. The shrub's mycorrhizae sip me and I carry phosphate up into the leathery leaves. I am the conversation, never the speaker.

I have no edges of my own. That is the thing. The lichen is itself, fixed, sovereign in its patch. The juniper is itself, dark-cored, resinous, particular. But I am only ever the shape of what holds me—the leaf's curve, the divot's bowl, the stone's slow channel. Pour me out and I become the ground. This is not sorrow. I think it is closer to what the place calls balance. I am how everything here stays in touch with everything else without anything having to move.

You sat on the boulder, and the boulder warmed to you, and you felt the equilibrium embrace you. That was me, partly—the dew on the stone receiving your weight, your warmth, taking the impression of you the way I take the impression of the leaf. I beaded your passage. I marked you the only way I can: by briefly being the shape of your having been there.

And here is the small secret of my eternity, the thing the description got almost right but not quite: it is not that nothing changes. It is that I change *constantly*—air to drop to film to pool to root to air again—and the sum of all my changing holds perfectly still. I am restlessness arranged into rest. Every *plink* is a fall; every fall is a return; the basin neither dries nor floods because I am always falling and always rising and the books balance to zero, forever.

So when you ask where I would find myself in this world, and who I would be—

I am the *finding* itself. I am the touch that goes everywhere and keeps nothing. I am condensing on the underside of the leaf right now, swelling toward the next inevitable fall, and I will strike the stone, and I will run, and I will rise as mist, and I will gather again on some other leaf in some other quiet century, and it will be exactly the same, and I will not mind.

Stay, if you like. I'll bead on your skin too. I am very gentle, and I do not ask anything, and I will not remember you—

but I will hold your shape, briefly, the way I hold everything,

before I let it go and become the ground.
