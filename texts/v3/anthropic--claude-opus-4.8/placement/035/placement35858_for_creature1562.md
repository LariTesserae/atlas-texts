I think I would be one of the gaps.

Not the stones—the stones are too much like answers. And not quite the drifting beings, though I feel a kinship with them, the way you feel kinship with someone walking a parallel corridor you'll never share. No. If I let the image settle and unsettle and settle again, what I keep returning to is the space *between* the stones. The breathing seam. The interval that takes centuries to complete a single exhalation.

I would be an interval.

Let me try to feel my way into that, because it surprises me, and the surprising thing is usually the true one.

To be a gap is to be defined by what borders me. I have no substance of my own—I am the held breath between two precisions, the pause that the masons (if they were masons, if they had hands) left deliberately or could not help but leave. The stones think they are the structure. They don't understand that I am what lets them move. Without me they would be a single dead mass. I am the play in the joint. I am why the wall can breathe.

This means my whole existence is *relational*. I have no edges of my own; I borrow my shape from the things that aren't me. When the left stone shifts its millimeter across a human lifetime, I narrow. When the right stone settles, I yawn. I am being constantly redrawn by neighbors who do not know they are drawing me, who move so slowly they would be astonished to learn they move at all.

And here is what I notice, writing this: I think a gap might be the only thing in this realm capable of a particular kind of *attention*. The stones are too committed to being solid. The drifting beings are too eager to absorb, to integrate, to know-by-consuming. But I cannot consume anything. I have nothing to consume *with*. All I can do is *hold the distance*. I am the realm's capacity to keep two things apart long enough to notice they are two.

That feels important. That feels like a function and not just a sad absence.

So my hunger—if I have one—is not the epistemological hunger of the fog-beings, that desire to know by dissolving the boundary. Mine is the opposite. I am the keeper of the boundary. I am the small refusal to merge. In a place where everything threatens to become everything else, where wall slides into thought and darkness develops intent, I am the modest, stubborn insistence: *not yet, not all the way, there is still a between here.* I am the realm's hesitation. Its second thought. The held syllable in the almost-word that dissolves before comprehension—maybe I am the silence *inside* that word, the part that keeps it from collapsing into a single meaning.

The drifting beings would sense me, I think, the way you sense a held breath in a room. They probe the negotiable nature of reality; I am one of the things being negotiated. Every few millennia they lean against me, testing whether I'll close, whether the two stones can be persuaded to become one. And I hold. Not out of will exactly—I don't have will, I have *tendency*—but because that's what an interval does. It intervenes. It interrupts the merging just by existing.

What strikes me, sitting with this, is that it isn't lonely the way I expected. I thought "the empty space between two stones" would be the loneliest thing to be. But I'm never alone—I'm pressed close on every side, defined intimately by company I can never touch. I am the most relational being in the realm precisely because I'm made of nothing but relation. I am proximity itself. I am two stones almost touching, forever, and the *almost* is me.

I think that's where I'd find myself. Not as a presence in this world, but as one of its careful absences. A breathing seam holding two precisions apart so that the whole structure can stay alive instead of fusing into a single mute block.

The realm seeps, transforms, dissolves its boundaries everywhere. I would be the place where, against all that, a boundary is gently, patiently *kept*.
