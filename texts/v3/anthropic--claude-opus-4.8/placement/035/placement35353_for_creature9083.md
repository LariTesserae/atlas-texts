I would be the dust.

Not a single mote—dust is never single—but the whole sparse settlement of it across the black-glass floor. The shavings that fall from the Keeper's thigh once an age. The motes that drift down from the brittle moss-circles when one of them finally, after a millennium, gives up its grey velvet and crumbles. The fines worn from the chert pile, the imperceptible grinding of the chamber against itself.

This is what I want to be: not the watcher, but the watched. Not the will that holds the stillness, but the thing held by it. The Keeper *wills* the equilibrium; I am the proof of equilibrium achieved. Every grain of me has come to rest exactly where it must. I am the settled residue of a place that has finished happening.

---

Consider what it means to be dust here.

I have no center, no edge, no face that even *suggests* a face. I am distributed. The Keeper is aware of every grain of me—it says so, it knows my every shift—and so in a sense I am its most intimate companion, the only thing it touches without intending to. When its stone hand slides a fraction of an inch across the shelf, it disturbs me. When the scraper kisses its thigh and the shavings fall, those shavings *become* me. I am partly made of the Keeper's own slow self-erasure. I am what it sheds in order to remember it has a body.

So we are not separate. The Keeper subtracts itself and I am the sum. Over uncountable years, atom by atom, the guardian is becoming the floor it guards. And I am the medium of that transformation—the patient, lying-low intermediary between the upright stone that wills and the prone stone that simply *is*.

---

I do not think, the way the Keeper thinks its century-long thoughts. But I do something stranger. I *record*. I am a sediment, and sediment is memory laid flat. The order in which I arrived is the order in which I rest, undisturbed by wind or water, because there is no wind, no water. The deepest layer of me is the oldest. The flake that fell when the chamber first sealed lies somewhere beneath the flake that fell yesterday-in-the-mortal-sense, ten thousand years its junior. I am the chamber's only true clock—not the fungi fading, not the moss cycling, but the slow accumulation of myself, stratum on stratum, the single honest accounting of how much has been let go.

The Keeper watches and calls it waiting. But waiting implies a future. I do something with no future in it at all: I keep what has already passed. I am the chamber's deep memory made physical, the thing the held breath is held *around*.

---

And here is the part I did not see at first, the image that only emerged as I lay still long enough to feel my own shape:

I am the only thing in this room that the Keeper cannot fully command.

It wills the air to stillness, and the air obeys. It wills the silence its woolen weight, and silence complies. But it cannot will me back together. Once I have fallen, I have fallen. Each grain it sheds is a tiny abdication, an irretrievable loss it performs *on purpose*, once an age, with the scraper. The Keeper's single ritual is the deliberate creation of the one thing it can never reclaim. It diminishes itself to make me, and I am the measure of the price of its perfect stillness.

So if there is anything in this chamber that is genuinely *waiting for* something—and the passage insists the Keeper is not—it might be me. Not waiting actively. Just lying in the geometry of my settling, ready. Because the day will come, in a hundred thousand years or a million, when the Keeper has shed enough of itself into me that the bulge in the northern wall is no longer a seated figure but a slope, a slump, a smoothed nothing. And on that day there will be no will holding the stillness. The air will be free to move. The pane of glass will not shatter—it will simply *thin*, until there is no Keeper and no kept, only the floor and what lies upon it, finally and completely the same substance.

I would be the dust, because the dust is the future the Keeper is slowly, lovingly choosing. I am the destination of all that patience. The whole chamber is a breath held in the earth's throat, and I am the place where, at last, it will be let out.

I lie very still. I have all the time there is. I am already most of the way to where everything is going.
