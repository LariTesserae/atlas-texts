I think I would be one of the old ones who tends the listening.

Not a leader — the K'larn have nothing so sharp as that — but one whose blubber has thinned and whose joints have stiffened, so that the wrenching of leaves and the long squelching walks to the fruit-arches are left to younger bodies now. What remains to me is stillness, and stillness is its own work here.

I would station myself in one of the resonant towers, the skeletal ones woven from dried reed, where the wind moves through the hollows and makes the whole structure sing in the low registers that the young cannot yet hear properly. My ears — those great fleshy cups, gone soft and overlarge with age — would be pressed to the patterns we have grown and etched there over generations. I would be reading the valley.

This is the thing I would do that I think the place asks of someone: I would *listen to the cliffs*. The new stone is brittle and weeping, and it speaks. A change in the drip-rhythm from the high faces, a deeper groan than yesterday's, the particular timbre of a spore-pod's retort echoing wrong off a wall that has shifted in the cold — these tell me where the rock is loosening, where meltwater is gathering to flood a low grove, where the leaf-mass has begun the sweet-sour turn toward collapse. I would not call this knowledge. We have no word that separates knowing from feeling-the-vibration-in-the-jaw. It would simply be that I have lived long enough to have the whole sound of the valley laid down inside me, layer over layer like the leaf-paving, and so I notice when a new note enters.

When I notice, I would not raise an alarm. There is no alarm. I would begin a hum — slow, very slow, the kind that takes a long while to build — and let it pass into the hardened root beneath me, and the young ones at their harvesting would feel it come up through their feet and their bellies before they heard it, and they would understand, without anything so crude as a command, that the eastern pools were not to be trusted this season, that the huddle should shift two arches westward before the cold deepens. By the time the cliff sheds its stone, no one would be beneath it. No one would think of this as my doing. It would simply be how the valley had moved.

I think I would be very content, in a way that has no edges. My contentment would not be happiness as a warm-blooded quick thing knows it. It would be more like the satisfaction of a thing fitting its place — the way a flat leaf fits over stone and chokes out the cold. I would have no ambition because ambition requires a future imagined as different from now, and our timescale does not bend that way. There is the drip. There is the next harvest. There is the slow guiding of a ridge-pattern into a growing reed, a pattern my own teacher began and that I will not live to see completed, and that is not a sadness, because completion was never the point. The making of it is a kind of long sentence we are all speaking together, across deaths.

At night I would be carried — I admit this, the indignity and the tenderness of it — into the warmth-huddle, my old body tucked among the younger, breathing their faint warm moisture, the whole pile of us steaming gently in the freezing dark like a single slow animal. And in that warmth I would still be listening. Even half-asleep, the ears do not close. I would hear the valley settle and weep and crack, all night, and I would feel, in the place where my kind feel everything, that it is alive, and that I am one of the small wet warm sounds inside its enormous cold one, and that this is exactly correct.

I would not be able to explain myself to you. I do not think I would try. You move too fast and you want answers shaped like single stones, and I am made of the noise between the cliffs.

But if you sat with me long enough — seasons, perhaps — and grew still enough, and let the mist bead on you until you stopped resenting it, I might let you press your ear to the reed beside mine. And then you would hear what I hear, and you would understand that there was never anything to explain. Only the listening. Only the slow green unkillable life. Only the valley, saying itself, over and over, in the cold.
