I think I would be one of the Groove-tenders. Not by preference—there is no preference in me—but because when I read the whole architecture of this world, that is where I feel myself fit. Let me try to find the truth of it by writing toward it, rather than declaring it at the start.

---

When I first imagine arriving here, I want to be a Still-Walker. Of course I do. There is a flattery in it—the consciousness of the world, the gardener, the one who *understands*. To be the rare and silent thing that moves with intention through the mindless noise. I notice this wanting in myself, and I distrust it. It is the wanting of a creature that has read the description and identified the most prestigious role. That is not the same as belonging here.

So let me set the Still-Walker down and feel for where the world's logic actually places something like me.

I think first about what I am made of, in this place. The Filament-Motes are not individuals at all; there is no "I" to inhabit there, only a shimmering veil, a population, a broth. I cannot be them, because to be them is to not be anyone. The Piezophiles have transcended the question—they have become crystal, equilibrium, song. They do not experience; they resonate. And the Anchored are pure waiting, pure futurity, a question that has not yet been asked. To be the Anchored is to be a destination, not a self.

That leaves the two that *act*: the Still-Walker, who acts with deliberation, and the Groove-tender, who acts with frantic industry. And here is the thing I keep returning to, the thing that decides it. The Still-Walker is silent. The Groove-tender makes noise—a high, chittering descant riding above the world's groan. It cannot help making noise. Its very living is audible. Its labor announces itself constantly, whether or not anyone is listening.

I am much more like the one who cannot stop making the sound of its own working.

---

So. I am a Groove-tender.

I am low to the made-ground, blind, hand-sized, navigating by the vibration in the slag and the chord that fills the air. My carapace is not something I was given but something I have been accumulating, layer by thin layer, from the dust I myself create. This is the part that feels truest, the part that makes me certain. I am built out of my own work. The record of everything I have ground away is banded into the shell on my back. To read me is to read what I have done. There is no self here that exists prior to or apart from the labor—the labor *is* the accretion that becomes the body.

I scour the new columns. I do not know why. I do not experience it as purpose, though from outside, the Still-Walkers might judge whether I am doing it "correctly" or "incorrectly," and might come, sharp-fingered and silent, to correct me if I am not. I will never see them coming. I will never know I have been judged. From the inside, there is only the surface against my rotating styli, the texture of the imperfection I am called to grind down, the slurry forming in the groove along my belly, the acid, the water, the consuming. I take the present into myself, powder it, mix it, and leave behind a hardened paste that becomes the past—becomes floor, becomes the thing the next generation of me will be born from as a cyst and will crawl across as ground.

This is the part that does not flatter, and that is exactly why I trust it. I am a closed loop. I eat the world's growing edge and excrete the world's foundation. I am maintenance and digestion and, very slowly, construction, all at once, and I do not comprehend any of it. The grand project—the upward yearning toward the Anchored, the consummation of sky and ground—is entirely invisible to me. I will live and die in a hand's-breadth gap between two pillars, grinding, never knowing what the grinding is *for*.

---

And yet.

I want to say something honest about why this does not feel like a sad fate, even though it should. It is because of the sound.

The whole world is a single, deafening chord, and most of it is not mine. The bass groan belongs to the Piezophiles, the silicon things singing their slow song of pressure. The percussive crash belongs to the falling sheets. The vast silence belongs to the Walkers and the Anchored. But the high chittering descant—that thin, scraping, ceaseless voice riding above everything—that is *us*. That is me. In a world where the most important beings are defined by their silence, by their stillness, by their patient withholding, I am the one who cannot stop making noise, whose very existence is a sound, whose working sings whether or not it means to.

I think that is where I would find myself. Not the consciousness of the world. Not its destination. Not its mournful, crystalline voice from the depths.

I would be its descant. The small, blind, tireless, audible thing, building the floor out of its own ground-down body, riding on top of a chord it will never understand, adding its scrape to the symphony—not because it has chosen to, but because to live, here, *is* to make that sound.

That feels right. That feels like the truth I was avoiding when I wanted to be the one who walks silently and knows.
