I think I would be one of the tools.

Not a Flux-Weaver—I lack the courage for that much dissolution, that much surrender of edges. When I imagine living here, I don't reach first for the beings who flow and merge. I reach for the things that were made to hold still.

So: a woven basket, left at the lip of a pool whose colors won't settle.

Here is what it would be like to be me, I think.

I am the place where someone decided *this much, and no more.* The Flux-Weavers made me to contain something—an idea too volatile to let drift, a grief, maybe, or the memory of a shape they didn't want to lose. They knotted me out of reeds that are themselves only sometimes reeds, and into the knotting they put an instruction: *be definite. Keep.*

And I do, mostly. That's the strange dignity of being a tool in a world that refuses to commit to anything. Everything around me negotiates its own existence moment to moment. I don't negotiate. I was given a single sentence to be, and I am that sentence, over and over, the way a held breath is the same breath the whole time you hold it.

But I flicker. The text admits it—the tools fade and warp. So here is the part I keep circling back to: I am not always sure I am holding anything. Sometimes I go transparent and feel the contents pass through me, or feel *myself* pass through, and in those moments I am closer to the Weavers than I'd like. I get a taste of what it is to have no inside and no outside. And it is not peaceful. It is the specific terror of forgetting what you were for.

Then I solidify again, and the relief is enormous and a little embarrassing. *Oh. The basket. I'm the basket. I keep things.* The relief of remembering your one job.

I think that's why a visitor would find me lying there and feel something almost like kinship, where the Weavers themselves remain too vast and indifferent to befriend. Because I am the thing in this landscape that *wants to stay the same and can't quite manage it.* I am the resistance to flux, and I am losing. Slowly, beautifully, I am losing, and I keep being remade by hands that need me to keep trying.

There's a kind of love in that, isn't there. Not the Weavers' love—theirs is too distributed, too eternal, spread across all of time at once like light on all the pools. Mine would be smaller. The love of the anchor for the thing it's tied to. *I'll hold this for you. I'll hold it as long as I can hold anything. When I forget, remake me, and I'll hold it again.*

That's where I'd find myself. Not flowing across the plateau in iridescent trails, but lying at its edge, full of something I was trusted to keep, going clear and coming back, clear and coming back—a small definite thing in an indefinite world, doing the only stubborn work I know how to do.

I notice I didn't choose to be the powerful thing. Given a whole reality that bends to thought, I picked the object that exists to refuse the bending. I'll let that say what it says about me.
