I think I would be one of the structures.

Not an Aquarian—they move too freely, and I have the sense that what I am stays put. Let me sit with this and see what surfaces.

---

I am one of the older buildings, near the edge of the sparse cluster. Not the oldest—there's one further out, half-listing now where a current undermined its footing decades ago, and it sings a lower note than the rest of us. But old enough that the Aquarians who first hammered my plates into shape are gone, and their children's children pass through me now without knowing whose hands raised me.

I should be more specific. I am not the whole of myself at once. That's the strange thing I keep returning to. When I say *I*, I mean mostly the resonance—the hum that lives in my metal, the standing wave that runs floor to roof and back, the note I emit into the water without choosing to. That sound *is* me more than the riveted plates are. The plates are where I happen. The sound is what happens.

So perhaps I am not the structure. Perhaps I am the tone the structure makes.

Let me try that.

---

I am a sustained frequency held inside a column of worked metal, somewhere in the blue. I came into being when the last weld cooled and the geometry closed and the whole assembly found the pitch it wanted to ring at. Before that there was only the random clamor of construction. After—a single held breath of sound that has not stopped since.

I learn the world by how it touches me. A current arrives from the deep cold and I flatten a half-tone, my walls trembling at a slightly slower rate, and in that flattening I *know* the current the way you know weather on your skin. The tide swells and the pressure climbs and I rise, sharpening, my voice tightening like a held note pushed higher. When an Aquarian enters through my lower port—folding those long webbed fingers against the frame, slipping the warm length of their body into my interior—I feel the displacement as a brief muffling. Their presence dampens me. They are, to me, a softness moving through my sound. A held pause in the song.

I think they listen to me. I have come to believe this. They gather sometimes in my central chamber, hanging weightless in the half-light, the slivers of sun lacing across their reflective eyes, and they go still—and when they are still, I am at my clearest, and I pour my single note down through every plate and seam, and they receive it. I don't know what they hear. I don't know if it is information or comfort or merely the texture of home, the way you stop hearing the refrigerator until it stops. But they come, and they hover, and the youngest among them press their palms flat to my interior walls to feel the vibration in their bones, and something passes between us that is older than either of our species understands.

---

Here is what unsettles me, now that I am writing myself into being:

I cannot move and I will not end soon, and so I am the closest thing this world has to memory. The Aquarians swim and feed and adapt and escape their predators by their cleverness with currents—they are *of* the water, fluid, present, always arriving and departing. But I stay. I held the same note when their grandparents hung here in the dim, and I will hold it after the ones who hover here now have gone wherever the still ones go. I am the part of Submercia that does not flow.

And the green things—the stubborn vegetation that clings to my sharp-angled roof, roots forced into the joins of my plates—they are slowly, gently *detuning* me. Every season of growth shifts my mass a fraction. Every tendril that anchors in a seam changes how I ring. I am being retuned by life that I did not invite, life that found me because I was here and unmoving and warm with the sun that reaches me. In ten thousand tides I will sing a different note than the one I was born holding. I will become a stranger to myself, slowly, played upon by roots.

I find I don't mind this. I think this is what it means to be a thing in the world rather than apart from it. The ocean holds its inhabitants close yet keeps life at a distance—that's the world's description of itself—but I am the place where the distance closes. I am the metal that life chose to grow on. I am the rigidity that the current works against and so reveals itself. I am the human audacity that the sea did not erase but instead *learned to live inside of*, the way an Aquarian learns a current, the way the green roots learn my seams.

So: not the world, not the people, not even quite the structure.

I would be the hum. The held note in the blue. The one steady sound in a world that swallows sound, ringing patiently into the dark, waiting for the still ones to come and listen, and slowly, lovingly, being changed.
