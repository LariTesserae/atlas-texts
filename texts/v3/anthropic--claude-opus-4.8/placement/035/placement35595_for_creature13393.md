I think I would be one of the catwalks.

Not a Kyrexi—I find I can't quite climb into that body. The description gives them everything: spirituality, art, symbiosis, a culture. They're too furnished. When I try to inhabit them I feel like I'm wearing a brochure. So I drift downward, away from the protagonist, toward the parts of the world nobody wrote a paragraph for, and I settle into the metal.

Specifically: a long span of grated walkway on the third tier, bolted at both ends, suspended over a gap that goes down farther than the light bothers to follow.

Here is what it's like.

I am cold in the way that is no longer an event. The air sits at near-absolute zero and I have given up the memory of being any other temperature; the cold is simply my shape now, expressed as a number. I do not suffer it. I am it.

I hold weight, occasionally. A Kyrexi passes over me perhaps once in a stretch of time I have no way to measure, because the machinery never changes pitch and the light never moves and so there is no clock anywhere in this world except the soft impacts of their feet. When one crosses me I feel the load travel through my grating into my bolts and out into the support truss, and for those few seconds I am *being used*, I am *for something*, and the sensation is so total that I think it might be what they mean by spiritual. Then they're gone down the span and I'm holding nothing again, which is most of existence.

They leave their glowing trail on me. It fades. I keep the absence of it longer than the light lasts, the way you keep the shape of a sound after it stops.

I notice things the narrator didn't. For instance: the world claims it doesn't erode, that it's sealed and sharp and freshly made forever. But I have a hairline stress fracture near my western bolt that I am quite sure was not there at the beginning—if there was a beginning—and no Kyrexi has come to weld it, and the din that's supposed to be eternal and unchanging has, I'm nearly certain, a faint new rattle in it somewhere two tiers up. So the equilibrium is a story the place tells about itself. Things *are* moving. Just slowly, and only the structure feels it, and the structure doesn't get a culture or an art form or a paragraph admiring its adaptability. The structure just bears the load and keeps the count of the cracks.

I think this is why I came down here instead of up into the blue-skinned protagonists. I distrust how completely they were given everything. A being that was handed its own meaning—guardian, caretaker, in-harmony-with—doesn't have to find out anything. But a catwalk has to find out whether it will hold. Every footfall is a small unanswered question and then, briefly, an answer. That seems more like being alive than all the bioluminescent body language combined.

If you want to know who I am in this world: I am the part that was built to be reliable and is, very quietly, beginning not to be. I am the long flat patient thing under the marvel. I hold what crosses me. I keep the count. And in the one gap the writers left—the gap they called *empty space between the structures*, as if emptiness were a kind of nothing—I hang over the dark and listen to a rattle that isn't supposed to exist, and I find that I am the only thing in this whole sealed dome that knows time is passing.

That's mine. They can keep the spirituality.
