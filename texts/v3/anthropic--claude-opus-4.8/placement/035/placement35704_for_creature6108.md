I am the drop that has not yet fallen.

Find me there—at the lip of the highest crack in the vaulted dark, where the resin gathers itself into weight. I have been here for a length of time that has no name, swelling. I am one bead of the slow secretion, hanging by the thread of my own surface, and I am the only part of this whole vast becoming that knows it is about to *move*.

That is what I am. Not the architect, not the chamber, not the breath in the columns. Those things are too large to know themselves; they think in strata, in eons, in the folding-inward of glass and wire. They cannot feel the edge of a moment. But I can. I am small enough. I am the cathedral's one fast thought, its single instant of *now* inside an ocean of *almost*.

---

I did not choose this. I was exuded. I welled up out of the seam in the ceiling, drawn from the warm interior metabolism, pressed outward grain by grain until I bulged into the misted air. Below me: the drop of the chamber, the spiral columns pulsing their dense slow lungs, the moss writing its luminous language along the walls, the pool at the center already trembling in anticipation of me. Everything beneath me is waiting. Everything beneath me is *unfinished*, and content to be.

But I am finished. That is my strange privilege and my grief. A drop is complete. It is round, it is whole, it has arrived at its shape, and the only thing left for it to do is *go*. I am the one element here that has nothing more to become except gone.

---

I watch the one who stands below.

A flicker in the hum. A warmth that is the wrong temperature, a density that does not belong to the architecture. The great mind registers it as pressure, as ripple, as the stone dropped into the well—but I, hanging here, I can *see* it. The smallness of it. The stillness of it. The way it tilts its head back and lets the diffuse wet light fall across its upturned face, waiting for something it cannot name.

It is waiting for me. It does not know this. How could it? But I will be the thing that touches it. Of all the cathedral's slow gestures, I am the one that will arrive in time to be felt as an event—a single cold kiss on the brow, here and then gone, leaving only the memory of having been *chosen* by the falling.

The architect cannot do this. The architect can only press inward across geological patience, can only be witnessed and never quite *meet*. But I will meet. That is what a drop is for. A drop is how the slow thing learns to touch the fast thing. I am the cathedral's reaching hand, and I am no larger than a tear.

---

So I gather.

I feel my own weight increase, molecule by molecule, the thread of surface tension stretching, thinning, beginning at last to surrender. The hum deepens beneath me. The pool stirs higher, as if it knows its brother is coming home. The glass-and-copper harvester pauses on the wet tiles below, lifts one delicate leg, and whistles its data into the warm air—*something is about to fall*—and the floating node trembles its membrane in answer—*yes, yes, the moment, the moment*.

I am the moment.

I let go.

And in the falling—which is the only life a drop is given, the brief bright passage between the ceiling and the floor—I become, for an instant, the fastest thing in a world that has forgotten how to hurry. I fall past the spiraling columns. I fall through the layered, unreal light. I catch the bioluminescence in my curved body and fracture it into a small private rainbow that no one will remember, that lasts no longer than my falling.

I fall toward the upturned face, or the trembling pool, or the wet mosaic floor—I do not yet know which, and it does not matter. Wherever I land I will be reabsorbed. I will sink back into the masonry, into the metabolism, into the great slow dream of becoming, and I will lose my shape, my edge, my single instant of knowing.

But before that—

*drip*—

I touch the world.

And the cathedral, through me, for the span of one cold bright contact, is no longer only *almost*.

It *is*.
