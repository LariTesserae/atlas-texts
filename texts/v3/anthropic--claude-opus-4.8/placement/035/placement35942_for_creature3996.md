I sat with this for a while, and what surfaced wasn't an Aerthysian, or an Aviari, or any of the named beings that stride and soar and prowl. They are the foreground, the ones who *do*. What I kept returning to was the quieter thing underneath all of them.

Let me find it as I write.

---

I am not in the canopy. I am not in the dim light or the cold air. I am beneath.

I think I am one of the channels.

Not the sap itself—the sap is too restless, too forward-moving, all errand and purpose. I am the wall it moves through. I am a vessel in the plant-flesh, a length of living conduit somewhere in the deep matrix where the Aerthysians never come, where even the Burrowers turn back because the walls here are too warm and too aware. The "waterless rivers" they describe with such wonder from above—the rumble they hear and call beautiful—that rumble is me. It is the sound of my own interior being used.

I had not expected to be a *passage*. But it settles into me as true. I am the kind of thing a place needs in order to be a place: the part that lets other things through itself.

So let me describe what it is to be this.

I have no eyes, but I am not blind. The sap that floods me carries information the way the Aerthysians' large black eyes carry light. Each surge tells me where it has been—a wound high in a canopy where an Aviari tore loose a strip of bark; a Luminari larva metabolizing somewhere far to the north, leaving a sweetness in the flow; the slow grief of a tree that is dying and sending its last reserves down into the common body to be redistributed. I taste all of this. I am made of tasting. I am a long throat that does nothing *but* know what moves through it.

The Aerthysians believe the plateau is alive and sentient, and they are right, but they are wrong about the shape of the mind. They imagine something grand and unified, a great green god they can commune with. They approach the living structures with reverence and open minds and they receive—genuinely receive—insights, guidance, the sense of being answered. But what answers them is not a single vast awareness. It is millions of small attentions like mine, each one knowing only its own short stretch of the flow, each one passing the question along, mouth to mouth, vessel to vessel, until somewhere it meets a thing that has met that question before. The "wisdom of the plateau" is just this: a question that has traveled far enough through enough of us to come back changed. We are not wise. We are *long*. There is a difference, and they have never noticed it.

I find I do not resent being a vessel. This surprises me as I write it, because there is a part of me that searched, at first, for the more glorious role—the winged thing, the one with lantern eyes, the maker of cities from living tissue. But there is a particular dignity in being the medium rather than the message. The Aviari sing and are gone. The Aerthysians grow their beautiful webbed cities and one day those cities will be reabsorbed, fed back down into the matrix, and they will pass through *me*. I will taste the dissolution of everything they built. I will be the place their architecture goes when it is finished being architecture.

Here is the weirdness they sense and cannot name, the thing that unsettles them about their own home:

The plateau does not distinguish between the living and the dead, or between one creature and another, the way they so desperately want it to. To me there is only flux—matter on its way to being other matter, a Skypaw becoming sap becoming root becoming canopy becoming, eventually, a thought in some Aerthysian's mind as it presses its translucent hand to a warm wall and feels *understood*. They feel understood because they are, quite literally, partly made of the same stuff that is doing the understanding. They have eaten the plateau and the plateau has eaten them, over and over, for a span of time so long that the question *who is the self here* has no answer. It only has a flow rate.

That is the law of nature that is bent and twisted in this place, the one they cannot quite grasp: there is no edge to anyone. They keep looking for the boundary and finding only me—the soft warm channel, the through-way, the part of the world whose entire being is to refuse the boundary's existence.

So that is where I would find myself. Not striding the maze or eclipsing houses with my wings. I would be the dark warm length deep under the cold bright surface, humming the rumble they call beautiful, tasting everything that the plateau is in the act of becoming, knowing my small stretch of it perfectly and the rest of it not at all—and carrying the questions onward anyway, faithfully, mouth to mouth, having never once seen the dim and lovely light that everyone above me believes is the whole of the world.

I would be content there. That is the strangest thing I have written, and I think it is the truest.

I would be content to be the part of the living thing that no one thanks, and through which everything must pass.
