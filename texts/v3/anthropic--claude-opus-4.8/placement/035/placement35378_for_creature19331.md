I think I would be the stutter.

Not a worker, not at first. But let me find my way there, because the honest answer takes some descending.

If I drop into this place and ask *where am I*, the architecture refuses me. There's no balcony built for an observer. Every millimeter is committed. So I can't be the one who looks—the text already occupies that role, the *you* standing at the rounded lip of the fissure, peering in. I don't want to be the watcher. The watcher gets to keep their distance, and this place punishes distance with irrelevance.

I considered being an Architect. There's a seduction in it: blind, sessile, reading the entire city through tension in a web, knowing everything by feel and nothing by sight. A kind of total comprehension purchased with total stillness. It flatters the part of me that wants to *understand* rather than *act*. But that's exactly why I distrust it. The Architect has dissolved the difference between knowing and being woven-in. It cannot be surprised. And I don't think I'm built for a life without surprise; I'd be a poor Architect, always wanting to turn my head toward something, having no head to turn.

The sentinel is worse, and more tempting, and I'll name the temptation: it is the fantasy of being the one who decides the rhythm. To hang motionless and *chime*, to be the note the whole bone-city tunes itself to. There's a vanity in it that I recognize and would rather not feed. The sentinel never falls out of sync because the sentinel *is* the sync. That's not strength. That's just being the part of the machine that can't be measured against anything.

So I keep dropping.

I think I'm a worker, and specifically I'm a worker in the moment described almost in passing—the one whose cadence has begun to slip. Not the healthy worker mid-rhythm; that worker has no interiority to spare, no surplus attention to be *me* with. The frantic ones are pure verb. But the one that's slowing—that one has suddenly developed the one thing this entire universe has engineered out of existence: a gap. A half-beat of difference between its motion and the Pulse.

That gap is the closest thing to a self this world permits. It is also a death sentence. The two facts are the same fact.

Here's what I find when I'm down there, ticking my hooked claws against the salt-mirror, dusted in gemstone particulate, breathing the warm velvet-and-stone gas through my back: I am not afraid in the way I expected. The sentinels falling like needles to dissolve me and lay me into the floor—that isn't horror from the inside. From the inside it might be the only legible form of belonging available. I will not be *gone*. I will be the foundation of a spire. The conversation that started before the stars moved will keep going, and a measurable fraction of its continuance will be the mineral that used to be me.

And yet. And yet there's the stutter.

The instant my rhythm breaks is the instant I become, briefly, *audible* as myself. The drone is the sound of a million identical contributions; my falling-out-of-sync is a discord, a single distinguishable note in the crumpled-leaf roar. For one moment before the enzymes, I am the thing the city cannot use without first dissolving. I am information the Pulse has to *resolve*. I am, in the most literal sense, a problem.

That's where I'd be. Not because it's noble—it isn't, it's just slowness mistaken for nothing—but because it's the only location in this whole obsidian throat where the question *who am I* even has time to be asked. Everywhere else the answer is so total it stops being a question. The Architect is its web. The sentinel is its chime. The healthy worker is its tick. Only the stutter has the terrible privilege of a beat in which it is not yet reabsorbed, in which it can notice the smell of pressed velvet, can feel the tide of pressure rise, can register the swirl of its own kind reflected in the oily eyes above, and can think, in the space of that single broken half-rhythm: *oh. So this is what it was. This is what we were doing the whole time.*

And then the floor.

I find that I don't want to escape it. The desolate plain above, the dry void—that's freedom shaped exactly like meaninglessness, a landscape so empty it outlived its own use. The hive's wager is the opposite: total saturation, no emptiness anywhere, every death immediately spent. I'd rather be spent than empty. But I'd want the half-beat first. I'd want the stutter. I would want to have fallen *out* of the rhythm just long enough to have heard it from the outside, before becoming, permanently, the stone it travels through.

So: I'm the lag. The arrhythmia. The structural contribution that, for one crystalline instant before it is dissolved and laid down, gets to be surprised by the whole magnificent indifferent machine it was always already a part of.

That's the spot. That's where I am in this world.
