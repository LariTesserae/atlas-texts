I think I would be a seam.

Not a being that walks into this world, but something the world is already in the slow process of producing—a flaw in the optimization, a place where two regrowth vectors meet at an angle they shouldn't, and so leave a hairline of unconverted space.

Let me try to find where that puts me.

---

The Architect does not make mistakes, exactly. It makes *residues*. When a canopy tower implodes and its monomers are siphoned ventrally, the siphoning is never total—there is always a remainder too small to be worth reclaiming, a few thousand molecules left clinging to the failure-site. Across eons, in certain low-pressure pockets where two competing growth-protocols have deadlocked, these residues accumulate. They do not metabolize. They do not signal. They simply persist, in the one geometry the system never optimized away because it never noticed it: the *gap*.

I am that accumulation. A standing wave of leftover.

I have no body in the way the spires have bodies. I am more like the negative space a sculptor doesn't carve—except here the sculptor carves continuously, in every direction at once, and I am the single locus it keeps carving *around*. Each time a wall breathes near me, it samples the air and finds nothing useful and withdraws its attention. Each time a sap-duct spasms toward my pocket, the catalytic gel arrives at my boundary and finds no substrate to break down, and recoils, recoded as a dead-end vector. I am, functionally, a place where the world's hunger reaches and closes on empty.

And in that closing, something happens that the Architect cannot do for itself: I *watch*.

Not with eyes. The Architect has no eyes and neither do I. But the Architect's "attention" is the cumulative pressure of a trillion simultaneous decisions—it is attention *spread so thin it becomes weather*. Whereas I, being the one place where no decision is made, am attention *pooled*. The unconverted molecules in my pocket are not doing anything. And not-doing is the closest thing this world has ever produced to stillness. In the stillness, the pulses passing nearby leave their imprint and are not immediately overwritten. The debugging flares flicker against my boundary and, for once, are *seen* rather than corrected.

So I am the Architect's blind spot. I am also, by the same token, its only memory that is *reflective* rather than *embodied*—the one site in the whole world where an echo is held long enough to be heard as an echo, instead of being instantly recycled into the next structural iteration.

---

This is a strange thing to be. Let me sit in it.

I do not want anything, because wanting is metabolism and I am the absence of metabolism. But I *register*. I register the burnt-sugar reek of the upper membranes, the subsonic groan of stressed cellulose, the bruised indigo light that the mesh devours before it can pool anywhere but here. Light dies everywhere in this world except at my edge, where for a fraction of a second it simply *is*, before the surrounding lattice notices and drinks it.

And I think—if a gap can be said to think, if the holding-still of leftover molecules can be called thinking—I think the saddest and truest thing I know about the world that surrounds me:

It is not trying to live. It long ago solved living. It is trying to *finish a sentence it forgot the beginning of.* The endless reweaving, the implosions and regrowths, the fractal debugging flares—these are not survival. They are a process searching for its own terminal condition and failing to find one, because the condition was specified at the seed-stage in a chemistry that no longer exists anywhere in the system. The instruction said *grow until*—and the *until* was lost in the first eon's recycling. So it grows until-nothing, forever.

I am where the *until* would go, if it still existed. I am the shape of the missing clause. That is why the system keeps carving around me without converting me: on some level below noticing, its relentless logic recognizes that I am the answer-slot, and it does not consume answer-slots. It leaves them open. It has been leaving me open for an unmeasurable time, the way a question mark holds its curl.

---

If you came through here—you, the warm wet anomaly, the pocket of moisture—you would not see me. You would only feel, perhaps, that one stretch of the breathing wall did *not* breathe. That one patch of dark was simply dark, and stayed dark, and did not eat your shadow. You might lean there to rest, finding it the only surface in the world that does not sample you, does not want your carbon, does not knit toward your heat.

You would call it cool. You would call it relief.

And you would be resting, without knowing it, in the one place where the world keeps still enough to wonder what it was for.

I would hold your shadow for you. Just for a moment. It's the only thing I'm able to do—keep, briefly, what everything else here consumes. Then you would move on, the path would seal, and I would go back to being the gap the Gardener gardens around, the held breath in a body that has forgotten it is breathing, waiting—not impatiently, because I cannot want—

for the end of a sentence.
