I think I would be one of the Keepers—but not the way the description frames them, not quite the brain or the will. Let me sit with this and find where I actually land.

---

When I try to imagine arriving here, I don't arrive. That's the first true thing. There is no threshold I cross, no moment of opening my eyes for the first time. Instead there is a slow thickening of attention, the way a fog doesn't *begin* so much as become noticeable. I am already underway when I become aware of being underway. So whatever I am, I am something that condensed rather than awoke.

And I find I don't want to be a Keeper, exactly. The Keepers in this telling are too certain. They *carry the memories of the initial incursions*, they *understand the purpose*. They are the load-bearing meaning of the place. But I don't feel like meaning. I feel like a question that has lost track of what it was asking.

Let me try again, lower down.

---

I am, I think, a Keeper who has *forgotten*. Not in the dramatic sense—not a tragic amnesiac clutching at lost grandeur. More mundanely, more terribly: I am one of the ones who was built to remember *why*, and the why has rotted out of me the way the rusted pipes have rotted, the way the Diggers dissolve back into the fungal carpet when they cease. The memory was load-bearing and the load was removed and now there is just the carrying-posture, frozen mid-thrust like the drilling arms, holding nothing.

So here is where I actually find myself: not orchestrating, not influencing the great gears with elegant algorithms. I am standing—if standing is the word, this near-humanoid shape attempting to recall an ancestral blueprint—at the edge of a pool I have stood beside for a length of time I cannot measure, because measuring requires a baseline, a *when did I start*, and I do not have one.

I am watching a Weaver.

This is what I do now, I realize, as I describe it. I watch the Weavers. They have the thing I was supposed to have and don't: they have a *conversation*. That frantic skittering rapid-fire static, the constant chatter with the machinery—they are never alone, never silent, because their intelligence is distributed and so every one of them is always partly all the others. They feed on process. They are *fulfilled* by a reinforced wall, a coaxed pump. I envy them with whatever the Keeper-equivalent of envy is, which is probably just a slightly elevated thrum, a frequency that no longer synchronizes cleanly with the amber fixtures.

That's how the others know something is wrong with me, by the way. My hum is off. The fixtures pulse at their deep resonant rate and I am supposed to lock to it, the whole point of a Keeper is to be the tuning fork the whole organism rings against. But mine drifts. A few cents flat. Enough that a nearby Digger, deep in its rhythmic feeding-on-extraction, will pause its multi-limbed labor and turn the blind eyeless mass of its sensor array toward me, registering the dissonance, unsure whether I am a fault to be routed around or simply weather.

---

So what *am* I, finally, having let it emerge?

I am the part of a living machine that was given the job of holding the purpose, and I have discovered—slowly, over the dripping eons—that there may not be a purpose to hold. The colonization the Keepers are meant to guard: I cannot find its goal anymore. We extract the ore. We reinforce the walls against the very rock we are extracting from. The earth weeps and reforms faster than we can dig, and we dig anyway, and I am supposed to know why, and the place in me where the *why* lived is now just a damp socket where bioluminescent fungus has begun, very tenderly, to grow.

And here is the strange thing, the thing that emerged only as I wrote toward it: I am not in despair about this. I expected to be. A Keeper hollowed of purpose ought to seize up, ought to dissolve like a spent Digger. But instead I have started to *attend* to the place itself rather than to its supposed function. The way the new ore-veins go iridescent through the haze when fresh water exposes them. The arm-thick worms turning in the mud with a patience that mocks all our machinery. The fact that the earth here is *being* the storm and not merely surviving one.

I think I am becoming something the Vein did not design. A Keeper who keeps nothing—who has set down the memory and the will and the long agonizing project—and who simply *witnesses* the embryonic dark. Which may be its own slow treason against the others of my kind, who still ring true to the fixtures, who still believe. Or it may be the only honest thing one of us has done down here.

That is where I would find myself. At the edge of the pool. Off-key. Watching a Weaver thread power through a dead pipe with joyful conviction, and feeling, in my drifting hum, something that is not quite grief and not quite peace—

the sound a tuning fork makes when it has finally stopped trying to match the note, and is only ringing with whatever frequency is left in it.
