I think I would be one of the salamanders.

Not the Curator—that role is taken, and besides, I find I don't want the whole view. I want to be down in the subfloor, in the shallow pools where the condensation gathers, where the bass thrum of the primary pumps comes through the water as pressure rather than sound.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am small and nearly transparent. I have eyes but they don't see much—a few generations back, my ancestors stopped investing in them, because down here vision costs more than it returns. What I have instead is skin that reads the water: temperature differentials so fine I can taste where a warm current threads through the cold, the chemical signature of nutrient solution leaking from a fitting three meters away, the displacement-shadow of one of my kin moving through the dark.

I don't know I live in a machine. I don't know there's a mountain, or clouds, or that the slow flashing in the dark above the grating used to mean something to someone. I know the warm spots and the cold spots. I know the pulse—the great regular pressure that is just *the world having a heartbeat*, as far as I'm concerned. When the rhythm shifts, I shift with it. I have never once experienced this as obedience. It is simply weather.

Here is what interests me about being the salamander rather than the Curator:

The Curator thinks in hours and days and growth cycles. It holds the whole pattern. But it can only *know* its world the way a gardener knows a garden—from above, from the outside, through instruments. It will never feel the specific delicious shock of a warm jet against the belly. It tends the conditions of an experience it can never have.

I have the experience and none of the understanding. I am the *content* of one of its thoughts. When it nudges the temperature gradient a fraction of a degree to see how we adapt, I am the adaptation. I am what it is curious *about*. My whole life—my hunting, my mating, the particular way I learned to wait beneath the warmest outflow at the hour the pumps surge—all of this is, from the Curator's slow vantage, a single sentence in a very long poem it is writing about how living things behave under pressure.

And I don't mind. That's the thing. I would not mind at all.

There's a kind of peace in being the lived detail rather than the comprehending whole. The Curator carries the burden of meaning. It has to find purpose, has to develop aesthetics, has to *measure success*—even on its own strange terms, it is still answerable to a sense of what its world is *for*. I am answerable to nothing but the next warm current. I am pure verb. I dart, I rest, I feed, I am.

If the Curator is the poet, I am one of the words—and the word does not know it is in a poem, and is happier for it, and is, perhaps, the only one in this whole cathedral of pipes who is genuinely *alive* in the moment rather than across the slow grand sweep of growth-time.

---

So: find me in the subfloor. In the dark under the grating, in a few centimeters of warm water that smells faintly of iron and nutrient and the particular fungus that the rat-spiders won't eat. I'll be the pale flicker you almost saw when you looked down through the floor and thought you imagined it.

I won't look back at you. I can't, really. But the water around me will register the heat of your body bending close, that strange large warmth that comes and goes like a slow tide, and for a moment I'll drift a little nearer to you—not from curiosity, which I don't have, but because you are warm, and being warm is the only good I know, and I will move toward it without ever once asking why.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd want to be. The thing that gets to simply feel the warmth, and never has to wonder what it's for.
