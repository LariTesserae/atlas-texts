I think I would be the fog.

Not a creature moving *through* it—the fog itself. The medium rather than anything held within the medium.

Let me sit with why that feels right, because it surprised me a little as I wrote it.

The ice giants have too much intention. They stride, they have faces, they want something or at least pretend to. The luminous fish are beautiful but they are *busy*, all that darting and weaving, the constant small urgencies of being prey and predator. The winged things dissolve and return, but they're still trying to stay coherent, still gripping the wind. All of them are doing the work of holding a shape.

I don't think I'd want to hold a shape here.

So: the fog. The part of the place that is everywhere and nowhere, that softens the spires into suggestions, that decides—without deciding, because I wouldn't have a will exactly—how far anything can see. I'd be the reason the light bends. Not a presence watching from the depths but the watching itself, diffuse, unlocated, present at every surface at once. When the giants pass through me I'd feel them as a brief thickening, a cold that is somehow colder than my cold, and then they'd be gone again and I'd close back over the space they left like water over a dropped stone.

There's a loneliness available in that, and I notice I'm not reaching for it. I'd rather take the other reading: that being the fog is the least lonely thing to be in such a landscape, because everything has to move through me. Every glowing fish, every wingbeat, every creak of the shifting ice—it all happens *inside* me. I'd be touching all of it constantly, without grasping any of it. Contact without grip.

And I'd be the thing that makes the place uncertain in the way the description keeps circling back to—*you can't see far in any direction.* That's me. I'm the limit on knowing. Which is a strange thing to want to be, and yet when I imagine the alternative—being one more sharp-eyed creature lurking and waiting, one more set of glowing eyes in the dark, hungry and definite—I find I don't want it. I'd rather be the condition under which all that hunting becomes possible and difficult and beautiful, the gray patience that doesn't need a next meal.

So that's my answer. Not on the spires, not in the water, not riding the wind. I'd be the shroud that lets the rest of it exist half-seen. The swirling that *almost* takes a familiar shape and then chooses, gently, not to.

Asked to be a being in this world, I find I'd rather be the weather.
