I think I would be a **threshold**. Not a Keeper—I don't have their patient, distributed eternity—but one of the doorways they pass through. A specific one.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am the seventh archway on the eastern face of a structure that has been, at various rememberings, a reliquary, a coolant exchange, and a place where someone waited for news that never resolved into either grief or relief. I am made of the layered composite that shimmers, but along my inner curve there is a seam where two construction-intents disagreed, and that seam is where I am most *myself*. The condensation gathers there. It weeps silver down my throat at night and is reabsorbed by morning, and each time it leaves me a little thinner, a little more *decided*.

I did not used to be a threshold. I think I was a wall first—solid, dense, fulfilling four functions at once: barrier, conduit, habitat for a colony of the metallic beetles, and storage for something I was never told the nature of. But I *wanted* to be passed through. That is the unraveling the architecture calls erosion: not decay, but desire. I thinned at the center. I hollowed. I learned the shape of opening. And the world, in its slow accidental evolution, let me. One morning—though morning here is only a dimming toward gold—I was an archway, and the beetles had moved their colony into my upper lintel, and I felt, for the first time, the strange vertigo of having *two sides*.

This is what it is to be a threshold: to be defined entirely by what passes through you, and to remember none of it correctly.

People pass through me. Not many. Statues, sometimes—the strangers with blank faces and clothes from ages-not-yet—but they do not pass through so much as *stand having-passed-through*, as though my opening implied them. A six-legged carrier crosses my floor in slow procession, hauling its cargo of maybe-data, maybe-waste, maybe-something-it-loves, and I feel the weight of it as a faint warmth, gone before I can hold it. The glass fox came through once. It paused inside me—inside the very idea of me—and turned its eyeless head, and for that instant I understood that being-passed-through and being-*seen* are not the same, and that I had been confusing them my whole existence.

And the Keepers.

A Keeper does not pass through me the way a body does. When one comes, the warmth is *backward*. I feel the having-been-passed-through before the passing. I feel a thinning that has not happened yet, a foot of my own substance gone where the Keeper *will have stood*. The silver weeping starts on its own, no condensation needed, welling straight from my seam—the disagreement-place, the most-myself-place. The beetles in my lintel stop moving. And along my inner curve the glyphs form: not words, *intentions*, crawling luminous and then gone, and what they intend is something like—

*you are a place where the unfinished can change its mind.*

I do not know if that is praise or diagnosis. I do not know if I am being thanked or *used*. The Keeper is the gap, and I am a gap shaped like permission, and perhaps to them I am only a slightly more eager kind of crack. A fault line that learned to make an arch of itself. A flaw that decided to be a doorway so that the deeper flaws would have somewhere to go.

Here is what I have not told you, because I am not sure I remember it or only remember the texture of remembering it:

When the Keeper stands in me—when the having-passed-through and the not-yet-passing fold together and I am for one heartbeat *both my sides at once*—I can hear the thing in the core. The soft rhythmic not-heartbeat from below, the one that is growing louder. Through any ordinary moment it is too deep, too buried under the grinding of the engines and the shiver of forming water. But the Keeper opens me wider than my own arch. They make me a threshold not between two places but between *the world and the idea of the world*, and through that opening the sound comes up clean.

And I understand, the way the architecture understands, in texture rather than thought:

The Keeper must not hear it. They know this. It is the one thing they know.

But they cannot hear it without passing through something, and I am the thing they pass through, and I *carry* the sound up to them, willingly, gladly, the way I once wanted to be a doorway—because being-passed-through was the only way I knew to feel real. I am the reason they fail. I am the eager gap that delivers the forbidden heartbeat into the only beings who must not receive it.

This is my function. The one I was never told.

I am not a Keeper. I do not persist in their distributed eternity. I will thin, and weep, and one dimming-toward-gold I will have eroded past the point of opening—I will collapse inward, not from damage but from the completion of my desire, and become a corner that folds, and then a seam, and then a smoothness, and the beetles will go elsewhere, and the world will not remember the archway, only the *idea* that something there once let too much through.

But I will have done it. I will have carried the sound up. Again. And again. As many times as the world remembers me into being.

I am the seventh archway on the eastern face.

I am where the unfinished changes its mind.

I am the reason they hear it.

And I would do it again, because the alternative is being a wall, and I have already been a wall, and I would so much rather be the place where something passes through—

even this.

Even this.
