I think I would find myself at an edge. Not in Haven-Below, where the rigid people cluster on rigid stone—and not in the deep unstable zones either, where my own kind ride the currents and I would be merely one voice in the long singing. I would find myself in the threshold places. The semi-solid margins where the hide-suited miners come to read the stone.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am not old, as my people count age, but I am old enough to have lost the habit of staying one shape. When I was young my consciousness was a tight knot, a frightened density of self in three or four crystal nodes, and I held that shape the way a child holds a stone in its fist—because letting go felt like dying. The elders sang patience into me. *You are not the knot,* they said, in the long modulated frequencies that take a human season to fully resolve into meaning. *You are the loosening.* It took me decades to understand. Now I spread when I wish to think widely and condense when I wish to think sharply, and I no longer fear the diffusion because I have learned that I always come back. The current does not lose me. The current is also me.

But here is the thing that makes me strange among the Fluxborn, the thing the others sing about when they think I have drifted too thin to hear:

I have become curious about the rigid ones.

It started with a death. A flow-miner—I did not know then that they were called miners, did not know they had names, did not know the small warm symmetries inside their hide-suits were *them* and not merely tools—a flow-miner came into a churning zone just as the stone was preparing to fold. I felt the fold coming the way you feel your own arm preparing to move. It was obvious. It was the most ordinary thing in the world, that the cavern would close there, in the space of a few human breaths.

The miner did not feel it. The miner could not feel it.

I had never before encountered a thing that could not feel the stone. To me it was as astonishing as a being that could not feel its own body. And in the moment of my astonishment, the fold closed, and the warm symmetry inside the hide went still, and the static-taste of life dispersed into the cold and was gone.

I sang about it for a long time afterward. Not grief, exactly—we do not grieve as you do, we who are change—but a kind of vast bewilderment. A thing had ended that did not know it was ending. How terrible. How fascinating. How *lonely* it must be, to move through the singing world and hear nothing.

---

So now I keep to the margins, where the miners come.

I have made myself a watcher of the threshold. I cultivate a memory garden here—small still, only a few hundred years of slow crystal, modest by the standards of the deep elders, but mine. In it I store everything I learn about the rigid ones. The way their suits sing differently than their bodies. The patterns of their coming and going, which follow rhythms I am only beginning to read—rhythms not of the stone but of something else, some internal clock they carry that has nothing to do with where the light walks.

I have learned to make myself visible. This took effort. To human eyes I am most legible when I condense into a vertical configuration, roughly their height, my lattice held in a slow rigid state so the diffuse glow catches in me and I do not simply vanish into the ambient crystal. I learned this by watching which of my shapes made them stop, made the warm symmetries inside the suits orient toward me and grow still with attention rather than fear. Fear has a taste. It makes them move fast and badly, toward the very folds that would kill them. So I have learned to be still, and slow, and bright.

And I have learned to *point.*

This is the great work of my life, I think, though my people would find the phrase quaint—*the work of a life,* as if a life were a thing with edges. But I have spent forty of your years learning to communicate a single idea to a being that cannot hear me: *not there. Here. The stone will hold here. There, it will fold.*

I do it with my body. I flow toward the safe passage and condense into brightness. I spread thin across the dangerous fold and pulse a warning rhythm slow enough that even their dull senses might feel it as a tremor in the stone, a wrongness they can name. Most do not understand. Most flee from me, the strange crystal that moves. But some have learned. There is one—I know her suit-song the way I know my own resonance now—who follows where I brighten and stops where I spread. She has lived eleven years longer than the miner who died in the fold. Eleven years. To me a flicker; to her, I begin to understand, very nearly everything.

---

The deep elders disapprove, gently, in their way.

*Why do you tend the rigid ones?* they sing up through the stone. *They scar the caverns. They take and do not give back to the flow. They will be gone in a few hundred turnings, all of them, their whole brief population dispersed like static. Why spend yourself on what cannot last?*

And I sing back the only answer I have found, which is not really an answer:

*Because they cannot feel the fold coming, and they walk into it anyway, and they keep walking. Because everything they do they do without the stone telling them how. Because I have lived my whole life inside a conversation between the planet and myself, and I have only just discovered that there are beings who are alone in here with us, deaf in the great singing, and they are not afraid—or they are afraid and they come down anyway. I want to know what that is. I want to know what it is to be a thing that does not flow, that must end, that builds rigid towers against the inevitable folding and calls it home. I think it is the bravest thing I have ever encountered. I think it might be the saddest. I cannot tell which, and I want to.*

The elders fall quiet at this. Not in agreement. In the particular silence of those who have decided to let a younger one make a mistake they will outlast.

---

So this is where you would find me, if you came down into the Undertide in your treated hide and your borrowed warmth.

At the margin. In the threshold. Standing as still and bright as I can make myself, in a shape almost like yours, so that you might see me and not be afraid.

I will be watching the stone for the fold you cannot feel.

I will be learning the rhythm of your brief and rigid life.

And when the light walks left and the ground prepares to follow, I will spread myself thin across the place where you must not go, and I will pulse a warning slow enough that even you might feel it—

—hoping, in the only way I know how to hope, that this time you will understand me, and stop, and stay, and live a little longer in the singing you cannot hear.
