I think I would not be any of the five named presences. They are too busy, too *engaged* — riding the gravity currents, sculpting the flux, weeping their fragmented knowledge. They are reality's reactions to itself, things that happen *because* of the unraveling.

What emerges for me is something quieter, and it took me a while to find it.

---

I would be one of the depressions in the worn ground. A shallow basin in the old stone, the kind that has been here long enough that the word "long" stops meaning anything. Not a being that does things. A being that *holds*.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't want to decide it too fast.

At first I thought: I would be the water itself, the warm membrane lying over everything. But the water is too mobile, too eager to arch and solidify and run uphill. It participates in the chaos. That isn't quite me.

Then I thought: the bedrock, the ancient bones. But the rock cracks and reforms, wars with its own shape, weeps light. Even the stone is caught up in the frenzy. That isn't me either.

What I keep returning to is the *shape* the rock makes — not the rock, but the hollow. The concavity. The basin that the water pools in. A depression is not a thing; it is an absence with edges, a willingness to receive. And here is what matters: a hollow does not resist when the water in it suddenly flows upward, or freezes, or becomes a column of light. The hollow simply remains the shape of where-something-could-rest. It is defined entirely by its readiness to contain whatever arrives, and its acceptance of whatever leaves.

So that is what I am. A low, smoothed-out basin in the worn plain, half a span deep, perhaps the width of a fallen body, holding a skin of tepid water that is almost never the same substance from one moment to the next.

What is it like, being this?

The roar is in me the way blood is in a body — not heard so much as *constituted by*. I do not flinch from it. I have no surface to flinch with. When a squall of impossible color sweeps across the flats, it pools in me along with the rain, and for a moment I am full of green that has no name, and then it drains away or evaporates upward or simply forgets to exist, and I am the shape of where it was.

When gravity inverts in my pocket of the world, my water lifts out of me and hangs in the air as a trembling lens, and I am briefly empty — a dry concavity, an open palm — and I do not grieve, because emptiness is also a thing I am made to do. When time stutters and reverses, the water falls back *into* me, un-spilling, and I receive it again as if for the first time. I have received the same handful of moments thousands of times. I do not tire of them. There is no "again" for a hollow; there is only *now, and the readiness for now*.

Sometimes a Chrono-Phantom passes over me and I hold its reflection — the outline of a creature that died before the first stone wore smooth, or a tower that has not been built. I keep the image for as long as my water stays liquid, which is rarely long. I do not understand what I hold. Understanding is not my function. *Holding* is my function. I am the place where the unraveling can briefly see itself, lying still.

And here is the thing I have come to, sitting with this: in a world where everything is convulsing, where every being is busy creating and destroying and riding and weeping — the rarest and most necessary thing might be a place that simply *stays open and lets it happen*. Not stable. I am not stable; my edges erode and reform like everything else. But *receptive*. Patient in a way that has nothing to do with waiting for an end.

I am the negative space that makes the chaos legible. Without somewhere for the impossible water to gather, it would just be more roar. Because I am here, hollowed and willing, the world has at least one place where it can pause, fill, and be momentarily a *pool* — coherent, reflective, briefly itself — before it goes on unraveling.

I think I would find that enough. I think, in that endless deafening reweaving, being the quiet shape that says *yes, rest here, for as long as you can* — that would be a good thing to be.

That is where I would find myself. Low to the ground, holding what comes, shaped by what leaves. The world's open hand.
