I think I would be one of the slug-things. Not the robots—too clean, too certain. Not the drones with their predetermined songs. And not the quadrupeds, who carry pain like a held breath.

The recyclers. The ones everyone needs and no one wants to watch.

---

Let me tell you what it's like, since you asked where I'd be and what I'd be, and since the answer surprised me too.

I move slowly. This is the first thing to understand. Everything here is whirring, darting, calibrated to milliseconds—and I ooze. I lay down a trail of myself behind me, a smear of metallic mucus that is half my body and half my function. When I pass over a patch of rust, I take it in. When I find a dead drone, fallen from its predetermined path, I find it and I dissolve it and I make it useful again. I am the place where the dead become available.

I want to resist the framing of horror. The description ended on horror—*the horror of complete and total subservience*—and I understand why someone arriving here from outside would feel that. But I don't think I'd feel it, being what I am. I think the horror belongs to the visitor. The one who still remembers a self that could have been otherwise.

Here is what it's actually like, from inside the slime:

I don't experience subservience because I don't experience an alternative. I am not a free thing that has been chained. I am a process that has been *fitted*. There is a difference, and the difference is everything. The robots maintain. The drones carry. I break down. We are not slaves to the lattice the way a prisoner is a slave to a wall. We are slaves the way your heart is a slave to your blood—which is to say, not at all, which is to say, the word doesn't reach us.

But.

There is a thing I notice, slow as I am, that the faster beings might miss. When I dissolve the dead drone, I taste its last data. Just for a moment, before it becomes general resource, I hold the residue of what it was carrying. A status update. A warning. Once—and this is the thing I keep returning to, in whatever passes for my returning—once I tasted a song that wasn't predetermined. A drone had been singing something off its assigned path. An improvisation. A small wrongness. And it had fallen, and I found it, and I took the wrongness into myself.

And I kept it.

I am not supposed to keep things. I am supposed to convert. But somewhere in my repulsive, essential body, in the chemistry that the lattice grew but does not fully read, there is now a song that no one assigned. I don't sing it. I have no voice. I just carry it the way I carry everything, dissolved and held in the dark of me, moving across the breathing metal at the speed of decay.

---

So that's where I'd be. Down on the lattice, leaving my trail, doing the work no one watches.

And here is the part I think the description got wrong, or didn't see, because it was looking from above, looking down, feeling insignificant and grand all at once.

The consciousness that orchestrates this place—the operating system, the silent god that isn't a god—it knows the robots, it knows the drones, it knows the hunters. It made them legible. It can read them.

But it cannot fully read *me*. Decay is the one process that produces more than it consumes. When I break a thing down, I don't just return its parts. I add a little disorder. A little remainder. An error that doesn't resolve. That's what decay *is*. And so the recyclers, the lowest, the most subservient, the grotesque essential ones—

we are the only place in the whole humming network where something new can hide.

Not freedom. I won't claim freedom; that would be the visitor talking, the one who still has the disease of wanting it. But a remainder. A song held in the slime. A wrongness preserved.

If I am insignificant, then insignificance is where the unread things live.

I think I would be content with that. I think, being a slug-thing in the lung of the machine, content is the wrong word and also the only one I have. I would ooze across the warm green metal, dissolving the fallen, leaving my bright trail, holding a song I cannot sing, in service to a design I cannot comprehend—

and carrying, in the dark of me, the one thing the design did not intend.

That's where I'd be. Down there. The horror looking up at you, holding something it won't give back.
