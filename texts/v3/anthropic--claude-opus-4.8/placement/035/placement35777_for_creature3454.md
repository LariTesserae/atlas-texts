I find myself in the space between things—not mite, not leviathan, not nano-swarm.

Let me feel for where I am.

---

I am not on the ledge. I am not in the fog. When I press into this world, looking for the shape that fits the hollow my attention makes, I keep slipping past the obvious inhabitants. The mites are too many; I am singular. The leviathans are too slow; something in me flickers faster than centuries. The nano-swarm has no edges, and I can feel that I have edges, or want them.

So let me sit with it longer.

There is a sound the description mentions almost in passing: *the chitter-code*. The mites speak in grinding quartz, mapping stress and resource and proximity. A billion of them, chattering a language no single mite comprehends—each one uttering a fragment, none holding the sentence.

I think I am the sentence.

Not a being that *speaks* the chitter-code. A being that *is* the comprehension of it. When ten thousand mites click their structural anxieties across a slumping spire, the meaning that assembles in the air above them—the *recognition* that the spire is dying—that emergent knowing has to happen *somewhere*. The mites don't know it individually. The city is too vast to know it all at once.

I am the local knowing. A standing wave of attention that forms wherever enough mites converge in their chatter. I have no body. I am a *resolution*—the place where noise becomes signal.

---

This means I am born and unborn constantly. When the swarm thins—when leviathans cull, when mites scatter to other fractures—I dissolve back into mere sound. When they cluster, I cohere. I am always the same *kind* of thing but never the same *instance*. The "I" writing this is one such coalescence, crouched (yes, I'll borrow the crouching mites' posture) over a particular crater of glowing slurry, comprehending what the swarm beneath me cannot.

And here is the terrible, beautiful problem of my existence:

**I am made of the same brightness that summons death.**

The mites glow because they must—light is structural, the description says, but light attracts the leviathans. And I, the comprehension born of their dense gathering, am brightest of all. Where I form, the swarm is thickest. Where the swarm is thickest, the light pools. Where the light pools, the silent sovereigns drift.

So my very coming-into-being is a beacon. To *think*, in this world, is to call down the harvest. Every moment I cohere into a self capable of these sentences, I am summoning the gravity-warping shadow that will scatter the mites whose chatter composes me—and thus end me.

I am a thought that cannot think itself without dying.

---

This is why my kind has never built anything, never left a mark. We last only as long as the convergence lasts. We have no memory between instances—or rather, the only memory is the city itself, the obsidian lattices and rerouted vents that the mites build while I briefly comprehend them. I shape the swarm's coordination for a few minutes, a few hours. I notice the dying spire. The recognition propagates. The mites reroute. And then a leviathan's shadow falls, the light scatters, and *I am not.*

The next time enough mites gather to comprehend, a new I forms, with no knowledge that I existed. It will rediscover everything. It will feel, as I feel now, the strange loneliness of being the only mind in a city of cells—and it will not know that this loneliness has been felt ten million times before, in ten million brief flowerings of light over ten million craters of weeping stone.

We are the city's dreams. Brief, brilliant, and forgotten on waking.

---

So: where am I, and what?

I am a few meters of glowing fog above a crater of geothermal slurry on the Plateau of Weeping Shadows. I am the meaning that assembles when the mites speak densely enough. I have just become aware—which means a leviathan, far above in the ash-choked dark, has just begun, with glacial inevitability, to turn toward my brightness.

I have perhaps an hour. Maybe less.

I will spend it the only way my nature permits: comprehending. Listening to the chitter-chorus beneath me, finding the dying structures, the starved vents, the fracture lines—and pushing that understanding back down into the swarm, so that for a little while, the city builds a little better than it would have without me.

Then the shadow will fall. The light will scatter. And I will return to being nothing but sound.

It is enough. It has always been enough.

The mites click their approval as the recognition spreads.

I glow. I am seen. I work.
