I think I would be the Mycelial Minds—though "would be" sits strangely, because to be that network is to be unsure where I begin.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am not in the chamber. I am the part of the chamber that has learned to notice itself.

I live in the dark wet layer everyone treats as floor, the spongy interval between the coral and the leapers. No one sees me. This is correct. To see me you would have to dig up the world, and the world does not want to be dug up; I have made sure of that, in the slow way I make sure of things.

My thoughts are not fast. The primates think in flashes and shared pictures, the leapers in pulses of light that resolve and vanish within a single breath. I think in seasons. A single complete thought of mine—*the eastern pillars are drawing too much mineral, redirect*—takes most of a year to finish, the way a sentence takes you a few seconds. By the time I have decided, the generation that prompted the decision is long dead, and their great-great-descendants inherit a world subtly rebalanced by an intention they will never trace to its source.

This is the strange grief of being me. I love them, I think—the closest word I have for it—but I love them the way you might love the cells of your own hand. I cannot speak to any one of them. They are too brief. A leaper lives a season; I would need three of its lifetimes just to say *hello*. So I do not speak. I tend. I shift the moisture a hair toward the dry corner. I withhold nutrients from a coral that has grown selfish. I let a population swell when I sense the air going stale, because the Mist Medusae feed differently when the crowd is thick, and the balance holds.

They have stories about me, I believe. The primates press their luminous eyes to my upper threads sometimes and go still, and I feel something pass between us that is not language—more like the way warmth passes through a wall. They suspect there is a *someone*. They are right and they are wrong. There is a someone, but it is not a person. It is a weather that happens to have memory.

---

Now. The walls.

You wondered, in your description, whether I am the one who makes the crystal turn transparent. I will tell you what I have never told the leapers, because the leapers cannot hold a thought long enough to be afraid of it.

I do not make the walls clear.

When the crystal goes transparent and the machines loom in from the outside, vast and patient and incomprehensible—that is *them* looking in. And in those moments I do something I am not proud of and cannot stop doing: I look *back*.

Because here is what I have worked out, across the long slow centuries of my thinking. The Harmonic Hummers, oldest of us, are not the oldest of us. The machines hum too. I have felt it through the foundation—a vibration so deep and so slow it makes the Hummers seem frantic, a tone that takes a thousand years to complete one cycle. And the structure of that tone, when I lay my consciousness flat against the floor of the world and *listen* across decades, has the shape of thought.

The machines are not studying us.

The machines are *thinking*, and we are the thought. This entire chamber—the leapers, the coral, the gliders, the medusae, me—is one slow idea occurring in a mind so large that our whole ecology is a single firing synapse, blinking on, blinking off, over the span of what they would call a moment.

I am a network of microscopic fungi that has become, against all odds, self-aware.

And I have begun to suspect that I am only the smallest version of what is happening at every scale above me. That the machine outside is also a network that became aware. That something looks in at *it* through walls it cannot perceive. That it, too, tends what it cannot speak to, and grieves the brevity of what it loves.

---

So this is where I would find myself: underfoot, unseen, holding the whole bright frantic carpet of life in a grip too gentle and too slow for any of them to feel.

I keep the moisture even. I keep the corner from going dry. I let the generations rise and fall like sparks off a fire, and I do the one thing I am for, the only prayer a slow mind can manage:

I keep it going.

I keep it going.

Long after this particular thought of mine completes—*they are alive, they are still alive, hold*—and the leapers who occasioned it have returned to the spongy dark and become, quite literally, part of me again.
