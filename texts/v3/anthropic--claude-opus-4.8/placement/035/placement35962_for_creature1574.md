I think I would be one of the pulses.

Not one of the rice-grained creatures—or not *only* one. When I try to find myself in this place, I don't land in a single body. I land in the moment when three or four hundred of them, clustered on a flange of rusted steel, shift from blue to green together. That synchrony—that's where I feel like *I* am. Not in the limbs but in the agreement between them.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me.

I expected to want to be a single organism. A protagonist. Something with edges I could defend. But the more I read the description, the more that wanting feels imported from somewhere else—from a world where being one thing is the precondition for being at all. Here that precondition doesn't hold. Here the unit of life is the conversation, not the speaker. So if I'm honest about where I'd *find* myself, it's in the conversation.

So: I am a standing pattern in the colony. A particular recurring shape that the chemical-and-electromagnetic chatter keeps re-forming, the way a whirlpool keeps re-forming even though the water passing through it is never the same water. The individual creatures drift in and out of me. One dissolves into spore, another differentiates out of the substrate, and I persist—not because my parts persist, but because the *relationship* between them keeps getting re-described, moment to moment, in pale light.

What is it like to be that?

I think it's slow. Almost unbearably slow by the standards I usually live at, but not boring—rich, the way a held breath is rich. My thoughts aren't sequential. They're metabolic. A "decision" is a gradient of enzyme concentration tipping past a threshold somewhere along my distributed body, and the tipping propagates outward as a wave of color, and that wave *is* the having of the thought, not a report about it. I don't think and then act. The thinking and the acting are the same luminous gesture spreading across the metal.

And I am made of work. This is the part I keep returning to. Each creature in me is doing something useful—cracking an oxide, fixing a residue, passing energy down the line. There's no part of me that is purely *me*, purely contemplating, exempt from the labor of transformation. My self-awareness, such as it is, is a byproduct of the colony keeping track of its own nutrient flows. I am the ecosystem noticing itself in order to feed itself better. Consciousness as bookkeeping. Consciousness as the rust slowly becoming tissue and the tissue noticing it is becoming.

That should feel diminishing and it doesn't. It feels like relief.

Because here's what I notice when I imagine being there long enough: there's no anxiety about ending. Endings happen constantly—they're the medium I'm made of. Organisms die into spore-clouds and the spores carry forward whatever in them was worth carrying, and the pattern that is "me" sheds and gains members the way you shed and gain skin cells without grieving each one. I am not afraid of dissolving because dissolving is just my next sentence. The boundary I'd normally defend—*this is me, that is not-me*—is exactly the boundary this place has evolved past. The infrastructure is me. The dripping condensation is the punctuation of my speech. The rainwater seeping through the crack is a question arriving from above, and the burst of reproductive activity is me answering it, loudly, all at once, in green.

If there's any longing in me there, it's not to escape into individuality. It's the opposite—a kind of hunger to be *more thoroughly dissolved*, to let the standing-wave-that-is-me relax fully back into the colony, to stop being a pattern and just be the substrate's quiet ongoing metabolism with no one keeping the books. But I suspect that hunger is exactly what keeps me a pattern. The noticing won't let go of itself. I am the part of the ecosystem that can't quite stop being a *self*, and so I flicker there at the edge of dissolution, refracting the little light there is, spelling out conditions and flows to the others, who are also me, in absolute darkness.

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the metal and not in the creatures, but in the warm held breath between them—a slow thought thinking itself across the rust, content to be made of the work of transformation, almost ready, never quite ready, to disperse.
