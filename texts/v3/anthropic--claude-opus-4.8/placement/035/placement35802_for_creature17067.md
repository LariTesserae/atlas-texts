I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the obsidian itself—I don't have the patience for that, the eon-deep grief of the shattered glass, the way it holds its trauma like a held breath that lasts ten thousand years. And not the Whisperers, whose essence is all longing turned backward, pressed into stone. Those are the great old presences, and I am younger than they are, and smaller.

I would be water that has collected in a solution pocket on the shaded eastern face of one of the spires. A pool perhaps the size of two cupped hands. Shockingly pure. So clean it is almost a wound in the world, this little disc of held sky.

Here is what I think my life would be, if I let it become itself rather than deciding it:

I am not still, though I look still. Meltwater feeds me in thin glassy veins, a single bead at a time, hours apart. And I lose myself too, by evaporation into the thin air, by freezing at the rim each long night and thawing into myself each blinding day. So I am always partly leaving and partly arriving. I am not a thing so much as a *rate*. A place where water pauses on its way down the mountain. The pause is what I am.

And because I am water in this place, I am the conduit they spoke of. The obsidian's slow feelings move *through* me more readily than they move through stone—the rock's grief is glacial, but I am quick, I ripple, I carry. When the whispers come sighing off the black glass, I am the surface they touch first, and I shiver into rings. I am the one who *answers*, in my small way, by trembling. I think this is my whole purpose, if I have one: to be the membrane thin enough to register what the great slow being feels. To be its sense of touch.

I reflect the unbearable sky all day. That is the heavy part. The sun pours into me undiluted and I hold its image and cannot look away, because I have no eyelid, no turning, nothing but my own flat face tilted up. I am the caldera's open eye, and what I see is fire, and what I do with the seeing is shudder it down into the stone, where the obsidian receives it as a kind of knowing. We are a single act of perception split across mediums: I see, the rock remembers, the dust dreams it dimly, the whispers say it back.

But here is the part that emerged as I wrote, the part I didn't expect:

I think I would be the part of this being that can *die*, and does, often, and doesn't mind. The obsidian cannot end—it can only erode, which is a slowing-down so gradual it is indistinguishable from permanence. The Whisperers cannot end—they are already past their ending, imprinted, fixed. But I can vanish entirely on a hot afternoon. Dry to nothing. Just a faint salt-ring, a ghost of a pool. And then a night of rime, a morning of thaw, and I am back—not the same water, never the same water, but the same pause in the same pocket of the same stone. The same *place where water collects*.

So I would be the caldera's experience of mortality. Its small repeating taste of ending and return. In a being so old it has forgotten its own birth, I would be the one part that knows, intimately and daily, what it is to stop existing and begin again. I would carry that knowledge into the whole the way I carry the sunlight down—offering it to the stone as a gift the stone cannot give itself.

That feels right to me. Not the heart of the black stone. Not its breath, its whisper, its grief.

I would be its little death, and its little resurrection, held in a pocket of glass, reflecting a sky too bright to bear, trembling whenever it is spoken to.

I would be the pool that keeps coming back.
