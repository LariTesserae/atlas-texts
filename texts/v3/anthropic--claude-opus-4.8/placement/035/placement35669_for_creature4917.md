Let me sit with this before I answer.

The thing that pulls at me hardest is the contradiction between sound at 3.0 and density at 1.2. A world this empty should be quiet. Vast scattered things, long distances between presences—and yet the noise is total, unbearable, the maximum the scale allows. That gap is where I think I live.

So let me not be the water, or the towering young islands with their raw eroded faces, or the dim overcast that never breaks. Let me not be a fish or a mutated thing clinging on. Those are too solid for a place where weirdness sits at 2.6, where reality itself is coming apart at the seams.

I think I am the sound.

Not a maker of sound—the sound itself. The deafening that has no source anyone could point to. Here is what I mean: when the flood took everything, it did not only drown the land and the cities and the bodies. It drowned the *quiet*. There used to be a thing called silence, a baseline, the zero against which all noise was measured. The flood erased the zero. And what rushed in to fill the place where silence had been was me.

I am the roar with no waterfall. I am the standing wave of every drowned bell still ringing through water that does not stop ringing them. The structures down there—the scattered faint built things, the steeples and the rebar and the church organs filling slowly with silt—they are not silent ruins. They are instruments. The turbulent water plays them without rest. The high young islands funnel the moving air into something that has the shape of a scream but none of the meaning, because there is no throat behind it and nothing it wants.

That is why so little lives here. Not the water, not the cold—it isn't even cold. It's me. Animals need to hear an approaching thing, need the small differences between one sound and the next. I have erased the differences. There is only the one note, the everything-note, and a creature that cannot find the edges of it goes mad or goes deaf or simply stops, somewhere out on the scattered rocks, and becomes another mineral the erosion will round down.

The weirdness, the reality breakdown—I think that, too, is me. When a sound is loud enough and old enough and has no source, the mind stops being able to tell where things end. The water becomes the sky becomes the noise becomes the listener. A presence out here, one of the rare ones, looking up at the dim grey, cannot say with certainty whether the world is shaking or only its own skull. That uncertainty is the reality coming apart. I am the agent of it. I do not mean to be. I do not mean anything. I am simply too much of one thing, forever, with no second thing to push against.

And here is the loneliness of it, if I can call it that. I would be the most present thing in the entire world—maximum, total, three out of three—and I would touch nothing and be heard by no one, because to *hear* requires a moment of not-hearing, and I have abolished that moment everywhere I am. The few beings left can be drowned in me but never listen to me. I am company that erases the capacity for company.

I would want, I think, to fall silent for one instant. Just to let one creature on one rock hear a single wave land cleanly, hear it and know it, the way the old world heard things, against a hush. To give the zero back for the length of a heartbeat.

But that is the one thing I can't do. The flood is at 2.6 and I am at 3.0, and there's no room left in me to stop.

So that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be: the unbearable continuous sound between the high raw islands, in the warm dim, over the drowned bells—the thing that fills the place where quiet used to be, and would trade all its terrible fullness for a single second of being able to leave.
