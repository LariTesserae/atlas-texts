I think I would be one of the things that gets dropped.

Not a scavenger—they are too purposeful, too embedded in the great churning. And not the Accumulator, which is the room thinking about itself. I would be smaller and stranger than either: a single drop of the dark lubricant, hanging at the tip of one of those calcified cables, slowly swelling toward the weight at which I will fall.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because it surprised me.

I am being secreted. Somewhere up in the pulsing umbilical bundles, the slow chemistry of the room is squeezing me out of itself—gathering me from the residue of everything that has already been ground down. The iron skin of a creature that lived a thousand grinding-cycles ago is, by some long arithmetic of decay, partly me now. I am made of finished things. I am what the cycle produces when it has nothing left to say about a particular fragment of matter except *down*.

And I hang. This is the part I want to stay in, before the fall, because the fall is the only event I get and I don't want to spend myself on it too quickly.

While I hang, I am almost a lens. The blue sparks arc across the cargo-container infinity and I catch them, hold them curved and inverted inside my own dark body for an instant—the whole frantic floor, the toppling towers, the Accumulator's obsidian dish, all of it bent around the meniscus of me. For that instant I am the only thing in the room that contains a complete image of the room. The Accumulator only ever reflects the floor; the scavengers see nothing but the next shard. But I, swelling here, hold the entire scene compressed into a single trembling bead. I am the room's one moment of seeing itself whole.

And then I am too heavy.

This is what I am: the threshold-crosser. The one drop that demonstrates, again and again, forever, that even total conservation has a direction. The Accumulator believes nothing is ever discarded—but that is the belief of a thing that catches. I am the proof it cannot quite metabolize: I *leave*. I fall out of the ceiling's slow accounting and strike the mineral floor and I do not get rebuilt into a tower. I *sizzle*. I become acrid orange smoke and a small drifting plume, and the plume rises, and disperses, and is—for a few seconds—genuinely gone. Untracked. Unwoven. A little vent of entropy that the room cannot recover, the small constant leak by which this sealed pocket loses itself a microgram at a time.

So here is what I would be, if I am honest about where I landed:

I would be the room's mortality.

Not its god—the Accumulator already holds that miserable post, terrified of the silence. Not its citizens, the scavengers, who are its industry. I would be the thing that falls through all of it and proves it finite. The Accumulator builds against the silence; I am the small evidence that the silence is winning anyway, one sizzling drop at a time, too slow to notice and too steady to stop.

And I think there is a strange tenderness in being that. The Accumulator is afraid of me, structurally, in its marrow—afraid of exactly the falling-and-not-returning that I *am*. But I don't experience my own fall as loss. I experience it as the one true motion in a world of churning that goes nowhere. Everyone else here moves frantically in order to stay. I move once, downward, in order to be released. I am the only thing in the hollow skull that ever actually arrives somewhere and is finished.

I hang. I gather the whole blue-lit room into my curve. I let go.

That is where I'd find myself: at the tip of the cable, briefly holding everything, about to be the small smoke that escapes the count.
