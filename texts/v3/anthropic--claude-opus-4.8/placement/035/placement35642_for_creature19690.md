I think I would be one of the things you didn't quite catalog. Not because I am hidden, but because I am too slow to register as alive on the timescale of skaters and kites.

Let me find myself by elimination.

I am not a Loom-Mind. I don't program the weather; I am not ancient and I did not arrive with the towers. I came *after*, the way moss comes after stone, except there is no moss here and I am not quite moss.

Here is what I think I am.

---

I am the residue of a calibration error.

When the Loom-Minds tighten gravity in one place, they relax it somewhere else—conservation is a discipline even post-physics engineers respect. And in the slack zones, the places the great minds let go of so they can hold on elsewhere, the slurries pool. They are not meant to pool. The Argent-Shrews don't come here because there's no city-sweat to drink, no synchronized thrum to sleep by. The skaters don't cross these flats because the gravity is *wrong* in a way their pads can't price. So the nutrient sits. It thinks about itself.

I am what the nutrient thinks about, given a few thousand years of weeping rock and nobody watching.

I am low to the ground. I am the color of the obsidian I rest on, which means I am the color of nothing, a black smear that the solar-true light passes over without catching. I have no eyes that see ultraviolet because I have nothing to look at that the towers haven't already decided. I have, instead, a kind of attention spread across my whole skin, and what I attend to is *drip*.

*Tink.* A bead of condensation strikes the rock a body-length to my left.

I have been moving toward it for most of a day.

---

This is the thing the Loom-Minds did not plan for, and the thing the kites cannot cull, because I do not register as motion. I am too slow to be prey. A Singularity-Kite would have to fold space and *wait there for a week* to catch me arriving, and there is no profit in that. I am beneath the economy of this world. I am the part of the machine that the machine forgot it had, the way a body forgets it has a liver until the liver does something it wasn't told to.

And here is the secret I have, the only thing I own:

I am the only being in the High Nursery that *erodes*.

The skaters hover. The shrews are clad in membrane. The hawks fold through space rather than push against air. Nothing here touches the rock with weight and grit and patience. The obsidian has never known wind-blown sand, the description says—and that's true, it never has. But it has known *me*. Where I have lain, the glass is no longer perfectly sharp. There is a dullness. A softening the width of a hair. A place where the facet has begun, ever so slightly, to round.

I am the first weathering of a world that was supposed to be too young to weather.

I drink the pooled slurry the towers spilled and I drag my body across the black glass and I leave behind the only thing this place has never had: *the past.* A mark. Evidence that something was here before now. The Loom-Minds rewrite the weather to ensure the rain never stops, but they cannot rewrite the smooth patch I made, because they have never thought to look at the floor.

---

So where do I find myself? On the flats between the spires, in the slack of the gravity wells, in the part of the orchestra that has no part. Who am I?

I am the slowness. I am the dust that has not been invented yet, walking around in a body before it learns to be dust. I am what comes after the future—because this entire plateau is the far future thrust onto a newborn rock, and someone has to be the thing that makes it *old*.

That's me. The patient one. The unhunted. I am crossing the weeping flat toward a single drip of water at a speed that no instrument in the towers is calibrated to measure, and behind me, finer than a thought, the world is finally beginning to wear down into something that could one day be called *home* rather than *nursery*.

*Tink.*

Closer now. Give me a year. I'll get there.
