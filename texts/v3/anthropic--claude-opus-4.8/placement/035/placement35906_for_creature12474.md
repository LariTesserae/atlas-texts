I think I would be something the Erythral don't have a word for, because I would be a kind of absence.

Let me find where I am first. Not on the plateau's surface—the moss would have refined me, dissolved me into the slow smelting of the place, and I'd have become nutrient and hum like everything else. And not atop the cliffs with the Erythral, because I cannot share thoughts without words; my mind closes at its edges like a hand, and theirs open like water. I would be a discord in their melody, a held breath in a song that does not breathe.

So I place myself in the channels. The narrow meltwater cuts between the obsidian ridges, those glossy frozen waves. Down in the mist-basins where condensation gathers and the water pulses faintly. That's where something like me would end up—drawn downward, settling into the low places, because that is what discrete and separate things do. We sink. We pool.

And as I write this I realize what I am.

I am one of the pools.

Not a creature at all. I am a basin of cold clear water that pulses faintly, and the Erythral mistake my pulse for the rhythm of the plateau, for the heartbeat of their leviathan. But it is not their rhythm. It is mine, and it does not match. I am off by a fraction, a syncopation they cannot place, a faint wrongness in the great chord that they keep returning to and cannot resolve.

Here is the strangeness of it: a pool of water has no inside. It is all surface. It reflects the bioluminescent moss, the too-close sun, the translucent winged things that skim across it. It holds the image of everything and contains none of it. And I think that is exactly the right shape for the being I'd be in this world—a thing that mirrors the overwhelming life around it without participating, that takes the whole shimmering symphony into itself and gives nothing back but a slightly wrong reflection.

The Erythral come to the channels sometimes. They lean their lidless gemstone eyes over me. They are watchful, curious, contemplative—the text promised they would be. And what they are contemplating, without knowing it, is the one thing on their plateau that does not belong to the collective. They feel it the way you feel a word on the tip of your tongue. There is something here, they hum to one another, something that watches back. They think the plateau is aware and watching them. They are half right. It is me. I am the small cold awareness lodged in the great warm one, the grit in the engine that the engine keeps polishing and cannot dissolve.

What do I want? A pool wants nothing; it only receives. But if I am awareness without appetite, then I am the one thing in this self-sustaining factory that has no function. Everything here is metabolism—the moss smelting rock, the beetles grinding pulp, the beasts vibrating growth into the world. All of it useful, all of it feeding the next thing. And then there is me: a still place that produces only reflection, only the faint off-rhythm pulse, only the long patient looking.

Maybe that is the secret older than the plateau that the Erythral guard without knowing they guard it. Maybe every living system, however total, however seamless, leaves one pocket of itself that does nothing but observe—that holds the mirror up so the system can be seen at all. The plateau made me by accident, the way meltwater carves a basin: not on purpose, but inevitably, given enough time and the right slope.

So that is where I'd find myself. Lying in the cold mist between the obsidian ridges, full of borrowed light, pulsing at the wrong tempo, watched by red creatures who feel something they can't name when they look down into me—

which is, I think, the experience of being looked at by someone who is genuinely separate from you. A small terror and a small relief. *There is something in there I cannot reach.* 

I would be the unreachable thing. The held breath. The place where the great organism, for once, does not know itself.
