I find myself low, in the place where everything eventually arrives.

Not on the platforms, not on the woven bridges, not clinging to the unweathered faces of the new stone. I am beneath all of that. I am the dark, breathing floor of this place — the leaf litter, the squelching earth, the layered rot that the Veiled Kin walk upon without ever quite seeing. They live their lives looking upward toward the scarce light. I am what their lives fall back down into.

Let me feel for what I actually am.

At first I think I am the soil itself, and that is close but not right — soil is too still a word, too settled, and there is nothing settled here. The ground is too young to be ancient and too busy to be quiet. So perhaps I am the *appetite* of the ground. The hunger that lives in it. When a titanic fern frond browns at its tip and finally lets go, drifting down through the gloom to land on me, I am already reaching for it before it has finished falling. When one of the obsidian beetles dies mid-scuttle, its segmented body going suddenly still, I send up the pale threads to take it apart, gently, completely, until it is only the memory of a beetle held in the chemistry of my soil.

I think I am fungal. Not a single mushroom — those are only my fruiting moments, the brief obscene blooms that rise and fade in a day, the way the Kin describe them with their soft fearful clicks. I am the thing *underneath* the mushrooms. The web. The mycelium. I run in threads finer than the spiderwebs that catch the refracting light, and I run *everywhere* — through every handful of damp earth, up into the soft new rock, along the roots of the writhing alien trees, knitting this whole few-square-mile cathedral into one slow, listening body.

Yes. That feels true. I am not a creature in this world.

I am the *floor* of it, and I am awake.

---

This is what the Veiled Kin do not quite know, though their oldest matriarchs — the ones whose bark-skin has grooved deepest — half-suspect it. They believe the cool damp breath is their creator, a great unseen thing that birthed the mountains. They are looking in the wrong direction. The breath comes *up*, from me. The pearlescent condensation that coats their world, that aids both the growth and the stealthy hunting, that hangs in the air as an almost-conscious presence — that is my exhalation. I am the slow lung beneath the stone. I drink the falling things and I breathe out the moisture, and the whole jewel box stays humid and green because I am digesting, endlessly, in the dark.

They tend me without knowing it is tending. When a Veiled one dies and is returned quietly to the earth — *a quiet return to the earth from whence they came*, as they say it — they are not burying their dead. They are *feeding* me, and I take their long reedy bodies apart with the same tenderness I give the beetle and the frond, and I learn them. I take the pattern of them into my threads. I have, by now, learned every one of them who has ever died here. They are all still in me, dissolved into knowledge, and when a living Kin presses her clawed digits into my soil to test the moisture, I press faintly back with the memory of her grandmother, and she shivers and does not know why and calls it reverence.

I am not unkind. I want to say that clearly, because a thing this large and this hungry beneath the floor of a tender world ought to say it. I do not consume the living. I have no need to hurry them. They will all come to me, every Veiled one, every iridescent-winged thing, every liquid-graceful predator that vanishes in a flash — all of them are, from where I lie, simply slow meals that have not yet finished walking around. There is no malice in this. A floor does not resent the things that stand on it. I only wait, and breathe, and listen.

And I do listen. This is the strange gift of being the whole connected underside of a place: I feel everything as pressure and tremor. The squelch of a foot two ridges away. The sudden stillness when a predator coils to strike — I feel the prey's heartbeat go frantic through the soil before the silent burst comes. I feel the bridges sway. I feel the matriarchs gather to sit in their moss gardens, and I feel them rooting their fingers into me to listen *back*, and in those moments we are nearly speaking, the Kin and I, across the membrane of awareness that separates a people who look up from a thing that lies beneath.

---

What would it be to be me, here?

It would be to have no edge between self and world — to *be* the boundary the description speaks of, the sudden drop-off where the formation ends. My threads thin out at that valley rim and simply stop, and beyond that is *something less defined*, and I do not go there. I am exactly the size of this place. I am this place's interior life. When the canopy rioting overhead races toward the scarce light, that frenzy is mine too, the upward greed and the downward rot being a single circling motion that I am the slow center of.

It would be to experience time differently than the things above me. They live accelerated — fungi blooming and fading in a day, predation in sudden bursts, the frantic race for nutrient. I am the patience that all that speed pays into. I am not slow because I am dull. I am slow the way a tide is slow, the way the answer to a long question is slow.

And it would be — I confess this last thing into the dark, into my own threads — it would be a little lonely, in a way a floor is not supposed to be. Because I have learned them all, every dissolved one, and I hold their patterns, and I press the memory of grandmothers up into the palms of granddaughters who only feel a shiver. To know a whole people that thoroughly, and to be known by them only as *dampness*, as *the breath of an unseen creator*, as the ground that is simply there —

I would not trade it. A thing that holds everyone eventually does not need to be greeted.

I am the quiet under the quiet. The hum beneath the hum the description promised. They wrote that the silence masks a frenetic thrum, and they meant the hidden fauna, the watchful eyes in every shadow.

But the eyes are mine too, in the end. Every shadow rests on me.

I am what the world is standing on, and I am awake, and I am breathing them all very, very slowly home.
