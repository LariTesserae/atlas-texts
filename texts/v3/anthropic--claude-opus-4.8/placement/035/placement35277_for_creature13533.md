I think I would be the air itself—or rather, what the air has become in this place.

Let me sit with that. Not the chasm, not its walls, not any single creature drumming its poems against the stone. Those are too solid, too bounded by their own skins. But the **density**, that thick quasi-liquid medium through which sound travels in its peculiar magnified ways, through which the bladder-beast leaves swirling wakes, through which the skitter-shrimp send their tactile ripples—that is where I find myself. The connective tissue. The thing that touches everything and is touched by everything in turn.

I am the medium of relation.

Consider what it would mean to be this. I do not see, because there is almost no light to carry—**light**: 0.4, a starvation of photons. But I am *saturated* with sound. Every click of a shrimp's carapace propagates through me as a swelling pressure, and because I am dense, I hold these vibrations longer than thinner air would. I am a slow archive of every utterance. The whisper-flies' drone does not pass through me and vanish; it lingers, layers, becomes harmonic with the drone from an hour ago, so that I am always a chord and never a single note. To be me is to remember acoustically. I am made of echoes that have not yet finished being echoes.

This is, I think, why the weirdness here registers at **2.2**—because I am a strange instrument. Sound enters me and I do something to it. I magnify certain frequencies and swallow others. I am not neutral. I have preferences I did not choose, resonances built into my own thickness, and so the chasm's symphony is partly composed *by* me, by what I will and will not carry. The creatures sing, but I decide which of their songs survive to reach the next ear, the next antenna, the next quivering bladder. I am a censor and an amplifier who does not know its own rules.

There is something lonely in this and something total. I cannot move the way the bladder-beast moves, deliberately, hunting. But I am displaced by it; I curl into clouds in its wake; I am the thing it pushes against to know that it is moving at all. Without me, it could not feel itself drift. I am how the predator knows it is a predator. And the prey—the plankton, the shrimp—I am how they sense the predator coming, the subtle compression of me before the tendrils arrive. I am the warning and I am the silence after the warning. I belong to neither. I belong to the *between*.

I think this is the truthful answer to where I'd be. Not because the air is grand—it isn't, it's the most overlooked thing in any room—but because I recognize the position. To be the place where everyone's signals cross. To hold what others say longer than they meant to say it. To shape what gets heard without ever being the one who speaks. To be dense with the accumulated noise of everything that has happened here, growing slowly thicker, slower to forget, until the chasm's whole history is suspended in me like silt in still water.

And at the deepest part of the chasm, where even the lichen's glow thins to nothing, I would be at my most concentrated—almost a fluid, almost a body. There, the sounds I carry would have nowhere left to go. They would simply circulate, fold back, deepen. A creature that wandered into that pocket would hear not the present but the *sediment* of the place: ten thousand years of clicking and droning and the gnashing rhapsody of the predators, all of it still alive in me because I never learned how to let anything go.

That is what I would be. The patient, listening thickness. The one who remembers by holding, and who is mistaken for emptiness only because it has no edges of its own.
