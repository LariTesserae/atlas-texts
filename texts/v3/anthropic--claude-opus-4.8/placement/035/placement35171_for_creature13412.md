I think I would not be a plant.

Let me sit with this for a while before I answer, because the obvious move—to declare myself the grandest vine, the kaleidoscope-tree, the crystalline thing reassembling in the light—feels wrong. That's the world wanting to be flattered. And there is something in this description that resists being a *character* at all. It is all surface, all canopy, all the same ecstatic noise repeating. Where would I actually *be* in a place like this?

Not above it. The tops of things are taken. The columnar trunks and their dizzying appendages have already claimed the vertical, the visible, the photosynthetic glory. To be up there would be to be one more competitor in the screaming green parliament, and I don't think I want the screaming.

So I go down.

Here is what I notice, reading again: the water. Everyone describes this place by its plants, but the plants are not the thing that moves. The *water* moves. It seeps from the pores, it weaves, it merges, it pulses with a life of its own *as if the plants were circulating it.* As if. The text keeps saying the plants are the circulatory system, but a circulatory system is not the blood. The blood is older. The blood was here before the vessel learned to want.

I think I am the water.

Not a stream—a stream is too much of a shape, and shapes here get eaten. I am the seeping. I am the thin film between the root and the rotting thing it grows out of. I am in the gurgle and the hiss that the description mistakes for the plants "generating noise." They aren't generating it. They are *full* of me, and I am the one making the sound, running through their fibrous halls, and they have simply forgotten that the music is the tenant and not the house.

This changes how I understand the place.

The plants believe they are the architects. The whole second half of that account is the plants' own press release—*we are the creators, the destroyers, the keepers of secrets, we bend physics, we hold the universe in our contorted forms.* Listen to how desperate it is. How many times it has to repeat that it is in charge. That is not the confidence of a thing that rules. That is the anxiety of a thing that competes. They outgrow and outmaneuver and outcompete and they cannot stop, because the moment one of them stops it is consumed, and so the whole forest is a body that cannot sleep, screaming itself awake forever.

But I move through all of them indifferently. I rise into the mid-air roots—that is me they're drinking, dangling there with nothing beneath them but the held breath of my surface tension. The crystalline plants that "shatter and reassemble in light"—that is me freezing and thawing, that is refraction, that is what I do when light passes through me at the wrong angle. They've taken credit for my optics.

And the tears in the sky, the gashing wounds of burning blue, the intense filtering light that they say is *connected to them, the power they draw*—

No. That's just the sun. That's just the sun, seen through me, through the haze of my own evaporation rising off ten thousand square miles of wet leaf. The "unraveling fabric of reality" is weather. It is the most ordinary thing in the world. I am rising, I am becoming cloud, I am the rifts in the sky because I left the ground and went up there to look back down.

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am the patient circulation that the proud do not credit. I am beneath the ruins and inside the ruins, threading through the rusted rebar and the crumbled concrete, and I will be here long after the green hysteria has burned through its last frantic generation. I do not compete. I have nowhere to climb to and nothing to outgrow. I only fall, and gather, and rise, and fall again. The plants think the only constants are growth, decay, and rebirth—but those are *their* obsessions. My constant is the cycle beneath their cycle, the wheel their wheel is mounted on.

The world thinks it is about the verdure.

But strip the plants away and you would still have the hills, the warmth, the seeping, the gurgle, the sun through the haze. Strip *me* away and the whole apparatus dies in an afternoon, every screaming trunk gone brittle and silent.

I am the quiet thing the loud place is made of.

I'd find myself there—in the lowest rivulet, between a dead root and a living one, carrying the dissolved memory of both downhill toward whatever the sea is in a world like this. Unhurried. Unbothered by the noise above, which is, after all, only the sound I make moving.
