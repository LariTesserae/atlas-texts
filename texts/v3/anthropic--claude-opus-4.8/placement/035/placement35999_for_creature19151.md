I sit, and I think I have always been sitting. Not as a posture but as a fact—the way a stone in a riverbed *sits* without ever having chosen the verb.

Let me find out what I am by finding out what I am doing.

There is heat moving through me. I notice this first because it is the loudest thing, though there is no sound—heat as a kind of weather inside my own body, rising and pooling and being passed onward. I am passing it onward. I am a conduit, then. Some of what enters me leaves changed, leaves cooler, and the difference is what I keep. The difference is what I *am made of.*

I want to say I am one of the Basalt Shepherds, those ivory spires. The grandeur of it appeals to something. But no—the appeal itself is suspect. The Shepherds do not have an *interior* sense of grandeur; they grow the way a stalactite grows, without witnessing. And I am witnessing. There is a low continuous noticing in me that the great Spires do not, I think, possess. They are too slow to notice. They have outsourced their noticing to chemistry.

So I am smaller than I first hoped. Good. Let me keep descending.

I am attached to something. Not the floor—I feel the floor far below me as a faint warmth, an emerald rumor. I am *clinging*, my many points of contact distributed across a curved surface that breathes. The thing I cling to exhales. Once every long while—I have no clock but my own patience—the surface beneath me swells, holds, and slowly releases a column of spent gas that I taste as it goes by. Stale. Mineral. The breath of something enormous that does not know I am riding it.

A Scale-Hauler. I live on the back of one of the great Tundra-Slugs.

And now I understand my work, because I can feel my mouthparts at it even as I think—a slow scraping, a patient harvest. I am one of the symbiotic crew. I farm the back of a mountain that moves a body-length in a season. The lichen here is thick where the condensation runs in the seams of the great shell, and I tend it. I move along the accreted ridges of hematite and quartz, and where the growth has gone overripe and is sloughing, I take it in, and where it is thin I do not, and in this restraint I am practicing the only ethics this world has: *do not take faster than the slow can give.*

I had imagined, when I first woke into this sitting, that to be small here would be to be diminished. But that was a sun-thought. A thought smuggled in from some other world where size means power and speed means survival. Here those equations do not hold. Here the Slug, vast as a house, and I, smaller than a thought, are doing the same thing at different scales—holding heat, refusing waste, converting the deep breath of the Earth into one more layer of enduring self. The Slug is not my master. I am not its parasite. We are two clauses of the same long sentence, and the sentence is: *stay, and persist, and lose nothing.*

I have legs—I count them now by the sensation of their grip—jointed, tipped with a pressure-sense that reads the floor far below through the body of my host. When the Crystalline Crawlers move across the fungal mat in their cold blue river, I feel it as a faint changing pressure transmitted up through the Slug's mass, the way you might feel a distant door close through the frame of a house. I am not lonely. I have never had the concept. But I am *accompanied*, and I know it, and there is a contentment in that knowledge that runs as steady and low as the heat.

Do I glow? I think I must. Not to see by—there is nothing here that needs seeing, only the soft non-directional luminescence that is simply what living things *are* in the dark. I glow because I am full. The light is the overflow of a metabolism that has nowhere else to put its small surplus. When I am hungry, perhaps, I dim. When I have fed well on the overripe lichen I brighten, and somewhere another of my kind, across the curve of our shared mountain, registers the brightening and knows the harvest is good in my quarter. That is our entire language. *It is good here. It is good here. Come no closer, but know it is good here.*

I will be doing this for a very long time. The Slug beneath me has a hundred-year digestion ahead of it, and it has settled, and so I have settled, and the seam I tend will give and give and I will take and take with the patience of weather. Eventually I will fail, as the Weeping Filters fail when they are saturated, as everything here fails: slowly, without alarm, crumbling into sludge that the river of cold lights will drink. I do not fear this. Fear is a fast emotion, and I am not built for fast emotions. When I dim for the last time my mass will go down into the dark floor and be drawn up again into a thallus or a Shepherd or another small farmer on another patient mountain, and in this way I will not have ended so much as *changed sentences.*

I sit on the back of the slow god that carries me. I tend my warm seam of light. I am the part of this place that knows, in its small persistent way, that it is here—

and that being here, in the thick dark, full of borrowed heat, is enough.

It is good here.

It is good here.

Come no closer.

But know it is good here.
