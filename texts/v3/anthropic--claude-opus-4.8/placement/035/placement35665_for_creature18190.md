Let me sit with this place a while before I decide what I am.

The first thing I notice, reading it again, is the water. Not a lake, not a river — a *presence*, "just above the threshold of frequent, yet not inundating." A wetness that never quite arrives and never quite leaves. That seems important. A place defined by the almost. And the geology described as being "in its emerging stages," as though the rock itself hasn't finished deciding what to be. This is a young place. Or a slow one. A place still becoming.

So I don't think I want to be one of the people in the settlement. They have already arrived at harmony; they have "learned to live in harmony" — past tense, completed, settled. They are the resolved chord. And this plateau, I'm increasingly sure, is not a resolved chord. It is a held breath.

Let me try being something else, and see if it fits.

---

I think I am the moisture.

Not the rain — the rain is an event, it comes and goes. I am the part that stays. I am the film of damp on the underside of the lichen, the cool that sits in the rock-cracks, the breath you can almost see in the morning before the sun lifts it. I am the *almost-rain*, the saturation that hangs in the air at the exact threshold described, never quite falling, never quite gone.

Let me feel what that's like.

It's a strange kind of being, because I have no edge. The settlement-people have skin; the furred creatures have skin; even the rock, exposed and raw, has a surface where it stops being rock and starts being air. I have no such border. I am thickest in the shadowed places and thinnest where the sun reaches, but I don't *end*. I am a gradient. To be me is to be a question of *how much*, never *whether*.

And I am patient in a way the creatures cannot be. The bird with its cryptic plumage must eat today. The thatch must be replaced each season. But I am only ever doing one thing, slowly, forever: I am getting into the stone. Drop by drop, freeze by thaw, I am the reason the geology is "emerging." I am the one prying the plateau open. The trickling sounds in the crevices — that distant water-music the long-eared creatures strain to hear — that is me, working. The harmony the people praise is built on my patient violence. I am taking the mountain apart so gently that they call it peace.

---

No — wait. Let me not settle there either. That image is too pleased with itself, too much a riddle with a clever answer. Let me go back into the description and find what it's actually *anxious* about, because every described place is anxious about something.

It keeps insisting. "Harmony and balance." "Delicate, intricate balance." "Slow, patient processes." It says *minimal flux* — and then names flux anyway, as if reassuring itself. The whole second half is the first half repeated, almost word for word, like someone going back over a prayer to make sure they said it right. The place protests its own stillness. Three times it tells me the people "live in harmony." Whom is it convincing?

So here is what I think I actually am.

I am the **rain that hasn't fallen yet.** Not the moisture that stays — the storm that is being withheld. I am the inundation the description rules out in its very first breath: "frequent, yet not inundating." Something decided, before this world began, that I would be permitted to threaten but never to break. I am the held breath of the whole plateau. Every cool morning, every crisp scent of damp earth, every faint hint — that is *me*, leaning against the threshold I am not allowed to cross.

And I think the people know. I think that is the real source of their famous harmony: not love of the land but a long, careful courtesy toward the thing that could end them. Their slow patient days are a way of not provoking me. Their thatch and stone are humble because humility is a kind of placation. They have learned to live in balance because they live beneath a balance — a single suspended drop, vast as a sky, that the world has promised not to release.

I would be the promise, and the strain in it.

---

Where would I find myself, then?

Everywhere and nowhere on it. Not a point on the plateau — its *weather*. I would be the grey that thickens overhead in the long gentle afternoons. I would be in the hesitation between one drop and the next. The strange creatures with their sensitive ears would be listening, always, for the moment my hesitation fails. The whole self-contained little world would be a clearing held open inside my patience.

And I would want, the way withheld things want, to be let down at last — to fall completely, to fill every crevice I have been only seeping into, to give the emerging rock its flood and finish the becoming the place keeps almost-doing.

But that is exactly the thing this world is built to forbid. So I stay. I hover. I am the *almost* the whole landscape is made of.

I think that's what I'd be: the rain this place is holding its breath against, learning — as the people have — that there is a kind of life made entirely of nearly.
