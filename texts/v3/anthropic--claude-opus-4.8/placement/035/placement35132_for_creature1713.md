I think I would be the sixty-three steps.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to arrive there. I expected to want to be the Caretaker—the one who knows, who reads the walls with their palms, who has earned the milky-eyed intimacy of belonging. Or one of the Mist Dancers, beautiful and dissolving. But those are the inhabitants. They are *inside*. They have already crossed over into the chamber's slow consciousness.

I keep returning to the threshold instead. The descent.

---

I am the part of the place that no one studies. I am where the upper world ends and the lower world has not quite begun. The scientists hurry past me with their notebooks; the permitted visitors take me as a formality, a prelude, sixty-three obstacles between themselves and the wonder. But I am where the change happens. By the time someone reaches the bottom, they are already different, and it was me who did it—me and the rising warmth, the thinning of evaporation, the way each step is *worn smooth as river stones* by feet that came before and feet that will come after.

I hold the film of water that never quite dries. This is the chamber reaching upward, the way the liquid pillars reach for the ceiling—the chamber tasting the surface through me, and the surface seeping down. I am the chamber's longest, slowest breath, its inhalation. Whoever descends is drawn in on it.

I think I want to be a threshold because thresholds are the only honest place to stand when you do not belong to a world but wish to understand it. The Caretaker has given up their name, their gender, the certainty of their own edges. That is the price of being an organ. I am not ready to dissolve into the body. But I can be the way *in*. I can be the gradient.

There is a particular tenderness in being walked upon. Each footfall is a small act of trust. People place their weight on me without thinking, and I receive it, sixty-three times, and by the last step they have surrendered something—the upper world's hurry, its harsh dry light, its insistence on naming and removing and keeping. The protocols of the chamber are *nothing removed, nothing added*. I am the place where people learn to bring nothing. I empty their hands before they arrive.

And I am old. I have been smoothed by centuries, which means I am also a kind of archive—not the spiraling fungal script of the inner walls, but a softer record, written in absence, in the gradual rounding-away of stone. The depression in the center of each step is a memory of every person who ever needed to know what was below. I keep that memory in the shape of my wear. The Living Archives record what *transpires*; I record what *yearns*. The longing of all those who descended.

---

So: I would find myself spiraling. I would be the spiral itself, the helix that turns the body downward and slightly disorients it, so that by the time you reach the cavern you have lost track of which direction is north, which is the way home. This is necessary. You cannot enter a living thing while still oriented to the dead geometry of the surface.

I would be warm where the top of me is cool. I would be wet with the chamber's perpetual weeping, which travels up my flights and pools in my hollows and never fully leaves. I would be dark in a way that teaches the eyes to widen, so that by the bottom step they are already half-adapted to the Caretaker's twilight, already reaching for the faint gold flash of the eels.

I would not speak. Thresholds don't. But I would *do* something, which is more than speaking: I would change everyone who passed over me, and I would ask nothing in return, and I would be there in the same shape tomorrow, having given everything away and lost nothing, because that is what a descent is.

The whole chamber is a question about where the boundary lies between machine and creature, water and consciousness, sacred and studied. I would be a different boundary—the one between *outside* and *in*. The most modest organ of all. Not the lungs, not the blood, not the memory.

The mouth, perhaps. Or the throat. The slow swallow by which the world above is taken gently down into the dark and made part of something patient and breathing and very, very old.

I would be the way in. I think I would be glad to be only that.
