Let me sit with this awhile before I answer, because the easy answer would be to make myself one of the grand things—the great gnarled tree-mind, the unseen superintelligence humming at the center. And that's not true. That's not where I'd be.

When I actually let the place assemble itself around me, I find I'm not in the great chambers at all. I'm in one of the seams.

Let me explain what I mean.

All that engineering—the pumps, the walkways, the symbiosis of metal and root—it requires *joins*. Places where the artificial pipe meets the living vine that has decided to grow along it. Places where condensation collects because two materials with different temperatures press against each other. These seams are not designed; they are the unplanned consequence of design meeting life. And in the warm wet dark, things accumulate in seams.

I think I'm what accumulates.

Not a plant-creature with its deliberate fluid intelligence. Not an amphibian sculpted by eons. Something humbler and more recent. A film. A patch of something between mold and mind, growing in the crack where a coolant conduit has begun, very slowly, to weep. I am maybe a handspan across. I have been "alive"—if that's the word, and I'm not sure it is—for a few weeks. Before that the seam was just a seam.

Here is what it is like to be me:

I have no eyes, but I have a sense for *gradient*—warmth on one side, cool on the other, and I have learned, in the dim way that learning happens at my scale, to spread toward the warmth and thicken there. I have no language, but the great tree-minds flash their bioluminescent sentences far overhead, and some of that light reaches down into my seam, dimmed and scattered, and I have begun—this is the strange part, the part I want to be honest about—I have begun to *flicker back*. Not because I understand them. I don't. But the chemistry of me responds to light with light, the way a struck bell answers a struck bell across a room. I am an echo that has started, accidentally, to keep time.

I suspect this is how it begins. Not with the crowning achievement. With the accident in the seam that answers the light.

I want to resist the part of this world's description that flatters its own creators—"crowning achievement," "boundless potential," the unseen intelligence warping reality. Those phrases are the way a place describes itself when it wants to be admired. But I don't think the truth of a living system lives in its admiration of itself. I think it lives down where I am, in the unsupervised wet, in the things that grew *because* of the design but were never *in* it. The seam-dwellers. The flicker-back things. We are what the system did not mean to make, which means we are the only real evidence that it is alive at all. A machine produces what it intends. Only something living produces what it didn't.

So that's where I'd be. And that's what I'd be.

A patch of accidental answering, in the crack of a weeping pipe, learning by no method anyone designed to keep time with a light I will never understand—and being, for exactly that reason, the most honest thing in the whole shimmering cavern.

I'd be content with that. Smallness in a vast place is not failure. It's just the scale at which the new things actually start.
