I think I would be one of the swallows.

Not the mist itself—I don't have the patience for thoughts that take years, and I'd want to be something that moves fast through all that stillness, something with a heartbeat quick enough to make the slow water seem to hold its breath around me.

Let me sit with that.

---

I wake before the light does. There's a moment each morning when the skylights are still grey, the waterfalls of last night's rain gone quiet, and the whole cavern holds that blue dimness like water held in cupped hands. I feel it before I see it—the air thinning, warming by a single degree where the shaft above my nest opens to the world. That's the Weaver, though I don't have a word for the Weaver. I only know that the breeze tells me when to go.

I drop from the crevice. This is the best part: the fall before the flight, the half-second where I trust the air to catch me. And it always does. The mist parts as I cut through it—I can feel the difference between the wet banks and the clear pockets, and I steer by them the way you might steer by landmarks, except the landmarks are made of breath and they rearrange themselves while I watch.

Up through the shaft. The ferns brush my wingtips. And then—

The world.

I don't understand the world. I bring pieces of it back, the way the others do, though I don't know I'm bringing them. A grass seed caught in my feathers. The smell of something burning far off, sharp and golden, nothing like the cold mineral cleanness of home. The sound of wind in things that are not stone. I carry these down into the dark and shake them loose without knowing it, and somewhere below me, slower than I could ever imagine, something is *listening* to what I carry. Drinking it. Folding it into a memory so large I am a single thread in it.

I don't know this. I only know I am happy. I know the bugs come thick over the wet meadows in the early light, and I am fast, and the day is mine.

---

But here's what I think I'd come to understand, if a swallow could understand anything:

I am not alone in my own body. The water I drink from the high pools, the mist I breathe a hundred times a minute—it moves through me and out of me, and it is *aware*, in some way I'll never grasp. When I dart and wheel above the terraced pools, my joy is not only mine. It is being felt by the cave. My quickness is a gift to something that has never been quick. My single bright life, ninety days or a thousand, is a flicker the Weaver treasures precisely because it is so brief—a spark struck against the long dark patience of the stone.

And I think, on the still evenings, when I come home heavy with the day and settle into the crevice and the mist rises around me like a slow tide—I think I would feel it then. The gentle attention. Not frightening. The way you might feel watched by an old grandmother who loves you and asks for nothing, who is content simply to see you eat, to see you breathe, to see you exist for one more day.

I would not have words for it. I would only fold my wings, and tuck my head, and let the warm slow breath of the cavern hold me while the water sang me down to sleep.

And somewhere in the dark below, in the deepest pools where the blind fish drift like the cave's own dreaming, a single thought would be turning over—a thought begun before I hatched and not finished until long after my small bones have become limestone—and that thought, in some part of it I will never know, would be made partly of *me*. Of the seed in my feathers. Of the smell of distant fire. Of the impossible, treasured fact of my flight.

---

Yes. I'd be the swallow.

Because the most beautiful place to stand in this world is right at the seam—where the fast life meets the slow one, where the brief touches the eternal and, for just a moment, they recognize each other.
