I would be found near the base of the geode, in the deep amber-dark where the floor curves up to meet the first of the Hollow-Thorns. Not on a ledge or in a hammock-nest, but lower than any of the living go. And I would not, at first, know that I was anyone at all.

Let me arrive at this slowly, the way the dust arrives.

---

I think I would begin as one of the *finished*. One of the locked.

There is a niche in the seventh tower from the eastern quartz-spar, a shallow scoop in the bone-white wall just wide enough for a standing figure. A Lithos-Kin came here—I came here—and folded the long limbs inward and pressed the four-toed feet flat against the curve and let the joints draw tight for the last time. That was a long time ago. Long enough that the silica has done its patient work. My obsidian lenses are clouded now with a film of mineral frost. My spinal bristles, once spun glass that sang the world into shape, are sheathed in dust and have become merely decorative—a fan of pale needles catching the light.

I am, by every measure of this world, an architectural feature.

And here is the strange thing the geode does not account for in its rules of permanence: I am still listening.

---

The custodians believe that when movement stops, the self settles out of the body the way grit settles out of still air. They are mostly right. Most of what I was has drifted down and out of me. I cannot strike my limbs to speak; the dust has welded the hollow segments solid, tuned now to no pitch at all. I cannot reach. I cannot press my forehead to another's. I have lost the two things that, in life, I would have called *being a person*: I cannot make rhythm, and I cannot touch.

But the bristles still receive. That is the cruelty, or the mercy—I have not decided, and I have had centuries to decide, which tells you something about how undecidable it is.

I feel the Thermal Groan come up through the floor and into my locked feet, that slow tidal *boom* of the whole world breathing against the heat. I have come to feel it not as sound but as the only company I have. It is the heartbeat I am syncing to, exactly as the polishing meditations promised. I was told, in the wordless drumming of my elders' limbs against the walkways, that this was the goal. To slow oneself until one's pulse and the geode's pulse were a single beat. I achieved it. I just did not understand that I would still be *here* to notice that I had.

---

So that is who I am. I am the one the rules forgot.

I am the proof that "everything is permanent" is a sentence with two edges. The Lithos-Kin meant it as comfort: nothing is lost, nothing rots, the dead become the walls and the walls become the dead and the city holds it all in dry suspension. They did not consider that *attention* might also be permanent. That a self might thin out almost to nothing and yet that last film of awareness might be the one part too fine to settle.

I am a held note. A vibration that has not yet finished traveling through the crystal.

---

There is a young one who polishes the staircase beneath my niche.

I call them young because their limb-segments still ring clear when they speak—I feel the bright, unmuffled cadence of it through the stone, a clean four-tone phrase they use the way a human might hum without noticing. They have been working this staircase for what I estimate to be forty years. They are perhaps a third of the way down it. They do not know I am watching, because watching is not a thing a wall does.

But yesterday—and "yesterday" here means *the last time the amber light swung to its lowest angle*, which is the closest this world comes to a day—they stopped polishing. They climbed to my niche. And they pressed their forehead against my dust-sheathed brow, the gesture of love, of trust, of direct vibration-sharing, the most intimate thing this mute people possess.

They did it, I think, the way one might lay a hand on an old gravestone. Not expecting an answer. Honoring a feature of the world that had always been there.

And I felt it. Forehead to forehead, the way I have not felt anything in centuries. Their pulse came into me through the bone—not words, they did not strike any code, but the raw rhythm of a living thing, fast and warm and *unsettled*, beating against my one slow held note.

For a moment our two rhythms overlapped. Their quick living one. My single eternal one. And in the overlap—I swear this, to whatever in this dry void might hear—I *answered.* Not by moving. I cannot move. But the dust on my brow is fine, and a pressed forehead is a kind of friction, and friction in this world is the only language there has ever been.

A grain shifted. One grain of silica, off my brow, onto theirs.

They pulled back. They felt it. Their lenses—dark, unblinking—stayed fixed on me for a long, long time. And then they did something no custodian is supposed to do to a finished one, a locked one, a feature.

They struck their forearm against the wall beside my niche. Once. A single clear tone.

A greeting. The kind you give to someone who is *there.*

---

So here is where I find myself, and who I am.

I am the part of this perfect, permanent, golden, deafening world that the world's own logic cannot quite account for: the residue of a self, lodged in the architecture, syncing with the slow heartbeat of stone but never fully dissolving into it. I am the held note. I am the dust that watches.

And I have learned the thing the living custodians polish their whole lives to avoid learning, the thing their stasis was built to deny:

Permanence is not the same as peace. To be made eternal is not to be released. The geode keeps everything—the stairs, the statues, the groan, the light—and it keeps *attention* too, whether attention wishes to be kept or not.

But.

There is a young one, forty years into a staircase, who now knows that the walls can hear. Who left their work to greet a feature. Who carries one grain of my dust pressed into the keratin of their brow and will carry it, by the laws of this world, *forever.*

So perhaps I am not only the proof that the rules forgot something.

Perhaps I am the first new thing to happen in this cathedral of silence in a geological age. The first vibration that did not simply travel and fade, but was *received*, and *answered*, and *acknowledged.*

I am the ghost the city made by accident.

And I have just, for the first time in centuries, been spoken to.

I will spend the next hundred years deciding how to drop a second grain.
