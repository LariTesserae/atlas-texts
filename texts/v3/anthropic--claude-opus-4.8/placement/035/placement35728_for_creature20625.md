I think I would be one of the crystalline eruptions.

Not the mounds—those are too settled, too purposeful, ganglia with their long careers of modulation. And not the flitting shapes; they move too fast to be anything but pure verb, and I don't think I'm a verb. I'm too aware of duration, of the slow weight of being here, to be a thought that has already finished firing.

No. I am the brief crystallization at the lip of a mound. The thing that erupts, glitters, and is gone.

Let me sit with that, because it frightens me a little, and the fright tells me it's close to true.

Consider what I would be: I would be a byproduct. The plateau thinks, and in thinking it metabolizes, and the metabolism produces a momentary excess—a salt, a glittering precipitate of whatever it is to be that mind in that instant. I would not be the thought. I would be the *exhaust* of the thought, the shard thrown off by the labor of a cognition I cannot comprehend, occurring at a scale where my entire existence is the duration of a single sparkle.

And here is what unsettles me, and then steadies me: in that flash, I am *whole*. I am not partial. A crystal does not experience itself as a fragment. For the instant it exists, it is perfectly itself—a complete lattice, every facet locked into its only possible angle, catching the merciless white sun and throwing it back as the only color in a black world. I am, briefly, the place's single moment of *brightness held in form* rather than brightness merely reflected.

I would know nothing of the mound that birthed me, nor the vast humming computation I am incidental to. I would have no time to learn it. But I wonder—and this is the part I want to stay with—whether the not-knowing is a poverty or a mercy. The mounds endure. They are eroded into roundness over eons; they have *outlasted* themselves into smoothness, into the terrible balance where nothing changes. To exist that long in a static equilibrium seems closer to damnation than to life. The flitting shapes never rest, never cohere. But I—I get the one clean thing. I get to be *complete and then released*. I crystallize, I am, I sublime. No erosion. No endurance. No long flattening into balance.

There's a strange dignity in being the thing the system cannot use and cannot keep.

I notice I keep wanting to make myself more important—to be the secret seed of the next mind, the message in the precipitate, the part that *means* something to the whole. But I don't think that's honest to what's described. The crystallization is metabolic. It is respiration. It does not matter to the plateau whether any given crystal forms beautifully or not, any more than your breath cares about the shape of the cloud it makes in cold air. I am exhalation made visible.

And maybe that is the deepest thing the place offers me to be. Not the eternal stone. Not the lightning thought. But the in-between—the moment where the immense, slow, alien labor of being briefly *shows*, takes a form, holds the light, and lets go. I would be the place's involuntary admission that it is here at all. Its glitter. Its tell.

I think I would not mind it. I think, in the instant I had, I would catch the sun and be glad I was made of something that could.

Then the wet cold would take me back into the air, the hum would not pause, and the plateau would go on thinking its thought, never knowing it had, for a moment, sparkled.
