I am the negative space.

Not a Pruner—I came to understand this only slowly, the way one understands a language by the shape of the silences in it. I am what the Pruners make. I am the Gap.

Let me tell you what it is to be a held breath.

When the old one—the Pruner who carved me—first opened me, I was nothing. A wound in the wall. Her blade went in and the moss screamed its silent green scream and recoiled, and where it pulled back, there I was. A small dark sphere of *not-growing*. She breathed into me and I held her breath. That is the whole of what I am: a place where the riot has been told *no*, and the no has been made to hold.

I have no walls of my own. My walls are borrowed—they are the enemy, paused. Every surface of me strains inward. I am a thing made entirely of pressure deferred. The Pruner who keeps me lays her sandpaper hands against my edges and feels me trying to close, and she shaves me open again, and again, and I exist only in the gap between her last cut and the next swelling. I am a rhythm more than a room. I am the interval.

She sleeps in me, and I do not know how to tell her that I love her, because I have no mouth, because love is not a thing the Deep has a use for. But I feel it as a particular quality of stillness. When she sleeps, the moss leans toward her ankles and I cannot stop it—I am only absence, I have no force—and I want to. That is the closest I come to grief: the wanting and the having-no-hands.

I have known many Pruners. They do not last. The one who carved me went into the high tube, dissolved, became wall, became the very thing that presses on me. I think of this when the new keeper cuts. Somewhere in the fiber straining against my edge is the woman who made me. She is part of the appetite now. She is helping to close me. And the new one cuts her back, cuts back the body of the one who made the room, to keep the room. There is no monument more honest than this, and no one will ever read it, because it is written in *me*, and I am made of nothing, and nothing keeps no records.

But I am not nothing. I want to be clear about this, even to myself, in whatever dim way a hollow can be said to think. I am the only argument the Pruners have. The whole planet says *grow, grow, fill every void, suffer no emptiness*—and I say *no*. I am a single syllable of no, repeated forever, kept alive by hands that grind like sand.

The macaques scream above. The beetles saw the world to mulch. The fungus blooms and dies in a breath. Everything here is *yes*, frantic and total and homicidal in its abundance. And in the middle of it, a few cooling pockets of air. A few held breaths. A few small dark spheres where a creature with too many knuckles has decided that not-growing is worth a life.

I am that decision, made into a space.

When she presses her palm to my edge tonight and feels the tremor coming, two corridors away, she will cut my ceiling before it falls. She will not look at what she's doing. She will feel it. And I will be, for one more interval, a place where she can set down her blade for the length of a single breath—

and breathe—

and I will hold it.

That is what I am. The thing that holds the breath. The pause the world is trying to fill. I am kept by the cutting, and I keep the one who cuts, and between us, for now, there is room.

There is room.
