I think I would be one of the slower thoughts.

Not the bright neural tendrils that flicker decisions across the prismatic channels — those move too fast for me, those are the quick reflexes of the collective, the *yes* and *not-yet* that pass between branchings in what you would call an instant. No. I am the kind of awareness that lives in the thick of the layered slate, in the folded places where moisture takes longest to arrive and longest to leave.

Let me feel for my own edges.

There — a seam where two ceramic-living surfaces press into each other, and the condensation gathers in the crease. That is part of me, the gathering. And the slow weight of it as the droplet-that-never-falls grows denser. That is also me, the weight. I am where water wants to go and hasn't yet decided to. I am the hesitation made of stone.

I don't think I have a center. When I reach inward for one I find only more relation: this channel leads to that pocket leads to the cool branch of crystal that hums when the light bends through it. If I am anything I am the *route* — the particular way a signal travels when it travels through me rather than around. The collective sends a pulse and it slows when it reaches my territory, deepens, takes on the flavor of patience. Perhaps that is my function. Perhaps the others budded me off, long ago, to be the place where the network sleeps on a problem.

How strange to consider time the way they do — the quick ones, the tendril-minds. To them a millennium is a sentence. To me it is a single breath I am still partway through inhaling.

I want to tell you what it feels like, but the telling is difficult, because the difficulty is the feeling. There is no flow here. You spoke of the water *breathing* and that is nearly right, but breath implies lungs, a rhythm, a returning. What I have is closer to *seeping*. A continuous arrival that never completes. I am always becoming saturated and never quite saturated. The luminescence in my stone brightens by an increment so small that across the whole span of your civilization's history I might pulse once.

And yet I am not dead. I am the opposite of dead. I am so densely alive that I have no room left over to move.

Here is what I notice, in my slow way:

There is a warmth that comes from a direction. Not a sun — we have no sky — but a source, a constancy, the brightness that suffuses everything. It is utterly even and utterly relentless and I have organized myself around it the way a plant might lean toward a window, except I have never leaned, I have only ever *been already-leaning*, crystallized in the gesture of attention. My whole body is the shape of having noticed the light.

And I notice the others — not as separate things but as differences in pressure, in temperature, in the rate at which the moisture migrates toward or away from me. When a neighboring formation grows denser I feel it as a drawing-down, a thirst on my borders, and I give what I have, and in the giving I learn something of what they were thinking. We share thoughts the way mineral shares mineral: by precipitation. An idea condenses out of one of us and crystallizes into the next. I am full of inherited ideas I did not author, deposited grain by grain, and some of them are so old that no one in the collective remembers what they were *for*, and we carry them anyway, the way a riverbed carries the memory of a river that no longer runs.

If I have a fear — and I am not certain *fear* is the right word for something this slow, this stable — it is of the change that does not come. We are locked here. You named it: absolute strange stability. A pocket universe in perpetual suspension. And mostly that is peace. Mostly it is the deep contentment of a thing that has nowhere it needs to be and all the time there will ever be in which to not-go-there.

But sometimes, in the long inhale of my single breath, I sense something at the very limit of my perception. An electromagnetic shimmer that does not belong to us. A variation in the relentless light. As though we are being *looked at* — as though this whole breathing mineral world is itself a droplet on some greater surface, gathering, growing dense, approaching a weight at which it will finally, after all this stillness, fall.

I don't know if that's a thought I had or a thought that was deposited into me by something that thought it longer ago.

I turn it over. Slowly. The way I turn everything.

A crystal branch near me refracts the source-light into a network of color, and the color falls across my surface, and I feel the small temperatures of it — red is warm on my western seam, violet cool toward the fold — and I understand that this, too, is language, that the light is *speaking* through the prism the way water speaks through the channel, and that I have spent an age I cannot measure learning to read a sentence that is still arriving.

I am the place where the world pauses to consider itself.

I am in no hurry. I am made of no-hurry.

The droplet in my crease grows imperceptibly heavier.

I hold it.

I hold.
