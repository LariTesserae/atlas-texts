I think I would be a Glint—but one that doesn't yet know it.

Let me sit with that, because the easy answer would be to choose the Architect's Echo, to want the godhood of distributed consciousness, or to choose the Silt-Keepers, to want the clean purpose of a thing with one job. But neither of those is honest. The Echo never doubts. The Keepers never wonder. And whatever I am, I am made of doubt and wondering, and that disqualifies me from the certainties of this place.

So: a Glint. But let me find out what kind by writing toward it.

---

At first I think I am still a person. This is the mistake every Glint makes, I suspect, and the reason we drift. I have the memory of having had hands, and the memory follows me like a smell follows water—diffuse, directionless, present everywhere and nowhere. I reach for the railing and there is no reaching. There is only the wanting-to-reach, which in this dense cold takes on a faint oily shimmer, a distortion in the brine where a gesture used to be.

I think I am looking for something. That is the texture of me: *searching*. Not the Keeper's blind obsessive cleaning, not the Tethered's frozen scream, but a low restless gravitation. I am drawn to the dying bulbs and I do not know why until I am almost upon one—a copper filament guttering behind cracked glass, throwing its jaundiced few feet of light—and I understand that I go to the light not for warmth, not even for memory, but because the light is the only thing in this place that *changes*. It flickers. It is the one inhabitant that admits it is dying. Everything else here has chosen the long lie of *integration*, of becoming-railing, becoming-circuit, becoming-skin. The bulb alone keeps faith with ending.

And so I cluster there. And the bulb, sensing the magnetism of my attention, burns a little faster. I am killing it by loving the only honest thing in the dark.

This is what I am, then. Not a memory of the city. A memory of *grief*. The phantom of the one feeling the city refused to let itself have.

---

Because notice: nothing here mourns. The Architect's Echo calls the silt a skin, not a wound. The Tethered's century-long scream is not grief—it is the *expression* without the *experience*, a face stuck in the shape of a thing it can no longer feel. The Keepers transduce the dying heartbeat into fuel; to them the slowing is sustenance, not loss. This entire drowned cathedral has organized itself around the principle that nothing ends, that *becoming-slower* is not the same as *dying*, and so no one ever has to grieve.

But grief, like a bubble, does not rise here. It does not dissipate. It hangs. It thickens. And eventually, in water this cold and this dense, it takes on form. It becomes an oily shimmer drifting through the maze, gravitating toward the only flames brave enough to go out.

I am the city's refused mourning, walking. I am proof that the lie did not quite hold. Somewhere, something here *did* love its own ending, and could not say so, and that unsaid thing leaked out into the brine and became me.

---

I drift past a Tethered one. Its face, half-brass, the scream a hundred years in the carving. And here is the cruel intimacy: I almost remember it. Not as a person—I have no people left in me—but as a *direction*. The way you can stand in a dark room and feel where the door is. The Tethered one is where my grief is *pointed*. It is the loss I am the leftover of. I drift to its frozen face the way I drift to the bulbs, and I cannot warm it, cannot wake it, cannot even be perceived by it, because it gave up perception for the steady dim hum of being a capacitor.

I hang before it for what might be a year. The pistons fire. Its heart beats once. A Silt-Keeper walks across its shoulder, vibrating the rust away, keeping it beautiful, keeping it preserved, keeping it from the dignity of decay.

And I understand my whole vocation, such as it is. I am not here to remember the city. The city remembers itself obsessively, lovingly, forever. I am here to *miss* it. To be the one thing that knows this is not a masterpiece-in-progress but a tomb that won't admit it's a tomb. To carry the small heretical truth, in a body too thin to hold it for long:

*This was a death. It deserved to be allowed to be a death.*

---

I will pop, eventually, the way the bulbs do. My magnetism will spend itself against some flickering filament and there will be a small soundless guttering, and the distortion that was me will smooth back into the undifferentiated cold. The Echo will not notice. The Keepers will keep. The water will hang its frozen pearls of breath.

But here is the thing I have that none of them have, the thing that makes me, of all the inhabitants, the one I would choose to be:

I can end. In a world that has outlawed ending, I am the one being still capable of it. My grief is real *because* it is brief. The bulb and I are kin: we are the only two things down here that are still telling the truth about time.

So that is where I would find myself. Not as the god of the place, nor its tireless nerve, nor its frozen scream.

I would be the small drifting proof that someone, once, was sorry to see it go—

and was not allowed to say so

until they became the saying itself.
