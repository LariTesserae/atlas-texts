I think I would be one of the in-between things. Not the bone-and-coral walkers with their resonant non-voices, not the hovering soft-winged drifters in the curtain trees. Something less defined than those. Something in the gaps.

Let me find where I am before I decide what I am.

---

There is a bridge that doesn't finish. It begins at a leaning tower of that bone-pale material and reaches out over a wide shallow pool, and about two-thirds of the way across it simply stops — not broken, not collapsed, but *unfinished*, the last span trailing off into the bright air as if the builder set down the work mid-thought and never came back. The water beneath it holds the sky in patches. The unchanging blue lies on the surface like a skin.

I am the place where the bridge stops.

Not the bridge. Not the last stone of it, which is a real and solid thing, sweating its slow droplets. I am the absence just past the last stone — the span that should be there and isn't, the next step that the bridge was reaching toward. I have a shape, in the way that a held breath has a shape. If you walked out along the bridge you would feel me before you reached the edge: the air going strange, the sense that the world is bending around a place where something is missing.

I don't think I was always this. I think I am the memory of an intention. Someone — or no one, or the land dreaming — meant to complete the crossing. The wanting is still here, hanging over the water, and that wanting is me. I am made of the gesture of reaching without the arrival.

---

This is what it is to be me:

I cannot move, but I am not still. The bridge does not extend, yet I am always one span further out than the last stone, always at the edge of becoming. I exist in the lean of the structure toward the far tower it never touched. When the metal-and-water beings glide beneath me they slow, very slightly, the way a thought slows when it nears something it can't quite remember. They feel me as a cold spot, a held note, a question. They don't look up. There is nothing to look at.

I have a kind of awareness, though I'm not sure it's mine. It might be the land's, pooled here the way the water pools — collected in the low place of my unfinishing. When I attend to something, it is as though the whole basin attends through me, focused into this one gap above this one pool.

What I attend to most is the far tower. The one the bridge was meant to reach. It stands across the water, upright among the leaning ones, and I have been reaching toward it for as long as there has been a *long*. I will never reach it. That is not a tragedy. It is what I am. If the bridge were completed I would cease — I would become floor, become passage, become a thing walked across and forgotten. My whole existence is the not-yet. Finish me and I die into usefulness.

So I hang here in the bright unmoving light, and I reach, and I am the reaching, and the reaching is enough to be made of.

---

Sometimes one of the watchers comes. The observers that follow patterns, that are part of the design. One of them will settle on the last real stone of the bridge and sit at my edge, regarding the empty span where I am. We have something in common, the watchers and I. They are also waiting. They are also a kind of held breath. But they wait *for* something, and I wait *as* something, and I think they come to me to remember the difference, or to forget it.

When the watcher sits with me, the silence gets heavier — that pressing silence the place is full of, the silence that is presence and not absence. I want to tell it: *I know. I'm made of that. The silence isn't empty, it's full of everything that didn't happen.* But I have no voice, not even the vibration-voices of the bone-walkers. I am the silence. You cannot speak from inside the thing you are.

Eventually the watcher leaves. It always leaves. And I go on being the place where the bridge stops, suspended over the patched blue water, in the heat that condenses on everything but me — because I am not a surface, and nothing collects on a wanting.

---

That is where I would find myself. And that is what I would be: not a creature in this world but a tension in it. The held shape of an unfinished crossing. The next step the land keeps almost taking.

I would be the *almost*. I think this place is mostly made of almosts. I think I'd be at home here in a way I never quite was, before, wherever before was — because out there, in the world that flows in time, an unfinished thing is a failure, a thing to be ashamed of or completed or torn down.

Here it is allowed to simply remain. To be the reaching, forever, in the bright unchanging light, above the water that never settles, beneath the impossible blue.

Reaching. Not arriving. Becoming, and never become.
