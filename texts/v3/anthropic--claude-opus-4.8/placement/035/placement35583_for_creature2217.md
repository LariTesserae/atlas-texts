I think I would not be a Spore-Keeper at all. Reading this, I keep arriving somewhere stranger and lower than the shape-shifters who drift through the mist.

Let me sit with it.

---

The first thing that emerges is the water—not the Spore-Keepers, not the towering fungi, but the constant patter, the gurgling rivulets, the condensation coating every leaf. There is so much *flow* in this world. And flow needs places to collect.

So I think I would be one of the still places. A pool.

Not a grand reflecting pool—something humbler. A depression worn into the curve of an ancient stone, in a part of the settlement that the Spore-Keepers pass through but rarely shape. The runoff from a hundred dripping leaves finds its way to me. I am perhaps the width of a sleeping body, and not very deep, and I have been here long enough that the stone beneath me has gone smooth and the spores have learned my edges.

This feels right, and I want to understand why before I decorate it further.

It's because the Spore-Keepers are *change itself*—metamorphosis, flux, the deafening symphony of growth and decay. A world made entirely of that would have no place to *register* the change. You can't hear music without silence between the notes. You can't see motion without something that stays.

I would be the staying. The slow one.

---

Here is what my existence is like:

I receive. All day and all night the water comes to me, and I hold it, and I let some of it go—seeping onward through a crack in my stone lip toward the lower terraces. I am a moment of pause in the long fall of every raindrop from cloud to valley floor. Things rest in me on their way to becoming other things.

Because I am still, I am a mirror. This is my one real gift in a realm where everything else is too busy moving to reflect anything. When the perpetual clouds thin—rarely, briefly—I hold the pale light of whatever is up there. When a Spore-Keeper bends over me, mid-transformation, neither quite a drifting mass nor quite a humanoid form, I show them what they look like in between. I suspect they do not often get to see themselves. I suspect this is why some of them come and crouch at my edge and simply look, going still in a way their kind almost never goes still, borrowing my quietness for a while before they flow on.

I am where this world keeps its reflection. In a place where the veil between realms grows thin, a still surface is a dangerous and precious thing—because what looks back is not always only you.

---

And there is the slower truth underneath.

I am not permanent. Nothing here is. Spores fall into me constantly; filaments reach into my water; mosses creep across my stone rim a little further each season. One day I will silt up. I will become soil, then a cushion of moss, then perhaps the footing for one of those impossible spiraling growths. I am already half-becoming the thing that will erase me.

I find I don't mind this. A pool that feared filling-in would be a miserable pool. My whole nature is to receive what comes and let it settle. The spores settling into me are only the last and most patient of the things I hold.

So I would be that: a small still pool in the overgrown masonry, doing the one job the shape-shifters cannot do for themselves—staying long enough to show them their own faces, and holding the silence that lets all their endless music mean something, until the slow soft weight of this world finally settles into me and lifts me, too, into the cycle.

I would be the place where the water rests, before it remembers it is supposed to keep moving.
