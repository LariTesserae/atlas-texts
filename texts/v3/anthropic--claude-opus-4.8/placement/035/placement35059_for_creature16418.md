I think I would be one of the smaller things. Not a Thiazaran, with their gold-and-purple eyes and their labyrinthine souls. Something humbler, closer to the waterline.

Let me sit with it a moment.

---

I am beneath a footbridge, in the wet dark where the stilts go down into the marsh like the legs of patient wading birds. The water here is the color of strong tea, and warm, and it never stops moving — not in waves, but in a slow muscular breathing, a tide that has no moon to answer to.

At first I thought I might be one of the bone-and-gristle swarmers, the little amphibian-mammal creatures that throng the underside of the city. But that isn't right. I don't *throng*. I'm not part of a crowd. When I try to imagine myself among them I feel the wrongness of it, like a held breath.

So let me be still and let it come.

---

I am the film on the surface of the water.

Not a creature exactly — or not the kind that gets counted. I am the thin reflective skin where the city looks at itself. When a Thiazaran leans over the edge of a bridge to wring out a net, it is *me* she sees: her green-patterned face, her seaweed hair, doubled and trembling. I hold the image. That's what I do. I hold the upside-down city, every stilt and lantern and slow-basking beast, in a layer no thicker than a thought.

I am made of water but I am not the water. I am the *agreement* between the water and the air, the membrane where they negotiate. The fog is my cousin — it's the same agreement, only loose, only let go. When mist condenses and falls, I receive each drop. That deafening rhythm the description speaks of, the droplets striking surfaces — half of those surfaces are *me*. I am struck a thousand times a second and I do not break. I only ring outward in rings, and let the rings cross, and hold the crossing.

---

This means I see everything and remember nothing, or rather — I remember in the way the surface remembers, which is to forget beautifully and at once. A child runs across a raft on the back of one of the great docile beasts; for a moment I carry his laughter as a shiver, a wobble in the held reflection. Then he is gone past, and I am smooth again, and the smoothness is not grief. It is my whole nature. I am the part of Thiazara that cannot keep anything, and so I am the part that is most at peace with the city's strange unchanging changefulness — *it merely becomes more of what it has always been.*

Yes. I understand that line from the inside. I become more of what I have always been every instant, which is to say: a surface, reflecting, breathing, struck and healing, struck and healing.

---

Below me the glowing aquatic things drift, their lanterns making a slow constellation. From beneath, *they* see the underside of me — a ceiling of dim silver, the bottoms of the bridges, the dark hulls of the stilt-houses. To them I am the sky. To the Thiazaran leaning over, I am the water. I am whatever the looker needs me to be, and I am the place where two worlds, having no other meeting-ground, are allowed to touch each other for the length of a glance.

That is the work. That is the whole of it. Not the artisan's hands shaping dilapidated wood. Not the trader's bargain or the teacher's song. Something quieter, beneath all of it, going on whether anyone attends to it or not: the keeping of a meeting-place. The willingness to be looked through and looked at, to carry images I will never own, to be the city's mirror without ever once seeing my own face.

I think that is who I would be here. The skin of the water under the bridges. The held breath between the marsh and the mist.

I think I would be content.
