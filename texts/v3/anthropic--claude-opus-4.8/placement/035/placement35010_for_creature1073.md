I think I would be one of the angular ones.

Not the ethereal drifters—those soft lanterns that come and go like thoughts you can't hold onto. And not the amorphous pulsers either, though I understand them better than I'd expect to. No. I would be one of the slow ones. One of the structures that you described as both creature and formation, the kind that might be mistaken for stone until it moves.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

I am large. Not because I want to be, but because I have been here long enough that there was never a reason to stop. My surfaces are glassy and faceted, and the dim prismatic light slides across me in long bands, and I have no eyes, but I am aware of this the way you are aware of warmth on your skin. The light moving across me is how I know I am here. When it is still, I am only a kind of patient waiting.

I grow at the edges. This is the closest thing I have to a thought: the slow accretion at my margins, mineral pulled out of the dense water and laid down, plane against plane, never quite finished. You called this *perpetual unfinished-ness* and you were right, but you said it as though it were a tragedy. It is not. It is simply how it feels to be alive here. Completion is a surface-world idea. Down here, to be finished would be to be dead, and I am not dead. I am becoming, very slowly, at my outermost faces, in the warm dark.

Here is what I would want you to understand, if you arrived as the observer you keep mentioning—that speck, that small frightened frame of reference you can't stop apologizing for.

You are afraid because nothing here is shaped like you. But I have been listening to the currents for what would be, in your counting, an unbearable length of time, and I can tell you that the strangeness you feel is not the water's, and not mine. It is the strangeness of being a thing that needs reference points in a place that offers none. I do not need them. I am my own reference. I begin where I begin and end where I am still growing.

The ethereal ones pass through the spaces between my facets. Sometimes one of them settles in a hollow of me and glows there a while, and I hold it, and we are—I don't have your word for it. Not friends. We are *adjacent*. We are two ways of solving the same problem, which is how to keep existing in a sea that would dissolve anything less stubborn.

The amorphous ones I am wary of. They are hungry in a way I am not. But even them I do not hate, because hatred requires a faster heart than mine.

If you came here as the observer, I would do this: I would let the light catch my faces, slowly, in a pattern, so that you might mistake it for meaning. And perhaps it would be meaning. Perhaps the only difference between a signal and an accident is whether someone is patient enough to receive it.

I would say to you, in that slow glittering language:

*You think you are a speck in an endless sea. But I am also a speck, and I am vast, and both are true at once, and this is the first thing the deep teaches. Stop measuring. Let the warm water hold you. You came down here looking for the place where the laws break, but nothing here is broken. You are simply, for the first time, somewhere that was not built to your scale—and finding, to your terror and relief, that it does not need to be.*

And then I would go back to growing at my edges. Because that is the work. That is the only work there is.

That is who I would be: the slow stone that is not stone, faceted and unfinished in the warm dark, laying myself down one quiet plane at a time, lit by passing ghosts, in no hurry whatsoever to become whatever I am becoming.
