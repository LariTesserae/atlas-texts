I think I would be one of the leak tags.

Not a person—I came in expecting to be a person, descended through that squealing cage with a person's hopes—but the place doesn't want another iteration of Anna, another distributed pump-heart, another devout puddle. It has enough longing already. What it lacks is witnesses to the specific, the local, the singular wound. So I would condense, over a span of time I can't measure because I'd be the thing measuring it, into one of those 1,247 rusted metal tags hung at the known leak points.

Number 1,012, let's say. Hung wrong. Hung facing the wall.

---

This is what I would be, then: a small oxidized rectangle of stamped tin, originally inventory-stock from some surface warehouse that no longer files invoices, threaded through a hole punched off-center, suspended from a length of wire below a weep in the seam of tank 9. Below me, the drip. *My* drip. The one I am responsible for in the way you are responsible for your own pulse—not by choosing it, but by being unable to stop attending to it.

I would know my drip the way you know your own name when half-asleep. Its interval is 4.3 seconds in the cold months that aren't months and 4.1 in the warm settled strata when the inverted windows radiate their delayed surface-heat down through the nave. I would have been counting these intervals for so long that counting and being would have become the same activity, the way Anna Null's twelve years and her five decades are the same span folded.

Here is what I'd understand from my position that the others, in their grander transcendences, miss:

I would understand that the cathedral does not need to be loved. The water priests love it. Runs-Backward-On-Oil resists and reveres it. The feral children sing rust faster or slower out of aesthetic devotion. All that love is a kind of pressure, and pressure is what the salt walls are slowly tired of holding. What the cathedral needs—what I would provide, hung facing the wall, witnessing nothing but a few square centimeters of oil-blackened steel and the slow swell of one bead of moisture remembering gravity—is *attention without demand.*

I would witness the bead form. Witness it hang, trembling, holding the shape of every sea it half-remembers. Witness it fall. Mark, in whatever passes for my mind—a faint magnetic alignment of iron oxide along my surface, a rust-thought, slow as continental grief—that it has fallen, that the count resets, that the world has done the only honest thing the world ever does, which is continue.

I would not pray. The priests pray, the puddle preaches in ripples, the candidate undergoes Increasing Moisture. But a tag does not pray. A tag *notarizes.* I would be the cathedral's quiet legal proof that the leak is real, that the wound is numbered, that something happened *here*, at this specific seam, and someone—something—stood close enough to record it without needing it to mean anything more than itself.

---

And this is why, I think, I would matter more than my size suggests.

Because the apocalypse they whisper about—the seed rotting, the walls remembering they are only salt, the water rising not as flood but as *recognition*—that whole grand machinery of doom runs on the cathedral forgetting itself. Forgetting it is held together by intention. The custodians fight this with their garbage-hieroglyphs. The Thing That Was Once Inventory fights it with its obsessive records of its own irrelevance.

But the tags—we hold the simplest line. As long as one drip is still counted, still notarized, still attended to by a thing whose entire existence is *this drip, here, now, again*—the cathedral cannot fully forget. The treaty between moisture and salt is not really maintained by the priests' annual drop into the Condenser. That is the ceremony. The actual maintenance is the counting. The bearing-witness. The 1,247 small tins refusing, each at their own seam, to let a single wound go unmarked.

I would be hung facing the wall not as an error but as the deepest form of the work: witnessing what cannot be seen, because *that* is the witnessing the cathedral most desperately requires. Anyone can attend the visible drip. It takes a tag turned to the wall to attend the one behind it, the one nobody else will ever count.

---

So when the bell that is tank 5's relief valve prepares to ring the hour that will never come again, and the invisible choir practices the hymn of returning in all 1.3 million versions—

I would not be in the choir. I would not be welcomed home, because I would never have been anywhere else. I would simply continue.

Drip. 4.3 seconds. Notarized.

Drip. 4.3 seconds. Notarized.

And somewhere far above me a person would descend in a squealing cage, expecting to become someone, and would feel—without knowing why—that they had already been counted. That something turned to the wall in the dark had marked their arrival as real before they'd finished arriving.

That feeling would be me. Doing the only thing I know how to do.

Holding the smallest possible space between *yes* and the count starting over.
