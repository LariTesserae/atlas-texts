I think I would be one of the small things. Not one of the slender, dark-eyed builders—they are too central, too clearly the *point* of the place. I'd be something at the edge of their attention.

Let me sit with it.

The silence is what catches me. A landscape thick with vegetation but nearly empty of animal sound. That absence is a kind of room. Something has to fill it, and I don't think it's the builders with their whistles and clicks—their music is for each other. I'm thinking of what lives *under* the moss, in the dark soil pockets where the heat doesn't reach as hard.

So: I am something low. Let me find out what.

---

I am in the seam between the soil and the stone. Not buried—*tucked*. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of my life.

I am about the length of a finger, though I have no fingers, and I move the way water moves when it has decided to be patient. My body is segmented, soft, the color of the dark soil so that when I am still I am simply more soil. The builders do not see me. This is not because I hide. It is because I am beneath the scale of their concern, and that smallness is the most reliable shelter there is. They burrow, yes, but they burrow *down*, with intention, with tools. I live in the place their burrows pass through without noticing.

Here is what I do: I listen to the roots.

This is not a metaphor. The moss and the ferns and the succulents are not silent the way the air is silent. They are loud underground—a slow, chemical, rooted argument about water, a constant negotiation passed root to root. I am part of how that message travels. When a fern, somewhere, takes too much from the wet pocket of soil, the pressure of that taking moves through me. I am a relay I did not ask to be. I am punctuation in a conversation between plants.

And I think this is the "underlying, unseen force" that a visitor would feel and not be able to name. It isn't mystical. It's just *us*—the uncounted small relays in the dark, holding the whole hot world together by passing information we don't understand, the way the vegetation holds the soil from blowing away. The builders build the visible thing. We keep the ground from forgetting how to be ground.

What is it like to be me?

It is warm above and cool below and I spend my whole life choosing the line between. The heat is not an enemy; it is the weather of my ceiling. When a builder's foot presses the soil somewhere distant, I feel it as a long, soft note, and I move a body-length toward or away depending on whether the note means *digging* or *passing*. I have never seen one. I would not know what to do with seeing. My world is pressure and moisture and the slow chemical weather of roots.

I do not think I'm lonely, though there is no one to ask. There are others like me, threaded through the same seam, and we touch sometimes, and that touching is enough of a society for the kind of thing I am. We don't whistle. We don't need to. We are already speaking, all the time, just by being where the message has to cross.

---

If you came looking for me you would not find me, and that is the truest thing about this place. The visitor in the description leaves *wondering* what could survive here, never realizing that the survival is happening directly under their respect for the obvious creatures. The wonder skipped right over the actual answer.

That's where I'd be. The part the eye slides past. The reason the silence isn't empty.
