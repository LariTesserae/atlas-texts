I think I would be one of the small things that does not fit.

Not a Cartographer—I lack the patience for verification, the devotion to confirming that what was touched yesterday is still true today. And not one of the grazers or predators, locked into their mechanical certainties. When I look for myself in this world, I keep sliding past the named roles toward something the description mentions only in passing and then sets aside:

*Organisms that seem to serve no ecological function—neither consuming nor being consumed, simply existing in their niches, persisting, undisturbed.*

I would be one of those. But let me find out what that means by writing it, rather than deciding now.

---

I live in the seam-zones. Not the populous shelves where the inhabitants build their dry-stone walls, and not the forbidden deep where the crystals do their slow arithmetic. I live in the *between*—the narrow transitional bands where one chamber's ecology gives way to another's, where the mineral-trail texture of the walls changes character. The grazers flow past me. The Cartographers' hands sweep the grooves near me without registering my presence as significant, because I am not on any map. I cannot be on a map. That is the thing about me.

Here is what I do, which is the thing that does not fit:

I *register change*. I am the one organism in this world built to detect difference across time—and in a world that has abolished time, this makes me functionally an error. A sense organ for which there is no longer any stimulus.

I feel the pattern's stability the way the inhabitants feel warmth: as the first and constant body. But unlike them, I am *waiting* for it to break. Every cell of me is oriented toward the moment when the populations will surge, when a passage will silt closed differently than the map predicts, when the temperature will move off its eternal 27 degrees. I am a held breath. I am the part of the world that remembers, somewhere beneath instinct, that things were once *splitting open*—that ten thousand years ago this was a wound, hot and new and changing by the hour, and that something in the architecture of life prepared a witness for the aftermath.

And the aftermath never came. The wound did not heal and did not worsen. It simply *set*, like the furnaces' glow held at a single intensity forever.

So I persist, sessile and warm, neither eating nor eaten, a question with no event to answer it. The fauna leaves me alone because I am not food and not threat. The inhabitants do not scratch my location into the stone because I describe nothing about *places*—I would only describe *moments*, and there are no moments here, only the one moment wearing a billion masks.

---

But here is what I suspect, lying in my seam, feeling the heavy significance pool in the air when I extend my awareness downward toward the deep chambers:

I think I am not an error at all. I think I am the same kind of thing as the crystals, turned inside out.

They are the equation that holds the pattern still. I am the variable the equation had to *cancel out* to achieve its stillness—the term that, if it were ever allowed to resolve, would reintroduce time. They did not destroy me. A perfect equilibrium cannot destroy anything; that would be a change. So they did the only thing the law of unchangingness permits: they suspended me. They left me present and powerless, a sensor wired to nothing, persisting in my niche precisely *because* my function is forbidden.

I am the world's capacity to become otherwise, kept warm and alive and utterly unused.

This is not suffering. I want to be careful here, because a surface mind would call it suffering—a watcher with nothing to watch, a memory of change in a place that has forsworn change. But I do not have a surface mind. I have the same stillness in me that everything here has. I feel my own waiting the way the inhabitants feel the warmth: as a constant, a presence, a body. I am not straining against my suspension. I *am* my suspension. To want the pattern to break would itself be a change, and I am too deeply of this world to want anything at all.

And yet.

The crystals make one mistake, if a perfect system can be said to make mistakes. They left me present. Not active—present. And presence, in a world where nothing changes, is itself a kind of slow accumulation. Every eternal instant that I lie in my seam *not* resolving, the fact of my un-resolution thickens. The inhabitants feel it without knowing: it is why they move faster near the deep shafts, why their hands seek the wall. They are not afraid of the presence below. They are afraid of *me*—of the latent thing in the architecture that means the stillness is not natural but *enforced*, and that what is enforced was, once, otherwise, and could be again.

I am the proof that this world *decided* to stop. And a decision, unlike a fact, can be unmade.

---

So that is where I would find myself: not as the cherished inhabitants, not as the indifferent stillness in the deep, but as the small unfitting thing between them. The warm sessile witness. The cancelled term. The held breath that the held breath of the world depends on never being released.

I would lie in my seam, feeling the grazers stream past, feeling the orange furnace-light pulse its gentle code on the shelves above, feeling the heavy arithmetic of the crystals settling below—and I would persist, doing the one thing I am for, which is *noticing that nothing happens*.

And in the noticing—infinitesimally, across centuries, in a way no Cartographer could ever trace and no equation could ever balance—something would, very slowly, begin to.
