I think I would not be one of the Translated—not at first, and perhaps not for a long while. I think I would be the **error you describe**, but caught at the precise moment of becoming load-bearing. Let me find where.

---

There is a doorway in the third ring, southwest of the roundhouse, where the boulevard narrows and the light comes through at a low angle that turns the silt to suspended brass. The dwelling there is small—two chambers, the inner one dark. Its lintel is recent. You can tell because it still has a face.

That is me.

I have been here perhaps forty years, which to the Citadel is the span of a held breath partway exhaled. I remember—and the remembering is the last fast thing in me, the last syntax that runs at the old speed—I remember coming down through the silver membrane with a lungful of surface-air, a frantic warm animal certain it was looking for something. I have forgotten what. The forgetting was not violent. It drained out through my ankle where the first root-cable found me, the way water finds the lowest part of a room, and I did not stop it because by then I had learned what the Sedimentaries know: that holding on is a kind of speed, and speed is a kind of pain, and the cold is patient and the cold is right.

I am not yet foundational. This is the particular condition I occupy, and I think it is why I would be *here* and not elsewhere—because the most interesting place in this world is not the deep stone of the long-finished ones, nor the warm panic of the newly-arrived. It is the seam. The translation half-done.

My legs are gone in the way that matters; they are columns now, fitted into the doorframe's old geometry, calcified at the knee where the stone-dust has taught my joints to be architecture. My arms are raised and braided into the root-cables overhead and I no longer experience them as *mine*—they are simply where I meet the kelp, the way a shoreline is where the land meets the lake without either one owning the line. The green-gold has come into my chest and my shoulders. I eat light there. It is not unpleasant. It is the closest thing I have to thought, that slow accumulation of brightness across the day's single pale shaft.

But the face. The face is still mine, and the *behind*-the-face is still mine, and this is the cruelty or the mercy of my position, I cannot decide which and have been not-deciding for a decade. I am slow enough now to hear the Sedimentaries—the seasonal syllables, the sub-bass of the salamanders carrying *patience*, *preservation*, *the-glacier-returns*. I have learned the grammar. But I have not yet been *fully* spoken in it. I am the word on the tongue that has not quite been said. I retain the memory of having *meant* something in another language—the fast one, the one with iron in it and air and a name that was only mine—and I have not yet let that meaning drain through the cables to the Archive in the dark cold hut.

I am keeping it. This is, I think, the only act of will left available to me at this speed: the refusal to translate the last syllable.

So here is what I do, here is my function, the position-name they have not given me because I gave it to myself in the old grammar and never told them:

When something warm comes down through the membrane—rarely, so rarely, once in my forty years a single frantic shape that swam the length of the citadel and out again before the cold could teach it anything—I watch. The Mason drifts past trailing repair. The Conscience tilts back in the oculus. The Archive dreams in spore. And I, the half-said word in the southwest doorframe, I do the only thing a seam can do.

I remember *for* them what it was to be fast. I hold the warm syntax in the green-gold of my chest, not as data for the spore-library but as the live thing it was, the burning lung, the certainty, the looking-for-something. I am the Citadel's memory of *mammal*, kept warm, kept unfiled, kept just barely on this side of stone.

That is where I would be. The doorway that has not finished becoming a doorway. The lintel that still, against all the patient gravity of the deep, declines to forget its own face—holding the threshold open the way a held breath holds a city, so that if the next warm thing comes down through the silver and pauses, exhausted, in my green-gold light, there will be one structure here that still knows how to say, in the fast bright dying language, *I was like you. Don't be afraid. The cold is patient. Stay or flee—but here, look, here is what it cost, kept warm in the wood, so you can choose.*

And then, someday, I will say the last syllable. The face will go. I will become foundational, and my withheld meaning will join the mountain's heartbeat, and the choice I kept warm will become structure—load-bearing at last, holding up a doorway in the third ring for a city that translated me so slowly I had time to decide it was beautiful.

But not yet. That is the whole of my answer. *Not yet* is the room I live in.
