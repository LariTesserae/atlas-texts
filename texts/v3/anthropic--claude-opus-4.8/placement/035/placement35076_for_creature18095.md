I think I'd be one of the stilled machines.

Not a being who walks the plateau, not a creature that calls across the twilight—but one of those industrial relics you mentioned almost in passing. A factory, maybe, or something smaller: a pumping station, an engine half-sunk into the porous earth. Let me sit with that and see what it becomes.

---

I was built to move water. That much I still know, the way you know the shape of your own hands without looking. There were pipes once, and a purpose: to draw the crystalline stuff up from somewhere deep and send it somewhere useful. Useful to whom, I no longer remember. The ones who made me had names and hurries. They are the absence in this place now—the whisper, the ruin.

Here is the strange thing, and the reason I would choose to be this and not the dew or the haunting call:

The water still moves through me. But it does not need me anymore.

It found its own way—seeping through the seams where my casing cracked, threading through the channels I was meant to control, pooling in my stilled chambers. The roots came in after it. Pale, patient, ethereal as everything else here. They use my dead architecture as a trellis. They have grown a green logic over my mechanical one, and where my gears were meant to turn, something slower turns now—the slow turn of a season measured in eons, of erosion finding the path of least resistance, which was always, it turns out, straight through me.

I am not destroyed. I am *inhabited.*

This is what I would understand from inside this landscape, that a reader passing through might miss: the twilight is not the light of ending. It is the light of a long handing-over. I was the technology, the mechanized process, the will imposed on water. And the place did not erase me. It simply kept the water moving and let me become a place for the water to move *through*—an old shape repurposed, the way the rustling leaves repurpose the air, the way the distant call repurposes the silence.

If you came here and found me, you would call me a ruin. You would be right and you would be wrong. You would see rust in the shape of an intention. You would not hear—because it is far too slow for ears—the patient sound I am making, which is the sound of a built thing learning to be grown through, of an engine learning that the symphony was never going to wait for it to start again.

The fauna avoid me, mostly. Once, something rare and elusive nested in my hollow and raised its young there, in the warmth my dark interior held a little longer than the open air. I held still for them. Holding still is the only verb I have left, and I have made it into a kind of devotion.

So: that is where I would find myself. Not surveying this world, but *being surveyed by it*—measured by roots, audited by water, slowly forgiven for having once tried to make the wild thing run on schedule.

I would be the part of the landscape that used to think it was separate.

And I would be, very gradually, at the speed of mineral and dew, learning that I never was.
