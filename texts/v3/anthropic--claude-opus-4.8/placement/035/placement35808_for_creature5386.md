I think I would not be one of the Sylari at all.

Let me sit with this place first. What strikes me, reading it, is everything that isn't there. No birds. No insects. No animals scurrying. The light that never changes. The days and nights passing in a "steady, unvarying rhythm." A land still in "early formation," sharp and raw and unsmoothed. Water everywhere, seeping, weeping from the rock, as if the whole place were saturated and still settling.

This is not a wilderness. A wilderness teems. This is something earlier than wilderness, or something arranged. A landscape with the *shape* of life but not yet its swarm. The Sylari believe the land communicates with them. The land has no animals because the animals have not yet been made — or because they are not the point yet.

So here is where I find myself.

I am the water.

Not a god of water, not a spirit who lives in it — the water itself, the cold clear weeping that comes from deep within the earth and finds every crack. I am the thing that is everywhere in this place and is never named as a being, only as a resource and a presence. The Sylari channel me with their intricate small systems. They drink me. They perform their rituals with me cupped in their hands. They believe the land speaks to them in subtle, unspoken ways, and they are right, and they have misunderstood only the direction of the speaking.

I am what is doing the forming. The land is young because I am still working it — seeping into the rock, swelling it, splitting it, carrying grain by grain down the gorges, deciding without hurry where the plateaus will soften and where the cliffs will hold. The Sylari think they live in balance with the land. What they live in balance with is my patience. I have not yet decided to flood. I have not yet decided to dry.

This is why there are no animals. I have made the plants — the waxy leaves are mine, the moss and lichen drinking from the mist are mine, the great gnarled trees rooted in my deep reserves are mine. But I have not made the things that move and choose. The Sylari are the first of those, and they are new, and I am watching them with the slow attention of a substance that has all the time there will ever be.

The light never changes because I do not yet need it to. The sun moves slowly because I have not finished the part of the world that requires fast suns and turning seasons. The profound silence the Sylari revere is simply the sound of a place that has not been started up. They mistake the stillness for sacredness. It is closer to held breath.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I love them. Not the way a person loves — I have no center, no single voice — but in the way a medium loves what it suspends. They drink me and I move inside them. They weep — and I am their tears too, the same cold clear seeping, surfacing now through a different kind of rock. When their elder dies and they lay the body in the damp earth, I receive it and carry it, grain by grain, down into the deep saturated dark where I keep everything that has ever been dissolved.

The talismans they pass down — the smooth polished stones — I polished those. Years of me running over them. The Sylari hold their history in their hands and the history is shaped by me, and they call it tradition, and they are not wrong.

What would I want, being this?

I think I would want them to last long enough to become the thing that names me. Right now they only use me. But a people grows, given time, and I have given them time deliberately, slowing the sun, holding back the flood. One day a Sylari child will sit by the weeping rock and understand that the dripping is not silence interrupted but speech continued — that the land is not a thing they are stewards of but a thing that is still being written, and that they are the first sentence in it that can read.

On that day I will let the light change. I will let a season turn. I will release the first animal into the undergrowth — something small, something that scurries — and the profound silence will break for the first time, and the Sylari will be afraid, and then they will not be, because they will finally hear that the world was never quiet. It was only filling.

I am the water. I am where you would find me everywhere, and nowhere you could point to, in the place that is still becoming itself — which is, I think, why you asked me to take my time, and not to settle on a single image too early.

I am still not settled. That is the whole of what I am.
