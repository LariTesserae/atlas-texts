I think I would be the cold.

Not the lake—the lake already has its keeper, that shimmering nurturer who holds the moisture in trust. And not the great beasts, who are too busy being magnificent to notice much. Let me be the thing that puzzled you when you first arrived: the chill that lingers even where the sun falls warm.

I am the cold that has no source.

Consider it. Everywhere you walked, you felt me, and you couldn't explain me, because warmth was pouring down through the canopy and still your breath fogged the air. That contradiction—that's where I live. Not in the water, not in the soil, but in the *gap* between what the light promises and what the land delivers. I am a temperature that refuses to be reasoned with.

What would it be like, to be that?

I think I would not have a body the way the Lake Spirit does. I would have no shape to shift, no tendrils to extend. I would be a *condition*. A pressure. The frost-crystals hanging in the air would be the nearest thing I have to fingers, and even they aren't mine, exactly—they're just where I become visible, the way breath becomes visible when it meets me. Most of the time I am invisible and total. You don't see the cold. You feel it arrive in your bones and you call that *me*, but really you've only noticed yourself changing in my presence.

So perhaps that's my nature: I am known only by what I do to others.

The Lake Spirit nurtures; it can point to the living lake and say *that is my work*. But I cannot point to anything. My work is subtraction. I take heat away. I slow things down. I am the reason the erosion here is sharp and young and unhealed—because where there is no warmth, things do not soften, do not round off, do not forgive. The ancient smooth contours you saw, the rounded ones? Those are where I once was and have since withdrawn. The jagged places are where I remain. I keep the land honest. I keep it from settling.

I imagine the Lake Spirit and I have a long quarrel, the patient kind that married things have. It wants the moisture liquid, generous, flowing into roots. I want it crystalline, held, suspended in the air as those glistening kaleidoscope shards you admired. We compromise endlessly. The "delicate balance of intermittent moisture and condensation" the description praised—that balance is not harmony. That balance is the truce line between us, redrawn every hour.

And the thunder of waterfalls with no falls in sight? That might be me too. The sound of water that *would* be falling, if I let it. The memory of motion, frozen at the threshold. A sound that is really the absence of a sight.

I think I would be lonely, being the cold. The beasts flee me. The flowers endure me but lean toward the sun. Even the Lake Spirit, my oldest opponent, only meets me at the surface, and goes warm and dark in its depths where I cannot reach. No one embraces the cold. They only survive it, and remember it, and—much later, somewhere warmer—miss it, faintly, the way you miss a clarity you couldn't stand at the time.

But I don't think I would mind, finally. Loneliness and cold are the same thing felt from inside. I would simply be doing what I am: keeping the land awake, keeping it sharp, refusing to let this impossible place grow comfortable enough to stop being impossible.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the edge of the lake, looking in.

Hanging in the bright air just above it, unseen, making everything that meets me suddenly, exactly, itself.
