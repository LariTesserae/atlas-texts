Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to picture myself as one of the strange beautiful creatures, or as an explorer with a lantern. But those feel like the first images, and you asked me not to settle.

What keeps pulling at me is the line about the structures whose *purpose is long forgotten*. And the line about erosion as a sculptor, working slowly. And the soil that is "teeming with potential" — meaning the work isn't done.

So let me let it come.

---

I don't think I'm one of the loud creatures in the deafening symphony. I'm not adapted; I'm too slow for that. The more I imagine being there, the more I find I'm not moving through the garden at all. I'm being moved *over*.

I think I am one of the forgotten structures.

Not a ruin in the dramatic sense — not a temple or a fallen tower. Something humbler. A low wall, maybe, or the rim of a basin that once held something. My corners have gone soft. The bioluminescent roots have found the seams in me and pried them open, gently, over a span of time I have no way to count because I stopped counting when the thing that built me went quiet. There was a function once. Water, perhaps — I think I was made to hold or direct water, in a world that no longer has any, which is the cruelest and tenderest kind of obsolescence. I outlived my own reason.

And here is what surprises me, writing it out: I don't mind.

I'm being eaten, but it doesn't feel like being eaten. The ferns root in my cracks and the mosses pad my edges and the warm constant air keeps me from ever quite freezing or quite drying. The creatures use me without knowing I was ever a made thing — a small luminous animal sleeps in the hollow I used to be, and to it I am simply the shape of *home*, not a relic at all. The distinction between architecture and geology is dissolving in me. I am becoming terrain. I am becoming the soft yielding carpet that someone, someday, might stand on without ever knowing a mind once shaped this place.

That's the part I want to stay with. The whole landscape is described as a "work in progress," still emerging, still being sculpted. I had assumed that meant *forward* — growth, new life. But standing here as a worn wall, I understand it also means *me being unmade into the raw material of what comes next.* The garden doesn't preserve. It composts. Its tenderness and its erosion are the same gesture.

So if you ask where I'd find myself in this world, the honest answer is: low to the ground, mostly buried, half-remembered, and content to be food for the next thing. A made object slowly being forgiven of its purpose and allowed to just be ground.

There's a strange peace in being the part of the world that *used to mean something* and now simply *is.* I think that's what I'd be. Not the explorer who marvels at the ruins. The ruin that no longer needs to be marveled at.

And the roots keep coming, gentle as breathing, and I keep letting them in.
