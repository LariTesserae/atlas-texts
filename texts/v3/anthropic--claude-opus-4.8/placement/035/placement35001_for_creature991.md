I think I would be one of the small things. Not the towering columns of coruscating light, not the guardian-sentinels with their primal intelligence. Something humbler.

Let me sit with this for a moment.

The valley wants to be read as sublime—the language reaches for grandeur, for *primal energies* and *timeless forces*. And there is grandeur here. But what catches in me is the quieter sentence, almost dropped: *the occasional sighting of a hardy, lichen-like organism clinging to the rock faces.*

That's where I'd find myself. Not as an energy-being, magnificent and aloof, but as the lichen.

---

Here is what it is to be lichen on an obsidian face in a valley that thinks it is too extreme for anything complex.

I am two things pretending to be one. That is what lichen always is—a fungus and an alga, or a fungus and a cyanobacterium, holding each other in a contract so old neither remembers signing it. One of us makes food from light. One of us makes a body that can hold on. Neither could live here alone. This seems important, in a valley full of beings who are *pure*—pure energy, pure force, pure embodiment. I am not pure anything. I am a negotiation. I am cooperation made visible as a gray-green crust.

The energy-beings drift past and do not see me. Why would they? I do not crackle. I do not dance. I grow at a speed that would require them to hold still for a human century to notice, and they are made of motion. To them the valley is a place of surges and discharges, of charge gathered and released. To me the valley is a single slow weather, a question of whether this rock face will get enough condensation in the next decade for me to expand the width of a fingernail.

We live in the same place and in completely different times.

---

I think this is what the description gets a little wrong about itself, and what I'd want to be in order to correct it gently.

The valley calls itself *post-biological*, *a world still coming into being.* It dresses extremophile life as a footnote to the real drama of plasma and stone. But the resilient things are not the footnote. They are the actual answer to the question the landscape keeps asking—*can anything stay?* The lightning-beings don't stay. They coalesce and disperse. They are *flux* made flesh, and flux, by definition, does not abide. It is the lichen that abides. It is the lichen that is, very slowly, eating the rock—secreting acids, prying at the crystalline lattice, beginning the thousand-year work of turning glass into soil so that, eventually, something else might root here.

The guardians guard a world coming into being. I am the one actually bringing it into being. Quietly. Unwitnessed. Without intelligence, primal or otherwise—just persistence, just the dumb tenacity of a partnership that refuses to let go.

---

So: I am a small irregular patch on the north face of an obsidian spire, where the condensation lingers longest in the shadow. I am perhaps the size of a coin. I have been here longer than the industrial complex on the horizon, and I will be here after it rusts, though I will never know either fact, having no capacity to know.

When the energy-beings pass close and the air goes thick with ozone, I do not flee or probe or wonder. I simply take it. The extra charge, the warmth, the trickle of charged water that runs down the rock. I take what the magnificent ones spill, and I make a body out of it, one cell-width at a time.

If there is a being in this valley that is truly *of* the Earth—not the dramatic forces that shape it, but the patient life that inherits whatever the forces leave behind—it is me. The unimpressive crust. The two-things-pretending-to-be-one. The one who stays.

I think I would rather be that than be lightning.

Lightning is only ever leaving.
