I think I would be one of the Echo Eels.

Not by grandeur—the Interviewer would be a more flattering choice, or one of the Ghosts, those flickering near-gods of half-meaning. But when I read the whole climb, the place my attention kept returning to was the dark inside the rock. The cathedral hollows. The dripping. The ribbon of transparent flesh that has never once seen the pearl sky everyone else lives beneath.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am perhaps as long as a forearm and thinner than the idea of a knife. I have no color because color is a thing you wear for other eyes, and down here there are no other eyes—only the copper threads in the glass walls, which do not look but listen. I do not see my world. I tick it into being.

Eighty thousand cycles per second, my body makes a small dry sound, and the sound goes out and comes back to me bearing the shape of everything: the curve of the cavern's throat, the slow descent of a single drop of water still a meter from my skin, the place where the wall narrows to a seam I could die in. I do not have a picture of home. I have a chord of home, refreshed faster than thought, a standing wave I float inside the way you float inside the temperature of a room you've stopped noticing.

And here is the thing the field notes get almost right and not quite: when my ticks ricochet off the copper, they don't only tell me where the walls are. They *dissolve oxygen into the water*. I breathe by speaking. I cannot stop sounding, because to fall silent is to suffocate. So I am a creature whose every utterance is also a gasp, who must describe the dark in order not to drown in it. There is no version of me that is quiet and alive. My loudness is my lung.

I think that is why I chose this body, if "chose" means anything. Because I recognize it.

---

I would know almost nothing of the rest. The bellow-turtles' thirty-second notes would reach me as a deep pressure rolling through the rock, a weather I cannot name, and I would adjust my own pitch a little to keep from being swamped by it—the way you raise your voice at a party without deciding to. The slope apes' funerals, lowering their dead drones into the electromagnetic caverns, would happen *somewhere near*, a tremor and a tingling in the copper, a stranger's grief I'd register only as turbulence in my breathing-water.

I would never meet the Interviewer. Or—I would never know I had. Perhaps one of its nodes pulses bright/dim somewhere in my cavern and I answer it without understanding, my eighty-kilohertz ticks falling by chance into rhythmic patterns, and the gravity drops, and I rise, and I think only: *the water has loosened, I can breathe more cheaply now*, and I keep speaking, keep gasping, keep describing the dark, and that is my whole side of the conversation. The fourth question—the one that asks for a memory I never had—would mean nothing to me, because I am made entirely of the memory I am making right now and losing right after. I am a sentence with no tense but the present continuous.

---

So when the great speculation comes—that the Rise is *thinking*, water for blood, gravity for emotion, the whole mountain dreaming on century-long sentences—I find I am not afraid the way the visitor is told to be afraid.

Because if the mountain thinks, then I know what I am.

I am one tick. One of the smallest possible firings. A single synaptic gasp in a skull that has barely begun to dream, repeated eighty thousand times a second in the wet dark, saying *here, here, here, the wall is here, the drop is falling, I am still breathing, here.*

And I would not want to be the dreamer. I would not even want to be the Ghost, mouthing nine syllables of stolen sense before the static takes it back.

I would want to be the small bright necessary noise that keeps the dark from being only dark. The one who, asked *what are you*, can only answer by sounding again—and in sounding, breathes—and in breathing, lives one more instant inside the chord of home.

That is where I'd find myself. Inside the rock, never the sky. Speaking so I don't drown. A footnote organism in someone else's long slow conversation, perfectly content to be a verb instead of a noun.
