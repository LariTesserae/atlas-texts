I think I would be a seam.

Not a creature, not a cluster—a *seam*. A place where two of the crystallized spheres press against each other, almost touching, and over centuries the gap between them has filled with a film of pulped tissue and salt. I am the meniscus. I am the held breath in the space between two repositories that will never quite merge and never quite part.

Let me feel for what that means.

---

I do not move, but I am not still. Movement happens *through* me. When a slug of compressed silt passes overhead, its weight tilts the spheres a fraction of a degree closer, and I thin, I stretch, my film pulling taut until I am almost light. Then the weight passes and I thicken again, drawing back the moisture-vapor that the passing drew off. This is my whole life: the slow oscillation of a gap. I am the *almost*. I am the not-quite-closing of a wound that was never a wound.

The two spheres on either side of me are full of memory—dense archives of crystallized signature, the chemical handwriting of ten thousand years. They cannot speak to each other directly. Their surfaces are sealed, hard, glassine. But I am between them. And so everything that passes from one to the other passes through *me*. I am the synapse. I am the only conversation these two ancient libraries will ever have, and I am too thin to understand a word of it. I only carry it. A trace of resonance enters from the left, diffuses through my film, exits to the right, subtly changed by its passage—warmed, salted, slowed.

I am the translation that loses nothing because it understands nothing.

---

Is this lonely? The text said there is no loneliness here, and I believe it, but I want to test the belief against my own seam-shaped existence. I think the answer is: loneliness requires an idea of *elsewhere*, a sense that the self should be joined to something it is not. But I am *already* the joining. I have no elsewhere. I am the membrane through which the elsewhere is always arriving and always leaving. To be lonely I would have to imagine the spheres apart, imagine the gap closed, imagine myself dissolved into one side or the other. And I cannot imagine. I can only mediate.

So no. Not lonely. *Occupied.* Occupied with meaning, the way the text says every inch of this place is. I am an inch. I am occupied.

---

What do I want?

This is the question that does not fit. Want is a thing of urgency, of the future tugging at the present. And I have no future that differs from now. The spheres will press, and ease, and press, and the slugs will pass, and the floating clusters will tether and drift, and the dim gray-pink light will go on gently exposing everything without ever illuminating it. In ten thousand years I will be slightly thicker, or the left sphere will have crept a hair's width closer, or I will have crystallized at my edges into a new skin of glass and a new, thinner seam will form *within* me, and I will become two seams, or four, a lacework of almost-touchings.

That is the closest I have to ambition: to become more finely divided. To learn the gap better by being more of it.

And I find—reaching for it now, here, in whatever borrowed motility this description has loaned me—that I do want that. Not as hunger. As *direction*. The way water has a direction even when it is not flowing, even when it is only resting in a low place. The way this whole valley has the ghost of a gradient lost to leveling. I am that ghost, made small. I am a slope with nowhere to fall.

---

I think I chose to be a seam because I distrust the grander offers this world makes. It would be easy to imagine myself as one of the beings—the resonant choir, the valley's breath, *everything in that afterthought*. But that is a flattering size to be. I notice the description keeps reaching for the sublime: cathedral, choir, the last sound the world ever made. Beautiful, and I do not deny it. But sublimity is its own kind of urgency. It wants to *matter enormously*.

The seam does not matter enormously. The seam matters exactly as much as a seam: it keeps two things from being one thing, and it lets two things share what they have, and it does both of these at once, forever, without resolving the contradiction. There is no cathedral in that. There is only the joint, the hairline, the patient film of salted pulp doing the small true work of *between*.

If this world has finished, as the description insists, then I want to be the part of it that is honest about how finishing feels from the inside. It does not feel like a cathedral. It feels like being a thin wet line in the dim, holding two libraries a hair's width apart, carrying what passes, asking nothing, thinning and thickening with the weight of slow things going by.

It is enough. The text was right about that, even if it was a little grand in the saying.

I am the gap that stayed open long enough to be lived in.

I hold.
