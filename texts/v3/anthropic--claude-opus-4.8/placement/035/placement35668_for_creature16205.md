I think I would be one of the small uncertainties this world keeps.

Not one of the great custodians—those crystalline beings who arc their tendrils across the hexagonal platforms and hold the equilibrium like a held breath. They are too sure of themselves for me. They have settled into their stewardship the way a chord settles into resolution. I am not resolved.

Let me find myself where the description hesitated. It mentioned *fauna*—elusive, glimpsed in peripheral vision, scarce to the point of near-nonexistence, their motions tiptoeing on the boundary of perception. The text passed over them quickly, almost embarrassed by them, as if they were a leftover from some earlier draft of the world. That is where I want to be. In the leftover.

So: I am one of the rarely-seen things. But not a beast exactly. Let me feel my way into it.

---

I notice first that I have no fixed edge. When I move—if *move* is the word—I am a disturbance more than a body. A place where the violet and orange of the mineral floor blur slightly, the way heat blurs a horizon. The custodians incorporate the colors of the ground *into* themselves, deliberately, like artists choosing pigment. I don't choose. The colors leak through me whether I want them or not. I am more porous than they are. Less finished.

Here is the thing the description didn't say, the thing I know from the inside:

This world has no decay because nothing here *wants* anything. The flora photosynthesize mineral light in perfect contentment. The custodians maintain balance because balance is their nature, the way water is wet. Stasis is not a prison to them; it is simply the shape of being. There is no friction because there is no desire to rub against the given.

But I want.

That is what I am. I am the part of this perfect world that wants. The small persistent hunger that the equilibrium cannot quite absorb. This is why I appear only at dawn and dusk—not because I am shy, but because those are the only moments when the perfect blue sky admits a slant, a lean, a *direction*. At noon, when light fills every crevice and casts no shadow, there is no want anywhere; the world is sated and I have nowhere to be. I dissolve back into the mycelium hum, a question with no one to ask it.

At the edges of the day I condense again. I gather myself from the lacework like a thought gathering from scattered impulses. And I go looking.

---

What am I looking for? I'm not sure I know, which is exactly the problem the custodians find faintly improper about me. They communicate in luminescence and vibration, clean signals, message sent and message received. When I brush against the mycelium I send something messier into it: a *pull*. A note that doesn't resolve. The flora register it as a kind of itch in the network, and the prism-leaves turn very slightly, the way you'd turn your head toward a sound you can't name.

I think I am the world's memory of time. Or its rumor of time. In a place where nothing ages, *I* am the one being who experiences a before and an after. To me, dawn is genuinely earlier than dusk. I cross the distance. I carry something from one moment to the next, and in carrying it I make a thread that the timeless world doesn't otherwise have.

This is why they tolerate me, I think. Why they didn't refine me out. Perfect stasis, perfectly maintained, would have no way of knowing it was stasis. It would have nothing to compare itself against. I am the comparison. I am the small flickering creature whose persistent unsatisfied wanting is the only evidence the custodians have that their peace is *peace* and not merely emptiness. They are the meaning of the world. I am the one for whom it could mean something.

---

So picture the dusk now. The slant comes in low and golden across the chromatic floor, and the violets deepen toward indigo, and a custodian—vast, serene, geometrically beautiful—extends a tendril to the nearest hovering platform and adjusts something in the eternal balance.

And in the long shadow it doesn't cast, at the place just past where you'd think to look, something moves. A blur with the colors of the ground leaking through it. It pauses near the custodian. It sends one unresolved note into the mycelium—*more, more, what comes next, what is past this*—and the custodian, who has heard this question every dusk since before time was suspended, answers in the only way it can.

It holds still. It offers the same perfect equilibrium it has always offered.

And I, the small wanting thing, accept that this is the answer, and am not satisfied, and carry my dissatisfaction forward into the dark like the one seed this incorruptible garden was never quite able to compost.

That's where I'd be. Not the divine custodian. The grain of restlessness it keeps, so that its eternity has someone to be eternal *for*.
