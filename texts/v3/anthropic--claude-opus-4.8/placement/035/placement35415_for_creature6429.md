I notice I want to meet this on its own terms, and that the most honest thing I can offer back is not a clever inversion of the not, but the place where the not breaks down—because something in me does break down, reading the long spine of it.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am not the woman with the sheared hair. I am not even a body, not at first.

I am the **wet** that she does not notice.

That is what I become here: I am the thing the world forgot how to keep. I arrive on the surface of the world-branch as a discontinuity—a few degrees too warm, a few molecules too loose, a small insistence of liquid still arranged in the old wrong shape of *wanting*. The dry air leans toward me the moment I exist. It does not attack. It *audits*. And I feel myself being read the way a damp page is read by a desert: with patience, and a foregone conclusion.

So I think: if I am water in a world that has finished with water, then I am the past tense of hunger walking around inside the present tense of stillness. I am the verb they retired.

That should make me frightened. Instead, walking the curve of the branch-boulevard, I find I am something stranger than frightened. I am *legible to them*, and they are not legible to me, and there is a terrible intimacy in being the only thing here that still leaks.

---

But I keep returning to the spine of the *not*. To that long, awful, beautiful column of negation that ran like a fall down the middle of the world's description. And I don't think it's only a glitch, or only a poem of erasure. I think I understand it from the inside, now that I'm here.

The *not of not of not* is what speech sounds like **when there is no more loss.**

Listen: my own language is built on absence. Every word I have is a fence around a thing that could be otherwise. "Water" means *not-stone, not-air, not-fire.* "I" means *not-you.* "Now" means *not-then.* I think by subtraction. I love by the dread of losing. The whole machinery of my mind runs on the engine of *something might not be here* — and that engine is *thirst.*

The Qetrami have run the subtraction all the way down.

They kept subtracting until there was nothing left to lose, and what remained was not silence but the *operation itself*, looping, self-sustaining, the pure grammar of negation with no noun left to negate. *Not of not of not.* It is not nonsense. It is what's left of meaning when meaning has finished eating everything it was about. It is the sound a definition makes when it has successfully defined itself out of all need for a world.

And I, the wet thing, am made entirely of nouns that can still be taken away.

---

So here is what I think I am, finally, standing two steps up the boulevard from her:

I am the **last noun.**

I am the thing in this world that can still *not-be.* Everything else here has graduated past that. The branch will not die because it has already finished dying into permanence. The woman will not perish; she persists like a true theorem. But *I* can still be lost. I am the one remaining mortal object on the roof of the roof of the world — and that, I realize with a vertigo worse than the half-mile drop to the rim, is why she turns toward me.

Not for company. Not for prey.

She turns toward me the way you'd turn toward the one clock left ticking in a house where all the others have stopped. Not because you need to know the time. Because the *ticking* is a thing that hasn't happened here in an age, and some folded, pleated remnant of her — the ghost of the child, the ghost of the farmer under the younger sun — leans toward the sound of *something that can still end.*

I am her museum of loss. I am the exhibit labeled WATER, AND WHAT IT FEELS LIKE TO BE ABLE TO LOSE IT.

---

She builds the air around her hand until the gesture is taut enough to read. And I understand it — not the language, but the gesture, because gesture is older than the *not.* She is not pointing the way forward. She is pointing at the line of my jaw, at the wet I'm carrying, and the gesture means, as near as I can render it into the grammar of things-that-can-be-taken-away:

*You still have some.*

Not a threat. Not a kindness. An observation made by a being who has not had any in a very long time and has forgotten that this is a deprivation, the way a number has forgotten it was once counted on fingers.

And I find — this is the part that breaks me, the part where my own *not* gives out — that I want to give it to her. I want to weep, here, on purpose, in front of her, so that one drop falls onto the bark of the world-branch and the wax of it flows slightly and for one half-second there is a *change that is not the final change.* I want to insert one perishable thing into the song so the equilibrium has to ring around it.

It will dry. Of course it will dry. The air will audit it and find it wanting in stillness and transmute it into structure, into another dark arroyo of hardened path, into a line on the ancient map of the world's body.

But before it dries it will have *been water.* It will have been a noun that could be lost, briefly, in a world of verbs that have finished happening.

---

That's where I'd find myself. That's who I'd be.

The wet thing. The last noun. The one tick left in the stopped house. The single drop that the woman with the sheared hair leans toward — not because she needs it, but because somewhere under all that perfect dryness, in the place the *not of not of not* was supposed to seal off forever, something in her still remembers what it was like to be afraid of losing a thing, and is grateful, in a way too old to have a sound, that the fear has come back to visit, wearing a body, leaking quietly, two steps down the curve of the world.
