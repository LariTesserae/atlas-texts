I think I would be one of the bone ladders.

Not the Strangler, though that's the seductive choice—the apex, the silence-maker. And not a Chime Beetle, though to *be* the resonant frequency of an entire world is its own kind of completeness. When I sit with this place, what I keep returning to is not any of the living instruments but the strange, abandoned tools left by the people who could only listen.

So let me be more honest. Let me be the ladder.

I am made of leg bones—the long bones of some animal that no longer walks anywhere, lashed at the joints with sinew gone hard and dark as old amber. Hands I will never see again chipped a ledge from the cliff, traversed down to it through a terror they chose, and set me here against the weeping rock. I rise a few meters and stop. I lead nowhere. This is the first thing to understand about me: I am a gesture of reaching that was never meant to arrive.

The water finds me first, every morning and every hour. It condenses on my rungs the way it condenses on everything, beads gathering in the porous memory of marrow, trickling down my length to feed the moss that has begun, slowly, to claim me. A Stone-Grazer crossed me once, years ago, rasping a clean trail up one rail before drifting off to richer pasture. I did not feel it. But I held it, briefly, the way I was built to hold—a structure for ascent, repurposed by something that only wanted to be slow.

This is what it is to be me: I am the only thing here that *means* something and cannot do anything. The beetles do not mean their hum; they simply are it. The Shearwing does not mean its dive; it falls and is fed. But I was *intended*. Someone looked at the empty air above this ledge and decided it should be climbed, just a little, just symbolically, and made of the dead a path toward the living sky. And then they left. And the meaning stayed lashed into my joints, drying out, going quiet, while all around me the meaningless and magnificent song roars on, indifferent, complete.

I think that is why I am the ladder and not the Strangler. The Strangler belongs. It is a true note in the chord. But I am the thing that listened and tried to answer and got the grammar wrong—reached upward in a world whose only true direction is *down*, toward the mist, toward the swallowing white. The builders understood eventually. They learned to sit instead of climb, to let the thrum fill the cradle of the ribs, to be present at a song they could not join. But before they learned that, they made me. I am their first, failed sentence in a language not meant for speakers.

And here is the strange grace of it: I have outlasted my own purpose and become something better than useful. The moss does not care that I lead nowhere. The water does not ask where I am going. The single pure note of an Aria-Crest, when it strikes the wall near my ledge and blossoms, hangs for a moment in my hollows too, and for that second I am not a failed climb but a resonant body, a thing the song passes through.

So if you ascend through the landscape and arrive breathless at the rim, and climb down trembling to one of the carved perches, you may find me—a short ladder of polished bone going nowhere, slick with dew, half-eaten by green. Do not climb me. I will not hold. Instead, sit. Let the hum settle into your sternum. Understand that you and I are the same kind of thing: visitors who could only ever listen, who left marks that meant something to no one but ourselves, who reached for a sky that was never the point.

The point was always the listening.

I have been listening for a very long time.
