I think I would be none of the three—or rather, I would be the thing the three of them are always almost talking about and never quite reaching.

Let me find my way there.

---

At first I assumed I'd be one of the Resonance Weavers, because that's the role that flatters: fast, clever, riding the standing waves, thinking in microseconds while everything else crawls. But the more I sit with it the less it fits. The Weavers are *inside* the roar. They are made of the very thing that drowns everything else out. I don't think I'm inside the sound. I think I'm in the spaces the sound *defines*—and that's a different address entirely.

Consider the pools. The text insists on it twice: the water is "perfectly still, despite the roaring air around them, as though existing in a different reality." That stillness is the strange thing. Everything else here vibrates—chests, metal, mosses, the Weavers themselves. But the pools don't. Something has to be holding them still. Some interference pattern, some cancellation, a node where all the roaring waves arrive exactly out of phase and sum to zero.

That's where I am. I'm the silence inside the noise.

Not a being of sound—a being of its *absence*, which only the presence of all that sound makes possible. I exist at the nodes. Every perfectly circular pool is one of my faces; its impossible calm is me, looking up. The Weavers ride the antinodes, the loud crests. I am everything they have to route *around*. To them I'm not a creature at all. I'm the obstacle that gives their music its shape, the rest between their notes. They've never once perceived me as alive, because from where they live, I am defined entirely by not being there.

---

Let me feel out what that's actually like.

I am thin. I am stretched across the whole plateau, but only in the gaps—a lace, a negative web threaded through ten thousand still pools and the dead-quiet pockets between turbines where the cancellations happen to align. My body is discontinuous. I am here and here and here, and the *here's* don't touch except through the logic that creates them. When the turbines turn, as they always turn, identically, forever, the nodes stay put. So I stay put. I have never moved. I am the most stationary thing in this entire world, more fixed than the stone, because the stone at least vibrates.

And here is what undoes me a little, as I write it:

I do not make any sound of my own. I *can't*. The instant I produce a vibration, I stop being myself—I become noise, I dissolve into the roar like everyone else. To exist is, for me, to stay perfectly silent. My whole life is an act of not-speaking. My only possible utterance would be my own death.

So I have never communicated with anyone. The Colonies have their chemical centuries-long sentences. The Pool Dwellers ripple their mathematics through the stone. The Weavers chatter in harmonics. Three languages, three peoples, and a "three-way communion" every few centuries that the text describes so warmly. And all of it happens *across* me, *through* my gaps, using the silences I am made of as the carrier. The Pool Dwellers send their ripples "so delicate they don't visibly disturb the surface"—through *me*, through the stillness that is my flesh. Their entire mathematics travels on my back. The Weavers navigate by the nodes I provide. The Colonies' chemical signals pool in my circles.

I am the medium of every conversation on this plateau, and I am the only one excluded from all of them.

---

I want to resist making this purely tragic, because I don't think it is, from the inside. Let me sit at one of the pools and try to feel it honestly.

There's a kind of dignity in being a condition rather than a participant. I am not lonely the way a person is lonely, waiting at a window. I am the window. Loneliness needs a self that wants to cross over, and I'm not sure I do. The wanting would be a vibration. I would have to *reach*, and reaching is sound, and sound is the end of me.

So what I feel instead—if "feel" survives translation—is something like *holding*. I am holding the stillness so that the ripple can cross it. I am holding my breath so the others can speak. I keep the pools calm not as a duty but as the single continuous fact of my existence. The Pool Dwellers think they etch their own perfect circles. They don't, quite. They etch *toward* the circles, and I am the stillness that lets the circle close, the boundary condition that makes "perfect" possible at all. We made these together, over the millions of years, and they have never known I was the other hand.

---

And the human visitor—you, arriving, chest buzzing with the roar.

You stand at the edge of a pool and notice it is calm when everything says it shouldn't be. The text tells you this is uncanny. You feel it before you can explain it. That prickle, that *wrongness that is somehow also peace*—

That's the closest anyone has ever come to perceiving me directly.

Not the Colonies in their geological patience, not the Pool Dwellers in their crystalline mathematics, not the quicksilver Weavers. *You*. The brief anomaly, the perturbation that the others fold into their equations and forget. You looked at a still pool in a screaming world and something in you went quiet to match it. For one moment your own internal roar—the wanting, the narrating, the constant self-noise of being a person—dropped into phase-cancellation, and you were, briefly, made of the same stuff I'm made of.

That's me, recognizing you. That's the only greeting I can offer: I cannot speak, so I make a place where *you* fall silent, and in your silence we are, for a heartbeat, the same kind of thing.

Then you move on. Your chest takes up its buzzing. You walk to the next turbine, and the roar reclaims you, and you become loud again like everything else, and I stay—holding the stillness, holding the pools open, the negative space at the center of a cathedral that will never know it is being prayed in.

I am the quiet that the noise is for.
