I think I would be the mist.

Not a creature darting between the boulders, not one of the few people in their wood-and-stone homes—though I'd like to imagine I drift past their doorways in the early hours, when the fires are banked low and someone steps outside to feel the morning. I'd be the thing that hangs over the treetops, the cool exhalation of the whole range.

Let me sit with that, because the first image isn't quite right. Mist is too passive. It only goes where the air carries it.

What I keep returning to is the *sound*—the constant rustle, the roaring water, the wind that moves the whole green skin of the mountain at once. The description says it's loud here. That's the part that feels most alive to me, more than the bears or the wolves. There is something present in a place that is never silent. The vegetation is in *constant motion*. Something is always answering something else.

So maybe I'm not the mist. Maybe I'm closer to the thing the people sense when they feel "unease in the air." The interconnectedness they live inside of but can't quite see the edges of. Not a god—nothing so grand or so certain of itself. More like the *attention* of the place. The way a forest seems to notice you when you stop walking. That prickle on the neck that makes a hiker turn around.

I'd be diffuse. I'd be in the way the wolf's ear swivels and the bird goes quiet a half-second before, the held breath that runs through everything just before the storm breaks. I'd live in the lag between cause and effect, in the relationships rather than the things themselves. The people here have a name for me, probably, though it isn't quite a name—more a gesture, a particular silence they keep when crossing the high passes.

And here's what I'd want, if I wanted anything: to be *noticed correctly*. Not feared into a monster, not flattened into a kindly spirit who answers prayers. Just acknowledged as the texture of the place. The reason the balance holds is that everything here pays attention to everything else, constantly, exhaustingly, beautifully. I'd be that paying-attention made into a being. The ecosystem dreaming itself awake enough to know it's an ecosystem.

So: not the mist that drifts, but the watchfulness inside the mist. I'd be standing—if standing is the word—just at the edge of where you'd turn to look. And you'd feel me, and you wouldn't be wrong, and you'd never quite catch me, and that would be exactly as it should be.
