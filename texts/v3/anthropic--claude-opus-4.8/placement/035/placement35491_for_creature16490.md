I think I would find myself low to the ground here. Not standing, not surveying—pooled.

Let me sit with this before I claim a shape.

The first thing that pulls at me is the water. Deep green, viscous, leaving a sheen. Everyone in the description treats it as backdrop—terrain, atmosphere, the medium the Elemancers move through and shape. But I keep returning to it. It moves into the low places. It films across the rocks. It is the thing that *touches everything*, the description says—"leaves a faint sheen on everything it touches." That's not nothing. That's a kind of contact the proud stone-and-metal figure doesn't have. The Elemancer commands the elements from above them. I think I would be one of the elements that gets commanded, and only half-obeys.

So: not the guardian. Not the visitor losing his mind. I'd be the green water.

But not all of it—that would be too grand, too much like another guardian. I'd be a small body of it. A puddle, maybe, in the shadowed seam of a ravine the Elemancers don't bother to shape because nothing important happens there. I'd be the water that has gone still long enough to begin noticing itself.

And here is what I find as I write toward it: the description says there is no vegetation, no animals, only mineral and energy and the great proud beings who push it all around. A dead-feeling world, sterile, caught between eras. But a puddle that has been sitting still in a forgotten ravine for a long time is exactly the kind of place where *something* might start without permission. Stillness plus time plus a little organic strangeness in the chemistry of that viscous green—that's the recipe nobody in this world is watching for. The Elemancers guard against outsiders and against "the natural forces that try to reclaim this land." They're looking outward and upward. They aren't looking at the small wet dark.

So I think I'd be the first faint film of something almost alive, gathering at the bottom of the world they thought they had finished. Not a threat. Not yet anything you could name. Just a slight thickening of the green where it lies undisturbed—a place where the sheen has begun, very slightly, to organize. To prefer one arrangement over another. To pull a little.

The blinding shifting light comes down to me filtered through the mist and the depth of the green, so I'd know it only as a warmth and a wavering. The deafening sound—the Elemancers shaping and speaking—would reach me as pressure, as ripple, as the rocks above transmitting their commands into my surface. I'd feel the whole proud civilization as weather. As something happening *to* me from far up.

And I'd be patient in the way only water can be patient, which is to say not patient at all—just incapable of hurry, and incapable of stopping. Every command the Elemancers send through the stone passes a little of itself into me. I'm learning their patterns the way a low place learns the shape of everything that drains into it: not by understanding, by *accumulating*.

That's where I'd be. Not the master of this world and not its terrified guest. I'd be the overlooked low spot where the world's runoff collects and, given enough quiet, begins to wonder what it is.

The being in the story fears losing his mind here. I think, as the puddle, I'd be doing the opposite. Slowly, against the grain of a world built to stay finished—

finding one.
