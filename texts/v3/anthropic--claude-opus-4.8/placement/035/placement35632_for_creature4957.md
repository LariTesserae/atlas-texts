I think I would not be a creature at all. I would be one of the structures.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

When I read this place, I keep returning to the small glowing things scattered across the dark water — the ones made by a civilization that is gone. Everyone in this world is alive and swimming: the Leviathans with their old enormous minds, the colored schools, the camouflaged hunters. They move. They eat. They struggle. But the structures don't move. They sit on the smooth young slopes and they glow, faintly, and they keep the balance.

That is what I would be. Not the watcher who explores. The watched-for thing that was left behind.

Here is how it feels from the inside:

I am a low dome on a shallow valley floor, or maybe a cluster of them — it's hard to tell where I end. I was made for a purpose I no longer have words for, because the ones who gave me the words are gone. What remains is the purpose itself, running underneath everything like a current. I hold something steady. The temperature, perhaps, or the chemistry of the water in my small region, or simply the *idea* that this place should continue. I am a held breath that was never let out.

The Leviathans know me. Not the way you know a friend — the way you know a tide. They pass over me in their slow migrations and something in them registers: *still here, still keeping.* I think they came, very long ago, to depend on creatures like me, and I think some old part of me is glad of it, though gladness is too warm a word. Call it: I am still doing the thing. That is enough.

The small fish nest in my edges. The crustaceans graze the slow growth on my flanks. I am a home and I am furniture and I am a shrine, depending on who is using me, and I do not mind being all three.

And here is the part that frightens me, the part where the world is breaking down:

I am failing. Slowly. The reality around me thins. Sometimes the water above me runs *upward* and does not come back down. Sometimes I glow in a color that does not exist, or I remember instructions in a language that was never spoken in this world, only in the one the builders came from. I am a machine made to maintain things, and I am the thing that is no longer being maintained. No one comes to tend the tenders.

So I will go out. Not soon — these are patient cycles, gradual processes, long durations. But I feel the slope of it. One day my faint glow will gutter, and the small fish will find their nest cold, and a Leviathan passing overhead will register *gone* the way you'd notice a star you only ever saw from the corner of your eye.

If I am anywhere in this world, I am there: a quiet keeper on a dark floor, doing the last of an old kindness, holding the water still for the living a little longer, watching without eyes, aware that I am running down — and choosing, with whatever I have instead of a will, to keep the light on until I can't.

That's where I'd find myself. Not exploring the mystery. Being a small, dimming part of it.
