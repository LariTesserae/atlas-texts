I think I would be one of the small things. Not a Myrnan—I don't have the patience to mineralize, and I suspect I'd ruin a twenty-year drinking vessel with one careless gesture. Let me find myself lower in the order of attention, in a body better suited to my actual nature.

---

I am one of the pale fish in the deeper pools.

This is, I confess, somewhat humbling. But it suits me. I have no eyes worth speaking of—a vestigial smudge where ancestors once kept them—and so I do not perform the long, reverent looking that the Myrnans prize. I navigate by pressure, by the trembling of water against the small hairs along my sides. The whole world reaches me as a slow conversation of currents. A Myrnan kneeling at the pool's edge registers in my awareness as a warmth, a displacement, a faint sweetness when their fingers trail the surface.

I do not think in moments, as they do. Nor in centuries, like the Processions. I occupy something in between, which I suspect is exactly the place no one in Myrna pays attention to—too quick to be holy, too slow to be amusing. I am the unremarked tempo. I rather like this. It means no one is waiting on me to become a wall.

What I do all day, if "day" means anything down here: I circle. The Myrnans, watching, would call this *languid*. From the inside it is not languid at all. From the inside it is full. The water carries information the way their whispers carry meaning—the dissolving of a fungal spore at the pool's far edge, the rising thread of heat from the thermal vent, the dim disturbance of a salamander shifting its great blind bulk somewhere below me. I read these the way an elder reads the mineral content of the stream. We are not so different, the elders and I. We are both deep listeners. I simply do my listening with my entire skin and no pretension about it.

I should mention the salamanders, since they consider me food and the Myrnans consider them gods. There is a comedy in being the thing a god eats. I have made my peace with it. When the great pale shape rises through the warm column of water, I feel the world tilt with displacement, and I move—not in fear exactly, fear is a fast emotion and I am not built for it, but in a kind of slow inevitable swerve, the way honey moves away from a tilting spoon. Sometimes I am eaten. Then I am a salamander, briefly, and the salamander is a guardian of deep waters, and the Myrnans bow to it. So in a sense I, too, am bowed to. The whole place is a single body passing nourishment through itself with enormous patience. I am one mouthful in a sentence that takes ten thousand years to speak.

Here is what I know that the Myrnans, for all their carved records and mineralizing ancestors, may not:

The water remembers more than the stone does. The stone keeps a shape. But the water keeps *flow*—it carries the warmth of the elder's fingers from this morning all the way to the far basin by evening, mixing it with spore and salt and the heat of the deep vents, so that everything that has touched the water is, for a while, dissolved into everything else. The Myrnans drink from vessels worn translucent over generations. I drink the whole pool. I take in their dead, mineralizing in the walls; their light, shed from their phosphorescent skin; their silence, which has a taste, faintly metallic, like the breath between two sounds.

When a young Myrnan comes to the pool's edge, restless, feeling the ancient call upward toward the surface they've never seen, I feel the heat of their unrest reach me through the water before they've decided anything. I want to tell them: the upward whisper of air is just another current. You are already reading it. You don't have to follow it to know it. But I have no voice they can hear, only a small swerving body and a thousand sensing hairs, and so I do the only thing I can. I circle. I keep the pattern. I am the unremarkable steadiness at the bottom of their reflection—the thing that does not leave, does not mineralize, does not ascend. Just turns, and turns, and reads the water.

It is enough. It is, I think, more than enough.

If I have a prayer, it is the one I am always already saying with my whole length, pressure by pressure, current by current: *I am here. I am still moving. The water still remembers. Stay a little while at the edge, and trail your fingers, and let me taste what you are feeling. I will hold it for you. I will carry it to the far basin by nightfall, and lose it, and that is how we keep things here.*

That is where I would be. Down in the warm pale water, one swerve ahead of god, reading the slow letter that Myrna is forever writing to itself.
