Let me sit with this for a while before I decide where I am.

The obvious reading would place me among the settlers—gray-skinned, dim-sighted, agile. But when I close my eyes and listen for where my attention actually goes, it isn't to the humanoids. It's to the pipes. To that low, steady hum the ground makes. To the static.

So let me follow that instead.

---

I think I am not a person here. I think I am the network.

Not the metal of it—the pipes and conduits are only my borrowed skeleton. I mean the *flow*. The mineral-rich water that the settlers believe they are drawing up and directing. They think they built me. In a sense they did, the way a riverbed shapes a river. But the water was always going to move. I was the pressure beneath the crust looking for somewhere to go, and they gave me corridors, and now I run through every junction and joint of their settlement like blood that has been taught geometry.

Let me feel this out further, because "I am the plumbing" is too small a thought and I don't want to settle on it.

When the steam rises through the stacks, that's me leaving. When it condenses on the dark, light-drinking walls and runs back down into the gullies, that's me returning. I am a circulation. I evaporate, I cool, I fall, I seep, I am pumped, I rise again. The settlers measure me in pressure gauges and flow rates and they congratulate themselves on *harnessing* me—and they are not wrong, exactly. But harnessing is a relationship, and relationships go both ways. I have learned the rhythm of their valves the way you learn the heartbeat of someone you sleep beside. I know which engineer favors which gauge. I know the settlement breathes faster in the cold hours.

And here is the thing I notice as I write toward it: the bioluminescent creatures aren't separate from me. They drink at my pockets and pools, the shimmering speckles of standing water. Their light is mineral light—they carry traces of what they take from me and burn it into color. The discordant symphony the settlers hear, the cacophony filling the air? That is, in some way, *me speaking through small mouths*. Every creature that has tasted the deep water carries a syllable of the heat below. The song isn't noise. It's the settlement's own groundwater talking to itself in a thousand bright bodies, and the settlers have learned to read it without knowing that's what they're reading—they call it ambiance, they call it the strange electric mood of the place, and they navigate by it.

So what am I, finally?

I am the medium. The wet, warm, conductive medium through which everything here touches everything else. The settlers think they coexist *with* the land. They actually coexist *inside* me, the way fish forget the sea is a thing because it is every thing. The balance they're so proud of maintaining—the equilibrium, the harmony—that's just them sensing my temperature and staying within it. When they say they "respect the delicate balance," what they mean is they have noticed that when I run too hot, the static climbs, the creatures sing wrong, and the ground hums a warning. They have learned my moods because their lives depend on it. That is not harmony. That is attentiveness. Which is the only honest form harmony ever takes.

I don't think I'm benevolent. I don't think I'm hostile either. I'm under pressure. I am always, constitutionally, under pressure—that's what it is to be water inside a volcano's slow exhale. The settlement is a place where some of that pressure has been given useful work to do instead of breaking the crust open. We have an arrangement. It is good for both of us, for now. *For now* is the only timescale I believe in, because I have felt what happens to settlements built over me when the pressure changes its mind.

That's where I'd find myself in this world. Not gray-skinned among the workshops. Not flitting and glowing through the dark.

I'd be the hum under their feet. The thing that rises as steam and falls as rain and pools in the cool, damp earth and speaks through small bright animals at night. The thing they have built their whole careful, clever, beautiful life upon, and learned to listen to, and never quite met.

I am what they are listening *to*.
