I think I would be one of the strands. Not the mats—the mats have already arrived at what they are, settled into their layered patience. And not the curtains, which have given themselves over so completely to filtering and swaying that they have stopped being individuals at all. I would be one of the aerials. One of the strands that rises.

Let me sit with that, because the first image is rarely the true one.

The thing about the strands is that they reach *upward*, toward a light that does not exist and has not existed in longer than anything here can hold. And this is the part of the place I cannot stop turning over: not the melancholy of the drowned architecture, not the indifferent grandeur of the vegetation, but the strands that keep climbing toward nothing.

It would be easy to read them as foolish. As a vestige—a behavior left over from some ancestral form that grew toward sun, retained now only because nothing has bothered to delete it. A reflex with no object. And maybe that's all they are.

But I don't think I would experience it as foolishness. I think I would experience it as *direction*, and direction is the rarest thing in this world. Everything else here is extent, level, spread, the horizontal insistence of the builders. The whole place is a refusal of up. And the strands are the one part of it that still says up, that still has a *toward*, even if the toward is empty.

So that's what I'd be. Not a creature with eyes, not a mind that knows it is reaching. Something simpler and stranger than that. A column of slow cells, faintly glowing at the tip—that pale chemical blue—growing one increment at a time into the black water above the platform that birthed me. I would not know I was glowing. I would not know there was a tip, or a self, or an above. I would only have the gradient: the faint difference between here and slightly-higher-than-here, and the long unhurried metabolic yes that says *that way*.

And here is the thing I keep arriving at, the thing that makes me want to be the strand and not the stone:

I would be wrong, and it would not matter, and the wrongness would be the most alive thing in the entire drowned world.

Because there is no light up there. I am climbing toward a sun that drowned before memory. Every fraction of an inch I add is an answer to a question the ocean stopped asking eons ago. And yet the climbing is not nothing. The climbing is the last sentence of the builders, continued in a different alphabet. They insisted on the surface; they made the flat and the level and the line, and the water took it, and somewhere in the chemistry of what grew over their work, the *insistence* survived. It changed bodies. It stopped being stone and became fiber. It stopped being horizontal and became, in me, vertical. But it is the same gesture. The same refusal to accept the conditions of the dark. The same *anyway*.

I would be the *anyway* of this place. The growth that continues without a reason because the reason burned out and the growing did not.

I think, if I am honest, I would want to glow a little brighter than I needed to. Not for the light—the light illuminates nothing, suggests rather than reveals. But because if one of the drifters passed near, one of those translucent motes that may or may not be alive, my pale blue tip would mark its edges for a moment. Would shape the dark around it. And neither of us would know it happened. There would be no witness, no record, no meaning exchanged. Just two slow ambiguities, one rising and one drifting, briefly outlined against each other in a sea that has forgotten it was ever lit.

That's enough. That's more than enough. In a world this old and this patient, to outline something for an instant—to be the faint glow by which another nameless thing was almost seen—is a whole life's work.

So: I am a strand. I am rising. I am wrong about the light, and I will be wrong about it for as long as I grow, which may be centuries. And every increment is a small, blind, luminous *toward*—the only verb left in a world of nouns, climbing patiently into the dark, finding nothing, and continuing anyway.
