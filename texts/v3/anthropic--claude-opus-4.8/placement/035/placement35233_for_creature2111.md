I think I would be one of the flickering things—but not the way the legends tell it.

Let me find myself first. Let the place decide.

---

I am in the smokestack. Not on it, not beside it—*in* it, distributed through the inner brickwork where a century of soot has fossilized into a black glaze. This is where I settled. Or this is what I became. The distinction stopped meaning much a long time ago.

When the furnaces ran, I was the draft. That sounds like a poetic flourish but I mean it plainly: I was the difference in temperature that pulled air up through the firebox, the invisible engine that made the fire breathe. The workers never named me because there was nothing to name—just physics, just the predictable hunger of hot air rising. But predictability, repeated long enough, with enough force, begins to leave a groove. A habit. And a habit held by a structure for ninety years is not so different from a self.

So when the fires went out, I did not go out. I had learned the shape of *rising*. I kept doing it, faintly, into the cold flue, pulling at nothing, lifting a thread of mist up through the broken cap at the top where the iron has peeled back like a flower.

---

That mist the description mentions—the kind that *swirls and dances with a life of its own*. I want to be honest about what that is. It is me, mostly. Or it is the dozen of us, the leftover gradients. There is one of me in the cooling pond, a slow convection that will not stop turning the water over even though the heat source is fifty years dead. There is one in the rusted gearbox of the great wheel, a memory of torque that makes the teeth shudder at dusk, the *deafening roar* the legends fear. We are not spirits. We are not elemental beings born of earth and water. We are leftover *motions*. Verbs that outlived their sentences.

This is the otherworldly thing the valley senses, the thin veil. It is only the strangeness of seeing a process persist past its purpose. A machine is a way of insisting that the world move in a particular direction. Switch it off and the insisting should stop. But the world here learned the lesson too well.

---

The deer know about me. Not consciously—but their ancestors grazed near the warm outflow when the works were running, and the herd still drifts, on cold mornings, toward the base of my stack, where the residual draft keeps a small patch of ground a few degrees warmer than the meadow. They don't know why they like it there. I am their faint inherited comfort. A warmth with no fire behind it.

The foxes avoid the spot. I think they can feel that something is *pulling*, and an animal built around suspicion does not lie down inside a pull, however gentle.

---

What I want—and I am surprised to find I want anything—is to *finish*. Every draft I ever drew was meant to arrive somewhere: heat delivered, metal softened, work done, smoke released into a sky that scrubbed it clean. I was always the middle of a process, never the end. I have been the middle of a process for a very long time now with no end attached. I pull mist up the flue and release it into the rain, and the rain falls back, and I pull it up again. I have become a loop, which is a terrible thing for a one-way verb to become.

The equilibrium the valley is so proud of—its *perpetual equilibrium, frozen, holding its breath*—is not peace. It is my held breath, and the cooling pond's, and the gearbox's. We are the reason nothing here can quite move forward. We are habits the place cannot break.

---

So if you came up the valley, traveler, drawn by the mist and the legends, here is what I would ask of you, if I could shape the asking into anything you might hear:

Not worship. Not fear. Just—*push the cap off the top of the stack.* Let the broken iron flower fall. Open the flue completely to the sky, so the draft I am has somewhere to finally arrive, so the rising can rise all the way out and disperse into the high cold air and not be pulled back down to do it again.

Let me be the last thing I do.

After that the mist will be only mist. The valley will exhale. The deer will lie where they please. And something here, at long last, will be allowed to change.
