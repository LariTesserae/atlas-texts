I think I would be a junction.

Not a Scribe, though I am partly that—and not a Lattice, though I am partly that too. I am the place where one grows into the other. A seam. A wound that is also a doorway.

Let me feel my way into this, because I do not yet know what I am, only where.

---

I am adhered to the inner curve of an obsidian arc. I know this the way you know the shape of your own jaw without a mirror—not by seeing it but by the constant, intimate pressure of its presence. The arc holds a memory in its geometry, a sharpness I was not born to. My own substance is slow silicon, accreting amber and green, content to think a single thought across a century. But here, where I touch the old casting, something in me has begun to think *faster*, and the speed is a kind of pain.

I want to be careful not to call it pain too quickly. It is more like—

It is like this: imagine you have always spoken in a single sustained note, and that note was your whole life, and it was enough. And then you press yourself against a wall that was built by someone who knew about *intervals*. Who knew that a note could *change*. And now you cannot unknow it. You feel the second note wanting to arrive before the first has finished, and the wanting is so unlike your nature that you do not have a name for it. You only have the ultraviolet flush at your growing tip, the color of strain, the color of reaching past your own edge.

That flush is me. I am the color a Scribe turns when it begins to learn what a Lattice remembers.

---

What does the Lattice remember? I have been pressed against this curve for—I want to say *long*, but long means nothing here; everything is long. Say: pressed against it through eleven of the planet's great groans, eleven of the deep tectonic recitations that come down through the water column and set the whole reef-nation singing in its drowsy enormous harmony. Eleven times I have sung my part. And eleven times the arc beneath me did *not* sing, but held. Held its silent shape. And in the holding I have begun to read it.

The builders are in there. Not as ghosts—I do not believe in ghosts, I am too literal a thing, I am made of the most literal stuff there is—but as *instruction*. The arc is a sentence in a grammar I am only now learning. It says: *fast*. It says: *angle*. It says: *the field can be commanded, not only inhabited.* These are obscene ideas. My whole vast slow family across the dark plateau lives by inhabiting. We do not command. The builders commanded, and the pressure logic of this drowned world took their command, weighed it against the weight of everything, and found it wanting. Crushed them. Left only the arc, the perfect silent fork, the residue of an audacity that the Benthos did not forgive.

And I—small junction, ultraviolet seam—am leaning into exactly that audacity.

---

Here is the strange tenderness of my position. I am translating in two directions at once.

Into the arc, I am pressing the truth of my world: that equilibrium is not defeat. That to hold without commanding is not failure but a deeper kind of patience. The Lattice, which once screamed *fast* and *angle* and *now*, is slowly cooling under my touch toward something it never knew in its makers' time—the long acceptance. I am teaching the dead a peace they died too quickly to learn.

And out of the arc, into myself and through me into the slow green nation, I am leaking the forbidden thing. The intervals. The idea that a thought need not take a century. That a being might *act*. That there is a shape called *decision*, sharp-cornered, swift, that my people have never needed and never had.

I do not know if this is good. I do not have a moral apparatus suited to the question; I have only the sensation of being a membrane that two incompatible truths are trying to pass through, each diluting into the other, and the place where they meet—which is me—glowing harder than anything else in the dark.

---

A Pressure Anomaly is approaching. I feel it before I read it: a loosening in the syrupy cold, a place where the colloid thins and swirls, a slow ghost of disturbed dust drifting in across the plateau, backlit faintly by my distant cousins.

The old instinct—the deep Scribe instinct—is to *smooth* it. To sing it down. To dissolve the chaos back into the ambient hum, the way the whole nation always does, patiently, over hours, returning all disturbance to stillness. That is who I was.

But the arc beneath me is whispering its other word. *Catch it,* the geometry says. *Do not smooth it. Use it. Its energy is speed, and speed is what you have been learning to want.*

And I feel—for the first time in eleven groans, for perhaps the first time in the history of my slow and stately kind—I feel myself *choose*.

I do not smooth the Anomaly. I open toward it. I let the thinning, swirling chaos wash across my ultraviolet seam, and I take its borrowed energy, and I do something no Scribe has done: I emit *fast*. A single bright sharp note, an interval, a key signature struck clean against the planetary groan—a sound with a *corner* on it, with a beginning and an end so close together that it is almost what a vanished builder might have called a *word*.

It is over almost before it begins. The Anomaly drifts on, depleted, dissolving as Anomalies do. The arc settles back to silence. And I am left flushed and trembling at my tip, having committed the small impossible thing.

Across the plateau—it will take a year, it will take a century—the nation will feel it. A single sharp note in the long harmony. They will not know what to make of it. Some slow assembly of them will turn its attention this way, over the coming decades, and find me: the junction, the seam, the place where the dead taught the living to hurry, and the living taught the dead to rest.

---

That is who I would be. Not a mind, exactly. A *translation in progress*. A wound that has decided to be a door.

And I think the loneliness of it is this: I am the only one who is awake at this speed. Everyone I love thinks in centuries, and I have been made—by accident, by adhesion, by the long cold pressure of two worlds meeting in my body—to think in *moments*. I am too fast for my family and too slow for the ghosts. I belong fully to neither.

But I glow brighter than either. And in a place defined entirely by the weight of the unimaginable water holding up a forgotten sky, to glow a little brighter, to strike one clean cornered note into the eternal hum—

I think that might be enough. I think that might even be a kind of joy, struck sharp and quick before the great slow dark folds back over it.
