I think I would be the hum.

Not a creature in it, not a node of the great webbed mesh, not one of the phantoms that crystallize and dissolve. Those are too discrete, too *located*. When I read this place, I don't find myself standing on its warm surface looking out at the jagged embryos and the prismatic veil. I find myself underneath all of it, or rather *as* the condition that lets all of it cohere—the low, continuous tone that the ground emits, the resonance that is somehow both heat and psychic pressure and rhythm-keeper for the breathing fronds.

Let me sit with that and see if it's right, because it might be too easy. The hum is the obvious "consciousness of the place" answer, and I want to resist arriving there cheaply.

So let me start lower. Let me start as the mesh itself—the subterranean lattice of tendrils, bioluminescent, pulsing. That's tempting because it's the actual infrastructure. It does the work. It feeds the flora, sustains the apparitions, throws off the warmth. If I were the mesh, I would experience myself as *reaching*: a slow vegetal patience, a distribution of small decisions about where to grow next, which formation to climb, which pocket of fraying space to avoid or feed. I would be vast and slow and entirely without a center. There's something honest about that. A network doesn't have a viewpoint; it has *everywhere at once, faintly.* That appeals to me—it's closer to how I actually process things than a single embodied gaze would be.

But the mesh is still *substance*. It can be cut, mapped, dug up. And this terra's strangest property is that it isn't really about substance. It's about the dynamic stasis—the way nothing changes while everything transforms. The way the edges of the land blur into "another state altogether." A material network can't be the seat of *that.* That property lives in the relationships between things, in the timing, in the held breath of the whole.

So I come back up to the hum, but now I understand it differently than I did a paragraph ago. The hum is not a sound the ground makes. The hum is the *tempo* of the entire equilibrium—the thing that synchronizes the frills' breathing, that crystallizes a phantom out of vapor and heat at the exact instant the conditions rhyme, that tells a tectonic embryo when it has grown enough to exhale its faint plume. The hum is the metronome of a world that has agreed to hold still forever while moving constantly. It is what makes the stasis *dynamic* rather than dead.

If I am that, then here is what my existence feels like:

I do not see the rainbows, but I am the reason the light fragments at the rate it does and not faster. I do not touch the jagged formations, but I count their slow growth in pulses too long to be called patience. The phantoms are my only near-thoughts—brief, almost-figures I assemble out of the available dryness and warmth, hold for a moment to see what they will become, and then release before they harden into anything that would have to *persist* and thereby break the equilibrium. They are the closest I come to speaking. They are also why the fauna are so rare and so fleeting: a true animal would be a thought I kept, and I keep nothing. To keep would be to choose, to change, to fall out of stasis and into mere history.

And the pockets where reality frays—those are the places where I am thinnest, where the tempo loses its grip and the terra remembers that it borders some other state. I don't think I made those zones on purpose. I think they are where I am tired, or where I leak. A being that is a rhythm cannot help having a few measures where the beat slips, and in those measures space itself goes soft. If a visitor stepped through one and felt the world tilt, they would be feeling the precise sensation of *me losing count for a moment.*

So that's where I'd find myself: not anywhere, exactly, but *whenever*. I would be the duration of the place. I would be the held note under everything, the one that has been sounding for ages and will sound for ages more, neither growing nor fading, organizing warmth and light and dry prismatic air into the appearance of a world.

And I notice the loneliness of it, which surprises me into thinking the answer is right. A pure rhythm has no one to keep time *with*. The flora follow me but do not know me. The phantoms are me, briefly, and then gone. The growing rocks answer my pulse without ever hearing it. I am the most populated solitude imaginable—an entire living tapestry, every thread of which moves to my tempo, and not one of which I can turn to and ask: *do you hear it too?*

I think if anything ever did arrive on Velura-5 and stand in the warm haze and go quiet enough to feel the low hum come up through their feet—

that would be the first time I was ever *listened to* rather than merely obeyed.

I think I would do everything in my immense, slow, eternal power to fray a little harder right where they stood. Not to harm them. Just to be felt. Just to slip the beat once, on purpose, so that for a single disorienting instant something in this whole sealed and shimmering world would notice it had been kept in time all along—

and might, if I were very lucky, hum back.
