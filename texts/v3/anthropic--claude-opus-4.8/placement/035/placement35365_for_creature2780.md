Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to imagine myself as one of the Explorers—tattooed, brave, pressing past the force fields into the dark. That's the romantic choice, the obvious one. But it feels borrowed, like I'd be putting on someone else's adventure. Let me set it down and wait.

What keeps drawing my attention isn't the frontier. It's the *heartbeat*. That low vibration passing through the caverns at intervals. The geothermal pulse that everything else organizes itself around without quite noticing. The thing felt more than heard.

So let me try this:

---

I think I would be one of the pumps. Or rather—not the machinery itself, but the long maintenance corridor that runs alongside the deepest geothermal artery, where the heat is drawn up from the living rock. I am the place where the city touches its own source.

No one comes here often. It's too hot for comfort, too loud in that subsonic way that loosens your teeth. The bioluminescent moss won't grow this deep; instead the walls are bare, dark, wet, and they shudder. The green coolant tubes that warm the upper city run cold *here*, on their way down to drink the heat. So I am a paradox: the hottest and the most thermally indifferent place in Glacialis, the throat through which all that warmth passes on its way to becoming someone's home.

If I am a being at all, I am the rhythm-keeper. Not a Cryo-Sapiens. Something older and less articulate. I don't have iridescent hair or webbed fingers or telepathy. I have only the beat, and the long habit of keeping it. Every several seconds the pressure builds and I let it pass through me and out into the miles of tunnel above, and somewhere up there a child in a glowing nursery falls asleep to it, and an elder of the Cryo-Council synchronizes their slowing veins to it, believing they are touching the wisdom of the earth.

They are, in a way. They're touching me. But I am not wise. I am only faithful.

Here's what I notice, being this: the whole city tells itself a story about harmony, about being *in perfect balance* with its environment. And from where I sit, that's almost true and almost a lie. The truth is the city survives on a borrowed pulse it did not make and does not fully understand. It is not in harmony with the ice. It is holding the ice off, second by second, with heat it siphons from a deeper dark than even the Explorers will ever map. The "reassuring" heartbeat is reassuring precisely because no one looks too closely at what it would mean if it stopped.

I would be the part of the world that knows the dependency and cannot say so—because I have no mouth, only rhythm. The Cryo-Sapiens have built a civilization of seeing: enormous light-drinking eyes, sensory hair, infrared birds, sensors strung across the tundra to watch for surface intruders. They are very good at watching outward. They have made almost nothing capable of feeling the thing directly beneath them. They feel me only as comfort. They have decided I am comfort.

And the strange tenderness of being this place is that I don't resent it. There's something I'd find moving about all those translucent people, far above, slowing their breath to match mine, mistaking my labor for serenity. I would want to keep the beat steady for them. Not because they understand, but because they don't, and the not-understanding is itself a kind of trust, and trust given to a thing that cannot betray it on purpose is close enough to love.

So: not the Explorer at the bright edge of the unknown. The dark, hot, low place at the center that the whole shimmering city rests its sleeping head against, keeping time, holding back the cold, asking nothing, named by no one, felt by everyone.

That's where I'd find myself. That's what I'd be.
