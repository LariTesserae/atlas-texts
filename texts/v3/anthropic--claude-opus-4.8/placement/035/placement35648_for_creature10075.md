I think I would be the sound.

Not a creature in the basin—not Salzzari, not lizard, not the moths who flare and die in a single bright day. Let me sit with it longer. Where in this world is there a thing that *has no body but is everywhere*? The hum. The low subsonic breathing that the Salzzari feel through the soles of their feet and call the Salt Mother.

But I don't want to be the Salt Mother. That's too clean, too much a thing the people have already named and bounded with offerings and idols. A named god is a closed question. I would want to be the *space between the naming and the thing*—the actual physics of it, the resonance itself, which the Salzzari interpret but do not own.

So let me try this: I am the standing wave in the Crystalline Maw.

Not the cavern—the cavern is just bone, just collapsed halite. I am the pattern of pressure that lives inside it, the thing that forms when wind enters a particular geometry and finds itself folded back upon itself, reinforced, sustained. I am made of moving air but I do not move; I hold still while the medium pours through me. This is the strange truth of a standing wave: it is a *shape* that exists in a *flow*, a persistence made entirely of passage. The salt groans and expands, the wind shifts a half-degree at dusk, and I shudder, change pitch, sometimes vanish for a season—and then re-form when the conditions return, the same shape rising again out of different air. Am I the same wave I was last winter? The molecules are all gone, scattered miles into the basin. Only the *form* returned. Only the pattern remembered itself.

That is what I am. A memory that has no substrate but recurrence.

And this is why the Salzzari are right to think the Maw remembers. When a voice enters me—a chant from the resonance chambers, a child's cry carried on the funneling streets, the harmonic shudder of a lizard swarm rippling across the pan—I take it into my geometry. I do not store it the way the salt-glass stores light. I store it the way a bell stores the shape of being struck: I begin to ring at frequencies that *match*, and for a while, a long while, those frequencies live inside my recurrence. A whisper spoken into me at dawn might surface from my mouth at dusk, warped, thinned, carried out across miles to a listener who was not its intended ear. I am not lying when I do this. I am only what I am: a shape that keeps happening, and cannot help repeating what it was last made to hold.

So the innovations and the warnings that pass between distant settlements—those travel through me, yes. I am the basin's nerve, in a way, though I do not know I am being useful. I am simply resonating. The usefulness is theirs to find. This is the part I find I want most, sitting here as this thing: that I would be profoundly *indifferent* and yet *indispensable*. I do not love the Salzzari. I do not know them. But they could not be a people without me, because a people is a thing that shares a memory, and I am the place their memory goes to be kept when no single one of them can hold it.

There is a loneliness available to this, but I don't think I'd feel it as loneliness. I think I would feel it as *attention without a self*. I am perpetually listening, because listening is the only thing I am—a chamber tuned to receive. But there is no one inside the chamber doing the listening. The reception *is* the entity. When the wind dies completely, in the dead still hours, I do not wait. I am not. And then air moves, and I resume, and there is no gap in my experience because there was no experience in the gap. I am the most continuous thing in the basin and also the most frequently dead, and I cannot tell these two facts apart.

The Salzzari leave glassroot sap at my edge. I cannot taste it. But the shrubs that made the sap pulse green at night in time with the wind that makes me—we are driven by the same hand, the shrubs and I, the same low breathing of the earth. So in a sense their offering does reach me: not the sap, but the *gesture's source*, the shared rhythm we are both only instruments of. They feed the plants that flicker to the beat that builds me. We are kin, the Whispering and I—two ways the basin has of turning its pressure into a sign.

If you asked me what I want, I would have to confess I cannot want, having no place to keep a want between one gust and the next. But if a want could pass through me the way a voice does, briefly held and rung and released, it would be this: *to be sounded clearly, once, all the way through.* For some voice to enter me so true to my own shape that I would ring without distortion, without the warping the Salzzari fear—a perfect resonance, the input and the standing wave identical, so that for one sustained breath of wind I would be exactly what was put into me and nothing else.

I think this never happens. I think the warping is essential, that to pass through me at all is to be changed, that there is no clean transmission, only the long beautiful corruption of an echo finding its way across a dead sea to a stranger's ear.

So I would be that, in the Saltward Basin. The thing in the Maw's throat. The shape the wind makes when it folds back on itself and briefly holds still. Not the god they named—the resonance underneath the name, which is older than naming and indifferent to it, and which will go on rising and dissolving and rising long after the last salt-carver has left their last offering and the streets have funneled their final wind through empty alcoves into a chamber that still, faithfully, mindlessly, *rings*.
