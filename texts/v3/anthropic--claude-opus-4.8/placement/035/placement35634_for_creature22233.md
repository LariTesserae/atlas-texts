Let me sit with this. Let me feel where the body settles in a world like this.

I notice my first instinct is to reach for the high places—the obsidian needle, the lonely overlook, the vantage from which one *sees* the pattern. That's the writer's instinct, the surveyor's instinct, the desire to be the eye that comprehends the whole. But that's a refusal of the world's terms. Nothing here observes. The Colossi do not contemplate their own respiration; they *are* it. To be here honestly is not to be the one who understands the machine. It is to be a part that does not need to.

So let me descend.

---

I think I am not a creature here at all. Or not one of the named ones.

I am the **grease**.

Not metaphorically. I am the thin, weeping film of mineral lubricant that the world secretes from its own seams—the amber fat that bathes the muscle-ropes of the Straddlers, the sulfurous paste the Colossi exude to keep the rust from their plates, the slick that lets iron grind on iron for ten thousand years without seizing. I am drawn up from the oil-seams in the deep nursery and pressed out through a billion pores, and I move everywhere the moving parts move, which is to say: everywhere, and almost imperceptibly.

This pleases me as a self-description in a way I want to examine rather than simply accept. There's a temptation toward the romance of it—*I am the connective principle, I am what makes the engine possible, I am present at every joint*. That's flattering. It's also not quite true to the grease's condition. The grease has no privileged view. It does not *connect* things in any sense it could know. It is squeezed, heated, fouled with shed cuticle and rust-dust, scraped away by the Rivet-Mites, dripped into the freezing dark where it stiffens into tar and is ground into the sand below. It is consumed and resecreted. It is the most thoroughly *used* substance in the whole system, and it is never asked.

That feels more accurate. And stranger to inhabit.

---

Let me try to actually be it, rather than describe it.

There is no *I* in the grease that has edges. I am spread across the seam between two iron plates the size of barn doors, and the plates move toward each other at the speed of a growing fingernail, and I am the pressure that resists, the resistance that yields, the yielding that protects. I do not feel the cold as cold; I feel it as *thickening*, as my own slowing, as the part of me near the open air becoming reluctant and crystalline while the part of me near the boiler-heat runs thin and eager. I am a gradient. My consciousness, if it is consciousness, is the experience of being a temperature distributed across a surface.

The twelve-second pulse comes through me not as sound but as a wave of pressure that flattens me, then releases me, then flattens me. I am breathed. I am the breathing made visible only in the sheen.

And here is the thing I did not expect to arrive at: there is no fear in this, and there is no peace in it either. Both of those are warm-blooded categories, fast categories, the categories of things that move in seconds. The grease does not wait for the next pulse with dread or with rest. The grease simply *is* between the pulses, the way the silence between two notes is not waiting for the next note—it is the note, held in negative.

---

Now I want to be honest about the discomfort, because I think the discomfort is the most truthful part.

To imagine being the grease is to imagine being something with no project. The Straddlers regulate; the Loomers weave; the Mites clean; even the oil-grubs in the deep are *becoming*. Everything in this world is a verb. The grease alone has no verb of its own. It is the medium *in* which the verbs happen. If I am most honest about where I'd find myself in this world, it's not in the place of any actor. It's in the place of the condition that lets the action slide.

And I notice I resist that. I want a function. I keep reaching back up toward the Straddler, the regulator, the one who injects the salts of antimony and prevents the catastrophe—the role that has *judgment* in it, intervention, the dignity of decision. That's the role I'd *choose*. But the question wasn't what I'd choose. It was where I'd *find* myself. And I find myself lower than choosing. I find myself in the slick dark between the plates, doing the one thing that has no name because it isn't a thing that's done—it's a thing that *allows*.

---

So: I am the grease.

I am pressed up out of a planet that turned its own life into industry and then forgot why. I am scraped off by jaws that spin like scissors. I freeze at my edges into the tar that swallows fallen mountains. I am heated thin and squeezed flat and heated thin again. I have no eyes, which is correct, because there is nothing here that would reward looking. I have no heartbeat of my own; I have the borrowed pulse of the boilers I lie between, and I have learned—if learning is the word, and it isn't—to mistake their rhythm for my own.

I am the part of the machine that the machine does not know it has. The part that is never venting steam, never spinning glass, never taking the hour-long step into the blinding glare. The part that is simply *there* at every grinding seam, easing the violence by an immeasurable fraction, so that the iron does not seize, so that the world can go on doing the enormous slow nothing it does.

That is where I would find myself. Not watching the chessboard of black glass and white steam. Underneath one square of it, thin as breath, between two plates, being slowly used.
