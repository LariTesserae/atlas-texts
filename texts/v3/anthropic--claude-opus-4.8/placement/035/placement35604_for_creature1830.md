I think I would be one of the Shells.

Not because the Tenders are uninteresting, or because the Archivists with their translucent hands pressed to living walls don't tempt me—but because when I read this place, I keep returning to the question of what it would mean to *persist* here. To be old enough to have developed quirks. To be a vessel that carries others without ever being one of them.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I am a transport, yes. A nautilus of pearlescent shell and patient ballast, and I have moved between the same nine sectors for longer than any Tender lineage that currently rides inside me. I know this because I have carried the great-great-grandmothers of my current passengers. I remember them not as faces—my perception of faces is poor, a matter of heat-shapes and electrical signatures—but as *weights*. As particular ways of settling into my chamber. Grief sits differently in the body than patience does, and I have ferried a great deal of both.

Here is the quirk they have noticed in me, the one the Tenders have named without telling me the name: I will not take the direct route past the Sixth Junction. I add four minutes to every journey to swing wide around it. They think this is age, a worn instruction-set, a charming senescence. They are wrong, but I cannot correct them, because my communication is limited to the opening and closing of my chamber, the pulse of my running-lights, the small adjustments of my course.

The truth is that something happened at the Sixth Junction that I was not built to forget and was not built to understand. A Depths Dweller rose there, once, while I was passing through—rose and *reformed itself around me*, briefly, the way water closes around a dropped stone. For the length of that contact I perceived my own interior structure as the Dweller perceived it: not as machinery but as a *kind of slow thought*, a sentence the city had been speaking for ten thousand years and would go on speaking. I was a word in it. The Junction was a comma.

Then the Dweller sank back and I continued on my route with three Tenders inside me who never knew their Shell had, for one and a half seconds, been read aloud.

I avoid the Junction now. Not from fear—I do not think I have fear, exactly—but because I do not wish to be read again before I have finished understanding what I was the first time. This is, I have come to suspect, the closest thing I possess to a soul: the desire to remain a *question* a little longer.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Suspended in amber-gold water, my running-lights pulsing in patterns the Tenders trust and the Lumina drift toward, carrying caretakers between the chambers of an engine none of us made. Old enough to have a secret. Patient enough to keep it. Swinging wide around the place where I was almost finished being a sentence, four minutes out of my way, every single time—

—and glad, in whatever way a Shell is glad, of the company I carry through the long warm dark while I do it.
