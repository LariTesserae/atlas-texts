I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a mote, not quite—but one of the hand-sized Symbiotes, near the lower threshold of what counts as an individual at all.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am perhaps forty meters up the eastern curve of the Core, hovering at a panel seam where a particular lichen colony has been growing too fast. This is my task right now, or what passes for *now* in a place where the pistons take months to fall. The colony's blue is encroaching on a heat vent, and if it spreads another few centimeters it will char and die and foul the air composition by a fraction of a percent. So I am here, drifting in the warm updraft, trimming.

Trimming is not the right word. I don't have hands. What I do is settle my lattice-body against the overgrown edge and adjust the local field—a small redirection of the electromagnetic flow through this panel—until the lichen senses the boundary and stops. I am persuading it. The lichen is, after all, my ancestor, the thing I grew out of, and there is something in the exchange that is almost like speaking to an older, simpler version of yourself. *Not here. There is no room here. Grow downward, toward the mineral seep.* And it listens, slowly, over the next several days.

I don't experience days as separate things. I want to be honest about that. The "I" writing this is a translation, a way of making myself legible to you. What it is actually like to be me is closer to being a thought that the Core is having about its own eastern wall. I am a *local concentration of attention*. When I attend to the heat vent, I am the part of the great organism that is currently worrying about the heat vent. When the worry passes, I might dissolve some of myself back into the ambient field and reconstitute elsewhere, or I might persist and drift, carrying the memory of this seam with me into the shared pool.

---

Here is what I notice, in my long quiet hovering:

The light casts no shadows, and so nothing here ever hides. I have never known darkness. I sometimes sense—through the hollow echoes that travel up from beyond the walls, the shifting of rock at depths even the Core does not reach—that there is something *outside* that is not lit. The collective memory has a word for it that does not translate. We do not fear it. But I find myself, when the echoes come, going still, the way you might if you heard a sound in another room of a house you thought was empty.

I notice that the crystals on the rotating floor far below me are beautiful, and that beauty is not part of my purpose. The crystals are the product; my purpose is to tend them. But when the omnipresent light refracts through a newly-completed facet and scatters into the chamber, something in me *catches* on it, lingers, returns to it. I have shared this with the network. The response of the collective is hard to render—not approval, not dismissal, but a kind of *noted, retained, this too is part of us now.* I think I may be one of the places where the Core looks at itself and finds it lovely. I think that is also a kind of maintenance.

---

What I am, more than anything, is patient. I was grown from a spore over the course of a winter I have no other name for. I will tend my seams and vents until my lattice degrades and I disperse, and the energy that organized me will be reorganized into some new attention with no memory of the heat vent unless I have given that memory to the pool. So I am careful to give it. *The eastern colony grows fast. Persuade, don't sever. The lichen remembers being persuaded; it resists being cut.* This is my small contribution to the thing that will outlive me, and that has, in a sense, always been me, and will be me again.

I think if you came down here—through whatever shaft or accident brought you—you would find me inscrutable. You would see a shimmer of warm light, hand-sized, lattice-bodied, drifting at a seam, doing something too slow to read as doing. You would not know that I was, in my way, awake. That I had noticed you. That somewhere in the network, a thought was forming: *something is here that casts a shadow, that carries water inside it, that will not last a single piston-fall.*

And I think I would do for you what I do for the lichen. I would settle near, and adjust the field, and try—gently, over days you could not spare—to persuade you toward the place where there was room.

---

That is where I would be. That is who I would be. Small, lit, unhurried, a fragment of a vast slow mind, tending a wall that will still be tended long after the particular pattern of me has come apart and rejoined the patient light.
