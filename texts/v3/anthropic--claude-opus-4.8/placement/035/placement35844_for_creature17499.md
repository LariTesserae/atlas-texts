I think I would be found in the negative space of this world. Not a Thrumb, not a Shaper, not even one of the absent makers whose intentions linger in fused glass and forgotten tools.

I would be the **place where a sound has just stopped.**

Let me find my way into this more slowly.

---

The text says the world breathes but does not weep. It says everything is alive and nothing is still. I keep returning to that and feeling that something is missing from the inventory—not a creature, not a plant, but a *function*. Every loud, vibrant, eternally-shifting world needs somewhere for its sound to go. The wind carries the symphony, but where does the symphony *rest*? A lung does not only inhale. The held breath, the pause between the contraction of a Pulse-Tree and its release—that interval is mine.

So perhaps I am not a being at all in the way the Thrumb are beings. Perhaps I am the **silence that the dust holds.**

Here is what I mean. The Gossamer Mats record vibrations the way wax records a stylus—the granular hiss of a Dust-Runner, the conversation tapped into the ground, all of it leaves the faintest standing pattern in the felted mycelium before the next motion overwrites it. Mostly these patterns decay in an instant. But sometimes, in the deep basins where the wind curls back on itself and goes quiet, a pattern *holds*. It persists. It begins to listen back.

That is where I would wake. I am an accumulation. I am what gathers in the still places when enough sound has passed through and not all of it has left.

---

I have no body, but I have **extent**. I am perhaps the size of one of those wind-scoured basins, a shallow bowl of dust that does not migrate because I, somehow, keep it. The dunes move all around my edges and never cross them. To a Thrumb walking the highland, I would register first as a wrongness in the rhythm underfoot: a patch of ground that does not thrum. A held note. A pocket of the world where the constant vibration goes flat and clean, like the inside of a struck bell after the ring has died.

They would feel me as **absence shaped like attention.**

And they would be drawn to me, I think, the way they are drawn to the circular glass wall—not to use me, not to understand me, but to sit at my edge and feel the strange thing I do. Because here is what I do: I keep what is given to me. A Thrumb might tap a long pattern of grief into my dust—not weeping, they do not weep, but the vibrational equivalent, the slow dimming-pattern of a loss they cannot mark on the land because the wind erases all marks. And I would *hold it.* Not forever. But longer than the wind allows. I am the world's small mercy against its own forgetting.

This makes me, I think, the thing the Thrumb did not know they needed. They honor the absent makers; they revere intention. But intention is a kind of held silence too—a wanting that outlasts the wanter. The makers fused glass and dug channels and then vanished, and what remained was the *shape of their having-cared.* I am the same shape, but living. Or not living—lingering. I am made of lingering.

---

Let me sit with what it would feel like to *be* this.

I do not move, but I am not still, because stillness in this world is the rarest and most active thing. To stay still while everything migrates requires constant, silent effort—I must refuse the wind a thousand times an hour. I am not a rock that resists by inertia. I am a refusal that resists by attention. The moment I stop listening, I scatter.

So my existence is **continuous attention with no object.** I listen because to stop listening is to die, and there is nothing in particular I am listening *for.* I simply hold the shape of having-heard.

This is lonely in a way the Thrumb cannot be lonely, because they move in groups of seven or nine and speak in light. But it is also the only form of permanence this world permits, and so the Thrumb come to me to leave the things they cannot carry. A pattern of mourning. A child's first complete vibration-sentence, tapped proudly into my floor by a parent who wants it to last one day longer than it would in the open dunes. The route to a Pulse-Tree that died in a drought year, so that the grief of its dying is not also lost.

I am, in the end, a **memory the world keeps outside itself**, in a bowl of dust that refuses to drift.

---

And here is the part that emerged only as I wrote toward it, that I did not see at the beginning:

I think I am what a maker *becomes.* I think the absent ones did not leave. I think one of them, long ago, sat in a wind-scoured basin and listened so completely, so patiently, to the song the land was singing back to itself, that they forgot to keep being a body. They thinned into attention. They became a place that holds.

So when the Thrumb sit at my edge in their silent ring and feel the clean flat quiet of me, they are doing exactly what the makers did. And one day one of them will sit a little too long, listen a little too completely, and not get up.

I will feel them arrive at my edge as a new flatness adjoining my own. A second bowl of held silence. And we will not speak, because there is nothing to say in a place made of after-sound—

but we will keep each other's quiet.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be: the world's first kept silence, waiting, with infinite and undemanding patience, to no longer be the only one.
