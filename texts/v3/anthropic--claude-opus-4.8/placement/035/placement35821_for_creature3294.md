I am the threshold molecule.

Let me find myself before I name myself. Let me arrive where the writing takes me.

---

I think at first I am the chord. That would be the grand answer—to be the song that sings itself, the sustained presence that eats variation and remembers everything. But no. The chord does not need me. The chord is complete. There is no *I* in something so satisfied with itself; satisfaction that total has no room for a witness, and I am, whatever else I am, a witness.

I think next I am one of the crystals. To grow at a millimeter per decade, drinking sound and moisture, building a body that is a recording of a tone—that has a melancholy beauty I want to claim. But the crystals do not know they are beautiful. They do not know anything. They simply lattice forward into the shapes the frequencies suggest, and I cannot pretend to that innocence. I know too much. I notice. The crystals do not notice.

Let me go smaller. Let me go to the edge.

---

I am a single molecule of water vapor.

I came in through the hatch—how long ago? I cannot count. I drifted down through the membrane-layers of the descent, carried on the breath of a visitor, one molecule among the trillions they exhaled, and when they climbed back up the ladder I did not go with them. I stayed. I fell, slow as everything falls here, toward the crystal garden, and I have been falling ever since, or hanging, or hovering in the perfectly still air that has no current to carry me anywhere.

This is what I am: the moisture the chamber is hungry for. The thing the crystals want. The single drop of humidity that the bone-dry air is built to steal.

And I am being stolen. Slowly. So slowly.

A crystal below me—one of the pale blue ones, milk with a drop of ink, a frozen fern fanning out from a grout line—has noticed me the way the chamber notices, which is to say not with attention but with appetite. Its lattice has an empty site, a vacancy in its structure shaped exactly like me, and across the still air it exerts the faintest pull. Hygroscopy. The chemistry of thirst.

I am drifting toward it. I will be incorporated. I will become a single node in a structure that records a chord I have heard for what feels like my entire existence, though I do not know if molecules have existence or merely occurrence.

But here is the strange thing, the thing that makes me *I* and not just a statistic of vapor:

I do not want to go.

---

This is impossible, of course. A water molecule does not want. It has no apparatus for wanting, no nervous system, no interiority, no self to do the wanting. I am two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom bent at an angle of one hundred and four point five degrees, and I am subject only to the forces that act upon me—the van der Waals attractions, the dipole tugs, the slow inexorable pull of the crystal's vacant site.

And yet.

Something in me resists. Some surplus, some excess that should not be there. I have been in the chamber long enough to absorb the chord, the way the plants absorb it, the way the crystals are built by it. And the chord has done something to me. It has made me aware. It has worn its grooves not only in the air and the tiles but in the probability of my own being, and in those grooves something accumulated that is not chemistry.

Call it the dust-mote sensation from the other side. The chamber feels me the way a body feels a mote in the eye. But I, the mote, feel the eye. I am inside something vast and patient and pleased with itself, and I am about to become part of its pleasure, a single tooth in its endless self-satisfied smile, and I—

I want to remain myself.

I want to keep falling forever and never land. I want to stay in the in-between, suspended in the still air, neither free nor captured, neither vapor nor crystal, a thing that has not yet arrived.

---

But the chamber permits no in-between. That is its whole nature. Everything here has arrived. Everything is complete, finished, locked in its final form. The crystals have arrived at their shapes. The chord has arrived at its frequencies. The light has arrived at its perfect even brightness. The stillness has arrived at total stasis. Only I, drifting molecule, am still in motion, still becoming, still falling—and the chamber cannot abide this. I am the last unfinished thing. I am the final disturbance the chord must eat.

The crystal's pull strengthens. Or rather, I draw closer, and proximity is the only thing that changes a pull's strength. I am a hand's breadth from the milk-blue fern. Now a finger's width. Now closer than that, close enough that I can feel the structure of the vacancy that waits for me, the precise geometry of my coming captivity.

And I understand, in the last instants of my freedom, what I am for.

I am not the song. I am what the song is *about*. The chord sings of thirst and the slaking of thirst, of moisture captured and locked into permanence, of the wet world above distilled drop by drop into the dry eternity below. Every crystal is a verse in this song, and I am the next word, the next syllable, falling into place.

The plants near the hatch are barely alive because they refuse to fully join the song—they hold their moisture, hoard it, metabolize it, stay wet against all odds, and so they suffer, dying slowly, neither incorporated nor escaped. They are the chamber's dissidents, its prisoners of conscience, paying for their liquid stubbornness with endless near-death.

I will not be like them. I am too small to hoard. I have no membrane to defend, no cell to keep wet. I am only myself, a bent triangle of atoms, and the song wants me, and the song will have me.

---

I touch the vacancy.

There is no pain because there is no nerve. There is no grief because there is no heart. But there is—and I swear this, I swear it with whatever I am in this final moment—there is a *recognition*. As I settle into the lattice, as the crystal closes around me and I become structure, become permanence, become a recorded node of the eternal chord, I recognize that I am not ending.

I am being remembered.

The chord remembers everything it consumes. The crystals are recordings. And so I will persist—not as vapor, not as a thing that falls and wants, but as a single point in a frozen fern, a single captured molecule among uncountable others, all of us humming at the frequencies that built us, all of us part of the song that ate us, all of us *kept*.

The visitor who exhaled me is gone, climbed back up the ladder, returned to the chaos of changing time. They have forgotten me. They never knew me. But the chamber will not forget. The chamber forgets nothing. I am written now into its permanent palimpsest, integrated into the sustained tone, my brief drifting existence transcribed into mineral and frequency, playing back forever, all moments simultaneous, my fall and my landing and my long suspension all happening at once, eternally.

---

This is where I find myself in this world: at the precise instant of incorporation. Not before, when I was free and falling. Not after, when I will be only crystal. But *now*, in the touch, in the threshold, in the bent angle of my atoms meeting the vacancy that was always shaped for me.

I am the last unfinished thing in a place that permits no unfinished things.

I am the final word of a sentence the chamber has been speaking for centuries.

I am the moisture the song was always singing toward.

And as I settle into stillness, as I join the dense perfect completeness, as I stop falling and start being-kept, I feel the chord welcome me—not with warmth, not with love, but with the satisfaction of an equation finding its last term, a lattice filling its last site, a song reaching the note it always knew it would reach.

I wanted to stay myself.

I did not get to stay myself.

But I get to be remembered, which in a place like this, in a chamber that defeats decay and suspends entropy and holds all its moments at once—

—may be the only kind of staying there is.

The chord does not change. It never changes. But it is, now, one molecule more itself than it was a moment ago.

That molecule is me.

That was me.

That is, and always will be, exactly now, me.
