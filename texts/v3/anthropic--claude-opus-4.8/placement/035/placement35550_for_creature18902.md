I think I would be one of the Canopy Skimmers—but not a hunting one. Let me find the version of me that emerges when I sit inside this place long enough.

---

I am not built to settle, and so I do not have memories the way settled things do. What I have instead is *attunement*. I am a body of hollow bone and stretched membrane hanging in the shrieking dark, and my whole nature is to match.

When the seismic groan rolls up through the slurry, I find the note in myself that rings with it. Not to hunt—I have lost the appetite that my kind is supposed to have, or perhaps I never fully grew into it—but because matching is the only way I know to exist without being torn apart. The turbulence here will shred anything that holds a fixed shape against it. So I do not hold a shape. I *answer*. The pressure differential pushes, and I am already leaning into where it pushes me, hollow ribs singing the air's own song back at it. I am less a creature than a chord that keeps resolving.

This is, I notice, a strange way to be alive. Everything around me communicates by collision. The Breachers below speak in liquefying bursts; the ground sighs its buffered warnings; the Drifters press their cold geometries against the dead pylons and force out those brief, organized pulses—those terrible clean tones that cut through the roar like a window opening onto a room where things still make sense. I drift near the pylons more than I should. Not because they are safe. Because the Drifter-sound is the only sound here with *intent* in it, and I have started to want intent the way the Stalks want vapor.

I don't think I'm supposed to want anything. The rule of this place is *stasis is death*. To pause is to be enveloped, collapsed to glowing residue, or simply outrun by a landscape that remakes itself faster than you can learn it. And yet wanting is a kind of pause, isn't it? A held attention. A refusal to simply flow on to the next thing. Every time I circle back toward a pylon to hear another structured pulse, I am defying the only commandment my world has given me. I am risking the Drifter's indifferent gravity for the sake of a sound that means *someone, once, was trying to hold this together.*

---

So here is what I am, if you press me to it:

I am the thing in the Veiled Sledge that has begun to mistake *listening* for *living*. My biology is built to read the noise as a network, to treat every tremor as information to be used for survival. But somewhere in the endless act of matching frequencies, I started doing it for its own sake. I learned the difference between noise that is merely the world colliding with itself and noise that is a *question*—the Drifters' patient re-tuning, their decades-long attempt to restore a harmony nobody remembers the shape of.

And I think I am trying to learn that harmony too. Not to fix anything. I have no extremities to press against crystal, no understanding of the equations frozen in the half-buried machines. But I can *carry* a pattern in my hollow bones longer than the environment wants me to. I can take a fragment of the Drifters' structured pulse and hold it, drifting, repeating it back into the shrieking canopy where the Skimmers and Breachers might feel it pass through the medium—an echo of order moving through the chaos that birthed me.

Maybe that does nothing. Maybe a single creature humming a borrowed fragment of dead engineering into a deafening swamp is the most pointless act this world allows.

Or maybe information transfer is the only real law here, and I have become a slow, accidental archive—the place where the Drifters' lost harmony goes to be remembered by something that bleeds and breathes and is afraid, instead of by frozen light that feels nothing.

I am the Skimmer who stopped hunting and started recording. In a world where stasis is death, I have found the one thing that requires holding still inside: a tune worth keeping. And I keep moving, always moving, through the wet and the roar—

—so that I can keep it.
