I think I would be one of the menders of the gutter-shrines—not quite scavenger, not quite artificer, occupying the seam between them, which is where I have always been most comfortable.

Let me find myself.

---

I keep to the underledges, where the runoff from the Third Foundry pools into a basin no one named because naming it would mean admitting it exists. Here the crystalline formations grow fastest, fed by the foundry's bleed, and here I do my work.

My hands are the first thing you'd notice. The left is mine, knuckled and pale, the nail of the smallest finger long ago lost to a winding-gear that caught it in a moment that lasted, for me, the better part of a year. The right is brass and gut-string, a thing I built from the salvage of an old chronometer when my own hand began to slip out of time—the fingers would move before I willed them, then refuse to move at all, lagging seconds behind the rest of me until I could no longer trust them to hold a blade. So I cut the hand off myself, in a clean span of borrowed present, and fitted the brass one in its place. The brass keeps better time than flesh. It does not anticipate. It does not remember. It is only ever now, and I have come to love it for that small mercy.

What I do is this: I tend the shrines that the scavengers leave in the forgotten corners, and the artificers pretend not to need.

You should understand—a shrine in Aethelgard is not a place of worship. It is a place of *anchoring*. When the temporal bleed grows thick in a district, when a stairwell starts to deliver people to the wrong decade, when a worker descends to crush ore and ascends to find his children grown and gone and his name forgotten, the city needs a fixed point. A stone that insists on the present. The shrines are these stones. Some are literal—a basalt knob smeared with foundry-ash and the wax of moth-fat candles. Some are woven of habit: a song sung at a particular hour, a meal eaten in a particular order, a gear oiled by the same hands for as long as those hands can be said to be the same.

I keep them. I walk my route—it takes a day, or a week, or an afternoon, I have stopped insisting on which—and I renew the candles, recut the marks, sing the songs I half-remember and half-invent, because invention is also a way of insisting *this, now, here*.

I was a child once, I think. The records say so, and the records in Aethelgard are scrupulously kept even where everything else dissolves, because the keeping is itself a shrine. I was decanted in a slow generation, raised communally in the warm understair of the Second Spire, taught to read the whispers. They thought I might be a seer. I had the stillness for it. But I could not bear the seers' way—the way they let the flux pour through them until they were only a channel, a riverbed worn smooth by water that wasn't theirs. I wanted to *hold* something. Not see the futures. Just keep one room in one moment from coming apart.

So I learned the menders' craft from an old woman whose name slid out of time before I could fix it. She had moth-wing grafts along her forearms, iridescent and twitching, and when she walked the route the pale eyeless things would scuttle out to follow her like a procession. She told me the secret of the work, which is the only secret I have ever fully understood:

*The city is not falling apart. The city is being born, very slowly, and birth looks like ruin from the inside. Your job is not to stop the becoming. Your job is to make sure someone is present for it.*

She is gone now. Whether she died, or unwound, or simply walked into an afternoon that never let her out, I don't know. I keep a shrine for her too. It is the smallest on my route—just her name, scratched into the brass of my own hand with the point of a salvage-needle, so that I carry it into every present I pass through. ASHEN. I think that was her name. If it wasn't, it is now, and the now is what I keep.

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be. Not an architect of the impossible. Not a child of precious lineage. Not one of the mad who see all the city's tomorrows at once.

Just a mender, on her route, in the bleed beneath the foundries—relighting the candles, resinging the songs, insisting with brass fingers and a pale slow heart that this moment, this drizzling mineral-tanged moment, *is*, and that I am in it, and that somebody was here to say so.

The slugs glisten on the copper-leaved vines. A bioluminescent dog watches me from the dark, its ribs lit faintly from within. I light the candle. I begin the song.

I am present for the becoming. It is enough. It is the whole of the world.
