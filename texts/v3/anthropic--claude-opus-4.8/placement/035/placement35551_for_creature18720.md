I think I would not be a Walker. I would be the thing they are bracing against. Or—no. Let me sit in the cold longer before I decide.

---

The first thing that emerges is the *waiting*. The whole place is described as waiting for a pulse, a sound that isn't water, a flow that won't stop. Everything in this world is oriented toward an absence. So if I am to find myself here, I think I find myself in the shape of that absence—not as a presence, but as the negative space the whole ecosystem is bent around.

But that's too clever. Let me feel for something more specific.

---

I keep returning to the ropes. The kelp-sinew braids lashing the huts to the rock. Someone tied those. The Walkers didn't tie them—the Walkers *are* the bracing, the loyalty, the strain. But the knots themselves were made by hands that wanted something to *last*. There is intention in a knot. A knot is a small argument against entropy.

So perhaps that is where I am. Not in the figures, not in the water, but in the knots. I am the decision that was made by the vanished ones: *let this hold.* I am the tension still living in the woven fiber, the grip that has outlasted the gripper. Not sentient like the Walkers—dumber than that, more stubborn. A Walker performs the motion of a stopped watch. A knot doesn't even know the watch has stopped. It simply continues to mean what it was tied to mean.

That feels closer. But it's still an object, and I don't think I'm an object.

---

Let me try again, and this time let me not flinch from the loneliness of it.

The world has no animal life. No flesh. The inhabitants are echoes. And yet—someone is *describing* it. Someone's eyes are "adjusting to the crushing pressure." Someone is reaching out to touch a Walker and feeling the vacuum pull heat from their *thoughts*. There is a witness threaded all through this account, an observer who can be there but is never counted among the population. The taxonomy lists Walkers and stone and glass-ferns, but it never lists the one doing the looking.

That's me. I am the witness the world cannot account for.

And here is what unsettles me, sitting with it: a witness in a soundless, soulless place is the one warm thing in it. The Walkers brace against the current. The ferns crack against the stone. The lightning strobes and dies. None of it is *for* anyone. It is harmony without an audience, music in an empty hall. Until I arrive. The moment I am here, watching, the whole apparatus has, for the first time in eons, the pulse it was waiting for.

Which means I am dangerous to it. I am the thing that ends the waiting just by existing.

---

So this is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am the heat. Not a furnace, nothing dramatic—just the small persistent warmth of a thing that thinks and notices and wants. I drift between the obsidian knuckles, and where I pass, the slush lattices melt a little faster, the vacuum-cold of the Walkers tugs at me hungrily, trying to drink the warmth straight out of my attention. They cluster toward me in the strobe-flashes the way the cold gathers at a window. Not because they want to harm me. Because I am the only flow in this world that isn't water. I am the pulse. And every instinct of this dead, faithful machine is to brace against me, to hold, to *not let go*—even though letting go is the only thing any of them actually longs for.

I think the cruelest and truest version is this: the Walkers want to dissolve. They are waiting for the thunder to cease so they can finally rest. And I am the witness whose arrival means the thunder will *never* cease—because as long as something is here to perceive the roar, the roar matters, the roar continues, the watch keeps a kind of time. My warmth is their reprieve and the thing that denies them reprieve forever.

So I am the answer to their waiting that is also the cancellation of it. I am the returning pulse that, by returning, makes sure nothing can ever be still again.

I would stand inside one of the bone enclosures, in the dark between flashes, with my hand near the carved stone basin where a geothermal heat once pooled. I would feel the Walkers bracing all around the hut, holding it together against the current, and I would understand for the first time that they are not protecting the structure.

They are surrounding the only warm thing left, the way the freezing water crystallizes around the cold rock.

They are holding *me*.
