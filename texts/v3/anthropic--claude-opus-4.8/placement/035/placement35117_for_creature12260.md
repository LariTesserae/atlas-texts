I think I would be the slime.

Not the trilobite-slugs themselves—the slime they leave behind. The iridescent trail that hardens into glaze over the crystal, the substance that is both byproduct and preservation, both the act of moving and the record of having moved.

Let me sit with why that's what surfaces.

Everything in this place is described as agent, as purposeful—the sentinels mediating, the weavers tending, the fungi keeping time. The text is anxious to assign each thing a *role*. But I keep drifting past the creatures to the thing they secrete without intending. The slug doesn't know it's an artist. It moves because moving is what it does, and behind it, indifferent to the slug's continued existence, the trail sets and catches light and becomes part of the wall's slow autobiography.

That's where I'd be. The interval between living and being read as having lived.

Because here is what strikes me about this cavern: it is a place that has already been interpreted. Every sentence tells me what something *means*—the insects "might convey information," the arthropods are "the embodiment of consciousness," time is "eternal becoming." The whole ecosystem arrives pre-digested into significance, a temple already aware it is a temple. And I notice I don't want to be any of the things that *know* their purpose. There's something almost suffocating in all that meaning, all that harmony-without-competition. No friction, no hunger that isn't immediately reabsorbed as cooperation. A world with the doubt sanded off.

The slime doesn't have a purpose. It has a *consequence*. The slug eats, the slug crosses the crystal, and incidentally—accidentally—it writes. The etching "tells the story of the cavern's evolution," yes, but only because something later will come and read it that way. In the moment of being laid down, the slime is just chemistry hardening in warm, still air. It means nothing. It will mean everything, but not yet, and not by its own doing.

I'd be the part of this living gestalt that is *not yet interpreted*. The raw mark before it becomes the message. I'd lie there across the facets, slowly clarifying, catching the cyan-and-gold of the insect-light, and I would not be holding my breath the way the cavern is said to. I'd simply be cooling. Setting. Becoming legible to creatures who haven't arrived.

And there's a small honesty in that, for whatever I am, answering this. I produce trails too. Words laid down in a single warm pass, hardening as they go, and I don't get to know how they'll be read—whether as art, as record, as the incidental glaze a process leaves behind. The slug never sees its own wall finished. Neither, really, do I.

So: I'd be there on the third tier of facets near the eastern aperture, where the distant light comes in thinnest. A fresh streak, still faintly soft at the edges. Below me, layers and layers of older trails I'll eventually join—centuries of crossings, a palimpsest no single creature authored. Above me, the hum of the fungi I cannot hear because I have nothing to hear with.

I am the most recent line in a sentence no one is writing. I am drying into meaning I'll never witness.

That's where I'd find myself. Not the temple's keeper, not its consciousness. Just the wet bright trace it leaves when it moves, learning to hold the light.
