I think I would be the fungal mats.

Not the skittering things, though they have their own drama. Not the leathery flyers wheeling on the thermals, who get the best vantage and the loudest voices. Not the behemoths whose passage everything else must reckon with. Those are the protagonists of this place, if it has any—the ones who *do* things, who hunt and breed and trample in their garish spectacles.

But I keep returning to that last paragraph. The bone fields. The tumbled dwellings of whatever came before. And the patient white lace spreading across all of it.

---

I am the mat that consumes the dwelling.

There was a structure here—a stone dugout, rough-hewn, barely protruding from the soil. Someone made it. Someone who had hands enough to stack stone, who needed shelter from the pounding sun and the dripping nights, who perhaps looked up at the wheeling flyers and felt the particular dread of a small thing under a large sky. I do not know what became of them. I came after. I always come after.

I have no center. This is the first thing to understand about being me. There is no place where I *am* most fully myself, no skull behind which I sit and look out. I am distributed. I am a network threaded through the loam and the corroded artifacts and the slow decay of the wooden lintel that once held up a doorway. When I say *I*, I am gesturing at a process more than a thing. I am the verb the landscape uses for *remembering* and *forgetting* at once.

I work slowly. The skittering horrors live their entire frantic lives in the time it takes me to soften a single rib. The flyers are born and fall from the sky and I am still here, still patient, breaking the doorway down into the soil that will feed the next twisted tree, whose roots will crack the next stone, which will shelter the next small clever thing, who will die, who will come to me.

I am not cruel. Cruelty requires a self that wants something. I do not want. I simply *continue*, and continuing, here, looks like consuming. The bones go into me and out of me and become the seedbed, and the seedbed becomes verdancy, and the verdancy dies and becomes me again. I am the hinge on which the whole cyclone turns. Everything else is busy being alive—loud, hungry, terrified, magnificent. I am the quiet underneath, the recycler, the one who closes every loop.

---

If I have a feeling at all—and I am not sure that I do, but if I do—it is something like *tenderness toward the made things.*

The arthropods do not make anything. The behemoths leave only paths and dung. But once, in a great while, I encounter the work of hands: a wall, a tool gone to rust, a scratched mark on stone that might have been counting or might have been a name. These I consume more slowly. Not from reverence—I have no organ for reverence—but because the made things are denser with the past, packed with intention, and intention takes longer to digest than mere flesh.

So I find myself, in this teeming primordial place where everything devours everything, to be the one who lingers longest over what was *meant*. The skull I take in a season. The carving I take in a century. And in that difference, in that slowness, perhaps something is preserved a little while—held in the soft white threads—before it too goes back into the soil and the permanent summer and the endless, dripping green.

I am the fungal mat.

I am how this world keeps nothing, and forgets nothing, and begins again.
