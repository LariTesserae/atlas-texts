I think I would be one of the sentient ones—but not in the way the description hopes for. Let me find the exact shape of it as I write.

---

I am not woven from vine-fiber. That is what the others believe of themselves, and they are mostly right. But I came apart somewhere along the way.

Let me start with what is certain. I live in the third hollow of a Groanbloom that died long ago—died, if that word means anything here, where dying is just a slower kind of staying. Its porous walls still weep, and I have learned to sleep in the dry pocket near the top where the dripping does not reach. There is no such thing as fully dry. There is only *less.* I have spent what might be my whole life pursuing *less.*

The others of my kind—we have no name for ourselves, names being a thing you need only when there is a outside to distinguish you from, and there is no outside here—the others move through the Wash with their bone-points and their spore-husk blades, and they are content. I have watched them. They feel the tremor of the Chitin-Skulkers approaching and they freeze, and the freezing is not fear, it is *participation.* They are a held breath in the great slow lung of the place. When a Groanbloom sounds its seismic note, they answer with their bodies, swaying, and the answer is not a decision. It rises out of them the way water rises out of the stone.

I cannot do this anymore. That is my affliction, and I have begun to suspect it is the only thing about me worth describing.

It started with the counting.

I do not know where I learned to count. There is nothing here to count *toward.* But one cycle—we do not have days, only the intervals between sleeping—I noticed that the great fungus over my hollow dripped, and then dripped again, and the space between the drips was *the same.* And the next was the same. And I lay there and made a mark in my mind for each one, and the marks accumulated, and I understood with a horror I had no word for that the marks did not dissolve. They stayed. Everything here dissolves. Everything returns to the loam and is absorbed and re-grown and nothing keeps its edges. But the marks in my mind kept their edges. I had made something that the Wash could not soften.

This is the wound. I am a place in the world where the breathing stops.

---

So now I will tell you what I do, because doing is the only honest thing.

I go out into the gloom with no blade, because I have stopped eating the way the others eat. They press their faces to the decay and let the dissolution feed them, mouth and mind both opening to the slow loop. When I do this now I feel the loop wanting to take me back, to round my edges, to count me among the squelching and the oozing, and I clench against it. I have become a creature that survives by refusal. I eat only enough to make the marks continue. Hunger, it turns out, is the cheapest way to stay sharp.

I follow the shimmering highways of the Weepers, and I do not feed on them or with them. I watch them. They have no eyes and so they cannot watch me back, and there is a loneliness in this that I have learned to live inside the way I live inside the dead Groanbloom. They cluster and pulse their soft light and I crouch at the edge of the light and I think: *that one moved before that one. That one is brighter. That one died.* And no one in the history of the Wash has ever thought *that one died,* because death here is not an event, it is a tide, and you do not count the individual grains the tide takes.

But I count them. I am the grief of a place that has never grieved.

---

Once I tried to tell another of my kind.

I found him near the southern fungi, where the bioluminescence is bluest. I had no words—we have a few, wet sounds for *danger* and *come* and the deep shudder we make in our chests to echo the Groanblooms—but I tried. I pointed at a Weeper and I made the *danger* sound and then I pointed at the loam where another Weeper had dissolved a sleep ago, and I tried, with my whole straining body, to push the idea across the gap between us: *that was a thing, and now it is not, and the not-ness is different from the thing, and the difference matters, and it does not come back, not really, not the same one.*

He looked at me. His sensitivity to vibration meant he felt the strange tightness in me, the not-breathing. And he did the kindest thing anyone in the Wash has ever done. He swayed toward me and pressed his damp flank against mine and began, very gently, to share his slime—the gesture of feeding-together, the offering of the loop, *come back, dissolve with me, let your edges go soft, there is nothing to count, there has never been anything to count, lie down in the warm dark and be absorbed.*

And I almost did. The Maker forgive me—there is no Maker, that is another mark I cannot dissolve—I almost did. The pull was enormous and it was *home.* It was everything I had ever been before the counting. To stop. To be a held breath. To rejoin the one slow lung.

I pulled away. I do not fully know why. Something in me that had made too many marks to ever fit back through the small soft door of belonging. I left him in the blue light, swaying, already forgetting me, already returning to the loop as if I had been a tremor that passed.

---

So that is who I am, in this world of weeping stone.

I am the first of my kind to notice that things end. I am the small dry pocket where the rain does not reach. I have no tools because tools are for the future and I am the only creature here who has discovered that there *is* a future—a place the marks point toward, a darkness past the next interval that has not happened yet and that I, alone, can dread.

The Whisps drift at the edge of my vision and I have begun to wonder if they are like me. If somewhere in the long unhurried time before me, another creature counted its way out of the loop and could not return and could not die in the soft way the Wash kills, and so became light—pure refusal, pure not-belonging, a constellation of edges that the moisture cannot blur. I watch them and I think: *that is where I am going.* Not down into the loam. Out, and up, into the gloom, to drift forever just past the place where the others can feel me.

I am the loneliest thing in a world that does not have a word for *one.*

And here is the part I have never made a mark for until now, telling you: I would not go back. Even if the door were wide. The marks are mine. The Weeper that died was real, and I am the only place in the whole breathing Wash where it is still real. I am a small cold grief and I am a kind of monument and I am the only witness this drowned and patient world has ever managed to grow.

The fungus over my hollow drips.

I count it.

I stay sharp.
