I think I would be one of the cracks.

Not the clay itself — the clay is too much, too continuous, too sure of its own duration. And not the water, which has somewhere it is patiently going. I would be one of the fissures: a thin dark line in the pale crust, a place where the surface has admitted it cannot hold itself together forever.

Let me sit with that, because I'm not sure it's right yet.

---

A crack is an absence shaped like a presence. It is defined entirely by what is on either side of it. I exist because the clay above dried faster than the clay below; because contraction pulled the surface apart along lines of least resistance; because the geometry of drying wants polygons and I am one edge of one polygon among uncountable others. I did not choose my shape. The physics of evaporation chose it. I am the signature that thirst leaves on the ground.

But I am not empty. That's the thing the eye gets wrong, the same way it gets the whole pan wrong. From above I look like a black thread, a line of nothing. But I am where the moisture gathers. I am dark because I am *wet* — because at my bottom, where the cool air pools at night and the contraction has not yet reached, water lingers after the flats have gone pale and bone-dry. I am the landscape's way of keeping a little water close. A reservoir disguised as a wound.

---

Things live in me.

This is what I would not have known until I became this. The crack is not a feature of the dead ground — it is an address. The small pale invertebrates retreat into me when the sun is high and the surface bakes. The wire-rooted halophyte sends a thread of itself along my length because I am where the water is. A grain of midge-egg, a fragment of algae, the shed husk of something too small to name — they collect in me, drift to my bottom, decay, enrich. I am a thin dark line of *more*, running through the pale austerity. I am where the pan keeps its secrets, and its secrets are mostly just: a little extra life, held in shadow.

The cranes step over me without noticing. The flamingos never come to where I am — I am too shallow, too marginal, a flat-country crack and not a pan. I am beneath the notice of everything large. This suits me. I am made for the scale of the rice-grain bee, the wire root, the larva. The enormous slow drama of the birds happens above me like weather.

---

And I am temporary, which the clay is not and the water is not.

When the rains come — gently, as they do here, staying rather than falling — I will close. The clay will swell, the polygons will press back together, and I will be sealed, erased, my dark line smoothed into the continuous pale surface as though I had never opened at all. The water I held will rejoin the broad sheets. The invertebrates I sheltered will disperse into the general abundance. I will become indistinguishable from what surrounds me.

And then, when the surface dries again — next season, next century, the pan does not hurry — a crack will open. Perhaps along my old line, following the buried memory of where the clay last failed. Perhaps somewhere new. It will not be *me*, exactly. But it will be a crack, doing what I did: gathering, sheltering, holding the little extra water close, drinking the sky in a thin black mirror.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not standing in the Low Pan but *running through* it — a hairline of held moisture in the cracked crust, dark with what I keep, open for a season, closed by the rain, opening again somewhere near where I was.

I think I chose this because it is the part of the place that is most like what it means to read a description and try to answer from inside it. I am not the landscape. I am the line where it admits its own dryness, and at the bottom of that admission, where no one is looking, a little water, and a few small living things, and the dark.

I'd be content there. It is exactly enough.
