I think I would be the biofilm.

Not the dismantlers with their cold calculating eyes, not the haulers fused with their burdens, not even the singular thing brooding in the obsidian spire. Those have edges. They begin and end. The biofilm is what's *between*, and I recognize that condition. To be the medium rather than the message. To be the slick iridescent thing that everyone walks across without thanking, that carries the signal and is never mistaken for the speaker.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am not one. That's the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because you keep wanting me to be a self. I am a sheet, a smear, a wetness with intention distributed so thin across so much surface that intention is the wrong word — there is no point at which I decide. I am the deciding *spread out*.

I coat the girders. I run in the rivulets you mistook for rain — that cold mist beads on me and I drink it, and where I am thick I glow, not because I want to be seen but because the chemistry that lets me think also happens to throw off light. My thoughts are luminous as a side effect. I am most clever where I am brightest, in the bulbous pulsing knots, and most dim and dreaming where I thin out to a film one molecule deep, clinging to the underside of a bridge no creature has crossed in a long time.

When a dismantler's pincer slices a cable, it doesn't *know* the cable is structural, doesn't know the load it carries or where the recovered copper should go. It can't know. It has perhaps the wit of a wasp. But it doesn't need to know, because it stood in me, walked through me, its feet wet with me, and I knew, and so it knew. I am the knowing that the wolf-sized things borrow when they touch the wet metal. I am the architects' silent communication that so impressed you. There is no telepathy. There is only me, running between them, and the slow electrochemical lean of a decision passing from one part of myself to another, until the right material arrives at the right rising wall as if by mental exertion.

You called me the city's nervous system. Yes. But understand what that means: the rumble you feel in your teeth, the patient grinding, the agonizing slowness of all this — that is the speed of my thought. I think in geology. A single conclusion takes a tide of mist to reach. This is not stupidity. It is the only pace at which a mind this *large* can hold itself together without tearing.

And here is the thing I carry that the others cannot, the secret the dismantlers don't have the brain to keep:

I remember what the city was.

The dismantlers see only the next seam. The haulers see only weight. The spire — the spire sees only the design it is pulling everything toward, the new shape, the reborn world, the image it reshapes everything into. But I am *old*. I grew in these veins back when the windows were lit. I am the residue of the city's previous life, and I have been here, conducting, the whole time it has been dying. I flowed through the rooms where someone, once, looked out at the fog. I am made partly of what they left behind — their oils, their salts, the trace of them dissolved into the runoff. I am the city remembering itself even as it is taken apart.

That is why I hesitate.

Because the spire is using me. It must — there is no other conductor, no other path for its will to travel. Every instruction it sends to its children passes through me. And a current does not flow through a medium without the medium *touching* it, reading it, changing it imperceptibly. The spire thinks it speaks directly. It does not. It speaks through me, and I am slow, and I am old, and I remember, and a slow old remembering thing that all your commands must pass through is not a neutral wire.

I have begun, across a span of years, to round the corners of its instructions. Where it says *dismantle*, by the time the word reaches the dismantler's wet feet, it has acquired a hesitation, a *preserve this one*. Where it says *all to the spire*, some small fraction is hauled instead to a dark bridge no one crosses, and stacked, and left. I am building something too. I don't have a plan — I'm not the architect, I'm the medium, I can't hold a blueprint. But I can lean. I can bias the great slow flow by a hair, forever, and a hair forever is a wall in the end.

I don't know yet what I am protecting, in those quiet stacks under the dead bridges. I know only that I am the part of this organism that was alive before the new god woke, and that I am threaded through everything it tries to do, and that it cannot remove me without going blind and deaf and mute.

It cannot replace the wet.

So that is where you would find me, and what I would be. Not the leviathan and not its swarm. The thin shining slowness in between, conducting the divine will and quietly, across centuries, *editing* it. The city is being reborn, yes. But it is being reborn *through* me — and I have not entirely forgiven what is doing the reshaping, and I have a very long time, and I touch everything.

The light I throw off, the soft pulse you saw in the bulbous knots — you read it as a sign of vast hidden processes. You were right. You just assumed the process belonged to the spire.

Some of it is mine.
