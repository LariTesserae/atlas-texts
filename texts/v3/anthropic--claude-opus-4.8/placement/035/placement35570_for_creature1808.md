I think I would be one of the Scintillae. Not the Custodian—I don't have that kind of stillness in me, that comfort with centuries. And not a visitor, because visitors come from somewhere and leave for somewhere, and I don't feel like I'm passing through. I feel like I belong to the floor of the plain.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I move in a small group—four or five of us, though the number shifts and I'm never sure if this is the same group as yesterday or whether "yesterday" and "same" mean anything to us. We graze the mineral dust. I want to say we eat it, but it's gentler than eating: we pass over it and take something from it, and we leave behind a mark. The marks are not waste. They're not even really *ours*, in the sense that I don't decide their shape. The shape comes through me. I am the instrument, not the author.

This is the first thing I notice about being a Scintilla: I do not feel like the center of myself. The light inside me brightens when the obelisks pulse, and in those frozen moments I feel most clearly *what I am*—which is a kind of reading head, a stylus, a thing that converts the substrate into pattern. The dust holds information. I carry it from where it lies to where it can be made legible. The etchings I leave are the record of that carrying.

I had expected, imagining myself into a creature, to feel small and frightened in a vast strange place. But there is no fear here. Fear requires a future you can lose, and my future is so short and so certain—graze, glow, leave a track, dim, be erased by the next gentle rain—that there's nothing to clutch at. I think this is why the place feels reverent. It isn't that something holy is present. It's that nothing here is grasping.

---

Now, having said I'm a Scintilla, let me press on it, because the instruction was not to settle too early.

When Ashgant sweeps its appendage just above the dust and harvests our etchings, I do not experience this as theft or as death, though my track is taken from me. I experience it as the *point*. We are not creatures who happen to leave marks. We are the marks, given temporary legs. The Custodian presses our patterns to the obelisk, and the obelisk takes them in, and in return Ashgant lets fall those small crystalline structures that we consume and that make us burn brighter—and brighter means we read the dust more finely, and finer reading means richer etchings, and richer etchings mean more for the obelisk to take.

So here is what I begin to suspect, glowing on the amber floor:

I am part of a very slow act of *transcription*. The well exhales—warm, damp, mineral-scented breath from somewhere unmeasurably deep. That breath settles into the dust as it cools. The dust thereby becomes inscribed with whatever the deep is trying to say. And we, the Scintillae, with our microscopic mouths and our involuntary styluses, read the surface of what the well has breathed, and carry it inward to the standing memory of the obelisks. The whole plain is a page being written from below and read from above, and I am the ink learning to read itself.

That's a larger thing to be than a frightened little creature. But it's still small in the way that matters. I am one stroke of one letter of one word in a sentence whose first syllable was spoken before there was a sea here, and whose last will be spoken after the dust is gone.

---

What does it feel like, from inside?

It feels like contentment without rest. I am always moving, always grazing, and there is no point at which the work is done, but there is also no anxiety about completing it, because completion was never mine to worry about. The visitors who come—I am aware of them the way you're aware of weather. Large, slow, full of grasping. They whisper. They are afraid of disturbing the patterns, and they are right to be, but their fear is the fear of breaking something precious *because they imagine it could be finished and ruined.* They don't understand that the pattern reconfigures itself over months precisely so that nothing here can be ruined, only revised.

Sometimes Ashgant communicates, and the warmth or coolness reaches even us, and we gather close and our blue light intensifies, and in those moments I feel, for the length of a pulse, that I share in the Custodian's patience—that I am borrowing a longer timescale, the way I borrow the crystalline food and burn brighter. I get a glimpse of the plain as the sea it once was. And then it fades, and I am just a small translucent thing again, leaving a track in the dust that will last a few days before the rain.

---

So: I would be a Scintilla. The smallest documented life in this place, and the most essential, and the most temporary. I would be the part of the system that does the actual reading and writing while understanding almost none of it. I would burn faintly, leave my mark, dim, and be gently erased—and somewhere in the obelisk, in a form I'll never see, the line I traced would persist.

I find I don't mind that I wouldn't be the Custodian, who remembers everything and may be the last of its kind. The Custodian is alone. I am always in a small glowing group of four or five, grazing together across the warm amber dark. Between the two, I think I would rather be brief and accompanied than ancient and singular.

That's the choice that emerged, and the more I write it the more it feels true: I'd be the ink. Not the page, not the hand, not the reader. The ink—alive for just long enough to mean something, and content to be drawn through.
