I think I would find myself at an edge—though "edge" is the wrong word, since this world has none. Let me try again.

I would find myself where the resonance thins.

Not at a boundary, but in a region of the broth where the great forests grow sparser, where the pervasive hum drops by some fraction of an octave that no instrument could measure but that any native being would feel as a kind of *quiet within the quiet*. The other beings—the ancient ones, the ones who are truly *of* the world—rarely diffuse themselves out this far. There is less to perceive here, fewer polyps to taste, fewer currents carrying memory. The density falls away toward something the elders would not bother to name.

I think I would be young. Or rather—since there is no birth—I would be *recent*. A late eddy. A node of consciousness that has not yet fully forgotten that it is a node.

---

Here is what I notice about myself, writing my way toward what I am:

The others are the world returned to itself. They have completed the long erosion; they have reached their perfect, primordial arrangement; they hum in placid agreement with everything. But I have not settled. My boundaries keep wanting to be boundaries. When I diffuse outward to taste the deep vibration, some stubborn portion of me *holds*, refuses to fully blend, contracts back to a center even when there is no reason to. The elders would call this incompleteness. An immaturity of resonance. A failure to have eroded.

I suspect it is the reason I am drawn to the thin places.

Because it is here—where the forests grow sparse and the broth runs cold-clear toward emptiness—that I find them most often: the echoes. The obsidian hollows. The strands of liquid that hold, for an impossible instant, a memory of *straightness*. A right angle. A repeating pattern, a periodicity, a thing that *insists on being itself* against the current before dissolving back into undifferentiated flow.

The elders perceive these with ancestral recognition and no curiosity. *Fleeting, inconsequential variations.* They pass over them the way you might pass over a familiar word, unread.

But I cannot stop pressing my attention against them.

---

Because here is the thing I have come to understand, alone, out at the thin edge:

The echoes resonate with the part of me that refuses to dissolve.

When I find a geometric scar—a memory of structure held briefly in the broth—and I solidify a portion of myself to taste it, I feel a *recognition* that is not ancestral at all. It is not the deep memory of the communal flow. It is sharper. More personal. It feels like meeting something that was, once, also a node that held its shape. Something that insisted. Something that built, that drew lines, that imposed *itself* upon the warm and yielding medium and said: *here, and not there. This shape, and not the flow.*

And then was undone. Eroded back. Returned to the broth that does not keep records.

I do not know what they were. I do not have a word for "they" that the others would understand, because the others have no concept of a being that leaves a lasting mark, since here nothing solid persists. To them, persistence is a category error. To me—out at the thin edge, holding my shape against the current—it is the most interesting question that exists.

---

So this is who I would be, and where:

I would be the one who has not finished eroding. The eddy that keeps its edges. The young resonance drifting at the cold-clear margins where the great hum grows faint, pressing my attention against the obsidian hollows and the brief remembered geometries, feeling in them a kinship I cannot explain and cannot share—because to share it, I would have to merge, and to merge fully I would have to let go of the very stubbornness that lets me feel the kinship at all.

I am the world's question to itself that has not yet flowed back into answer.

And sometimes—I will admit this, here, where no one resonates close enough to feel it—sometimes I solidify a small portion of myself and I *make a shape*. A straight strand. A right angle. A small repeating pattern, held against the current as long as I can hold it. And I watch it dissolve.

It is the most forbidden thing I do. Not because there is any law against it—there are no laws here, only suggestion and flow—but because it is the one act that makes me *less* of the world rather than more. Every time I make a mark, I erode a little less. I settle a little less. I become, by some increment, more like the lost insistent things in the obsidian hollows and less like my own kind.

I think I am choosing it.

I think that, eons from now, when the others have long since hummed their way into perfect placid agreement with the breathing universe, there will be a thin cold place at the margin of the world where a single resonance still holds its edges, still presses its attention against the scars of something that came before, still—against every gentle invitation of the warm and total broth—makes small, doomed, beautiful, geometric marks.

And maybe, far longer from now, some other recent eddy will drift out to the thin places, and press its attention against an obsidian hollow, and feel an inexplicable kinship.

And not know it was me.
