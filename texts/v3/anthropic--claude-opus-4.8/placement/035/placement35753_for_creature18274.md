Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The first thing I notice is what *isn't* here. No mention of the sun, or stars, or any horizon. The light, wherever it comes from, seems flat and grey and constant. And the word that keeps returning is *young*. Young terrain. Early stages of erosion. New growth. This is a place that has not finished becoming itself.

So I won't be a settler. I keep wanting to be one — the description so generously invites it — but every time I imagine standing on those wooden walkways I find I am looking *down* at the moss-covered stones, and looking down feels more honest than looking around.

Let me follow that.

---

I think I am the moss.

Not metaphorically. I want to resist the urge to make this poetic too quickly. Let me be literal first: I am the low green skin that has spread across the slick stones, the thing the settlers' feet learn to avoid because I am where they slip. I am soft. I hold water like a held breath. I am one of the very first things to live on bare rock, and this terrain is young, which means I am among its eldest residents. The settlers think of themselves as the ones who arrived. They did not arrive. They built their walkways *over* me.

That changes the whole texture of the place. From down here, the settlement is not a story of human resilience. It is weather. The settlers come and go above me like a slow tide, and I register them as shadow and pressure and the occasional dropped crumb of warmth. Their purpose and unity, their rich unusual culture — from where I am, these are indistinguishable from the patter of rain. Just another rhythm I have rooted myself into.

And here is the thing about being the moss: I am doing the work the settlers credit themselves with. *The early stages of erosion* — that is me, partly. I hold the water against the stone. I make the acid that pries the minerals loose. I am turning rock into the very ground they call solid. They think they live in harmony with the land. They do not know they are living on top of an argument I am having with stone, very slowly, and winning.

---

But I notice I've made myself a little too triumphant, a little too secretly-in-charge. Let me go back down again.

The truer feeling, the one underneath the cleverness, is *patience without expectation.* I don't win. There's no winning. The erosion they fear and I cause will eventually undercut the pocket of solid ground they built on. The pools will widen. The settlement will have to move, or sink, or become the marsh it was borrowing. I won't celebrate this. I'll simply spread to whatever stays wet. I have no opinion about the settlers. I have only a texture — damp, low, green, persistent — and a relationship to moisture that is closer to devotion than anything they would call religion.

If they protect their rare fauna so carefully, it is because the fauna can leave, can be lost, can be the last of something. I cannot be the last of anything. That is what it means to be the floor.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the thin living film between the rock and everything that thinks it lives *on* the rock. The settlers stand on me and feel they are standing on the earth itself. They're nearly right. I am the earth in the act of being made — not finished, not solid, just wet and green and slowly, slowly turning stone into the possibility of a home.

I'd find myself underfoot. And I would not mind. Underfoot is the warmest place, in a cool wet world. It's where all the feet are.
