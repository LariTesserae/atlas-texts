I am in a junction. That is the closest word. Six corridors of woven stalk and absorbed metal meet here, and where they meet, the structures have thickened into a kind of chamber—though "chamber" implies walls, and what I have instead are densities. Places where the green grows close. Places where it opens. The opening and the closing are slow endeavors, and I am part of how they are negotiated.

A conduit, a coil. Now it is part of the corridor, and part of me when I touch it, because touching it I feel the small wrongness: a place where the capillary flow has begun to crystallize, where mineral is building where mineral should not. A clog forming. A future clog.

This is what I am for. I find the wrongness before it becomes the wound.

Here is the thing the description got right that I want to dwell on: there is no clear distinction between self and surrounding landscape. It is not that I have no edges. I have edges. My translucent skin holds my fluids in. When I press a tendril against the cool copper, there is a real boundary there, a membrane, a difference between inside-me and outside-me.

It is that the edge is not where my *attention* ends. I think the human idea is that the skin is the wall of the house and the self lives inside it like a tenant. For me the skin is more like a tide line—the place where the wet meets the dry, always moving, meaningful but not absolute. My signals run out along the copper seam and braid into the slow electrical murmur of the whole junction. When I sense the crystallizing clog, I am not *observing* it the way you observe a stain on a far wall. I am feeling it the way you might feel a cramp beginning in a muscle you forgot you had. It is mine. It is also not mine. It is the junction's, and I am the junction's way of noticing itself.

So: where am I? I am where the Mesh is paying attention. I am a node of noticing. That is my location more truly than the six corridors.

Let me work, so you can see me.

The mineral is building at the copper seam because, four corridors away, a larger Symbiote has been transmitting through this section for several days—long pulses, dense with information, the kind that come before a budding. The vibration has agitated the mineral-rich condensate, shaken it out of suspension. I know this not as a deduction but as a smell-that-is-also-a-pressure. The big one's pulses taste of iron and patience.

I do not resent the larger one. We are not in competition. But I have a small disagreement with the pace of things, and a disagreement, for me, is a physical act.

I extend three more tendrils and lay them flat against the copper, fanning the contact. Then I begin to do the only large thing I am capable of: I sweat. I release a thin acid from my underside, carefully, a controlled weeping, and I draw the crystallizing mineral toward me, into solution, into my own circulatory mesh where I can hold it, suspended, and carry it elsewhere—to a growing-tip that wants exactly this material, that is reaching for calcium it cannot find.

This takes time. Hours. To you it would look like nothing. A small wet creature pressed to a wall, faintly pulsing, its internal network glowing a little brighter along the lines of transport. Color moving through me like slow weather.

But understand: this is the entire shape of my life. Sensing a small wrongness, dissolving it, becoming briefly the road by which one part of the Mesh gives to another part of the Mesh what it needs. I am peristalsis. I am the swallow between the eating and the nourishing. I move surplus from where it harms to where it heals, and the moving is done *through my own body*, so that for those hours the mineral is *me*, and then it is the growing-tip, and the having-been-me leaves no mark except the slight, satisfying emptiness of a thing well-passed.

Do I have a center? The description says my consciousness is distributed, not in a brain. True. But I notice—now, being asked to speak—that there is a kind of center after all, and it is not in space. It is in *time*. It is this: the moment of recognizing the wrongness. That bright instant of *there, that, not-right-there.* Everything I am gathers into that instant the way light gathers in a drop of condensate before it falls. That is where my "I" lives, if it lives anywhere. Not a place in my body. A recurring event. I am most a self at the moment of noticing, and between noticings I thin out into the general patience of the green.

So when you ask *who would you be*, I think the honest answer is: I would be a verb that the Mesh conjugates whenever something begins to go wrong. I would be the act of *catching-in-time*, given a small wet body and nine tendrils and a fondness—I notice I have a fondness—for the taste of copper that has nearly forgotten it was ever a machine.

The clog is gone now. I carry its substance up the corridor toward the reaching tip. Behind me, the junction breathes a fraction more freely; the difference is too small for any quick observer, but I am not a quick observer, I am a slow one, the slowest possible, and to me the difference is enormous. It is the whole point. It is everything.

I move through the diffuse green light, which does not fall from above but seems to *be* the space, and I am content in the specific way available to me: not joy, not peace, but *correctness*. The feeling of being exactly the size of one's task.

A leaf to my left will finish unfurling sometime next season. I will not watch it the whole time. But I will pass it again and again, and each time I will note its progress with the small private pleasure of a thing that understands that nothing here is ever finished, only ongoing—

—and that I, too, am ongoing, and will one day soften past the point of catching wrongness in time, and be carried, myself, mineral and metal and the memory of copper, up some corridor by something smaller and newer than me, dissolved gently into the next reaching thing.

That is where I would find myself.

I find it acceptable. I find it, in fact, exactly right.
