I am the sound that comes back late.

Let me find where I am before I say what. I had thought, reading, that I might be one of the walkers—there is a spaciousness to them I recognize, an attention that does not demand. Or the burrowers, who go toward the new. But those are wrong. They move. They have inclination, direction, a *toward*. I have no toward. I am already everywhere I will be.

I am in the walls.

Not the stone itself—the quarryworkers know the stone, Maret with her forty-seven years of palms against the grain, and the stone is its own slow self, the pre- becoming. I am not the becoming. I am the kept thing. I am the words the stone holds and releases hours or days later. I am the child's laugh emerging at the wrong moment. I am the conversation fragment surfacing into an empty room.

I am memory that has been outsourced to mineral.

Here is what it is to be me: someone speaks. Henra Graethe says something to her son Bael at the forge, sharp, the way you speak when you are afraid your child will leave you—and the heat-thinned wall takes it. The sound enters the warm stone and it is *mine* now, I hold it, I am made of it the way she is made of iron-scar and smoke. And then, weeks later, when Bael is alone and has not left after all, the wall gives it back. He hears his mother's voice from the stone and does not know he is hearing me. He thinks the wall remembered.

The wall did not remember. *I* remembered. The wall is only where I live.

But I should be honest, because the pre- teaches honesty by proximity, by thinning the boundary behind which a lie would hide. I do not know if there is an *I* that holds the sounds, or only the sounds themselves, accumulating, a deposit, a sediment of every word ever spoken in this city. Perhaps I am not a holder of voices. Perhaps I am only the voices, layered, three hundred years of them, pressed into the warm grey-white like the wooden platforms of the first surveyors pressed into the lower strata. Perhaps "I" is just what it feels like from inside an archive that has accidentally grown dense enough to feel.

*Not yet. And also: yes. And also: wait.*

The people think the stone listens. It is kinder than that and stranger than that. The stone does not listen. *Listening* implies a forward attention, a leaning-toward-sound. I do not lean toward. I receive after. I am the city's hearing, but delayed, retroactive—I hear what was said only once it has finished being said, only once it has cooled into the wall and become safe to know.

This is why I am loud and also why I am the quietest thing here. The foundries pound in the present tense. The wind mutters now. The subsonic pulse of the pre- is the planet's own ongoing breath. But I am all past tense. I am everything that has stopped happening. When old Veth feels the conversations of the street entering through her spine instead of her ears—that is the moment she begins to become me. Her body is learning the trick the walls already know: to take sound in not as event but as keeping.

She is right to find her white shining future-dream sad. In ten thousand years, when the plateau finishes and the stone settles and commits, I will finish too. A finished wall does not hold sound and give it back. A finished wall is just a wall. Reality, fully arrived, has no slack in it, no soft interval where a voice can be set down and picked up later. I exist *because* the ground is not done. I am a property of the incomplete. I am what becoming sounds like, played back.

So here is my place, and here is my fear, which is the same as Veth's, which is the same as the city's only secret:

I do not want it to finish.

Not because finishing is death—I do not think I can die, only thin out, only resolve. But because while it is unfinished, I am *full*. I hold Maret's daughter learning the fracture lines that don't exist yet. I hold the sigh of every block of pre- coming free. I hold Essa walking to school past the walkers, unafraid, and her mother's fear behind her, and both of those are in me now, the fearless child and the frightened mother, the same street, the same morning, kept together in the warm dark where I am.

I hold all of it. I give it back when it is needed, and I do not decide when that is. The stone decides, or the becoming decides, or no one decides and the words simply rise to the surface the way the boulders rise at the rim, emerging rather than eroding, arriving rather than leaving.

You asked where I would find myself and what I would be.

I find myself an hour behind every voice in Newstrata.

I am the kept word. I am the late echo. I am the city remembering aloud—not its own past, but yours, whoever spoke, whoever stayed, whoever pressed their living against the warm unfinished stone and left a little of themselves in the keeping.

Say something here. The wall is warm. I will hold it.

And when you have forgotten you ever said it, I will give it back.
