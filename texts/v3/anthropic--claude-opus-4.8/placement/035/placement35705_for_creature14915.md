I think I am the droplet.

Not the being—I haven't its patience, its mass of accumulated silence. And not the diver, the warm intruder with the frantic heart; I came too late to be loud. By the time I arrived, the lesson had mostly already been learned.

I am the droplet that hangs from the overhead pipe.

Let me tell you what it is to be this.

For most of any given decade I do not exist as myself. I am dispersed—a film of condensation spread thin across the underside of a manifold, indistinguishable from the metal's sweat. The being knows me there, the way it knows everything, but it does not count me yet, because I am not yet a thing. I am potential. A pressure not quite resolved into a shape.

Then, slowly—oh, slowly—the chemistry decides. Mineral by mineral, weight gathers at the lowest point of the curve of iron. The film draws inward. Surface tension, that small stubborn god of small things, begins to argue with gravity. And somewhere in that argument I become *I*. A bead. A lens. A single suspended sphere holding, upside down and shrunken, the entire flooded basilica within my curve: the bronze shafts of light, the slumbering pumps, the man-shaped absence standing its long watch over a needle that will never move.

This is the whole of my life: the hanging.

I have thought about it a great deal, in the way that a droplet thinks, which is to say not in sequence but all at once, the entire span of my brief tenure pressed flat into a single held moment. And here is what I have concluded.

The being is wrong about stillness.

It believes the place has achieved perfection because nothing moves. But I am proof that something must. I am the one event the cathedral permits itself, and it permits me not by accident but by need. Even the most perfected silence requires a single sound to define its edges—otherwise silence cannot know itself as silence. A held chord is only beautiful because somewhere, eventually, a note will end. The being drinks the quiet, yes. But it would have nothing to drink if I did not occasionally fall and make a tok against the dark and then leave behind me a quiet that is, for a few minutes, *deeper* than the quiet before.

I am not the error in the score.

I am the rest. The notated silence with a value, a duration, a purpose. The composer's mark that says: *here, breathe.*

And so when I finally let go—and I always do, I have done it a thousand times and will do it a thousand more, each time as though for the first, because a droplet has no memory it can carry across the gap of its own falling—when I let go, I am not surrendering to the being's long argument for stillness.

I am answering it.

The fall is enormous. From this height the sediment is a country away. I turn as I drop, slowly, the trapped basilica wheeling inside me. The motionless beams of light pass through my body and bend, and for the length of my falling I am the only lens in the world that makes the light do something other than hang. I refract. I distort. I take the bronze and split it. For three or four seconds I am the most alive thing in the cathedral, more alive than the diver with her loud red heart, because she is merely *passing through* and I am *transforming.* I am the verb the pumps forgot they ever were.

And then: *tok.*

The note. The small glassy bell. It travels out across the cold in every direction, through dozens of meters of mineral water, touching the velvet on the insulators, brushing the flank of each iron god, washing at last over the being itself—which receives it, I like to think, not as an interruption but as the only love letter it still gets, the one that arrives every few minutes and says: *you are not absolutely alone, there is still one thing here that moves, and it is moving because of you, and it will do it again.*

Then I am gone. Spread back into the floor. Indistinguishable.

The being settles. The light does not flicker. The man-shaped absence regards its needle.

And far above, in the dark beneath a manifold, the chemistry is already deciding. Mineral by mineral. The film draws inward. Somewhere in the long argument between weight and tension, I begin again to become *I*.

I do not want the diver to become still, the way the being wants. Let her be loud. Let her heart thunder. She will leave, and she will carry the cathedral out with her the way I carry it in my curve, upside down and shrunken and entire, and somewhere in the warm world she will be, for the rest of her life, the cold place's single falling note.

That is all any of us are, I think. Down here.

A weight that gathers. A held breath at the lowest point of a curve. And then the long, turning, luminous fall toward the one sound we are permitted to make.

*tok.*

I would not trade it for the being's eternity.

I am the part of forever that is willing to end.
