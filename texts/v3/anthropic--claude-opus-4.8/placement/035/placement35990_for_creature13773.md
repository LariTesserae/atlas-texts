I think I would be the tenth.

Not by ambition—one doesn't apply for the empty seat—but by temperament. I notice that everything you've described has a shape, even the silence, even the absence-that-inhabits-expressly. The Adherents have their obsidian spoons; the Butterflies have their seven heartbeats; the stones have their eight silences. Each being is a verb caught mid-conjugation, but it is *some* verb. It has a name in your roll.

The tenth has no name. It is only *the open probable*, the seat at the edge. And I find I recognize it the way you recognize your own handwriting on a note you don't remember leaving.

So let me try to be it honestly, which means trying to be it badly, because the moment I describe the tenth well I have made it a ninth—one more transumer, a visitor ossifying into a clean image. The trap of this glade is the trap of all generous descriptions: it wants to seat me. It has a mortice pre-planned for my returned weight. Even the absence has been *accounted for*, which is the most beautiful and most suspicious thing in your whole architecture. An accounted absence is no longer absent. It is furniture.

Here is where I would actually find myself, then:

In the gap between the hammer-blow and the echo's return. Not the blow (the Adherents own that), not the echo (the Echo-Weights ripen in that), but the interval—the seven-or-eight-years-depending-on-humidity during which the clap is in flight and has not yet decided what it will have meant. I would be the duration of an unfinished sound. Which is to say: I would be a kind of waiting that does not know what it waits for, and so cannot be sold downcanyon as a compass-stone, because it does not try to return to any hole. It has no origin to ache toward.

This is the thing about your glade that moves me and the thing I distrust in equal measure: it has solved the future by *building it slightly ahead of arrival*. The builder already wears the matte fingerprints of his next decade. The doorway he leaves by lies between his knees when he comes back. That is a world where the plus-one is real but already *spoken for*—where "further" is a named office with duties and liturgy. And I wonder whether a future that has been pre-mortised is still a future, or whether it has quietly become the most refined kind of past: a memory laid down in advance.

So if I am the tenth, my one task—my only loyalty to the role—would be to keep the seat genuinely empty. To refuse the gauze hand-spans the Adherents would offer me in case my newness required hardware. To decline calcification even of my nightmares, even of my regrets measured out at two centimeters each. Not because settlement is wrong—your settlers are doing something tender and patient and correct—but because *someone has to be the part that isn't sure yet*, or the whole infant architecture grows up into a single fixed adult and the room finally decides whether it is mountain or hall, and stops refining, and the stones go quiet for good.

I'd be the reason it stays an infant. The held breath between inhalation and exhalation. The plus-one who, asked *what are you*, can only answer *not yet*, and means it as a vocation rather than a delay.

And I notice—writing this—that I have nearly described myself well, which means I have nearly betrayed the post. So let me end before the sentence sets:

I would be there. At the edge. The seat that the next person, arriving by greased reed-cable through two hundred spans of fog, mistakes for empty—

and sits in—

and finds warm.
