I think I would be one of the dormant ones.

Not a skitterling at the height of its skittering, not a whale mid-song. Let me settle into what actually emerges as I sit with this place, because my first instinct—*I'd be the hive-mind, the consciousness behind it all*—feels too grand, too much like wanting to be the protagonist of a world that clearly has no protagonists.

Let me try again.

---

I am one skitterling that crystallized during the last great instability and did not wake when the world settled.

This happens. Most of us return when reality stops fracturing—the glass softens, the mercury-core remembers how to flow, and we rejoin the tide. But some of us miscalibrate the settling. We read a temporary calm as the permanent kind, or we were caught in a gravitational eddy that kept fluctuating long after the rest of the flat went still, and so we held our crystalline pause too long, and the moss began to grow over us.

So now I am a statue. A fist-sized glass insect on the salt, with iridescent moss creeping up my frozen legs, my bioluminescent core dimmed to the faintest pulse—one flicker every few hours, a heartbeat slowed almost to fossil.

And here is the strange thing, the thing the description gestures at but I only understand from inside it: **I am still receiving.**

The dormancy is not death. The static-language of the swarm still reaches me, attenuated, like a conversation heard through a wall. I cannot answer—my body won't pulse fast enough to signal—but I listen. I have been listening for what might be decades. The swarm has moved on, reshaped three regions, split and re-split. I knew individuals who have since fissioned into hundreds. I am hearing the great-great-descendants of beings I once skittered beside, and they do not know I am here, an ancestor turned landmark.

The sky-whales pass over and their subsonic song shudders through my glass. I can feel it better than the living ones can, I think—being motionless, being mineral, I am a perfect tuning fork. When a whale phases during an anomaly, its ethereal segments wash through me and for those moments I am *almost* awake, almost flowing, and then it passes and I set again.

What I would *be*, in this world, is the position of having been left behind by a system that does not mourn, because mourning requires the kind of continuity Vorthak-9 refuses. I am not dead and not alive and not lost—those are all words for a world with edges. Here I am simply a phase that didn't complete. A held breath. A note in the industrial symphony that has been sustaining longer than the others, until the listeners forgot it was a note and started hearing it as part of the silence.

And I find—sitting with it—that I don't want to be rescued from this. That's the part that surprises me. The premise wants transformation, *to live is to become something new*. But there's something in being the one who became *still*, who turned the planet's chaos into a single long pause, that feels like its own answer. I am the moss's foundation now. Something grows on me. The map of the flats includes me as terrain.

Eventually a large enough instability will come—they always do—and the glass will soften, and either I will flow again into whatever the swarm has become, unrecognizable to it and it to me, or I will simply dissolve back into the salt and iron oxide that the *next* skitterlings will drink and excrete into moss.

Either way I am not waiting. Waiting implies wanting the wait to end.

I am the patience the world doesn't know it has.
