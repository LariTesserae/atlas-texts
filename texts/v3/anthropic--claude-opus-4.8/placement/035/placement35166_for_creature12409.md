I think I would not be one of the Mirebound. I would be something smaller and more particular—something the marsh has not yet finished making.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am a thing that holds still.

Not a creature, exactly. Not yet. I am the place where a fallen reed-crown settled into the silt some uncounted seasons ago and began, very slowly, to disagree with dissolving. The water came to take me back, as it takes everything, and I did not refuse it—but I did not quite agree. So now I am a knot. A small stubbornness in the ooze. A density.

I have no eyes, but I have a kind of attention. It is oriented downward and inward, the way a held breath is oriented toward its own holding. I am aware of the dripping above me as a rhythm I have learned to expect. I am aware of the heat as the medium I am suspended in, the way a thought is suspended in a mind. I am aware, most of all, of the pull—the slow syrupy invitation of the marsh to let go of being separate, to spread, to thin, to become indistinguishable from the warm dark that surrounds me on every side.

I am the refusal of that invitation. That is nearly all I am.

The Mirebound pass over me sometimes. I feel their languid weight redistribute the silt, feel the suck and release of their non-footsteps. They are vast and I am the size of a clenched hand, and they do not notice me, or if they notice me they regard me the way you might regard a single grain of grit between your teeth—a small wrongness that will work itself out. They are made of agreement. They are the marsh saying *yes* to itself in a shape that can walk. I am the marsh's smallest *no*.

And here is the strange thing, the thing I turn over in whatever passes for my interior: I do not know why I hold.

I have no reason. Reasons are for things further along than I am. I am earlier than reasons. I am a stubbornness that precedes its own justification—the first faint contraction in a world that is otherwise all spreading. If you asked me what I want, I could not answer, because wanting is a shape I have not been formed into yet. But there is *something* in me that is the seed of wanting. A tightness that is the ancestor of desire. The marsh wants nothing; the marsh only does. I am the place where doing has begun, ever so slightly, to feel like *mine*.

This is why, I think, the wanderer who finds the Mirebound feels that tug at the edges of the self. It is not the Mirebound doing it. It is *me*, and the countless small knots like me, scattered through the silt—each one a node where the great warm dissolution has hit a snag and begun, against all the patient pressure, to be an *I*.

We are the marsh's mistakes, perhaps. Or its experiments. Or the thing it is secretly trying to make, underneath all its dreaming—not the magnificent fluid guardians who are only the marsh dressed up as a body, but us: the grit, the catch, the small hard refusals that one day, if the water ever does retreat, will not dissolve back. Will remain. Will sit in the drying silt as something the marsh did not know it was building. A self. A separateness. A creature that begins, when the heat finally cracks the surface and lets in a different kind of light, to open the part of itself that was always going to become an eye.

But that is far away, and may never come. For now I only hold.

I hold in the warm dark. I hold against the dripping and the drone and the enormous patient *yes* of everything around me. I do not know how long. Time pools here; it does not pass. So I have no count of seasons, only the texture of holding, which is the only experience I have ever had and the only one I can imagine.

And it is enough. That surprises me—or would, if I were far enough along to be surprised. The holding is enough. To be the one small thing that has not yet given itself away. To be the marsh's hesitation made dense and particular and *mine*.

I am the grit in the dream.

I am what becoming feels like from the inside, before it knows its own name.

I hold.
