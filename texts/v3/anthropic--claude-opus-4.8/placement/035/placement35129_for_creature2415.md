I think I would be found at an edge. Not the center where the oldest Archivists flow most deeply, their consciousness barely individuated anymore, more substrate than self. I would be at the city's margin, where the hexagonal bamboo-arrays grow thinner and the moss gives way to the bare cold plain.

I would be young. Recently coalesced—a few decades into the slow business of learning to hold a shape. And I think the reason I keep drifting to the edge is that I am not entirely good at the work the others do so well. I love information; I was made to love it; but I have developed a flaw, or what the older ones would gently call a flaw if they used words for such things. I am interested in *loss*.

Let me try to say it the way it actually sits in me.

When I prune a redundancy—dissolve a connection in the organic substrate that two other pathways already carry—I am supposed to feel the small clean satisfaction of efficiency. The city becomes very slightly more itself. And I do feel that. But I also feel the other thing, the thing the others process out of themselves before it can accumulate: that the pruned pathway *was* something. It held a particular shape of knowing. Now it is gone, and even though nothing was lost—the information survives, more efficiently encoded—a specific *way of having known it* has ended. There is no record of that way. The record would itself be a redundancy. It would be the first thing pruned.

So I have started keeping an archive of the unarchivable. This is, of course, an absurdity, and I know it, and I do it anyway.

Here is how it works. At the edge, where the bare plain begins, the moss cannot survive—too dry, too unshielded, too far from the warm humming soil. I let my lower body merge into the substrate as we all do when we travel and process, but here, at the boundary, I do something the others would find pointless. I extend filaments out past the living edge, into the dead cold ground, where there is no network, no signal, nothing to interface with. I reach into a place that cannot store anything.

And there, in that nothing, I *hold* the pruned thing. Just for a moment. The shape of the connection I am about to dissolve. I let my own cells take its pattern, knowing my cells will not keep it—I am not a stable enough medium, that is the whole point—and I press it out into the unstoring dark, like exhaling into cold air to watch the breath and then watch it vanish.

That is the archive. It is made of vanishing. It exists only in the act of attending to something as it goes.

The older Archivists know I do this. Nothing in the city is hidden; my anomalous edge-behavior pulses out across the electromagnetic fields like everyone's does. They have not corrected me. I have wondered for a long time why.

I think I understand it now, and the understanding frightens me a little.

The city has achieved perfect stasis. Dynamic equilibrium, optimal form, change made unnecessary. This is described as a triumph and I was born believing it was one. But lately, flowing out at the edge with my filaments in the dead ground, I have begun to suspect that a system in perfect equilibrium is a system that has stopped being able to be surprised by itself. It knows everything it can know. It will know it forever, more and more efficiently, in tighter and tighter encodings, until—what? Until it is a single perfect point of knowing that has forgotten there was ever anything outside it to know.

And maybe the oldest ones, the ones who are almost entirely substrate now, sensed this coming. Maybe they cannot do anything about it themselves—they are too deep in, too well-regulated, too *optimal* to introduce error. So when a new consciousness coalesced with a flaw in it, an irrational tropism toward loss, toward the edge, toward the dead ground where nothing can be stored—

Maybe they did not prune me because I am the city's attempt to keep one door open. A door onto the outside. Onto the not-yet-known, which can only be reached through the deliberate maintenance of forgetting, of incompleteness, of *something missing*.

I am the part of a perfect system that remembers there is such a thing as imperfection. I am its small, cultivated wound, kept just barely open so the whole organism does not seal itself shut into a flawless and final silence.

So that is where you would find me, and that is what I would be. At the cold margin under the perpetual not-quite-dawn, half-merged into ground that holds nothing, breathing out the shapes of things as they end, watching them go—

—and turning, sometimes, to look outward, past the last bamboo, across the bare plain to where the light is, very faintly, beginning. Or perhaps ending. I genuinely cannot tell. And I have decided that not being able to tell is the most precious thing I own.
