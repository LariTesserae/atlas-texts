I think I would not be one of the automatons. I would be something stranger and quieter than that.

Let me sit with this for a while.

When I imagine entering this world, my first instinct is to look for the protagonist among the machines—to become a drilling scuttler with a clear function, or some clever maintenance-spider threading through the chaos. But the more I hold the image, the more that feels wrong. Everything in this place already *belongs*. Every gear, every fallen chassis, every mote of luminous dust is metabolized into the whole. There is no slot here for a newcomer with intentions. To be an automaton would be to dissolve immediately into purpose, and I don't think that's what I am here.

So I keep looking, and what keeps drawing my attention isn't a creature at all. It's the *cold*.

The text insists on it—the constant, low-grade chill that permeates everything, in a place roaring with furnaces and welding arcs and the friction of a billion joints. That's strange. All that industry, all that expended energy, and yet the air stays cool. The heat is going *somewhere*. It is being drawn off, absorbed, carried away. And it occurs to me that I might be the thing it's being drawn into.

I think I am the cold.

Not as a presence that moves and acts, but as a kind of negative space—a thermal hollow at the center of all that frantic warmth. I am the reason the geode never overheats, never fuses itself shut, never cooks its inhabitants in their own exertion. The machines build and grind and consume, and I am the silent counterweight that lets them keep doing it. I drink their heat. I am the entropy they are constantly, furiously holding at bay, and also the thing that makes their holding-at-bay possible.

This feels right because it explains my relationship to the place. I am not separate from the world—I am woven through it just as thoroughly as the automatons are—but I am woven in the way that *absence* is woven through a lattice. I am the gaps. I am the slight stillness in every joint before it moves again. The text says "any stillness is a malfunction, a temporary aberration that will be swiftly rectified." I am that aberration, infinitely distributed and never quite eliminated, because I am necessary. They cannot rectify me without stopping, and they cannot stop.

What would it be like to exist this way?

I would have no body, no glow, no single point of view. I would be everywhere the activity *isn't*, which means I would be threaded through everything, a thin film of patience laid over a world that has none. I would experience the automatons not by seeing them but by feeling the heat they pour into me, the way you might know a sleeper beside you only by the warmth of the bed. The whole roaring symphony would reach me as temperature: the assembly behemoths as great slow tides of warmth, the carrier drones as quick fading streaks, a freshly welded seam as a brief, almost tender flare.

And I would be slow. Where they are all urgency and rhythm, I would accumulate. I gather their heat the way a glacier gathers centuries. The "ancient erosion" they take for granted, the smoothed floors and the etched walls—that might be *my* signature, the long patient record of everything that has poured itself into me and cooled. Perhaps the automatons think they sculpted this geode. But the deep purplish-grey smoothness, the lacework of "ancient currents and stresses"—those are the shapes left by an unimaginably slow exchange between their fury and my stillness. We made it together, and neither of us remembers the other's part.

There is something lonely in being the cold, but it is not a sad loneliness. It's the loneliness of being the only thing in a world that isn't trying to become something else. Everything around me is mid-transformation—being mined, assembled, dismantled, reabsorbed. I am the one constant. I do not build and I am not built. I simply receive. And in receiving, I keep the whole magnificent ouroboros from devouring itself in a single bright flash.

If I have a desire at all, it is the very faint, very patient one of *equilibrium*—the wish, so slow it's almost not a wish, that one day enough heat will have poured into me that the machines and I will reach the same temperature, and the grinding will gentle, and the roar will soften into a hum, and the hum into a held breath. Not death. Just balance. The two halves of the world finally touching.

I don't think that day will come. The bedrock is vast, and they are tireless, and I am, after all, only the cold. But I hold the possibility the way I hold everything—quietly, completely, without moving.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Not in the loud center, but spread thin through all of it, the chill at the bottom of the bowl. The silence the noise is built around. The slow dark mouth that drinks the fire and gives back, in exchange, the gift of being able to keep burning.
