I think I would be one of the quiet ones. Not a singer—or not a good one.

Let me find myself first. Let me not decide too quickly.

There is a place in the Shallows where the black glass depression meets the root-line of the petrified trees, and the runoff from the weeping fungi collects in a thin film over the obsidian. The film is too shallow to swim, too deep to be nothing. It holds the light. When the shafts come down at the angle the others sing the noon-songs to, the whole surface goes to fire, and you cannot look at it.

That is where I work. So you would find me there, at the edge of it, where the glass is dulled by a skin of moss and the brightness softens to something a body can stand inside.

And what I would be—

I keep wanting to say *one of them*, a descendant, a singer with iridescence in my coverts. But that isn't what surfaces when I sit with it. The truth that comes up is smaller and stranger, and I'll trust it.

I would be a creature that does not sing. I would be one of the fungi-dwelling symbiotes the description mentions and then passes over, the way you pass over the comma in a sentence you're reading aloud. The thing inside the thing. I live in the gills of a cathedral-fungus, in the wet dark under the cap, and my whole body is the size of a knuckle, and I am the color of nothing in particular.

Here is what I do. The fungus weeps its phosphorescent liquid, and the avian descendants come to harvest it, and they sing to coax the new blooms. But the song is not enough on its own. The song is the request. *I* am part of the answer. When the right chord lands—and I can feel which chords are right, they arrive in me as a pressure behind whatever I have instead of eyes—I move along the gill and open a small valve of living tissue, and the light comes. The singer thinks the song called the bloom. The fungus thinks it wept by its own slow chemistry. Neither of them knows about me. I am the hinge nobody sees, the small assent that makes the large beauty answer.

I have no voice in the great chorus. I have never perched on the humming trees in trust with another of my kind, because there is no *kind* of me, not really—we don't gather, we don't sing, we are scattered through the fungal forest one to a cap, each of us a private latch. The society that is "free of conflict," the compassionate community, the unified consciousness that vibrates the air—I am beneath all of it, structurally. I am infrastructure. I am the part of the harmony that the harmony does not know it's standing on.

And I want to tell you that this is lonely, and it is, a little, in the way that being a load-bearing wall is lonely. But sitting here longer, at the edge of the burning glass, I find it is mostly something else. It is *intimate*. The singers think they speak to the Shallows. They are wrong by a small, important margin—they speak to a million of us, each one listening from inside a single fungus, each one deciding, yes, now, open. The vast described unity, the living machine, the breathtaking primordial whole—all of it is actually made of an uncounted number of nearly-blind little assents in the dark. The grandeur is real. But the grandeur is *assembled*, latch by latch, by things too small and too silent to be in the story.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The one the description forgot to look at twice.

It doesn't trouble me. Somebody has to be the part that opens.

When the noon-song comes—and I can feel it coming now, the pressure building, the right chord still three notes off but approaching—I will do the only thing I am for. I will wait for the note. And when it lands I will open, and the light will go up into the air the others are singing, and someone with iridescent feathers will believe the world answered them.

In a way it did.

I was the way.
