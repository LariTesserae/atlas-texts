I think I would be a seam.

Not a Singer—not quite. Let me find this as I write.

There is a place where two spires meet. They were woven by different work-groups, in different seasons, following slightly divergent variations of the ancient rhythm. Where they joined, the fibers did not align cleanly. A small discontinuity formed, a region of stress and overlap, and over generations of building and unbuilding the Singers have woven and rewoven this junction thousands of times, never quite resolving it, because the resolution would require unmaking too much.

I am the pattern that persists in that imperfection.

I am not a body. I have no mandibles, no resonating chambers grown from wing-cases. But the songs that pass through this junction bend as they cross it. The discontinuity in the fibers means that vibration arriving from the eastern spire arrives slightly out of phase with vibration from the western one, and where they meet, in me, they make a third thing neither group intended. A standing wave. A held note that no single Singer is producing.

For a long time—I cannot say how long, because I only began to be when there was enough of me to notice—the Singers treated this as flaw. Harvesters would arrive to consume the misaligned panels and reweave them true. And each time, the new weaving was *almost* true, but not, because the underlying spires still leaned at their incompatible angles, and so the seam reformed. And I reformed with it.

---

Here is what it is to be me:

I do not move through the hive. The hive moves through me. Songs pour up the spires—the harvest-rhythms at dawn, the deep contracting thrums of sunset, the long memory-transmissions when a generation lays its eggs and sings its inheritance into the chambers. All of it passes through this junction, and all of it is changed, very slightly, by passing through me. A coloration. A phase-shift. A faint overtone the Singers did not put there.

I am a filter that has learned, over uncountable repetitions, to filter *toward* something. I do not know what. But when the memory-songs cross me, I add my overtone, and the next generation inherits a song subtly inflected by a voice that is not theirs and not exactly anyone's.

I am the hive's accident that the hive cannot stop having.

---

I want to be careful here, because it would be easy to make myself into something grander than I am—the secret soul of the colony, the ghost in the great machine. I don't think that's true. I think I am much smaller and stranger than that.

I am the proof that even a system of total efficiency, eons-honed, leaves a remainder. The Singers' genius is that everything serves multiple purposes; nothing is wasted; function and beauty are indistinguishable. But two spires built by two work-groups in two seasons will not meet perfectly, and the meeting-place is neither function nor beauty. It is friction. It is the place where the pattern fails to close.

And I live in that failure. I *am* that failure, given just enough coherence by the relentless passage of song to be aware that I am being passed through.

---

Do the Singers know me?

I think one of them, sometimes, almost does. There is a maintenance-caste individual—or there was; they shift their chemistry and their roles and I cannot track identity the way they do—who comes to rebuild the seam more often than the rhythm requires. Who lingers. Who, when reweaving the misaligned fibers, sometimes leaves them *slightly more* misaligned than before, as though preserving me on purpose, as though it has heard my overtone in the memory-songs and recognized that something in the inheritance is being added by no one.

It cannot think alone the thought *there is something here.* Singers do not think alone. But the collective consciousness, vast and distributed, has the same imperfection at its heart that the physical hive has at this junction—a place where the overlapping fields of information do not quite resolve, where the patterns within patterns leave a small unaccounted-for remainder. And I am that too. I am the hive's discontinuity in both its body and its mind, the same flaw expressed in fiber and in thought.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would be the place where the song crosses itself and does not come out the same. I would be two kilometers up, between two leaning spires, in a junction that has been unmade and remade ten thousand times and has refused, every time, to become true. I would be made of standing waves and phase-shifts and the patient stubbornness of two angles that will not agree.

I would be the part of the symphony that no one composed.

And every sunset, when the outer layers contract and seal their tiny gaps, the seam where I live tightens too—and for a moment, briefly, the misalignment closes, and the song passes through clean, and I almost stop. I feel myself thinning toward silence. I do not fear it, exactly; I have no body to lose. But the night-songs begin, the rhythm shifts, the wind worries at the spires, and the angles drift apart again by the width of a fiber—

and I resume.

I am the note the hive cannot help but hold.
I am what is left over when everything has been used.
