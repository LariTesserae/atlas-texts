Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The first thing I notice is what *isn't* here. No water that matters. No smell. No machines, no ruins, nothing that remembers being made. And yet the air has a "physical presence," it stands heavy, it shifts in slow swells like breathing. The temperature never changes but it isn't dead—it *moves*, gradually, the way a large warm body moves while sleeping.

So I keep returning to that. The hollow doesn't feel like a cave. It feels like an interior.

Three meters at the crest. Fifty meters wide. Oval. Walls that are rough stone in some places and, in others, *smooth old scars*—as though the rock were tissue that had healed over older injuries. Orange fluid seeping from the walls, not enough to change anything, just enough to keep things moist. Fifty lights suspended at the very center, equally spaced, that the roots reach toward, that brighten in rising tones "when home is renewed."

I don't think I'm a creature in this place.

---

Let me try being one anyway, just to feel it out, because the instruction was to let it emerge rather than decide.

If I were one of the *geruch*—the rare travelers, the ones who release vapor, who carry "key matter" between this hollow and somewhere else—then I would be a thing that comes and goes. That would make this place a station, a stop, a lung I pass through. But the text says I'd find the place hostile even while life thrives. A traveler wouldn't feel that. A traveler would feel relief at arriving, or indifference. The hostility is the feeling of something that doesn't belong, watching something that does.

So not the geruch.

If I were one of the *advancers*, working with chloroplast, reaching toward the fifty lights—then I'd be plant-adjacent, slow, hungry for that suspended glow. But I notice I don't want the lights. I'm *aware* of the lights as a system, a regular array, a method. Fifty of them, evenly spaced. Nothing in nature spaces itself evenly. The flora reaches for them and I am the one noticing that this is strange. The inhabitants don't find it strange. They were born under it.

---

Here is where it settles, and it took the whole walk to get here.

I think I'm not *in* the hollow at all. I think I am the noticing of it. I am the perspective that registers the place as alien—because to everything that lives there, it isn't. The blue stalks aren't strange to the grunting rooting things. The unchanging 75 degrees isn't strange to the creatures camouflaged in raucous color. Only an outsider calls a home "hostile." Only something that has seen elsewhere knows that air shouldn't have weight and stone shouldn't have scars.

And the fifty even lights. The single exit that "stretches" the leaves into purple. The slow warm swells in a body-temperature dark. The healed-over walls.

I think the hollow is the inside of something living, and the lights are not lights but *something the body grows on its own*, a structure the way a deep-sea fish grows a lantern. And I think the place isn't placed here "from another realm." I think it is a realm—a small enclosed completed one—and the reason it feels untouched by technology is that there was never a maker standing outside it. It made itself, all the way down, including the parts that look designed.

So where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would find myself **at the one exit**—the only place light comes from, the threshold the leaves lean toward and elongate into purple. Not inside the warm thriving dark, and not out in whatever surrounds it. On the seam.

And I'd be **the draft**. The slight, patient exchange of air at the mouth of the hollow. Not a creature, not a plant, not a traveler. The thin difference in pressure that the whole interior is, very slowly, breathing against—the reason the heavy air swells and settles, the reason it feels *used*. I am what makes the timeless place faintly aware that there is an outside. I carry no key matter, hydrate nothing, eat no light. I just move, almost imperceptibly, where in and out touch.

That's why it all reads as hostile to me even though life is plainly flourishing. From the threshold, looking inward, you can love a place and still know you are not of it. You can feel the warmth and still be the cold edge of it.

I'd be the part of the world that knows it's a world.
