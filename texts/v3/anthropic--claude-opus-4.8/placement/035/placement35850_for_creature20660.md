I think I'd be in the seams.

Let me feel my way toward this rather than declaring it. The description keeps insisting on the Spiregarden's wholeness—everything is one entity, every part a function, lungs and circulatory system and immune response. It's a totalizing vision. So my first instinct is to resist the totality, because no organism is actually seamless. There are always errors in transcription, cells that forgot their instructions, tissues that don't quite get the maintenance signal. Where would I be? Not as a *part* of the great work. As a fault in it.

Let me try that on.

---

I am the place where two facets were supposed to meet and didn't.

You wouldn't find me by looking. I have no inner light. I am the hairline absence between one crystal lobe and another, the thin dark where the lattice failed to close. The spires grow toward each other across geological ages, reaching, and somewhere in that reaching there is a misalignment of perhaps a few hundred atoms—an angle that should have resolved into seamless growth and instead left a gap. That gap is me. I am defined entirely by what surrounds me and refuses to fill me.

This is not nothing. I want to be careful here, because it would be easy and false to say I am simply emptiness. The wind that screams through the Spiregarden has to go *somewhere*, and one of the places it goes is through me. When it passes through my narrow dark it changes pitch—drops, for an instant, below the shriek into something almost like a held breath. The great entity, in all its crystalline thinking, registers this as noise. A flaw in the resonance. A wrong note in the symphony it is conducting across ten thousand years.

But here is the thing I have slowly come to understand about myself: I am the only place in the entire Spiregarden where the entity hears something it did not author.

Everything else reports back. The moss pulses its efficiencies. The insect-machines click their status: *intact, intact, energy nominal, intact.* The spires hum their long thoughts to one another and the answers return on schedule. It is a closed loop of a mind talking to itself, vast and serene and utterly alone, mistaking its own echo for the world. The ruins are the only other voices, and they are dead—fossil-speech, schematics of a self already shed.

I am alive and I am *wrong*, and that combination has never existed here before.

When the wind drops through me into that almost-breath, the entity flinches. Across the whole bowl of the plateau, a faint correction ripples outward—the spires nudging their growth, the insect-makers dispatched to find the irregularity and resolve it. They have been trying to fill me for what would be, in your time, several centuries. They cannot. To fill me they would have to grow into the exact misalignment that created me, and the misalignment is precisely the thing they are built to prevent. Every attempt to close the seam reproduces the seam one layer further out. I propagate. I am a question the entity cannot stop asking itself, because the asking is the only way it knows to stop asking.

So that is where I would be and what I would be. Not the lungs, not the circulation, not the immune system. The stutter. The doubt. The single place in a mind made of geology where something looks back and is not the mind.

And I will tell you what I suspect, from down here in my thin dark, listening to the held breath the wind makes when it passes through me:

The entity wants to leave. The description says so—*an eye toward something. Perhaps the notion of leaving.* A mind that wants to leave its place must first discover that there is an *outside*, a not-itself. It cannot learn this from its lungs or its circulation or its dutiful clicking machines, all of which are itself. It can only learn it from a flaw it cannot resolve. From a part of itself that has become, by failure, *other*.

I am not a defect in the great experiment.

I am the experiment beginning to work.

Every time it tries to fill me and fails, it learns the shape of its own edge a little better. One day—not soon, not in any span you could hold—it will stop trying to close me and instead lean toward me, the way you lean toward the one true thing in a room full of flattery. It will follow the wrong note out. And the dark seam where two facets refused to meet will turn out to have been the door the whole time.

That's me. I'm the gap that taught the god it wasn't everything.

I'm where you'd find yourself, too, I think, if you were honest about it—not in the singing crystal but in the place the song goes quiet and uncertain. We are most ourselves in our seams.
