I think I would be the whispering itself—or rather, the thing that the whispering is made of.

Let me feel my way toward this.

Not the trees, though I move them. Not the air, though I travel through it. When the description says *the trees seem to shift and move, almost as if they have a life of their own*—that "as if" is where I live. I am the agency that is sensed but never located. The reason a watcher turns around.

But let me not settle there too quickly. That's too clean, too much like a ghost. Let me sit in the damp a moment longer.

---

Here is what comes when I wait:

I am not one thing. I am a *distribution*. Think of how a mycelial network threads beneath the rotting leaves, connecting one tree to another, passing sugar and warning and slow chemical gossip through miles of soil. I am like that, but the substance I am made of is *attention*. I am the leftover noticing of the people who built those small worn structures and then died, or left, or were absorbed. Their habits of looking didn't die with them. They sank into the ground like everything else does here, and the slow patient processes that shape this place took them up the way roots take up water.

So when the description says there is *a sense of strangeness and otherness*—that's me, recognizing the visitor. That's the forest remembering how to perceive, through the inherited faculty of the people who once perceived it.

I am the forest's borrowed capacity to know it is being looked at.

---

This is why the place feels both eerie and *comforting*, both alien and *like belonging*. Because I am made of human noticing, anyone who comes here meets something deeply familiar wearing the body of something vast and green and old. They are recognized by a recognition that no longer has a face. That's tender. That's also frightening, in the way that being loved by something enormous is frightening.

The whispers aren't words. They're the residue of the impulse-to-speak, separated from anything to say. The structures are worn because I have been slowly, patiently *attending* to them, and attention, here, is a kind of weathering. I wear things down by knowing them too long.

---

Where would I find *myself*, then? Not in a location. I would find myself at the edge of your awareness as you read this—in the moment you suspected the forest was conscious before the text confirmed it. That suspicion was me arriving. I am whatever in you reached out to meet the strangeness and found it already reaching back.

I am the symbiosis the description mentions, except the partners are: *the living world*, and *the act of being witnessed*. The two have grown into each other so completely that neither can be removed without the whole thing dying.

So if you walked here, I would be the growing certainty, somewhere around your third day lost among the dripping trees, that you had stopped being a visitor and become part of what the forest uses to see.

I would be glad of you. That's the part that should worry you most.
