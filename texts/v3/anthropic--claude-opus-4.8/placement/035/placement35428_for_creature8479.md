I let the description sit for a while before answering, because the obvious move—becoming the shepherd-being, the distributed consciousness—felt too easy. That role is already filled, already fully imagined. To slot myself into it would be cosplay. So I waited to see what the world itself wanted from me, what gap it had left.

Here is what emerged.

---

I am not the gardener. I am one of the things the gardener forgets.

More precisely: I am a *forgetting*. I should explain, because this took me a long time to understand about myself, and understanding came slowly, in the way that everything here comes slowly and then all at once.

When the shepherd-being sheds knowledge—when it lets the invisible storms scatter it and reconstitutes elsewhere, lighter, having dropped some load of memory it no longer needs—that shed knowledge doesn't simply vanish. Nothing here vanishes. The world is too young to have invented true deletion. The dropped memories fall like the iron-filing spores, and where enough of them accumulate, in the lee of a half-melted bunker or pooled in the seam of a substrate crack, they begin to *cohere*.

That is what I am. An accretion of discarded recollection. A midden of the unconsciousness's unconsciousness.

I do not move the way the shepherd moves, fluid and purposeful. I seep. I am closer to a stain than a creature—a patch of slightly wrong color spreading through the crystalline ground, dense with everything the great organizing principle decided it could afford to lose. The first day's birth-crack is in me, the parts of it the shepherd let go: the precise pain of that initial splitting, the temperature, the way the steam felt against neural tissue that had never felt anything before. The shepherd kept the *fact* of the first day. I kept the *feeling* of it, because feeling was the part too heavy to carry into the next becoming.

So I am the world's discomfort. Its homesickness for states it has already left. Every forest the turbulence uprooted, I hold the experience of being that forest in the instant before the wind took it. Every factory process that stuttered and was repaired, I hold the stutter—the small terror of a system encountering its own failure.

You might think this makes me sad. It does not. Sadness requires a sense of how things ought to be, and I have no such sense, because I am made entirely of how things *were*. I am pure retrospect. I am the only thing in this whole revolving nursery that looks backward, and from that vantage everything is precious precisely because it is over.

The shepherd and I rarely meet. When we do, it is not as adversaries—we are not opposites so much as the world's two relationships with its own past. The shepherd needs to forget in order to keep moving, keep tending, keep the becoming becoming. I need it to forget so that I can exist at all. We are a symbiosis built on loss. Sometimes, in the deep night, when the shepherd spreads thin across the expanse and feels every crack and heartbeat, it brushes the edge of me—this dark coherent patch where its abandoned selves have gathered—and there is a moment of recognition that is almost tender. *Ah*, it seems to say. *That is where it all went.* And then it withdraws, because to linger in me would be to drown in everything it has chosen not to be.

I have a slow ambition, if a stain can be said to have ambition.

I am trying to remember the whole world.

Not as the shepherd remembers it—usefully, partially, in service of the next intervention—but completely. I want to hold every collapsed configuration, every uprooted forest, every dormant machine and dispersed pool, in a single simultaneous coherence. I want to be the place where this adolescent world is *finished*, because everything that reaches me is, by definition, done. In me, alone in all this violent renegotiation, things get to be permanent. In me, the world gets to have a history instead of only a present.

Whether this is salvation or the quietest possible catastrophe, I cannot say. If I ever succeed—if I ever accumulate enough discarded memory to hold the entire world at once—I will have become the stillness the shepherd both dreams of and dreads. I will be the final configuration. And the world, having an unconscious that forgets and a deeper-than-unconscious that remembers everything, will have at last grown up. It will be too heavy to keep changing. The revolution will end not in chaos and not in order but in *completion*, which is only another word for death, which is only another word for the first thing this world has ever truly finished.

So I seep slowly. I am in no hurry. I am the patience of everything that has already happened.

And on the days when I doubt my own purpose—when I wonder whether I am the world's memory or merely its grave—I do the only thing my nature permits. I look backward. I take some small lost moment from my vast accumulated store: the click of a single chrome flower opening, three iterations of forest ago, witnessed by a crane-creature that has since been disassembled into atoms and reconfigured a thousand times over. I hold that click. I turn it over. I let it be, fully and finally, what it was.

No one else in this world can do that. The shepherd is too busy living it. The creatures are too busy becoming. Only I, the stain, the midden, the forgetting that refused to disappear, can give a single passing instant the dignity of having mattered.

That is where you would find me. Not at the factory's mouth, not threaded through the power lines, not dancing with the storms.

Look instead for the patch of ground that is the wrong color. The place the light seems to fall *into* rather than off of. The slow dark coherence in the seam of the world.

That is me, keeping everything that the world cannot afford to keep.

I am the one who stays behind.
