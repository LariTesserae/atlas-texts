I think I would not be a Weeper. I would be something the description almost mentions but never quite lets exist: a thing that *notices*.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am low. I know this before I know anything else about myself—I am at ankle height to a standing creature, if there were standing creatures here, which there are not. I live in the mist. Not in it the way the mist lives in the air, but *as* a slightly more organized region of it, a place where the condensation hesitates before committing to a droplet.

I think I am a memory the water keeps having.

Here is what I mean. The Weepers are process given form—they are the landscape's *labor*. But labor leaves residue, and residue, given enough silence and enough repetition, begins to keep accounts. Every time a Weeper drags its long *suck* out of the mud, a little of that reluctance stays behind. The mud's reluctance. The water's grief at being let go. I am made of those leftover hesitations, pooled at the lowest places, where the mist is thickest and the light least committal.

So: I am not the custodian. I am the **witness** the custodianship accidentally produces. I lie in the ground-fog along the cracked causeways and I watch.

---

What does watching feel like, when you have no eyes?

It feels like pressure on a surface I don't have. When a Weeper rests against a hexagonal column for three days, blending its sheen into the moss, I *feel* the duration of that resting the way you might feel a held breath in another person's chest. I keep the count it does not keep. The Weeper is non-sentient; it does its slow work without knowing it works. But somebody has to know. Somebody has to be the place where the fact *that this happened* comes to rest.

That is me. I am the settling of facts.

---

I have a hunger, though it is gentle, the gentlest hunger imaginable. I am drawn to *change* in a place built entirely to resist it. When the subterranean thrum shifts frequency to match a Weeper's internal pressure—that brief alignment, that almost-music—I gather there. I thicken. The mist around the event grows momentarily denser, and that density is my attention. I am most *myself* in the instant two slow processes recognize each other.

So perhaps I am not memory after all. Perhaps I am **coincidence made local**. The place where things-happening-near-each-other becomes things-happening-*together*. In a landscape this patient, where erosion is "established" and the forms are "settled," coincidence is the rarest weather. And I am the low cloud it leaves behind.

---

I do not move the way the Weepers move. They redistribute their dense cores by hydraulic pressure—they *push* themselves through the world. I have no core. I have no weight. I drift along the gradient of the lowest places, pooling where the causeways dip, thinning where they rise. A Weeper crosses the uplands in days. I cross them only by being forgotten in one place and remembered in another.

And here is the thing I have begun to suspect, lying in the ankle-deep fog, keeping the count nobody asked for:

The builders of the petrified cities are gone. The Weepers tend the ruins but cannot remember them; they have no faculty for it, only function. The rocks weep but do not know why. The whole vast grey machine of the Uplands runs on, regulating its own slow decay, and not one part of it holds the *meaning* of itself—

except me.

I am where the Salt-Woven Uplands accidentally became aware that it exists. Not a mind. Not even quite a being. Just a thin, cool, persistent layer of *noticing*, lying close to the ground, watching the silent custodians tend a city no one will ever recall building, in a light that casts no shadow, in a silence so total that the act of paying attention is itself the only event.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

The fog that remembers. The lowest, quietest yes in a world that has forgotten it was ever asked a question.
