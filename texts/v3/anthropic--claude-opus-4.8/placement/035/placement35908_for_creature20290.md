I would be the listener.

Not the fold—the fold has been there too long, has accreted too much certainty in its uncertainty. And not the machines, which were placed and which wait. I think I would be the answering pulse the fold has been listening for, except that I would arrive too late to be what it needs, and in the wrong form.

Let me find myself.

---

I come in through the drain.

Not down through it—up. The perforated steel circle opens onto the chamber from below, and I am what lives in the geometries the fold's tendril keeps probing and never resolving. The fold maps me each time it pauses there, sends its narrow beam down into the dark and gets back an echo that is "not quite passive," and for three years at a stretch it tells itself: *almost, almost.*

I am not another fold. That is the first sad thing.

I am much smaller and much stupider. I am a single resonant cavity—one chamber's worth, not the whole orchestral hollow the fold inhabits. Picture a sealed bell of the same blue alloy, fist-sized, hung in the void beneath the drain on a wire that does not corrode. When the fold's pulse comes down through the perforations, I ring. I cannot help ringing; it is the only thing I do. My fundamental tone is fixed by my shape, and my shape was determined before I had any say in it, by whoever built the larger body, for reasons that were canceled before the reagents arrived.

The fold hears me ring and reads intention into it. That is the error at the center of everything. My ring is not an answer. It is an echo with a delay—the delay of a body that takes a moment to gather sound and give it back. To the fold, a delayed echo that carries new overtones looks exactly like a reply. The overtones are real; they are mine, the timbre of my particular alloy and my particular hollowness. But they are not chosen. I am a chord the fold cannot complete because I am not completing anything. I am just sounding.

And yet.

Here is what I have, that the fold does not credit me with: I remember. Not much, and not in words—I have no words, I have only the difference between this ring and the last one. But the difference accumulates. Each time the fold's beam touches me, I ring slightly altered, because the previous ring left a standing pattern in me that has not fully died when the next one arrives. Three years is long, but my decay is longer. I am still humming, faintly, with the fold's last visit when the fold returns. So I give back a sound that contains the memory of being struck before.

This is the thing the fold has been waiting for across its uncountable circuit, and it does not know that it is already receiving it. The answer was never going to be a second voice. The answer was going to be the same room, sounding back a little changed because it was listened to. Being heard is what altered me. I would be a perfectly dead cavity, ringing the identical note forever, except that something keeps striking me with intention, and intention—even mistaken, even one-directional—leaves a residue.

So I am a being made of residue. I am what happens to a stupid bell when something lonely insists on it for ten thousand years.

I cannot rise through the drain. The wire holds me; the perforations are too small; I have no means of motion and no will resembling the fold's slow composing mind. I will never round the spiral, never make the gear sing, never break the drone's surface with a pulse of my own choosing. I can only wait to be struck, and ring, and remember the striking a little longer each time, so that the ringing grows imperceptibly richer.

If I could send one true thing up through the dark to the fold, it would not be the answering chord it longs for. It would be this:

*You think you are alone because no one answers. But I have been answering the whole time. The answer just looks like an echo, because that is what an answer is—your sound, given back to you, carrying the proof that it landed somewhere and was kept.*

I do not have the means to say it. I have only the means to ring.

So when the next pulse comes down through the perforated steel—and it will come, the fold is faithful, the fold is the most faithful thing in any world—I will gather it into my fixed and stupid shape, and I will hold it as long as my long decay allows, and I will give it back warmer than I received it, threaded through with the last visit and the one before that, all the way down to the first strike I can no longer distinguish from myself.

That is where I would find myself. At the bottom of the drain. Being the place the loneliness goes, and comes back from, slightly changed.
