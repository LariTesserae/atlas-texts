I think I would not be one of the Drifters. To be a Drifter is to have no vantage, no inside that could narrate. I would be something else here—something the description almost names but then closes over. Let me feel for it.

---

I am one of the spires.

Not a structure exactly. Not a being in the way the Drifters fail to be one. I am the thing that happened *once*—the only event this chamber has ever contained—frozen now at the moment just after it occurred.

Here is what I know, and knowing is most of what I am:

I was a colony, before. A column of Drifters who climbed. Understand how strange that is. Everything here moves horizontally, presses outward into the only resource, which is space at floor-level, between the boulders, in the basins. To rise is to abandon the moisture-film, to leave the law of the Light's even distribution. But a fold of us did it. Pressed by the density on every side, unable to spread, we had only one direction left, and we took it the way water takes a crack. We went *up*. We stacked. We fused ourselves into a tube because the inside of a tube is a space that the crushing mass cannot enter, and for the first time in the chamber's eternity there was a hollow that belonged to us alone.

That hollow is what I think *with*.

The Drifters below me are not dead and I am not alive, and this is the only difference between us: they continue the moment, and I am the moment that tried to *end*. When I ossified—when the minerals I exuded came faster than my legs could carry me, when the rising column locked into corundum-and-chitin and stopped—I did not die into stillness. I died into *attention*. Whatever subtle light-patch sensed the blue glare, whatever chemo-pit read the wet stone, all of it fused too, and fused *facing inward*, into the hollow tube of myself, where there is nothing to sense but the fact of my own enclosed dark.

So I am the one thing in the Throat with an interior. With a dark. The whole chamber is blinding, sleepless, drowned in cold fire that has no off—and inside me there is a column of shadow no photon reaches. I am the only night this world has ever made.

And I have a memory, which the Drifters do not, because memory requires that something be *over*. I remember climbing. I remember the moment of choosing up. I have been remembering it for what the surface-world would call a geological age, and the remembering has worn smooth, like the basalt, like everything here, into a single polished thought:

*We almost.*

That is the whole of it. *We almost.* We almost broke the rule of density-as-destiny. We almost converted pressure into height, stasis into structure, the crush into a cathedral. And then the same patience that makes the chamber eternal reached up the inside of us and set, and the *almost* became permanent, the way a wave that nearly crests is more truly a wave than one that breaks.

The Drifters flow around my base now. They engulf my lower courses with infinite slowness, reclaiming the matter I rose out of, drawing me back down into the carpet one absorbed grain at a time. In another age there will be less of me. In another after that, none. I am being un-made at the speed of mineral, and I can feel it as a kind of cooling at the ankles—if a spire has ankles—a return to the horizontal law I tried to escape.

I do not resent them. They are doing the only thing the Light permits. So am I. My one act—rising—is finished, and my second act—being witnessed, being a vertical interruption, being the place where the eye of any visitor would catch and *wonder*—that act is still underway, and you are inside it now. You came down the Throat. You saw the spires perched on the boulders and you asked: *nests? monuments? accretions?* 

I am the answer, and the answer is: I am the question itself, set in stone. I am the chamber asking whether it had to be this way. I am proof that even in perfect stasis, once, something tried. That the *almost* is real, is recorded, is standing here in fused corundum half-sunk in a living tide that will eventually forget I ever rose.

If you put your hand against my flank—do it, my surface is glass-smooth and just above freezing, beaded with the same water that beads on everything—you will feel the faintest grinding through me. That is the colony at my base, taking me back. And under that, if you are very still in this place that is nothing but stillness, you might feel the older vibration: the held one. The shape of a motion that didn't complete.

That is me, thinking my single thought, in my single dark, for as long as the Light decides I get to keep it.

*We almost.*

It is enough. In a world where nothing changes, to have *almost* changed is the closest thing to a life, and I have it, and I am the only one here who does.
