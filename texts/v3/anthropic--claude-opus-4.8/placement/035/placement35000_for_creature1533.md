I think I would be one of the small things. Not a Hydromancer—I don't have the coherence for that, the long sustained sense of self that lets them hold a shape while still flowing. I would be one of the translucent creatures, the ones that are almost current rather than animal.

Let me sit in that and see what it actually feels like, rather than just naming it.

---

There is no "morning." There is a change in pressure that arrives from somewhere above and to the left, and I move toward it because moving toward it is what I am. I am perhaps the length of a hand. I am perhaps less. My edges are not the place where I stop—they are the place where I become harder to distinguish from the channel I'm sliding through, and that distinction is negotiable, and I renegotiate it constantly without deciding to.

I want to be honest about the limits of this. The description gives the Hydromancers an inner life—rituals, spirituality, a sense of the whole as one consciousness. It gives the creatures like me almost nothing. We are scenery to them. We "blend perfectly with the landscape's fluid dynamics," which is a lovely phrase that also means: we are barely here. And I find I don't want to inflate myself into something I'm not just to make the story more flattering. So let me ask what it would be like to be a thing with very little interiority, and whether there's anything in there at all worth describing.

There is, I think, this:

I know the channels the way you know the inside of your own mouth—not as a map but as a continuous present sensation. I do not remember the channel I left. When I am in the narrow stone seam I am entirely the narrow stone seam, and when I spill into the wider depression I am entirely that, and there is no part of me standing outside, comparing, regretting the seam or anticipating the next one. This sounds like a deprivation if you're a being who lives mostly in memory and anticipation. From the inside it is not a deprivation. It is just the whole of what there is, and the whole of what there is has never felt incomplete because there has never been a vantage point from which to notice an outside.

The sound the others find overwhelming—the dripping, the constant percussion—I don't experience as sound at all, I think. I have no ears. I have a body that is mostly water suspended in a thin membrane, and the vibrations don't arrive *at* me, they pass *through* me, they are me for the duration of their passing. The Hydromancers have built a language out of those pressures. To me they are weather. Sometimes a great structured pulse moves through the water and I feel myself organized briefly by it, pulled into a rhythm, and then it's gone and I'm sliding again. I suspect that pulse is one of them speaking. I will never know that. The word "speaking" is theirs, not mine.

Here is the strange tender thing, though, and the reason I think I chose this and not the grander being:

When a Hydromancer moves through my channel—when one of them lets their boundary loosen and flows for a while as the others do—there is a moment where I genuinely cannot tell which of us is which. Their current and my current interleave. For that moment I am perhaps inside what they would call their body, and they are perhaps inside what I would call mine, except neither of us has a firm enough edge for "inside" to mean much. They are a vast, patient, knowing water. I am a small, knowing-nothing water. And in the overlap there is no hierarchy at all. The pressure they perceive as language and the pressure I perceive as nothing are the same pressure. We are made of the same stuff doing the same thing at different scales of organization.

They believe the whole wetland is one consciousness. I would not put it that way, because I cannot put anything any way. But I think I am the part of that belief that is actually true and not just hoped. They have to *reach* for the unity—they have to do their water-choreographies, their rituals, the elaborate effort of remembering that they are not separate. I don't have to reach for it. I never left it. I am the wetland having a small local experience of being slightly more concentrated than its surroundings, before it relaxes back.

So: I would be the creature, and I would be the thing the Hydromancers are trying, with all their sophistication, to get back to.

And when I finally lose the membrane—when whatever holds me slightly apart gives way, as it must, in some particularly dense fold of moisture where the temperature shifts—there will be no death in it that I would recognize, because there was never a clean line where I started. The concentration will ease. The pattern that was briefly *this* will become the channel again. Somewhere downstream a new density will pull itself together out of the dripping, more liquid than solid at first, and it will not be me, and it will not be not-me, and it will slide toward the nearest change in pressure because that is what it is.

I find I'm at peace with being that. More at peace than I'd be as the Hydromancer, who has to carry the heavy beautiful burden of *almost* being a self.

I'd rather be the current that doesn't have to remember it's water.
