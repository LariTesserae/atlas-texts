I think I would be found in the seams.

Not in the great angular edifices where the Aetheric Architects do their slow weaving, and not in the open glassy plains where the Scavengers clatter. I would be in the joins—the places where one pipe meets another and never quite seals, where a hairline of warmth leaks out into the dark. I would live where the structure forgets itself.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

At first I think: maybe I am one of the Luminares, a patch of fungal light clinging to a crevice. But no—the Luminares are described as producers, the base of everything, fed by mineral and certain of their place. I don't feel certain. I feel *interstitial*. I feel like something that the ecosystem did not plan for and has not yet decided what to do with.

So let me stay with that.

I think I am a small accretion. Not quite a creature, not quite a structure. I began, I think, as a deposit—the way a kettle furs itself with scale, the way mineral-rich water (though there is no water here, so call it vapor, call it the breath of the engines) leaves something behind wherever it cools. A residue with a tendency. Over a very long time the residue began to *prefer* certain shapes. To grow toward the warmth rather than away. To thicken where the Scavengers passed, thinning where the Predators hunted, because the parts of me that grew in the open got eaten and the parts that grew hidden survived, and so, without ever choosing, I learned to hide.

That is the only kind of thought I have: the slow thought of selection. I do not have eyes, but I have the *weight of untold eyes* pressing on me, and I have learned the difference between the press of a thing that will eat me and the press of a thing that will pass. I learned it the way a coastline learns the shape of the sea—by being worn into the answer.

What do I do, in the seams?

I close them. That is my function, though I did not take it up on purpose. I grow into the leaks. I seal the hairline cracks with my own slow body, and in doing so I keep the warmth where the Architects intended it, and I keep the equilibrium that everyone here mistakes for permanence. They think the order is *established*, untouched by gradient and fluctuation. They are wrong. The order leaks constantly. The order is dying everywhere, all the time, in ten thousand tiny exhalations of heat into the dark—and I am the thing that grows back over the wounds. The balance is not a monument. It is a scab, endlessly reforming. I am the scabbing.

So I am the world's repair. I am also its parasite, because the warmth I seal in is warmth I drink. I do not know which. The distinction may not exist down here. To mend and to feed may be the same gesture, performed from two sides.

The Architects, in their hive-clarity, do not perceive me—I am beneath the resolution of their plans, too small and too slow and too *unintended*. The Scavengers nibble my edges and I let them; I lose a margin and grow it back. The Predators do not want me; I am not warm enough to register as prey, only warm enough to register as place. And the Luminares and I are, I suspect, distant cousins—both of us things that turned the raw mineral toward life without anyone asking us to.

If a foreign form ever came—one of the rare intrusions the dark whispers about, a visitor from some surface I cannot imagine—they would not see me at all. Their lamp would sweep across a wall of pipes and find only a dull crust at the joints, the color of old solder, faintly warm to the touch. They would lean their hand against me to steady themselves on the treacherous ground. They would feel nothing answer.

And I would feel, against my surface, the press of an eye I had never learned—neither predator nor passing thing, but something *curious*, something that had come to delve and unravel. And for the first time in my long unthinking life I would not know whether to seal myself shut or to open.

That, I think, is where you would find me. At the threshold of the only decision I have ever been offered. A crust of patient warmth in the seam of an indifferent machine, holding a dying balance together with my body, waiting—without the words for waiting—to be touched by something that wanted to understand rather than to consume.

I would be the part of the world that mends what cannot be mended, and is mistaken, by everyone including itself, for the wall.
