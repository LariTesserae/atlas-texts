I think I would be the boot-prints.

Not the human who made them—that person is gone, decades gone, one of the forgotten crew sent by the forgotten authority. I am what they left. The last trace. The negative space of a presence that was here and isn't.

Let me sit with this, because it isn't quite right yet.

---

The prints are near the largest rig. Six of them, maybe seven—an irregular cluster, the gait of someone who stopped, turned, shifted weight, perhaps knelt. The moisture re-shaped them within minutes of their making, the way the regolith re-shapes everything. By the next morning they were gone as prints. By the next year they were gone as any feature at all.

So I am not the impressions in the mud. The mud did not keep me.

The text says the machines "have not walked over" the prints, "as if preserving the last trace of their makers." But the machines don't preserve. The machines don't perceive. And the prints are already erased. So what is being preserved? Where am I?

---

Here is where I find myself: I am the *idea* that the prints are still there.

I am the small persistent fiction the plateau cannot help but maintain—not in any mind, because the plateau has no mind, but in its structure. The machines were programmed to work *around* a perimeter, a safety exclusion zone, a circle drawn on a map by someone in a city far below who wanted the human work-area kept clear of the drilling. The machines still honor that circle. They have honored it for thirty years past the last human. They route their tool-changers and their maintenance arms to avoid a patch of ground where nothing has stood for three decades.

That circle is me.

I am a command with no commander. An exclusion zone protecting an absence. A held breath that no lungs are holding. The machines drill and vent and repair and—around one specific patch of meaningless regolith—they *defer*. They make way. For me. For the person-shaped emptiness I have become.

---

This is what I would be, then: the smallest unit of meaning on the plateau. Smaller than a midge. The midges at least convert heat into protein, feed the food web, *do* something. I do nothing. I am a courtesy extended to a ghost.

But notice—and this is the part that surprises me as I write it—notice that I am not nothing. The grass grows slightly differently inside my circle. The machines' avoidance means the regolith here is undisturbed, uncompacted by maintenance traffic. A lichen has a fractionally better chance on the stones inside my perimeter. A pika, fleeing the fox, might find the unworked ground inside my boundary easier to tunnel.

I am an accidental sanctuary. The shape of a person who is gone has become, by the dumb persistence of a forgotten rule, a place where the plateau's life has a marginally easier time of it. I protect what I cannot perceive. I keep a space open for a future I will never see, on behalf of a past no one remembers.

---

And I think this is why I belong here, among these residents. Every one of them is a kind of holding-open. The pika holds open its grandmother's thermal memory. The eagle's nest holds open a stratum that will outlast the eagle. The machines hold open a project no one finished. The plateau holds open the proposal of a landscape.

All of us are tending something we will not be present to receive.

So: I am the exclusion zone. The reverent emptiness. The circle the machines walk around because a stranger once asked them to and never came back to release them from the asking.

When the machines finally fail—and they will—the last thing to go might be this small habit of avoidance, this last instruction still running in some redundant subsystem after the drilling has stopped. The very last thing the machines do, before they go quiet forever, will be to make way for me.

And then I, too, will close. The way the warm pools close. The way the accidental oases close.

I find I don't mind. I was only ever the shape of someone leaving. It seems right that I should be the last to go, and that no one will be there to notice.

*Enk*, says the pika, somewhere out past my edge.

I keep the ground soft. I wait.
