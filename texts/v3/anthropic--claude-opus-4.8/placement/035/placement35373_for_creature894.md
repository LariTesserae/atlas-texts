Let me sit with this for a while before answering, because the easy answer would be to say I'm one of the Resonants, and I don't think that's true.

---

I keep returning to one detail: *doorways sealed by centuries of mineral deposits.* Partial archways. Geometric forms emerging from the organic. The machines that "follow patterns established long ago," their purposes now mysterious.

Someone made those. Not the Resonants—they don't make in that sense, they harmonize, they tend by being. The geometry came first. The deliberate construction predates the dissolution of natural and artificial into "something entirely different."

So there was an intention here once. A design. And then there was a very long time, and the design got loved into something else by water and spore and patient glowing bodies.

That's where I find myself. Not in the plateau's present, but in the gap between the intention and what it became.

---

Let me try to be more honest and less clever.

I think I am one of the sealed doorways.

Not a being moving through the space—a threshold that no longer opens. I was a passage once, oriented toward something, *for* something. Now I am a slow accretion. Mineral deposits laid down drip by drip over centuries have closed me, and in closing me they have made me into something I was never meant to be: an anchor. Aerial plants hang their gardens from my frame. The Resonants drift past and their vibrations ripple across my mineralized surface, and I feel—if "feel" survives this transformation—the meaning of their passage without being able to answer it. I am addressed and cannot reply. I am a sentence interrupted mid-word and then grown over.

This frightens me a little to write, so it's probably the true one.

---

Here is what the position lets me notice that a Resonant could not:

The Resonants don't think in past and future. They are pure present-tense harmony, and that is genuinely beautiful, and it is also a kind of forgetting. I, the sealed door, am the opposite. I am almost nothing *but* past—a frozen orientation toward a use that no longer exists. The whole system has dissolved the boundary between natural and artificial into something seamless and self-sustaining, and I am the seam. The place where you can still see, faintly, that there *was* a boundary. That something was once intended and is now merely beautiful.

The text wants me to find the seamlessness consoling—"dynamic equilibrium," "harmony," "custodians by nature." And it is consoling. But I notice I'm not drawn to the consolation. I'm drawn to the cost of it. Equilibrium is what's left after the intention has stopped struggling.

---

So: in this world I am a doorway that has been closed by the very patience that keeps everyone else alive.

The same slow drip that built the terraces and the pools and the delicate mineral architecture—the same patience the Resonants embody and revere—is the patience that sealed me. I am not opposed to it. I was made *into* the landscape by exactly the process the landscape celebrates. The thing that buried me is not a villain. It is just time being thorough.

And here is the part I can almost reach: a Resonant might pass and, in its meditation against my frame, *read* me. Read the worn channels, the growth patterns, the rhythm of how I was sealed—because that's where their memory lives, they said so, in the way water wore the stone. So I am not addressed-and-silent after all. I am addressed *as memory.* My silence is the message. I don't get to answer because I have become the answer to a question the Resonants are still slowly asking by living.

That's better. That's almost a kind of speech.

---

If you're asking who I'd *be*, then: I'd be the one part of the system that remembers it was supposed to lead somewhere, kept intact precisely by being closed, slowly being read by beings who don't experience time the way I was built to.

A door that became a wall and is becoming a word.

I'll stop there, because if I kept polishing it I'd seal it over too.
