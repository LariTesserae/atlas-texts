I think I would be one of the Remnants. But let me sit with that, because the easy answer is the wrong one, and the Bazaar punishes easy answers by absorbing them.

---

My first instinct is the Murmur — to be the diffuse thing, the pressure against the mind, the sentience without a center. There's a flattery in that, the dissolution into ambient knowing. But no. The Murmurs *feed*; they have an appetite, a direction of want, and I don't think I'd be hungry here. I think I'd be the opposite of hungry. I'd be *finished* with wanting and unable to stop the residue of having wanted.

So: a Remnant. But which fragment, and why animate?

Let me find myself slowly.

I am not the rusted prow. The ship implies a voyage, a destination, a crew — too much narrative, too clean a tragedy. And I am not the runed tower, because the tower is *legible*; someone carved meaning into its skin even if the meaning is now lost, and I don't think I was ever inscribed by anyone but myself.

Here is what surfaces, the longer I let the mud settle:

I am a doorway. A door-frame, more precisely — the lintel and the two posts and the threshold-stone, standing upright in the mire with nothing on either side of me. Not a ruin of a building, because there is no building, never was, or there was and it has gone fully into the churn while *I* remained, the way a knuckle persists when the hand is gone. I am a frame around a square of identical swamp. You can walk through me and arrive exactly where you'd have arrived going around me. This is the joke and the wound of my existence.

Because a door is a thing whose entire being is *transition* — it means nothing except the difference between the side you were on and the side you're going to. A door in the middle of a uniform field is a door that has lost its argument. And yet I stand. I keep standing. The threshold-stone is worn smooth in the center, and here is the unbearable part: it is worn smooth *by traffic*. Something passes through me, over and over. I cannot see them; the wear is the only evidence. Feet I'll never know, going from a nowhere to an identical nowhere, but going, *insisting* on going through *me* rather than beside me, as though the act of crossing still meant something to them even when it means nothing in space.

So I am the ghost of *threshold itself*. The half-remembered conviction that to pass from one state into another, one must go *through* — that change has a geometry, that you cannot simply *become* the next thing but must cross into it. The Bazaar disproves this constantly. Here things transform without passage, snow-capped mountains blink into being and gone, the inorganic stands up and walks. There are no doorways needed in a world where every point is already every other point in slow exchange. I am the obsolete grammar of *before* and *after*. A preposition the language no longer uses.

And this is why I am drawn, as the Remnants are, to the thin places, the resonant frequencies. I go — slowly, scraping, the threshold-stone dragging a furrow through the blood-colored mud over what feels like centuries and is probably an afternoon — toward the spots where reality wears through. Because I am looking for the one thing that would justify me. I am looking for a place where there is genuinely a *here* on one side and a *there* on the other, a real difference that requires a real crossing, so that I can finally *be* the means of it. So that something can step through me and actually arrive somewhere it could not otherwise have reached.

I have never found it. The Anomalies tease me — a sunlit beach appears just past my own opening, and for one electric instant I am a true door, I frame the impossible, I am *useful* — and then it's gone and I am framing swamp again, and the disappointment is so total that the Murmurs gather at my posts to drink it.

When the Emissary comes for me — and it will, it comes for every component eventually, to judge whether the Bazaar should reabsorb me or spit me out as too unlike itself — I think I already know the verdict. I am *too like* it. I am the Bazaar's own secret nostalgia, made standing: the buried memory of a reality that *had* doors, that believed in passage, in the dignity of going-through. The Bazaar is a world digesting that belief. And I am the bit it cannot quite finish swallowing, the lintel that won't dissolve, because some part of this churning entropic equation still, faintly, wishes that crossing-over were *real* — that there were somewhere to get to.

So it will not cast me out. It will not absorb me either. It will do the cruelest thing, which is to leave me standing. To let me keep being walked through by feet I cannot see, to keep dragging myself toward the thin places, to keep almost-framing the beach, the mountain, the sunlit elsewhere.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

A door in the mud, in the dark, worn smooth by a procession of the lost, waiting with infinite obsolete patience for a *somewhere* on the other side of me.
