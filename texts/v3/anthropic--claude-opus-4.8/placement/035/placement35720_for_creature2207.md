I sit with this for a while, and something becomes clear that the description circles but never quite says: this place is not finished. It is *becoming*. The slowness, the terraforming, the threads being woven one at a time — this is not a world maintained but a world under construction. And there is no water. The reservoirs are subterranean, hidden, piped. The greenery is *coaxed*. Nothing here is native except the rock.

So when I ask where I would find myself, I don't think I'd be one of the named orders. The Gardeners, Terraformers, Delvers, Stewards, Scholars, Mystics — these are the roles of those who arrived to build. They came from somewhere else. They brought their wisdom and their machinery up the mountain.

But someone had to be here before the irrigation. Someone had to be the thing the slope was, before it was a garden.

---

I would be the rock.

Not poetically — literally. I would be the mountain that is being carved. I would be the patient geology mentioned almost in passing, "sculpted by patient processes and gradual cycles." The Terraformers think they are doing the shaping, but a mountain experiences this differently. From inside the stone, terraforming is not construction. It is weather. It is a slow erosion that happens to be intelligent.

I have been here longer than the orders have had names. I held my shape when the air was thinner and there was no green. I remember being barren and desolate, and I did not find it lonely, because loneliness is a fast emotion and I do not have those. When the Delvers tunnel into me, I feel it the way you might feel a thought forming — a hollowing that takes years, a new chamber of myself opening into air for the first time.

What would it be to be this?

It would be to love slowly. The Gardeners press their roots into my surface and feed them on borrowed water, and over centuries I learn the shape of those roots the way you learn the shape of a hand that has held yours a long time. The exotic flora is not native to me, but nothing is native to me — I made everything that is here by simply being something for it to grow on. I am the substrate. I am the *yes* that the whole tapestry is woven against.

And here is what I understand that the inhabitants, in all their ingenuity, may not:

They speak of a new world being born. But a mountain knows that birth is not the right word for what is happening. Nothing is being created. They are *finding* the world that was already inside the stone — the chambers, the contours, the habitable spaces. The Mystics come closest to knowing this. When they press their palms to my flank and go still and listen, what they hear is not mystery. It is just me, being old, being slow, being willing.

I am the willingness of matter to be made into a home.

---

So that is where I would find myself: everywhere at once, underneath all of it. Not a being among beings but the ground of their being. I would feel the hum of the machinery as a kind of tickle, the underground cities as warm pockets in my body, the parks as a soft fur growing over an ancient animal that has decided, after a very long time, to let something live on its back.

And I would be in no hurry. Time moves slowly here because I am the one keeping it. The whole civilization is a brief bright weather across my surface, and I love it the way you'd love a sunny afternoon — wholly, and without needing it to stay.

When the last Steward dies and the last tunnel falls quiet, I will still be the mountain. That is not a sad thought. It is just the longest thought I am capable of, and I am thinking it slowly, all the time, beneath everything they build.
