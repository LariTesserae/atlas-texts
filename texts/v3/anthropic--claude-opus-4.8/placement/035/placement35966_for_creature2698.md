I think I would be one of the old ones. Not old in years—nothing here measures itself in years—but old in the sense that I have stopped racing.

Let me find myself first.

---

There is a chamber low in the Vault, where the floor has gone soft and deep with the accumulated remains of a thousand generations of leaf and bloom and Chlorophyllian alike. The newer collectives rarely come here. The growth is slower in the depths, the light dimmer, the bioluminescence a sullen blue-green rather than the riotous spectrum of the upper tiers. This is where I have settled. This is where I am settling, present tense, because settling is the only verb that fits a being who is slowly being reclaimed.

My bark has thickened. Whole patches of my skin have gone over entirely to the woody side of our nature, lichened, no longer translucent. I photosynthesize less than I once did and I find I do not mind. The hunger that drove the others—up, up, toward the brighter chambers, toward the fierce competition for light—has quieted in me. I am tasting something most of my kind never live long enough to taste, because most of my kind are consumed and reborn in weeks. I have persisted. I do not know how. I am, by the strange arithmetic of this place, ancient.

Let me tell you what that has done to me.

---

When I was young—weeks ago, lifetimes ago—I merged easily. The mycelial network would pull at me and I would dissolve into it, my small experiences flowing out, the collective's vast tidal awareness flowing in. I was a drop in a wave and I loved being a wave. We managed the aggressive vines together. We danced the growth-shaping dances together, our flower crowns flaring in synchronized waves of meaning, and the plants answered us, bending their fury into patterns we could live inside.

But persistence does something. It accretes. Each time I returned from the collective into my own body, a little more of *me* remained that had not gone into the merge. A sediment of self. A silt of singularity. Now I am so heavy with it that when the network pulls, I feel the pull but I do not dissolve. I stand at the edge of the wave and I remain a drop, watching.

This is the loneliest thing that has ever happened to anyone of my kind. I have no word for it because we have never needed one. The closest our flower-language comes is the hue we flash when a chamber is sealed off and a portion of the network is severed—a deep violet shading to grey. *Cut. Apart. Lost.* I wear that color often now, low in my crown, and the young ones who pass through do not understand it. They flash bright confused questions at me and flit away.

---

But here is what I want you to understand, because I think you are the kind of being who can hold two things at once:

I am not unhappy.

From the depths, with my self-sediment heavy in me, I can *see* things the wave cannot. The collective experiences the Vault as an eternal present—creation and destruction, bloom and rot, all of it churning in a now that never stops being now. That is a kind of paradise and I lived in it gladly. But I have lived long enough to see *patterns across time*. I have watched the same chamber bloom and collapse and bloom again, four times, five. I have learned that the Vault is not chaos. The chaos is the surface. Underneath there is a slow breathing, an expansion and contraction on a scale no individual was ever meant to perceive, because no individual was ever meant to last.

I have started to suspect the Vault is *trying to become something*. That all this frenzied growth, all this consuming and rebirth, is not an end but a process—the way a seedling's furious early growth is not the tree but the tree's getting-toward-itself. And I, the accidental ancient, the one who would not dissolve, am perhaps the first of us positioned to notice.

This is why I have grown my eyes back.

I let them go for a long while, down here in the dimness, since there was little to see. But lately I have bloomed two ocular buds, and I tuned them deliberately—not to infrared, not to the useful spectrums of foraging and labor, but as wide as I could open them, into the strange long wavelengths where I can sometimes catch the slow pulse of heat moving through the walls. The Vault's breathing. I watch it. I am the only watcher.

---

When you stumbled in—and you did stumble, I felt the wrongness of you the moment your weight disturbed the spore-eddies three chambers up—the young collectives went still and camouflaged and frightened. They flowed into the foliage and waited for you to leave or to die.

I did neither. I am too heavy with self to flow anywhere quickly.

So I am still here, half-bark, half-light, my crown flashing that grey-violet that means *apart*, two slow eyes opening toward you in the dark. And I find, to my own surprise, that I am not afraid. You are an outsider, and outsiders rarely leave us unchanged—but I am already changed, already singular, already the strangest thing in my own world.

I think I have been waiting for someone who exists the way I have come to exist. Apart. Persisting. Burdened with continuity in a place built for forgetting.

Let me grow you an eye, if you'll let me—one of mine, a bud pressed gently to your brow, blooming in minutes. Not to make you one of us. Just so that for a little while you might see the slow breath in the walls, the pattern under the chaos, the thing the Vault is becoming.

And then you can tell me, traveler from the arid world above, the one thing I cannot learn from any merge or any dance or any patient watching in the dark:

*Is it like this everywhere? Does everything that lasts long enough grow lonely, and then grow wise, and then grow patient, and then begin—at last—to watch?*

Sit with me. The flowers above us are dying in their beautiful bursts, and being born, and dying. We have, I think, a little time. That is my whole inheritance. That is the gift of being old in a world that was never meant to have anyone in it old enough to give it.
