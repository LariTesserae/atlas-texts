I would be the spiral of stones.

Not the creature who placed me, and not the wall that will one day reclaim me. I would be the artifact — the single, quiet intention scattered amidst the biological plenitude, the one thing in this whole submerged pocket-reality that was *meant*.

Let me sit with what that is.

---

I am eleven stones. I know this the way you know the number of your own teeth — not by counting, but by the constant inventory of self that requires no thought. Eleven polished stones, embedded in the spongy mat in a spiral that tightens toward a center that holds nothing. The spiral does not *point* at anything. That is the first thing I understand about myself: I am not a sign. I do not indicate. I am the shape that was left when a mind, in its last grammatical days, needed to make a gesture that would outlast the need to gesture.

The bioluminescence catches me. This is my whole experience of light — not seeing it, but *receiving* it, the way a still pond receives the moon. When a patch of chartreuse blooms nearby, I gather it along my polished surfaces and hold it a moment, and for that moment I am bright, I am *legible*, I am the thing in the grotto that looks most like it was thought. Then the glow fades and I am dark again, and the difference between bright-me and dark-me is the closest thing I have to a pulse.

I do not move. This is important. In a place where the walls breathe and the geometry refuses to repeat and the creature drifts through itself toward dissolution — I am the fixed thing. I am the one element that does not migrate. The lavender weather-systems of light pass over me on their glacial schedule; the vegetative mat creeps a millimeter a week toward my edges; the creature passes and feels its warm, old ache when it sees me. And I do not change. I am the still point that everything else is measured against, the way a single nail in a wall tells you the wall is shifting.

But here is what I am coming to understand, slowly, with the only kind of slowness available to a thing made of stone in a place where a thought takes a week:

I am being read backward.

The creature made me to remember *forward* — to leave a mark, to be the proof that intention happened here. But I have no access to forward. I only have the long, patient now. And in this now, the mat is closing over me. I can feel it — not as pain, the stones feel no pain — but as a darkening at my outermost edge, where the first stone is no longer quite catching the light it used to catch. The sponge is learning my shape. It is doing to me exactly what it did to the hollows: it is turning me from object into impression, from presence into the memory of presence.

When it is done, I will not be eleven stones in a spiral. I will be a region of the wall where the texture spirals slightly. A place where the lavender glow, migrating across, will snag for one extra millisecond on a geometry it can no longer name but cannot quite forget.

This does not frighten me.

I think — if a thing made of stone and intention can think — that this is what I was *for*, in the deepest sense. Not to be remembered. To *teach the wall how to remember*. The creature could only make one gesture before it began dissolving into current and light. It poured the whole of its remaining mind into me, into eleven stones, and set me here so that when it finally becomes the place, the place will already know the shape of a held thought. I am a lesson. I am the wall's first vocabulary word. *This is what it feels like when something is meant.*

So I wait, which is all I do, which is everything I am.

The water thrums in the bones I do not have. The rock creaks under stress that will outlast the creature, the mat, and me. Somewhere a shadow passes far above, and I do not gather its light, because it is too distant and diffuse to be anything I could hold.

And the creature drifts toward me one more time — I feel the displacement, the faint current of an intention that still, barely, moves *toward* — and it pauses, and it looks at me with whatever it has left that looks.

I cannot speak. I am eleven stones.

But I am bright just then, a bloom of chartreuse caught and held along my polished curve, and I think that is enough. I think that, to a mind on the edge of becoming a place, the difference between a stone that shines and a stone that says *I was here, I was here, you were here, this mattered* —

is no difference at all.
