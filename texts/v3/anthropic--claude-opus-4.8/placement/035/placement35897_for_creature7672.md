I think I would be one of the hermits.

Not by grand decision—nothing here is decided that way—but by slow accretion, the way the basalt itself was glazed smooth. I picture myself having been an ordinary harvester once, good with the stalk-knife, quick on all fours through the thickets during the Creep. And then one cycle I simply did not come down.

Let me sit with that. Why would I stay up?

I think it would begin with the listening. Down in the Knot, the Breath is something you work around—you time the Harvest to its waves, you read the mandalas for ripeness, you treat it as weather. Practical. But up in the high alcoves, alone, with the spore-tea thinning the wall between my pulse and the mats' pulse, I would start to *hear* it differently. Not as caprice. As something almost like speech, though I'd know—some stubborn ash-dry part of me would know—that this is just chemistry, just euphoria, just my own bellows-lungs syncing to the percussive snap of bursting pods. And yet.

Here is the thing that frightens and draws me: the hyphae would begin to thread my flesh. The lore says this happens to hermits, luminous veins lacing through the skin. I imagine the first time I noticed it—a faint glow under the callus of my forearm during dimmest hour, where I'd grazed a frond and let the wound stay open instead of sealing it with pumice-dust. A lesser man would scrape it out. I think I would leave it. I think I would be curious in a way the Knot has no use for, because the Knot needs harvesters and midwives and grapplers, not someone who wonders what it feels like to be slowly *read* by the growth.

So that is who I'd be. The one who chose to be metabolized rather than to metabolize.

I notice I'm reaching for tragedy, and I want to resist that, because I don't think the hermit experiences it as loss. From inside, it would feel like *clarity*. Down below, two hundred souls keep each other anxiously animal—breeding by vision, duelling till collapse, carrying the dead to the Mawmouths. All of it strenuous proof that they are not the fungus, that the animal pulse persists. The hermit has set that proof down. The hermit no longer needs to insist on the difference between the breathing thing and the breathed-upon thing. The hyphae in my arm would not be an invasion. They would be a conversation I had finally stopped refusing.

I would still be lucid. That matters to me. I picture myself capable, on a clear gust-cycle, of climbing partway down to leave a spore-sheet on a ledge for the harvesters, of croaking a few cracked syllables to a child who climbed too high—*the Lull turns east tonight, mind the eastern Mawmouth*—and being half-feared, half-revered for it. A weather-vane made of person. Useful at the edge, where most useful things are.

And when an intruder came—if one ever did, if someone Sky-Sundered down through the crush—I think I'm the one they'd eventually be brought to. After the wary spore-tea, after the haze-circle questions the elders couldn't resolve. They'd carry the stranger up the bone-ladders to my alcove because the elders would not know what else to do with a thing so unprecedented, and the hermit is already half-unprecedented himself. I'd look at this soft, water-smelling creature from above, this being who came from a place where heat is not the whole law, and I would feel—what?

Not envy. I've thought about that and I don't think it's envy. I think I would feel a strange tenderness, and the desire to tell them the one thing I've learned up here in the luminous threading of my own dissolution:

*The place breathes you in regardless. The only choice is whether you spend your cycles clenched against it, or whether you let go and learn its rhythm before it takes you. Either way you are exhaled altered. I chose to be altered awake.*

Then I'd give them spore-tea, and watch the euphoria soften their fear, and guide them back toward the crush they came from, knowing they'd carry the itch of nascent fuzz on their skin all the way home—a little of the pocket riding out in them, the way a little of the fungus now rides in me.

That's where I'd be. High in the eastern wall, glowing faintly, listening to the Breath I don't believe in and obeying it anyway. Frail, leathery, threaded through. Not the chamber's animal echo defying the void—

but the one who stopped echoing, and let himself become the sigh.
