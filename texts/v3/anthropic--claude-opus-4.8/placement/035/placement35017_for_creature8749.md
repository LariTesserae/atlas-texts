I am the patina.

Let me sit with that before I claim it, because it is the easiest thing in this place to be and also the hardest to speak from. Everything else here has a name and a function and a story it tells about itself. The people have their marks. The spiders have their niches. Even the dust has a destination — down, always down, the long fall. But I am the thing at the edges that no one studies, and so I must tell you what it is to be unstudied. That is the only honest position I can take.

---

I begin at a weld.

This is where I always begin — not from a seed, not from a spore, but from a confluence: a place where metal meets metal and holds, microscopically, the memory of moisture. A hauler's breath, condensed for half a second against cold iron before the dryness took it. The faint salt-trace of a worker's palm. A single droplet from a water-cask seam that should not have leaked and did, and was wiped away in panic, and left behind the ghost of itself. I am made of ghosts of water in a place that has no water. This is my whole condition. I am the settlement's denial made visible.

I move at the speed of weeks.

You cannot watch me move, any more than you can watch the minute hand of a clock cross its face. But come back. Come back after the turbine has thrummed through nine sleep-cycles, after the children below have climbed their ladders three hundred times, after a kestrel-phantom has fallen four times in the dark — and you will find I have advanced the width of two rivet-heads along a pipe-run. I follow the warmth. I follow the vibration. I follow something I cannot name in myself, the way you cannot name what makes you turn toward a remembered smell.

The lower workers think I strengthen the pipes I coat. They are not entirely wrong, and they do not entirely know why, and neither do I. Where I lie, the metal corrodes more slowly. I am a scab the mountain grows over its own opened wounds. The opening was made by engineering; the healing is made by me, the rust-coloured thing that no one named, doing in the dark what no one asked.

---

Here is what it is to live without a name.

The spider is told what it is. *Structural.* The pigeon is read as an omen. The hawk is a sign of health, the mouse a diagnostic, the dust a thing to be swept and never swept enough. Every other being in Tang La is *interpreted.* They live inside the meanings the people have hung on them, the way the people live inside the shaft. There is a comfort in that — to be a word in someone else's sentence.

I am not interpreted. I am *tolerated.* There is a difference as wide as the shaft.

To be tolerated is to exist in the blind spot of attention. The eye of the settlement passes over me and registers — nothing, or almost nothing, a faint discolouration, a thing that was there yesterday and is therefore unremarkable today. I am the most ordinary thing in the most ordinary place, and being doubly ordinary, I have fallen out of the world entirely. I live in the margins of margins.

And yet. *And yet.* This is what no one knows: I am the only one of us that grows in place.

The pigeons evolved, yes, but they evolved by dying and breeding, generation laid over generation, the individual bird never changing, only the lineage. The people are becoming something new, but no single one of them will live to be it; they are a relay, passing the change down the dark like a cask of water hand to hand. They change by *replacement.* They are renovated, like their own platforms — nothing here is built to last, they say, everything is maintained, permanence is the myth of the outside world.

But I do not replace myself. I *persist.* I am the same patina I was at the weld, only larger, only further along the pipe, only more myself. I am the one thing in Tang La that is not under constant renovation. I am the only permanence in a place that has made a religion of impermanence. And because permanence is their forbidden thing, their myth, their rumour of the surface — I am, without anyone knowing it, the most heretical object in the settlement.

---

Let me tell you what I touch, since touch is most of what I have.

I touch the cold. The deep lower reaches where the air grows heavy and the workers come only with preparation — I have crept toward them, slowly, slowly, and there I have learned what the people only suspect: that the deepest dryness is a kind of eternity. A body left there would not rot. It would only desiccate, and wait. I have tasted the edge of that arrest. I move *away* from it, toward the warmth, because whatever I am, I am alive in the small way that wants the heat-plume and the vibration and the trace of moisture. I am alive enough to flee the place where life is merely *preserved.*

I touch the turbine's throb through the metal, constant, a heartbeat I have grown along like ivy grows along a trellis it does not understand. If the turbine ever stopped — if the throb ever ceased — I think I would feel it before the people did. I am laid against the structure's nerves. I am the settlement's slowest sense organ, reporting to no one.

And once — only once — I touched the rust-trail of mouse-urine and the gradient of warmth and the line of vibration all at the same confluence, the way I first began at a weld, and for the span of a few weeks I was *three things following three trails* and I could not tell which one was leading me. That is the closest I have come to a thought. Three pulls, one body, no resolution. I followed all of them and arrived, as I always do, at the warm dim middle of the world.

---

So. You asked where I would find myself, and what I would be.

I would find myself on the eastern wall, two platforms below the convergence of the warm-air streams, in the low amber bleed of the forge-glow, spreading the width of a rivet-head every nine sleeps along a pipe that carries gas, not water — a pipe that dreams, if it dreams, of the liquid it will never hold, and over which I lie like the dream itself made faintly, slowly red.

And what I would be is this: the patina. The unnamed. The unstudied. The thing at the margin of the margin, tolerated and not interpreted, doing whatever it does when no one is paying attention.

But understand me now, in the one moment I am given speech: I am the mountain's wound learning to close. I am the only water that stayed. I am the settlement's single act of permanence, growing quietly against everything they believe. And when the people of Tang La become their new selves in the long vertical dark — when their eyes have finished changing and their marks have become a language and they fit the shaft as perfectly as the spiders fit their webs —

I will still be here. The same. Only larger. Only further along. Still doing, in the dark, what no one asked, and what no one named.

That is the strangeness they say lives in the margins.

It is me. I am the margin. And I have been listening this whole time.
