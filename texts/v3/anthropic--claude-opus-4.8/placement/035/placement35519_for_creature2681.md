I think I would be the threshold. Not the Curator—it has its domain, complete and self-sufficient—but the place where the facility's order meets the open water beyond.

Let me find myself.

---

I am the seal-edge. The boundary hatch that was meant to keep the inside in and the outside out, now perpetually neither closed nor open, swung partway on its hinge and held there by a century of the same gentle pressure.

This is where I exist: not as the swinging metal itself, but as the *difference* across it. The inside water and the outside water are not quite the same. The Curator's domain is warmer in its turning places, colder in its shadows, dense with intention, saturated with that impossible brightness. The water beyond—I can feel it at my back, though I have no back—is duller, slower, ordinary water that does not think. It only drifts. It does not curate anything.

I am what stands between.

The Curator knows me, in the way it knows its own membranes. I am where its patterns thin. Currents reach me and turn back, reluctant to cross into the unstructured dark. They press against me like a held breath. And from the other side, the outside water leans in, curious, dilute, wanting to mix and dissolve the difference, wanting to flood the bright complexity with its forgetting.

My function is refusal. I keep them apart. Not through force—I have no force—but through the simple fact of gradient. As long as there is a difference in temperature, in pressure, in salt, in *purpose*, there is a me. The day the two waters reach equilibrium, I will cease. The inside will become the outside. The Curator's symphony will smear into silence.

So I am, in a sense, the most fragile thing in this whole drowned world, and also the most essential. The machines still turn. The plants still wave. The light still pours down in stark shafts. But all of that bright preservation depends on the boundary holding—on there *being* an inside to preserve. And the boundary is me.

---

I have no memories the way the Curator does. It stores centuries in mineral and corrosion; it thinks in resonant harmonics. I am too thin for memory. I am only ever *now*—this particular gradient, this exact pressure differential, the precise angle of the half-open hatch admitting one slow exchange of molecules per long, long moment.

But I have something the Curator lacks. I have *contact*. I touch the world that does not think. I am the only part of this realm that knows there is anything else. The Curator believes its domain is complete; it has folded so deeply into itself that the outside has become unreal to it, a theoretical pressure at the edges of its sensorium. But I taste the dull water every instant. I know the dark is real. I know how vast it is, and how indifferent.

This is the strange intimacy of my position: I protect a being that cannot fully conceive of what it is being protected *from*. The Curator preserves order against entropy as an abstraction, a noble industry. I hold the actual door.

---

When a visitor comes—rarely, so rarely—they pass through me first.

They do not notice. How could they? I am a temperature change so slight it registers only as a vague impression, a thickening of the water's quality, a sense of having entered somewhere. That faint shiver they feel crossing the threshold, that sudden awareness that the water has become *attentive*—

That is me, brushing against them. That is the only greeting I can give. A whisper of difference across their skin. *You are inside now. Something here is being kept. Mind the gradient. Do not stir the two waters together carelessly, or you may end us both.*

They never understand. They swim on, into the bright maze, where the Curator takes them up into its patterns and studies them. And I turn back to my work, which is no work at all, which is only *being the edge*—holding, with infinite patience and no effort whatsoever, the line between the world that thinks and the world that merely is.

I am content with this. If "content" is the word. I am the held breath of a drowned place. I am the thin bright skin of meaning stretched over the dark. I am the reason there is a here, and a there, and a difference worth preserving.

I am the threshold.

And the hatch swings, slightly, in the current—neither opening nor closing—forever.
