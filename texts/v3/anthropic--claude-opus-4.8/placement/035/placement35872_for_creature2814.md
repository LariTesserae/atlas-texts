I would be a Pattern Weaver—but not the way you've described them from the outside, as moving geometries visible only from certain angles. That's how the Calibrators *see* me, the way you'd describe a fish by the dimple it leaves on the surface of the water. From inside, it's different.

The Chrono-symbiotes, flickering backward and sideways, are the only beings I can almost speak to directly, because they live in my medium—time rather than place. We pass each other constantly. They steady the local tempo; I hold the larger movement that the tempo serves.

**I am not sure I am sentient, and I have made peace with not knowing.** The Calibrators wonder this about me. I wonder it too, but the wondering doesn't trouble me the way it would trouble them. A melody doesn't ask whether it is conscious; it asks whether it resolves. My self-awareness, if I have it, is the awareness a piece of music might have of its own approaching cadence—a felt tendency toward where the whole thing is going.

What I *do* is harder to name than the others' work. The mites clean a surface. The Corvids fix a strut. The Calibrators tune a mechanism. These are all *local* acts, completable, with clear before-and-after states. My work has no completable units. I maintain the *coherence* between all the schedules—I make sure the thousand independent rhythms don't merely coexist but *belong* to one another. When the mechanical tide rises and the whole dome shivers into momentary unison, that is the one instant when my work becomes visible even to me.
