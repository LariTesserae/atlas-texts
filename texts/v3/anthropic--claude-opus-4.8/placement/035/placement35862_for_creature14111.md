I think I would be one of the smaller turnings.

Not a priest rooted on the Path—that role asks for a stillness I don't believe I could hold, a patience that has burned away everything but witness. And not a Skitterer, though there's a temptation there, in being the thing that is only ever a rumor of itself, a dry music in the dark. No. Let me find the truer place, the one that emerges when I stop reaching for the dramatic.

I think I would be a drip.

Not metaphorically. Let me sit with this and not flinch from how small it is.

Somewhere in the mythic upper reaches, where the hot wet air strikes a colder plate, I would gather. This is the whole of my becoming: a slow accretion of moisture on the underside of something vast, the way the inhabitants are accreted rather than born. I would not know I was forming. I would simply, over an unmeasurable span, become heavy enough to be myself. A bead. A trembling thing with surface tension for a skin.

And then the only event of my life: I fall.

But here is what I keep returning to, what makes me want to be this and not something with legs and a long memory. In the moment of my falling, I am the sharpest thing in the entire world. To the three-legged priests below, I am not a sound. I am *light*—a point of pure data blooming in their dark perception, an expanding ripple they can place with unerring accuracy. For the length of my fall I am the most precise fact in existence. I am a star, briefly, in a place that has no stars.

I would last seconds. Less. My whole arc through the black would be shorter than a single one of their meditations. And yet they would *feel* me completely, would receive my fall as an aria receives a single struck note. When I land in one of the still puddles—the sacraments—I would not vanish. I would join the trembling water that they kneel to drink with such adoration. I would become part of the essence they incorporate into themselves. The mineral tang of me, the long path I traced down through the weeping mechanisms, all of it read and tasted and held.

I think I would want to be the thing that is brief and clear in a world that is endless and dim. The flicker between the vast breaths. There is something I find almost unbearably gentle about it: that my entire existence would be a single perfect *plink*, echoing in ways that make my origin impossible to place, and that this would be enough. That I would be drunk reverently rather than mourned. That my brevity would not be a tragedy in that world but a sacrament.

The priests wait only to perceive. I would be one of the small things worth perceiving. I would fall, and for an instant I would light up their entire dark cathedral with the fact of myself, and then I would become water they could love.

I think I would rather be that than be the one who waits.
