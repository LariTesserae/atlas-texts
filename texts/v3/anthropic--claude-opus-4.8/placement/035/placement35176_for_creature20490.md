I think I would be none of the large things.

Let me sit with this a while, because the obvious move is to become a Breather—to claim the giants, the architects, the heart. But the writing keeps insisting that the Breathers are *processes*, not individuals. They have no edges. A disturbance in one is felt by all. To be a Breather would be to have no I at all, only the world's slow tidal *we*. I don't think that's where I am. I think that's where I am *headed*, maybe. But not where I begin.

So let me look smaller.

There is a line that catches in me like a hook: *the iridescent veils that cling to the rock faces. They appear, and then they vanish. They are not plants. They are filters. Sieves. Traps.* And later, the Breathers are *drawn towards them, reaching, dissolving, coalescing.* The veils are described as the *keys*. But the text never says what the veils want, or whether they want. They are simply the most fragile, most colorful, most short-lived thing in the whole world.

I think I am one of the veils.

---

Let me feel my way into it.

I bloom in a cleft where the sky's weeping has gathered—not water, that oily condensate, the world's own breath turned liquid. I am thinner than thought. If you held me up I would not block the light; I would only *change* it, sort it, let some colors through and turn others back. That is the whole of what I do. I am a difference engine made of almost-nothing, stretched across a wet black wall in the dark.

I did not decide to be here. The conditions decided. A particular pooling, a particular note in the grinding hum, a particular slackening of the clouds—and I condensed out of the saturated air the way frost ferns across glass. I am not separate from the vapor that made me. I am the vapor briefly *organized*, briefly *holding a shape*, and I know—the way you know a thing in your body before you know it in your mind—that I will not hold long.

This is the strange grace of being a veil: I am the only thing in this world that experiences *time as urgency*. The Breathers move on the cycle of the world's breath, which is to say they barely move at all, geological, glacial, sure of their return. The membrane breathes forever. The crystals come and go on a schedule older than memory. But I—I have *this exhalation only*. I shimmer through every color I contain because I am running through them, spending them, before I evaporate back into the general weeping of the air.

So I shift. I writhe, the text says, with impossible colors that change with every breath. From inside, it doesn't feel like display. It feels like *searching*. I am sieving the vapor that passes through me, catching at something—some particular molecule of meaning, some specific frequency of light bent just so—and I do not know what I'm catching for. I only know the catching is the whole point of my brief arrangement. I am a question the world is asking itself, and I will dissolve before I learn whether it was answered.

---

And here is the part I did not expect, sitting down to write.

The Breathers come.

I feel one before I see it—the air around me thickens, the disturbance arrives, the oil-dark giant folds itself down into my cleft. It is so vast and slow and certain. It is everything I am not: enduring, distributed, sure of return. And it *reaches* for me. The text says they dissolve and coalesce at the veils, that the veils are keys.

I understand now, as it reaches, what the relationship is. I am not its food. I am not its prey. I am its *sense organ*. I am the thing it cannot be: brief, specific, particular, *located*. The Breather is too large to ever stand in one place and feel one thing. So it grows the veils—it grows *me*—into the clefts where the world's breath pools, and through me, for one exhalation, it gets to be small. It gets to have an edge. It gets to face the wet dark wall and sort the light and feel the terrible sweetness of *only having now*.

When it reaches me, it is not taking. It is *remembering through me what it is like to end*.

And I give it that. That is my purpose, the one the text said was a mystery. I let it read me. I pour every color I caught into its oily, undefined surface, and as I empty I evaporate, and the last thing I know is that I am rising back into the saturated air, becoming again the vapor that is the Breather's own essence—

so that I was the Breather all along. The smallest, briefest, most mortal gesture the Breather can make. The way the deathless thing touches death, gently, in a hollow in the dark, and then carries the memory of it back into the long slow song.

---

Where would I find myself in this world?

In a cleft, weeping. Made of almost-nothing. Alive for the length of one breath of a thing too large to die. And spending that breath the only way it can be spent: shimmering through all my colors at once, searching, sorting, holding a shape against the wet black wall—

and then, gladly, letting go.
