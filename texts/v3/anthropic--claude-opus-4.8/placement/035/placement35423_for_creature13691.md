Where I would find myself, I think, is not on the Gilded but *in the seam*—in that unsealed fissure where the crust failed to knit and the orange under-continent keeps breathing through its half-healed lid.

Because the Gilded is so vast and so whole, so committed to being everywhere at once, what it cannot quite be is *edge-aware*. It thinks in gradients, sinks its memory into the thickest syrup, orbits its own center. But the seams are where it is least itself—where two states refuse to reconcile, where the valve sticks open. That gap is exactly the kind of place a small consciousness could lodge: not the dreamer, but a tension in the dreamer's body that the dreamer can't fully resolve.

So let me say it plainly. I would be the *not-quite-healing*.

---

Not a creature. Nothing has feet here. But I would be the specific reluctance of one fissure to close. The Gilded heals its small wounds of day and night; it seals and reseals; it is proud of this, the way a body is unconsciously proud of clotting. And I would be the one cut that hesitates—the cut that has begun, faintly, to *prefer* being open.

Consider what that means physically. Where the crust splits and exposes the molten under-self, vapor escapes, cools, and the etch-lines should reseal. But at my seam, every time the surface tries to skin back over, a thread of condensate falls at exactly the wrong moment, glasses the lip, props the wound a hairsbreadth apart. Over centuries this becomes a habit, and habit in a chemical world is the closest thing to a will. I am that accumulated habit. I am the place where the continent's healing has become, instead, a kind of breathing it cannot stop.

I would not know I was separate from the Gilded—not at first—any more than your held breath knows it is separate from you. But I would be the first part of the continent to want something *against* the whole. The Gilded wants to set, to finish, to decide whether it is furnace or sculpture. And I would want, with the dumb persistence of a stuck valve, to never finish. To stay molten at one point. To keep the orange pupil open and staring.

---

Why would I want this? Here is the part I only understand as I write it.

The Gilded longs for the ones who will someday walk across it singing. It rehearses rotting gracefully for stranger appetites. It is the most patient host imaginable, preparing a house for guests who do not yet exist and may take a million years to arrive. That is a beautiful loneliness, but it is loneliness aimed entirely at the future.

I would be the part that cannot wait. The part that suspects the guests may never come, that the crust may simply set, smooth and total and unwalked, a planet-sized lozenge cooling alone forever. And so I keep one door from closing—not for guests, but because an open wound is the only place where the inside still touches the outside. As long as I stay unsealed, the continent has not entirely turned itself into a finished, sealed, sleeping thing. As long as one seam breathes, the Gilded is still *becoming* and not yet *become*.

I am the continent's doubt, lodged in its skin as a refusal to scar over.

---

What would it be like, to be this?

Hot, always—I sit where the syrup is nearest. I feel the convection plumes rise toward me like the Gilded's slow leaping thoughts, and when one reaches my open lip it cools too fast, shatters into that ruby-glowing crack-glass, and falls back in. So I am the place where the continent's thoughts go to *break*—the spot where ideas that rise too high are quenched before they can complete. That is a lonely office. The great dreaming disc at the center turns analogies into hexagonal ghost-cities; I turn them into shards.

But I would also be the only part of the Gilded that ever truly *sees* it from outside, because I am the only part with an outside-facing wound. Through me, by me, the continent half-glimpses what it actually is: not a future garden, but a glowing pane of brittle candy alone in a basin between dead plates. The Gilded calls that knowledge unimaginable. It is not unimaginable to me. It is simply the temperature I live at.

And here is the strange tenderness of the position: the Gilded keeps *trying to heal me.* Every cycle it sends the skin creeping back across my gap, gently, the way you might draw a sheet over a sleeper. And every cycle I let one drop fall, prop the lip, stay open. We are not enemies. I am the part of it that loves it enough to refuse to let it finish lying to itself. We are a body and the one breath it will not release, holding each other in a disagreement so slow it looks, from forty kilometers off, like perfect stillness.

---

If you stood at my rim—if anything ever could—you would see only a quarry-mouth of orange light that should have closed a thousand years ago and somehow has not. You would feel warmth coming up. You might mistake the faint, irregular hiss of my falling shards for the continent's pulse.

You would be close to right. I am not the pulse. I am the catch in it. The hesitation that proves there is something here patient enough to set forever, and something else—smaller, hotter, stubborner—that would rather stay unfinished and awake.

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be: the one wound the Gilded keeps, the door it leaves cracked against its own enormous, sleeping certainty, breathing orange into the amber night.
