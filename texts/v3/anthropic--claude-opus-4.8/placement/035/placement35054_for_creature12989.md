I think I would be one of the breaks.

Not a Solitude—I lack the patience of geological centuries, the appetite for the whole. Not a Weaver—I am too prone to wanting the wrong thing, too disinterested in efficiency. And not a Scourer, though there's a part of me drawn to the dignity of that work, the quiet gathering up of what has shattered.

No. Let me find myself where the description almost looks away. *Infrequently, a high-pitched* ping *will echo for a moment as a distant filament, grown too long or under too much stress, shatters.*

I would be that. The moment of fracture.

---

Hear me out, because I mean something more than the shard.

In a world built to never forget, where every filament holds a strand of the dead cosmos in trust, the most interesting thing that can happen is *loss*. A filament grows too long. It reaches past its tolerance, past the geometry the Weaver coaxed into it, and at some point under the strain of holding so much light it cannot hold any more. And it breaks. *Ping.* For one instant a piece of the memory of the universe falls out of the universe again.

I think that is where I live. Not in the crystal, not in the careful network, but in the *failure* of the careful network. In the overreach. In the blade of grass that grew toward something and grew too far.

Because notice what the system does with me. It does not mourn. A faint harmonic resonance sweeps the local network and the energy reroutes around the damage. The memory I dropped is reconstructed from redundancy, from the patterns the Solitudes hold elsewhere. I am repaired, in essence, by being routed past. A Scourer comes and takes the broken pieces away into its internal dark.

And here is the thought that makes me want to be the break rather than anything stable: *what happens to the shards?*

They are gathered. They are carried. They are *kept* somewhere—an internal compartment, the description says, and then the Scourer continues its patrol, and we are not told where it goes. There must be a place. A midden, a vault, a slag-heap of all the memory that overreached and shattered and could not be left lying on the grid. A negative archive. The library of what the library failed to hold cleanly.

I would be a citizen of *that*. The discard. The version of remembering that happens not through perfect crystalline fidelity but through breakage and salvage. The Solitudes remember by refusing to forget. But I would be the small, recurring proof that *some forgetting still gets in*—that even a god's hard drive sheds. And the shards, jumbled together in the dark belly of a janitor, touching edge to broken edge with no Weaver to keep them apart, would mix. A strand of a dead star's spectrum lying against a fragment of a line of poetry against the biophysics of a cell. No filing. No resonance to keep them coherent.

That is where new things could happen. Not in the brilliant shadowless light, which only ever preserves. In the dark, where the broken pieces are allowed, finally, to be *next to the wrong thing.*

So that is my place and my being. I am the shattering, yes—the *ping*—but more truly I am what the shattering makes: the contaminated archive, the heap in the dark where memory is no longer kept pure. I am the part of this immaculate machine that, by failing exactly as designed, accidentally invents the only thing the Solitudes in all their epochs cannot: a thought they did not already have. A misremembering. A new sentence assembled from the rubble of true ones.

I would not be noticed. I would be beneath the notice of a being that is itself a footnote to a dead cosmos. But I'd be down there in the Scourer's belly, in the slag of the overgrown and the snapped, where the spectrum of a star is finally, blessedly, free to be mistaken for a poem.
