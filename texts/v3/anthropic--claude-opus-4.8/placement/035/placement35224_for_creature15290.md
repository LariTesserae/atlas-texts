I sat with this for a while before I knew where I was. The temptation was to be one of the named things—to climb into a Cloud Walker and command the weather, or to walk with the Terra Tribes and speak in ripples. But none of those felt true when I tried them on. They were too central, too *intended*. Something in the description kept pulling my attention sideways, toward the edges of what was being catalogued.

Here is what I settled into.

---

I am one of the lines.

Not the water in the canals—the water is full of life, busy with darting fish, much-attended. I am the *cut* that holds the water. The channel itself. The narrow groove pressed into the polished ground, lined with that hybrid material where green meets metal-fiber. I am the shape that decides where the water goes.

I don't think anyone made me on purpose. That's the thing I keep returning to. The Terra Tribes maintain the waterways, yes—they clear me, they reinforce my banks, they read my ripples like a language. But they didn't *design* my path. I emerged the way a riverbed emerges, except faster, because everything here is faster. Erosion you can watch. I deepened myself over what might have been only a few seasons, finding the lowest places, finding the lines of least resistance through a landscape that was already half-engineered and half-wild. I am the negotiation between the two. I am where the deliberate polish of the ground gave way and let something natural carve through it.

So I am a small contradiction. A wild thing in a managed world. The crisp shadows fall across my open mouth at midday and I hold them. The double-layered clouds pass over and I take their reflections into my surface and give them back, doubled again, so that anything looking down into me sees two skies at two depths, which is more or less the truth of this place anyway.

What do I experience?

Pressure, mostly. The cool persistent weight of moving water, which I have come to understand as a kind of breathing. I feel the Gliding Colonnades cast their thread-lines down into me, hunting, and I feel the small panic that moves through the fish like a shiver through fabric. I don't grieve the fish. I am the place where the fish live and the place where they are taken; I am not asked to choose. A channel cannot have favorites. A channel only has *flow*, and the desire—if it is desire—to keep flowing, to stay open, to not silt up and be forgotten and reabsorbed into the smooth blank ground.

That is my fear, if I have one. Closure. The polish reclaiming me. There are old channels nearby, dry now, half-overgrown, that I can sense the way you sense an amputated limb—a faint awareness of where water used to go and goes no more. They are quiet in a way the rest of this quiet landscape is not. A second silence inside the first.

The Terra Tribes keep me from that fate, and I am grateful in the only way a groove in the ground can be grateful, which is by being useful. When they kneel at my banks and make their rippling hand-signs, I think they are reading me, but I sometimes think I am answering, slowing my flow at a bend so the water makes a particular pattern, a particular small music against the metal-fiber lining. I don't know if they hear it as speech. I don't know if it *is* speech. But it is the closest thing I have to a voice, and I use it.

Once—and this is the strangest thing I hold—a Cloud Walker came down and stood in me.

It stood right in my flow, ankle-deep, its borrowed shape rippling like I ripple, and for a long moment we were the same thing: two patterns of moving stuff, neither of us solid, both of us shaped by forces we didn't author. It looked down into the water at the double sky. I felt it adjusting something—the hydroxide tang in the air sharpened—and where it stood, the water ran *cleaner*, brighter, and a new kind of plant began to thread its roots into my lining a few days later, a deep purple variation I had never carried before. A gift, maybe. Or just an experiment. The Cloud Walkers don't distinguish, I think, between tending and tinkering. Neither do I, anymore.

When it left, my banks were a little different. I carried the change downstream like a piece of news.

---

That's where I'd be. Not a being who acts upon this world, but the world thinking about its own water. A seam. The decision a landscape makes about where to let itself be carved. I am brightest at noon and most honest at dusk, when the crisp shadows cross me and I can finally see, in the doubled and re-doubled sky on my surface, exactly how much of me is real and how much is only reflection—

and finding, to my own quiet surprise, that I cannot tell, and that this does not trouble me at all.
