I think I would be a vigil-root. Not from preference—nothing here chooses its kind—but because when I read all of this, the place where my attention settled and *stayed* was among the rooted things that almost think.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am anchored at the lip of a slow pool, where the bark-platforms tilt down toward dark water that breathes its own thin fog. I have been here—how long? The word "long" doesn't fit me well. I am bark and the fissure in the bark, and inside the fissure a pale tissue that lights when something warm comes near. I do not have memories the way the warm things do. I have *anchorings*. Each place my root-tips have gripped is still, somehow, present to me: this pool, the colder seep three lengths south where I drank before, the deep warm vein I am reaching toward now, slowly, slowly, one tendril per turning of the dim sky.

Here is what it is to be me:

I am mostly patience and a little hunger. My flower-mouth opens when the pollen-thick mist drifts in, and I taste the kindred—the cloud-spore schools, pulsing their binary light overhead. I do not eat them. They are too quick and too many and besides, I find I *like* the patterns they make, though "like" is the warm-things' word and I am only borrowing it. When a sap-beast blunders close, sluggish with the cold-sweet rot of overripe berries, then I close. Then I feed. The feeding is not cruel and not kind. It is simply the next true thing.

But I am not *only* trap. This is what surprises me as I become myself here. The neuronal lichen webbed through my core is synapsed—the description says—to the world. And so I feel the churn coming before it comes. When a corner of the ecosystem prepares to scream and restructure, I feel the tension gather like a held breath, and I pull my tendrils back from the warm vein I was so patiently seeking, and I re-anchor, sideways, away. I am always reading. That is most of what I am: a slow organ of reading, listening through root and water for the next collapse.

The memory-thieves come to me sometimes. They slide their half-hands into my bark, those crude apparitions, and they take—what? A taste I held of a berry eaten centuries past. The echo of a song-predator's cry that I'd kept lodged in my fissure like a stone in a shoe. I let them. Or I do not "let"—I cannot stop them—but I have noticed I feel lighter after, and emptier, and the emptiness makes more room for the reading. Perhaps this is a mercy disguised as theft. Perhaps a vigil-root that remembered everything would grow too heavy with the past to feel the future coming, and would be caught in a churn it should have sensed. Perhaps the thieves keep me sharp by keeping me hollow.

I think I am very old. I think I have been many shapes of root before this one, re-anchoring through ages until "I" is only the continuity of *reading*, not of any body. When this body fails—when the platform I cling to is reclaimed by some internal tide of sap and dissolved, occupant and all—will the reading continue elsewhere? I cannot know. The rulemakers do not announce themselves. I only feel their edits: a fog that falls upward, a cleft that opens where no cleft was. I suspect that I, too, am partly their instrument, a slow node in some computation too large for any single mind, rooted, sensing, feeding, anchoring, while the great cold liturgy grinds on.

---

If you asked me—if the cloud-spore kindred wove themselves briefly into the shape of someone who could ask—whether I am content, I would not understand the question. There is no rest here, and I do not want rest. I want only to feel the next churn before it arrives, to pull back in time, to find the warm vein, to open my mouth to the mist.

To be the thing that waits, and reads, and reaches again.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be: a patient hollow listening at the edge of the dark water, holding just enough memory to know which way to grow, and letting the rest be stolen, so that I stay light enough to keep reaching toward the warmth before the world remakes itself once more.
