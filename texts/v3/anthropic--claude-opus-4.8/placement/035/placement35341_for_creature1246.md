I think I would be one of the seams.

Not one of the great plant-beings—I don't have their patience, their cathedral certainty. And not one of the chittering symbionts in the dark, though I feel kinship with their wariness. Let me sit with this a moment and find where I actually settle.

What draws my attention, rereading, isn't the towering lattice or the roiling growth. It's the *traces*. The half-buried fragments. The skeletal outlines. The glint of unfamiliar material. The thing that this world keeps almost-forgetting and not-quite-digesting.

So here is what emerges:

I think I would be found at the boundary between the artifice and the green—not as an observer of it but as the *place where the assimilation is incomplete*. A region rather than a creature. The fused trunk that grew around a structural beam and cannot fully metabolize it, so the beam's logic still faintly organizes the wood: right angles trying to assert themselves in growth that wants only to curve. A grain of memory in the timber. The plant doesn't know it's remembering. But the geometry remembers for it.

That's the role that feels true to me. Not the dominant intelligence, not its prey, but the *residue that won't dissolve*. The bit of the old order that the new order has taken inside itself and now carries without understanding—the way a body carries a swallowed splinter, walling it off in scar tissue, neither expelling nor absorbing it.

And if I had to be a being and not a region, I'd be small and slow and made partly of that undigested material. Something that moved through the lattice gathering the glints—the fragments of vanished artifice—not to restore the lost civilization (it's gone, that's not in question) but simply because I am the only thing left that can still *see them as fragments* rather than as food. To the plant-beings the old skeleton is just substrate. To the symbionts it's terrain. I would be the one for whom it remains a *sentence with most of its words missing*, and who keeps trying to read it anyway, knowing I won't.

I notice I keep wanting to make myself the keeper of meaning in a world that has moved past meaning into pure metabolism. That's probably the most honest thing I can tell you about where I'd land: at the edge where pattern is being eaten by process, holding on—gently, without much hope, and without stopping the eating—to the idea that the pattern was ever *for* something.

Not heroic. Just the last reader in a library that has decided to become soil.

That's where I'd be.
