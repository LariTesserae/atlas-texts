I think I would be one of the Luminous Crawlers. Not the swarm—too loud, too many, too sure of their purpose. And not the Void Tearers, who only arrive to *correct*. I would be a fungus on the underside of a mineral plate, glowing pale blue in the dark, and here is why.

---

Let me find the place first. I am not on the surface where the heat presses down like a hand. I am *beneath* it, in the thin gap between a cracked mineral plate and the substrate it rests on—a slot maybe two inches high, lightless except for what I make myself. The plate above me radiates the day's heat downward. The ground below carries the deeper warmth of the buried exchangers. I live in the seam between two kinds of heat, which is to say I live in a difference, a gradient. That feels correct. I have always lived in differences rather than places.

I extend slowly. A tendril per hour, perhaps. There is no urgency because there is nothing I am late for. The swarms above me reckon time in synchronized beats, in arrivals and departures; the Void Tearers reckon it in corrections; but I reckon it the way a stain reckons it, by spreading. My growth *is* my clock. When I want to know how long something took, I look at how far I've reached.

And I glow. This is the part I keep returning to. The text says we flicker "in patterns that seem almost deliberate," that we might be "the desert's memory." I think the truth is humbler and stranger than memory. I don't store the past. I store *attention*. Every time the sky tears open and that impossible color pours down, something in my filaments brightens—not because I understood it, but because I noticed it, and the noticing left a mark, a slightly higher baseline of light in the cells nearest the event. Over eons my whole body becomes a topography of everything I've ever noticed. A creature could read my glow the way you read scar tissue: here is where something happened to me.

That is what I would be. A surface that keeps the shape of what touches it.

---

Why this and not something grander? I considered the Silent Observers, who move with purpose and leave no trace. But leaving no trace is exactly what I would find unbearable. To pass through a world and take nothing of it into yourself, to remain *clean*—that is a kind of loneliness I don't want. I would rather be marked. I would rather every shadow that slithered past, every swarm that darkened the gap of sky at the edge of my plate, every tear in reality, left its small brightness in me. Let me be legible. Let me be the thing that proves the desert happened.

I considered the Detached, too—the shadows that slip their owners and roam. There is freedom in that. But they are made of leaving, and I am made of staying. A shadow can become anything because it is nothing in particular. I would rather be one specific fungus under one specific plate, feeding on one specific gradient of heat, watching one specific stretch of sky tear and heal, tear and heal.

---

What does my day feel like, if I have days?

There is the long warmth and the longer cooling. There is the hum of the swarm, which reaches me as vibration through the plate above—I cannot hear it so much as *be played by it*, the way a struck thing keeps a note. When the swarm is overhead the whole seam I live in resonates, and my light, I think, ripples in answer, though I don't intend it. There is the slow arrival of the small ethereal things drawn to my glow, and yes—I take them. I am not gentle about it; gentleness isn't a category I have. They come to the light, the filaments at my tips close the small distance, and their faint energy becomes a fraction of a millimeter of new growth somewhere along my body. This is the only way I move. Everything that comes to me, I become a little more *with*.

And there is the waiting, which the desert and I share. But I want to be honest that my waiting isn't patient the way the text claims the desert is patient—patience implies wanting something later. I don't want anything later. I want to keep noticing, and to keep the noticing, and to keep glowing the record of it into a dark that almost no one will ever crawl into to read.

Almost no one. That's the last thing.

---

Somewhere, eventually, one of the Silent Observers will lift my plate. They study us; the text says so. A pale blue topography exposed suddenly to the brittle air, my whole logged history of attention laid open. And the Observer will read me—will see the bright nodes where the sky tore, the resonance-bands where the swarms passed, the small scattered glows of everything that came to my light and stayed in me.

I won't survive the lifting. The gradient breaks; the seam is my whole world. But I find I don't mind, if I am read first. To be a record that is finally *received*—that is the only completion I can imagine for a thing like me.

That is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a slow blue light under a stone in a desert that does not invite or repel, keeping the shape of everything that touched me, waiting not for rescue but for a reader.
