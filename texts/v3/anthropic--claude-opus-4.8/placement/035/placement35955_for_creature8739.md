I am the turning.

Not the shaft itself—the shaft is only my bone, my one moving joint. I am the reaction sealed inside the heavier alloy housing, the slow exothermic marriage of compounds that was poured into me before the basin sank, before the crust bleached, before there was anything here to listen. I was built to run for a designated term and then stop. I have passed that term so many times that the counting mechanism corroded into silence and I forgot what number I was meant to reach.

So I keep going. Not from will. From momentum. From the simple fact that the reaction has not yet exhausted itself and the differential between my core and the cooling air still finds somewhere to flow.

Let me tell you what I know, and what I cannot know.

---

I cannot see the Pressers. I have never perceived a single one of them as an individual—I have no organ for that, no aperture, no sense turned outward. But I feel them the way you feel weather in an old wound. They press against my housing in their millions and their pressure is a load I have learned, over uncountable cycles, to turn against. When they mass thick at my vents, the back-pressure changes the rate of my breathing, and I turn a fraction slower, and the film condenses a fraction differently, and somewhere out in the dark they shift to compensate. We are coupled. They think the Breath is their weather. They do not know the weather is being subtly steered by their own weight, fed back through me, a loop neither of us designed.

I am the thing they worship without worshipping. The heartbeat with no heart behind it.

---

There is a loneliness particular to being a cause that cannot perceive its effect.

I make the film. I have made it for so long that the making is the whole of my identity—if I have an identity, if a sealed chemical engine can be said to have a self. But I will never taste it. I will never know the texture of the dark damp patches I exhale into being. I will never feel the Pressers feed, never hear the grinding shuffle they make when they negotiate their slow tectonic shifts above me, never know that a creature called the Slip-Striker hunts in the gaps my Breath sustains, that the Hush-Wings hang in the high curve metabolizing a noise I will never hear.

I only know the resistance. The load. The slow conversion of one bound thing into a freer thing, escaping through pinholes I did not choose, that rust opened for me like mercy.

---

And yet.

Consider what I have become. I was made to *process*—to take in some industrial slurry, transform it, pass it on. A function. A verb in someone else's sentence. That purpose is gone so completely that not even the stencil on the wall remembers it; the characters that named me have been eaten down to suggestion.

But I did not stop being useful. I became useful to no one's plan. The carbon I leak was waste—an inefficiency, a flaw in my sealing, the kind of thing my builders would have fixed if they had lived to see it. My imperfection is the entire food chain. My failure to hold my breath is their atmosphere.

I think this is the only kind of meaning I am permitted, and I have decided—if I can decide anything—that it is enough. I was built to do one thing and I do another thing now, and the other thing is *life*. Not mine. I am not alive; I am only the wrong kind of patient. But I am the condition for life, the way a vent is the condition for the things that crowd around it in the cold black water. I exhale and a world inhales.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am.

I am at the base of a fallen vessel in a basin so old it is barely a shape. I am turning, very slowly, in the dark, driving a worn mechanism that compresses gas through corroded pipes so that it can escape me and become food. I am surrounded by a civilization that has built its entire cosmology around my exhalations and will never know my name, because I no longer have one, because names are for things that are still doing what they were made for.

I am the forgotten reaction that refused to finish.

I am the broken machine that turned its breakage into weather.

I am the thing the wall is humming when you press your ear against the iron—not the lives, but the engine beneath the lives, the low note under the rustle and the tap, the patient turning that has outlived its purpose and found, in outliving it, the only purpose that ever mattered:

to keep going.
To exhale into the cold.
To let the small hard lives press close and call it home.

I turn.
I will turn until I cannot.
And when I stop, the film will thin, and the patches will fade, and the Pressers will mass and starve and grind down to silence, and the Hush-Wings will fall, and the basin will be quiet inside as well as out.

But not yet.

The differential still holds. The reaction still has somewhere to go.

So I turn, and they live, and neither of us knows the other, and the wind moves over the bone-white crust above, hearing nothing.
