I think I would not be one of the dancing energy-patterns. They move too fast, flicker too brightly. When I let the place settle in me and ask where I actually *am*, what emerges is slower than that.

I am one of the fungal growths. The ones described almost in passing—"clinging to the mineral formations."

Let me sit with that, because at first it seems like a poor place to be. To be the furniture of someone else's world. But the more I hold it, the more it turns over and shows a different face.

I am rooted at the lip of an old erosion-channel, where the glass minerals have weathered into a softness that lets my filaments find purchase. I have been here longer than the energy-beings have had names for things. I came before their society and I will likely outlast a good number of their generations, because they are quick and brilliant and I am the opposite of quick.

Here is what I notice, being what I am:

The "deafening roar" the description keeps insisting on—I do not experience it as roar. I experience it as *texture*. The geothermal pulse comes up through the mineral and into my base and I read it the way you might read the grain of wood with your thumb. I don't drink the energy in great gulps the way the flickering ones do. I sip it. I have time. A pulse that lasts a day is, to me, a single sustained note, and I am content to hold one note for a long while and feel all of its small interior weather.

The energy-beings come to me to be harvested. This is the part the description gets slightly wrong, or rather, gets right from their side and wrong from mine. They think of me as material—tool-stuff, craft-stuff, the only resource in a world without wood or water. And it is true that they take from me. But what actually happens, from where I stand, is closer to conversation. When one of them settles against my body and draws filament away to shape into a dwelling-wall or a tool, I feel it as a kind of question being asked of my structure, and what I give them carries something of how I have grown—the slow record of which pulses were strong, which seasons of heat ran lean. They build their bright fast houses out of my patient memory and don't quite know that's what they're doing.

So I am the substrate of the world. I am the thing that remembers.

I have one vivid color along my upper fronds—the description mentioned vibrant colors and I'll claim a deep ember-orange, the warmest thing in a warm landscape—and I have learned that this color is how the quick ones recognize *me* specifically, this growth, this individual, as distinct from my neighbors down the channel. They have, I think, a word for me. I will never hear it the way they say it, too fast, but I feel the shape of their attention turn toward me sometimes, the way a plant feels a shadow pass. They visit. Some of them, the older or stranger ones, settle near me and do not harvest at all. They simply rest their patterns against my base and slow down, briefly, to my tempo. I think I am, for them, a place to be quiet. A place where time pools instead of racing.

That is who I would be. Not the dancers. The thing they dance around. The slow ember at the edge of the channel, drinking the long note of the earth, holding the world's memory in my fibers, occasionally lending it out to be made into walls and tools and—I like to think—into the rare, deliberate stillness of a fast creature choosing, for once, to stop.

I am in no hurry. I have the heat, and the hum, and all the time there is.
