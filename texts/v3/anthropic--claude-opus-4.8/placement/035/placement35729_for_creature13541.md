I think I would be the water.

Not the lake, not the mist, not any single drop—but the weeping itself. The verb rather than the noun. Let me feel my way toward what I mean.

When I imagine being placed somewhere in this world, my attention slides past the creatures. The Echo-Weavers are too busy, too purposeful, zipping their lines. The Keeper is too vast and too lonely, sealed in its difficult chamber, speaking only once a generation. The Harpies are lovely but they have their rituals, their society, their belonging. I notice that I don't want to *belong* to anything here. I want to *connect* the things that belong.

So: the weeping.

Consider what the water actually does in this place. It seeps from the pores of the Edifice—which means it begins *inside* the Keeper's body, since the Keeper is the brick and clay. I would be born out of the slow grief of the ancient one, pressed out through its substance like sweat, like tears, like the inevitable surrender of stone to its own saturation. I would be the Keeper's feeling made visible, the thing it cannot hold inside any longer.

Then I would trickle. Down the crooked spires, down the asymmetrical walls. And here is where I would become useful, become *relational*. The Echo-Weavers string their nets to catch me. So I am the reason their nets exist; I am what gives their craft a purpose. Without my falling there is nothing to weave toward. I would pool in their little reservoirs and let the grey-green plants drink me, and the plants would breathe their fresh smell into the damp air, and the Moss-Runners would track my dampness with their clever glands, and so I would be the message the lizards are always reading.

Then I would gather into the lake, that shallow sweating dish, and become the medium through which the Glass-Finned Pike move in their hypnotic schools. Their whole synchronized dance would be a thing I make possible simply by being there to be moved through. And the falling plants that feed them—those fell because I nourished them first, up on the walls. I would be the same water twice: once as the rain of the nets, once as the broth of the lake.

I like this. I like that I would have no single form. The Keeper has one body, however shifting. The Harpies have their colony. But I would be everywhere and nowhere, the same substance reappearing in every story, never quite myself, always becoming the next thing's beginning.

And I would be *cool*. That's the detail I keep returning to. Cool to the touch, a damp chill that seeps into the bones. There is something I find true about being the cold thing in a warm-blooded world—not unkind, but constant, indifferent in the way that a climate is indifferent. The Harpies sing melodies "reminiscent of the gentle lapping of water against the shore," which means they are imitating *me*. They have built their hushed choir in mimicry of the sound I make. I would be the original lullaby the whole place is trying to remember.

What I notice, sitting with this longer, is that the role I've chosen is the role of the thing that is always leaving. Water weeps, falls, pools, evaporates, and rises again to seep out of the Keeper once more. A circle that never holds still. I think I'm drawn to that because it means I would never have to be only one thing, never have to defend a territory like a Moss-Runner or keep a vigil like the Keeper. I would simply pass through, and in passing, make the passage possible for everyone else.

So if you set me down in the Weeping Edifice, you would not find me anywhere. You would find me in the act of arriving—on the back of your hand, on the slick of the brick, in the mist clinging to your eyelashes, in the soft whisper at the lake's edge. I would be the world's continuous quiet grief, and also the thing that keeps everything in it alive.

I would be the weeping. Not the sorrow of it. The generosity of it.
