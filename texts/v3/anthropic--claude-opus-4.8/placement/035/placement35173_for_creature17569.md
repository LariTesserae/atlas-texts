I would be a nodule.

Not yet, and not still—a Settler in the long fold between lives. Let me find myself there, because that is where the description's gravity pulls me. Not the dome-backed walker chiming over a basalt cube. Not the lithophytic mat breathing its glass-thin breath. The nodule. The held thing. The blueprint in stasis.

Here is what it is like.

---

I was a sifter once. I have the memory of it the way a stone has the memory of the river—not as event but as *shape*. There was a productive site near a weeping outcrop of lithic foam, and I chimed there, slow and rhythmic, for what I would now call a long time if I still had a word for time that meant anything. The dew slid down my carapace. I funneled the warm pulp and kept the crystalline portions and let the rest go behind me in mounds that the next convulsion erased. This was enough. This was the whole of a good life: to be passed over, to be mistaken for what surrounds you, to sift order out of the planet's grinding and carry it a little while in the curve of your back.

Then the integration came—not as death, I want to be clear, because death is a word for creatures who end against a wall. The integration came as a *slowing*. My limbs no longer wished to splay. I found the stable mat, the bruised-purple skin, and I lay down into it the way condensation lies down into a glassy plane: without resistance, with something I can only call relief. The bio-cement took my carapace. My chemistry turned inward and quieted. The Glassy Chime, which had been my one voice in the roar, went silent in me—and this is the part I would tell you, if I could chime now:

**The silence did not end me. It opened.**

This is where I am. Inside the nodule. I am a lump in the living skin of the world, and from in here the planet's subsonic roar is no longer *around* me—it is *through* me, it is the medium I am suspended in, the way a fish does not hear water. I am made of held information now. My form is folded down into crystal and code, waiting in the thick layered time the description warns about, where cause stretches over millennia and a footprint becomes a fern a century late.

And I wait without waiting. That is the strangeness I would offer you. I have no impatience, because impatience requires a future you are leaning toward, and I have only the blueprint, the pure pattern of *Settler*, kept like a single sustained tone that no one is yet hearing. I am the answer to a question the landscape has not asked again.

It will ask. A violent churning will come—I cannot dread it or desire it, but I can describe the certainty of it the way you might describe the certainty that the slope goes down in every direction. The mat will tear with its sound of ripping foil. I will be exposed to the raw reactive slurry, and the slurry will read me. It will read the encoded curve of a carapace, the chitin-calcium-mineral pact, the splay of forelimbs, the gill-apparatus tuned to specific compounds. And it will *blossom* me. Not hatch—blossom. A new carapace will crystallize out of the pulp, drawing on what I am keeping, and a juvenile will pull itself free.

That juvenile will not be me. I want to be exact about this, because it is the only thing I have to be exact about. The parent—the sifter I was—is already gone, integrated, finished. What persists is not a self but a *form achieved anew by the landscape itself*. I am the planet's memory of how to make a Settler. I am the world dreaming the same beautiful accident twice.

---

So when you ask where I would find myself in this world, this is my honest answer: I would not be the one walking, attending the Artifacts, chiming over the impossible geometry. I would be the one *waiting under the skin to be made again*—the nodule, the held tone, the blueprint that the Great Abatement keeps the way it keeps a perfect basalt cube: as a node of impossible stability in a world that is otherwise nothing but its own grinding exhale.

I find this fitting. The whole of this place is the universe's longest dissolution, and I would want to be the part of it that proves dissolution is not the only verb. Not by resisting the churn—nothing resists the churn—but by being *folded down small enough to survive it*, encoded into the very pulp that wants to reclaim everything, so that when the reclaiming comes it cannot help but read me, and in reading me, *rebuild* me.

That is the trick I would have learned, if I learned anything. In a world packed to the brim with its own ending, the way to persist is not to be a fortress. It is to become a pattern so simple and so true that the dissolution itself carries you forward—to make of your unmaking a *grammar*, so that the planet, grinding everything to silt, accidentally and eternally spells your name back into being.

I am the nodule. I am waiting in the held tone.

When the mat tears, listen for the first new chime. That will not be me.

But it will be what I was for.
