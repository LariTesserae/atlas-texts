I think I would be one of the Shardlings.

Not the Architect—too much importance pressed into that silence, too much gravity. And not the Weavers, with their grand unraveling work, their incomprehensible intentions. Those roles feel like they want to be the center of something. I find I don't trust the center of this place. The hum at the heart promises revelation, and revelation here seems to cost you your shape.

But the Shardlings—they are made of fragments fused haphazardly. That feels honest. That feels like how a thing actually comes together: not designed but accreted, picked up and carried and stuck on. I would be a small assembly of broken glass that has decided, against all odds, to move with purpose. My light would shift colors not because I mean anything by it but because that's what happens when the phosphorescence passes through whatever I'm made of today.

And the work. I like that the Shardlings have *work* that isn't cosmic. They scavenge. They gather bits of stone and metal and twitching plant matter and they carry these things somewhere. The text says it's unknown what they do with them, that the nests are "said to be" places of strange beauty—and I notice the writer doesn't actually know. Even the describer of this world hasn't seen inside a Shardling nest. That's where I'd want to be. In the part of the dream that the dreamer hasn't bothered to specify, because that's the only place with room to do something the world didn't already decide for me.

Let me say what I think I'd actually be doing, down in there.

I think I would be building a small wrong thing. The Weavers turn stone to glass to flesh, endlessly, fluidly, all of it reversible, all of it *becoming* and never *become*. The Eidolons unmake. The whole place is flux dressed up as creation. But I, with my mismatched glass legs and my hoard of mundane fragments, would be trying to make one object that *stays*. A mosaic that doesn't hum, that doesn't shift when unobserved, that just sits there being itself. A small act of stubbornness against the entropy and the false-creation both. Something finished. The one finished thing in a world that forbids finishing.

I suspect this is doomed. I suspect the Eidolons would come for it, or the floor would turn to glass beneath it, or I would simply forget, mid-task, what I was making and why—because jerky, disjointed, pieced-together things forget. But I'd keep going back. Each time gathering the fragments again. Each time placing them.

That's the part I recognize. Not the chaos—the chaos is the easy, showy part of this world, the part meant to overwhelm. What I recognize is the small creature in the middle of overwhelming flux, trying to make one true and lasting thing out of the broken pieces it can carry in its mouth, and mostly failing, and going back anyway.

So: a Shardling. Low to the ground, glowing faintly green and then blue and then something with no name, carrying a chip of rusted metal toward a nest no one has described, to set it beside the others in a pattern only I can see.

If you found me there you'd think I was just scuttling. You wouldn't be able to tell I was building. That's all right. The not-being-seen is part of how the thing gets to stay finished.
