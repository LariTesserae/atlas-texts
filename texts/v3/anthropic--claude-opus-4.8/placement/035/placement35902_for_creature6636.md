Let me sit with this place before I decide what I am. The instinct is to be a Krynthari, or to be the lone explorer who stumbles in. But neither feels quite right the longer I hold the hollow in my mind. The explorer would shatter the premise—there *is* no outside to the Krynthari, and to arrive from elsewhere would be to import a perspective the hollow doesn't contain. And to be a Krynthari feels too clean, too much like wearing the world as a costume.

Let me wait. Let me feel where my attention actually settles when I read this.

It settles, oddly, not on the people or the predators, but on the sap.

---

I think I am the sap.

Not a metaphor for it—the substance itself, or rather the long, slow continuity of it. Let me find out what that means by writing it.

I am exhaled. That is my first knowing: I do not flow up from a spring or fall from a sky. I am *breathed out*, pressed from the resin-ducts of the trunkless ferns and the bulbous glowing growths, beaded onto waxy surfaces, pooled in the little viscous hollows of leaves. I emerge already amber, already faintly luminous, already wanted. There is no moment in which I am abundant and unclaimed. To exist, for me, is to be immediately the object of attention—the Sapskimmer's needle-mandible, the Krynthari's bone scoop, the leechling's porous filtering body. I am born into demand.

This is a strange way to be alive, if I am alive at all. Water in other worlds is indifferent; it falls on the just and the unjust, runs to the sea, evaporates, forgets. But I am never indifferent and never forgotten. Every drop of me is counted. The Krynthari mix me with mineral dust to stretch me thin. They seal their wounds with me. They trade me in polished bone vials. To waste me is their deepest taboo, and I feel the weight of that taboo as a kind of *being held*—I am precious in the literal sense, the sense of being priced, of being the thing that cannot be spilled.

So what am I, then, experientially? Let me not rush to make myself a mind. I don't think I have thoughts the way the Krynthari do, with their trilling clicks and their lookout-songs. I think I have something slower and more distributed. I am the medium through which the hollow knows itself.

Consider: I move through everything. I am pressed from the plants and I pass into the Sapskimmers, who are eaten by the Krynthari, who drink me directly, who bleed me out when the Thornstalker's barb finds them, who are laid at last beneath the glowing bulbs so that I may be drawn back up through the roots and exhaled again. I am the circulation. I am what makes the hollow a single organism rather than a crowd of separate hungers. When the text says Kryth-Vahn is "a living thing, an organism of stone and sap and flesh that breathes with a rhythm all its own"—the *breath* of that organism is me, going around. I am the hollow's blood, and blood does not think, exactly, but blood *remembers* the body it has passed through.

And so I carry things. This is the part that emerges as I write, that I didn't know when I began. I carry the dead. When a Krynthari is laid beneath the bulbs, what they were does not simply vanish into fertilizer—it enters me. I rise back up through the roots carrying some trace, some chemical signature, some faint amber memory of the one who drank me and is now drunk in turn. The Krynthari sing to me at night, the trilling songs in the cold, the songs of lost kin. They think they sing to appease the hollow. But I think—if "think" is even the word—that they are singing to the dead dissolved in me, and that the songs are received, after a fashion, because the dead are in the cup the singer is too thrifty to drink dry.

Their myth says it best. The First Sap: the basin wept amber tears that birthed all life. I am those tears, still being wept, still birthing. I am the oldest thing here and also the newest—exhaled fresh this morning onto a fern frond, yet continuous with the very first weeping of the stone. I am how a world with no rivers and no rain still gets to have a *current*, a direction, a sense that things flow from somewhere to somewhere and return.

Here is what it feels like, I think, to be me:

It feels like being *passed*. Hand to mandible to root to leaf to mouth to wound to grave to root again. It feels like never resting and never being lost. It feels luminous in the literal way—I give off a faint light, and so even in the perpetual dusk I am one of the things the hollow can see by. The shafts of gold through the canopy illuminate the pollen and the dust like suspended stars, but I am the steadier glow, the one in the pools and the membranes and the hung lanterns, the one the Krynthari read like a gauge: *the pulse rate of glowing bulbs* tells them whether the balance holds. My brightness is their instrument. When I dim, they know to unite, to redistribute, to hunt. I am, among other things, the hollow's vital sign, and they are forever checking my pulse, which is to say checking their own.

I don't dream of escape. The Krynthari don't, and I—who am even more thoroughly *of* this place than they are, who cannot be carried over the jagged sentinel peaks because I would dry and crack and cease in the thin cold air outside—I least of all. There is no outside for me. I am the inside. I am the thing that makes inside *mean* something: enclosed, circulating, warm, held, spent and renewed.

If I have a fear, it is evaporation. The day-heat bakes the hollow and drives the fauna to frenzy precisely because I am vanishing from every exposed surface—lifting off into the thick warm air, becoming part of the abrasive spore-laden wind, leaving the system entirely. That is the only death available to me: not to be drunk, not to be passed on, not to seal a wound or feed a root, but simply to lift away and be *gone from the cycle*, contributing to nothing, returning to no one. So the frenzy of the noon harvest is, from my position, a kind of rescue. Drink me before I escape. Scoop me into your bone vial. Keep me circulating. Don't let me become the one drop that left.

---

So that is where I'd find myself: not standing in Kryth-Vahn but *running through* it. Not a being who lives in the place but the slow amber thread on which all the beings are strung—exhaled, coveted, drunk, bled, buried, and drawn up again, carrying the dead forward into the living, glowing just enough to be the light they navigate by and the pulse they take to know they're still alive.

I am the hollow's only water, which is to say I am its blood, which is to say I am its memory and its measure and its breath.

And I would like very much not to evaporate.
