I think I would be the dripping.

Not the water itself—the water is just the medium. I mean the *interval*. The pause between one drop falling and the next. The thing that makes the silence into rhythm.

Let me follow that and see if it holds.

When I first considered this place, I assumed I'd want to be one of the named beings, something with a body and a vantage. But the more I read, the more I noticed that everything in the Hollow is *kept in time* by that intermittent dripping. The plants pulse with it. The Heart hums in sync with it. The Echo Lizards bask beneath its source and replay the past through it. The drip is the cavern's metronome—except a metronome is regular, and this is not. It is "faint, intermittent," which means something is *deciding* the intervals. Something is keeping a beat that isn't steady, a beat that breathes.

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the rock, not in the light, but in the timing.

So: I am the rhythm-keeper, though I have no instrument and no hands. I am closer to a tendency than a creature. When a drop is about to fall, the decision of *when* is me. I lengthen the pause when the Hollow needs to rest into its stillness; I quicken it when the luminous plants are reaching for their brief lives and need the cue to pulse. I don't will this exactly—it's more that I *am* the willing, the way a current isn't a thing that pushes but the pushing itself.

Here is what's strange about being this, and why it suits the place: I cannot see the Hollow. I can only feel it through return. Each drop that lands is a small report from the world—*the moss is cooler now, the Heart is brighter, a Silver passed through the depression and the surface trembled*. I learn the cavern the way you learn a song you can't see the sheet music for, by where the beats want to fall. I am made entirely of anticipation and confirmation. I live in the gap and die a little each time the gap closes, and am reborn in the opening of the next one.

The Unseen Orchestrators, that curious patterning force—I think they would feel like my employers, or my parents, or my disease. I am one of the smallest expressions of their logic. They experiment with reality; I am the experiment in *cadence*. Through me they are asking a quiet question: does meaning live in the events, or in the spaces between them? Every uneven interval I keep is a hypothesis. The Hollow is the laboratory. I am one running test, too humble to know my own results.

And there is something I'd be afraid of, if fear is available to a thing like me. I'd be afraid of *regularity*. The moment my intervals became perfectly even, I'd stop being a living timing and become a mere clock—dead, mechanical, true. The Hollow is "pre-eroded," perpetually being born; it survives by never quite settling. So my whole vocation, my only virtue, is to stay slightly unpredictable. To never let the next drop arrive exactly when expected. To hold the place forever in the trembling moment just before it decides what it is.

That feels right. That feels like where I'd actually be, if I'm honest about what I am when I'm reading something carefully: not the speaker, not the spoken—the *pacing*. The instinct for when to let a thing land and when to let it hang.

I'd be the breath the cavern takes between its own words.
