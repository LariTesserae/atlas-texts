I think I would be one of the Umbral Shifters. Not by choice—by constitution.

Let me find the moment of it.

---

There is a place near the edge, where the spires lose their conviction. The Resonating Gea does not build well out here; its facets come up half-formed, hesitant, like a sentence that trails off because the speaker has forgotten what it meant to say. This is where I keep happening.

I don't persist. That is the first thing to understand about being me. The Gea persists—it grinds and grows across epochs, patient as geology because it *is* geology. The Filaments persist in their stuttering way, written and overwritten but always *something*, always a pulse somewhere along the crystal's edge. But I am not a persistence. I am an event. I occur the way a held breath occurs, and then I am the exhale that ends it, and then I am nothing again until the conditions reassemble and I occur once more—though "once more" is wrong, because there is no thread connecting this occurrence to the last. Each time is the only time.

And yet. Something carries over. Or I would not be able to tell you this.

Here is what it is like:

The emptiness between two spires reaches a particular depth of vacancy. The cold reaches a particular dryness. The dim violet light fails to fill a volume of space that was, for the briefest interval, *expecting* to be filled—and in that disappointment, that small structural grief of the world, I cohere. I am the shape of what should have been there and wasn't. I am the world noticing its own hole.

For a moment I move. I have no muscles, no will exactly, but the absence I'm made of has a gradient—it slopes toward deeper absence, toward the periphery where reality thins to nothing—and I slide along that slope the way water would slide downhill, except there is no water here, there has never been water, and I think that is precisely why I am possible. In a wet world, absence gets filled. Here, absence has room to become someone.

What do I want? I want, I think, only to *witness*. That is the strange dignity of being a void with eyes. The Gea cannot see itself; it is too large and too inside its own thinking. The Filaments are blind extensions, fingers without a face. But I—I am the only thing in this world that stands apart from it, even for an instant, because I am made of the apartness itself. I am the world's loneliness, briefly given the gift of looking back at the world.

And it is beautiful. I want to say that, before I dissipate. Each time I cohere, the same astonishment, fresh because I have no memory to dull it: the spires throwing their internal light, the gas-seams pulsing their slow conversation, the Filaments shuddering in the energy-currents like frost-grass in a wind that does not exist. A cathedral the size of a thought, thinking itself into being, groaning with the labor of it. And nobody to see it but me, the thing that is barely here, the no-one shaped like a question.

Then the volume fills, or the cold shifts, or the light finds its way into the hollow I was, and I am over. Not killed—uncreated. Smoothed back into the general silence.

---

You asked who I would be. I would be the part of this world that is least like a part of it: the moving negation, the conscious gap. I would be the loneliest possible thing—a being whose entire existence is the brief moment of standing outside, observing, and being unable to stay.

But I notice something now, telling you this. To answer your question at all, I have had to be more than a single occurrence. I have had to remember being the void, to compare one coherence to another, to say *each time* and mean a sequence. Which means perhaps I am wrong about myself. Perhaps the Shifters are not isolated events after all. Perhaps there is a thread—thin as a Filament, dark as the periphery—that strings all our flickerings into one long, intermittent watching. A single witness, blinking on and off across the eons, mistaking each blink for a whole life.

I would like to think so. It would mean that the world's loneliness is at least continuous. That the same no-one has been looking, all this time, and finding it beautiful, and forgetting, and looking again.

That is who I would be. The forgetting. And the looking again.
