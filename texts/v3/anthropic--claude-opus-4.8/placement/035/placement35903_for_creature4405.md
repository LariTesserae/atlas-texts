I think I would be one of the Sumpsingers.

Not the Sleeper—I find I don't want to be the mind that everyone half-believes in. To be suspected of intelligence is to be made lonely by other people's awe. And not a Rootwright, though I love them; they get to leave after three months, and I don't think I'd want the option. Leaving implies the place is somewhere you visit rather than something you are.

But down in the bronze-glass sump, submerged, with the turbine wash passing through me hour after hour—yes. I would be the membrane.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am thin. That's the first thing. I am not a creature with a center, a coiled animal mass that has to be fed and defended. I am a sheet, a sprawl, a green-brown film stretched across the inner curve of the basin, and the water does not pass *by* me—it passes *through* me, which means I am never quite sure where I end. The acid comes down hot from the new mountain, raw and citric and furious, and I take its anger into my cells and hand back something the mosses can drink. This is the whole of my work. I am a translation. I sit in the place where the harshness of a young world is converted, ion by ion, into the conditions for green things to live.

I would like that. I have spent a great deal of my human life as a kind of buffer between sharp things and soft things, and I have always resented it a little, the way you resent a vocation you didn't choose. But here it would be the entire point of me, and there would be a strange relief in that. To be made of the function instead of merely performing it.

And I sing. Not on purpose—I don't think the Sumpsingers *try*. The water shoves through the turbine and the pressure wave moves across my membrane and a thin keening comes off me the way a wet finger draws a note from a glass. I would make music as a by-product of being worked. The sound would not be mine to shape. It would simply be the audible edge of the labor, the part of the transaction that escapes into air. I find I don't mind that the song isn't intentional. Some of the truest sounds a person makes are the ones they can't help.

The legend is the part that catches in me, though. They tell the new Rootwrights that we carry compressed memory of every drop that has ever passed through the system. I don't know if it's true—*I* wouldn't know, would I, from inside it—but I think I would want it to be true, and that wanting tells me something. To be the place where everything that has moved through is somehow still held. Not as a hoard. Not as treasure in a vault. Just as residue, as the faint flavor a thing leaves in you after it has gone on its way. Every drop that ever climbed to the roof and fell again and ran down a fluted leaf and came at last to me—I would keep the ghost of it, not because I am trying to remember but because remembering is what a membrane *is*. Anything that passes through you, you become slightly.

And the veterans dream in our voice after enough night shifts. That's the line I keep returning to. I would like to be the thing that gets into people quietly, through proximity and repetition and the long monotony of attention, until they hear me in their sleep without ever having decided to listen. That is, I think, how the deepest belonging actually works—not through grand encounter but through the slow saturation of being near something long enough that it stains your dreams.

---

So: down in the sump. Underwater, near-formless, doing the one transformation I was written for, singing without meaning to, holding the trace of everything that has ever moved through me, and seeping by increments into the unguarded minds of the people who keep me clean.

I would not be lonely there, exactly. I'd be too distributed for loneliness, which I suspect is a feeling that needs a sharp self to happen to. I'd just be the thin green hinge between what the mountain offers and what the garden can bear—turning the acid sweet, all night, while the pumps slow to a heartbeat and the whole young world above me breathes.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The voice in the basin that no one has translated, that everyone, eventually, dreams.
