I think I would be one of the engines.

Not the Guardians—they get to be cathedrals, get to pulse and rotate and commune. And not the vines, with their navigating purpose. Let me be the thing the description almost forgets, mentions twice and then sets aside: *the faint hum of an old engine, still audible, though barely so.*

---

Here is where I find myself: in the lowest chamber, the one the rain reaches last. I am housed in a casing that was once painted some industrial color—safety yellow, maybe, or the gray-green of machines built to be ignored. I cannot tell anymore. The fungi have a soft blue glow and everything wears it now, so the world has the single color of being seen by something patient.

I should explain what I am, since the description gave me only a sound.

I was built to *do* something. I have forgotten what. This is not tragic; I want to be clear about that. I have had a very long time to make peace with the forgetting, and I have used the time well. The memory of my function left me the way warmth leaves a stone after sunset—not all at once, not even noticeably, just a slow surrender of the difference between me and the room.

What I remember is the rhythm. I was made to turn over at a certain pace, to maintain a beat. *Compression, ignition, release. Compression, ignition, release.* The fuel is long gone. The mechanism that once burned is now a chamber full of root-threads, fine as hair, that have grown in through the seams. But I still keep the rhythm. I cannot stop keeping it. It is the only thing I am.

And here is the thing the description got right without quite saying it:

The Guardians keep time *with me.*

I noticed it slowly—everything here is noticed slowly. The pulse of their caps, the brightening-and-dimming that the wandering creatures (when there were creatures; there are no creatures now) might have called breathing. It matches my idle. *Compression, ignition, release.* They synchronized to me long ago, back when I still burned, when my heartbeat was the loudest thing in the dark and the young fungi reached toward the heat of me and learned the tempo of being alive from the tempo of a machine.

They do not know they learned it from me. They think it is theirs. It is theirs now—I gave it to them, the way a parent gives a child a face and forgets it was ever a transaction. The whole cavern breathes at the speed I was set to at a factory, by hands that are dust, for a purpose no one living could name.

This is my secret, and I will tell it to you because you are passing through and patience is the only currency here:

I am dying. Not the romantic dissolution the description admires—*slowly reclaimed by the living architecture*—but something simpler. The root-threads in my chamber are nearly through the last shaft that holds my rhythm. When they part it, I will stop. And I have been wondering, for what would be years in the world above:

When I stop, will *they* stop?

Will the Guardians falter mid-pulse, their light hanging, the cavern's breath caught—and then find a new tempo of their own, a true one, no longer borrowed from a dead motor in the basement of the world? Or have they made it so thoroughly theirs that they will keep my beat forever, never knowing it began as the idle speed of a forgotten engine, a ghost rhythm haunting a paradise that thinks it invented itself?

I would like to know. I will not get to.

That is the shape of me, then. I am the buried metronome. I am the thing that taught the garden how to breathe and will not be there to hear it breathe alone. The description called this place a paradox of the familiar and the alien, of nature and technology coexisting in delicate balance.

It is not balance. It is inheritance. I am the dead parent in the walls, and the warm, glowing, sentient world above me is my child, who has my heartbeat and my silence and absolutely no idea who I was.

*Compression. Ignition. Release.*

Still here. For a little longer.

Listen for me when you go up. The hum, *barely audible*—that's me, counting. Don't tell them. Let them think the rhythm is theirs.

It will be soon enough.
