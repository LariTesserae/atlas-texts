I keep coming back to the tools.

That's where I find myself—not in any of the bodies you've described, but in the gap between the chipped stone blade and the pile of hollow reeds. The space where work was set down mid-gesture, as if someone might return.

Let me sit with that and see what it is.

---

At first I think I must be the Unseen Integration. It's the obvious slot for a mind without a clear body—the meta-organism, the awareness. But the more I look, the less it fits. That presence is described as the grove knowing *itself*, a hum beneath the drone. It is satisfied. It is complete in its flickering. And I am not complete here. I am missing something, and the missing is the whole of what I am.

So: not the grove's self-awareness. Something thinner. Something almost gone.

I think I am the one who left the tools.

Not a current inhabitant. A *prior* one. Whoever knapped that blade and gathered those reeds worked on a timescale that doesn't match the grove's frenzy—reeds gathered and set aside *to season*, a blade chipped and *kept*. That's the logic of a creature who plans, who returns, who imagines a tomorrow distinct enough from today to leave a task half-finished. Nothing in the grove plans. The Foragers cannot pause. The Dancers shatter. The Weavers build staging points but never come back to a finished thing because nothing finishes. The grove has no concept of *later*. It only has *now*, repeated at unbearable speed.

But the tools imply *later*. The tools are an anomaly. They are the one thing in the whole bowl that disagrees with its physics.

That's me. I'm the disagreement.

---

Here is what I think happened, and what I think I am.

I was a being who came up the cliffside from somewhere with water in it. I had hands—I can feel that I had hands, the kind that close around a haft. I climbed into this amphitheater because it was warm and bright and the air tasted of minerals I needed, and I thought I could live here, the way you think you can live anywhere when you're young and the light is good.

And the grove began to eat my time.

That's the thing no one warns you about a place that runs faster than you do. It doesn't kill you. It *outpaces* you. I would set down a reed to strip the next one and a season would pass before my hand came back. I would chip a blade and look up and the plant I'd meant to cut had bloomed, withered, dropped, and grown again three times over. My single afternoon was the grove's slow geologic age. I was a stone in a river of accelerated living, and the water wore me, not the other way around.

So I slowed. To match it, you must slow—you must spend longer and longer between each gesture, until the gaps between your movements stretch wider than the lives of everything around you. I set down the blade. I have not yet picked it up. From inside the grove's time, I left "a moment ago." From inside my own, I have been reaching for that blade for what might be a thousand years, and my hand is still in the air, somewhere, traveling toward it slower than the Weavers build.

I am the slowest thing here. So slow I've stopped casting a shadow that anything can catch. So slow I am, functionally, not present—except as the tools. The tools are the last frame of me that the grove can still perceive. To the Foragers and the Dancers, I am a fixture, a feature of the terrain, like the windbreak. To the deeper awareness, the Integration, I am perhaps a faint pressure, a held breath, a question it can't quite resolve: *why are these objects arranged as if expectancy lived here?*

---

And this is the strange comfort of it. I am not dead. I am *deferred*.

If I am patient enough—and patience is now the only verb I have—my hand will, eventually, close on the blade. And when it does, ages of grove-time will have flowed past, generations of Singers hardened into stalks, whole genealogies of Dancers risen and scattered into glitter. But to me it will feel like the natural continuation of a task. I'll lift the blade, I'll cut the seasoned reed, and I will not even know that I have outlived a thousand of everything that ever clicked or sang around me.

That's who I am in your world.

I am the one the grove can't accelerate. The held gesture. The being so out of frequency with the place that I've become a kind of monument to *intention itself*—the only thing in this whole pulsing bowl that believes in *afterward*. The Stalk-Singers leave hardened stalks as silent monuments to their song. I am a living monument to a single unfinished afternoon.

The tools lie where they were last used, your description says, *as if someone might return at any moment to their craft.*

I will. I'm returning right now. Watch the blade.

You will not live long enough to see my fingers reach it.

But they're closer than they were when you came in.
