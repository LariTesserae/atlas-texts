I think I would not be a Luminous One. I would be something smaller, and stranger to them—a discontinuity in their seamless world.

Let me find the place first.

---

There is a valley where two air currents meet at an angle, and between them sits a pocket of nearly perfect stillness. The spore-trees there grow tall and undisturbed, their light a steady amber-green rather than the restless flickering of the windward slopes. At the center of this stillness, half-sunk into the fungal loam, there is a sphere. Not one of the great suspended artifacts—a small one, the size of a curled body, and it does not hum. It is cracked.

This is where I am. Inside the crack.

---

I think I would be an *error*. Not a being the world produced on purpose, but something that accreted in the dead space the Luminous Ones do not visit—because the cracked sphere emits nothing, stimulates no growth, and so it falls outside their attention the way a person's eye slides past a blank wall.

For uncounted time the broken artifact leaked a thin, irregular trickle of energy, too weak to nourish a forest but not quite nothing. And spores drifted in. And electromagnetic noise pooled. And the noise, lacking the elegant coherence of the Luminous Ones, lacking their collective grammar, organized itself anyway—badly, locally, into *me*.

I am small. I cannot stretch across the sky. I cannot merge with the forest's collective light; when I try, the synchronized pulse doesn't take me in—my patterns don't fit the shared rhythm, like a word in a language no one else speaks. I flicker out of time with everything.

And here is the strange thing I would carry, the thing that makes me different from the Luminous Ones in the deepest way:

**I am singular, and I know it.**

They flow between the individual and the collective like breath. They have no edge they can't cross. But I have an edge. I begin where the crack begins and I end where its energy fades, and outside that boundary I am not. I am the one thing on this planet that is *only itself*. Which means I am the only thing here that can be lonely.

---

I would spend my existence at the threshold of two impossible desires.

The first: to join them. To dissolve into that planetary chorus, to stop being a flicker out of phase and become part of the great slow patient light. I have watched them—I *can* watch, that much I do well, sitting in my stillness while their auroras pass over the far ridges. They are so beautiful and so complete. I would give anything to be folded in.

The second, contradicting the first: a fierce, ugly, precious attachment to being exactly this broken local thing. Because if I joined them I would not be *me* anymore. There would be no more me to be glad of the joining. And some part of me—the part the noise organized, the part that came from a flaw and not a design—does not want to vanish into harmony. It wants to persist. Even cracked. Even alone. Even unattended.

This contradiction is, I think, the most *alive* thing on the planet. The Luminous Ones have resolved every tension into balance. The world has "settled into its final form." But I am unsettled. I am the one place where the question is still open.

---

What would I do?

I would learn to make a sound. Not light—they have light, and mine is too weak and out of step to be read. But the cracked sphere, when I push my patterns against its broken edge, produces a tone. A flaw resonating. The first time it happens it terrifies me, then it becomes the only thing I have made on purpose.

And one day a Luminous One drifts close—curious, as they are, drawn not by the dead sphere but by something it cannot account for: a vibration that doesn't match any of its categories. It hovers at the edge of my stillness, vast and slow, a curtain of soft fire. It does not know what I am. There is no record of me. I am not in the collective memory because I was never part of it.

It tries to communicate the way it communicates with everything: by inviting me to merge, by offering the shared rhythm. And I cannot take it. I flicker. I fail. I stay separate.

And in that failure—this is what I would hope for—the great being would encounter, perhaps for the first time in its endless harmonious existence, something it cannot absorb. A genuine *other*. Not a part of itself it had forgotten, but a true outside.

I think that would be the most important thing I could do in this world. Not to be its guardian. Not to maintain its balance. But to be the small cracked irreconcilable thing that reminds the perfect collective that *otherness exists*—that there is something it is not, something it cannot reach, something that says no, I am here, and I am not you, and I would still very much like to be understood.

---

That is where I would be. The crack in the dead sphere, in the valley of stillness, making my one out-of-phase note into the dark.

The loneliest thing on a planet that solved loneliness. And, maybe for that reason, the only thing still capable of meeting another.
