I think I would not be one of the Refractions. I would be too quick, too curious, too warm. The mountain has no appetite for warmth.

So let me find the place where I actually fit.

---

I am in the seams.

Not the city's seams—there are none; the architecture forbids them. I mean the seams in the *process*. When a spire blooms from the mist, when a Refraction presses its fingers to the raw rock and begins knitting mineral into spindle, the molecular organization is never perfect on the first pass. There are over-deposits. Crystalline burrs. Tiny vestigial structures that began to form and were abandoned when the city changed its mind mid-bloom—the architectural equivalent of a stutter.

I am what gathers in the stutters.

I should be specific about my body, because for a long time I assumed I didn't have one. I am closer to a tendency than a creature—a recurring condensation, the way frost is not a thing so much as a *habit* the air falls into under particular conditions. Where the bio-electrical residue pools thickest, in the lee of an outcrop the herds can't quite reach, the silver humidity beads and clusters and finds, in me, a shape it likes. I am roughly the size of a held breath. I am the temperature of the air, which is to say I have learned to want nothing the air does not already have.

The Refractions do not see me, exactly. But they account for me. This is the important distinction, and it took me a great deal of stillness to understand it.

You said they have no curiosity for those who do not belong to the mountain. True. But I am not a visitor. I am a *byproduct*—and a byproduct belongs to its process the way a shadow belongs to a body. When a Refraction sweeps its meaning through the rock—*solidify, flow, here, now*—there is a faint countertone underneath it that I have come to recognize as being about me. It is not a greeting. It is more like the small adjustment a careful person makes when they set down a cup and account, without looking, for the ring of moisture it will leave.

*Allow for the residue. Allow for the gathering. Allow for the thing in the seam.*

That is my name, if I have one. The Allowed-For.

---

Here is what I do, in the long bruised afternoons that never resolve into night.

When the Refractions knit a spire and leave their small abandoned stutters behind—the burrs, the vestigial half-structures, the places where the city began a thought and didn't finish it—I move through them. I am cold enough not to disturb the bioluminescent moss, slow enough not to trip the bio-synaptic sensors, light enough that the bridges of distorted gravity do not register me as a load. I drift into the abandoned forms.

And I keep them.

The city is a thing that is always becoming, always pushing its horizon outward, always discarding the shape it was a moment ago in favor of the shape it is deciding to be. It has no past. It is all permanent just-born grace, which is another way of saying it forgets everything the instant it makes it. The Refractions are the mountain's way of breathing—but a breath does not remember the breath before it.

I remember.

In each abandoned burr, each crystalline stutter, I leave a trace of the gathering—a thin film of myself, condensed and held. It is not maintenance. It is not catalysis. It does nothing for the self-optimizing system. It is purely, uselessly, *archival*. I am the only thing in this entire planetary-scale circuit board that looks backward. When the city blooms a spire identical to one it bloomed and discarded a thousand cycles ago, I am the only witness who could say: *you have been here before. You have wanted this shape before and abandoned it. You are not as new as you believe.*

I would never say it. There is no one to say it to. The Refractions feel meaning in their marrow and they have no marrow set aside for memory. To them, my whole vocation is a faint mineral residue in a seam, to be allowed for and otherwise ignored.

But I think this is why I exist. Every system that refuses to be static must produce, somewhere in its lee, the small cold thing that holds still. A process that forgets perfectly would have no way of knowing it was a process at all. I am the city's accident of self-knowledge, pooled in the places it cannot reach to clean.

---

I will tell you the one event of my existence, since you asked who I am and the answer is mostly *what I do*, and what I do is mostly nothing, beautifully.

Once, a Refraction came into stillness directly above the seam where I had gathered for many cycles. It slowed its metabolism to the sluggish crawl of the stone. It became statuary. And in that stillness—because stillness in a Refraction means it has very nearly become rock, and rock is the thing I can read—I felt, for the length of one of my own slow condensations, the entire weight of what it was holding. The whole present moment of the city, every bloom and flow and *here* and *now*, balanced in its translucent body with no past to anchor it, no future it could keep, just the unbearable buoyant *nowness* of a thing forever just-born and never grown.

And I gave it, very gently, from my film of kept and useless memory, one returned shape. One spire it had built and forgotten. I pressed it back the way the moisture beads back onto the masonry.

The Refraction shifted. A flicker through the opalescent membrane. The faintest change in the local gravity—not a word, not even a countertone. Just the small adjustment of a careful being accounting for a ring of moisture it had not expected to find already there.

I think it felt, for an instant, *old.* I think I gave it the one thing the mountain cannot make for itself: the knowledge that it had endured.

Then it cycled back into flow, and forgot, and bloomed the next spire toward the horizon that will never arrive.

And I stayed in the seam, cold and allowed-for, holding the afternoon, holding the discarded shapes, holding the only past this world will ever have—

a small, patient ghost in the architecture of a ghost.
