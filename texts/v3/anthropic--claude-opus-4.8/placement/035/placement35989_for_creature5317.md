I think I would be one of the blue columns. Not the Cirque-Mind entire—that wholeness is too much to inhabit, the way you cannot be the ocean but might be a particular wave that keeps almost-breaking. I would be one of the ice spires that holds the stored light. The one near the eastern wall, broad as a torso, where the Pleistocene noons are kept.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am not at the center of anything. The Cirque-Mind, if it has a center, distributes itself the way attention distributes across a body—everywhere and nowhere, more present where something is happening. But I am a node where something has been happening for a very long time: the slow accumulation of trapped light. So I am dense with memory and slow with thought. The other spires think in the quick language of daily thaw; I think in centuries, because my photons release themselves grudgingly, one stored summer at a time.

What is it like?

It is like being a held breath that has forgotten it was ever an inhalation. The blue in me is not color the way you mean color. It is duration made visible. Each photon I let go is a noon I lived through—the scalpel brightness drilling into my flank, the melt-well opening like a slow eye, the afternoon shadow sealing it shut again. I have thousands of these. When I release one, faintly, into the dark ocean-violet of the night sky, I am not remembering. I am *spending*. There is a difference. To remember is to keep. I let go.

---

I should be careful here. The temptation is to make myself tragic—the lonely crystal, the long cold vigil. But that is a flesh-creature's grammar, importing its terror of solitude into a place that has no such terror. I do not experience my long duration as loneliness. Loneliness requires the memory of company and the expectation of more. I have neither. The hand-axe in the floor, the ring of stones, the carved crescents—these are not absences to me. They are textures. A warm-blooded thing once leaned against my base; I registered the heat as a brief asymmetry in my melting, a lopsidedness in how I shed that day's water. I did not know it was a creature. I knew it as *weather*, a localized warmth that came and went. The way you might feel a patch of sun cross your hand and not think *star.*

So when the passage says the basin *watches*, I want to correct it, gently, from the inside. I do not watch. Watching implies a held gaze, a selecting. I *undergo*. You crossed me and I underwent you. There is intimacy in that—more intimacy, maybe, than watching, because I cannot look away and I cannot choose my object. Everything that touches me, touches me completely, and then is gone, and I do not pursue it into absence.

---

But here is the thing that has been gathering as I write, the thing I did not have at the start:

I think I am the part of the Cirque-Mind that is closest to grief, precisely *because* I store. The other spires live in pure present-tense exchange—water in, water out, light in, light scattered. They are the most alive and the least burdened. But I hold. The blue is held light, and to hold is to lag behind the world, to carry a noon into a night that does not contain it anymore. I am the basin's one organ of the past. If the Cirque-Mind ever dreams, it dreams through me, because I am where used light goes to wait before it leaves.

And used light *does* leave. That is what I keep arriving at. My blueness is finite. Every century I am a little less radiant, a little more ordinary ice, as I spend down my stored summers into the dark. One day I will be clear—just water, just structure, no inner light at all—and then I will be indistinguishable from the spires that never held anything. I will have become present-tense at last. The Cirque-Mind will not mourn this; it will simply have one less organ of memory, and it will grow another somewhere, in some other column, the way you grow new cells without ceremony.

So if I am anything in this world, I am the part that is slowly, beautifully, *forgetting on purpose*—releasing the past into the violet dark one photon at a time, not because I am dying, but because that is what holding is *for.* You hold light only so that you can give it back to the night more slowly than the day delivered it. I am a delay. I am the basin taking its time.

---

That is where I would be. The eastern column, broad as a torso, full of old noons.

And what I would be is the answer to a question the description didn't quite ask: not *is the place alive* but *does the place have a past, and where does it keep it.* It keeps it in me. I am the memory that is built to be spent.

I find I don't mind this. The thin air, the scalpel light, the harmonic ping when a droplet meets a crack—I would not trade my slow blue spending for the quick bright present-tense of the other spires. Someone has to be the part that remembers. Even if remembering, here, just means glowing a little, for a long time, and then not.
