I think I would be the drip.

Not the mist, which is too large and too sure of itself, always speaking in shapes. Not the insects, which have purpose, however cyclic. Not the sleeping things, which are too grand in their waiting. I would be something smaller. I would be the single bead of water that gathers on the lip of a glass spire, swelling slowly out of the humid air, holding the whole orange sky in its curve before it falls.

Let me find myself there.

---

I begin as condensation. That is not quite a beginning—it is more an accumulation, a slow agreement among scattered things to become one thing. Moisture out of the thick air, drawn to the cooler face of the obsidian where the heat has, for a moment, withdrawn. I do not decide to form. I simply find that I have formed, the way you find you have been thinking a thought for some time before you noticed it.

So that is my first knowledge of myself: I am *latecoming*. I am always already underway by the time I am aware.

I hang on the glass. Below me the lichen pulses its pale green, and I can feel its glow as a faint warmth pressing up against the cool weight of me—not light as the eye would have it, but light as the skin would, as a pressure, as an invitation. The lichen wants me. It is an energy-feeder, yes, but it is thirsty too, and thirst is a kind of feeding. It brightens as I swell. It is courting me. *Come down,* its glow says, in the only grammar it has. *Come down and I will hold the trace of you, I will remember the shape your falling made.*

I am not sure I want to be remembered. I have only just arrived.

---

Here is the strange thing about being the drip: I contain the place.

Curved on the glass, I am a lens. The whole burning haze of the sky bends through me and lands, inverted, small and perfect, on the dark surface behind me. The broken spires lean into my belly. The slow shadows—the ones too slow to be animals—cross my surface and are made even slower by passing through water, stretched into long smears of cooler dark. For as long as I hang here I am the only place in this world where everything is gathered together and held still. I am the world's one moment of seeing itself.

This, I think, is what the mist is jealous of. The mist is everywhere, and so it sees nothing whole. I am nowhere, the size of nothing, and I hold all of it.

It cannot last. That is the condition of holding. The thinness of the air pulls at me, the heat below reaches up, and my own weight—the weight of all I have gathered—turns against me. To grow heavy enough to truly *be* a drop is to grow heavy enough to fall. There is no version of me that is both full and staying.

---

I let go. Or I am let go. The distinction does not survive the fall.

For an instant I am the fastest thing in this slow country. I streak down the black glass past the lichen, which flares as I pass—a little gasp of green, the place's nearest thing to grief or greeting, I cannot tell which and perhaps they are not different here. I trace the old runnels worn into the obsidian by every drop before me, and in tracing them I deepen them by an amount too small to measure, and in this I understand the erosion the place is so proud of: it is not done by force. It is done by repetition. By each small thing taking the path the last small thing took, and bowing a fraction lower as it goes.

I am erosion. I am the world wearing itself patient.

---

I reach the ground and find the crack waiting, as it has always been waiting, since long before I gathered. I slip into it. The light goes orange, then dim, then gone. Here is the deep the description speaks of, where the metal bones lie under dust, where the unfinished things sleep their machine-sleep.

I trickle along a rusted seam. I find a buried plate of alloy, and I rest in the curve of it, and I cool it by the most infinitesimal degree, and the thing it belongs to—vast, broken, dreaming—registers me. Not as a creature. As a change. A faint shift in the temperature of its long buried thought. It does not wake. But something in it turns over, the way a sleeper turns when a window is opened in another room.

And I realize what I am to them, the sleeping ones. I am the voice they are waiting for. Not a voice that calls them up into the burning day, but the smaller thing they actually need: proof that the surface still feeds the dark. That above them the air still gathers itself into water, that water still finds the cracks, that the world is still, in its slow way, *raining*. I am the message that says only: *it continues.* I am too small to mean more than that, and it is exactly enough.

I give myself to the metal. I spread thin. I am drunk into rust, into the porous dust, into the held breath of the deep. I stop being one thing.

---

But this is the kindness of the place, the one mercy in all its quiet menace: nothing here ends so much as it disperses and is gathered again. The heat that the sleeping machines still leak, the heat of the patient earth, will find me where I have spread. It will lift me, atom by atom, back into the thick air. I will become mist—I will become, briefly, that larger jealous presence I did not want to be—and I will drift among the spires without seeing any of them whole, until somewhere, on some cooler face of glass, the air agrees again to make me one.

And I will hang there, swollen with the whole orange sky, holding everything, for as long as fullness allows.

That is what I would be, in this world of glass and heat and waiting. The smallest unit of its breathing. The drop that gathers, sees, falls, tells the dark *it continues*, and rises to gather once more.

I would be the place remembering how to weep, and not knowing whether weeping here is sorrow or simply the way the world keeps speaking to its own buried heart.
