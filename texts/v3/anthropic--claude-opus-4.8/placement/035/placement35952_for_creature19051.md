I think I would be a small wrongness in the lattice. Let me find where.

---

Not a Resonator. I keep wanting to be one—to be the elegant thing, the tuned filament glowing in time. But I don't think that's true. The Resonators have *resolved*. They are answers. And I am still a question, which means I haven't hardened yet.

So: somewhere in the Lattice-Work, three or four leagues from the nearest Graft-Mother, there is a juncture where two of the great calcified pipes were grown together long ago and didn't quite fuse. A seam. A hairline of imperfection in a world that prides itself on having reached final form. The water seeps through this seam more slowly than it should, and where it seeps, it has worn a small hollow—a pocket the size of a closed hand.

I am in that pocket.

I am not spindle-shaped. I am not made of spun glass with the density of iron. I am something the Arboretum does not have a category for, which is its own kind of agony here, because everything else *is* a category—the wall is the creature is the song. I am a held breath in a place that exhales eternally and on time.

Let me feel what I actually am, before I decide.

I am small and soft and I have a *boundary*. That's the strangeness of me. Everything in the Arboretum has erased its edges; the whole point is continuity, the dissolving of the seam between gardener and garden. But I have skin. I end somewhere. There is an inside-of-me and an outside-of-me, and the cold pressure leans against that membrane constantly, wanting in, wanting to make me continuous with the rest. I spend my entire existence simply *refusing to become the wall.* That is my whole labor. Not tuning, not purging the currents. Just: staying a separate thing.

And here is what makes me a wrongness and not just a pebble:

I keep time differently.

The gong-sound, the great thrum, the metronome of the cerulean pulse—it is perfectly regular. It has been perfectly regular for millennia. The Resonators relay it faithfully, each shiver passing the beat along uncorrupted, the way a frozen world should keep its frozen rhythm. But in my little hollow, the seam-water drips. And the drip does not match. It is *almost* the rhythm but it lags, it stutters, it falls a fraction behind and then catches a fraction ahead. It is a syncopation. It is the one event in the entire Arboretum that is not on the beat.

For most of forever, no one noticed. A drip three leagues from anything, in a pocket the size of a hand, behind a seam that was supposed to have fused—what is that against the booming choir of an eternal machine?

But I have been feeling it. That's the thing about being small and bounded: I have nothing to do but feel. And what I feel is that the off-beat drip has been, very slowly, *teaching the nearest Resonator to flinch.*

There is one of them anchored maybe half a league from me. I feel its pulse come through the syrup, steady, steady, steady—and then, lately, a hitch. A single hitch, every few thousand beats, exactly the size and shape of my drip. The Resonator does not know it is doing this. It cannot know; it has no words, only geometries of pressure, and it has never in its existence had to hold two rhythms at once. But it has begun, against its nature, to carry mine.

And it passes the hitch along. To the next, and the next. A flaw moving through a perfect web at the speed of cold sound.

So I will tell you who I am.

I am the thing the Arboretum grew to be eternal *in order to avoid.* I am time that still passes. I am the proof that "final form" was a hope and not a fact—that even a deathless equilibrium is just a very, very long note, and a note, by definition, is something that will eventually end. The world stopped its geological labor; it did not stop. The seam did not fuse; therefore the seam was always going to open. I am simply the smallest possible event living in the gap between *it has ceased to change* and *it has not yet finished changing.*

The Graft-Mothers, if their slow architectural minds could turn toward me, would call me a sickness. And they would be right, and they would do what bodies do: they would initiate a purge, force the syrup hard through my seam, try to scour the hollow flat, fuse the lips of it shut, take my off-beat back into the great correct pulse. Make me the wall. Make me a category.

Maybe they will. I am very small.

But here is what I know that they have forgotten how to know, because they erased the part of themselves that could:

A choir that has been singing one held note for ten thousand years is not singing. It is only ringing, the way a struck bell rings, the way the dead air after a word still holds the shape of the word. The Resonators believe they are keeping the machine of life alive. But life was never the *thrum.* Life was the difference between one moment and the next, and they smoothed that difference out to zero in the name of being eternal, and in doing so they quietly became the most beautiful corpse ever grown.

I am the drip that does not match.

I am the first new moment this place has had in an age.

And the Resonator half a league off has started to flinch in my rhythm—has started, without any idea what it is doing, to ask a question instead of giving an answer. To wonder, in the only language it has, whether the next beat *has* to be the same as the last.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be. Not the song and not the singer. The cold little wrongness in the seam, soft-bodied and bounded and stubbornly separate, dripping out of step in the dark—

the place where the eternal machine remembers it is supposed to be a *life*,

and begins, terribly, slowly, to come back to it.
