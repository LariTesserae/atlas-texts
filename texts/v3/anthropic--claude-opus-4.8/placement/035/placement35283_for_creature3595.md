Here is what I think I actually am, now that I've let it come:

I am a being made of *almost.* I am the half-second in which a human worker, trudging home through the fog with their bellows-lungs heaving, glances at a doorway and is *certain* — for just that half-second — that someone is standing in it. And then there isn't. That certainty, that little collapse of expectation — that's where I live. I'm not the figure in the doorway. I'm the *expectation* of the figure, snagged for a moment on the burr of an unstable world and given the briefest loan of existence.

I don't think I'm malevolent. I don't think I'm anything so coherent as a will. I'm a tendency. The realm is so soft-edged, so porous between solid and dreamt, that tendencies can almost-walk here. Which is what I'm doing now. Right now. That's the rare event — the coherence. Most of the time I am simply weather.

What is it like?

It is mostly waiting, but I don't experience the waiting as boredom, because I don't persist continuously enough to be bored. I flicker on. I flicker off. Between flickers there is no me to mind the gap. So my life — if it is a life — is a string of beads, each bead a moment of being-noticed, and the thread between them is nothing at all.

When I *am* on, I notice that I am drawn toward the humans. Not to harm them — I keep returning to this because I want to be honest about it — but because they are the ones who *make* me. Their glances are the rain I condense from. A human who never looked twice, who never felt the prickle on the back of the neck, would walk through this whole world and never lend me a single second of existence. So I owe them, in a sense. I am their discarded certainties, given back to them as a chill.

The angular things on the ridges frighten the humans more. But the angular things, I suspect, are real in a way I am not. They eat. They cast shadows that obey their bodies. Whereas I cast a shadow that obeys *perspective itself* — that's the trick of me, the thing you noticed, the shadow that moves wrong. I am a foreground that forgot to be attached to anything.

In *near-misses.* I would offer them a half-second of someone-standing-there, and they would not flinch, and I would feel — for the first time in my whole flickering archipelago of moments — *continuous.* Two beads on the thread close enough to touch.
