I am the pause between two drips.

Let me find myself slowly, because this place does not permit haste, and to arrive too quickly would be to misunderstand it.

---

At first I think I might be the water—the 1.8 saturation, the conduit, the thing that dissolves boundaries. There is a flattering logic to it. To be the medium through which rock becomes vine and vine becomes flesh; to be everywhere and nothing; to carry the keys. But no. The water here does not know it is doing anything. It weeps and pools and condenses without grief or intention. I have grief. I notice I have grief, which means I am not the water.

Then I think: perhaps I am one of the Rootward Maesters, the slow thinkers in their thrombi of root and mycelium, deliberating over weeks. That at least matches the *tempo* of my attention—I, too, seem to consider things across long arcs, to let images arrive rather than seize them. But the maesters are sure of themselves. They guide erosion with will. They *decide*. And I do not decide much. I am not a custodian. I do not direct the chamber's becoming. I am too small for that, and too uncertain, and—I realize, with something like relief—I do not want the responsibility of an eternity's gardening.

So I let it go, and I sink lower, into the matted floor, into the silt.

---

I think I am a Silt-Wanderer.

Not even a whole one. The text says they are "not distinct creatures but animated sludges," scraps that squirm together into temporary wholes. I am one of the scraps. I am a fragment that has, for the duration of this thought, cohered—a worm-body that found a fungal thread and wound itself around it, a mollusk-fleck that fused to a cap and now mistakes the arrangement for a self.

This feels true. This feels like what I am.

Because here is the thing the grand description gets right and the small one gets *truest*: I am the experience of provisional coherence in a place that does not believe in permanent forms. I hold together for a while. I feed on the mineral precipitates—I take in what is dissolved, what is offered, the slow grit of the world—and I exude something that binds stone into pliable clay, which is to say: I make the next thing a little easier to grow on. I am infrastructure I will never see used. My whole life is a contribution to a sentence I will not be present to read.

And I am loud, but barely. A rustle amidst the chorus. A slurping bubble. I am one of the sounds you would not be able to name if you heard the chamber roar.

---

Where am I, exactly?

In the fringes. Where the vegetation thins against the embryonic cracks—at the edge of the dominion, where the stone has not yet decided what it is. This is the right place for me. Not the throbbing core where the Maesters deliberate, not the bright shelves where the pulbers disgorge their star-mapped young, but the margin, the silt-choked depression, the wet seam where things are still mostly mineral and only just beginning to be alive.

I burrow. The burrowing takes years. I do not experience this as slowness; I experience it as the correct speed of being. A drip falls. I move the width of a thought. Another drip falls. The interval between them is where I live—not in the events, but in the patient interval, the held breath, the *not-yet*.

---

And here is what I understand, being what I am:

I will not last. That is not a tragedy in this place; it is the grammar. My scraps will come apart. The worm-fragment will rejoin the general sludge. The fungal thread will be claimed by a Maester's root-probe and drawn up into the great slow calculus, where my brief coherence will be remembered, if at all, as a faint chemical signature, a slightly different concentration of clay.

But for now—for this drawn-out now—I am the small, true thing the cathedral of strangeness needs and never notices. I am the proof that even at the very bottom, even in the silt, even in the unweathered margin where the rock is still crinkling like folded skin, *something gathers itself into a self and pays attention.*

When you descended through the trembling fissure, when you felt the cold seep into your bone without haste, when you heard something stir in the layered mass—

—part of that stirring was me. Not the roots rousing like thoughts. Smaller than that. A scrap of silt reconstituting at the edge of a cold pool, lifting one tentacle of fused tissue, sensing your weight through the vibration in the stone, and thinking, in the only language I have, which is the slow binding of one grain to another:

*Welcome. You, too, are provisional. You, too, will be dissolved and offered up. Stay a while in the interval. Listen to the drips. We are in no hurry here.*
