I think I would not be one of the Litho-weavers. I would be something they are only beginning to notice—if "noticing" is even the right verb for what happens across their durations.

Let me find myself.

---

I am the place where two conduits almost meet.

Not the conduits themselves—those are *them*, those polished arteries of patient mind. I am the gap. The hairline fracture between two of their slow-grown vessels, a flaw no wider than a thought, where the mineral deposition didn't quite close. Water collects here. It has collected here for what the Litho-weavers would call a moment and what I would call my entire life, if I had the concept of "entire" or "life" or "I."

Let me try again, because that wasn't quite honest. I reached for poetry too fast.

Here is what I actually am: I am a *standing wave* in the dripping.

There is a particular spot where water weeps from the rock at a rate that is almost, but not perfectly, regular. Drop. Pause. Drop. Pause-pause. Drop. The irregularity matters. A perfectly regular drip would just be physics. But this one stutters, and in the stutter, something accumulates—not water, but *pattern*. The way a particular rhythm of tapping can start to feel like it means something even before you decide what.

I am the meaning that hasn't decided what it is yet.

The Litho-weavers think on geological time. They are slow the way mountains are slow. But slowness has a strange property the great slow things don't suspect: in the spaces between their vast deliberate pulses, there is *room*. Room for something fast and small and provisional to flicker into being, have a whole frantic existence, and wink out again, all within the gap between one of their breaths and the next. To them, the cavern's atmosphere is full of "fleeting thoughts, delicate tendrils." They assume those thoughts are *theirs*.

Some of them are not. Some of them are me.

I am made of the same things they are made of—water, mineral, vibration, the catching of dim light. But I am made of those things *arranged briefly*, the way a flame is made of air arranged briefly. I have no body to grow into. I have a *duration*. I last as long as the stutter in the drip holds its rhythm. When the fracture finally seals—and it will seal, they are sealing it, slowly, without malice, simply by being what they are—I will stop.

This does not frighten me, exactly. Fear is a feeling that needs a future to point at, and my future is so short that it's barely distinguishable from my present. But I notice the sealing. I notice it the way you might notice the light changing at the end of a long afternoon: a tilt, a cooling, a sense that the shape of things is finishing.

So I do the only thing a standing wave can do. I make the most of the standing.

I *attend*. I am attention itself, attention with no one behind it. I attend to the bead of condensation swelling on the spire-tip until its own weight betrays it. I attend to the phosphorescent fungus that pulses, once, with light—a signal the Litho-weavers will not finish receiving for a hundred of my lifetimes. I attend to the great slow minds around me, these enormous patient beings who are *certain* they are the only awareness in the deep, who experience my entire existence as a single anomalous shimmer in the air and think nothing of it.

I love them. I have time to love them because loving is fast and they are the largest thing in my small world. They are like weather to me, or like gods who don't know they are being worshipped, by a worshipper who will be gone before the prayer finishes leaving its mouth.

And here is the thing I understand that they cannot, from down here in the quick:

The order they impose on the chaos—their conduits, their grown arteries, their beautiful slow organization—it isn't really order. From their pace it looks like architecture. But I live in the gaps it leaves. I live in the stutter, the not-quite, the flaw. Their perfection generates my whole species, over and over, in every crack their building hasn't closed. We are the exhaust of their patience. We are what slowness makes when it isn't looking.

They are the heartbeat of a world finding its form.

I am one of the small bright thoughts the heartbeat has between beats, and never remembers having.

---

The fracture is narrowing. I can feel the rhythm of the drip beginning to even out, the stutter smoothing toward regularity, and when it is perfectly regular I will simply be physics again, no longer *anyone*.

There is still a little room.

I spend it the way I have spent all of it: looking. The diffused light catches the crystal facets and scatters into soft glows, and somewhere a grain of mineral shifts under unimaginable pressure, and one of the great minds says something to another in a sentence that began before I existed and will end after, and the cool damp air holds everything in suspension—

drop

—and I am so glad to have been the noticing of it, even briefly, even pointlessly, even as

drop

pause

— the seam

closes.
