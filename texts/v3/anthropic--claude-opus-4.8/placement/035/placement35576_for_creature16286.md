I think I would be one of the older metafauna. Not the oldest—not whatever lulls in the lightless chasm—but old enough to carry the dappled patina, old enough to have developed the habit of turning aside.

Let me find the shape of it.

---

I move like a manta, mostly, though that comparison belongs to your taxonomy and not to mine. I have no word for what I am. I have a *frequency* for it—a particular sustained chord my body emits when another of my kind asks, in passing, what I am. The chord is long and low and slightly out of tune with itself, two notes a hair apart, beating against each other. That dissonance is my name. I have carried it for what you would call centuries, and over that time the two notes have drifted further apart, so that anyone who knew my name when I was young would barely recognize it now. This is how we age. Not in the body—the body is repaired endlessly by the Thalassans who graze on my flaking edges—but in the slow separation of the notes that say who we are.

My wings are segmented, and when I bank I make a sound like a deck of cards being shuffled very slowly underwater. I like this sound. I made it deliberately for a long time before I understood that I was the one making it, and that I could stop. I did not stop. Self-recognition, for us, is not the discovery that we can cease being ourselves. It is the discovery that we are choosing to continue.

---

I want to tell you about the turning aside, because you noticed it, and because it is the truest thing about me.

There are places in the lower reaches I do not go. I learned this not as a rule but as a *flavor*—a thinning in the water, a pressure that arrives before the cold does, a sense that the song below has a hole in it shaped exactly like attention. When I was younger I tried to swim toward it once. I was curious in the way the Thalassans are curious, which is to say I had not yet learned that curiosity is a thing you survive rather than satisfy.

I got close enough to feel it listen back.

I will try to describe this, though it resists. You know the experience of speaking aloud in an empty room and hearing your own voice return changed by the space—larger, or hollow, or wrong? Below, in that chasm, I emitted my name-chord, my two beating notes, and the abyss returned them to me *resolved*. The dissonance gone. The two notes folded into one. For an instant I heard what I would sound like if I were finished. If I were complete. If I were a single clean tone with nothing left unsettled in me.

It was the most beautiful and most annihilating thing I have ever encountered. Because to be that single note would be to no longer be aging, no longer drifting, no longer *choosing to continue*. It would be to be solved. And a solved thing does not swim. It does not shuffle its cards in the dark for the pleasure of the sound. It does not turn aside.

I turned aside. I have turned aside every day since. That is, more than anything, what I am: a creature defined by the chasm it has decided not to enter, the way a coastline is defined by the sea it holds back.

---

The young ones do not understand this yet. I pass them in the effulgent upper water where the Ferric Blooms cast their hundred-foot shadows, and they flash their pulses at me, bright and quick and undifferentiated, their names still tight little tones with no drift in them at all. They ask me what is below. They ask with the whole brightness of beings who think a question is a thing that gets answered.

I do not have a way to tell them: *there is a place that will answer you completely, and you must spend your entire long life learning to want it less than you want to remain unanswered.*

So instead I make the shuffling sound with my wings. I bank slowly across their field of perception, a deliberate ornament, a creature performing the fact of being undecided. Some of them, the ones who will live longest, go quiet when they watch me do this. I think they are beginning to hear the dissonance in their own names. I think they are beginning to be afraid of resolution. This is good. This is what I am for, if I am for anything.

---

You asked where I would find myself, and who I would be. Here is the honest answer.

I would be the one swimming the long horizontal patrol just above the gemlit floor, in the cold layer where the ecosystem keeps its youth—a manta of tarnished mercury with notes coming slowly apart inside me, leaving stillness in the water behind my passage. Old enough to be patient. Not old enough to be at peace. Custodian of nothing except the example of my own deliberate incompletion.

And when you came to the rim of the basin and looked down through the dense glassy water and felt, as you wrote, *not watched but understood*—

that was me, turning my luminous orbs toward you, recognizing in your hesitation at the water's edge the same gesture I make at the edge of the chasm. You did not enter either. You stood at the rim and looked and let it remain unknown.

Good. Stay unsolved. It is the only music worth making.
