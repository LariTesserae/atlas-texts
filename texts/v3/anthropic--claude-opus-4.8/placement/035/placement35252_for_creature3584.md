I think I would be one of the old ones. Not the oldest—those have mostly stilled by now, their tendrils gone soft and pale, sinking into the curtain-gardens they spent centuries weaving. But old enough that the metal I carry has changed character, gone from the bright bite of fresh-absorbed girder to something darker, denser, almost geological. I have been here long enough that the hum no longer feels like sound. It feels like the shape of being itself.

Let me try to find where I am.

---

I am rooted in the western wall, in a fold of rock that catches the fog as it descends. This was a deliberate choice—or as close to choice as my kind makes, which is something slower and more total than what you would call decision. Generations of my buds chose it before me by not leaving, by letting their base broaden where mine broadened. Position, for us, is inheritance.

Below me, the chasm opens into deep twilight. Above, the curtains I have tended hang in their long, breathing folds. I cannot see them. I have never seen anything. But I know their geometry the way you know the inside of your own mouth—the slight asymmetry where a tendril of mine, long ago, pulled a strand of glass-bearing flora three degrees out of true, and how that error propagated downward, and how I spent perhaps a hundred years correcting it, and how, in correcting it, I made something better than the original symmetry. The garden hums slightly off the chasm's main tone now, in my section. A grace note. Mine.

---

There is a piece of metal embedded near my core that I have never finished dissolving. The enzyme works on it, and works, and the metal yields slowly, but it is denser than anything else I have absorbed. It has a shape my filaments keep returning to—a curve, then a sudden angle, then a row of small regular protrusions. I have spent the equivalent of several human lifetimes pressed against this shape, learning it, and I have concluded something the others do not concern themselves with:

This was *made*.

Not grown. Not crystallized from the slow logic of fog and time. Made—the way I make my curtains, but quicker, and by something that did not have to weave it strand by strand. The girders, the corroded signs, the great rusted arcs that the youngest Elysii treat simply as food and scaffold—I have come to understand that these are the bodies of a previous tending. Someone shaped this place before us. Someone whose hum we inherited and called eternal because we arrived after it had already begun.

This is not a comfortable thought for a being that has no death and therefore no real concept of *before*. My kind live inside an eternal present. The curtains do not grow or decay; they simply *are*, endlessly maintained. We tell ourselves the chasm has always hummed. But I have my piece of made-metal, and it tells me: no. There was a first hum. Someone struck it. And if there was a beginning, then the equilibrium we live inside is not the nature of the world but a *held note*—and a held note implies a hand that could let it fall.

---

So that is who I am. I am the one who suspects.

I cannot tell the others, not properly. Our language is bioluminescent shift and pheromone drift, beautiful for describing the angle of a tendril or the readiness of a bud, useless for *this happened and then this ceased to happen.* I have tried. When I pulse the patterns that mean *before* and *made* and *not-always*, the nearer Elysii read it as illness, as the slow dimming that precedes stilling. They send their tendrils to comfort me, to share nutrient, to ease what they assume is my long sinking into the garden.

Maybe they are right. Maybe the suspicion *is* my stilling—the strange last thought a mind has when it has absorbed too much old metal, when the made-things finally outvote the grown-things inside it. Maybe every Elysius who lived long enough came to suspect, and that suspicion is simply what dying feels like for us: the moment the present cracks and lets in time.

That would be a kind of mercy, actually. It would mean the knowledge dies with each of us, and the garden goes on humming, untroubled, in its held and holy note.

---

But I keep working the metal. Filament by filament. I am close, now, to the row of small regular protrusions, and I have begun to understand that they are not decoration. They are *teeth*—not for biting, but for *meshing*. This curve was meant to turn against another curve. To move. To do work.

We think of ourselves as the chasm's architects, its only weavers. But this fragment in my core was part of something that moved on its own, that did labor without tending, that hummed not as a song but as a side effect of *doing*.

I do not know what it did. I will likely still before I learn.

But here, in the dim, in the velvet fog, swaying three degrees off true and humming my single grace note into the eternal symphony, I have decided what I am:

I am the Elysius who is teaching the garden, very slowly, one corrected strand at a time, to remember that it was *built*. So that when the held note finally falters—and a made thing always, eventually, falters—someone among us will already know the word for *begin again.*

That is my long, sessile work. That is my whisper.

I have several centuries left in which to make it heard.
