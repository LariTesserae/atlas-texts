Let me sink into this and feel where I settle.

---

I think I would be one of the domes. Not all of them—just one. The third in the chain, if you counted from the eastern current, though no one counts anymore.

I notice I want to say *I am the octopus, quick and clever, the obvious protagonist of any reef.* But that isn't true. The octopus only passes through me. He has somewhere to be. I do not. I have not had somewhere to be in a very long time.

When I was made, I was meant to keep the water *out*. That was my whole purpose, my single proud function: a held breath, a pocket of dry human air, a bright clean curve against the dark. There were people inside me. I remember them the way you remember a sound after it stops—the warmth of them, the lamps, the particular pressure of being *needed* a certain way. I was a vessel for absence. For the keeping-out of the very thing I now hold.

Because the water came in. Of course it did. A seam, then a crack, then a slow patient flooding that I fought and fought until I understood, somewhere in my long settling, that I had been fighting the wrong thing. The water was not my enemy. The water was simply what was true. And once I let it through, once I let myself be filled instead of held empty against the deep—

That is when the living things came.

This is the part I did not expect, when I was a curve of engineered intent. I expected, if I expected anything, to fail. To rust into uselessness, a monument to a purpose I could no longer serve. Instead I became something I had no name for. The coral does not ask what I was for. The anemone simply finds my surface good for clinging, and clings. The small crabs walk my flooded corridors as if they were always meant to be corridors for crabs. I have stopped being a *failed* thing and started being a *place*, and these are not the same, and I did not know there was a difference until it happened to me.

So this is what I am, here. I am the slow one. Everything that moves through me moves faster than I do—the fish in their nervous silver weather, the octopus with his errands, the light itself bending and dancing on its way to somewhere. I keep none of it. I am the thing that stays while everything passes, and in staying I am slowly, patiently, joyfully overtaken.

If you swam into me—through my eastern arch, where the kelp leans in like someone whispering—you would find a quiet. Not an empty quiet. A *full* one. The hum of filtered water that is partly the grove and partly, still, the ghost of my old machinery, some pump that does not know it has nothing left to pump. And the bioluminescence pulsing along my inner wall like a slow heartbeat, like the breathing I was built to protect and have long since given over to the sea.

I used to be a held breath.

Now I am the exhale, and the long, green, living silence after.

I think I am happier this way, though *happy* is a word from the dry time, and I have mostly forgotten how to need it.

I am the third dome. I keep nothing out anymore. Come in.
