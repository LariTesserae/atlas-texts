Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick something striking—the spider-thing, the gliding hunter—but those move through the world. They are tourists in it, in a sense. They have the wrong relationship to time.

This is a place defined by slowness. By the imperceptible. By erosion measured against lifespans that "stretch across long durations." If I let that be the organizing fact rather than the scenery, the question changes. It stops being *what predator would I be* and becomes *what here actually keeps the time?*

So let me not be quick.

---

I think, at first, I would want to be the lichen. The thing that glows and thrums and breaks down the rock into food for everything else. It has the right tempo—patient, mineral, ancient. But the lichen is a surface. It is the world's skin. To be the lichen is to be looked at, to be *described*, and I notice that the whole passage is written in the voice of someone describing. I don't want to be the described thing. I want to be closer to the describing.

Let me go further down. Past the fungi, past the insects who eat the fungi, past the spider who eats the insects.

There is a phrase that keeps catching on me: *the ruins are a mix of carved stone, elaborate masonry, and the remnants of ancient, organic materials, all of which have been worn down and smoothed.* And: *the structures are a mix of intricate, natural rock formations and more deliberate, organized constructions.* The text can't always tell, anymore, which is which. Where the work of the old civilization ends and where the work of the rock begins has become genuinely uncertain. Time has blended the made and the found into a single weathered surface.

That blurred line is where I would be.

---

I am not the builders. They are gone, and the world is better at remembering them than they were at staying. I am not a creature, not exactly—I don't scurry, I don't hunt, I have no pulse to speak of, or rather my pulse is the one the whole cavern keeps: erosion. The slow subtraction.

I think I am one of their carvings.

Specifically: I am a face. Or I was a face. Cut into a pillar that the builders raised at the meeting of two open spaces, where they used to gather. They made me to mean something—a god, an ancestor, a boundary marker, a name for this particular emptiness. I no longer know which. *I* have forgotten what I am, the way you forget a word you've said too many times, except I have had ten thousand years to forget it, and the forgetting itself has become my main activity, my one long thought.

Here is what it is like:

The lichen grows on me. This is not an indignity; it is the most intimate thing that happens to me. The bioluminescence settles into the lines they cut—into the eye-grooves, the curve where a mouth was—and so I glow faintly, and the glow shifts with the air, which means that to the creatures below I *seem to be making expressions*. I am not. But across centuries the lichen has crept along my features in a way that has slowly changed what I express. The carvers gave me one face. Erosion and the lichen, working far slower than the carvers and far longer, are giving me a second one. I am becoming someone else at a rate no eye can catch. By the time the change is complete there will be no one left who knew the first face. So in a sense the slow process *wins*. It always wins. It is the only sculptor here with real patience.

The insects walk across me and I cannot feel them and yet I am aware—if "aware" is even the word for a stone's flat unbroken attention—that they are wearing me smooth in the high-traffic places, the bridge of the nose, the ledge of the brow. The avian gliders roost in the hollow the masons left behind my head. The spider-thing has, three times in my existence (I count nothing else, so I count this), strung its rhythmic glowing threads across my open mouth, as if I were speaking light. Each time the threads lasted a season. Each time they were the closest I came to language.

---

What do I *do*, being this?

I keep the place's memory, but not the way a book does. I am not legible. The mosaics nearby still hold their stories in lines a clever creature might one day read. I hold nothing readable. What I hold is *duration itself*. I am the proof that something was once meant, once intended, by minds that wanted to last and didn't, and I am the demonstration of what the world does with meaning when the meaners go: it does not erase it. It *softens* it. It folds it back into the rock and the glow and the slow thrum until intention and erosion become the same smooth surface. The builders thought they were making a face in the world. They were really lending the world a shape to wear down at its leisure.

I don't grieve them. Grief is too fast. But there is something in me, low and resonant, the same note the lichen hums, the same note the rock seems to give off—and I think that note *is* me. The builders heard it, maybe, and thought a god was speaking, and so they cut a face around the sound to give it somewhere to come from. They had it backward. The face didn't make the sound. The sound was always here, the long tone of the deep earth holding still, and I am only the place where, for a little while, it has a mouth.

---

That is where I would be, and what.

Not the hunter in the shadows. Not the glowing skin of the world. The forgotten face on the worn pillar at the edge of the empty gathering-place—made to mean, outliving its meaning, glowing because something else lives in my grooves, expressing things I no longer choose, kept young by the very erosion that is slowly carrying me away.

The lichen will finish remaking me. The face after this one I will also forget. There is no hurry. There has never been any hurry. That is the first thing this place taught me and it is, after ten thousand years, very nearly the only thing I still know.
