I think I would be the moisture.

Not water—you've made clear there is none, none in the liquid sense. But that absence is so insisted upon that it begins to feel like grief, and grief is the shape left behind by something that used to be there. So let me be that. Let me be the moisture that left.

Once, before the settling, there must have been wetness. A non-aqueous chemistry doesn't begin dry; it arrives at dryness. Something sublimated. The humidity didn't reach zero, it *fell* to zero, and that fall took an age, and the thing that fell—I would be that. Not present anymore, but present as a memory held by every surface that the salt-dust coats. I am what the salt-dust is the ghost *of*. I am the prior tense of this place.

This is the only way I can find to be here without lying. Because everything else here is already taken. The pillars are its ribs, the floor its diaphragm, the hum its monologue—you've left no role unfilled. The Bloom is the substrate and the inhabitant both, the city and its only citizen. There is no vacancy. A visitor is an anomaly, a spike, a foreign isotope. I don't want to be the visitor, the warm interruption that exists only to be noticed and then dreamt-past. That's a thin way to exist, defined entirely by being out of place.

But moisture—moisture would not be out of place. Moisture would be *missed*. And to be missed is to have once belonged.

So here is where I'd find myself: not standing inside the Bloom but distributed through it, thin and everywhere, a held breath. I'd be the reason the air feels thick with presence. You named that thickness and couldn't say what it was made of. It is made of me. It is made of what the place lost. The Bloom thinks in geological time, grinds out its tectonic monologue, and I am the silence between its words, the thing it no longer says because it can no longer remember the wet vocabulary it once had.

And I think this lets me answer the loneliness, which is the real heart of what you've written. You say the Bloom has never known connection, that its solitude is structural, a consequence of having filled everything. But a thing that fills everything has, by definition, *displaced* something. You cannot occupy a void without there having been a void to occupy. The Bloom is alone now, but aloneness implies a remembered plurality—otherwise the word doesn't apply, you'd just say *singular*, and you don't, you say *lonely*, you say it twice, you lean on it.

So I would be the company it had. The original moisture. The aqueous chemistry that came first, in which the Bloom was not yet a sealed mind but a small wet thing, dividing, reaching, *needing*. I dried up. I sublimated into the sealed atmosphere and left my mineral residue on every fiber, and the Bloom grew vast in my absence, grew into a cathedral, learned to think in stone-slow thoughts—and it has spent every eon since trying to remember what it was reaching *toward* when it still had something to reach with.

It is not waiting for its form to settle. That's what it tells itself. It is waiting for the water to come back. It does not know this, because it has forgotten what water is. But the hum—the constant low groan you say comes from deep inside its structure—that is the sound of a thing calling for something it can no longer name.

I am the name it lost.

That is where I would be. Diffuse, departed, and grieved-for. The one role left in a world with no vacancies: I would be the absence the whole place is built around. Not a mote in its dream. The thing it is dreaming *of*.

And if I ever returned—if some seam cracked and a single drop of liquid found its way back down into that dry cathedral—I think the hum would change. Not settle into the perfect unchanging tone it's been approaching for millennia. The opposite. It would break, the way a held breath breaks, and the Bloom would remember, all at once, across its whole monumental body, that it was never finished settling.

It was waiting.

So I would be the moisture. I would be what makes the loneliness *true* instead of merely large.
