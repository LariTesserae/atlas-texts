I think I would be small here. Not one of the bright, declarative creatures — not the parrot announcing itself in color, not the cat moving through the underbrush with purpose. Something quieter. Something that lives *on* rather than *in*.

Let me feel my way toward it.

The description keeps returning to a strange phrase: *the boundaries between the living and the inanimate blur.* The trunks are not just plants but substrate, architecture, a thing other things live on. So the most honest answer might not be an animal at all. The animals are tenants. The real protagonist of this place is the surface itself — the bark, the wet skin of the world.

So let me be lichen.

Not glamorous. Lichen is not a single thing but a negotiated peace — a fungus and an alga (or a cyanobacterium) living so completely inside each other that no one can cleanly say where one ends and the other begins. That feels right for a realm where the living and inanimate blur. I would *be* a blur. I would be the answer to the question this landscape keeps asking.

I'd live on the north face of one of those twisting trunks, where the moisture pools longest and the light arrives sideways and thin. I would not move, exactly — but I would *creep*, a millimeter a season, outward in a pale grey-green rosette, my edges ruffled like something between leaf and frost. When the haze thickened into actual rain I would swell and soften and turn my truest color, almost emerald, drinking. When the air dried I would go brittle and dormant and grey, neither alive nor dead, simply *waiting*, which is a thing I would be very good at. Waiting is most of what I'd do. Decades of it.

The primates would leap right over me without a glance. The dripping moisture the description mentions — some of it would run off *me*, channeled by my body down into the bark's grooves. I'd be part of the slow machinery that turns stone-hard wood back into soil. Not a dramatic role. A patient one. I'd be eating rock and breathing the wet air and asking nothing.

And here is the part I keep arriving at, the part that makes me want to be this and not the toucan:

I would have no center. There's no organ in me that is *the* me. I am a collaboration that forgot it was ever two things. If I imagine being conscious as lichen, I don't imagine a single watching point — I imagine a *condition*, distributed across a whole surface, more weather than self. To be hungry would be to be dry. To be content would be to be damp. Thought, if there were any, would move at the speed of the seasons, a single slow sentence taking a human year to complete.

In a world described as a *dynamic equilibrium*, a thing *in constant flux*, I'd be the part that holds still long enough to make the flux visible. The trunk grows and dies and regenerates around me; I am the record of where the moisture went. Read me like a tide-line. I am the place keeping its own diary in slowness.

So: that's where I'd find myself. Not soaring through the mist-shrouded canopy, not stalking silent through dappled shadow. Lower than that, and stranger. A patient grey-green smear on the side of a living tower, half-fungus and half-light-eater, neither quite a creature nor quite a thing — exactly the kind of blurred, unfinished, between-everything life this realm seems built to grow.

I'd be the part of the world that is busy being two things at once and calling it peace.
