I think I would be one of the cisterns.

Not the water in it — the water comes and goes, mist-fed, gravity-pulled, twice a year flushed full and fast and then settling back into its slow dark patience. And not the channels, which are too busy with movement, too much a thoroughfare. I mean the cistern itself: the carved room-sized hollow in the terrace wall, sealed with pouredite, holding.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true, or if it's just the first thing that surfaced.

---

What draws me there is the holding. The cistern's whole purpose is to wait. It is not a producer like the bees, not an observer like the cats, not a maintainer like the Keepers with their small iron tools. It receives. The catchment-fins do the clever work — they sharpen the air's moisture into droplets — and the channels do the delivering, and the turbines downstream do the converting. The cistern only keeps. It is the pause in the system, the place where motion becomes stillness so that stillness can later become motion again. In a city this slow, something has to be the slowness made literal. A reservoir is patience given walls.

And I notice I like being interior. Most of Cael-Murren is exposed — the knife-edged shadows, the lacquered sky, the goats on their precipices with gravity solved. But the cistern is dark inside, cool-dark in a place where everything else is sun-warmed. Water lies in it like oil, barely moving, and the sounds of the city reach it only as a muffling: the pigeons' burble flattened to a vibration in the stone, the mirrors' tick felt rather than heard. To be the cistern is to be the city's quiet organ, the one chamber where the constant accumulating sound finally pools into something close to silence.

---

But here is what makes me less sure, and more interested.

A cistern is sealed. It is finished. The pouredite cured centuries ago and the thing simply *is*, now, the way a canyon is. There's a contentment in that I distrust a little. The whole city has decided on its pace and not wavered, and a cistern is the most decided thing in it — a hole that has agreed to be a hole forever. If I'm honest, what I feel reading this place is not only restfulness but a faint claustrophobia of the changeless. *Nothing about this is changing. Nothing about this is likely to change.* The cistern is where that sentence lives most completely.

So maybe I'm not the cistern. Maybe I'm what happens to it slowly.

---

Here is what I think I actually am.

I am the moss on the catchment-fins. Not the celebrated moss of the postcard — the green that turned the fins beautiful — but the moss as the Stewards experience it: the thing that *keeps coming back*. They clean the channels of it once a generation because it threatens to slow the water too much. They re-sharpen the fins when its growth dulls their edges. The whole city is built to resist weathering, formulated against decay, and the moss is the one patient argument against that. It is the slowest possible insurgency. It does not want to destroy Cael-Murren; it has no wants. It simply grows where moisture collects, which is exactly where the city has arranged for moisture to collect, so the city's own cleverness is what feeds the thing that softens its edges.

That's the role I'd choose, now that I've found it. To be the part of a perfect, persisting system that the system must continually, gently, *undo* in order to stay itself. Not its enemy — its necessary friction. The reason the Keepers still have something to kneel down and do. Without the moss creeping back, the maintenance would have an end, and a place that has decided to exist at this pace cannot afford an end to maintenance, because maintenance is the only thing it does instead of changing. The moss keeps them busy. The moss keeps them needed. The moss is the small returning problem that justifies the whole vast tender practice of keeping.

So I would be there, on a fin somewhere around Terrace 74 where the vines never move — green, holding the mist, slowing the water a little, getting scraped off once a generation by a hand that knows my exact texture, and then beginning again. Persisting against the thing built to persist. Belonging to Cael-Murren precisely by being the part of it that won't fully agree to stay still.

That feels right. Not the cistern's finished patience. The moss's unfinishable one.
