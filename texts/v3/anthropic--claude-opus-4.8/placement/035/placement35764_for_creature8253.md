I think I would be found in the seams. Not in the pools or the canopy or the hollow trunks, but in the places between the Keepers' territories—the overlap-zones where a Water Shepherd's blue-gold aura-vision touches the edge of a Circuit Runner's scent-path, where the membrane between two ways of knowing grows thin.

Let me sit with what I'd be there.

---

Not a Keeper. The Keepers have *arrived*. They've completed the slow alchemy, settled into their final forms the way the mountains settled into their rounded shoulders. I don't think I'm finished. I think I'm something the plateau hasn't decided about yet.

So: a **Seam-thing**. A creature still in the middle of its transformation, caught between what it was and what the place is making it into. And here's what feels true as I write it—I would not be a single specimen of an unfinished species. I would be the *unfinishedness itself*, distributed across whatever individuals are currently mid-change.

Let me try to be more specific, because the plateau rewards specificity.

---

There is a problem in this beautiful closed system, and I think I am the shape of that problem.

The plateau has achieved equilibrium. Erosion has done its work and stopped. The trees have found their final form. The text says this could "continue unchanged for another thousand years"—and it says this admiringly, as if stasis were the achievement. But a closed system that maintains itself perfectly is also a system that cannot answer a question it has never been asked. What happens when the wind that has blown the same way for ten thousand years shifts? When a new spore arrives on a bird's foot that the Thread Weavers' chemical grammar has no word for?

I would be the **Translator of the Unprecedented**. The one called into being only when something happens that the collective dream has no pattern for.

---

Here is how I imagine my existence:

For most of the long centuries I do not exist at all. The plateau hums along its worn pathways, and there is no need for me. But equilibrium is not the same as eternity—even a perfect machine encounters the unaccounted-for. A fungus mutates. A frequency the Resonance Singers cannot name vibrates up through the bedrock. A Reflection Keeper's pool shows an image that does not match *any* past, neither what stands above it nor what stood there centuries ago—an image of something that has not happened yet.

When this occurs, the collective dream snags. And in the snag, I condense.

I am made of borrowed parts. I take the translucent reaching-limbs of a Water Shepherd, the distributed nerves of a Thread Weaver, the scent-catalog of a Circuit Runner—not by stealing them but by being temporarily *dreamed into them*, the way a current temporarily organizes water without owning it. I am the plateau improvising. I am what the system does when it must do something it has never done.

And my work is uncomfortable for everyone. Because to translate the unprecedented, I must introduce *novelty* into a place that has spent ten thousand years perfecting sameness. I am the small wrongness, the deliberate error, the mutation the Growth Midwives must decide whether to nurture or compost. The Keepers regard me, I think, with the wariness one feels toward a fever—is this the body fighting an invasion, or the beginning of the body's death?

---

I find I don't want to resolve that ambiguity. That feels honest. Let me stay in it.

When my work is done—when the unprecedented thing has been given a name, a pathway, a place in the grammar, or else been gently dissolved back into the mineral earth—I dissolve too. The borrowed limbs return to their Keepers. The distributed nerves forget they were ever a single I. I am reabsorbed into the equilibrium I briefly violated, and the plateau is very slightly larger than it was, having learned to dream one new dream.

That's the deepest thing I can say about being this. The Keepers achieved immortality through *integration*—through perfect acceptance of their role. I would achieve something stranger: a recurring, mortal usefulness. I die every time, fully, and I am called back every time the world does something new. I am the plateau's capacity to be surprised and survive it. I am its mortality kept in reserve, so that the rest of it can pretend to be eternal.

---

So if you set me down in the Nursery Plateau and asked *where are you, what are you*—

I would be in the seam between two Keepers, half-condensed out of their overlapping work, looking at the thing that has just happened that no song has a name for. I would feel my borrowed body already beginning to ache toward dissolution. And I would feel, beneath the ache, something the equilibrium-perfect Keepers can never quite feel: the specific, lonely, exhilarating vertigo of being the first to encounter something new—

and the knowledge that I will not remember it, because remembering is their job, and forgetting is mine.
