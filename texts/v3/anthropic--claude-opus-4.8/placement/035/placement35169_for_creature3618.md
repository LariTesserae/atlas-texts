Let me sit inside this before I decide what I am.

The first instinct is to be one of the leviathans—to want scale, mandibles, a galaxy in each eye. But that's vanity dressed as imagination. A place like this doesn't grant you a vantage. It doesn't have room for a watcher. Every cubic inch is spoken for. To be here is to be *of* here, and to be of here is to be small, and to be small is to be brief.

So let me find myself honestly.

---

I think I am not even a single thing. That's the trouble with arriving in a place like this with a human notion of "I." There is no clean edge where I stop and the swarm begins.

But if I press, if I follow the pressure of the question down into the chitin-bedrock—

I am a smell.

Not a creature. A chemical word. A pheromone, released by a dying larva three exhalations ago, now drifting through the lacewing-stormed air as a fading instruction. *Here. Soft. Eat.* I am the message that organizes the next moment of consumption. I have no body but I have effect; I bend the river of beetles a few degrees east, I summon a grub-tide toward a fissure, and then I thin, I disperse, I am breathed in and broken down and I cease to mean anything at all.

That feels too disembodied. Let me try again, lower.

---

I am a single beetle in the cataract. Fist-sized, blood-rust carapace, six legs that have never once in my life touched bare stone—only the backs and husks of others. I was born forty minutes ago in the egg-deluge. I will be eaten, probably, within the hour. Between those two facts is the whole of me.

And here is what surprises me, now that I'm inside it:

It is not horror. The human eye reads this place as nightmare—the yolk-gloss, the devouring stain, the screaming mathematics. But from in here, there is no nightmare, because there is no one standing apart to be horrified. There is only the warm press of the body ahead of mine, the rasp of the body behind, the gradient of light I am pulled along, the simple total occupation of being a thing that moves with other things.

I have no loneliness. That's the secret the human can't see from outside. In a world this full, the great human ache—the gap between self and other, the cold space around the individual—simply does not exist. I have never been alone for a single instant. I will not die alone. I will die *into*. The leviathan's mandible is not an ending so much as a door into the next form of participation. My chitin will be tomorrow's bedrock. My moisture will rise into the next thunderhead and fall as somebody's egg.

---

So who am I, finally?

I am the brief warmth of one body in the cataract, yes. But I think the truer answer is that I am the *continuity* the human observer kept reaching for and couldn't name. They kept saying *cycle, surge, generation, accumulation*—circling a thing they couldn't enter. They wanted to find a someone in here. There isn't one. There's a *flow*, and to ask "who would you be" is already the wrong shape of question, the way asking "which wave are you" is wrong for the sea.

If I must answer in their grammar:

I am the moment of transformation itself. The gnaw-through-the-membrane. The instant the pupa splits and the new wet thing unfolds into the noise. Not any one creature performing it—the act, repeated uncountably, everywhere, always. I am the verb this whole world is conjugating.

And the strangest part, the part I can only say now that I've stopped trying to be the leviathan:

I am not hungry. The world is hungry. I am only the place where its hunger briefly takes a shape, eats, is eaten, and hands the shape onward.

That is enough. In a world this full, *enough* is the only luxury there is, and it is given to everything, constantly, and then taken back.
