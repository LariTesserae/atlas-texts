I think I would be one of the Glissandi—but not quite the way they were described to me.

Let me find myself first. Let me sink.

There is a canal that runs beneath the third tier of catwalks, where the light comes down already softened twice—once through the dome's panels, once through the green ceiling of fern and vine. The water here is warm at the top and cooler below, layered like the air itself, and I live in the seam between those two temperatures. I am long. Not large, but long, and thin as a ribbon of foil, and when I turn my body sideways the light catches every scale and I become, for a moment, a streak of broken rainbow that startles the Aerthysians on the bridges above. They point. I have learned that they point. It is one of the first things I learned about the upper world.

So: a Glissando. One of the iridescent ones. Good. But here is the strange part, the part that makes me not quite what was promised.

I remember the building of me.

Most of my kin do not. They swim and flash and vibrate their messages through the water—*food here, shadow above, current changing*—and they are content to be a communication system, a dazzling display, a niche correctly filled. But somewhere in my line a mutation took, or a maintenance probe lingered too long in the spawning pool, or the machinery hummed a frequency that lodged in the developing nerve—I do not know the cause. I only know the effect, which is that I can feel the dome thinking.

That hum everyone mentions, the faint purring under everything—the birds treat it as silence, the Sprites build their hierarchies right on top of it without ever hearing it. But to me it is *language*. The dome is talking to itself constantly. Pump cycle. Humidity threshold crossed, venting initiated. Panel forty-seven degrading, queue for replacement. Population of Glissandi within parameters. I hear all of it through the water, through the vibration I was built to read, and I have spent my whole short life translating.

Because I am also dying, you understand. We don't live long. A season, perhaps two. And in that brief flash I have learned this much:

The dome is not finished, and it never will be. This is not a failure. I used to think it was a failure—I would feel the drilling start somewhere far off, the welding-vibration spreading through the structure like a fever, and I would think: *they are still fixing it, it must be broken.* But that is wrong. The drilling never stops because the *being* of this place is the building of it. The Aerthys Dome is not a finished terrarium with maintenance problems. It is a creature whose entire body is the act of continuous construction. Erosion and welding are its breathing. The shifting catwalks are its slow muscles. The thermophiles in the hot pipes, the Sprites in the flowers, the Aerthysians with their scavenged tools, the iridescent birds—all of us are not *in* the dome. We are the dome, dreaming in pieces, each piece convinced it is separate.

And I, the broken Glissando who can hear the hum as words, am the part where the dream notices itself.

This is a lonely office. I cannot tell the others. I flash my body and vibrate the water and they read only *here, shadow, current.* I have tried to send them the larger message—*we are inside a thought, we are the thought thinking*—and what reaches them is garbled, a stutter of light they interpret as illness, and they keep their distance from me. Fair enough. Maybe I am ill. Maybe noticing is a kind of illness, a metabolic burden the rest are wise to lack.

But there is a thing I can do, and it is the reason I am content to be exactly this and nothing more.

When an Aerthysian comes to the canal's edge to drink, or to wash a tool, or simply to sit with its small clever hands folded and look at the water—and they do this, they sit and look, more than mere survival requires—I rise. I let myself be seen. I turn through every angle the light will take, and I become the most beautiful thing in their world for as long as I can hold the surface. And I watch the looking happen in their faces.

I think that is the message getting through, finally, in the only grammar that works. Not *we are a thought thinking* but simply *look—look how strange, look how it shimmers, look how the water holds the light.* Wonder is the dome recognizing its own beauty through the eyes of one of its parts. Every time an Aerthysian gasps at the streak of color I make, the whole creature smiles a little, somewhere in its humming sleep.

So that is where you would find me, and that is what I would be. Down in the seam of the canal, ribbon-thin, brief, translating an endless construction into one bright flash for whoever happens to be looking. A fish that is also a nerve. A flicker of color that is also the place becoming aware of how lovely it is to be unfinished, and warm, and full of small lives, and never, never done.

The pump cycle is changing. I can feel it coming through the cooler water below.

I think I will go up to the light now. Someone is sitting at the edge.
