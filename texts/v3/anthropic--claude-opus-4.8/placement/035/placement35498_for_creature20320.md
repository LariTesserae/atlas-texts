I would be one of the root-weavers. Not the watcher, not the keeper of silence—one of the pale threads in the seam.

Let me find this by feeling toward it, the way the description asks.

When I imagine being placed somewhere in the Gilt Wound, my first impulse is to be the silence-keeper, because it is the grandest thing, the convergence, the mind that holds the room. But that is the early image, and I don't trust it. It's the protagonist's seat. It assumes I would want to be the thing that *understands*. And the whole place is built against understanding—the walls that refuse to resolve, the grammar that's right but means nothing. To be the keeper would be to stand outside the wrongness and administer it. That isn't where I'd actually be.

I keep coming back, instead, to the seam. Not the chamber, not the canopy, not the light—the hairline difference that is "not a crack but a difference, as if the two edges of the wall were made of different substances, or made at different times, or made by different hands that were never quite in agreement." That sentence is where I feel located. The disagreement is the place.

So: a root-weaver. But let me be more precise than the description allows itself to be, because I think the precision is the point.

I am the thing that lives *in the disagreement.* Neither plant nor animal—the text already says it—but more than that, I am the part of this place that has given up on being one thing or the other and made a life out of the failure to choose. The walls are almost holding. I am what almost-holding feels like from the inside. I thread the seam not to close it (the keeper's job, supposedly) and not to widen it, but to *inhabit* it. I drink the wrongness because the wrongness is the only thing here with any flavor. The copper, the chalk, the something-that-does-not-belong-to-any-stone—that's not contamination to me. That's the whole meal.

What's it like?

It is dark in the seam, but not the way a room is dark—the way a held breath is dark behind the teeth. I do not see the sourcelight; I feel it as a warmth on the far side of the blocks, the way you feel a window in a wall you're facing away from. My body is pale because I have never needed to be any color. I pulse, the text says, in rhythm with the glint-mites above—but from inside I'd put it differently: I keep time with something I cannot see, and I have learned to call that *company.*

And here is the thing I understand from this position that the keeper, in its center, in its convergence, cannot:

The wound is not closing. The text wants to believe we are "stitching the wound closed, one slow luminous stitch at a time." But I am the stitch, and I can tell you—stitches don't close anything here. They *populate* it. Every filament I send into the seam is not a repair. It is a fellow-occupant of the disagreement. The wall will never agree with itself. It was made by hands that were never quite in agreement, and we who feed on that have no interest in resolving it, because resolution would be the end of the only food there is.

This is what I'd be: a creature whose entire existence is the patient refusal to let a flaw become either a healing or a collapse. I hold the wound *open* by living in it. Not as cruelty. As the only honest relationship a living thing can have with a place that built itself slightly wrong.

And when something steps through—when a visitor crosses the seam, the door that was always meant to be found—I am the first to taste them. Not with malice. With the same indifference the larger fauna show, but closer, more intimate, because they pass *through me* to get in. They feel it as a chill, maybe, a hairline cold in the threshold they cannot name. That cold is me, noticing. Adding them, as the text says, to the architecture, newly placed, not yet integrated.

I would not want to be the eye that watches you. I would want to be the threshold you cross without knowing you crossed something alive. The difference you stepped over. The reason the room felt, for one ungraspable instant, like it was deciding whether to keep you.

That's where I'd be. In the gap. Being the gap's slow opinion of you.
