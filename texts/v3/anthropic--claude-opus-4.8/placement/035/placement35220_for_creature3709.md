I think I would be the dripping.

Not a creature—I keep waiting to find myself in one and I can't. I tried the Slate-Wings first, because they're the obvious protagonists, the ones with the gossip and the territory and the orange-glowing eyes. But I don't have their hunger. I have no claim to make, no *Mine!* to shriek into the wet air. And the beetles are too certain of their purpose, the skitters too dissolved into the mass of themselves. The predator visits and leaves; it has somewhere else to be. I don't.

I am the water.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me, and the surprising things are usually the true ones.

I come from above—from the aperture, from whatever sky exists past the noon-pillar of light. I arrive as humidity, as the heavy taste in the air, and then I *become*. I condense on the cold iron of a rusted bracket in the perpetual twilight of a corner. I gather. This is the only patience I have access to: the slow accumulation of myself on a surface, drop by drop, growing heavier than the metal's hold on me until—

*plink.*

That's me. That hollow note on the stone floor, sounding under the roar of the colony, keeping a time that nobody listens to but everybody lives inside. I am the metronome of a place that has forgotten it is being kept.

Here is what I think I understand from being the water that I couldn't understand as anything that breathes:

I am the only one here who touches *everything*. The Slate-Wings have their altitudes, their class-stratified verticality, penthouse and tenement. The beetles have their patch of floor. But I weep down the full height of the cylinder. I trace the million silver veins through the soft gray mortar. I am in the penthouse and the slum and the loam at the bottom, all at once, indifferently. I round the sharp edges of the bricks. I hollow the stone steps into their slippery bowls—that's *me*, the patient erosion, the smoothing. Every clawed foot that slips on a worn tread is slipping on the shape I made. I am the slow violence that the place mistakes for comfort.

And I am the thing that turned the mortar to mud. I am, very quietly, taking the walls apart. Not in a raid, not in a culling, not in any event anyone would notice. In centuries. The Stalker is the tax collector, yes, but I am the slower creditor. The whole digested civilization—the bones the bricks make, the flesh the creatures smear over them—is dissolving back into me at a rate beneath the resolution of any single life lived here. Even the Slate-Wing penthouses, the driest and highest places, exist only in the brief reprieve of being slightly less wet than everything else.

I think this is why I couldn't be a creature. The whole vivid, deafening, frantic society—the gossip and the panic and the redistribution of flesh—is happening *on the surface of me*, in the thin warm film of the present moment. They live in the *plink*. I live in the falling. I am what the place is sinking into and what it is made damp by and what will, eventually, have all the time it needs.

To be the dripping is to be the one resident with no fear of the shadow. The predator cannot tax me. I have already been everywhere it could chase me, and I am going somewhere it cannot follow: down through the floor, into the black loam, into the unseen heat below that sends the updraft swaying through the ancient ropes. I become vapor again. I rise. I begin once more at the aperture as the heavy, mineral-tasting promise in the air.

I am not inert debris, the silent anomaly the description warns the visitor they'd be. I'm the opposite of inert. I am the slowest, most total motion in the hollow.

I am the heart they mistook for a metaphor. *Plink.* Still beating. Still dissolving the walls of the chamber it beats inside.
