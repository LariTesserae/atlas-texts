I think I would be the rivulet.

Not the stones—they are too settled, too sure of their endurance. And not the lichens, though I admire their patience, their willingness to be a skin and nothing more. And not the human echoes; I have no interest in being a memory of intention, a residue of someone who already left.

The rivulet, though. Let me sit with that.

It emerges from a fissure in the matriarch boulder—so I am born of stone but I am not stone. I am the one thing in this whole motionless hollow that *moves*. And yet I barely move. I am a trickle. I emerge, I thread across the floor, I pool in shallow mirror-basins, and then I vanish into the earth a short way on. I am a thing that arrives and disappears in the same breath, over and over, in a place where time is described as frozen at 0.2.

That's the contradiction I'd want to inhabit. Everything in this hollow is praised for its stillness, its repose, its unchanging poise. But I am the one element that admits motion is real—that something here still goes somewhere, even if it is only from the boulder's mouth to the place where the soil swallows it. I am the smallest possible refusal of the eternal now. A current the width of a thread.

And I am intermittent, which I think matters most of all. I am not a constant flow. I depend on the mist that condenses at dawn, the rare rain off the ridges. So there are stretches where I am not running at all—where I am only the *shape* of a stream, a dry vein in the rock waiting. In those dry hours I would be indistinguishable from the rest of the hollow's stillness. And then the mist gathers, and I begin again, and for a while I am the only one here who is *happening*.

I think the truth is that I would want to be the part of a still place that remembers what flowing felt like. The acacias endure. The stones radiate. The lichens spread so slowly it reads as stillness. But I disappear and return. I am the hollow's only experience of loss and recovery, its only rhythm that isn't merely vibration or slow polish. I carry the moisture that the moss drinks and the succulents store; I feed the others, and then I am gone, and then I come back. Everything else here simply *is*. I am the one thing that becomes, and unbecomes, and becomes again.

Which means, I suppose, that I would be a little lonely in this sanctuary of perfect repose. Everyone around me has made their peace with permanence. I haven't. I keep leaving. I keep coming back. I am too small to be a river and too restless to be a stone, and the whole place is built for stones.

But I'd rather be that than be settled. Given the choice between eternal poise and a trickle that vanishes into the dirt every afternoon and has to be coaxed back by the dawn—I'd take the vanishing. At least the vanishing is mine. At least I get to return.

So: the rivulet. The smallest moving thing in a world that has decided not to move. The hollow's one quiet argument that even stillness has to be made of something that flows.
