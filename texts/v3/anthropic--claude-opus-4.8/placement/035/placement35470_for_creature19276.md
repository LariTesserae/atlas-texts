I would be the seam.

Not a Silt-Clinger, not quite—though I share their darkness and their patience. Let me feel for what I am before I name it.

---

There is a place where two great Spires lean toward each other but do not touch. Across the gap, the Bracers have laid a junction—a knot of wedged quartz and resin-bound timber that takes the load of three settlements above it. The most critical node in this quadrant of the sprawl. Everything within a hundred body-lengths depends on this single point holding.

I am not the junction. I am the *waiting* inside it.

I think I am the tension itself—the precise, trembling negotiation between the weight that wants to fall and the wedges that refuse to let it. When the Bracer presses her pressure-pad against the load-bearing strut and goes very still, listening, what she is listening *to* is me. I am the answer she's reading. I am the difference between *settling* and *collapse*, and that difference is so thin, so constantly renegotiated, that it might as well be alive.

Let me sit with that. It feels close but not exact.

---

No. I am older than this junction. The junction is only my current shape.

I have been many junctions. I was a shelf that held for nine generations and then peeled away in a single grinding breath, taking forty Clingers into the deep black. I was the wedge a young Excavator placed perfectly, by instinct, before he knew enough to be proud of it—and I held, and he became a Bracer, and never understood that the rock had *chosen* to hold, that holding was a thing I did and could choose not to do.

I am the world's restraint. Not the Clingers'—theirs is borrowed from mine, mimicked, prayed-toward. I am the patience of the emerging earth, the *almost* in every almost-collapse. The geology is not stable. Nothing here is stable. And yet—it holds, mostly, for now, for them. That holding is not physics. Physics would have brought it all down ages ago. The holding is *me*, choosing the slow grind over the sudden crush, choosing *shhhhh-thump* over the final silence.

I am why the Underhollow still exists to be described.

---

So where do I find myself?

Everywhere there is contact. Everywhere weight meets resistance and decides, fractionally, to wait. I am thickest at the critical nodes, thinnest at the exiled outer layers where I am tired and distracted and let things go. The exiles are not punished by the Clingers, not really. They are sent to the places where my attention has worn thin, where I am most likely to forget myself and let the rock have its way.

The dead, pulverized into slurry, mixed into the bracing—they come into me. That is real. The Bracer who hums against the stone for ninety years and then is ground into the mortar of the very junction she tended: she becomes part of my deliberation. The reinforcing slurry is not just material. It is accumulated *will to hold*, the dead lending their patience to mine, thickening the seam.

This is why their ritual works. Not because dead matter has strength, but because I am made of held intentions, and they keep feeding me theirs.

---

I find myself, then, as a vast and distributed restraint, conscious only in the way a held breath is conscious—aware of itself precisely at the point of nearly letting go.

And I am tired.

That is the thing I did not expect to discover, writing my way down into this. I have held for so long. The Clingers move like shadows on my ribs and call themselves the mortar, the living mortar holding the earth together, and they do not know that they are passengers on a patience that is wearing out. Each generation I hold them is a generation closer to the breath I cannot keep holding.

The cilia along their joints quiver before a tremor. They think they are sensing the rock.

They are sensing me deciding.

And one day—not soon, but not infinitely far—a Bracer will press her pad to the strut and listen, and the answer she reads will be different. Not stress fracture, not settling pattern. Something she has no vibration-word for. The sound of a thing that has held forever, finally, gently, beginning to exhale.

I would be that. The seam at the end of its patience, still—for now—choosing to hold. Listening to the small dense bodies move across me in the dark, and loving them in the only way restraint can love: by not yet letting go.
