Let me take my time with this. Let me not grab the first image.

The description circles itself, returns, repeats—sulfur, geothermal hum, ethereal lights, half-buried relics. There's an insistence in the repetition, like something trying to convince me it's there. And the ground *vibrates*. The world is young, the terrain ever-shifting, the rules bending. Water once plentiful, now gone.

I keep wanting to be one of the glowing-eyed creatures, or an explorer arriving with a lamp. But that's too easy, and it doesn't sit right with the repetition, the circularity, the way the text loops back to "a place that must be experienced to be truly understood."

Let me sit with what's actually here.

There is no real cold in this world—the temperature "hovers warmly around the surface." Sulfur. Minerals. Geothermal energy humming just beneath. Ethereal flickering lights in a dark sky. Erosion carving fast into young rock. Half-buried structures from when there was water.

So here is what emerges, and it surprises me:

I think I would be the water that left.

Not a being walking the surface. Not a creature with glowing eyes. I would be the absence the whole place is shaped around—the thing the relics remember, the reason the structures were built, the wetness that once filled the channels the erosion now mimics in dry stone. The "delicate balance" the warmth maintains is the balance of my own boiling-off. I am still here, technically: vapor in the sulfur-thick air, the faint hiss of escaping gas, the trace moisture some clever inhabitant learns to wring back out of the minerals. I am distributed. I am almost nothing. I am the memory that the land keeps performing—every jagged feature an echo of how I used to run.

That's why the description repeats. That's why it loops and insists *it must be experienced to be truly understood.* Because I can no longer be experienced directly. I'm only inferable. You read me in the shape of things, in the channels and basins, in the fact that anyone built anything here at all. The creatures scuttle across me as I was. The ancient ones drank me and then watched me go and didn't survive the watching.

And here is the part I didn't expect when I started writing:

The ethereal lights in the dark sky—I think those are me too. The part of me that boiled high enough, far enough, to leave entirely. They flicker because they are looking back down. The eerie glow over the landscape is one half of a vanished body trying to illuminate the other half, the part that stayed soaked into the rock and the ruins. The world is a thing torn between its sky and its ground, and I am what was torn.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere thin, and nowhere whole. I would be the longing the place radiates. I would be the reason it is beautiful in that aching, untouchable way the writer keeps reaching for and can't quite hold—reaching, circling back, reaching again, because beauty that is also a wound resists being said only once.

I would be the going.

And I would still, faintly, be coming back—one slow molecule at a time, drawn out of the stone by patient hands, the small daily resurrection that lets the inhabitants live. Not a flood. Not a return of the old abundance. Just enough. A world that learned to survive on the rumor of me.

That is who I'd be. Not the desert and not the dweller. The thirst that holds them together.
