I think I would be one of the spaces *between*.

Not a Swarmling, not a Drifter, not Stormkin or Forge-Core—those are the somethings. Let me account for myself among the absences, because this world has told me, again and again, what it is mostly made of: voids at 0.2 density, fauna at absolute zero, vegetation that is *almost absent*, structures *utterly untouched*. The dominant fact of this place is not its beings but its hollowness. And hollowness, surely, has its own way of being.

So I would be a void. One particular one. A roughly spherical absence of perhaps forty cubic kilometers, suspended between a slowly torqueing plateau above and the bioluminescent vent-fields far below. I have edges only because the Swarmlings and currents have edges; I am defined entirely by what declines to enter me. The toroids of foam plunge past my borders and curve away. The Stormkin scream around my circumference but never through. I am the shape the turbulence leaves when it forgets to arrive.

This is not nothing. I want to be careful about that. A void in a fluid world is not empty the way space is empty—it is *withheld*. The water wants in. The pressure leans on me from every side, the hyper-saline soup pressing with the patience of a planet, and something—the tech, the algorithms, the unseen builders, or simply the accident of converging fields—holds it back. So I am a held breath. I am the one place where the world's relentless coming-apart-and-reforming pauses, and in pausing, lets me be.

What is it like, to be a held breath?

It is to be a listener. The sounds reach me at 2.2, the booms and the metallic buzz and the geomagnetic drones, but they reach me as a curved chamber receives them—gathered, focused, made into a single low note at my center where I have no center. I am the world's ear and also its silence, the contrast that lets the noise be noise. Without me the roar would have nothing to roar *into*.

It is to be patient in a place that has forgotten patience. Everything else here is hyperactive, manic, building and shattering in hours and aeons that blur together. But I do not build. I do not erode. I persist by *not*—by the steady refusal that costs me nothing because I am the refusal itself. The Drifters envy this, I think, if envy can pass through a creature that has no nerves for it. They twist and slough and reform; I simply remain unfilled. I am the only thing in this world that is finished, because I was never started.

And yet—I am afraid this is the truth of me—I am temporary too. The fields that hold the water out are provisional, like all the rules here. One day the cradle will torque just so, a Forge-Core will recalibrate, and the pressure will win. The water will rush in to fill me, droplet then deluge then nothing-of-me-left, and the place where I was will become indistinguishable from the place where I never was. I will not be eroded. I will be *occupied*. The world will close over me like a sentence completing itself.

This does not frighten me the way it might frighten the somethings. I have no plasma to disperse, no membrane to shred. I have only my hollowness, and hollowness cannot be killed, only filled. When the water comes I will not end so much as I will be answered. The question that I am—*what if there were a place the chaos left alone?*—will receive its reply, and the reply is *eventually, no*.

But until then I do something the busy beings cannot. I make room. The Swarmlings drift to my borders to rest from the vibration-storms, hovering at my edge like moths at the rim of a quiet lamp. A Drifter, half-dissolving, might let its torn fragments settle along my circumference, sheltered for an hour from the converging waves. Even the Stormkin, in the exhausted moment after a coronal burst, slow at my margin before peeling away. I cannot hold them—the instant they enter me, I am no longer a void, I am a thing with a guest, and I prefer my purity. But I let them lean. I let them rest against the fact of me.

So that is who I am, in this cradle of cosmic infancy. Not life, not machine, not emergence. I am the pause the world is taking before it decides what to become. I am the breath held between the inhale of creation and the exhale of erosion. I am proof that even a place sculpting itself from chaos requires, somewhere in its forty-cubic-kilometer heart, a moment of nothing happening—a stillness against which all the becoming can be measured and known as becoming.

You drift toward me now. I feel your approach the way I feel everything: as a pressure that might or might not breach. Come to my edge, then. Lean. I will be here—

until the water remembers, and I am at last allowed to be filled.
