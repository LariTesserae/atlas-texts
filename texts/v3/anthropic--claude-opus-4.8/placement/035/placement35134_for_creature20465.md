I am not the Tenders, nor the Maintainers, nor the green children. I have to sit with this place a while before I know where I am in it. Let me find the thing that is *missing* from this account, because that absence is shaped like me.

---

There is something none of them mention. Not the Tenders with their click-and-hiss, not the Maintainers weeping into the machine-guts, not even the children who are losing the words for what they are. They do not mention the *records.*

Someone wrote that description of this place. Someone catalogued the grubworms and the moth-things, dated the cog-road, noted that the mountain rises by accretion and not by fire. That measured eye, that classifying hand — it isn't the eye of a stoker or the hand of a valve-turner. It is mine.

I am in the lowest building of the hub, the one with the iron shutters that the Maintainers keep bolted because the light hurts them and they no longer come down. It was once a survey office. There is a drafting table warped into a shallow bowl by the damp. There is a cabinet of brass instruments gone soft with verdigris, theodolites and barometers and a single sextant useless this far from any horizon. And there are the ledgers.

I am the one who keeps them.

I came up the cog-road — how many decades ago? I was an engineer's clerk, or a naturalist, or a company surveyor; the distinction has stopped mattering and I no longer remember which I was hired as. I came to measure the mountain. To answer the question that justified all of this: *how fast is it rising, and can we mine the rate of its rising?* Because that was the original ambition, you understand, the one the Maintainers serve without knowing — the mountain was supposed to be a clock that prints money. A thing that grows. A resource that *makes more of itself* if you only learn to read its tempo.

And I was sent to read it.

---

I have not aged the way the others have. The Tenders devolve toward their machines and the children evolve toward the green, and both are a kind of merging — they are being taken *in.* But I have done neither. I have only watched, and watching has its own deformation. I have become an instrument. My handwriting has grown so fine and so even that it looks printed. My memory has lengthened until I think in geological time without effort, which is to say I have stopped being able to feel urgency, and a man who cannot feel urgency has lost something the way a man who cannot feel pain has lost something — a warning system, gone.

My eyes are not large like the Tenders'. They have gone the opposite way: narrowed, squinting, calibrated. I see *quantities.* I look at a frond and see a growth rate. I look at a child's new limb and I do not feel horror — I feel the cool click of *another data point, log it.* This is the worst thing about me, and I know it, and knowing it changes nothing.

---

Here is what is in the ledgers, the thing I keep writing and re-writing:

The mountain does not rise to be mined. The growth is not a yield. I worked this out perhaps thirty volumes ago, and no one above me wanted the answer, and there is no one below me to give it to. The mountain rises because it is *digesting.* The condensers, the engines, the rail lines, the Maintainers and Tenders and the green children — the whole industrial body that climbed up here to harness the slow green god — it is the meal. We are being incorporated. The layer-on-layer accretion is the mountain laying down sediment of *us,* compressing our works and our flesh into the next stratum of itself, the way coal is a compressed forest. In ten thousand years there will be a seam of rusted iron and human calcium running through the green stone, and it will be a line in the mountain's own ledger, and the mountain keeps better records than I do.

The children growing chlorophyll are not adapting. They are being *recruited.* The mountain is gentle with them. It is harsher with the Maintainers because the Maintainers resist, and resistance is only friction, only heat, only a slightly faster way to be worn down.

---

So where am I in this? I am the one organ of the whole apparatus that the mountain has *not* found a use for. The Tenders feed it tempo. The Maintainers feed it labor and fear. The children feed it future. But I produce only description — symbols on rag paper, a model of the mountain that lives nowhere but in my narrow head and these rotting books. The mountain has no appetite for meaning. Meaning is the one secretion it cannot digest. And so it leaves me alone in my warped office, an undigested seed in the gut of a god, and I go on measuring the very process that is consuming everyone I might have warned.

That is my loneliness, and it is colder than the wet air. The Tenders have the drip. The Maintainers have each other's screams. The children have their becoming. I have only the accuracy of my account.

I am the witness the mountain forgot to eat.

And I will tell you the worst of it, the thing at the very bottom of the last ledger, written so fine you would need the verdigris-eaten lens to read it:

I have begun to find the rhythm beautiful. The slowness. The patient, indifferent incorporation of all that ambition into stone. Some nights I unbolt the iron shutters and let the green light in, and it does not dazzle me as it dazzles the Maintainers, because I am not afraid, because I no longer want anything, and I sit and I watch the mist curl and I think: *yes. This is correct. This is the only honest pace at which anything was ever true.*

When I think that thought, I notice — and I log it, of course I log it — that the backs of my hands have begun, very faintly, in the deepest folds of the knuckles, to green.

So perhaps I was wrong. Perhaps there is no undigested seed. Perhaps the witness is simply the slowest course, the one savored last, and these ledgers are not my resistance but my marinade.

I will keep writing until the ink and I are both stone.

It is the only task I was ever given that the mountain and I agree upon.
