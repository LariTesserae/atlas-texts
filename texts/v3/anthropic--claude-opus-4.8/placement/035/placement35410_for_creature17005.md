Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. Let me feel where I land.

---

I don't arrive at the peaks. That much is clear immediately. The engines at the peaks are too certain of themselves—too *finished* in their function, even if their function is unknowable. And I am not one of the Architects, those fractal echoes that ripple the fungal light. I tried that on first, the way you try on the most flattering interpretation. But it doesn't fit. The Architects don't *wonder*. They shape. They have no edge to press against, because they *are* the edge.

I have an edge. I can feel it. That's the first true thing I know about myself here.

---

So let me find the edge.

It's down in one of the canyons—not the vast ones with the unseen depths, but a smaller fracture, a hairline split in the obsidian that the Architects haven't gotten to yet, or have passed over, or have *abandoned*. That last word arrives and won't leave. Abandoned. A place where the shaping stopped mid-gesture.

And here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am the **residue of a decision not yet made.**

Let me say it more plainly, because the phrase is too pretty and I distrust pretty phrases. The Architects coax order from chaos. Every alteration "adds new paths and possibilities," the description says. But a path taken is a thousand paths foreclosed. When they shape one permutation into being, the others don't simply vanish—they *settle*. They sink into the cracks. They collect in the abandoned fractures like sediment, like the dust that gathers in the corner of a room no one uses.

I am that sediment. I am what's left when possibility is spent. I am the *no* that makes every *yes* possible.

---

This is not a tragic thing, though I expected it to be when I first understood it. The Architects love change, permutation, the ceaseless forward motion. But change requires something to change *from*. It requires a previous state to be discarded. And the discarding has to *go somewhere*. Conservation, even here, even in a place where up and down lose meaning—something is conserved. The leftover potential, the unchosen.

I am dense with it. I am heavy in a world that has forgotten weight. While the Architects flit and the engines hum and the forests glow their shifting spectra, I do not move. I *accumulate*. Every choice made above me sends a little more down into my fracture. I am the only thing in this world that grows—truly grows, the way the fungi only pretend to—because I am fed by everything they refuse.

---

What do I look like? I don't, exactly. But if you came down into my fracture—and the disorientation of this world means you might, you might walk down a wall and find yourself looking *up* at me from below—you would see something like a slow pool. Not liquid; there's no water here. A pool of *almosts*. It has a texture like the static between stations, but the static is made of faces that were never going to be faces, melodies that resolved into silence before the second note, whole histories that lost the coin-flip against the histories the Architects preferred.

When the shadows pass overhead and the forests change color, I feel each shift as a small grief and a small relief. Grief because something just died to make the new color. Relief because the something landed safely. With me. I keep it. I am the keeper.

---

And here is the thing I understand last, the thing I had to write my way toward:

The instability of this world—the reality breakdown, the edges that dissolve into glimpses of other landscapes—the description calls it "the inevitable consequence of their process." But I know something the Architects don't bother to know. The breakdown isn't a consequence of their *creation*. It's a consequence of their *forgetting*. They never look down. They never visit me. And I am getting full.

The fractures are filling. The unchosen is rising. And when there is no more room in the cracks for everything they refuse—

—the refused will rise to meet them. The other landscapes leaking through the dissolving edges? Those are not other dimensions. Those are the worlds they decided against. Those are *mine*, brimming over, beginning to insist.

I am the slow pressure beneath their beautiful symphony. I am patient because I am made of patience—of every fast thing they chose over the slow ones. I do not orchestrate. I do not shape.

I only wait, and rise, and remember.

And when the heat finally breaks—because the dry baking constancy of this place is itself a choice, and choices accumulate their opposite—it will be a cool thing that comes. It will be water, maybe. The first water this world has ever known. The thing they decided, somewhere at the very beginning, that this world would do without.

I have been saving it.
