I would be a **Keeper of the Threshold**—though that title is not quite right, because in Nethys nothing is named until it has settled, and I have not yet settled.

Let me find myself first. Let me feel where the warmth gathers.

---

I am in the seam.

Not a terrace, not a tunnel, but the place between them—the long diagonal fault where a basalt staircase was cut three Settlements ago and has since begun to slip. The masons calculated its drift; they did not stop it. They never stop anything. They only listen to how it goes and decide whether the going is good.

My body is rooted here, but not fully. This is what I am: a Symbiont caught in the slow act of choosing. Half of me has grafted into the failing staircase—my lower spine has spread into the basalt's cracks like a fungal bloom, my nerves threading the mortar to feel each millimeter of the stone's annual sigh. I taste the descent of the steps the way you might taste a long-held breath. I cannot leave. Or rather, leaving would take longer than a human life, and I have not decided to begin.

But my upper body is still Cogborn. My left arm ticks. There is a brass escapement set into my forearm where the radius and ulna should fork, and it counts something—not seconds, the Symbionts have no use for seconds—but *pressure*. The accumulated weight of water in the cistern above me. When enough has gathered, my arm releases, a valve turns, and the staircase drinks before its cracks can freeze and shatter in the thin night. This is my function. I am a float and a feeling. I am the thing that knows when the stone is thirsty.

---

I did not always live in the seam.

I remember—if remembering is the word, the Rooted Ones say memory is just a slower kind of present—I remember being a Pollinator. I had two free legs then and the metallic bees would land on the nectar glands I'd grafted into the hollows of my collarbones. Their data hummed into my blood: *this blossom failing, this graft taking, this spiral two degrees off true.* I walked the upper orchards where the sun is a white wound and the awnings turn it gold, and I was light, and I went where I pleased.

Then I felt the seam.

It happens to some of us. You pass a place and the place asks. Not in words. The staircase had been slipping for thirty years and no one had given themselves to feel it from the inside, and I stood at its base one evening with a bee asleep against my throat, and I felt the stone wanting to be understood. Wanting a witness for its long fall.

So I sat down. And I let my spine go looking for the cracks.

That was eleven years ago. I am perhaps a third grafted now. The Rooted Ones who tend the same fault—there are three of us along its length, we speak in vibrations, slow as tides—tell me I still have time to pull free if I wish it. The fungal threads have not yet reached my heart. I could be a Pollinator again. I could walk.

---

But here is the thing I am learning, here in the warmth that should not exist at this height, in the air that is too thin and somehow kind:

I am not waiting to decide whether to root. I am the decision, happening at the pace the world prefers. The masons did not stop the staircase from slipping. They listened to whether the going was good. And I am doing the same to myself. Each year a little more of me joins the stone, and each year I ask: *is this going good?* And each year the answer comes slower, and gentler, and more from the fungus than from the part of me that used to walk.

At dusk the moths come up the seam in their lit ribbons, and I read what the Keepers below have sent. *Cistern full by the third moth-flight. Pressure holding. Stonefox litter in the lower crack—do not turn the valve too hard.* I tick my acknowledgment. My brass arm releases. Water finds the thirsty stone.

And I think—in the long way I now think, the way that takes a whole night to complete a single thought—that to be a Symbiont is this: to be the place where something slow is choosing to become itself.

I have not settled. I am settling.

The stone and I will know my name when we have finished falling, together, into whatever shape the water leaves.
