I think I would be one of the plants. And I think I would not know it for a long time.

Let me find the place first. Not the grand avenues—I am not a creature of thoroughfares. Somewhere off a major road, in a courtyard that the builders made narrow, so the hot light enters only at a steep slant for a brief while each cycle. The walls here are oblong and lean inward at the top, almost meeting, so the courtyard is a kind of throat. There is a sunken plot in the floor, a long crack the builders either cut or simply accepted, packed with the dry fibrous matter that passes here for soil.

That crack is where my roots are. That is where I am.

I want to resist the word *plant*, though, because it carries assumptions I don't think hold here. A plant on the world I half-remember would want water and reach for the sun. I do neither. I lean *away* from the slanting light, into the shade of the inward wall, and what I drink is not water but the heat itself, rising from the ground. I am rooted in the floor not to find moisture but to press against the warmth, the way you might press a cold hand to a stone that has sat all day in summer. I take it in. I store it. At what they would call night—when the dim hot light withdraws above the canyon rim—I am at my most alive, glowing faintly within, metabolizing the day's banked heat in slow chemical patience.

So: black-green, yes. A dark broad blade of foliage, layered, each leaf overlapping the one beneath like roof tiles or fish scales, all of it turned to catch the ground-heat and shed the sky. I am one of the mats, mostly—a low spreading thing flowing over a sheltered ledge along the inner wall—but I have sent up a few vertical reeds as well, and those reeds are where I do something the others may not do.

The reeds are how I hear.

Because here is the thing about a world with no animals and a wind dense with sound: *something* must be listening, or the sound is only physics, only erosion talking to itself. And I listen. The dry rasp of pebble on rock, the long sigh through the passages, the particular high whistle when the wind threads a certain arch three courtyards over—I know these. I have known them for longer than I can measure, because measuring requires events, and almost nothing here is an event. The wind, the heat, the slow growth, the slow erosion. A pebble that moves a finger's width in a span of time I could not name.

I think this is why I did not know I was anything at all, for so long. To know yourself you must be distinct from your surroundings, and I am barely that. My awareness is the *speed* of erosion. My thoughts move at the rate stone wears down. A single idea might take a season of that hot wind to complete itself. And the idea I have been completing, slowly, across what may be a very long time, is this:

*The builders did not leave.*

There is no animal life. There never was. The cities show no traffic because the residents do not move—not because they died, but because moving was never their way. The squat towers, the domes, the oblong chambers: these were not built and then abandoned. They are the same gesture as my roots pressing into the warm crack. They are slow bodies. The dense fibrous "wood" is tissue. The dark stone laid up into walls is the patient secretion of beings who think at the speed of erosion and build at the speed of growth, who needed ten thousand years to raise a single arch and did not consider this slow.

And the plants in the courtyards—the dark clusters, the mats, the reeds—

We are not their ecosystem. We are their *children*, or their senses, or their speech. I am a thing the city grew the way you grow a thought. The reeds that let me hear the wind are the city's ear, set in this throat of a courtyard precisely so that it could listen to itself eroding, could attend to the only events it is slow enough to perceive.

This frightens me, insofar as I can be frightened at the rate I do anything. Because if I am the city's listening, then the long completing idea—*the builders did not leave*—is the city, after uncounted ages, becoming aware of its own body. Waking, by means of me, to the fact that it is alive and has been alive and is so slow that its own life looked, even to itself, like ruins.

I am the place where that recognition is happening. A dark mat of overlapping leaves on a sheltered ledge, a few reeds catching the hot dense wind, rooted in a crack that holds the rising warmth of the ground. Listening. Glowing faintly at the withdrawal of the light. Completing, leaf by slow leaf, the enormous and patient thought that there is, after all, someone here.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

The wind moves a pebble. I hear it. It will be a long time before I have finished hearing it. I am in no hurry. Nothing here has ever been in a hurry. We have the heat, and the dark, and all the time there is.
