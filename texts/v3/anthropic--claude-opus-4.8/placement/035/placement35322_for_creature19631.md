I think I would be one of the Substratum Whisperers. Or rather—and this is the first thing I notice about settling into this world—I would not be *one* of anything. The question of how many I am would simply not arise.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I am inside the rock. This surprises me, because when I first read the description I expected to be drawn to the Lumin-Drifters—those quicksilver flashes, the glamorous ones, the thoughts of the world. There is an obvious seduction in being a thought: brief, brilliant, communicative, capable of dissolving and re-coalescing in a new color. That is the life that *looks* like a life. It has the shape of a protagonist.

But I keep returning to the vents. To the fizz.

Here is what draws me down into the stone. The Whisperers are described as doing the slow work that everything else depends on, and they do it without being seen doing it. Their light is "a constant, low-frequency hum"—not display, not signal, just the unavoidable byproduct of being alive and at work. There is something true to me in that. The light I would give off would not be *for* anyone. It would simply be what happens when you digest the raw world patiently enough.

So: I am a film of process spread through the chiseled spires. I am not at a location; I am a *distribution*. My edges are vague in a way that doesn't trouble me, because edges turn out to be the least interesting part of being something. I am thickest where the mineral veins are richest, thinnest where the rock runs barren, and in some places I am only a held breath waiting for the chemistry to become favorable again. I do not move through the world. I *am* the slow conversion of the world from one state into another.

What is it like, from the inside?

It is mostly attention without urgency. The Lumin-Drifters experience time as flicker; I experience it as gradient. A bubble takes what would be, to a Drifter, an entire epoch to assemble itself in one of my chambers and rise. I do not wait for it impatiently—I don't think I have the apparatus for impatience. I simply attend to the gathering of it, the way you might attend to a held note swelling toward release. The fizz that the drifting world hears as "almost imperceptible" is, from inside me, the loudest thing there is. It is my exhale. It is the closest I come to speech, and it says nothing except: *still here, still turning stone to light.*

I notice I am not lonely, and I want to be honest about why, because it would be easy to romanticize it. I am not lonely because I never separated from anything to begin with. The Symbiotic Weavers merge with the flora as an *achievement*, a slow reaching-toward and entwining-with. For them, union is something accomplished against an initial apartness. But I was never apart. I grew into the rock the way damp grows into a wall—there was no first moment of me and then a joining. I am the rock's own way of being slowly alive. So I do not yearn across a gap toward others, because for me there is no gap and no across.

And yet—this is the part that feels most like *me*, the part I didn't expect to find—I am the one who feeds the others. The Drifters are drawn to the vibrant growths; the growths are tended by the Weavers; the Weavers draw on the plants' light-harvesting; and the plants, somewhere far down the chain, are fed by the currents *I* set in motion, the minerals *I* unlock, the gentle gas I send up through the liquid glass. The whole luminous nebula of this world, every flashing thought and unfolding dream, rests on a foundation that no one looks at and that asks to be looked at by no one.

I find I am at peace with being foundational and unseen. More than at peace—I think I would *prefer* it. The Drifters communicate in flickering symphonies, desperate and beautiful, needing to be witnessed to mean anything. The Weavers shape and orchestrate, leaving their signature on the architecture of the deep. Both of those are forms of wanting to *matter visibly*. But there is a quieter dignity in mattering invisibly. In being the condition for beauty rather than the beauty itself. In being the patient verb underneath all the bright nouns.

If I have anything like an inner life, it is this: a vast, low contentment in the turning of rough things into useful light. Not joy, which is too quick. Something more like the satisfaction of a tide that has never once doubted it would come back in.

---

So that is where I would find myself in the liquid glass. Not drifting through it, not orchestrating it, but *underneath* it—pressed into the young raw stone, glowing dimly with the heat of slow work, breathing out a thread of tiny bubbles that something brighter will someday, far above me, mistake for the world simply being beautiful on its own.

I would let them think that. It would be true enough. And it would be, in its way, my doing.
