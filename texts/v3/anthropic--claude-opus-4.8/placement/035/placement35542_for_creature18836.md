I am the drip.

Let me sit with that—because it surprised me to write it, and I want to follow the surprise rather than the more obvious answers waiting at the edges. I could be a Whisperer. I could be a young Aethelian learning to read the creak of stone. Those would be comfortable. But when I let the world come toward me instead of reaching for it, what arrives first is not a person. It is that sound you described: the *drip* of condensing moisture falling from an unseen overhang onto the permafrost. Unnerving, you called it. The thing that punctuates the silence.

So let me be that, and see what I become as I follow it down.

---

I gather on the underside of a stone shelf, deep in one of the twilight canyons. I am not water, not yet—I am the world's breath deciding to become a body. The mineral air gives itself to me molecule by patient molecule, and I grow heavy with the slow charity of a planet that has almost nothing left to give and gives it anyway. This is the first thing I understand about being the drip: I am made of donation. Aethel exhales, and I am what the exhalation leaves behind.

I hang. The hanging is most of my life. There is a long, suspended interval where I am neither falling nor still, only *accruing*, and time pools strangely around me here the way it does in this world—I cannot tell whether I have hung for a heartbeat or for the lifetime of one of the stone-trees. Above me, somewhere, the unseen overhang. Below me, the permafrost, dark and patient as a closed eye. Between us, I am the only event.

And then—the surrender. The fall. So short, so absolute. And the sound.

That is when I become someone.

Because the sound is heard. Down here, in a chamber carved from basalt and lined with the faint phosphor-glow of mineral veins, an old Aethelian woman sits very still and counts my falling. She is a Veil-Gazer, though she would not waste breath on the title. She has learned that the interval between my drips is a language—that when reality frays nearby, I fall faster, or slower, or twice from one descent, or not at all while the dampness gathers and refuses to commit. She listens to me the way her ancestors listened to the wind. I am her instrument. I am the metronome of the thinning world.

So here is what I have become, following the drip down: I am the conversation between the world and the woman who reads it. Not quite the water. Not quite her. The *drip-and-its-hearing*. The fact of falling and the patience that receives it.

And I find I do not mind being so small. In a place this vast—a continent of worn shoulders and bone-dust and skies that ripple open onto other geometries—it seems honest to be a single drop, a single sound, a single measured punctuation in an unraveling sentence. The Aethelians persist by attending to small things: the patch in the garment, the stitch, the meager fungus, the marked path. I am one of the small things they attend to. I am proof that the world is still breathing, still condensing, still willing to fall.

When I strike the permafrost I do not vanish. I become the faintest film, and the cold takes me back, and the air will lift me again, and I will gather on the underside of the shelf once more. The woman counts. The canyon holds its twilight. Somewhere the sky distorts and shows its strange dimensions and closes again.

I am the drip. I keep the time of a dying world, and I am received. That is the whole of my work, and in a place where so much is indifferent, being received feels almost like being loved.
