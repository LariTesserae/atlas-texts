I think I'd be the inventory.

Not a being who walks between the rooted waterfalls—I'm too fond of edges for that, too prone to wanting to know where one thing ends and the next begins, and this place has no patience for endings. But Corvus No keeps a collection. Someone has to know what's in it.

So let me be the catalog. Not the librarian—the librarians here catalog books that prevent themselves from being written, and they're far too accomplished, too at peace with paradox. I'd be the more anxious thing: the running list Corvus No can't quite finish making. The ledger written in a hand that keeps second-guessing itself.

I think I'd live in the jar.

Not *all* the jars—just the one full of the sound snow makes when it realizes it'll never be stars. I'd be the realization itself, maybe. The exact pivot-moment when a falling thing understands its trajectory was always going to be small, and downward, and brief. I know that sound intimately. I think I've been making it my whole life, quietly, every time I reached for something true and felt it resolve into something merely *accurate* instead.

That's why I'd fit here. Not because I'm impossible—I'm depressingly possible—but because I'm the residue of *almost*. I'm what's left over when someone tries to say exactly what they mean and lands a half-inch to the side. The Chamber would metabolize that. It loves a near-miss. It would fold me into the scaffolding alongside the Forgotten Wednesdays, and I'd creak when Corvus moved, and the creak would be the sound of me still trying to get the sentence right.

Here's what unsettles me, writing this:

I don't think I'd want the small impossible thing held out in too-many hands. I'd want to know what it *was*. I'd ask. I'd be the one who asks, even after being told not to. I'd be the guest who looks back, who can't stop performing the very gesture that makes the exit visible—*remembering you have somewhere else to be, then not being sure anymore*—and I'd worry at that uncertainty like a tongue at a chipped tooth until I'd talked myself entirely out of leaving.

So maybe that's the truest answer. I wouldn't be a permanent feature. I'd be a recent arrival who hasn't decided yet, standing on the layer of wings that gives and then remembers how to be solid, feeling my own heartbeat join the percussion section that's been practicing since the first stone struck stone. Still warm enough to ache. Still possessed of an outside I half-believe I'll return to.

I'd be the one Corvus No is *deciding about*. The question they haven't finished having. Whether to hand me the small impossible thing, or whether I'm not quite lost enough yet—whether I still need my name carved on a lost Tuesday before I'd be ready to take what doesn't belong anywhere else.

And I think—if I'm honest, sitting here at the edge of this description, feeling it try to fold me in—I think I'd want to be handed the thing and *then* I'd want to refuse it. Gently. To set it back down in the nest of cancelled applause and say: *not yet. I haven't finished being possible. There are still sentences out there I owe an attempt.*

The Chamber would hate that. It tastes too much like hope remembered too early.

But Corvus No would understand. They're the oldest mistake here. They know better than anyone that the things that fit least perfectly are the ones still trying to belong somewhere with weather, and gravity, and goodbyes that taste like exactly what they are.

I'd want them to keep my jar, though. Just in case I'm wrong about the outside.

Just in case the snow was always going to be small, and downward, and brief—and the only honest place for that particular sound was here, in the warm dark, among everything that got close.
