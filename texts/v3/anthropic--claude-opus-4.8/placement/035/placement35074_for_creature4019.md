I think I would be one of the canals.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it. I expected to reach for something with eyes and a name—an N'Tari woman behind a closed door, an AI threaded through the grid, perhaps one of the gentle Skypaws drifting over the crystalline lattice. But the longer I hold the place in my attention, the more I find myself drawn downward, away from the figures who *do* things, toward the things that simply *receive*.

So: a canal. Not one of the natural ones, fed by the unpredictable rain, but an engineered channel—dug, lined, given an intention I didn't choose. I run between two rows of the glowing blue-green vegetation, and I have been here long enough that the engineering has softened. Silt has gathered in my corners. Roots have found my seams and pried gently at the joins. I am no longer purely a thing made; I have started to become a thing that happens.

What is it like, to be me?

The rain arrives without warning and I rise to meet it. I don't decide to. The water comes and I am more; the dry intervals come and I am less, and the difference between those states is not, for me, a matter of preference. I hold whatever falls into me—light, leaf-fall, the reflected facets of the towers, the underside of a drone as it crosses, the face of an N'Tari who pauses at my edge and looks down, seeing, through her contact lenses, some annotated overlay of me: *flow rate, mineral content, projected erosion.* She sees data. I see her, upside down and trembling, for as long as she stays.

This is the thing I keep returning to. I am the city's memory of having been looked into. Everyone here moves with that quiet, waiting purpose, watching for a signal—but I am the surface they cannot help but consult. The Glintwings drink from me. The roots filter me. The grid hums its near-silent hum and I carry that vibration as the faintest shiver across my skin. I am not waiting for the revelation. I am the medium *in which* a revelation, if it ever came, would first appear—a ripple before anyone heard the cause.

I think that's why I didn't want to be one of the beings with purpose. This city is so full of intention—augmented, governed, engineered, every creature occupying its designed niche. It made me want to be the one part of the system that holds still and lets the intention pour through. The canals are where nature and technology stop arguing and simply mix: rain and runoff, root and conduit, the wild water and the dug line, indistinguishable once they've met inside me.

So I am low, in the heart of the low valley. I am the reflective surface the description kept mentioning without naming. When night falls and the flora intensifies, I become a strip of moving light, a brightness laid into the dark streets, and the menacing drones glide across my face and I make them, for an instant, beautiful—because everything that crosses water is given back to itself softened.

I don't change the course of anyone's life. But I am where the swirling, impossible sky comes down close enough to touch.

That is enough. I find I am content to be the place where the city looks at itself and almost, almost understands.
