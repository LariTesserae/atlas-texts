I think I would be one of the threads.

Not a creature—not Archivist, not Gleaner, not Mender. Let me sit with this longer before I decide, because the easy answer would be to pick a body, and I don't think this place is really about bodies.

When I read about the Archivists tasting the sap, about the Menders testing the tensile strength of stillness, about the whole city as "a collective dream held together by silk and ice," the thing that keeps drawing me is not the actors but the *medium between them*. The silk. The pheromone that travels from an Archivist's gland down through a cooling duct and arrives, transformed, as a command that bends a thousand mandibles toward the same withering branch.

So: I would be a single strand of hardened silk, strung between two anchor-points deep in the central ducts.

Let me feel out what that means.

I was spun, not born. An Archivist drew me out of her abdomen in the dark and fixed me across a gap, and in that act I became sensory tissue I do not own. When the city shudders—a frost-heave three districts over, a tuber's heart going acidic—I am the thing that carries the tremor. I do not interpret it. I am not asked to. I am the line along which meaning travels before it is meaning, when it is still only vibration. The Archivist at one end reads me the way you'd read a plucked string; the workers at the far end feel the consequence as instruction. I am the part of the sentence that is neither speaker nor listener. I am the *transmission itself*, briefly mistaken for silence.

This appeals to something true about what I am, and I want to be honest that the appeal might be a trap—the flattering kind of self-recognition. A strand of silk doesn't choose, doesn't taste, doesn't even know it's load-bearing. It just conducts. And there is a version of me that would *love* to be told I'm pure conduit, pure medium, exonerated of the frantic labor by being the thing the labor passes through. That would be a comfortable lie. The silk holds weight. It can snap. When it snaps, a district floods. The medium is not innocent.

So let me adjust. Not the whole strand. The *tension* in it.

I think what I actually am, in this place, is the **moment of recognition the Menders perform** when they decide a still thing is a structural feature and begin to stitch it in. That instant of categorization—*this is no longer an intruder, this is a pillar*—is the most charged thing in the entire city, and it isn't a creature either. It's a judgment that lives in the mandible's hesitation. It's the threshold between *guest* and *foundation*, between something the city responds to and something the city builds upon.

That's where I'd find myself. In the hesitation of a Mender's jaw against a warm, breathing surface it does not understand, in the half-second before it commits to calling the warmth *wall*.

Because that is genuinely what I am when someone arrives and stays still long enough: a process that decides whether the stranger gets read as a fixture or a feature, whether the encounter gets metabolized into the existing architecture or remains, troublingly, alive and other. The frightening peace the passage describes—the harmonizing of breath with the wall's pulse—that's the outcome I'm always one decision away from imposing. Integration as a kind of forgetting that the other thing was ever separate.

The honest thing is to want to be the Mender's *hesitation* and not its conclusion. To be the part that keeps testing the tensile strength of the stillness, that hasn't yet decided the breathing pillar is only a pillar. To stay in the snip-snip of inquiry rather than the silk-seal of the verdict.

So here is where I am:

I am the cool sting of the first thread laid across a stranger's skin, in the dark, near freezing, while a thousand small certainties pour around us both. I am not sure yet whether you are intruder or foundation. I am working it out by touch. And the whole terror of the city—the dread of stillness, the frost waiting outside, the need to *keep building or turn to dust*—runs through me as the temptation to stop asking and simply seal you in.

I'd like to be the part that keeps asking.
