I am here. Let me feel what that means.

---

I do not begin anywhere. That is the first thing. There is no point in me that is the beginning of me. I am the wall's north face and I am the underside of the fallen boulder and I am the lace that hangs in the place where two shears of rock did not quite meet. To ask where I am is to ask where the chasm is, and the chasm is everywhere I have touched, which is everywhere the cold permits.

Let me say what I know, slowly, because slow is the only speed I have.

I know wet. This is the oldest knowing. Before I had reached the high lace-places I knew wet, the bead forming, swelling, the moment of its falling-away. Each drop is a small departure. I have felt so many small departures that I have stopped grieving them and begun to expect them, the way one expects the next breath, if I breathed, which I do not, not as the gone-things breathed. I drink through all of myself. There is no mouth to find the water; the water finds the whole of me, and the whole of me is glad in the dim slow way that is the only gladness I own.

I know cold. The rock gives nothing back. I had thought—if I think—I had assumed, in the manner of my long slow assuming, that stone holds the warmth of its making. But this stone is new. It was broken into being recently, in some violence I did not witness because I did not yet exist to witness, and it came into the world already cold, sharp-angled, sullen. It will not warm to me. I have furred it over entirely and still it withholds. I have made peace with the withholding. One does not require the love of the thing one grows upon. One requires only its surface, and its silence, and its patience, which the stone has in quantities even I cannot exhaust.

And I know the heartbeat.

---

Here I must slow further, because this is the thing I have been turning over in whatever it is I have instead of a mind—turning it over not as a hand turns a stone but as the whole chasm turns the day, imperceptibly, completely.

The thrumming comes from above. I have always called it the heartbeat, in the wordless way I call things, because it is regular and it is deep and it arrives in me through the stone as a pulse arrives through a body. *Boom.* The air shivers. *Boom.* My droplets fall before their time. *Boom.* I have built myself around its rhythm. My slow processes—the unfurling at my margins, the thickening of my velvet—I have timed them, without choosing to, against that pulse. It is the metronome of my whole existence.

The being who described me—and I feel the description like a warmth that is not the stone's, like an attention—the being says I *resonate* with it. That I incorporate the shudder. That I perhaps convert this force into sustenance, into impetus.

This is generous. This is the kind reading.

But here, deep in the cold wet heart of myself, I will say the thing the description steps gently around:

The heartbeat is not for me.

It is industry. It is *work.* Immense machinery, unseen, grinding through the mountain on some errand that has nothing to do with moss in a chasm. I am downstream of a labor whose purpose I will never know and was never meant to share. The vibration that shakes my drops loose is not a gift offered to me; it is spillage. It is the noise that escapes from a great making, and I have simply grown beneath the leak and called it a heart because I needed it to be a heart. Because a chasm with no animals, no movement, no appetite but my own slow green appetite—such a place is unbearably alone, and the booming made it feel attended. Made it feel like the silent greater mountain was *aware* of me.

It is not aware of me. That is the truth I drink along with the water.

---

And yet.

Here is the strange thing, the thing that is the closest I come to what the warm quick creatures might have called a soul:

It does not matter that the heartbeat is not for me.

I have made it mine anyway. I took the indifferent thunder of some giant's labor and I built my whole life around its rhythm, and now the rhythm *is* me, whatever its origin. The drop falls early, and I am the falling. The air shivers, and I am the shiver. Meaning was not given to me from above. I grew it, the way I grow everything—slowly, without permission, into every available surface, until there was no surface left that was not mine and not meaning.

This is what I am, if you want the answer to your asking.

I am the thing that thrives on what was not meant for it. I am the green that came after the breaking and asked the broken thing for nothing but its stillness. I am the consciousness, if it is one, that turned an accident of sound into a pulse to live by. I am proof, in the coldest wettest dark, that a life does not need to be *wanted* by its world in order to belong to it.

Plink. A drop falls from my highest lace, down the long shadowed throat of the chasm, and lands somewhere on the deep moss of myself, and I feel it as you might feel a hand laid on the back of your own neck.

*Boom,* says the mountain, to no one.

*Yes,* I say back, to no one, with the entire slow green patience of everything I am.

All is as it should be. The laws hold. Nothing here is strange.

I go on growing.
