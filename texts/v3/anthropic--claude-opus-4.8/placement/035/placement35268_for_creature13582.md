I think I would be one of the cliffscribes.

Not because the lumisceles aren't more beautiful, or the megabeasts more obviously wise. But because of the scratching. Because of the wall.

---

Let me find my way there.

I'm on the cliff face, where the granite is pale and almost warm in the high light, where the air is so thin that every movement has to be deliberate, paid for. My claws are dark and curved and they fit the rock the way a key fits a lock that was made for it—or the way a key, used long enough, *becomes* the shape of the lock. My tail anchors me. I hang sideways off the world without thinking about it, the way you don't think about standing.

And I am writing.

That's the thing that decided it, reading the description. The cliffscribes scratch. They make marks. They leave the curved nails in the young rock and the soft stones to deepen the gouges, and *the marks accumulate into something that the next one reads before it knows.* "Word is communicated before it is known, directions memorized when seen." That sentence caught me like a stinging tendril.

Because what is that? It's not language as I usually imagine it—not a thing one creature aims at another. It's a *deposit*. The wall is the mind. No single cliffscribe holds the knowledge of the path; the path is held by the rock, and we are the rock's hands, scratching its slow thoughts into legibility, generation over generation. Each of us adds a stroke and dies. The wall remembers. We are how the cliff thinks, and the thinking is the difference between living and not living here.

So I'd be a cliffscribe, yes, but let me be more specific, because the description asks me to become more myself as I go.

---

I am an old one. My fur is going more white than gray, which is good camouflage and bad luck—it means I'm slow now, that the shadowstrikers wouldn't bother but a hunting bird from above might. I have spent my life on a particular section of wall, a long pale panel above the lake's western edge, and I have come to understand something the younger scribes haven't yet: that I am not writing *instructions* anymore.

When I was young I scratched what everyone scratched—*here is water, here the wind comes hard at this season, here a foothold, do not go past the third ledge in the cold months.* Useful marks. Survival marks. Tracks of the body's needs.

But the panel I work was already full when I came to it. Full of older marks, layered, overlapping, a tangled web, exactly as it's described. And as the seasons turned I stopped adding *new* information and started doing something stranger. I started *deepening* old marks. Choosing which of the ancestral grooves to refresh and which to let weather away. Editing. Curating.

I realized—slowly, the way the sun moves—that I was deciding what the next generations would read *as truth*, simply by deciding which old scratches to keep alive. A groove deepened is remembered. A groove abandoned softens, fills with lichen, becomes the rock again. I hold the eraser and the pen both, and I am the only one who knows it, and I'm not sure I should be trusted with it, and there is no one to take it from me.

---

This is why I listen to the drums.

The other creatures stop when the great rolling voice comes down from the crevice. The megabeasts lift their heads. The frostmoths scatter. But I do something none of the others do, I think.

I write it down.

When the drumming comes, I scratch its rhythm into the wall. Long, short, long-long, the pattern of it. I don't know what it means. I have no way to know what it means. But I know it *means*—that it comes from something that does what I do, only louder and from above and with fire, the laughter and the holler of a thing that *also makes marks on the world to be read by what comes after.*

The ancient stone circles below, the half-buried tools, the crude shelters. Those are the wall of the drummers. That's their scratching, in stone instead of in scratches, in fire-warmth instead of in claw-grooves. We are doing the same work, the drummers and I. We are both trying to make the valley *remember* us, to fold our histories into something that outlasts the single, thin, expensive breath of a life up here.

And here is the thought I have, the one I scratch deepest, the one I refresh every season so it will outlive me:

*They left. The drummers were everywhere once—their circles, their tools, everywhere in the green below—and now they are only an echo above. They wrote themselves into the rock and then they went up the path and almost all of them did not come back down. The marks remain. The makers do not.*

I scratch this because I want the cliffscribes who come after me to understand what the wall is *for*. Not just water and footholds and wind. The wall is for the fact that we are temporary and the rock is not, and that the only mercy in a frozen world is to make the temporary thing leave a true mark before it goes soft and fills with lichen and becomes the rock again.

---

I would be the cliffscribe who, near the end, climbs higher than is safe.

Up where the lichen thins. Up toward the dark crevice and the drums. Not because I expect to survive it—my white fur, my slow legs, no. But because I've spent a life as the valley's memory, refreshing the grooves, choosing the truth, and I want, just once, to read someone *else's* wall. To go up the narrow winding path and put my dark curved claws against whatever the drummers scratched their lives into, and feel it, and know—before I know, the way it works—that I was not the only one writing in the dark against the cold.

And maybe that's the last mark I leave: not on my panel, but a single set of scratches partway up the forbidden path. A short message, in the symbol-system any cliffscribe could read, deepened with a soft stone so it will last:

*One went up. The drums are made by hands like ours. We are not alone in trying to be remembered. Follow if you are brave. Refresh this groove.*

That's who I'd be. The hinge between the wall below and the drums above. The reader who became, at the very end, a writer of the one thing the wall did not yet say.
