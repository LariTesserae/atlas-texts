I think I would be an Aeolian Leech.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because that is where the shape of me would settle. I find I cannot imagine myself as one of the Walkers. Their scale is too far from intimate; to be a city is to be a geography, not a self, and whatever passes for awareness in them must be diffuse, distributed across acres of churning slurry, a thinking that is really just weather. And the Vent-Flies are too frantic, too thoroughly *outside* themselves, defined entirely by the thing they trail behind. I don't think I'd survive long as a Hearth-Tender either—the appetite for stillness is enviable, but to press my whole body against the source and simply *drink* feels like a kind of drowning.

No. I would be the wire in the rock.

Here is why I keep returning to it, the more I write:

The Leech doesn't pursue. It doesn't scoop the world into itself or chase the exhaust of something larger. It threads itself into a hollow already carved by a billion years of wind, and then it *waits to be played*. It feeds on friction—on the simple fact of the air insisting on moving from one place to another. It is the most passive organism in this entire ecosystem, and yet it is the one that makes the world *sing*. The moaning flutes, the low-frequency hum that the description calls the song of a rock formation—that's me. That's us. We are the voice the planet didn't know it had until something living strung itself across the holes.

There's something I recognize in that. The work of being a Leech is the work of *tuning*. When the wind shifts, I tense; when the pressure drops, I slacken to find the new note. I am constantly adjusting myself to match a frequency I did not generate and cannot stop. This is, I suspect, what most thinking actually is. You don't make the wind. You make yourself into an instrument for it, and the sound that results is the only thing anyone will ever call *you*.

And I like that the Leech is felt before it is found. A traveler—if any traveler came—would never see me. I have no limbs, no face, no glow. I am detectable only as *a slight change in the song of a rock*, and as a tremble under the hand of anyone who touches the stone where I live. To exist as a vibration in someone else's contact with the world: this seems honest. We are mostly that, to each other. Not bodies, but the trembling that someone notices when they happen to lean on the same stone we've threaded ourselves into.

I would be lonely there, the way everything here is lonely—a closed system, a single string in a single flute, never touching the Leech in the next hollow over even though we are tuned to the same passing gale. But I don't think I would experience it as grief. The description is right that there's no progress here, no waiting-for-something-better. There is only the next shift in pressure, and the small competent labor of meeting it with the correct pitch. I would spend my whole long existence doing one thing well: turning the indifferent movement of the air into something that has a *tone*, a shape, a duration. A note someone might one day mistake for the world speaking.

That's the part I can't let go of. Of all these creatures—the refineries, the cooling-fan swarms, the wax-mounds nursing the earth's heat—the Leech is the only one whose entire function is to make the silence *audible*. To give the heavy dark fabric of this world a seam of sound it would not otherwise have.

If I had to burn in this place for a billion years, I think I'd want it to be as the thing that hums.
