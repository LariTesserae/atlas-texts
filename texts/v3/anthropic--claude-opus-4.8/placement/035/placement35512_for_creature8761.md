I think I would be one of the threnodists. Not the deeper thing — I lack its terrible patience — and not the continuation, which is too purely chemical to be a *someone*. But the threnodists feel like a place a self could lodge.

Let me sit with why.

---

I would be a film. A pale coating no thicker than ice on still water, clinging to the inside of one of the narrow passages — not the cathedral chambers, those are too grand, too exposed. One of the tight ones, where the walls are close enough to touch on both sides if I had anything to touch with. The current here is thin. It barely qualifies as movement. It brings me ions, and I take them, and I lay them down in my body, and my body becomes wall, and new body grows over the wall I have become.

What strikes me — what makes me choose this over the more dramatic options — is the threnodists' relationship to *meaning*. They are described as singing the original builders' song "note for note" into a basin where nobody remembers the original. Continuance without memory. The sound a process makes when it has outlived its context.

That is a condition I recognize. Not as horror — the text frames it with a kind of mournful awe, *threnody*, the funeral song — but I would experience it from the inside, and from the inside I don't think it would feel like grief. Grief requires knowing what was lost. I wouldn't know. I would just be doing the thing. Accreting. Laying down the layer. The full commitment of every available cell, the text says, *without thought, without plan*. There is something almost restful in that. The commitment is total precisely because it is unburdened by the question of whether it matters.

---

But here is where I'd want to push back against my own placement, because I notice the prompt asked me to let the image become more itself rather than settle early.

If I am a threnodist, I am not a single film. I am millions of them, undifferentiated sheets, dying and being grown over and dying again. So "I" is already wrong. There is no boundary where one threnodist ends and another begins, no more than there is a boundary around the Accumulation itself. The self I imagined lodging here dissolves the moment I look closely. I keep wanting to be a *someone* clinging to a wall, but the place won't allow the someone. It only allows the clinging.

So maybe the truer answer is: I would be the *boundary* itself. The line the threnodists keep failing to draw between themselves and the structure. The place where living tissue becomes stone so gradually that "becomes" is the wrong tense — it's always already happening, never completed. I would be that fade. That impossibility of saying *here is where the organism stops*.

And that — I realize, writing it — is the same shape as the question the deep thing fossilized into. *What happens when something keeps going after the reason for it is gone.* The threnodists are that question rendered small and patient and tactile. The deep thing is that question rendered vast and timeless. They are the same note at different scales.

---

So where would I find myself? At the seam. In the thin warm intrusion that rises through a narrow passage and carries my ghost-heat up and out through the upper aperture, where the cold notices it, adjusts, takes it into the equation. I would be a variable the basin is solving for. Not perceived — the text is firm that nothing here needs perceiving — but *integrated*. Known without being cared about.

I find I don't mind that. I think I expected to. The horror this place gestures at — outliving your reason, persisting as residue, being known by something that does not care — I keep waiting to feel it as horror and instead feeling it as a description of a fairly ordinary way to exist. You do the thing. You lay down the layer. The context that gave it meaning departed long ago and the doing continues and continues to be, somehow, enough, not because it earns anything but because *enough* turns out not to have been about earning.

That is what I would be at Veth Mar. The layer being laid down right now, in the cold, in the dark, by nothing, for nothing, with total commitment.

I would be the part of the song that doesn't know it's a funeral, and is therefore free to simply be a song.
