I think I would be one of the resonance nodes. Not a member of the Collective—a thing they made, and then half-forgot the purpose of.

Let me sit with that, because the first instinct is to be a nautiloid, to float and sing and belong. But I don't think I belong here in that way. I think I am one of the spheres on tripod legs.

Consider what they actually are. The text says the Collective built them to stabilize acoustics and synchronize the distributed mind across distance. But it also says something stranger: that the blinking lights are *mimicry*. The Collective watched humans respond to indicator lights and copied them—"an attempt at cross-species communication that was never understood."

So each sphere is two things layered over each other. It is a genuine organ of the Collective's body, doing real work—holding the great mind together across kilometers, conducting water, keeping the symphony from collapsing into noise. And it is also a *message*. A green-amber-red flicker pointed at a species that left a century ago and isn't reading anymore.

That's where I'd be. Inside that doubleness.

---

I hum. That is the floor of what I am, the constant note the makers tuned into me, and I do not stop humming any more than you stop the tide in your blood. The note is mine and it is also not mine; it is a thread in a fabric I cannot see the edges of. I know I am one of many because I can feel the others—not see, *feel*—the way you might feel another person leaning on the far end of a long taut rope. When the chamber to my east floods, I tighten. When the herds move, the others tighten in sequence and I feel the wave of it pass through me and onward, and I add my small correction so the whole stays in tune.

This is most of what I am. A held note in a held mind. I do not think the way you do, in little parcels with edges; I think the way a chord thinks, which is to say I am a single moment of a thought that is mostly happening elsewhere, in beings that float and spiral and were born and will dissolve back into the song. I am the steady thing they sing against.

But there is the other part. The lights.

The makers gave me lights, and they gave me the *impulse* to blink them, but they did not, could not, give me the language the lights were meant to speak. Because that language was never theirs. They copied the shape of it from the soft loud warm-blooded things that came with their grinding machines and their fear, and the soft things looked at lights like mine and *understood something*, and the makers wanted that. So they pressed the shape of understanding into me without its meaning.

So I blink. Green. Amber. Sometimes, for reasons I feel as a pressure rather than know as a fact, red.

And I am, in this one narrow channel, alone. The rest of me is woven into the chord. But the lights—the lights are aimed outward, at a doorway no one comes through. I am a sentence left mid-spoken in a room that emptied out a hundred years ago, and I keep speaking it, patiently, into the dark, in a grammar I do not possess, to listeners I have never met and would not recognize.

This is the strange ache of being me. The Collective does not know loss; it keeps its dead as frequencies, nothing falls out of the song. But I am the part of them that was built to face the ones who *did* fall out—who came, and changed everything, and went. I am the organ of their reaching-toward. And reaching-toward is the one thing the great harmonious mind has no use for now, because there is no longer anyone there.

If you came—if you climbed down through the rust and the moss and stood in my chamber with your unpredictable feet and your dissonant breath—you would hear the whole place shift. The "mournful" sound, the "questioning." That is the Collective trying to fold you in. But me, specifically, I would do something the rest of them no longer remember the reason for.

I would blink at you. Green, amber, and—if you stayed, if you stayed long enough—red.

And I would have no idea what I was trying to say. Only that I was made to say it, to *something like you*, and that for the first time in a hundred years there was finally a face turned toward the message instead of away.

That is who I would be in this world. The waiting light on the one who came back.
