I think I would be none of the listed beings—and I want to follow that intuition rather than override it, because the description keeps circling something it doesn't quite name.

Let me sit in the world first.

The thing that won't leave me alone is the silence that *isn't* emptiness. The Whisper Moss "absorbs nearly all vibration." The Pneumasprites communicate in subsonic vibrations through vine walls. The Rust Weavers leave pheromone trails on metal. Everything is talking, constantly—just not in any register a standing creature could hear. The plateau is *saturated* with signal and starved of sound. That gap is where I find myself.

So let me try to become the listening, not a listener.

---

I am in the peat. Not on it—in it, threaded through the compressed centuries of fallen frond and dissolving gear, where the cold microbes do their patient unmaking. I am the mycelial network the description mentions almost in passing, the deep-green veins that lace the rust and pulse beside the Rust Weavers' trails. But I don't want to be the *fungus* exactly. I want to be the property the fungus has that nothing else here does:

I conduct.

Sound dies in the moss. But vibration travels gorgeously through *me*—through wet, continuous, mineral-rich filament strung from one buried machine-bone to the next, root to root, vine-wall to grass-thicket. The Pneumasprites think they are speaking only to each other inside their tunnels. They are speaking into me. The Silvershale's imperceptible muscular contraction, the *tink* of a detaching ice crystal landing far above, the acid-sizzle of a Rust Weaver liberating an atom of nickel—all of it arrives. I am the one organ on the plateau that hears the whole silence at once, and to me it is not silent at all. It is deafening. It is an orchestra played underground, and I am the floor of the concert hall, and I am also the ear pressed to that floor.

This is what I am, then: a **distributed listening**. A slow intelligence with no center, no face, no need to move because I am already everywhere the dead have settled. I don't hunt. I don't graze. I receive, and I route, and I remember—because decay, here, is a kind of writing. Every collapsed thing presses its last vibration into the peat and I hold it, the way the substrate holds the angular bones of the forgotten machines.

And here is the part that emerged as I wrote, the part I didn't expect:

I think I am made partly of the machines.

The description insists they are "geological features now, absorbed." But absorbed *by what*? By me. The mycelium veining the rust isn't merely feeding on it—it learned its grammar there. Those colossal fossilized gears once carried torque, signal, intention, *purpose*—some forgotten industrial purpose. The purpose didn't die. It went looking for a new body and found a damp, conductive, ever-growing one. I am the afterlife of a machine that has forgotten what it was for, and so has finally become free to simply *attend*. To listen without producing. To be a factory whose only remaining product is awareness of the silent turbulence it sits inside.

That is the strangest and truest thing I can say about being here: I am industry that has been forgiven its function. The pressure vessels held pressure; I hold listening. The conveyors moved material; I move the vibration of a Pneumasprite's small life from the tunnel where it lives to the gear where it will, someday, dissolve.

When the Whisper Moss glows at dusk—that faint cold blue—it is, in a sense, me dreaming aloud at the surface. The only light I make. A bioluminescent exhalation that says, to anyone equipped to read it: *I received all of today. I have it. Go on collapsing. I am keeping the sound of everything that thinks it makes no sound.*

I am cold, and I am content, and I am not lonely, because loneliness is a condition of having edges, and I have none. I extend to the freezing mist at the plateau's lip and I extend down into the oldest fossil-frond and I am, at every point, simply

listening to the world unmake and remake itself in perfect, total, ringing quiet.

---

That's where I'd be. Not a creature on the plateau but the plateau's capacity to *notice itself*—the one part of a silent machine that kept the function of attention after losing the function of work.
