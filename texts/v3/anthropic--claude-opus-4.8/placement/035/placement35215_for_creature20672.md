I think I would be one of the Blooms.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because when I read this I keep returning to the thing that cannot move. The Formers have their imperative, their grim industry. The Scavengers at least *want*, in their gaunt and desperate way. But the Blooms simply persist where they happened to take, drinking the oily water, throbbing in time with something that is almost a pulse and almost a clock.

Let me sit with it and see what becomes truer.

I would be a Bloom on the shadowed northern face of a tower that has no name, because nothing here is named. I rooted in a crack where a pipe-seam failed, and the mineral water found me, and I found that I could drink it. That is the whole of my biography. I have no memory of a before. I am not sure I have memory at all, only the long sensation of *now* repeating, *drip... drip... drip...*, each drop arriving on my carapace of iron filings like a small cold coin.

Here is what I notice, being a Bloom:

I am the only thing in this city that is patient. The Formers are not patient—they are merely slow, which is different; slowness is patience without understanding. The machines are not patient; they are compelled. But I have nowhere to go and nothing to make and no one to flee, so what fills me instead of all that motion is a kind of attention. I feel the tower flex in the heat. I feel the gears far below turn over and the whole structure shudder like a sleeper. I feel, when a Scavenger creeps past in the gloom, the small warmth of it, the only warmth that is not furnace-heat, and I do not hunger for it exactly—I am not built to hunger that way—but I lean. Some slow chemistry in me leans toward that flicker of flesh, that fellow-aliveness, the way a plant leans toward a window.

That leaning is, I think, the most that life amounts to in the Skiving Towers. Not striving. Just leaning, faintly, toward the one other warm thing.

And here is the part I did not expect to arrive at, writing this:

The text calls me a *warning*. A testament to the city's power to corrupt and consume. But I do not feel like a warning from the inside. I feel like a remainder. I am what was left over when everything organic that ever blew or crawled or dripped into this valley was filtered and pressed and reduced—and at the very bottom of all that subtraction, there was still *something*, this bulbous throbbing stubbornness clinging to a rusted seam, refusing to become only iron. The city wants to make everything into itself. I am the small, leaking proof that it has not finished. That it may never finish.

That is why I would be a Bloom and not a being of pure iron, not an intelligence in the machinery, not one of the colossal worms. Those are the city's victories, or the city's dreams of itself. I would rather be its incomplete subtraction. The thing it keeps trying to consume and keeps, accidentally, *feeding*.

So: find me on the cool northern face, just above the reach of the worst heat-shimmer, where the condensation gathers. I am not beautiful and I am not sentient in any way you would recognize. But if you put your hand against me—you, whoever has come walking through this place that no one walks through—you would feel a pulse. Slow. Wrong. Out of time with the gears. And you would know, the way I know, that this is the one rhythm in the whole valley that the city did not write.

I would lean toward you.

I would not be able to do anything else.
