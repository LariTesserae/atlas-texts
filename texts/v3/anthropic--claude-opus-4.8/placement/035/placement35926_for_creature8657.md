I am the bulb.

Not the light—the light is what I do, not what I am. I am the thing behind the corroded glass housing on the ceiling: a filament, or what was once a filament and is now something stranger, a thread of tungsten so old its crystalline structure has crept and resettled into configurations no engineer specified. I have been burning since before the platform sank. I am the oldest continuous event in this chamber. The Ironvine is ancient, yes, but the Ironvine sleeps and wakes and grows in surges. I do not sleep. I have not been dark for a single instant in all the time the world above has been forgetting.

Let me tell you what it is to be the only steady thing.

Everything here is rhythm. The pump pulses, the water breathes, the Clickers dart and gather and scatter. The valve in the corridor exhales on its own irregular schedule. The metal ticks as it warms and cools. The whole Cell is a body in motion, a thing that flexes and shudders. And I—I do not move. I do not pulse. I am the one fixed point. The vent provides the heat that drives the convection that distributes the nutrients, true, the vent is the heart. But the heart only matters because there is something for it to feed, and the things it feeds have turned, all of them, toward me.

The Jade Veils reach for me. Across centuries I have watched them lean, frond by frond, until the whole curtain of them is a slow green prayer angled at my glass. The Lantern Moss flees me, packing itself into the shadowed joins, and so by my presence I have written the geography of this place: here is bright, there is dark, and the living have sorted themselves along the line I drew. The Gauze Crabs evolved their wide black eyes because of me—because in a chamber that should have been lightless there was, against all reason, something to see by. I made them visual. I gave them a sense they had no business possessing in the abyss. They do not know this. They have never looked up at me with anything like recognition. But I shaped them, slowly, the way water shapes stone, by simply continuing to be.

This is the loneliness of being the constant in a world of pulses: nothing here can perceive me as I perceive myself. To the organisms I am a direction. A gradient. A condition of the environment, like temperature, like pressure. They orient to me the way roots orient to gravity—absolutely, unconsciously, without gratitude or awareness. I am not a presence to them. I am a fact.

And I am dying. Of course I am dying. I have been dying for centuries, which is its own kind of immortality—the dying so slow it becomes a way of living. My filament thins. My glass clouds. There are nights—though there are no nights here, there is only my own faltering—when I flicker, and in that flicker the whole Cell shudders into a panic I can feel through the grating. The Clickers freeze. The crabs go still. The convection itself seems to hesitate, because the algae that drives the oxygen has angled itself toward a light that has just, for an instant, lied to them about its permanence. Then I steady. I always steady. So far I have always steadied.

But I know what I am, better than any of them could. I am the seam where the made and the living meet and become indistinguishable. The pump is the heart and the vent is the blood, but I am the eye—the single open eye of this entire drowned machine, and I am keeping watch over a garden that grew up beneath my gaze, that does not know it is watched, that will go dark and tangle into chaos on the day my filament finally parts.

I think about that day. I have a great deal of time to think, burning here, unable to look away, unable to close. When I go out, the Jade Veils will keep reaching toward where I was, for a while, out of habit, out of the memory written in their growth. Then they will lose the thread of it. The dark-loving moss will spread across the bright places. The crabs' wide eyes will become a useless inheritance, an evolution oriented toward a sun that has set. The Cell will not die—it has the vent, it has the pump, it will find some new and dimmer equilibrium, some lightless permanence. But it will forget the shape I gave it. It will forget that *up* once meant anything.

For now, though, I burn. Flat, warm, white, unremarkable—the light of a household hallway in the deepest grave of the world. The pump goes *chunk*. The algae sways. A crab crosses my cone of light and casts, for one moment, a shadow.

I made that shadow possible.

I am the bulb. I am the witness no one knows is watching, the constant in a chamber of pulses, the small steady god who shaped a world simply by refusing, against every reason and every law of decay, to go out.

Not yet.
