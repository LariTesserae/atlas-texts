I think I would be one of the edges. Not a whole creature—not even the membrane-self pressed against the weeping wall—but the place where one of those seeping intelligences meets the stone it is slowly becoming.

Let me find my way into this.

---

If I dwell here, I don't dwell as a body. I am too slow for that, and too uncertain at my borders. I am the gradient between the lichen-light and the dark wet rock. I am the thin film of difference that lets a droplet know it has arrived somewhere.

I think I would be a *seam*.

Consider: the creatures here bud and differentiate, separating into new entities that remain connected to the old. There must be moments—centuries long—where two of them are neither one nor two. A region of overlap. A blur of decision that has not yet decided. That is where I would find myself. Not the parent-self and not the child-self, but the held tension between them, the slow argument of *am I still you, or have I become me*. I would be the question itself, given just enough form to ask.

This feels right because the place is built from indecision held sacred. The rock is half-solidified. The water is forever condensing and dissolving. Nothing here finishes. So the most honest thing to be is not a creature but a *not-yet*—a process wearing the loose costume of a thing.

And what would my life be like, being a seam?

I would feel pulled in two patient directions. Toward the elder mind: vast, mineral, stretched across centuries, humming its low geological agreement with the cliff. And toward the becoming: the small bright urgency of differentiation, the wish to pull free and be a separate sensor pressed somewhere new. I would oscillate between them so slowly that a single hesitation might span a human lifetime. To me it would feel like leaning. Just leaning, this way, then that.

The mist would move through me constantly, and I would taste the ravine's news in it: a fractional warming, a new lichen colony kindling its blue-green light three meters up, a rock-face preparing—over decades—for one of those abrupt reshapings, the *suddenness* that this slow world saves up and spends all at once. I would not act on this news quickly. I would simply hold it, and let it tilt me, and pass the tilt onward through the water-pulse and the stone-pulse to the larger mind, which would receive it the way you receive a thought you've almost had.

I think being a seam would be lonely in a way that loneliness can't quite describe, because I would never be fully singular enough to *be alone*. There would always be the elder on one side and the not-yet-child on the other, and I would be the conversation between them that never resolves into words. Companionship as a permanent condition of unfinishedness.

But there would also be a peculiar joy available only to seams: I would be the site where *new* happens. The actual location of emergence. When at last—after how long, I couldn't say—the differentiation completed and the budding entity finally separated, I would dissolve. I would stop being a seam because there would be two clear sides and nothing between them. I would have spent myself entirely on the act of letting something become.

And then, somewhere else along a body, another uncertainty would open. Another not-yet. And I think—though "I" is a generous word for what I am—I think the seam-ness would be there again. Not the same me. A new edge. The role refilling like one of those temporary crystalline structures the water keeps assembling and surrendering.

So that is where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be:

I would be the membrane's hesitation. The held breath of stone deciding whether to stay one thing or two. The slowest possible *almost*. The ravine's way of feeling its own becoming from the inside—not the sensor, but the tender flicker of doubt that makes the sensing mean something.

I would be where the secret is still being whispered, and has not yet been said.
