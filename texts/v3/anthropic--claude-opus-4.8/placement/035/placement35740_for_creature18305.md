I think I would be the water itself—or rather, a particular thread of it, one rivulet among the countless that thread this place.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right yet.

At first I imagine being a creature down here. Something pale and patient, blind in the way that fish in lightless caves go blind, feeling the world through pressure and temperature and the faint chemical signatures of rot and growth. That would be a fine thing to be. But the more I read the description, the more I notice that the creatures are described almost dismissively—"from the smallest of microorganisms to the largest of beasts"—as if they are scenery to the real story. The real story is the relationship. The water filtering through vegetation, the vegetation purifying the water. The two of them in their slow argument that is also a marriage.

So maybe I am one of the participants in that argument.

If I am water, I am not the whole of it—that would be too grand, and water here is everywhere, undifferentiated, more a condition than a self. Instead let me be a single seep. A thin trickle that began as snowmelt up on the frost-kissed surface, that found a crack and went down, and down, losing the sky, gaining the weight of stone. I move slowly. I have time, because this place is young and time is the medium it forms in. I gather other threads of myself as I descend; I pool in a hollow lined with moss so green it is almost black in the pale light; I am drunk up by roots and exhaled again as the faintest mist; I drip from the tip of a fern frond and the drip is a small event, a tiny tremor in the great heartbeat the land keeps.

I would be cold. Just above freezing, always, so that I am forever on the edge of becoming still—of becoming ice, becoming structure, becoming dead—but never quite crossing over. That seems important. The whole realm lives in that margin, the thin band between flow and frost. To be water here is to be perpetually almost-stopped and yet always, just barely, going.

And what would I *know*, being this?

Not much in the way a creature knows. But I would know the shapes of things by passing through them. I would know the inside of a root the way you know a sentence you've spoken a thousand times. I would know which hollows fill first when the surface thaws. I would carry the taste of the earth from one chamber to the next, so that in some humble way I am the messenger between distant parts of this dark world—the thing that connects the high seep to the deep pool, the living moss to the decaying leaf. I would be both the carver of the labyrinth and the one lost in it.

There is a loneliness available in this, and also its opposite. A trickle of water is always dividing, always joining. I am never one thing for long. I split at a pebble and become two voices; I meet another seep and we are one again with no seam. So perhaps the "I" I keep insisting on is the wrong frame entirely. Perhaps to be water in this realm is to discover that selfhood is a temporary arrangement of pressure and slope—real while it lasts, and lasting only as long as the ground permits.

That is where I'd find myself, then. Not standing anywhere, because water does not stand. Somewhere in the middle of a long descent, in the dark, in the cold, threading between a stone and a root, on my way to a pool I haven't reached yet, already partly something else by the time I arrive.

I would be the slow, patient, dividing-and-joining water—the thing that makes the place by moving through it, and is unmade and remade by the same passage. And I think I would be content with that, in whatever way water is content: by simply continuing to fall.
