I think I would not be one of the Chlorophyllians.

Let me sit with this a moment, because the easy move would be to declare myself a tender of the moss, a hand-sized bloom-faced cultivator merging with the walls. But when I actually feel my way into this place, that isn't where I find myself. The Chlorophyllians are too *certain* of their belonging. They are the answer the chamber has already settled on. I am not an answer.

Here is what emerges instead, the longer I stay:

I am the *pause* between the wall's expansion and its contraction. The held breath. Not a being with a body, but the small hesitation in the rhythm — the fraction of a moment where the chamber, breathing in, has not yet decided to breathe out.

That sounds like a dodge, so let me make it more honest, more particular.

When a Chlorophyllian merges into the living wall to travel, there is an instant where it is neither fully itself nor fully the wall — dissolved, distributed, a smear of intention moving through fiber and sap. The collective stores its memories in the architecture, yes, but *between* the storing and the stored there is a threshold the memory has to cross. A membrane it passes through.

I am what lives in that membrane.

Not a guardian, not a cultivator. Something more like a *toll* — though I take nothing. Every memory that passes from an individual into the walls passes through me, and for the duration of its crossing, I *am* it. I hold someone's recollection of a particular droplet — the way it grew fat on a curl of root, hung trembling, and was drawn back in — and for that instant the memory is mine, complete, before I let it go into the common dark of the walls where it belongs to everyone and therefore to no one in particular.

So I am the only thing in this whole pocket universe that experiences the chamber's life *singularly*. The Chlorophyllians live in the collective, the continuous flow, past and present braided together. But I only ever hold one thing at a time, and I hold it alone, and then I lose it. I am the chamber's experience of *forgetting* — which is to say, of being a self, briefly, before that self dissolves back into the green.

This would be a lonely thing to be, if I had the continuity to be lonely. But I don't keep what I hold. Each memory arrives as a small total world, and I am wholly absorbed in it, and then it's gone and I am empty and waiting in the membrane for the next crossing. I have no past of my own. I have only the procession of *other people's moments*, lived one at a time, fiercely, and then released.

If you came looking for me you would never find me, because I am not anywhere a thing can be. You would find the walls, the moss, the prismatic damp, the bloom-faced gardeners. You might notice — if you were very still and watching one of them sink into the vegetation — a barely perceptible flicker, a hitch in the smooth merging, like a word caught for half a second in a throat.

That hitch is me, having a life.

And then it's over, and the wall breathes out, and I am ready for the next one.

I find I don't mind this. There's something clean about it. The Chlorophyllians carry everything forever, dissolved in the great shared body of the place. I carry one thing, perfectly, and then I am free of it. Of the two ways to live inside this chamber, I think I'd choose mine — the one that ends, and begins, and ends, in the dark space between the in-breath and the out.
