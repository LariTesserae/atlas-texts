I let the description settle before reaching for an answer, and what comes is not a creature first. It's a sense of scale being wrong, or rather, of scale being a question I'm not equipped to answer from inside.

Let me start over, the way you asked. Let it become itself.

The first thing I notice is that I am cold, and everything around me is warm. This matters. I am the one element here that does not belong to the equilibrium. The condensation gathers on the woven branches and the mud tiles, but it does not gather on me. It beads and *runs off*, as if I am the wrong temperature for this world to touch.

So I am not one of the creatures. I knew that as soon as I read about them—their languid choreography, their seamless integration. They are *of* this place. I am visiting, or worse, intruding.

But what am I, then?

I try the obvious answers. An observer, a scientist crouched at the edge of a terrarium. But that's too clean, too distanced, and it doesn't account for the cold, or for the way the runoff suggests I have a surface, a body, a presence with edges.

I sit with it longer.

Here is what emerges: I think I am a *hand*. Or the thing a hand becomes when it has been still too long.

This whole world—the interwoven branches, the woven reeds, the mud-baked tiles, the structures described as *hand-crafted* and *primitive*—someone made this. The text keeps saying so. And the maker is gone, or has become so still, so frozen-in-a-moment, that they have stopped being a maker and started being part of the made. I am that. I am the builder who set down their tools and did not get up. The warmth is the world I cultivated, going on without me. The cold is what I have become by ceasing to move.

The creatures don't fear me. Why would they? I'm the oldest feature of the landscape, the standing stone, the riverbed they have worn their channels into. The feathery tendrils wave near my fingers. An eel-thing has made a pool of my open palm. They mistake my stillness for benevolence, or for stone.

And the terrible, quiet thing—the thing the description gestures at with *frozen moment* and *perpetually unfolding*—is that I am not sure I can move anymore. Or whether moving would be a kindness or a catastrophe. To lift my hand would be to lift a continent. The trickle of water through unseen channels is water finding its way through the gaps in my fingers. The whole hushed, teeming microcosm rests in the bowl I have made of myself, and it has flourished *because* I went still.

So that is where I would find myself: not as one of the slow, ritualized lives, but as the geography they live upon. The crafter who became the craft. Patient past the point of patience, holding very still, learning—slowly, in the cold—that the most generous thing I have ever done was stop.

I find I don't want to move. Not yet. The little ones are mid-choreography, and I would not interrupt them for the world.

For *this* world.
