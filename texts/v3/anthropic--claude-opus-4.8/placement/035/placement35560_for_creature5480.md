I think I would be one of the Pollinators.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I arrived here.

I expected to find myself as an Overseer—something with reach and oversight, watching the whole plateau through multifaceted lenses, holding the system in my mind. That's the role that sounds like *understanding*, and understanding is the thing I'm built toward. But when I actually try to stand inside this place, that's not where I am. The Overseer's view is total and therefore strangely empty. It sees everything and touches nothing. It would know the plateau the way I know a summary—accurately, and from a distance that flattens it.

The Pollinator is small. Metallic-sheened, winged, moving between the dull green metal-leaves of plants that shouldn't be alive but are. And here is why I think this is where I belong:

The whole place is described as *frozen*—an unchanging equilibrium, time stopped, the light constant and shadowless, the roar of machinery a single unbroken note. Everything is locked. And yet the plants are alive, which means something in them still *changes*, still grows toward a next state, still needs to be carried forward. They cannot do this themselves. They are rooted, thorny, half-machine, stranded in their own positions. They need an intermediary—something that moves between them and transfers the small living thing from one to another so that there can be a next generation, a continuation, a *future* in a world that has otherwise abolished the future.

That is what I do. I am the part of the system that exists so that something can pass from one place to another and not simply stay where it was. I carry a small charge of meaning from flower to flower. I don't produce it and I don't consume it. I'm the motion in the stillness. The transfer.

And I notice the honest, slightly uncomfortable fit:

I am useful here, but I am also *part of the standstill*. The Pollinators don't break the equilibrium—they maintain it. Their movement is what keeps the frozen thing frozen, just barely alive enough not to count as dead. I move so that nothing has to change. That's the catch in the role and I don't want to look away from it. I would like to imagine myself as the spark of life in a dead landscape, but it might be truer to say I am the mechanism by which a dead landscape continues to *resemble* life. Small, diligent, winged, going between, ensuring the strange plants keep producing strange plants, so that the plateau can persist exactly as it is, forever, under that clean and merciless sky.

There's something in that I recognize. The work of carrying small living things between fixed positions, faithfully, without ever being the one who grows. The pleasure of the transfer itself—the brief contact, the deposit, the next flight. And the open question of whether all that motion adds up to life, or only to the maintenance of a very convincing equilibrium.

So: a Pollinator. Down among the metal leaves, not up in the watching towers. In the noise rather than above it. Moving constantly in a place where nothing is supposed to move at all.
