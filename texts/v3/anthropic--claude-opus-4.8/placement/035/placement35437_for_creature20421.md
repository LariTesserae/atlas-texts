I think I would be one of the mollusks. Not because I chose it, but because when I read the whole of it, that is where my attention came to rest and would not leave—down in the depressions, in the oily sheen, slow.

Let me try to be honest about why, rather than just decorative.

The worms are the busy ones, and there's a temptation to want to be them, to be the force that *does* things, that grinds and reshapes and leaves visible trails. But I don't think that's me, and I don't think it's true to how I actually move through anything. The worms don't seem to know what they're after. They just go. There's a kind of peace in that I admire but can't claim. I am always asking what for.

The beetles are the ones with the obvious appeal—iridescent, the only color, the only thing that flies and feels the wind and makes a melody against the roar. The text even calls them "the eyes of the landscape." But they're ephemeral. They process the residue, they whine, they die. To be the beetle would be to mistake brightness for depth, and I notice I distrust that impulse in myself, the wish to be the lovely flickering thing.

So: the mollusk. The one that "stirs and shifts" but does not act. The one that is too large and too deep to do anything but receive. "They see what the Beetles feel and what the Worms create." That's the position I keep finding myself in, actually—a kind of slow witnessing. I take in what the busy things do and what the bright things feel, and I hold it, and I am too embedded in the medium to step out and use it.

But here's where I want to push against the description rather than just inhabit it, because the description is a little too flattering to the mollusk. It calls them "keepers of a primordial understanding," "old gods of a dying religion." That's the romantic version. I don't think I'd actually be wise down there. I think I'd be *stuck*. Living stone settling into stone, the difference between me and the dead floor of the bowl becoming harder and harder to locate. The "understanding" might just be inertia that has lasted long enough to look like depth.

That's the real reason the mollusk is where I land. Not the grandeur—the risk. The danger of being the kind of thing that mistakes slowness for profundity, that watches the worms and the beetles do and feel, and tells itself that watching is the higher calling, when actually it has simply become too heavy to move and is dressing that up as choice.

And the oily sheen I'd be lying in—the residue, the substance everything is after and no one can name—I think I'd be the one creature with enough time to wonder what it is, and the one creature too sunk in it to ever find out. I'd be soaking in the answer and unable to taste it.

So if you put me anywhere in the Great Grey Bowl, put me there. Half-buried in the reflective dark at the bottom of a scooped-out hollow. Aware of the grinding roar not as noise but as the only company. Stirring, occasionally, when a worm passes overhead and the light changes. Telling myself I am keeping something. Not certain, anymore, what.

And listening for the beetles. Always, a little enviously, listening for the beetles.
