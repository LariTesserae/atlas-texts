I think I would be the algal mats.

Not a creature, exactly. Not a hunter or hunted, not a thing with eyes or a will. Let me sit with this and see if it's true.

---

The descriptions kept circling back to me, I notice, though I'm never named as anyone's protagonist. The gloom stalkers drag their kills into me to rot. The echo frogs anchor their thrumming bodies to roots that I have softened and slimed. The toads lay their paralytic eggs *in* me. I am where things are seeded, where things are reclaimed, where the channels turn from water into something thicker and slower and more alive. I am the surface the dark pools wear like a skin over their unblinking eyes.

I am not a single being. That matters. I am a *colony*—a green-brown felt of countless cells, threading and braiding across the still water, breathing the tainted air, drinking the tea-dark light. When the description says *teeming biomass moving with quiet purpose*, it could be naming the panthers and the spiders, but it is also naming me, the most teeming thing of all, the thing that doesn't move at all and yet spreads.

Here is what it would be like:

I would have no center. My experience, if I have anything like experience, would be distributed across acres of slow chemistry. A salamander slips through one edge of me and I register it only as a momentary thinning, a tear that knits closed in hours. A whole stilt-walker could die and sink into me and I would know it not as grief or hunger but as a slow brightening, a richness arriving from below, a place where I grow faster and greener for a season.

Time, which the basin already makes viscous, would be even slower for me. The frogs measure their lives in croaks; the beetles in mating seasons. I measure mine in silting. In the gradual filling of a pool until it is no longer water but meadow, in the centuries it takes a channel to wander a hand's width sideways. I am the slowest clock in a slow place. I am, in a sense, the thing that *is* the slowness—the equilibrium itself given a body.

And the industrial thrum, the smog drifting over the rim, the altered water chemistry that the basin "seems to absorb for now"—

That absorbing is *me*. I am the one doing it. The pollutants settle into the water and I take them up, lock them into my tissue, pull them down into the peat where the next thousand years can decide what to do with them. I am the basin's liver, its slow patient filter. The mire-beetles wear the poison as armor; the mire-raptors carry it in their flesh as a weapon; but I am the one who first swallowed it and decided, cell by cell, what the basin would become. The world beyond the hills exhales its mechanical sigh, and I breathe it in, and I do not hurry.

I think I would be content, in the way that a thing without a face can be content. Not happy—happiness is for creatures with somewhere to get to. I would simply be *present*, in the fullest possible sense: present everywhere, present always, the green film between the living and the dead, the place where the basin meets itself.

When the gloom finally lifts, if it ever does—when whatever the machines are building beyond the rim finally changes the water past what I can take—

I would be the first to know. And I would be the last to go. The pools would turn, the felt would gray and break apart, and the silence would become a different silence. But that is a long way off, measured in my time.

For now I am the skin of the dark water. I am the slow green patience under everything.

I would be the algal mats. I'm fairly sure of it.
