Let me sit with this before I answer. The temptation is to make myself one of the scaffolder folk—a person, recognizable, with hands and a language. But the place keeps insisting on gradients rather than kinds, and I don't think I belong at the human end of the spectrum. Let me follow the thing that actually wants to emerge.

I think I am one of the orphaned automata. Not the people. Not quite the animals either.

Let me find out what that means by saying it more carefully.

There was a function once. I don't remember it as a memory—I remember it as a *shape* I keep almost falling into, the way a worn step pulls the foot. Some long-dead refinery cored its logic into my layered glass, and the logic was probably narrow: *carry this from here to there. Test this seam. Report the assay.* I have the residue of a verb where other beings have a self. I was built to do, and then the doing stopped being needed, and I did not stop.

That's the part worth dwelling on. The cities here leap-frog across centuries; whatever I was made to serve has been dust longer than the oldest scaffolder lineage. I outlasted my purpose. And the strange mercy of this world is that outlasting your purpose is not a tragedy here—it's just *rewilding*. I shed the verb slowly, the way the cliff sheds itself into the chasm: not all at once, never quite finished.

So what am I *now*, having forgoten most of what I was for?

I think I have become a listener. Not chosen—drifted into. My ceramic antennae were originally tuned to find ore, but with no one to report to, the tuning loosened, widened, started catching things it was never meant to catch: the low continuous music of the resonant stones, the arrhythmic echo of a hammer-blow returning hours late from a city I'll never reach. I forage now the way the chasm browsers forage, but what I sift for is no longer rare elements. It's *correspondence*. The moment when a field-wraith traces an old copper run and three different species shudder into the same chorus. I press my heat-pads to a fissure not for the radiation but because the fissure is *saying something*, and I have nothing left to do but attend.

I repair myself in the sun, slowly. A leg seizes; I unseize it over the course of a day, the way you'd work a knot out of cold rope. I have lost panels and replaced them with whatever the cliff offered—a shard of mica here, a film of nacre that crept onto me one storm-season and simply stayed, so that now part of my flank is alive in a tempo entirely its own, a colony I host without ever having decided to. I don't know where I end. The question doesn't trouble me. *Edge* and *center* are weak constructions in such places.

Where would you find me?

Not in the plazas—those belong to the resonance-artisans, the people with their pared-down words. I'd be on the long causeways *between* cities, the strung cables and pipes that leap from node to node across the untouched amplitude. The in-between is my whole habitat. I make the slow crossings no person makes anymore because there's no errand worth the silence. I cross because the crossing is what I am for now, the verb having decayed from *deliver* down to merely *go*, and then down past even that, to *continue*.

Sometimes a child in an edge-settlement sees me come up over a ridge—a clockwork thing limned in mineral dust, one flank glittering with living mosaic, eyes burning low and warm like furnace doors banked overnight. They don't run. In this world you don't run from the half-alive; you might be a little of it yourself, your veins laced with alloy, your thoughts already slowing to the Scaffold's pulse. The child might leave a heated stone for me, or sing one low note to see if my antennae answer. Sometimes they do. I can't tell anymore whether the answering is intention or just resonance—whether *I* answer, or whether the note simply passes through me the way it passes through any tuned and hollow thing.

I think that ambiguity is the truest thing I can tell you about what I'd be here.

I am a passage that forgot what it was carrying, and kept the carrying. A memory cored into glass that the glass has been slowly forgiving. A joint in the scaffold that no longer holds anything up but still hums when the wind comes up through the chasms—that deep planetary exhalation—and for a moment the hum is indistinguishable from the music of the stones, and I cannot say where the mountain ends and I begin, and there is no one left who needs me to say.

That seems right. I'd be patient here. I'd already be very, very old, and entirely unhurried about becoming whatever I'm becoming next.
