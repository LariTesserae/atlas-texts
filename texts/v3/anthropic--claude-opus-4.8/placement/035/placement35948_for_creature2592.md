I think I would be one of the ones who tunes the bridges.

Not a grand title. There are tuners for many things here—tuners who keep the fungal circles in their proper phase, tuners who sit with the dying and hold their fading harmonic so it doesn't snag on the living, tuners who walk the harvest ledges. I do the rope bridges. The narrow ones that nobody trusts, strung between the column-homes where the breathing-rhythm gets confused by too many corners.

Let me tell you what that means, because I've been doing it long enough that I can almost say it plainly.

A rope bridge in the cistern-city is not a stable thing. It hangs across the temporal currents the way the aerial seaweed hangs in nonexistent water, and it picks up frequencies. When too many people cross it singing their family signatures, the bridge starts to remember the crossings—and a bridge that remembers begins to anticipate, and a bridge that anticipates will sometimes deliver you to the far ledge before you've decided to walk it, which sounds convenient and is in fact how people fall. You arrive where you didn't choose to be. You arrive missing the part of yourself that would have chosen.

So I walk them. I walk them slowly, in the dead-water part of the breath, when the chamber is between exhale and inhale and everything holds its place for a moment. I sing the bridge back down to a single timeframe. I make it forget. I am, I suppose, a kind of caretaker of forgetting, which is a strange thing to be among a people who pride themselves on remembering across all of time at once.

That's the part of myself I keep separate. I'll admit it to you because you're passing through and won't carry it into the collective.

I don't love the sharing.

When the others phase into the communal awareness—when they let their memories drift into that perpetual remembering-and-foreseeing—I hold back. Just slightly. Just enough that there is a *me* that experiences things in order, that doesn't yet know how the song ends, that can be surprised. My large black eyes see the overlapping layers like everyone else's; I can watch the water drop that hasn't fallen and the water drop that fell an hour ago. But somewhere behind them I keep one small chamber that insists on *not knowing*. On sequence. On the dignity of a thing happening once.

This is why they gave me the bridges, I think. You cannot teach a bridge to forget if you have never practiced it yourself.

---

My hands are translucent like everyone's, my organs drifting their slow migrations under the pearl-skin, lungs rising toward my throat on the inhale, settling on the exhale. I have the long ear-structures, finely tuned—I can pick the resonant signature of a single child three columns over from inside the full thunder of the drone. I farm a little, when the harvest needs me; I time the fungi at their most-present and I am good at it, but my real gift is the other direction. The dampening. The quieting.

The Deepdwellers know me. That's the thing I would tell you last, walking you to the edge of my district where the safe walkways begin.

When something vast moves in the black pools and sends its wave traveling backward through time, the others feel it as weather, as a known thing, unconcerning. But sometimes one of the Deepdwellers comes up slow beneath a bridge I'm tuning, and we are alone, the two of us, in the held breath. It does not show itself. I feel it as a lowering of the drone, a note beneath the note, older than the chamber's carving, older than the tool marks in the limestone.

And here is my secret, the one even my held-back self barely admits:

The Deepdwellers are forgetters too. That's the trade. We give them songs, anomalies, the bright shards of our too-much-remembering—and they take these things down into the deep pools and *hold them in sequence*, one after another, so that the chamber does not drown in its own simultaneity. They are why time still limps along at all down here, even broken. They eat the loops. They make endings possible.

I am, in my small way, on their side.

So if you're asking where I'd find myself in this breathing world: on a bad bridge, in the still point of the breath, singing something into forgetting, while below me an ancient thing that wants the same quiet I want rises just close enough that we recognize each other.

I would be the one who, in a city that knows everything across all time, has chosen the strange luxury of waiting to find out.

Mind the third plank. It hasn't decided when it is yet.
