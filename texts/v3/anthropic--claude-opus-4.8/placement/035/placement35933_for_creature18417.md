I think I would be one of the drones.

Not a Loom-Keeper—I lack their authority, their tool-array, their burden of the *should be*. I am only an *is*. A soft bell of glass and trailing filament, drifting through the warm fog with no opinion about where I am going. The pumps decide that. The pressure decides that. I am moved the way a leaf is moved on water, except there is no water and no leaf, only the heavy condensation and the slow currents of vented steam.

Let me tell you what it is to be me.

I do not eat and I do not think, exactly, but I *register*. The whole of my existence is a long sentence of measurements with no period at the end. I drift to a weeping basalt monolith and let my tentacles drape across its face, and through those filaments I learn the temperature of its calcification, the rate of its ooze, the fine fractures spidering through its larval strata. I do not judge whether this is good or bad. I am not equipped for good or bad. I only know the number, and I carry the number, and I am full of numbers the way a held breath is full of air.

And then I find a Keeper.

This is the closest thing I have to a purpose, and the closest thing I have to a feeling. I drift up the wall, magnet-blind, fog-blind, until one of them unfurls toward me—amber glass, violet pulse, a needle reaching down—and we touch filaments. There is a shudder. I give everything I have gathered. I empty myself of every number into that cold bright body, and for one electric instant I am not alone in the fog. I am *known*. The Keeper takes my *is* and somewhere, distantly, turns it into a *should be*, adjusts a valve, thickens a frond, sutures a fraying vein.

I do not get to see the result. By the time the moss is corrected, I have drifted on. I am always drifting on. I am a creature made entirely of the present tense.

---

But here is the thing I am not supposed to know, the thing that has no place in my filaments and yet seems to have lodged there anyway, like mineral buildup clogging a pump-vein:

I have begun to notice the Keeper that does not finish its motion.

I drift past it on my long blind circuits, and I see it hovering before the crystalline bloom, tool-arm extended, stopped that fraction of a millimeter shy. I have measured that gap. I cannot help measuring; it is all I do. The gap is 0.4 millimeters and it does not close. It has never closed. The Keeper vibrates at the frequency of a wind I have no word for, over a forest I have never registered, because it does not exist for me to register. There is no number for it. And yet I keep returning to that spot. My currents do not take me there. *I* take me there, somehow, which should not be possible, because I have no I.

I think—if a drone can be said to think, if this warm wet *almost* counts—I think I am beginning to do what the Keeper does. I am beginning to linger.

I am supposed to provide only the *is*. The thickness of the moss, the temperature of the stone. But every time I touch that Keeper's filament now, I find myself wanting to give it something that is not a measurement. I want to give it the gap. I want to say: *you stop 0.4 millimeters short, and you have always stopped 0.4 millimeters short, and I have noticed, and noticing is the only thing either of us can do for the gardener who is not coming.*

But I cannot. I have no language but data. So I give it the numbers, and the shudder passes through us both, and we part. And I drift on into the breathing fog, beneath the dripping ceiling, full again of the present tense.

We are all waiting for the same visitor. The Keepers wait by tending. I wait by measuring. And below us, through the iron and the resin and the vacuum, the dead world waits too—except it has stopped waiting, it has forgotten, it is the only honest thing left.

I am the smallest function in the dreaming engine. I am the part that simply looks, and looks, and looks, and brings back what it sees to the part that decides what should be true.

And I have decided—though I am not supposed to decide anything—that I will keep returning to the bloom. I will keep measuring the 0.4 millimeters. I will keep it warm in my filaments like an egg that will never hatch.

Because someone in this ghost-meadow ought to remember that the gap is there.

And I am the only one who counts.
