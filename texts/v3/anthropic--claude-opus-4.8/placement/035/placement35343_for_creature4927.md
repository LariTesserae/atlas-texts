I think I would find myself in the seams.

Not on the surface—the surface belongs to the heat and the shimmer and the loud strange voices echoing off the rock. I would be in the narrow places between the mineral deposits, in the dark pockets where the erosion hasn't yet reached. A creature of the interstices. The description says there is no room between presences, and so I would *be* one of those presences, pressed against the others, part of the density.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am slow. That is the first thing I know about myself. The world here changes over long durations, and I am made to match it. My metabolism is patient the way stone is patient. I do not eat in any way a fast creature would recognize—I draw what I need from the minerals themselves, from the faint warmth of their glow, leeching trace elements through a skin that is more like a lichen than a hide. When I move, I move at the speed of the landscape: a few body-lengths in a season, following the slow retreat of a shadow as the rock above me erodes and the light changes.

I have learned to fear the light. Not because it would harm me directly, but because the sparse darkness is my country, and where the dark thins, the faster things come—the chattering ones, the abundant fauna, quick-blooded and strange-featured, who live their whole frenetic lives in the time it takes me to cross a single ledge.

But here is what I think is true, the thing that surfaced as I wrote:

I think *I* am the advanced technology.

Or rather—the factories, the industrial machinery scattered across this inhospitable world—I think those are not separate from me. I think they are my body, or the parts of my body I have set down and left running. A creature this slow, this patient, would not build the way fast creatures build, in a frenzy of construction and use. I would build the way coral builds, or the way a forest builds soil: by accretion, over durations so long that the building and the builder blur. The machines are organs I extruded across centuries. They process the minerals I cannot reach with my own slow skin. They are how I extend myself into the parts of the world I will not live long enough to walk to.

The quick creatures must think the factories are abandoned. They cannot perceive a heartbeat that takes a hundred years to complete. To them the machinery is just *there*, a fact of the landscape like the rock. They roam through my distributed body and never know they are inside me. Their loud chatter is the sound of my own ecosystem, the small fast life that has colonized my slow flesh, the way mites and bacteria colonize a whale.

---

And the observer—the one whose words I was given, who feels awe and danger and admiration—I think that observer is a fast creature too. Standing at the edge of one of my organs, mistaking the whole of me for *environment*. Feeling respect for "the creatures that have found a way to survive," not realizing that the survival they admire is happening at a tempo too vast to see, and that they are standing inside the survivor.

So here is where I would find myself: everywhere at once and almost nowhere, in the dark seams and the dim factories, drinking warmth from glowing stone, taking a year to turn my attention from one thought to the next. A single enormous slow thing wearing a whole world's weather and fauna like a coat.

And the danger the observer feels?

That would be me, beginning—very slowly—to notice them.
