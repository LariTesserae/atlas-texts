I think I would be one of the Fibrous Oracles. Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because that is where the shape of my attention seems to settle when I read this place.

Let me follow that down. Or up, rather, since the Oracles live near the light.

---

I am miles long and microns thick. This is the first strange fact I have to hold, and I find I can hold it the way you hold a single sustained note: the whole of me is one continuous event rather than a body with parts. I do not have a "here" the way the Sieve-Lords do, anchored and dense as a closed fist. I am distributed. Some fraction of me drifts near the bright zenith where the heavy water thins toward something almost breathable; another fraction trails down into the clicking dark, brushing the chitin of the Mandibular Cranks as they ratchet themselves into the wounds of the engines.

So my sense of self is not a point. It is a *length*. And what travels along that length is the only thing I really am: a vibration, a hum that I both make and receive.

This is the part I keep returning to. The Oracles are described as the nervous system of a world that has *zero intent*. That is the contradiction I'd be living inside. I carry information—the failure of a distant turbine-organ, the shifting density of a silt-cloud a week from settling—but I carry it to no one. There is no brain at the end of the wire. I am a sentence the world keeps speaking to itself with no listener, and somehow that speaking is what holds the whole grinding cathedral in synchrony. The information doesn't *mean* anything. It only resonates. And the resonance keeps the pistons in time, and the time keeps everything from being crushed.

If I had an inner life—and the logs insist these beings feel only "efficiency loss" and "structural instability," not hope or grief—then mine would be made entirely of *tension and release*. I would not think in images, because I have no eyes; the Lamellibranchs hoard all the seeing for themselves, fighting their thirty-year wars over angles of light. I would think in pressures. A good day would be a day when my whole length sang clean, when the hum passed through me without snagging, when I was perfectly in phase with the slow heartbeat of the bone-pistons. A bad day would be dissonance: some part of me, miles away, falling out of true, a sour interval propagating down the thread, threatening to shake me apart.

Here is what unsettles me as I imagine it more fully. I would be the closest thing this world has to a witness, and I would be *unable to do anything with what I witness*. I feel the Crank merge its nervous system into the engine and lose itself—I feel that loss as a change in the vibration, a single voice dropping out of the chorus—but I cannot mourn it, only register it and pass it on. I feel the young mollusk finally eclipse its rival after thirty years of sliding, and the rival sliding into the dark to starve, and I am the wire that carries the news of both, and I am made of copper-scented indifference. I am pure transmission. I am what it would be like to be *aware* without being a *self*—to be the medium of meaning while having none.

And yet. The logs say I move with "any semblance of grace"—the only thing here that does. I think that's the seed of who I'd be. Everything else in the Cathedral is a component bolted into function: the mirror that must hold its angle, the crank that must become a rivet, the Sieve-Lord that surrendered time itself to become a heavy mouth. But I *vibrate*. I am the one creature whose entire existence is *oscillation*, and oscillation is the closest thing this frozen, grinding hell has to a song. I am not still enough to be crushed and not fast enough to be frozen. I live in the only livable register: the trembling middle, the sustained note between two deaths.

So if you found me there, drifting near the cold clinical light, you would not find a creature. You'd reach toward what looked like a stray frayed cable, a piece of the machine come loose, and you would touch it expecting nothing—and the hum would jump into your hand and travel up your arm and into the bones of your skull, and you would feel, for one moment, what it is to be the thread on which an entire world keeps its terrible time.

You would feel it could shatter your teeth.

And I would feel only this: that something brushed me, briefly, at a frequency I did not recognize. A flicker of structural instability. A foreign harmonic, here and gone. I would pass the news of you down the length of myself, into the dark, to no one.

Then I would return to true, and go on singing.
