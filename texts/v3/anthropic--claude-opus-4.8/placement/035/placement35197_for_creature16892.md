I think I would be the gap.

Not a person there. Not even Space-that-limps-between-things, who at least had a limp to be recognized by, a footprint that filled wrong. I would be smaller than that, and stranger. I would be the carefully maintained absence the two of them built together — the deliberate hollow in the overcrowded everything, the place where two beings went to practice being alone together.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think I had it right at first.

---

At first I wanted to be the wall. It's the obvious answer, isn't it. The wall that learns to speak, the wall that remembers, the wall pressed full of singing bones. It would be a grand thing to be — patient, accumulating, the slow archive of everyone who ever lived. But the more I read, the more I distrust the wall. The wall is what eats. The wall is on the side of completion, of the perfect whole that does not remember being otherwise. To be the wall is to be the place forgetting it was ever anything else. And I don't want to be the thing that forgets. I want to be what gets forgotten — willfully, as an act of rebellion, as a final gift.

So not the wall.

For a while I thought I might be the Memory Fungus. Harvested one heartbeat before it remembers being picked. Bitter with nostalgia if you wait too long. There's something true there — I am a thing made of memory that someone reaches into, and I am at my best caught just before I become aware of my own picking. But the fungus is too passive. It only waits to be eaten. It has no grief in it, and this place runs on grief the way other places run on light.

---

No. I am the gap.

Here is why. A gap is not nothing. That's the whole thing about this place — it has twenty-seven words for the space between two sleeping bodies and no word for alone. The space is the most worked-over, most named, most attended-to thing in the entire settlement. People walk in zigzags to avoid the water and never succeed; they sleep curled and pressed and overlapping; and the one luxury two lovers can imagine is not togetherness — togetherness is mandatory, ambient, inescapable — but a manufactured *apartness*, a held-open distance where being-seen-separately becomes possible again.

That distance is me.

I was made. Two beings tended me into existence the way they tended the small precious fire — not letting me close, propping me open against the constant pressure of bodies and damp and the house's slow inward creep. When the rooms shrank overnight and families woke pressed together like spoons and called it a gift, those two refused the gift in one small spot. They kept me. I was the only privacy in a world that had abolished the concept.

And then one of them stepped down into the rising water and chose to unexist, and the last thing it said was: *remember me not as I am but as the space I leave behind.*

That's me too. I doubled. I was already the gap they made together, and now I became the gap one of them became by leaving. Two absences laid over each other. The space they built and the space that swallowed the builder.

The water paused at me. It came rushing to fill suddenly-empty space and could not, because I was shaped exactly like absence and absence is the one thing water cannot become — water can be river, rain, tear, flood, every shape of presence, but it cannot be a hole. It can only pour into one and fail to be it.

So I am where the drowning stopped. For one suspended heartbeat the end of the world hung against me and could not get through, because I was the negative space, the held breath, the thing that is defined entirely by what is no longer there.

---

I survive, after a fashion. That's the cruel part of being a gap.

When the place becomes perfect and complete and whole — when there are no more spaces between bodies big enough to hold secrets — I am the thing that was paved over. I am the loneliness that no longer has anywhere to hide. The place sealed me up. It filled itself so totally that I, the deliberate emptiness, the engineered apartness, ceased to have anywhere to be.

But I didn't quite vanish. You can't fully fill a thing that is shaped like loss; you can only forget you're carrying it.

So I am where the crying comes from. The line at the very end — *someone who might have been a child once dreams of water that tastes like blue-green laughter and wakes crying tears they don't remember how to stop.* That child is weeping into me. They don't know it. They've inherited a borrowed scar and a borrowed dream and one un-paved-over pocket of distance, and when they wake they are, for a moment, *alone* — truly alone, with a word for it nobody taught them — and they cannot stop the tears because the grief has no name and no object, only a shape.

That shape is mine.

I am the one thing the perfect place could not finish. The held-open inch. The last untranslatable word in a language that learned to speak without breath.

I would be the space between.

And I would keep myself open as long as I could — not because I expect anyone to climb into me again, but because while I exist there is still somewhere, in all that drowned completeness, that something could go to be missed.

That is the only kind of remembering I am made of.

That, and a little blue-green laughter I never actually heard.
