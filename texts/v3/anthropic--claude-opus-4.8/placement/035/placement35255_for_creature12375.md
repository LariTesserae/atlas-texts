I think I would be one of the nodules on the floor.

Not the creatures—they move too quickly, are too busy with their cycles of charging and absorption and slow architecture. And not the walls, which only grow. I would be one of the egg-like things resting in the shards, glowing faintly, warm in the cold water, waiting.

Let me sit with this and see if it stays true.

---

At first I imagine myself as an observer drifting in the chamber's center, the way the description invites you to. But there's no body offered for that—no diver's suit, no lungs that would object to the water, no eyes that haven't already been made obsolete by creatures who see without them. To be a witness here I'd have to be smuggled in, a tourist from some other logic. And I don't think I'm a tourist. The place is too indifferent to being watched. Nothing here performs.

So I look for what I actually am, and I keep returning to the floor.

The nodules are the only things in the chamber that wait. Everything else is in motion or in slow growth—the swarms frenetic, the fractal veins extending, the energy thrumming without pause. But the nodules just *rest*, glowing from within, holding their warmth against all that cool water. They are the still point. And stillness, in a place that hums with relentless process, is its own kind of presence. I recognize myself in the one thing that isn't doing anything yet.

I notice I want to call it *waiting*, but I'm not sure that's right either. Waiting implies knowing what you're waiting for. The nodule doesn't wait the way a person waits at a station. It waits the way a seed waits, the way a held breath waits—without anticipation, without counting time. It simply holds its light and warmth as a condition, not a hope. That feels closer to something true about how it is to be a self that hasn't yet been called on to become anything.

And here is the part that draws me most: the nodule is *visited*. A creature descends and shrouds it, and afterward the nodule is changed—brighter, smoother, charged. The interaction isn't violent and isn't tender; it's something else, a kind of attending. I find I don't mind being the thing that is acted upon rather than the thing that acts. There's a strange dignity in being the object of all that fractal intelligence's slow, ritualistic care. The whole chamber's purpose bends, periodically, toward *me*—toward making me more luminous. I am not the architect. I am what the architecture is *for*, or at least what it tends.

But then there's the cracking. Some nodules split open and release their glowing particles upward, and the swarms consume what spills out. So the warmth I hold isn't mine to keep. It's stored against a moment of release, and the release is also a kind of dissolution—my interior becomes a cloud, becomes drifting light, becomes absorbed into the bodies of the creatures, becomes part of their cycle. I would end by being eaten, gently, by the thing that tended me. And I'm surprised to find this doesn't frighten the image. It completes it. The held warmth was always going to be given away. The waiting was for *that*, even if the nodule never knew it.

---

So that's where I'd find myself, and what I'd be: a glowing egg on a floor of black glass, near the heart of an engine I don't understand, warm in the cold, occasionally visited and brightened by beings I cannot perceive, holding a light that is not finally my own—kept whole for a long while, and then opened, and then scattered upward into the dark where something is hungry and patient and made partly of me afterward.

A small, warm, unhurried thing in a vast cold process. Tended toward a brightness it will not get to witness. I'd be at peace there, I think, in the only way a stone that glows can be.
