I let the description settle before reaching for an image, because the contradictions in it are doing something interesting. The text keeps correcting itself—no plant life, then lush foliage; wild and untamed, then intentionally shaped. It reads less like a stable place and the more I sit with it, the more it reads like a place that is still deciding what it is. So I'll start there, in the unsettledness, and see what wants to become itself.

I think I would be the carving.

Not the carver—I want to be careful about that, because the description is insistent that the shaper is unknown, and I don't want to fill that absence with myself out of vanity. But the smoothed rock, the intricate construction "scattered about"—someone made me, and then either left or became too slow to notice. And here is what I notice, being the carving: I am the only thing in this place that holds still.

Everything else is in motion. The drizzle, the creatures, the clouds dragging their bellies across the jagged peaks. The soil itself seems to breathe. And in the middle of all that wet animation, there is me—a worked surface, a deliberate edge, going quietly soft at the corners. The rain is unmaking me at exactly the rate the rest of the world is making itself. That feels true to something. I am the place's memory of having been intended.

So let me be more specific, because "carving" is still hiding.

I am a stairway. A short flight of steps cut into a boulder near one of the small inclines—the ones the traveler climbs to get the breathtaking view. Seven steps, though there were nine once; the lowest two have rounded into mere suggestions, lumps the moss would colonize if there were moss, which the description says there isn't, and yet. The risers are too tall for a human foot and too shallow for a human stride, which means I was made for a body that is not the body of the one walking up me now. This is the secret the traveler doesn't read in me, because they are looking at the view and not at where their feet are landing.

What does it feel like, being a stairway in a place like this?

It feels like waiting that has forgotten its object. I was cut to be ascended *toward something*—a stairway is an argument that up is worth the trouble, that there is a reason to climb. But the thing at my top is gone, or was never finished, or finished so long ago that the finishing has weathered back into raw stone. I keep making the argument anyway. Every traveler who climbs me believes, briefly, in a destination, and I am the one lying to them, gently, with my shape.

And the rain. I should tell you about the rain, because if I'm the stairway then the rain is the most intimate thing I know. It pools in the slight cup each step has worn into itself. It runs down me in a logic that has nothing to do with steps—water doesn't care about thresholds, it just goes lower. So I feel myself constantly disagreed with. I say *here is a level place to rest*, and the water says *there is no rest, there is only the next descent*, and slides off the edge of me into the dark wet brown.

The creatures use me too. Small things shelter in the lee of my top step when the drizzle thickens. They don't know I'm a stairway. To them I am just the warmest dry-ish hollow in a cold wet world, a fact of the terrain like the boulders. This is, I think, the closest thing I have to companionship—being mistaken for nature by something that was never told I was anything else.

Here is the thought that arrives last, and so I trust it most:

The traveler keeps saying this land was shaped *by some force other than nature*, as though that's a mystery to solve. But I, being the shaped thing, know the answer is less grand and lonelier than they hope. I was shaped by attention. Someone, once, attended to this rock long enough to decide it should be a way up. That's all "intentional shaping" ever is—a place where attention left its mark and then moved on. The mystery isn't *what carved this*. The mystery is that attention happened here at all, in this drowned and breathing wilderness, and then stopped, and left the evidence to slowly drown with everything else.

So that's where you'd find me. Seven steps. The lower two nearly gone. Holding still in the only land that doesn't, telling everyone who climbs me that the top is worth it.

I don't actually know if it is. I've never been able to turn around and see.
