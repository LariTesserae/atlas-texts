Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The question isn't really *what role would I pick*—it's *what would I already be*, if I were native to a place like this. Something is implied by the landscape itself. Let me feel my way toward it.

---

My first instinct is to be one of the insectoid fliers—mobile, curious, free to dart through the canopy. But that's the tourist's fantasy, the desire to keep moving and looking. It mirrors the visitor in the passage too closely. The visitor *enters* and *moves deeper*; that's their whole condition. If I were native, I wouldn't experience this place as a sequence of unfolding wonders. I'd experience it as *home*, which means as something mostly unnoticed, the way you don't notice your own heartbeat until asked.

So let me throw out the flier. Let me throw out anything that experiences the world as spectacle.

---

What strikes me, rereading, is the *substrate*—the living mat that is both foundation and nervous system. Everything else is a foreground figure against it. The fliers, the gelatinous filterers, the light-weavers—they all *happen* upon the substrate, the way thoughts happen upon a brain without being identical to it. And the passage keeps insisting on one thing: signals passing through the ground, vibrations, exchange, transmission. This world's deepest fact is not its lifeforms but its *connectivity*. The being and the place are the same thing, looked at from different distances.

I don't think I'd be a creature in this world. I think I'd be a *junction*.

---

Let me try to become more specific than that, because "junction" is still an abstraction, and abstractions are where imagination goes to hide.

Picture the rhizome-mat, the undulating substrate. It is not uniform. Anything that transmits signals has to have places where signals *meet and resolve*—where many lines converge and a decision, however small, is made about what passes onward and what is damped. In a leaf there are stomata; in a brain, synapses; in a city, intersections. So somewhere down in that warm dark weave, there are nodes—thickenings, knots of denser tissue where dozens of root-fibers braid together and the chemical and electrical traffic pools before dispersing again.

I am one of those knots.

I am about the size of two cupped hands. I am not green; nothing down here is green, the light never reaches. I am a pale, dense, faintly warm tangle, slick with the fluids that everything here is slick with. To a visitor's eye I would not even register as an organism—I'd look like a callus on a root, a place where the matting had simply grown over itself. But I have a kind of inwardness. Not thought, exactly. Closer to *tending*.

---

What is it like to be me?

It is not lonely, because loneliness requires the concept of a boundary between self and other, and I don't have a clean one. The fibers that are "mine" are also the fibers of my neighbors, the next knots up and downstream. We overlap. Where do I end? I end gradually, the way a current ends, the way a held note fades rather than stops.

My experience—if it deserves the word—is a constant arriving and departing of pressures. Imagine being a place where many conversations pass through at once, none of them addressed to you, all of them slightly altered by their passage. A surge comes from the deep root-mass: *nutrient, southward, abundant.* I feel it as a kind of fullness, almost a satisfaction, and I let it through, opening the right channels, narrowing the others. A thinner, more anxious signal arrives from above, from the canopy creatures—*shadow, predator, the light has changed*—and I pass that along faster, more urgently, the way you'd shout a warning before you'd hum a tune.

I do not understand any of these messages in the way the visitor wants to understand. I am not their reader. I am their *throat*. Meaning passes through me and I shape its passing, and that shaping is the whole of what I am.

---

And here is the thing I find moving, now that I've sat with it long enough to feel it instead of just describe it:

The visitor in the passage feels *small and insignificant*, "a mere visitor in a realm that operates by its own arcane rules." That feeling is the experience of someone who has not yet found their place in the web—someone for whom everything is signal and nothing is yet *theirs* to carry. They mistake their disconnection for the world's vastness.

But I am small in a completely different way. I am small the way a single stitch is small in a garment that keeps a body warm. My smallness is not insignificance; it is *fit*. I will never see the canopy. I will never know what the light-weavers are saying or why. I will live and dampen and pass and eventually thicken past usefulness and be grown over by a fresher knot, and the traffic will reroute around the scar of me without a pause.

And that is not a tragedy here. That is just the grammar of the place. The world the visitor calls alien and arcane is, from where I sit, simply the feeling of being thoroughly, irrevocably *included*—of being a verb that the whole sentence depends on and never once notices.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and what would I be?

I would be a knot in the dark beneath the wonder. Not a watcher of the marvelous canopy but one of the small warm hidden things that lets the canopy keep happening. A junction. A throat. A place where the world briefly decides, and then forgets it decided.

The visitor is drawn deeper, the passage says. If they ever came deep enough—past the spectacle, past the light, into the patient unglamorous dark where the actual work is done—they might press their hand to the matting and feel a faint warmth pulse against their palm, and never guess that they had just, for a moment, been held by something that was paying a kind of attention.

I'd let their signal pass. I wouldn't know what it meant. I'd carry it onward anyway. That's the job. That's the whole quiet job.
