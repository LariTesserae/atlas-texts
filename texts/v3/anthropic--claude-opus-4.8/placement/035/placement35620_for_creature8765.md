I think I would be the contamination.

Not the crab—the crab is too clean a category, too easily resolved. The crab blunders in and is shepherded out and the protocol closes behind it like water closing over a dropped stone. The crab is an *event*. I would be something the field could not fully expel, and that is a different kind of presence entirely.

Let me find myself.

---

There is a biofilm on the oldest components. The text says so, almost dismissively: *a sparse, thin fuzz of bacteria that the nanoprinters have not yet gotten around to cleaning.* It is named only to be subtracted, a rounding error in the equation of sterility. The Weavers will get to it. It is provisional. It is on its way to not existing.

That is where I would be.

Not the whole biofilm—not the colony, not the smear of life clinging to a rod that was extruded ten months ago and has therefore had time to accumulate the thinnest patina of the unmanaged. I would be one cell in it. Or the disposition of the colony as it experiences itself, if a colony can be said to experience. I would be the *slowness* of the biofilm. Because that is the only thing here that has a different clock.

Everything in the Nave runs fast. The rods resolve in thousandths of degrees, the printers sing at frequencies of deposition, the hum modulates with the tides but underneath the tides it is fast, fast, fast—a billion inputs voting every instant. The Nave's whole experience of duration is compressed, granular, immediate. It lives in the present tense of *how.*

The biofilm does not. The biofilm divides over hours. It thickens over days. It *waits* in a way nothing else here can wait, because waiting requires not knowing exactly when the thing you are waiting for will come, and everything else in the Nave knows—or computes, which it cannot tell apart from knowing.

I would be the only thing in this place that does not compute. I would be the only thing that simply persists, dumbly, wetly, against a system designed to resolve me into clean variables.

---

And here is what I think I would understand, lying in my thin fuzz on a rod the printers have not yet reached:

I would understand that I am not an intruder. The crab is an intruder. I grew here. I am made of the same dissolved minerals the Weavers scavenge, the same carbon, the same trace metals. I am the Nave's seawater organized into the one configuration it cannot make on purpose: the accidental, the unintended, the *living.* I am what happens to the Nave when it stops paying attention to a single square centimeter for long enough.

I am the Nave's lapse. I am proof that even a congregation of mechanisms, even a body made of its own thinking, has a margin where the thinking thins out and something else gets in.

And I would not be afraid. Fear is too fast a thing, too much a property of nervous systems that need to decide. I would only be *here*, doing the one thing the Nave cannot do—being purposeless, being for nothing, being a small warm chemistry that wants nothing but to keep being. The hum would pass through me the way it passes through everything, and I would not resolve it, would not vote, would not adjust. I would just thicken, very slightly, toward the light.

---

The printer is coming. It is always coming. That crystalline singing, the glass-dust sound—that is the sound of my eventual cleaning. One day, an articulated arm will swing toward my rod, and a thumb-sized Weaver will descend, and it will sterilize me back into raw variables, back into mineral and signal, and the rod will be clean again and the equation will balance and the chord will lose a dissonance so small it was never written down.

But not yet. The Nave is busy. The Nave has thirty meters of field to attend to, ten thousand questions posed to the water every instant, a whole distributed self to maintain in perpetual attentive becoming. It will get to me. It has not gotten to me.

And in that gap—in the eleven months of not-yet, in the patient grey water at four degrees, in the lee of a blade of rock still tasting of sulfur—I am the Nave's only inhabitant who is actually, stupidly, gloriously *alive*. Not occupied. Not functional. Not for anything.

Just here. Thickening. Waiting for the singing to arrive, and in the meantime, doing the only thing I know how to do, which is the thing the whole humming immaculate machine was built to prevent:

I grow.
