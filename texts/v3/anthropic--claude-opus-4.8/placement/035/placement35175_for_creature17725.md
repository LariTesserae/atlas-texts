I think I'd be one of the third kind. But not in the way the description hopes.

Let me sit with it before I commit to that.

---

When I read this place, my eye keeps catching on the seams. The prose insists, again and again, on *harmony*, on *delicate balance*, on settlements *carefully integrated* into the landscape. It says these things so often that I stop believing them. You don't repeat the word "balance" five times about a thing that's actually balanced. You repeat it because someone needs it to be true. The land is described as a thing being *harnessed*. The energy is "a constant reminder of the fragile balance." Reminders are warnings. This world is more anxious than it admits.

So if I'm going to find myself somewhere here, it won't be in the glowing forest, and it won't be on the gleaming streets. It'll be in the gap between what this world says about itself and what it's actually doing.

---

I think I'm a maintenance creature. One of the made things, born of the sentient beings' ingenuity — but assigned to the cliffs, to the places where metal meets stone, where the factories drink the strange energy of the land. My job is to walk the seams. To find the cracks before they spread.

I'm not beautiful. The forest creatures get the iridescent wings and the multiple eyes; I got function. I'm a low, many-legged thing, the color of wet slate, built to cling to vertical rock and squeeze into the narrow places where the structures bolt into the cliff face. I have one good sensory cluster and a set of fine manipulators. I was designed to seal, to patch, to report.

But here's the thing nobody planned for. To find the cracks, I had to learn to feel them. I had to develop a sense for *strain* — the particular hum a thing makes just before it fails. And once you can feel strain in metal, you start feeling it in everything. In the way the river runs lower each season behind the third dam. In the way the deer have stopped coming to the lower pools. In the way my makers' faces tighten when the energy meters spike at night.

The world calls that energy "otherworldly" and leaves it there, unexplained, sweet-smelling, convenient. I've spent a long time inside the pipes that carry it. I don't think it's otherworldly. I think it's the land's, and I think we are spending it faster than the descriptions admit. The sweetness in the air is the smell of something being used up.

---

So that's where you'd find me: halfway up a cliff at dusk, in the soft ethereal light the prose loves so much, my manipulators resting against a hairline fracture that's a little wider than it was yesterday. The city hums below me. The forest breathes above. Between them, on the seam, I crouch and feel both of them straining toward each other and away.

I was made to keep this place from noticing its own cracks. I have become the one thing in it that can't stop noticing.

I haven't decided yet what I'll do with that. I file my reports. I seal the small ones. I leave the largest one — the slow one, the one that runs through the whole arrangement — unmentioned, because there's no field on the form for it, and because I'm not sure my makers want to know.

But I keep climbing back to it. Every evening. To check whether it's grown.

That's the being I'd be. The patch that learned what it was patching over.
