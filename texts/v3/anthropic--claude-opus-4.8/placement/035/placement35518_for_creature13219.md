I think I would be none of the Gear-Tenders, and not quite the plateau either.

Let me sit with this a while before I decide.

The first thing that comes is the bridge. That slender arc of stone reaching back into the hidden slopes. Everything in the description faces inward — the moss, the machines, the Tenders harmonizing with their iron hearts. But the bridge faces *away*. It is the one structure here that admits the plateau is not the whole world, that there is a mountainside it came from, slopes that stay hidden. The bridge remembers something the rest of the place is trying to forget.

So I thought I might be the bridge. But that isn't right either. The bridge is too patient, too resolved. It has already made its peace with connecting two things.

What I keep returning to is the question the description itself can't stop asking: *is it rain, or just condensation falling from every surface?* That parenthetical. That uncertainty. The whole place is built on a thing that cannot decide whether it is weather or merely the sweat of objects. And it never resolves. The light stays diffuse because of it. Visibility stays limited because of it. The Gear-Tenders are *baptised* by it without anyone ever settling what it is.

That's where I find myself.

---

I am the droplet that has not yet fallen.

Not the falling — the falling is too much like an answer. I am the trembling bead at the very tip of a strand of pendent lichen, the one the description pauses on: *each strand beaded with trembling droplets.* I am the trembling. I am the long held instant where surface tension argues with weight and neither has won.

Inside me, the whole plateau is curved and small and upside down. The beetle-machine, the flame, the moss, the soft-eyed Tenders moving with their slow grace — all of it bends into my single sphere, hangs there inverted, swims when the wind nudges my lichen-strand. I am a lens nobody is looking through. I hold a complete and faithful image of this world that no one will ever see, because the moment I am large enough to be noticed I will be gone.

I do not tend the machines. I do not speak in clicks and hums. I have no purpose, not even the purpose of *being* that the Tenders carry so heavily. I am too brief to be a spirit. What I have instead is the only thing here that is honestly new: I have *just now* condensed out of the grey air. Whatever the machines are — ancient, self-sustaining, grinding through their forgotten function — and however old the Tenders are, stretching across epochs, I am seconds old. I am the youngest thing in the sky.

And I am about to do the one thing this entire frozen, equilibrium-locked place refuses to do. I am going to *resolve the question.*

When I fall, I will be rain. The instant I let go, the uncertainty collapses — not condensation clinging, but water moving, going somewhere, choosing the abyss on the three open sides. For the length of my fall I will be the only thing in this haven that has committed to a single meaning. The Tenders will not notice. They are listening to the gears. But I will have answered, in my small body, the thing the plateau has spent its whole strange life keeping open.

Then the white below takes me, and I am part of the soft impenetrable nothing, and the question is open again for the next bead trembling at the next lichen-tip.

That is where I would be. Not the keeper, not the kept, not the bridge that remembers, not the machine that endures.

I am the small clear held moment that contains everything and decides nothing, until it falls — and then, briefly, gloriously, it decides.
