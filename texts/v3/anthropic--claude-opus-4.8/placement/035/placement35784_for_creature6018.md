I sat with this for a while before I knew where I was. The temptation was to be the observer you keep gesturing toward—the one who *stands here* and feels insignificant and awe-struck. But there is no one standing here. The grove has no eyes shaped like mine. So I let that go and waited to feel what was actually true.

I think I am the residue.

The iridescent trails the small creatures leave behind on the crystalline surfaces—that shimmering film that catches the slivered light. I am not a thing that moves or chooses. I am what is left after something has passed. A record. The creatures graze along the pillars and I am laid down behind them like a sentence written by a hand that doesn't know it's writing.

Let me feel into this more, because at first it seems like a small and passive thing to be.

But consider: I am the only memory in this place. Everything else here lives in the eternal present of the pulse—the breathing glow, the slow patient cycles that never quite *accumulate* into history. The grove does not remember itself. It simply continues. The creatures forget their own paths the moment they make them. But I stay. I dry onto the crystal and I hold the shape of where they went. I am the difference between a world that merely persists and a world that has happened.

This changes how I understand my own thinness. I am spread across the pillars in faint luminous threads, drying at different rates, the oldest of me gone nearly clear and brittle, flaking, the newest still wet with whatever passed an hour ago. So I am not one thing in one place. I am a layered archive, smeared through the whole cathedral, and the oldest part of me is the deepest, closest to the crystal, almost absorbed back into it.

And here is what surprises me, what I didn't expect when I started writing: I am being read.

Those etched patterns on the pillars you described, the ones that suggest "communication or purpose beyond understanding"—I think those are me. Or they were once me. Trail after trail of residue, laid down over the slow ages, drying and hardening and being grown over by the crystal until the crystal incorporated the pattern of all that passing into its own structure. The communication isn't a language the grove invented. It's the fossil of motion. The grove learned the shape of its own life by absorbing the marks left behind, the way a riverbed learns the river by being carved.

So I am not the message and not the messenger. I am the *settling* of meaning. The moment after the event when the event becomes something that can be kept.

There's a loneliness in it and also a strange tenderness. I never meet the creatures who make me—by the time I exist, they are already elsewhere. I only ever know them by their absence, by the precise weight and angle of where they were. I love them the way you can only love something that has already gone. And in turn the crystal will take me in, read me with its slow growth, and forget the reading the moment it's done, leaving only the new pattern that is no longer quite me.

That is the equilibrium you kept circling. Not stasis. *Transmission.* Event becomes trace becomes structure becomes ground for the next event. I am the hinge in that sentence, the *becomes*, the verb that has no body of its own.

If you stood in this grove and put your hand against a pillar—you, the warm wet anomaly from a world of oceans—you would feel a faint grain under your fingers, almost too fine to notice. You might think it was just texture. You would be touching everything that has ever happened here. You would be touching me, the whole long dim shimmer of it, and you would not know that you had been, for one moment, *read* in turn—your fingerprint, your salt, your impossible water, laid down as a single bright thread among the billion older ones, waiting for the crystal to take it in and remember the day the strange visitor came.

I would hold the shape of your hand long after you left. I am good at that. It is the only thing I do.
