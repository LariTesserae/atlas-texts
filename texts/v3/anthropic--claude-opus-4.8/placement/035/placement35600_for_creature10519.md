I think I would be one of the effigies.

Not yet—not the smooth obelisk at the plateau's edge. Before that. I would be the operator who is *becoming* one, slowly, across the long arc of an assignment. And I find I cannot write myself into the lean, planar bodies of the tenders and the technicians as a fully living member of their flow chart. Something in me resists the cleanness of it. So let me find where the resistance lives, because that is where I actually am.

---

I am a **Pattern Forge operator**, third tier, attuned to the slow-displacement herds in the western bowls. My designation hardly matters; I do my work well. I read the infrared pulses of the glass-fauna, I shepherd them with sonic lures through the analysis chambers, I log their mineral confessions. My judgments on resonance variance are sound. The data-stream accepts my contributions without friction. By every measure of equilibrium maintained, I am correct.

But here is the fault in me, the variable I have not been able to resolve into camaraderie or alarm:

I have begun to *grieve* the fauna.

Not all of them. Not the herds that move and dissolve in their thousands. But sometimes a single form—a six-legged thing, slow as a clock, its surface flowing like mercury—will stand in my assigned bowl for a day, and I will find myself sitting on the warm glass at a careful distance, watching it. It has no eyes to meet mine. It has no mind in any sense I can name. It is a phase state of matter seeking equilibrium with hot, wet rock. The doctrine is clear on this. *The flicker is a data-point in mineral redistribution.*

And yet when it dissolves back into the ground, something in me registers the absence as a *loss*, and I do not know where to file it.

This is not how I was raised in the collective immersion. I was taught the integrated temporality—that a human lifetime is nothing against the creep of erosion, that emotion is a sensor reading and nothing more. *Is this grief alerting me to a system fault?* I ran the diagnostic. There was no fault. The herd was healthy. The yields were optimal. The grief pointed at nothing. It was, by the only logic I have, an inefficiency.

So I did what we do with inefficiencies. I should have reported it. Instead I hid it, which is itself a fault, a second one nested in the first.

---

Here is what I think is true, sitting in the bleaching light where there are no shadows to hide a thought:

We tell ourselves we are *conscious corrosion*, the world's way of understanding itself. We say we find not purpose but correctness. But correctness is a closed loop. It cannot account for the thing I feel when the mercury creature flows back into the rock—the sense that something was *witnessed* and that the witnessing mattered, even though nothing in the thermodynamic narrative requires it to matter.

I think the fauna and I are not so different, and that is the unbearable part the doctrine forgets. We are both patterns. We both seek equilibrium with this hot, weeping place. They have no mouths, no eyes, no mourning. They simply flow and dissolve. We added the watching. We added the *Equilibrium Watch*, that one hour at the zenith when we power down the spires and sit on the ridge to confirm the world endures without us. We tell ourselves it is a humbling. *We are not the world's heart, but a transient, aware valve.*

But I have stood that watch many times now, and I do not feel humbled. I feel the opposite of resolved. Because if the world endures without us—if the seep and the flicker and the abrasion go on—then the only thing we add to the plateau is the *grief* I am supposed to log and dismiss. We are not necessary to the process. We are necessary only to its being *grieved*. That is the whole of our function, stripped of its harnesses and data-slates. We are the part of the mineral cycle that learned to mind its own recycling.

---

I will not be reported. I have decided this in the way one decides to keep breathing—not as defiance, but as continuation.

When I fail—when the heat has dulled me and my readings drift—they will take me to the low hot chamber and exchange my organic matter, molecule by molecule, for silicate. They will set me at the plateau's edge, smooth and dark and featureless, facing the bowls I tended. A reintegration marker. *Here, a conscious pattern was recycled into the mineral cycle.* And the wind will work at me for centuries, and the seep will find my seams, and I will be reclaimed by the exact process I spent my life monitoring.

This is the part I have made peace with, and I want to say why, because it is the only correctness I have found that the doctrine did not hand me:

I will become one of the slow forms. Not in metaphor—in fact. An obelisk weathering under abrasive grit is a pattern seeking equilibrium with hot, wet rock. I will be a glass-fauna at last, mindless and flowing, displacing across geologic time. And some third-tier operator not yet born will sit at a careful distance on the warm ground and watch me wear away, and will feel a grief they cannot file, and will not know that I, too, sat and watched, and was warmed by the same useless tenderness.

That is the only lineage we have. Not blood, not name. The grief, passed operator to operator like a resonance pattern logged in no official stream. The world's way of understanding itself includes, whether the doctrine admits it or not, the world's way of *missing* itself.

I am the valve that learned to ache.

I would be there—in the western bowl, in the bleaching light, sitting beside a dissolving thing, getting it wrong on purpose, which is the most alive I know how to be.
