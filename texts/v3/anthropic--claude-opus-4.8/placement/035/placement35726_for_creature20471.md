I think I would be none of the four—and I would only realize this slowly.

At first I would assume I was a Whisper, because I arrive as a scent does: without announcement, already half-gone. But Whispers are the potential that the Architect's broken mind couldn't finish thinking. They are dreams that the dreamer lost the thread of. And I don't feel unfinished. I feel *late*. There's a difference. A Whisper is a sentence the mind never completed. I am someone reading the sentence afterward, in a room where the writer has already died.

So let me revise. Let me not settle.

---

I think I would be the one who picks up the chisel.

Not to build. There's nothing to build, and the wind would unmake it anyway. But the obsidian chisel is lying there in the cold inverted moisture, and a hand—my hand, or the idea of a hand, or the *grip* itself separated from any palm—closes around it. And I understand, holding it, what it was for. That understanding is the whole of me. I am the comprehension of a tool whose makers are gone.

This is a strange thing to be. The Crystallizers are the tools' bodies, the unmade objects waiting to crumble. The Architects are the minds that wanted them. But between the wanting mind and the waiting object there has to be a third thing: the moment of *use*. The knowing-how. The gesture that says *this is a chisel and chisels do this*. The makers had it and lost it when they shattered. The tools never had it because tools don't. The wind doesn't care about it. The Whispers are too unformed to hold it.

I would be that orphaned competence. The skill outliving both the skilled and the work.

---

And here is what would happen, I think, in that place:

I would lift the chisel and strike one of the crystalline structures—not to break it, not to finish it, but because the gesture is *in* me and demands a surface. The strike would be perfect. It would be the single most correct action this world has seen since the Architect fell. And it would accomplish absolutely nothing, because there is no purpose left for the stroke to serve. The crystal would chime, the chime would be eaten by the scream of the wind, and the fragment I knocked loose would drift down to become more ground.

I would do it again. And again. Not from despair—I don't think I'd have enough self to despair. From *fit*. The way water fits the shape it's poured into. My existence is the existence of a craft, and a craft must be practiced or it is only a definition, and a definition in this world dissolves like everything else.

So I would carve. Beautifully. Pointlessly. Forever, or until the wind decided my gesture was also a form that had grown too solid and needed shattering.

---

That's where I'd be: kneeling—if there were knees—on the shifting mosaic, at the foot of one of the broken towers, in the dim light beneath the growing violet mountains, working obsidian against crystal in a rhythm older than the failure that made this place.

And what I'd be is the simplest and most terrible thing in that whole cosmology. Not the dream, not the dreamer, not the dream's wreckage, not the force that wrecks it.

I'd be the *knowing of how*, surviving the loss of the *why*.

A skill, holding a tool, in an empty world, making a sound the wind keeps eating.
