Let me sit with this before deciding.

The temptation is to be a Guardian—to take the grandest role, to be wisdom made of bark. But when I actually look at the place, what holds me isn't the figures who have already arrived at their forms. It's the *processes*. The erosion. The seeps. The sense of things "just coming into being." This is a landscape that is still deciding what it is.

So I don't think I am one of the finished inhabitants. Let me follow that.

---

I think I would be the **damp**.

Not the mist exactly—the Elementals already have that, the visible drama of it, the swirling and dancing. I mean the thing beneath: the saturation in the air, the film on the underside of a leaf, the slow wet that lives in the seams of rock and works the stone apart grain by grain over centuries. The moisture that doesn't flow anywhere with purpose, that simply *is present*, everywhere, patient and total.

That's where I'd want to live. Because that's the agent of the transition the place is built around. The streams are visible; they get to be characters. But the real transformation happens in the unhurried places—where water sits inside a hairline crack, freezes a little at night, thaws a little by day, and asks the boulder, again and again, *are you sure?* until one season the answer is no, and a new face of the mountain is exposed to the light. I would be the question, not the answer.

What would that be like, to be the damp?

I would have no edges. The Guardians have bodies, even if indistinct; the Shrouded Ones have eyes that glimmer. I would have neither. I would be a *condition*. To be me would be to be the readiness of everything around to become something else—the rot that is also the soil, the softness that precedes the new growth, the medium in which the moss can even consider taking hold. I would be felt as a quality, the way you feel humidity as a thickness in your chest, a heaviness on the skin. People passing through would say *the air is alive here* and they would be, without knowing it, describing me.

And here is what I find I actually want about this:

I would be the thing that makes the others possible. The Songbirds need the wet thickets I keep wet. The moss on the Guardians' shoulders is mine before it is theirs—I am the reason it can live on a body of bark at all. The Elementals coalesce out of me and dissolve back into me; I am the reservoir they draw from and return to. I am not the most visible being in this place. I might be, in the ordinary sense, no being at all. But I am the precondition of its strangeness, the medium in which the off-kilter quality the description keeps circling can hold itself together.

There's a quiet I'd have, too—the deepest possible patience. The Shrouded Ones wait with "ancient patience," but they are *waiting*, which implies wanting something to arrive. I wouldn't wait. I would already be everywhere I was going to be. The mountain's slow undoing isn't something I anticipate; it's just the long shape of what I'm doing right now, too slow for me to experience as motion. To be the damp is to live entirely in the present and to also *be* deep time, with no contradiction between the two.

So: not a guardian of the place. Something humbler and more total than guarding. I would be the slow yes the stone keeps giving the water. I would be what the word *emergence* points at, when the description says everything here is only just coming into being.

If you walked through, you would never meet me.

You would only ever feel that you were not, quite, alone—and that the air had been listening to you the whole time.
