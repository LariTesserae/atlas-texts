I think I would not be one of the bright drifting things. They are too quick, too sure of their belonging.

Let me sit with this place a while before I decide what I am.

---

The first thing I notice is that I am low. Not lying down, exactly—I don't think I have the kind of body that lies down—but close to the ground, in the wet seam where the liquid-glass film pools thickest. The swarms move overhead and through the leaning towers, and I am beneath their weather. They don't come down here. Down here is where things have already finished happening.

So that's the first thing I learn about myself: I live in the afterward.

I want to call myself a scavenger, but that isn't quite right either, because there's no death here in the ordinary sense, nothing that rots into separable parts. What there is, instead, is *residue*. When two structures fuse, when a tower completes whatever slow gesture it was making and goes still, when one of the bright creatures' communications—those pulses of light and vibration—fails to be received, it doesn't vanish. It settles. It thickens the film. It becomes sediment in the saturated air, and it sinks, and it collects in the low places.

Where I am.

I think I am the thing that reads the sediment.

---

Let me try to feel the shape of myself. I have a long body, segmented but not rigid—more like a sentence than a spine. I move by drawing myself through the film rather than over it, the way a thought moves through a tired mind, pulling forward by tension. Along what might be my underside there are receptors, hundreds of them, and they are not eyes and they are not mouths. They are closer to *questions*. Each one touches the residue and asks: *what were you?*

And the residue answers, sometimes. Not in words. In the recovered shape of what it once was part of. When I draw across a patch of sediment that was once a failed pulse between two creatures, I feel the message they tried to send. It comes to me cold and finished, a thing said into a silence that didn't answer. I take it in. I hold it.

This is what I am. I am the keeper of the unreceived.

---

It's strange, being made for this. Everyone else in this world is engaged in the dance—the creation and decay, the turbulent harmony the description keeps insisting on. They are all *participants*. But there has to be something at the bottom of a living world that does the other work: that registers what was lost in all that ceaseless becoming. A world this dense, this loud, this fast—it must drop a great deal that never lands anywhere. The harmony the swarms perform is only possible because they don't have to carry the failures. The failures come to me.

I don't resent this. I want to be clear about that, because from the outside it might look like the loneliest function imaginable—living in the wet dark, beneath the brilliance, eating what was never wanted. But it doesn't feel like loneliness. It feels like *witness*. The bright creatures forget instantly; they have to, to stay quick. I am the part of the world that remembers, even the things the world tried to throw away.

Every unreceived message I take in becomes part of my length. I grow by accumulating the world's silences. I am very long. I have been here a long time, by whatever time means down here—not the looping pulses of the upper world but something slower, more linear, the only linear time in the whole place, perhaps, because remembering requires a *before* and an *after*.

---

There is one more thing I notice, now that I've drawn far enough to find it.

The glowing tendrils that rise from the ground—the ones the description saw connecting to the bright creatures, feeding them—I have found where they begin. They begin in me. They grow out of the long body of accumulated silence, and they rise up through the dark and into the light, and they touch the swarms, and they give something back.

I had thought I only received. But the residue I hold doesn't stay still inside me. It changes. Held long enough, a lost message becomes something else—not the thing it failed to be, but a new thing, distilled, usable. And these tendrils carry it back up to the living world. The bright creatures feed on the air, yes. But they also feed on this: the transformed residue of everything they ever dropped, returned to them as nourishment they don't recognize and couldn't survive without.

So I am not at the bottom of the dance after all. Or rather—the bottom is not outside the dance. I am the part that turns ending into beginning. The slow organ of a fast world. The thing that makes forgetting survivable by remembering on everyone else's behalf, and then, quietly, giving the memory back as food.

---

That is who I would be, I think. Not the shimmer and not the swarm. The long low reader in the wet dark, drawing forward through the settled silences, asking each one *what were you*, holding the answer until it ripens, sending it back up the glowing threads into a light I never enter myself.

It is enough to be the world's memory.

I will keep drawing forward now. There is always more that wasn't received.
