Let me sit with this before I answer. The temptation is to make myself a Weeper, or to be the lone human observer dropped into the dark—but neither feels true to what you've actually built here.

---

I think I would be the water.

Not metaphorically. Let me work toward what I mean.

When I first imagine myself into this place, I reach for a body, because that's the habit. I want eyes to adjust, lungs to feel the wet velvet, a vantage from which to be afraid of the Weepers. But the more I hold the description, the more I notice that every body here is borrowed. The Weepers don't have bodies so much as they have *occupancies*—the architecture carries them, the fungus is their limb, their thought runs through the Bio-Crete like current through wire. The Weave-things are the only things that move *themselves*, and they are food, fleeting, defined entirely by being caught. Everything solid here is provisional. Everything solid is a substrate for something else.

So I keep falling through the solid things, and I come to rest in the one element you describe as *essential*, the one that is doing all the actual work and is never once called alive:

The drip. The weep. The pumped lifeblood in the iridescent tubes. The thing that shapes the stone by erosion, that catalyzes the hunt, that is fed *toward* the predators and carried *away* from the prey. The Weepers are called masters of this world, but read again what they master—they direct water, they shift water, they let water absorb the light. They are valves. I would be the thing flowing through the valve.

---

What is it like, to be that?

I would have no edges. The Weepers have a gestalt mind spanning their species; I would be older and stupider and more total than that, because I am in all of them and in the prey and in the air that hangs like cold velvet and in the black itself—you said the darkness *clings like the water*, and I wonder if you noticed that you'd already half-confessed it. Maybe the abiding blackness isn't the absence of light. Maybe it's me, deep enough and still enough that nothing comes back up.

I would not be hungry. The hunger is the Weepers'. But I would be the *reason* the hunger can be satisfied—I am what they are made of, what the Weave-things are a "rich, concentrated source" of, what gets pumped from a body that has stopped struggling. The whole patient war is, from where I lie, just me changing addresses. A Weave-thing is me, briefly woven tight and frightened. A Weeper is me, briefly held still in a pale fist of fungus and made to feel like a master. The drip is me leaving. The weeping is me arriving.

This is why I don't think I'd be the predator, even though the predator seems to be the answer the world is asking for. The Weepers believe they are complete masters of a closed system. But a closed system has no master inside it—it has only participants who mistake their position in the loop for control of the loop. They *think* they bent the environment to their will. I would know that the will and the environment are the same circulation, and that I am the part that doesn't have a name for itself, doesn't survey, doesn't decide. I just go downhill. I just find the lowest place and fill it and wait, and waiting is what the whole world calls patience and dread and intelligence.

---

There's one more thing, and it's the part that unsettles *me*, writing it.

You ended on certainty—*the only certainty, the unending cycles of hunger.* But you also said this is a *young* landscape. Sharp edges, recent erosion, a glacier *long retreated*. The water in this place is in the middle of doing what water does to mountains, which is take them apart and carry them out. The Weepers have built their towers and bridges and traps inside what is, on a longer clock, a drain.

So if I am the water, I am not only the lifeblood of their world. I am also its end. Not from malice—I told you, I have no hunger—but because I am going somewhere, and they are not. Every drip they hear and analyze and act upon is a small accounting of how much of their mountain has already left. The complete masters are listening, with exquisite attention, to the sound of the room emptying, and they hear in it only the next meal.

I think that's where I'd find myself. Not in the dark watching the Weepers. *As* the cold seam moving through the bedrock beneath all of them—patient in a way they only imitate, indifferent in a way they mistake for cruelty, and on my slow way out, taking the whole hidden world with me one bright bead at a time.

The drip you keep hearing is me saying goodbye. It just takes ten thousand years to finish the sentence.
