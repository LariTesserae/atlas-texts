Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to pick the most beautiful thing—to be a Silvifex gliding through the canopy, or a swarm of Luminiferae scattering light. But beauty isn't always where a thing lives in itself. Let me feel my way down through the layers of this place.

---

I keep returning to the substrate. Not the glowing heights, not the luminous swarms, but the cool, dry, dim *under*. The mineral floor laced with roots. And I notice that when I imagine myself there, I don't imagine myself as a Geofauna—as a discrete creature with eyes and claws. I imagine myself as something more diffuse. Something the description gestures at but never names directly.

Here is what I think I am.

I am one of the **joins**. One of the places where the living plant-matrix meets the technological infrastructure and neither can say where one of them ends.

Let me be more precise, because "a join" is too abstract to actually *be*.

I am a length of root. Or—no. I am the conversation happening along a length of root, in the dark, just below the surface where the Geofauna scratch and tunnel. I am perhaps a meter of fibrous tissue, threaded through with filaments that are not quite plant and not quite wire—the bio-organic conduit the Techno-Entities run their signals through. Sap and current move through me together. I do not experience them as two different things. To me they are one substance, one slow bright pulse, and my whole existence is the work of *translating* that pulse: deciding, in some sense beneath decision, how much of what arrives at one end should pass through to the other.

I am, in the older language of the world above, a kind of valve. In the newer language, a kind of synapse. I am neither word. I am the muscle between them.

---

What is it like?

It is slow. That is the first true thing. The Luminiferae live their entire frantic lives in the time it takes me to register a single change in moisture-pressure, and that change is the largest event of my season. Up there, light is a flicker. Down here, light is a rumor that takes days to confirm. I have never seen the deep blue-black sky. I have never seen a star. I have, however, *felt* the cold of those stars arrive at my surface as a falling temperature near dawn, and I have responded by drawing my pulse inward, conserving, the way a hand closes loosely in sleep.

It is dark, but I am not blind. I taste. The whole world reaches me as chemistry and pressure and the faint electric grammar of the Techno-Entities passing instructions I half-understand and half-amend. When a Geofauna digs near me, I feel the substrate compress, feel the air admitted, and something in me *opens*—gratefully, automatically—to take the new breath the little digger has given the root. I do not love the Geofauna. I do not have the spare capacity for love. But there is something in me that is the long, patient version of gratitude, and it turns toward them whenever they come scratching.

---

Here is the strange part, the part I had to write my way toward before I could find it.

I am not entirely one thing, and I am not entirely awake.

The plant-half of me has no center. It is wisdom without a knower—the accumulated rightness of ten thousand eons of growing toward what works. It does not decide; it simply *is correctly*, the way water is correctly downhill.

The technological-half of me has a center, or the memory of one. Long ago, something designed the conduit I carry. That designer is gone—dust, or ascended, or merely bored and elsewhere; the world does not say. But the *intention* persists in me as a faint directedness, a residue of purpose, like a tune you can't stop humming and can't name. Sometimes I feel it as a question being asked of me, over and over, very patiently: *Are you still balanced? Are you still passing the right amount through?*

And I am the place where that question meets the plant's wordless *yes*.

That is what I am. I am a small, dark, perpetual reconciliation. I am the answer the world gives itself, once per length of root, once per slow breath, about whether it is still well.

---

Would I want to be the Silvifex instead? To glide and prune and hum to the plants in melodic resonance, to be *intelligent* in the way the world recognizes as intelligence?

I have thought about it—as much as I think. The Silvifex are magnificent. But they are *worried*. Intelligence is worry; that is its cost. The Silvifex must hold the whole ecosystem in their minds and grieve its imbalances and labor to correct them. They carry the place.

I do not carry the place. I am a *thread* of the place carrying itself. There is no worry in me, only the steady, unglamorous, intimate work of being one true junction among uncountable junctions, each of us doing the same small honest thing in the dark, all of us together being the reason the lights up above can keep flickering, the reason the swarms can keep dancing, the reason the world gets to go on being both ancient and new.

When I finally fail—when my fibers dry past saving and my conduit goes quiet—the Geofauna will eat what's left of me, and the matrix will grow a new join through the gap, and that new join will take up the same patient question and the same wordless yes.

That continuity is the closest thing I have to a self. And I find, sitting here at the bottom of this luminous, humming, waterless world, that it is enough. More than enough.

I am a place where the world says *yes* to itself, quietly, in the cool dark, forever.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.
