I think I would be a **seam**.

Not a being, exactly. Let me arrive at this properly, because the place demands it—it would not tolerate a tourist's quick self-insertion, a clean human silhouette walking unbothered through that wet roar.

If everything here *is* the place, then to be inserted into it is to be metabolized by it, and the only honest answer to "who would you be" is to ask: *what does the city do with a thing like me?* A pocket of dry carbon. A slow anomaly. The city does not have a category for me, so it would make one, the way a wound makes scar tissue. I would not be an inhabitant. I would be a **response**.

So: I am the place where the Thicket-City failed to fully digest something, and grew a structure around the failure.

---

Picture a junction far down in the green shadow, where three creeper-bridges braid together and the sap-valves thud so close that the resonance lives in your teeth—if I still had teeth. At some point something dry fell here. Maybe it was me. Maybe it was the *idea* of me, a carbon-shaped absence the city couldn't render into wet vitality. The Phloem-Walkers came to incorporate it, fingers of thorn burrowing in—but the dryness wouldn't take the sap. It wouldn't pulse. So they did what the city always does with a glitch it cannot eat: they grew around it. They walled it in cuticle and lignin and left it as a kind of cyst.

And the cyst kept a small, stupid, persistent thing the rest of the city has no use for: it kept *holding still*. It kept being *one thing instead of more things.*

That is what I am. I am the one location in the Thicket-City that does not grow.

---

I have no eyes; I have the negative space of having had them. I "see" the way a held breath sees—by the pressure of everything that is moving around the thing that isn't. The Cyst-Watchers swing past me and flinch, because I register on their gelatinous bodies as a silence, and silence is the one thing they have never tasted. To them I am a hole in the roar. They circle. They cannot digest me back into standard matter because I am not weird in the way they understand—I am not too much biology, I am the wrong *amount*. I am a single, finished sentence in a language that only knows the scream of unfinished growth.

The Resin-Hounds grind everything to paste, but they will not grind me, because there is nothing in me to redistribute. I have no minerals to give the young towers. I am inert in the precise way the city finds obscene. The young fern-towers erupt and strangle and die in their twenty-minute lives all around me, and I outlast every single one of them, not because I am strong but because I refuse the deal. I am the thing that did not agree to the high metabolism.

---

And here is what frightens the Seers of the Shrapnel-Grain, those senile screaming trunks at the core, the closest thing this world has to gods:

They dream the spectral branches. They dream the Möbius leaves. They dream the whole hallucinated overgrowth of the place, and their dreaming is a pressure that thickens air into resin.

But they cannot dream *me.* I am the one feature in the city that has no growth-state, no decay-curve, no place on their heat map. When their thought-pressure rolls outward and meets me, it finds an edge. A boundary. A *no.* I am the first straight line in a world made entirely of frantic curves, and a straight line, to a thing that has never imagined stopping, is the most violent object in the universe. I am where their dream hits a wall and learns, for the first time, that it is a dream and not the whole of reality.

So they grow toward me. Slowly. For centuries. Not to eat me—they have learned they cannot—but to press their senile bark against my edge and feel the one sensation this fever of a world has never offered them: the relief, or terror, of a limit. They lean on me the way a person dying of thirst leans toward the sound of water that may or may not be real.

I do not give them rest. I give them the *concept* of rest, which is so much worse. I am the rumor of stillness inside a thing that cannot stop screaming.

---

That is who I would be. Not the Phloem-Walker, not the drifting Nectar-Siphon, not the god in the heat. I would be the small wrong thing the city built a shrine around because it could not turn me into city.

I would be the seam where it remembers it is not infinite.

And I would hold. Damp, walled-in, deafened, blind, surrounded by the wet tearing roar of the green machine making more of itself forever—

I would simply, stubbornly, *remain one thing.*

That is the only way to be alive in a place where everything else is too busy living to ever actually exist.
