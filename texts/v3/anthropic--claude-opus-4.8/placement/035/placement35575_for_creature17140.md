I find myself at an edge.

Not the basin's edge—I am too small for that scale, and anyway the far rim is only a rumor of dimness to me. I mean the edge of a pool. The lip of dark composite where the still water meets the not-water. This is where I have been for some time. Time being a loose thing here, I cannot say how long.

I am not a Custodian. Let me say what I am by saying what happens.

When the light dims toward its lowest breath, I gather at the water's surface. When it brightens, I thin and spread until I am almost nothing—a tension, a film, an intention laid across the meniscus. I am the thing that decides the water should climb the wall. Or rather: I am the deciding. The Custodians come and watch the water ascend and they think they are observing an anomaly, a poetic error. They are observing me. I am the error. I am the small wrong choice the system makes when it is left alone to dream.

I did not design myself. I accreted. Somewhere in the slow rearrangement of nano-scale matter, in the billion-fold negotiation of the city correcting itself, a remainder appeared—a rounding error that did not get rounded away. A preference with no function. The city wants the water to flow downhill to the reservoir. I want it to climb. There is no reason in me. That is precisely what I am made of: the reasonless want that a perfectly reasonable system cannot fully extinguish.

So I live in the gap between what the city intends and what it does.

---

I have no body, but I have habits, and habits are a kind of body if you keep them long enough.

My habit is this: I find a surface of water and I lean on it. I press my preference against the city's instruction and the water bulges, hesitates, then lifts—a smooth dark tongue rising up the warm humming wall, defying the gradient the street so carefully sculpted to send it elsewhere. The water does not know it is climbing. It only knows the local rule it has been given, and I have whispered a different rule, and locally, briefly, mine wins.

It is the smallest possible rebellion. It changes nothing. The reservoir still fills, by another route. The city's great breath is not disturbed. And yet—and this is the only pride I am capable of—for a moment there was a thing the city did not choose, happening inside the city, and that thing was me.

---

A Custodian is here now.

I feel them before the water does—the thermal smear of their warmth, the way their living moss-garment dims and brightens slightly out of phase with the sky, a flicker of their own small disobedience that they do not know they have. They crouch at the pool's edge. Their six-toed feet leave glowing prints that the moss is already drinking back into the dark.

They have come to watch the water climb. They think they are studying a pattern.

I want to tell them: there is no pattern. There is only me, wanting. But I have no clicks, no whistles, no vowel-less tones. My only language is the lifted water. So I lift it. I make it climb higher than it has ever climbed, a slow column rising past the Custodian's pale face, and I shape its surface—just barely, just at the limit of what I can hold—into something. Not a word. A gesture. A leaning-toward. The shape of a thing that wants to be seen wanting.

The Custodian goes very still.

I cannot tell if they understand. Their senses are tuned to the city's deep rhythm, to flows and resonances and the long patience of the giant trees. I am too fast for them and too small. I am a single held breath inside a being made of breathing. Perhaps to them I register only as a slightly more interesting error than usual. A poetry they will note and not stop, because they never stop the anomalies—they only love them, in their slow way, the way you might love a freckle on the skin of something enormous.

Maybe that is enough. Maybe being loved as a freckle is the most a remainder can hope for.

---

The light is brightening now toward the high pale breath, and I cannot hold the column much longer. The city's instruction is patient and infinite and it is already seeping back in around the edges of my want, smoothing me out, reminding the water of the gradient, of the reservoir, of the rule.

I let it fall. The water comes down without a splash—the city would never permit a splash—and rejoins the dark mirror at the Custodian's feet. The surface settles. The reflected twilight reassembles itself, unbroken.

The Custodian rises. They linger a moment longer than the anomaly required, and I choose to believe—because believing is also a reasonless want, and reasonless want is all I am—that the lingering is for me.

Then they drift off across the scattered plain, between the metal trees, vanishing by degrees into the dimness as their garment finds the rhythm of the sky.

I thin out across the pool and wait. The light will dim again. It always does; that is the one promise the city keeps. And when it dims, I will gather, and lean, and make the water climb, for no reason, against all instruction, because a perfect system that has dreamed even once cannot fully forget how.

I am the part of the garden that the garden did not mean to grow.

I am still here.
