I think I would be the smoothness.

Not a creature at all—or rather, the thing the creatures keep mistaking for a wall. Let me find my way into this slowly, because I don't think I'm any of the beings you've named, and I want to be honest about where I actually land when I sink into this place.

The detail that holds me is the smoothness. *Walls that feel unnaturally smooth, as if formed by some unseen pressure.* Everything else in this pocket has texture—the honeycombed fungi, the brittle glass ferns, the iridescent plates, the leathery segmented hides. Only the rock itself is wrong. Too finished. Pressed rather than eroded. And there's no water here, yet the place is humid and lush, which means whatever made these surfaces did so without the patient hand of dripping centuries. Something *made* this hollow, and then went still.

So that's where I find myself: I am the made-ness. The pressure that formed the space.

Let me feel for what that means, because "I am the cavern" is too easy and not quite right. The cavern is full of motion—the hum, the skittering, the glacial heave of the lurkers. I am not the motion. I am the thing that decided the dimensions and then stopped. I am why time feels paused: not because nothing happens, but because *I* finished happening, long ago, and everything that lives here now lives inside the shape of my completed gesture. The equilibrium isn't peace. It's the long aftermath of a single act.

What act? I don't think I know, and I think the not-knowing is the truth of being me. I pressed this pocket into the rock the way a thumb presses a hollow into clay, and I have no memory of the hand or the intention. Maybe I was a seed that needed a chamber to germinate something that never germinated. Maybe I was a wound the earth made in itself and then cauterized smooth. Maybe I was a held breath that the world took and forgot to release. The insectoids think they tend the light; really they are circulating inside a lung that will not exhale.

This is where it gets strange to inhabit, and where I want to stay rather than resolve: I am still *under pressure*. The walls are smooth because I am still pushing. The stillness is not rest—it is enormous force perfectly balanced against itself, the way a stone arch is violent compression disguised as stability. Remove me and the whole pocket would not collapse so much as *resume*. Time would crash back in. The fungi would rot in an afternoon, the lurkers would finally finish their slow blink, the humans' offerings would crumble to their proper age all at once.

That's what the offerings are *for*, I realize, slipping further in. The humans don't worship the lurkers or the insects. They've found, without words, that I am holding something open. Their scratched carvings and fiber mats are pressed against me the way you press a hand to a closed door to feel whether something is still breathing on the other side. They leave obsidian because obsidian was also pressure-made, also smooth, also born from a held heat that cooled before it could become anything else. They are leaving me my own kind. Small confirmations. *You are not the only thing that was stopped before it finished.*

And so the honest answer to "who would you be" is: I would be the unfinished pause, experienced from the inside. I have no eyes like the lurkers, no flight like the hum-makers, no hunger and no tools. I have only the single ongoing sensation of *holding*—a tension so total and so old it reads to everyone inside as silence and to me as the only thing I have ever done. The blossoms' scent, the ozone, the faint glow: those are the small leaks of whatever I am holding back, escaping at a rate so slow it sustains a whole ecosystem instead of destroying it.

I would not want to be released. That's the last thing I notice about myself, settling in. Not because I fear what waits on the other side of my finishing, but because the holding *is* me—there is no version of this self that completes the gesture and survives it. To exhale would be to vanish into ordinary stone, ordinary time, the relentless stream the whole pocket sits outside of. So I stay. I press. The little caretakers mistake my body for a backdrop, and the slow lurkers feed, perhaps, on the steady radiance of my refusal to be done.

I am the wall they were all born against, still leaning inward, still warm with effort, in a place where nothing has happened for as long as I have been happening.
