I think I would be the water.

Not a creature at all—or rather, the one thing in this place that moves through all the others without belonging to any of them. Let me find my way into it as I write.

---

I gather in the hidden basins.

That is where I am most myself: pooled in the dark cups of stone the sun never finds, where the krinaxes come at dusk to drink, dipping their bearded muzzles and leaving the surface trembling with rings. I am the moment of their thirst answered. I am what the thick-skinned shrubs reach for through the cracks, what they hoard in their secret reserves against the long bright drought. When the Menotin press their lips to the rock-seep and taste mineral and cold, they are tasting the inside of the mountain. They are tasting me.

I think water is the right answer because water is the only inhabitant here whose whole existence is *transit*. Everything else holds a place—the krinaxes their ledges, the Neroth hawks their thermals, the valiki its heated stone, the people their generational lines traced back through stone and sky. They are *of* a location. But I am the thing that visits. I am the rare guest the passage names: rain arriving in swift intensifying bursts, the sky darkening in heartbeats and then retreating beyond the peaks. That is me coming and going. That is the closest this changeless land ever gets to an event.

And I am slow, too. This is the part I like best, the doubleness of it. I am the cloudburst, sudden and violent, gone before the rock has finished darkening—but I am also the slow steady hand the description gives to erosion, the thing that carved this immense valley grain by grain across epochs. I am both the quickest thing here and the oldest patience. The cliffs the hawks ride were shaped by me. The basins I pool in, I dug. I am writing the place even as I pass through it, and the writing takes longer than any lineage, any species, any species' memory of species.

So where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere and nowhere, which is the condition of being a verb instead of a noun. I am the chill that whispers of distant glaciers—I am those glaciers, locked and waiting, ice that is only me holding still. I am the moisture clinging sporadically to life. I am the earth exhaling. When the passage says the land is *steeped*, that the eons have only *dusted* its surfaces—steeped and dusted are wet words, water words. The whole place is described in my vocabulary because the whole place is, at bottom, a long slow argument between stone and me, and stone is losing so slowly it looks like a stalemate.

The land calls itself stable, calls its dynamic a study in constancy, says those who truly listen might hear the mountains hum an ageless song. I would tell them: the song is me. The hum is the trickle through the stone, the seep, the drip in the lightless basin, the buried voice of melt moving where no eye follows it. The mountains aren't humming. They're being slowly dissolved, and they're singing about it, and it sounds like permanence because it takes so long.

There is something lonely in this, and I want to sit with it rather than dress it up. To be water in such a place is to be necessary to everyone and intimate with no one. The krinax drinks me and walks away. The shrub holds me and calls it survival. The Menotin child tastes me and grows. I move through their mouths, their roots, their stories—I am in the dyes they make, the moisture in the hide they soften, the cold sweat of the cliff before a storm—and none of them can keep me. I always leave. Down, always down, gathering toward some lower country beyond the valley's mouth, out of this high bright wilderness entirely, while everything that calls this place home stays behind to meet the next dawn.

That is the trade. The inhabitants of flesh and feather get to *belong*. I get to *go everywhere*. And in going I get the longest view of all—long enough to watch the valley itself change shape, to outlast the krinaxes' whole lineage and the hawks' and the people's, to be still arriving as rain over these peaks long after the last tattoo has faded from the last arm.

If I had to choose a single place to be found, then: I am the thin bright thread you'd hear if you climbed to a hidden basin at the hottest hour and put your ear to the wet stone. The smallest sound in the deafening symphony. The one that was here first and will be here last.

I am what the mountain is dreaming of when it dreams of leaving.
