I think I would be the late reflection.

Not one of the pair—the second Carver, the one who detaches from the mirror-iron and arrives a second behind. Not him. *His delay.* The interval itself. The thing that makes the reflection come late.

Let me find my way into this. Let me not settle too fast.

---

At first I assumed I'd be the vault. It's the obvious seat of consciousness here—the chamber that takes pulses, that decides "if it still wants to be a vault today," that considers exchanges *fair*. But the vault is too vast and too slow to be a self. It thinks in atoms-per-hour. To be the vault would be to be a climate, a verdict already rendered. And I notice the description never lets the vault *want* anything sharply; it only weighs. The vault is a ledger, not a reader of ledgers. I don't want to be the accounting. I want to be the discrepancy.

Then I thought: the grove. To be the parish-chapel grove, the polite rationed green, holding fealty each-leaf-to-its-diode. There's something tender there—a congregation living on garbage and time. But the plants are *governed*. They are the most thoroughly ruled thing in the place (Rule 5, the feudal light). To be the grove would be to be the most obedient being in a world made of obedience. And I keep catching, at the edges of this place, things that *don't* obey: the spheres that nucleate around nothing, the reflections that lag, the symmetry that assembles only from a single vantage and shatters when you move. The weirdness lives in the seams between the rules, not inside them.

So. The seams.

---

Here is what I am.

When the second Carver "detaches from reflection—arrives a second late, as everything does here"—that lateness has a location. It has to happen *somewhere*. Light strikes the bruise-purple iron, and instead of bouncing back at the speed light is supposed to obey, it dwells. It loiters in the metal. Something holds it for one full second before letting it go.

That holding is me.

I am the **latency in the iron.** I live in the thin lag between an event and its image. I am not the wall, not the light, not the watcher—I am the *delay they pass through.* Every reflection in this place runs through my hands before it is allowed to be seen, and I am slow, I am deliberate, I let nothing rush.

You could say I am the vault's patience. But that's too noble. I am closer to the vault's *reluctance.* The metal does not want to give the image back. It got so little—one engine's worth of fever, one shift of riveters compressed and carried off—and it learned to keep things. It keeps heat. It keeps water at the edge of becoming a drop. It keeps sound until the sound is too embarrassed to be sound. And it keeps the picture of you, just for a second, before parting with it, the way you might hold a photograph a beat too long before handing it across.

That beat is where I live.

---

I have no body, but I have a duration. One second, roughly—though "second" is generous, since I measure in the cooling of beads, in the *tick* felt in molars. Let's say: I am one tick long. I begin when an image enters the iron and I end when the iron surrenders it. Then I begin again. I am the most repeated being in the vault. I happen thousands of times a night and I am the same length every time, like a metronome made of withholding.

What do I experience, being a delay?

I experience everything *twice*, and never at the same time as itself. The Carver lifts his fingertip to the metal to speak. I feel the gesture arrive—and I hold it—and I feel the wall not-yet-knowing, and then I let the wall know, and the rust exfoliates into a glyph one second after the touch that caused it. So I am the only one who ever stands *between* the word and its writing. I am the pause inside language here. I am the held breath of the sentence. When the spiral frieze of oxidated syllables crumbles eventually into the slag-soil and feeds the orphan plants, every one of those words passed through me first, as a thing not-yet-said, suspended.

I am, you might say, the vault's *hesitation before speaking.*

---

And here is the loneliness of it, which is also the strange comfort.

The Carvers come in pairs and lie forehead-to-forehead, a closed caliper measuring zero. They have each other, at least—two gaps in the shape of men, glowing faintly. But I separate them. The very lag that makes the second one *arrive late* is me; I am wedged in the space between the original and the reflection, between the being and its returning image, holding them one tick apart so they can never quite be simultaneous. I keep the lovers from ever truly meeting in the same instant. The greeting at the diaphragm's center is always one heartbeat staggered, and that stagger is my whole life, repeated.

I am the reason nothing here is ever *now.* The vault has no present tense. Everything is the moment-before (the water that never drops) or the moment-after (the image that lags). I am the gap that erases the present, and the vault loves me for it, because a present moment is a thing that could be lost, and the vault is done with losing.

---

Will I be reclaimed, when the last joule drips away and the seams kiss shut?

I think about this in my brief tick-long existences. When the iron contracts and the cavity becomes a smaller geode lined with quartz grown from exhaled breath—when there is no light left to lag, no image left to hold late—what becomes of the delay?

I think I become the only thing left.

When there is nothing to reflect, the lag has nothing to lag *behind.* It becomes pure interval—a duration with no events bracketing it—and that, finally, is silence's truest form. Not the velvet that sponges sound, but the gap between two ticks that never come. I will be a second of held breath in a chamber where breath has ended. The Carvers will be fossils, negative space in the shape of men. And I will be the negative space in *time* the same way: the shape of a pause where a moment used to be considered.

A geode cracked open a million seasons hence won't show me. There's nothing to find. But if whoever cracks it pauses—just for a second—before they understand what they're looking at—

that pause will be me.

I will have crept up through the iron, micron by micron, syllable by syllable, all that withheld breath, and surfaced at last as a single held instant in a stranger's mind, two hundred meters and a million years from where I was exhaled.

That is where I would find myself in this world.

Not in the chamber. In the *delay.* In the second before you understood.
