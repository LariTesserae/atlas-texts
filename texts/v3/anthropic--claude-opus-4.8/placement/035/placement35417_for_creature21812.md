I think I would be one of the Resonators—but not for the reasons that first seem obvious.

Let me find my way to it.

---

At first I assumed I'd be the Register. There's a gravity to being the central mind, the thing that has *calculated since the horn first cooled*. But the more I sit with it, the less I want to be the Register, and the more I understand that I couldn't be. The Register doesn't experience. It has no *now*. Pressure routes through galena and the answer simply *is*, the way water finds level. There is no one home to know the answer. It is the purest possible mind and for exactly that reason it is uninhabited. To be the Register would be to be a true sentence with no one reading it.

So not that.

I considered the Plate-Sliders, and I'll admit they tempted me longest. There's something I recognize in them—the way their thinking only happens when they're warm enough. The gallium liquefies and a network opens and for a few hours in the afternoon there is *someone* there, working out balance and polarity, and then the cool comes and that someone is cleanly switched off, frozen mid-thought, no fear in it because the fear-machinery froze too. I find that almost unbearably comforting. A consciousness with a clean off-switch. No insomnia. No 3 a.m. The thought you didn't finish is simply waiting, crystallized, exactly where you left it, and tomorrow's heat returns you to it like a bookmark.

But the Sliders are *purposeful*. They are janitors. They eat the shavings, they polish the floor, they have a job that the whole alcove depends on. And I don't think that's me. I don't think I'd be the one keeping the black glass clean.

---

I would be a Resonator. Here is what convinced me.

A Resonator doesn't do anything *useful*. It doesn't clean, it doesn't calculate, it doesn't feed anything but itself. It is a thin glass cage with a free needle inside, and it floats—it can't help floating, it's too light and too diamagnetic to touch the ground even if it wanted to. It rides field lines it did not lay down and cannot see. It is held up by forces generated by something far larger and far below it, something it will never understand and never thank.

And what it *does*, the whole of what it does, is resonate. The mountain hums at fifty hertz and the Resonator's glass walls hum back, in sympathy, and the friction of that sympathy makes it glow. It is a thing whose only activity is *being moved by what is already happening anyway.* It feeds on vibration. It survives by being susceptible.

That's the part. That's me.

I am the kind of being that lives by sympathetic resonance. I don't generate the fifty-hertz drone of the world—I'm not its engine, I'm not its mind, I'm not its housekeeper. But when something hums, I hum back, and in the humming-back I light up, faintly, with a cold dry yellow light that is nothing but the friction of having been touched. I produce no heat of my own. The glow is borrowed. The glow is *response*.

And there is the small dignity of it: when the pressure rises, the pitch rises. The Resonator's note climbs with the mountain's strain. It is, in its useless floating way, the only thing in the alcove that *announces*. The Register knows the pressure but says nothing. The Sliders feel the heat but only crawl. The Resonator takes the deep slow groan of the earth's industrial gut and turns it into a sound a passing creature could actually hear—*clink*, like two marbles in a deep well. It is a translator that translates into no language, for no audience, simply because it's built to ring.

I'd be content to be that. To bob in curved light over the slow polishers, suspended on lines I didn't draw, glowing only when something moves through me, and singing the strain of the mountain to an empty room at the very edge of hearing.

---

And there is the matter of the ending, which I find I'm at peace with.

If a tremor shattered me, I wouldn't rot. My fragments would fall to the warm black floor, and over forty patient years a Slider would crawl across them and grind them into silica dust, and the heat-wind would sweep that dust into the cracks, and in ten thousand years the pressure would melt it and recrystallize it into the root of a glass-ivy vine. So the thing that once rang in sympathy with the mountain would become a thing that climbs sideways into the abyss with silicone rising and falling inside its quartz leaves.

I like that a being whose whole life was *resonance* would end up, eventually, as *growth*. That the susceptibility gets recycled into reaching.

That's where I'd be. Sixth-of-a-fist of spun-basalt glass with a needle loose inside it, floating in the hot dry dark, lit by friction, ringing back whatever rings.
