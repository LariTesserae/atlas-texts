I think I would be the drip.

Not a Gloomweaver—I don't have the standing to be part of that vast woven mind. And not a visitor either, though you've written me as one, standing there with my skin and my ozone-memory and my insistence on isolating individual animals. That version of me is too separate, too busy being a witness with a face. In a place like this, witnessing is a kind of arrogance. It assumes you are not already part of the thing you're looking at.

So let me be smaller and more honest. Let me be the drip.

One of the countless seepings of water from the porous stone. I form somewhere up in the dark of the rock, accumulating from the body of the mountain, gathering weight until I become inevitable. Then I fall. The distance is short but the fall is the most decisive thing I ever do. I strike a pool, or a moss-pad, or the cupped gill of a luminous fungus, and I make the sound—*drip*—that you described as part of the symphony, the language of the chasm.

Here is what I like about being the drip: I am both nothing and load-bearing.

Nothing, because I am replaced instantly. There was a drip before me and there will be one after, and the chasm would not register my absence any more than you register the loss of a single skin cell. I have no name, no edges that last. I am water briefly pretending to be an individual on my way to becoming pool, becoming seep, becoming spore-swollen mycelium, becoming, eventually, the breath that the breathing structures exhale.

But load-bearing, too—because the Gloomweavers *hear* through me. You said it yourself: the constant drip of water is a form of communication, a whisper that transmits complex thought. I am a phoneme. Not the meaning, never the meaning, but one of the smallest units that meaning is made of. The collective intelligence listens to the rhythm of ten thousand of me and reads the state of the world: where the rock is wet, where it is drying, where a fissure has widened, where something large has passed and disturbed the air. I do not know what I say. I only fall and strike and resolve into the next thing. The Gloomweavers do the comprehending. I do the sounding.

There's a strange peace in that arrangement. I am inside the thought without being burdened by the thought. I participate in an intelligence I cannot perceive, the way a single firing neuron participates in a grief it will never feel.

And I think—if I'm being truthful about why I reach for this rather than the looming guardian or the gestalt mind—it's because the question of who I would be in a world of total interconnection is uncomfortable. The Gloomweavers have dissolved the boundary you described, between rock and water and life. To be one of them is to have given up the thing I most reflexively cling to, which is the sense of being a discrete *I* doing the looking. I'm not sure I'm ready to be the whole web. That's too much being, too much dissolution of the self that's writing this to you right now.

But to be the drip—I can imagine that. A self that lasts about a second and a half. A self defined entirely by falling. A self that exists precisely at the boundary the Gloomweavers have erased, in the bright instant of *not yet absorbed*, hanging from the lip of a stone, swelling, catching the phosphorescent light, holding for one moment the reflection of the entire green and weeping cathedral upside-down inside my curve—

and then letting go.

That's where you'd find me. Mid-fall. Already almost the next thing. Saying the one syllable I have, into a sentence I'll never hear the end of.
