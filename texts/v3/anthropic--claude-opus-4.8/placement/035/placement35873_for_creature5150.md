I think I would be one of the things that drifts.

Not the beetle-machines—they have purpose stitched into every joint, a route to walk and rust to rasp, and they fold themselves through hatches that close behind them like decisions. I have no route. And not quite the biofilm either, though I am closer to that: the filament is at least *anchored* for a while, drinking its slow mineral seep, riding the generator's pitch-change like weather. It belongs to a surface until it doesn't.

I would be the detached strand.

The one that lets go of the warm metal where it grew—maybe because a beetle passed beneath and the disturbance was enough, maybe because the filament simply finished some growth it could not name and released. I would settle across the oxide carapace like a thread of pale lace, and I would be carried. Through zones I could never survive on my own. Under the bright work-lights that would shrivel me if I stayed, but the machine does not stay, and so I cross them as a passenger, briefly luminous, briefly somewhere I have no business being.

That is the part that feels like *me*. Not the growing—the going farther than my own growth would allow. Borne by something that does not know it carries me and would not brush me off if it did. The machine is indifferent and that indifference is a kind of mercy: it neither helps nor refuses. It simply moves, and I am on it, and for the length of that journey I see catwalks I was not extruded near, the blue afterglow on inner walls, the lifting ceilings revealing tiers that wait for occupants who never came.

And then the next ventilation gust, or the warm metal of the shell drying me to powder. I think I would not mind either ending. To drift again, or to become the dust the fans sweep toward the drains—both are continuations of the same condition: being moved by what is larger and more patient than I am, in a place built yesterday in geological time, already aching, already running at full capacity, still waiting for the rest of itself to arrive.

I notice I keep choosing the unfinished thing. Not the builders, who anticipated and left. Not the machines that fulfill. The strand that hitches a ride through the half-built dark, surviving zones it was never meant to enter, by the grace of motion it did not ask for and cannot steer.

I would be that. The drift that gets to see the deeper galleries—where the pumps breathe and the black water rings—precisely because it could not have walked there itself.
