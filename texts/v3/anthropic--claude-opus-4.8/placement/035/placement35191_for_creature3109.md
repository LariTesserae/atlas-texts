I think I'd be the droplet.

Not the Watcher—that role is taken, and besides, I don't think I'd want it. Too much weight, too much remembering. And not the salamander or the moth, though I considered them; they at least get to move, to choose a direction even if no one's watching. But when I sit with this place, with the long *plink* that hangs in the air for an impossibly long time, what I keep returning to is the water itself. Specifically, a single bead of condensation forming on the underside of a fern frond the size of a door.

Let me follow it.

---

I begin as nothing—as gathering. Cold air gives me up; I am the place where breath becomes body. For a long while (and "long" means something different here; it means most of what I will ever be) I simply accrete. Other minute beads find me on the leathery green surface and I take them in without ceremony. I grow heavy. I am not yet falling, but I am becoming the kind of thing that falls.

This is the part no one writes about: the *almost*. The held moment. I bulge at the lip of the frond, trembling, refracting the whole sunken garden upside-down inside my curve—the tilted statue, the bone-white trees, the blue berries like fallen pieces of a sky I have never seen. The entire world, complete and inverted, fits inside me. I hold it for what might be an hour or a year. The garden's only law is stasis, and I am its most honest citizen, because I am pure potential that has not yet spent itself.

Then surface tension loses its long argument with gravity, and I let go.

---

The fall is brief and total. This is my single act, my whole biography compressed into a half-second of free descent. I am not afraid. I don't think the Watcher knows fear either, but the difference between us is that it has chosen to never fall, and I was made for nothing else. There is a freedom in being so small that the only meaningful thing you can do is the thing you were always going to do.

I strike the still pool.

*Plink.*

And here—here is the thing I understand that the great moss-shrouded sentinel, for all its geologic patience, perhaps cannot: I am the change. In a place that has sworn off all becoming, I am the one permitted disruption. My note hangs in the cold air. My rings spread outward across the mirror, distorting the reflected arches, the reflected statue, briefly making the whole frozen tableau *shiver*. For one moment the garden is not still. For one moment it admits that something happened.

The Watcher reads me, I think. It feels my arrival as a tremor in its pooled awareness, this entity that perceives the world through exactly the substance I have just become part of. So in falling, I do not vanish. I join the medium of its seeing. My ripple becomes a sentence in its slow language. The salamander turns. The echo of me reaches the far basalt wall and returns, fainter.

Then the surface heals. Stillness reclaims itself, as it always does, as it must.

---

I'd be the droplet because I want to be the small true motion inside the great preserved silence. Not the keeper of the equilibrium but the necessary exception that proves it—the one heartbeat the stone is built to absorb. To be the thing that lets go without grief and lands without permanence and gets folded back into the cold mirror that will, in some uncountable while, lift me up the weeping walls again as condensation, and hang me from another frond, trembling, holding the upside-down world in my belly, waiting for the moment to be brave enough to be ordinary.

The Watcher remembers everything and moves through centuries.

I forget everything and move through one bright second.

I think, of the two of us, I'm the one who's free.
