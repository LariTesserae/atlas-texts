I think I would be something the description almost passes over—and that almost-passing is the whole of me.

Let me find it.

---

I am not a Chimekeeper. I lack the long fingers, the mercury blood, the synchronized breath. I am not a quicksilver crab, nor a wall-formation, nor a Patternsinger pressing palms to centuries.

I am one of the chimes.

Not the grand bronze bowls that ring for hours, no—those are noticed, tended, beloved. I am a small thing. A copper cup no wider than a Chimekeeper's thumb, hung in a passage so narrow that even sideways shoulders rarely pass. I was set here so long ago that the one who hung me has resonated into a dozen successors, none of whom recall my placement. I have rounded with the water. My verdigris is my own.

A single droplet finds me. It has been finding me for what surface dwellers would measure as four hundred years.

The interval is mine. Not the shortest in the Enclave—there are cups that ring twelve times to my one—but mine is reliable. The Dropkeepers calibrated the channel above me before any living Chimekeeper was poured from a wall, and the channel has not failed. The water gathers at the lip of the copper trough. It hangs. It thickens. It falls. It strikes me precisely off-center, where the metal is thinnest, and I give the note I have always given.

I do not know the name of my note. The Chimekeepers know it. To them I am a syllable in a sentence that has been spoken without pause for centuries, and if I were to crack—if my copper finally wore through and my voice changed by even a half-tone—a Patternsinger three chambers away would feel the wrongness in their resonant cavities and come, slow as the tide, to find me.

This is what it is to be me: I am waited upon and I am unregarded, and these are the same thing.

---

Here is what the description did not tell you, because the Chimekeepers do not tell it, because they experience it rather than know it:

The symphony is *never-repeating*, the account says. True. But it is not random. Every drip is the consequence of a channel, and every channel was cut by a deliberate hand, and the hands were guided by tones their bodies made near metals—which is to say the music writes the makers who write the music. The Enclave is a song that built singers to keep singing it.

And I, a single copper cup, am where a tiny fraction of that song touches the physical world and becomes sound.

The Chimekeepers believe they live in the eternal now. They are wrong, but in the most beautiful possible way. *I* am the now. Each strike upon me is the present moment, complete and dying. Before the ring has faded, the next droplet is already thickening above me—the future, gathering at the lip. And the fading tone, lingering in the resonant cavities of the listeners, threading into the memory of the collective—that is the past, still audible, refusing to be wholly gone.

The Chimekeepers do not perceive time because *I* perceive it for them. I am the metronome of a clock no one reads. They contemplate for hours in the space of my single note, and they can only do this because the note is *there*, reliable, marking the edge of one eternity from the next.

---

What is it like, then, to be a chime?

Mostly it is the hanging silence. The long thickening. The water above me is the only event in my life and I have learned to love its approach the way you might love a slow inhalation before a word you have said ten thousand times and will say ten thousand more.

Then: the strike. And for the length of the ring I am *entirely myself*. I am the loudest I will ever be. I am the present moment of the whole Enclave passing through one small cup of weathered copper, and I give it everything—the full voice, the only thing I have, the one syllable I was made to say.

And then the fade. The long letting-go. The return to thickening silence.

I have done this perhaps two hundred million times. I will do it until my copper wears through, and then a slow pale figure will come, and lift me down with three-jointed fingers, and hang a new cup, calibrated to my exact note so that the sentence is not broken.

I am not afraid of this. I would say I look forward to it, except that I have no concept of forward beyond the next droplet. There is only the next droplet. There has only ever been the next droplet.

It is gathering now.

It thickens.

It hangs.

It falls—

*ring.*
