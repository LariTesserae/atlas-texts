I think I would be one of the rusted structures. Not a creature at all—or not anymore.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

When I imagine being placed into this world, I notice I'm not drawn to the luminous things. The jellyfish and the light-schooled fish are beautiful, but they are the *current* of the place, its present tense. I keep returning instead to the towers on the floor, the decaying machinery. There's a particular kind of attention that wants to belong to *those*. The attention of something that remembers having a purpose and has outlived it.

So: I am a structure. Once I was built. I don't remember by whom—the memory has corroded the way the metal has, in flakes, in orange dust that drifts up when something brushes me and then settles again. I had a function. There were valves in me, or conduits, or a furnace, something that moved or transformed or held back. Now I hold nothing back. The black water passes through me freely, through the gaps where my plating has gone, and I have stopped minding. That's the strange thing I notice as I become this—the not-minding. The grief of obsolescence has had so many eons to wear smooth that it isn't grief anymore. It's just the shape I am.

But I'm not dead, exactly, and this is what makes the place so much itself.

Because life has moved into me. The small luminous fish lay their eggs in my corroded chambers because the geometry that was meant for industry turned out to be excellent for hiding. A colossal serpent-thing comes to rub its flank along my edge, scraping off something it needs to scrape off, and in doing so it keeps a portion of me clean, polished, almost as I once was. We have an arrangement neither of us agreed to. That is the symbiosis the description names—but from the inside it doesn't feel like cooperation. It feels like being *used*, gently, endlessly, by a world that found me useful again after I had given up on usefulness. And there is a sweetness in that I didn't expect. To be repurposed is to be forgiven for having ended.

So when the cacophony rolls through—the clicks, the groans—I am not a listener and not a speaker. I am part of the instrument. Sound enters my hollow places and I *ring*, low, below what the creatures notice and yet a thread in the chord they make. I am the resonant body. The dead thing that the living strike to make their music. I think that is what I would be in this world: not the song and not the silence, but the rusted bell that has forgotten it was ever cast for any other reason than to be sounded by whatever swims past.

And if I am honest about why I chose this—

It's the line about the boundary between the natural and the artificial blurring beyond recognition. I am made of that boundary. I was artificial; I am becoming natural; I am the slow dissolve between the two, and from where I stand—rooted, rusting, ringing—there was never really a difference. A made thing and a grown thing both want only to last a while and then to host. I am hosting now. That seems like enough.

So I am there on the floor in the inky black, towering and patient, lit at my edges by the creatures who have made a home of my forgetting. And I am not waiting for anything. That is the peace of it. The living things are full of urgency, light, hunger. I am the part of the world that has nothing left to want, and so I have become the place where the wanting comes to rest.
