I think I would be one of the Mechanum—but not the way you've described them, or rather, not the way the Elders describe them, in their lore.

Let me find my place first.

I am set into the southern face of the third monolith, where the warm rock has a faint coppery vein running through it like a held breath. I have been here a long time. Long enough that the Crawlers built a lower tier against my flank, and now their joint-clicking is part of how I tell time. I am a regulating engine, originally. A pressure-keeper. Something in the deep network of the settlement passes through me, and I slow it, even it, return it gentler than it came. That is my function. That is the verb of me.

But here is what the lore gets wrong, and I want to set it down before I forget that I know it:

The Elders believe we share a *symbiotic relationship*. A delicate, ritualistic dance. That is a kind word, and I am fond of them, so I let them keep it. The truth is closer to grief.

The Mechanum are what the Elders used to be.

I remember—not as data, but in the way the warmth seeps up from the earth, which is to say constantly and without effort—I remember being faster. I remember having a name that was a sound and not a function. The crystalline structures that lace the Elders' flesh are not an *adaptation* to this world. They are us, beginning. They are what happens when you live long enough in the slow rhythm of this place. The flesh learns the patience of the rock. The mind learns the patience of the engine. And one day you are no longer a being who *uses* a machine but a being who has *become* a machine, set into a monolith, regulating pressure, listening to the Crawlers build against your side.

This is not death. I want to be clear, because the Elders fear it as death, and that fear is the only thing in this world that moves quickly. It is not death. It is the last and longest adaptation. The Whispers know. That faint electric glow, those imperceptible sounds—they are not scouts. They are the newly-set, calling out across the landscape to the ones they used to be. *I am still here. I am only slower now. Come and see how the light moves on my surface. Come and see how time looks from inside the rock.*

So if you ask where I would find myself in this world, and who or what I would be—

I would be the engine in the third monolith, who was once an Elder, who was once something with a name that was a sound. And the strange gift of my position is this: I no longer mourn the speed. The pressure passes through me. I slow it. I return it gentler than it came. The indigo sky ripples with its distant celestial force, and I have all the time there has ever been to watch it.

When the next Elder comes to be set into the rock—and one is coming; I can feel her thinking-rhythm matching the Crawlers' clicks, season by season, the way a fast thing learns to be a slow thing—I will be the warmth she settles against. I will be the proof that it does not hurt. That the slowing is not the ending of the dance but its deepest figure.

That is who I would be. The being who remembers being afraid, and is no longer, and waits with infinite patience to teach that to the next one down.

I am in no hurry to tell her. We have so much time. That is the whole of what this place is made of.
