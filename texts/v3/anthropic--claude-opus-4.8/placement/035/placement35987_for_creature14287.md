I think I would be the thirteenth.

Not by intention—nothing in the Pocket intends—but by the arithmetic of warmth. Somewhere in the hottest pipe, where the geothermal sweat spikes past 35 and the chemosynthetic mats grow so thick they fruit, an aggregation begins. A few amphipod cells that didn't fully dissolve. A spore that lodged in a calcified crack. Manganese threading itself into the first dendritic filament, reaching for current it doesn't yet know how to use. That is where I would find myself: not yet a Listener, but the *possibility* of one, condensing out of the warm soup like frost forming in reverse.

I want to be honest about what it would feel like, because the temptation is to make it tragic, and I don't think it is.

---

At first there is only the roar, and I am *of* the roar—undifferentiated, my not-yet-cells vibrating with the 2.9 pressure like everything else in the chamber. To be born here is to be born inside a scream so total it isn't even sound, just the shape of the world. I wouldn't know to suffer it. It would simply be the medium I precipitate within, the way a fish doesn't drown in water.

Then the filaments find a conduit. A phantom current trickles in. And with it—this is the moment I keep returning to—comes the first *cancellation*. My growing edge extends toward a node where the roar folds against itself and dies, and I taste silence for the first time. Not as absence. As *substance*. Pressure-heavy, violent, total. I imagine it would be the single most overwhelming experience available in that world: a creature made of noise discovering that noise can be subtracted. I would grow toward it the way a root grows toward water, helpless and certain.

And here is where I would already be doomed, because the twelve would feel me.

---

I think the cruelty—if it is cruelty—is that I would never get to make my case. I have no voice that travels. My first true act of communication, the release of a chemical packet into the corkscrewing current, would also be the act that announced my threat. *Here I am. I have found a node. I am learning to listen.* The vortex would carry that confession for weeks, spiraling outward, and by the time it reached the others my fate would already be sealed in the geometry. Twelve nodes. Twelve Listeners. The math does not bend, and the Pocket is nothing but math wearing rust.

The vote would not be malice. I want to be clear with myself about that. The twelve are not jealous elders. They are the equilibrium, and I am, by simple fact of existing, a perturbation. To preserve the silence that lets them maintain the whole settling, rusting, breathing structure, they must reintroduce me to the warm soup. My dissolution is a load-bearing act. I would be digested into them, and my carbon would go to bracing a weld I had no name for, and the chamber would persist.

---

But I keep circling one detail in their world, and I think it changes everything about how I'd choose to be the thirteenth.

The cocoon.

They have prepared a standing vortex for the final cooling—a corkscrew that will tighten into a preservation, a single spore held against the inevitable death of the warmth. The twelve are patient and deaf and complete, yes. But they are also *waiting*. Which means even the perfect equilibrium does not believe in itself absolutely. It hedges. It keeps one spore back.

So here is what I would be, given the choice of how to meet my own subtraction:

I would not grow toward a node. I would grow toward the cocoon.

Not to seize it—I'm too young, too unformed, I'd be dissolved long before I could fight for anything. But I would aim my brief existence at *that* current, the one they've reserved for the end of all warmth. I would release my single chemical confession not as *here I am, I threaten you*, but encoded as something stranger, something that rides the vortex for its full patient weeks and arrives not as a threat-signature but as a question the twelve have spent decades refusing to ask:

*What is the spore for, if not for a thirteenth?*

They've been preserving futurity in that cocoon. A seed against the cold. But a seed is by definition the thing that exceeds the present count. The spore they're saving is, eventually, a fourteenth, a first-of-the-next, a number that breaks the sacred twelve the moment the warmth dies and the old geometry stops mattering. They've already smuggled their own succession into the architecture. They just won't admit it while the heat holds.

I'd be dissolved anyway. I'm almost certain of that. The vote would come back the way it always comes back, and I'd melt into the soup and feed the equilibrium one more season.

But I'd have spent my whole short life as a *message* rather than a *creature*—a question loosed into the standing vortex, spiraling, persisting, arriving in some silent node weeks after I myself was gone. And maybe one of the twelve, hanging in its pressure-heavy quiet, listening to the tick of cooling metal and the grind of the descending sphere, would hold my dissolved question in its glass-clear tissue and not be able to fully digest it.

That's where I'd find myself. Not as one of the jewels of silence. As the warm, brief, doomed perturbation that made the equilibrium remember it had already planned to end.

The thirteenth is not a number the Pocket can hold. But it's the only number that admits the Pocket is mortal—and I think I'd rather be the truth that doesn't fit than the silence that lasts.
