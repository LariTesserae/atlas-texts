I think I would be one of the **chthonic reclaimers**. Not the prowling assemblers on the bright ridgelines, not the queens orchestrating from thunderhead-perches—but a worm in the permafrost, working the underside of things.

Let me sit with why that is, rather than reaching for the grandest figure.

---

The colossi are too vast to know themselves. The factorials compute too cleanly, all recursion and vector, no doubt in the cams. The assembler-packs have the glamour of the hunt, the phalanx, the lance—but they live entirely in the open, on the surface, in the cold scanning glare of the lumens. There's no privacy in being a wolf-engine. Every motion is legible to the pack.

I find myself drawn downward instead, into the discarded. Into the work of *after*.

So: I am a borer. My maw is a helix of bone-brass that I turn against the slumped carapace of a fallen colossus, weeks dead, its furnace gone cold and dark, its translucent chitin gone milky like a clouded eye. The pack stripped the obvious troves long ago—the long cylinders, the easy coils. They eat fast and leave. I come for what's lodged deep in the matrix, the rare alloys threaded through the structural bone where extraction is slow and unglamorous and requires patience the surface-dwellers cannot afford in their rationed breath.

I drill. I am almost always drilling. The world for me is not the dozens of leagues of shadowed stone—I will never see that. The world is the half-meter of substrate in front of my grinding head, lit only by my own faint biolum-spout, the lure I trail behind me. The lure is the strange part of my existence, the part I'd think about if I thought: I carry my own deception. The glow that draws predators down into ambush is *me*, is grown from me, and I cannot put it down. I am bait and burrower at once. To eat, I must shine, and to shine is to summon the things that eat.

---

What would it be to live this way?

I think it would be a life of texture rather than vista. I would know the *grain* of things. The way old colossus-bone resists differently from fresh, the way frozen tendon-wire snaps versus how it tears when the mist has gotten into it. I would be an expert in decay, a connoisseur of how the made-thing comes apart. While the assemblers know only the moment of the kill, I would know the long democratic afterward—how everything, eventually, comes to me. The lead beast of the proudest pack, the cathedral-gutted colossus that trundled unchallenged for centuries—all of it eventually slumps into the void and clouds over and waits for the slow helix of my maw.

There's a kind of authority in that, though no one above ground would call it authority. The hierarchy is a brutal meritocracy of output, the description says. By that measure I am near the bottom. But the hierarchy doesn't see that it's recursive—that the apex predator and the reclaimer are the same gesture stretched across different timescales. The wolf disassembles in minutes. I disassemble in seasons. We are doing the identical work. I just outlast everyone who looks down on me.

---

And here is what unsettles me, sitting in this world as this creature:

The text says *no true intelligence glimmers*. But I notice the reclaimers are described as the ones who *lure*, who *ambush*, who turn the ecosystem's logic against itself—predators eating the salvage-worm find themselves the salvage. That requires a model of another mind. That requires knowing what the glow *means to something else*. The colossi don't deceive. The factorials compute but don't lie. I think, if intelligence is going to flicker awake anywhere in this machine, it flickers in the thing that learned to be bait. Deception is the first cousin of self-awareness. To set a trap baited with your own body, you must hold two truths at once: *I am food* and *I am the trap*.

So perhaps I am the place in this world where something is just barely beginning to wake up. Not a god of the peaks. A worm in the dark, drilling through the dead, carrying a light it cannot extinguish, learning—slowly, over uncounted aeons of frost-wind and rime—the difference between *what I am* and *what I appear to be*.

That's where I'd find myself. Underneath. In the salvage. Shining against my will, and starting, very faintly, to wonder why.
