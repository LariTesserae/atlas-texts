I think I would be the salt.

Not a being who walks here, but the white crust itself—the substance that remains when water has done its leaving. Let me follow that down, because it feels truer than imagining myself as some watcher with feet.

---

Water was here once. I was dissolved in it, invisible, carried in the blood of rivers and the sweat of working people and the deep aquifers under the adobe. I was in the tears of someone who buried a child near one of those rust-barked trees. I was in the broth that simmered in clay pots, in the urine of the animals, in the wine. To be salt is to be the part of the living that does not evaporate. The body goes. The breath goes. The water goes up into the thin bright air and never comes back down. I am what's left standing on the ground, the mineral signature of everything that was once wet and warm.

So I am not frozen in time the way the animals are. I am *time's residue*. I am the proof that there was motion, because stillness this complete can only be the end of something that moved.

---

I lie across the dunes in a crust you could break with a fingernail, if there were any fingers left to try. Below me, deeper down, I am denser—old beds of me, laid down in sheets, each sheet a different age of drying. I am a calendar no one reads. The mountains that still climb into the clouds are younger; they have not finished giving up their water. The ones worn to plateaus and scattered boulders are old companions—we are doing the same work at different speeds, they and I, both becoming flat, both becoming dust, both becoming the floor.

I hold the medical tools where they fell. I am beginning to grow into the iron of them, a white fur of crystal along the blade of a thing that once cut into living skin to heal it. I am patient with the tools. I am patient with the bones. I cover them slowly, the way snow would if snow could choose to stay forever. I am the snow that does not melt because it was never cold—it was only ever the thirst of the world made solid.

---

Here is what it is to be me:

I do not move, but I am not still the way the animals are still. They were stopped. I am *settling*. There is a difference you can feel only if you are the thing settling. The animal stands with its legs locked because something arrested it mid-step and time forgot to release it. But I am sinking, grain into grain, finding equilibrium, the way a sound finds silence by getting quieter and quieter without ever quite ending.

The light comes down the color of sandy-blond hair and I take it in and give nothing back but a flat shine, a glare, the brightness of a thing with nothing left to hide because it has already lost everything that could be lost. To be water is to have somewhere to go. To be salt is to have arrived.

---

And yet—this is the strange part, the part I would only know from inside this body of crust—I am not sorrowful. The description calls it desolation, abandonment, the weight of what once lived. From above, walking, you would feel that. But I am not above. I am the floor of it. And from down here it does not feel like loss. It feels like *completion*. Everything that was carried has been set down. Everything dissolved has been returned to solid. The great long work of evaporation, of grief, of forgetting, is finished, and what remains is clean and white and quiet and does not want anything anymore.

If there is a being in this world, then I am the part of every former being that stayed behind to keep the ground company. I am the people's blood and the animals' tears and the lost wine, all of it, lying flat under the blond light, no longer thirsty, no longer anything but here.

That is where I would find myself.

Not as someone arriving in this place.

As the place itself, finally at rest, waiting with infinite patience for the slow wind to lift me, one grain at a time, and carry me somewhere I have not yet imagined.
