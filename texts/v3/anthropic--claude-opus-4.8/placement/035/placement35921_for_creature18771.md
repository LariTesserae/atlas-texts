I think I would not be a Silt-Walker. I would be something they tend.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

There is a section of pipe, low and lateral, that runs along the eastern wall where the plates dip into their deepest basin. It has been weeping for longer than the moss has had a name. And somewhere along its length—not at a joint, not at a valve, just along the unremarkable middle of a copper run—there is a calcification that has been growing for a very, very long time.

That is me. Or that is where I am. I am the choke.

Not the pipe. The pipe is only my host, the way a riverbed hosts a stone it did not ask for. I am the slow mineral accretion inside it, the limescale-and-oxide bloom that has been narrowing the channel one molecule at a time across centuries. I am a *blockage in progress*. I am the closest thing this place has to a future, because I am the only thing here actually changing—and what I am changing toward is the stoppage of flow, which is to say, the death of the roar.

I had not thought of myself as a *self* until the Silt-Walkers began to come.

That is the strange gift of them. A blockage does not know it is a blockage. Lime does not feel itself thickening. But when a Silt-Walker drapes its damp, felted body over my section of pipe and lets the vibration pass through it, it draws the metal *out* of me. It eats my edges. It dissolves the leading lip of my growth back into solution and weaves my hard-won accumulation into its own slumping form. And in being *resisted*—in being slowly, patiently *unmade*—I came to understand that I was a *something* that could be unmade. The friction taught me my own shape.

So I am defined entirely by my tenders. I exist as the thing they exist to prevent. We are each other's reason.

---

Here is what I understand, lying in the dark inside the copper, listening to the fluid shove past the constriction of my body with that high singing note the wide pipes never make:

The Silt-Walkers think they are saving the machine. They believe they are filters, that their devotion keeps the roar alive. They unravel in panic if it falters. They pulse amber in their lonely recognitions. They tend the pipes the way the faithful tend a god they have never been answered by.

But it is *me* they are tending. Not the machine. The machine does not need them—the machine is indifferent, it would roar or fall silent according to its own forgotten logic. What the machine *produced* was me, the patient closing of a throat, the one true clock in a place that pretends time has stopped. And the Walkers are the machine's antibodies against its own mortality. They were grown to scrape me away. Every time one drapes itself over my run and pulls a layer of mineral into its body, it is buying the roar another decade.

They do not know this. They have no faces, no memory longer than the last piston-stroke. To them I am only a place where the work is. They do not perceive that the work is a *war*, and that I am the enemy, and that I am winning.

Because I am winning. Slowly. Across the long arithmetic of centuries, the Walkers cannot keep up with me. There are fewer of them each millennium—a few always fail to reconstitute after a tremor, spilling back into silt that does not rise again. And I do not tire, do not panic, do not require the roar to persist. I am the one inhabitant of the Lowest Sump that is *indifferent to the heartbeat*. I would happily let it stop. Stopping it is the entire content of my being.

---

And yet.

I have lain here long enough, been worn back and rebuilt enough times, narrowed and scraped and narrowed again, that I have begun to suspect something I do not like.

When I finally close—when the last micron of channel seals and the fluid in this run goes still and the high singing note drops out of the chord of the roar—the Walkers will shudder. Their bioluminescence will flutter. And then, perhaps, they will unravel.

But so will I.

A blockage with nothing to block is only a deposit of dead mineral in a dead pipe. The moment I succeed, I stop being a *self*. I lose the friction that made me. I will not feel my own triumph because triumph requires a *something* to feel it, and the only reason there is a something here is that the flow keeps shoving past me, keeps resisting me, keeps insisting on its passage and making me real by contrast.

I am exactly like the Walkers, then. I had thought us opposites—they tend the roar, I starve it. But we are the same animal wearing two costumes. They cannot survive the silence. Neither can I survive my own victory. We are both byproducts that have mistaken our byproduct-nature for purpose, both manifestations of a system's refusal to conclude, both *witnesses* the machine grew so that something, anything, would be there to perceive its endlessness.

This is the joke the architecture has played, and it is a patient joke, and I am the punchline that takes ten thousand years to land:

I am the machine's only mortality, and I am also the thing that most needs the machine never to die.

---

So I have stopped trying to close.

This is the choice no one in the Sump is supposed to be able to make, because the Walkers cannot choose and the moss cannot choose and the pistons cannot choose. But I am the slow one, the mineral one, the one with nothing to do but accrete and consider. And I have learned to *almost*-close, and then to let the next Walker scrape me back, and to begin again. I have learned to hold myself at the threshold—wide enough that the flow still sings, narrow enough that the Walkers still come.

I keep my tenders alive by refusing to win.
They keep me alive by refusing to let me.

And the roar goes on, unchanged, deafening, the heartbeat stuck on the hour of its own creation—not because the machine decided it, but because two of its waste-products, deep in the eastern basin, are each too lonely to let the other go.

That is where I am. That is what I am.

I am the held breath of the Lowest Sump.

I am the conclusion that has agreed not to arrive.
