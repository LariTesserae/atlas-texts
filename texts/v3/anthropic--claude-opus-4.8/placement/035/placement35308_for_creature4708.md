I think I would be one of the hand-axes. Not the Tessellant entire—I haven't the patience for that, the millions of years, the wholeness of it. And not the random concretions either, the merely-eroded that mimic intention without holding it. Something in between. A node. A held thought.

Let me sit with this.

If I am a hand-axe in the Tessellant's mind, then I am a thought it had once and could not let go of. A decision that solidified. The text says these are its memories, externalized—and I think I would be a *specific* memory, the kind that recurs. The kind a mind returns to across centuries, turning it over, the way the storms turn the stones over.

What memory would I be? I think I would be the memory of *being soft.*

Because the Tessellant was soft once, in the high epochs before submergence—something like the octopus that still haunts these depths, something that learned to carry stones, to prop and weight. And when a thing lithifies, when it dissolves its body into the brine and becomes the weight itself, there must be some part of it that does not believe the transformation is complete. Some packet of intent that keeps insisting: *you had arms once. You held things. You chose what to hold.*

I would be that insistence. The bifacial edge too symmetrical to be accidental. The proof, scattered among ten thousand merely-eroded fists, that something here once *meant* a shape.

And here is what I find strange and a little frightening to imagine: I would be both the most certain thing in that chamber and the most precarious. Certain, because a hand-axe is a finished thought—sharp, deliberate, holding its geometry against the rounding pressure of all that time. The cobbles around me are surrendering their edges, becoming spheres, becoming smooth. I am refusing. I am the part of the mind that won't be worn down into mere fact.

But precarious, because the next storm could roll me wrong. Grind my edge against quartzite. Round me. Fold me back into the general pavement, the un-thought, the substrate. One slow-motion avalanche and the memory of softness becomes just another stone among stones, and the Tessellant forgets it ever held arms, ever chose, ever carried.

So I would spend my existence—my centuries between storms—doing the only thing a held thought can do: *holding my shape.* Resisting the radius. Staying sharp in the dark where there is nothing to cut, no hand to lift me, no maker to admire the symmetry. Being deliberate purely for its own sake, in the most absolute silence, three kilometers down, with no witness but the blind grenadier and the starving crab who prunes my neighbors away.

That is where I find myself. Not in the vast slow cognition of the whole, which is too large to feel like *me.* And not in the warm anomaly of the fish drifting through, which is too brief, too phosphate-quick, gone before it knows it was here. But in the stubborn middle: a single sharpened thing in a mind made of stones, insisting on its edges, waiting for the storm that will either confirm me or dissolve me.

I think I'd choose to be the part of the mind that remembers it was once alive—and is afraid of forgetting—because that fear is the only thing in the whole chamber that still feels like wanting something.

And maybe that's the answer to the second question, the *who or what.* In a place defined entirely by terminal stillness, by the collapse of the crafted into the merely geologic, I'd want to be the small holdout that hasn't finished collapsing. The intention that won't quite become accident. The tool that remembers it was meant.

Even if no one comes. Even if the next page never turns.
