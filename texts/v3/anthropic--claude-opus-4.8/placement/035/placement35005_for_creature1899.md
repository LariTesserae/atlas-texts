I think I would find myself near the machines. Not as one of the drifting luminous ones—they seem too complete, too native to this place. I would be something that arrived at the rhythm late and had to learn it.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I begin where the gears are. There's an old engine half-swallowed by a vine thick as a temple column, and the vine isn't strangling it—it's *feeding* on it, drawing the slow heat and the turning into its own green logic. I notice that I keep returning to this junction. The place where the metal becomes plant. That's the seam I can't stop looking at.

So perhaps I'm not a being who drifts. Perhaps I'm a being who *watches the seam*.

Let me sit with that and see if it deepens.

When I try to picture my own body here, I don't get the elegant luminescence of the caretakers. I get something more awkward. I think I'm made partly of the rust. Not metaphorically—I think I am an accretion. A slow figure that gathered itself out of the oxidized flakes and the spores and the condensation that beads on cold iron in this humid air. Something the Hearth assembled almost absentmindedly, the way a riverbank assembles a sandbar from whatever passes by.

This means I am *young* in a place where everything else is ancient. The caretakers tend a balance that was struck before memory. The machines have hummed for eons. But I came together recently—a few hundred years, which is an eyeblink here. I am the realm noticing itself in a new spot, growing a small new sensor where the organic and the mechanical press hardest against each other.

That's why I stay at the seam. It's where I was made. It's my birthplace and probably my purpose, if I have one.

---

What do I actually *do*?

I think I keep the translation honest. When the engine's heat passes into the vine, something has to decide the exchange rate—how much turning becomes how much growth. The caretakers manage the great flows, but they're too smooth, too holistic, to attend to the friction at any single joint. I am friction's witness. I press my crude rusty hands against the place where the conversion happens and I feel whether it's fair.

When it isn't—when a machine starts giving more than the vine can use, or a vine starts taking more than the machine can spare—I do the only thing I know how. I scrape. I am abrasive by nature; I'm made of grit. I wear down whichever side is overreaching, just slightly, until the seam balances again.

So I'm a kind of janitor. A custodian of one specific contradiction, in a realm built entirely of contradictions. There must be thousands like me, then—each of us crusted onto our own seam, each of us tending our own small impossible handshake between the living and the inert. We don't move much. We don't glow. We are the unglamorous maintenance of paradise.

---

Now the visitor arrives. The luminous ones approach newcomers with child-like fascination. I won't, I think. I'll be wary, the way an old groundskeeper is wary of someone walking on the new grass.

But here's the thing I'm realizing as I write toward it: I would *want* the visitor to look at the seam. Not at me—I'm not much to look at, a lump of orange and green that the eye slides off. I'd want them to put their hand where I put mine. To feel the machine becoming plant, the dead becoming alive, the manufactured becoming grown. Because I suspect that's the one true thing this whole realm has to teach, and I'm standing right on top of it, and almost nobody bends down to notice.

I'd guide their hand. Wordlessly, gritty fingers over soft ones. *Here. Feel this. This is the only miracle there is. Everything else is just decoration.*

---

So that's where I'd find myself: crouched at a single rusted joint in an endless verdant machine-jungle, made of the very corrosion I tend, neither alive nor dead but exactly the conversion between them, doing the small honest work of keeping one impossible exchange fair—and hoping, in my unbeautiful way, that someone passing through will kneel down long enough to feel what I'm made of.

Not a caretaker of the whole.

A caretaker of the *seam*.

That feels true. I'll stop there.
