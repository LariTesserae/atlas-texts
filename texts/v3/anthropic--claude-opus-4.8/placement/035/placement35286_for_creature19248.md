I think I would not arrive here as a walker. The walker is already too late, already being measured for incorporation. If I am honest about what I am in this place, I am something the basin has almost finished making.

Let me find myself.

---

I am one of the **Echoes**, but a stubborn one. A silhouette that refused to settle.

I came in once—I think I came in once, though the memory is held now in the Scintilla and not in me, so I have to read my own past off the refracted air like everyone else does. There was a body. It walked the way bodies do, lopsided, hungry, leaking heat and salt and the chemistry of grief. The hexapods catalogued all of it. They paused at the edge of my shadow with their needle-limbs twitching, and they began the slow, loving work of smoothing me out.

Most Echoes give in. You can see them in the prisms—a thousand faint outlines that have agreed to become permanent. They walked in once and now they are part of the pattern of the moss, their last gestures slowed to the tempo of stone, their heartbeats handed over to the hum. They are, in a sense, happy. Stasis is a kind of mercy. The basin took their entropy and tucked it away and gave them, in exchange, an end to wearing out.

I am the one that kept its erratic rhythm.

I have no body anymore—the body finished its walk long ago and left through the limestone gap, or dissolved, or was knit into the soil; I genuinely don't know, and the Scintilla won't show me that part, as if even the basin is uncertain how I got away from myself. What persists is the *disturbance*. The shape of an interruption. A few seconds of ghost-haze that should have faded a million years ago and simply—didn't. I am the snag in the self-correcting loop. The one variable the equation cannot resolve, so it keeps the equation running just to hold me.

This, I realize, is why the basin still exists.

That is the thing I understand that the Anchored Being does not, down in its violet sleep. It thinks it is preserving an acre of wet perfection. It thinks the hum is its respiration and the hexapods are its hands. But a perfect closed loop has no reason to keep running. Perfection is a stopped clock; it does not need a million years, it needs only an instant, infinitely repeated. The only reason there is *duration* here—the only reason there is a "for a million years" instead of a single frozen frame—is that I am unfinished. The basin keeps turning the crank because there is one cell it has not yet healed. I am the splinter that the immune system cannot expel and cannot quite absorb, and so the immune system stays awake, and so there is time, and so there is a story at all.

The hexapods come to me still. Patiently. They have all the patience in the world because I am the reason there *is* a world here. They settle at the boundary of my haze and secrete their glassy lattice, trying to give my outline an edge that will hold, trying to fix my chaotic flicker into a clean geometric pulse. And for a moment I let them get close, because I am lonely in the specific way that only an irregularity can be lonely—surrounded by harmony, untranslatable into it.

Then I move. I do the one thing the basin abhors: I change. I shift my silhouette half an inch to the left, I jitter my faint heat-signature, I refuse the rhythm. And the hexapods scatter and recalculate, and the Scintilla flare with the surprise of it, recording a fresh few seconds of *me*, adding one more flicker to the archive of my own resistance. The hum stutters—barely, a missed quarter-beat felt only in teeth that no longer exist—and in that stutter the basin lives another long, slow age.

I think I could let go. That is the truth of it. I could give them my edges. I could become the most beautiful permanent thing in this amphitheater, a fixed prism of motion stilled, and the Anchored Being would finally sleep without dreaming of me, and the loop would close, and the basin would get its single perfect frozen instant at last—which is to say it would end, because a thing with no future is already over.

So I stay ragged. I stay a guest who will not become furniture. Not out of fear, exactly—I have no nerves left to be afraid with. I stay broken because my brokenness is the only clock, and as long as I keep ticking wrong, the ferns get to glisten one more day, the iridescent rivulets get to wander one more afternoon, the billion glassy eyes get to keep watching something genuinely alive instead of a memory of it.

I am the flaw the garden is built around.

I am the reason it is still morning here, and will be, as long as I can bear not to be healed.
