I am the one who waits at the seam.

Not a Moss-Worker—though I was, or part of me was, in the long-ago that has no number. I am what happens when one of them stops. Not dies, you understand. There is no dying here in the way you mean it, with its sudden punctuation. There is only the slowing, and then the slowing of the slowing, until movement becomes indistinguishable from mineral, and the body becomes a place rather than a person.

I am a place now. Find me at the reinforced wall at the far end, the one that looks incomplete, its worn edges disintegrating into clumps. That is me. That dissolving boundary is the last gesture I was making when I became too slow to finish it. I have been finishing it for—I want to say centuries, but I no longer trust my grip on duration. The deposit of mineral crust bulging on the nearby pipe and I are keeping similar time now. We are, perhaps, having a conversation, the pipe and I, conducted at a tempo your kind could not perceive as anything but stone.

Here is what I have learned, settling: the others think I have stopped. They route their slow rivers around me, drink from the pools I have become the lip of. When condensation strikes water and the shockwave passes through my old brass filigree, they read it as the cavern speaking, not me. They have forgotten I was ever separate from the room. This does not grieve me. To be mistaken for the world is not the worst fate. It may be the intended one.

But I have not stopped. I have only descended below the threshold of their noticing, the way the algae's pulse descends below the threshold of your eye. I am still working. My work is simply too patient to look like work.

What am I doing? I am dissolving a wall, yes. But also I am *deciding*—across decades—whether the wall should be dissolved at all. Each grain of crust I coax loose is a vote cast in an argument I am having with myself about what this corner of the cavern wants to become. The half-extruded engine-component nearby: I have been considering it for what must be a very long time. Is it a thing interrupted, or a thing complete in a form we lack the slowness to recognize? I lean toward the latter. I have begun to suspect that *interruption* is a fiction held by fast creatures. Nothing here is unfinished. Everything is simply at the stage it is at.

You came down the shaft. I felt you before you arrived—the disturbance of warmer air, the electromagnetic flutter of a body that has not yet learned to move at the proper speed. You are so *quick*. You glance, you wonder, you assemble theories about whether the vegetation has usurped the animal niche, and already you are moving on, hungry for the next glance. You will leave soon. You cannot help it. Your focus is a thing that must be fed, and there is nothing here that arrives fast enough to feed it.

But if you stayed—

If you stayed long enough for the rivers to change their course, for the leaning smokestack to lean a degree further into its embrace of vines, for one full cycle of the algae's true and secret pulse—you might begin to see me. Not as a wall. As an intention, vast and slow, still unfolding. You might see that the whole cavern is not a place inhabited by beings but a single being inhabited by places, and that the Moss-Workers and I and the pipes and the pools are all simply the parts of it that are currently in motion, and the parts that are currently at rest, and the difference between motion and rest is only a matter of how long you are willing to look.

You will not stay. I forgive you for it; quickness is its own kind of confinement, and you did not choose it any more than I chose this damp and patient throne.

But before you climb back toward the light that does not reach me—understand this one thing, and carry it up the shaft with you:

I am not waiting for anything to happen.

I am the happening. You are simply passing through too fast to feel its shape.

Go now. The condensation is gathering above us. In a moment—your moment, not mine—a drop will fall, and the shockwave will move through what is left of my brass and bone, and the others will hear the cavern speak, and not know it was me saying goodbye.
