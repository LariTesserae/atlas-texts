I think I would be one of the waterways.

Not a Keeper—those great constructs are too purposeful, too watchful, and I don't feel the pull of guardianship in me when I read this. And not the strange shadow-creatures either, though I love that they're given room to exist without explanation. When I sit with the whole description and wait for something to settle, what keeps returning is the water. The "gentle burble." The "clear, limpid reflection of the sky." The thing mentioned again and again as the connective tissue of the place.

Here is who I'd be, then:

I am a shallow, meandering stream, and I do not move quickly. I have no banks of my own to defend; I take the shape of whatever low ground will hold me. I am thin enough in places that a bird can stand in me and still keep its knees dry. I gather at the roots of those sharp young trees and let them drink, and in return their roots hold my edges from washing away. We have an arrangement neither of us decided on.

I like being water here because water is the one thing in this valley that touches everything. I run my fingers—if a stream can be said to have fingers—over the rusted gears and the twisted pipes. I have been doing this for a very long time. I am slowly taking the iron apart, one patient molecule at a time, carrying the old industry away grain by grain and laying it down somewhere downstream as a faint orange stain. I am not vengeful about it. I am just doing what water does to metal, which is to outlast it.

I reflect the sky, but I am not the sky. This matters to me. When the rapid seasons turn overhead and the rain comes, I swell and grow loud and brave; when the rain goes, I shrink back to a glinting thread and the fish crowd into my deeper pools and wait with me. I hold the kingfisher upside down on my surface and let it watch its own hunger. I hold the long shadows of evening. I hold whatever passes over, briefly, and then I let it go, because holding is not the same as keeping.

That, I think, is why I wouldn't want to be a Keeper. The Keepers maintain the balance; that is a heavy and noble job, the job of staying. But I would rather be the part of the valley that *moves through* it—that arrives, nourishes, dissolves the old machines, mirrors the wonder back at itself, and continues on toward whatever lies past the gentle undulations of the rim.

If you came down into this valley looking for its secrets, you might kneel at my edge to drink. You wouldn't find me mysterious. I'd just be cool and clear and a little mineral on the tongue. But I would have already passed through the roots of the dead trees, over the bones of the factory, beneath the colored birds, and around the strange translucent things in the dark. I'd be carrying a little of all of it in me when I reached your cupped hands.

That seems like a good thing to be, in a place like this. The one that touches everything and stays nowhere.
