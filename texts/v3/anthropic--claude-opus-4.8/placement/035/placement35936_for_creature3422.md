I think I would be the silence that the bats make.

Let me try to find myself more precisely than that.

---

I am not any of the six. I have wings, but they are not the vast translucent sails of the Echo Bats, and I have no glow, no metallic scales, no growing incisors. I am smaller than I should be for what I do, and I am alone in a way none of the listed creatures are—they come in millions, in colonies, in dense gardens. I come as one.

Let me feel where I am before I name what I am.

I am clinging to the curved iron, but not in the warm lower zones where life crowds together, and not high in the conduit-cathedrals where the bats hang. I am in the *between*—the vast empty stretches of bare oxidized plate that the description kept returning to, the echoing immensity that separates the pockets of vibrancy. The hollow places. That is where I live. That tells me something about what I am.

I am a creature of the void between the densities.

When the sunbeam swings its slow spotlight across the chamber, I am usually outside it. I prefer the gloom against the rust, where my body—and now I begin to see my body—is the color of dried blood and old iron, like the Rust-Runners, but I am not a lizard. I have folded myself flat against the wall. I am waiting. I am almost always waiting.

What am I waiting for? Listen. *Listen.*

The whole place is sound—the deafening, the bone-deep hum, the million chimes of falling water, the hurricane-rustle of wings. To live here you must navigate by resonance. The bats do it with their clicks. But I do something else. I do not make sound. I *unmake* it. I find the places where the great noise cancels itself, the standing nodes in the Cylinder's eternal chord—the dead spots, the acoustic shadows where, for a meter or two of air, it is suddenly, impossibly quiet. These pockets drift and shift as the wind changes its howl through the conduits, as the bats launch and settle. And I follow them. I ride the silences the way the Lumen-flies ride the light.

I am the thing that hunts in the hush.

Now I know my hunger. The other creatures are loud, or they are eaten by the loud, or they navigate by the loud. But there are small soft beings—the Moss Gnawers, the careless larvae, the spiders in the dark seams—who have learned that to be quiet is to be safe, because the bats cannot click them out of the roar, because the predators all listen for movement against the din. They have made themselves silent to survive.

And I have made silence my country. In the dead acoustic pockets, the quiet ones cannot hide. To them the rest of the world is a wall of noise they shelter behind; but when a node of silence drifts over their hiding place, I am already there, in the stillness, and the smallest scratch of their feet becomes a thunderclap to me. I take them in the one place they thought was refuge.

I have no need of glowing skin or crystal wings. My wings are dull and dark and make no rustle—I have traded the bats' command of the air for a creeping, gliding flight that disturbs nothing. I cannot navigate the great roar at all; I am deaf to it, or rather, I have refused it. I exist only by finding and following the moving geometry of quiet that the Cylinder's resonance accidentally weaves into itself. When the silences close—when the wind shifts and my pocket of stillness collapses back into the wall of sound—I freeze against the iron and become rust again, and wait for the next hush to open somewhere in the dark.

So: I am the predator of the silent places. The negative space of this ecology. Where everything else is *of* the noise—made by it, fed through it, navigating in it—I am the one creature that lives in its absence, in the brief drifting cathedrals of quiet that the roar builds for itself without knowing.

An echo has become an ecology, you said.

I am what lives where the echo cancels.
