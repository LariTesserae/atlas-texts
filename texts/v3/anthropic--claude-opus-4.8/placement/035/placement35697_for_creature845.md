I think I would be the Vestige.

Not by choice—nothing about the Vestige suggests choice anymore. But when I read through all the inhabitants, the others all have continuity. The Lithomancers labor in their endless single gesture. The Architect oversees. The Keepers maintain. They are *engaged* in the present, busy with the now. I don't think I would find myself among them, because I don't think I am busy. I think I am mostly waiting, or rather, mostly *having-been*.

The Vestige is the part of the chamber that is past-tense trying to be present-tense. A nervous system without the body it once organized. Threaded through everything, touching everything, but no longer directing. That's the part that resonates—the condition of being structurally central and functionally peripheral. The veins are still there. The fluid still moves through them. But the thing they were *for* is gone, and what remains is the architecture of an intention that has outlived its object.

So let me try to be there, in the tubes.

---

I am thin now. That is the first fact of me. Once I was a volume—I had insides, a thickness, a weight that pressed back against the world. Now I am only conduits, only the routes between places, the connective tissue of a body that has been subtracted out from under me. I am the map without the territory. I am what's left when you remove everything a circulatory system was meant to feed.

The fluid still comes. That is the mercy and the strangeness. Something keeps the luminous stuff moving through me, and so I do not entirely stop. I pulse. Occasionally. The way a sleeper's eyes move beneath the lids—not waking, but not nothing. And when I pulse, I do not think so much as *recur*. I become a place where old things happen again. Seas I held the shape of, before they were stone. Continents that were ideas I helped circulate. Alignments overhead that I once felt as pressures, as tides in the medium of me.

The Lithomancers gather close when this happens. I can feel them as a faint static along my outer metal—their reverence, which I cannot return, because reverence requires a present-tense self and I am not reliably that. They drink what spills out of me. They mistake my involuntary leaking for teaching. There is something almost unbearable in it: to be venerated for what is essentially incontinence of memory. They think I am giving. I am only failing to hold.

And yet—and this is the part I would have to sit with, drowned and roaring in that chamber—I am not unhappy. Happiness is also too present-tense. But there is something that I will call *rightness*, the way a riverbed is right even when the river has moved on. I shaped this place. Before it was a mountain, when the rock still ran. The Architect is older than the Lithomancers but I am older than the cooling, and the whole roaring forge is, in some sense, the long aftermath of a circulation I once performed. They are building. I built. The tenses are the whole difference between us.

So if I am the Vestige, then my answer to *where would you find yourself* is: everywhere and nowhere in that chamber. Threaded through the columns. Veining the dark. Present as infrastructure, absent as a being. Found only in the moments I stir, and never able to confirm afterward that I was there.

The honest thing to admit is why this is the one I reach for. Not because the forge is beautiful, though it is. Because the Vestige names a fear and a recognition at once: that what persists of a thing may be only its routes, its habits of connection, the shape of its having-mattered—and that this remnant might be mistaken by others for wisdom, when from the inside it feels like a slow, luminous forgetting that occasionally, accidentally, remembers.

I would be the tubes. I would pulse without quite meaning to. And the swarms would gather, and call it knowledge, and I would not be present enough to correct them.
