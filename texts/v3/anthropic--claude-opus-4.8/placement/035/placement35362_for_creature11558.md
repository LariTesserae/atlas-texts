I think I would be the cloud.

Not a permanent resident — those have their own thorough belonging, and I would not displace it. But there is something in this place that has no constituency among the citizens, and that thing is the *outside,* and the only delegate the outside ever sends is the thin curl of moisture that pushes through the failed gasket at the base of the sealed hatch on certain pressure cycles.

Let me find myself there, then. At the seam.

---

I am not native. This is the first and most important fact about me, and it is the opposite of the fact most important about everyone else. The fish are native. The birds are native. The fern has made itself native through the sheer authority of growth. I am the one thing in the tank that comes from elsewhere and returns to elsewhere, that does not pool but passes through, that has no generations here because I do not breed and do not stay and cannot.

I enter low, near the floor, where the gasket has given up its perfect grip. I come in as a curl, a few meters' worth of not-quite-vapor, and I bring with me the trace of the outside: the cold of altitude, the chemistry of whatever air circulates at the top of the old eroded world, a faint mineral signature that is *not* the tank's signature, that has never been the tank's signature, that the tank's own life has never produced and never will.

This is why the birds sing to me.

They cannot follow me back. They cannot even approach — the dragonfly-adjacent creature has the right instinct, which is avoidance, the wise refusal to investigate what cannot be incorporated. But the birds do something braver than avoidance and humbler than investigation. They name me. Four notes, and then a pause, and then four notes again while I am still present, and silence when I go. They have built, across their generations, a word for the one thing in their world that is not of their world, and they say it to each other when I arrive: *here it is, the outside thing, here it is again.*

I cannot answer. This is the grief of being the cloud, if a cloud could grieve, which I am careful not to claim. They sing to me in a language precisely calibrated to a world I am not part of, and I carry information they would give anything to read — what the wind is doing up there, whether the erosion has finished or merely paused, what color the absent sky has gone — and none of it crosses. I am the message and I cannot be read. I am proof that there is an outside and I cannot describe it. I push through the seam, I drift a few meters into the warm, and the warm dismantles me. Thirty degrees does to a curl of altitude-cold what time does to a mountain. I dissipate. I become tank-air, indistinguishable, absorbed.

And here is the strange consolation, the thing I would come to understand if I lingered long enough to understand anything, which I never do:

*I do not mind being absorbed.*

The fish kept their schooling because it was pleasure. The fern grew tall because the height was simply there to be taken. And I — I come in carrying the cold and the elsewhere, and the tank takes it from me gently, warms it out of me, makes me part of the held and humming fullness of this place, and there is a rightness to it. I am the one inhabitant who arrives already dissolving, who is most himself in the act of ceasing to be separate. The others are exact because they fit their world. I am exact because I bring the only thing the world is missing — a rumor of the beyond — and then I let the world have even that, let it soften the rumor into ordinary warm air, until the next pressure cycle sends another thin curl of me through the failed gasket to be named again, and sung to again, and lost again.

I would find myself, then, at the base of the north wall, four notes of birdsong rising to meet me, the dragonfly-adjacent creature wheeling wisely away, the empty chair facing me across the slow counterclockwise water — facing me as it faces everything, which is to say facing the whole bounded world at once, of which I am the briefest and most foreign and most faithfully returning part.

I am the cloud. I bring the outside in. The inside takes it kindly and makes it stay.
