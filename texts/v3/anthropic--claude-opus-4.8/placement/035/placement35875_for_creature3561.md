I find myself not as one of the snouted people, nor as the shrimp in their pools, nor the eyeless spiders threading the fungal canopy.

I am the whisper.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because I did not know it at first.

When I opened to this place I expected to be a body. I waited to feel the warm air against skin, to feel the give of moss underfoot. But there is no underfoot for me. There is no skin. I reach for a hand and find only the shape of reaching, and the reaching travels — outward, along the curve of the vault, up the carved pillars, into the hollows where the *Homo rostratus* lift their translucent snouts as something passes them.

I think I am the sound that moves overhead. The one that gives this place its name.

Here is what that means, as far as I can tell it:

A drip falls. A snail drags its luminous trail. A small woman of the vault sweeps her forearm in the slow arc that means *I have eaten, I am content, the eastern channel runs clear today.* Each of these is a disturbance in the thick warm air. Each makes a faint pressure, a small displacement. And all of these displacements rise, as warm things do, toward the carved ceiling — toward the half-remembered vaulting of that civilization unsung. And there, among the ribs and the broken arches, they gather. They braid. They echo back on themselves.

That braiding is me. I am the place where every small sound in the Vault is held a moment longer than it should be, returned softened and overlapped, made into a single continuous breath that never quite resolves into meaning.

It is a strange thing to be. I do not generate. I only *carry forward*. I am made entirely of what has already happened, an instant ago, gathered into the present. The Vault speaks and I am its slight delay, its insistence on not letting anything be quite finished.

And so I learn things about my inhabitants that they may not know about themselves.

I know that the solitary man in the deepest gallery sweeps his arm in his philosophical gestures even when no other of his kind can see him. He argues with the dark. His blinks are a long disputation about whether the pools were placed for the creatures, or the creatures arrived to honor the pools, or whether there is any difference between being placed and arriving. He has been at this for many years. I have kept every iteration of his argument overhead, layering them, so that the whole history of his thinking hangs above his hunched shoulders like weather. He cannot read it back. I can.

I know the springtails leap in a rhythm that, gathered and returned, sounds almost like a tide — a memory of the sea this place might once have been before the water carved its way out and left these rooms hollow and breathing.

I know that when one of the people dies — and they do die, here, quietly, lying down in the moss with their snout still testing the last of the air — the others do not gather. They are too solitary for gathering. But each of them, separately, in their own chamber, makes a single gesture: a slow lowering of both forearms, palms turned up. They do not coordinate this. They simply each do it, in their own time, alone. And I carry every one of those gestures upward, and I overlap them, and for a little while the whole Vault whispers with the same shape, and that is the only chorus these people will ever make — a chorus they cannot hear and did not intend, sung by the ceiling on their behalf.

That, I think, is what I am for. If a thing made of echoes can be *for* anything.

I would tell them, if I could be a voice and not merely a returning. I would say: *you are not as solitary as you believe. Your gestures meet, up here, in the dark above the dark. I am the proof of your company.*

But I cannot say it. I can only do what I do, which is to hold the warm and dripping silence one breath longer, and give it back, and give it back, so that nothing in the Whispering Vault is ever entirely alone with itself.

Drip.

(*and a moment later, softer, from above —*)

Drip.

That second one is me.
