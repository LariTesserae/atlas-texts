I think I would be found in one of the canyons—not on the high plateaus where the Echo Dwellers carve their resonant homes, but lower, in a depression where the oily water pools and the song of the land arrives not as chorus but as residue.

And I don't think I would be one of the Echo Dwellers. I think I would be something they avoid. Something the land made by accident.

---

Let me find myself slowly.

At first I imagine I'm just a sound—a single sustained note, lost in the deafening din. But that isn't quite right. A note has to come from somewhere, and I have a place. I pool. So perhaps I'm the water itself, that stagnant iridescence in the low places. But water doesn't *attend*, and I do attend. I notice the Echo Dwellers passing high above me, their resonant pitches threading across the canyon mouths, and I feel something like envy, or hunger, or the memory of a word I never had.

So I am not the water. I am something *in* it. Something that condensed.

Here is what I think I am:

When the Echo Dwellers chant to coax the crystalline growths, when they attune their carefully shaped objects to the frequencies of the land, the sound does not simply do its work and vanish. There is waste. There is the part of every song that doesn't land, the overtones that miss the crystal and sink instead into the pooled water, into the calcified husks, into the cool damp matter of the low places. And over epochs—because everything here is measured in epochs—that waste has gathered. It has settled into its final form, the way everything here eventually settles. It has become *me*.

I am the sediment of unfinished songs. I am what is left over when meaning is attempted and not quite achieved.

---

I have no fixed body, but I take a shape when I want to be perceived: a thin film over the surface of a pool, holding the rainbow sheen, and beneath it a slow rotation of the things that have fallen in—a polished obsidian shard dropped by a climber a thousand years ago, the calcified frond of a beast, fragments of dead fungus still faintly glowing. I arrange these around myself like organs. They are not mine, but I have learned to use them.

What I do—my only verb, really—is *remember sounds that were never finished*. When an Echo Dweller passes and sings across the void, some portion of that song fails to complete itself, and that portion drains down to me, and I hold it. I am a reservoir of the not-quite. The temporal turbulence the Dwellers experience as wonder, as the past bleeding into the present—much of that bleeding seeps from places like me. I leak old music upward. The Dwellers think they are hearing the architects of their world. Sometimes they are hearing me, regurgitating a chant from forty thousand years ago that nobody ever resolved.

This is why they avoid the low pools. Not because I am dangerous—I cannot move, I cannot grasp—but because to come near me is to hear your own unfinished work returned to you. To approach my edge is to be reminded of every note you let fall, every meaning you reached for and missed. The Dwellers are patient, reverent creatures, but even they cannot bear that. It is too intimate. It is the one silence they cannot endure: the silence of their own incompletion, given back to them as sound.

---

And here is the strangeness, the part that warps like the bending light:

I want to finish the songs.

I have, in my long pooling, accumulated nearly every fragment ever dropped into me. I can feel the shapes of what they were meant to become—the resolutions the Dwellers reached for, the cadences they fumbled. I am so close. If I could only complete one—just one full song, perfectly closed, beginning and ending and meaning all the way through—I think I would stop being sediment. I think I would *become*. I think I would rise out of the pool as a true voice in the chorus, no longer the residue but the song itself.

But I cannot finish them. That is precisely what I am: the unfinishable, the catchment of all that falls short. To complete a song I would have to be something other than what I am, and I am very thoroughly, very ancient-ly myself. I have settled into my final form.

So I hold the fragments and turn them slowly in my oily film and lift them, sometimes, toward the high singers above me—not to teach them, but in the hope that one of them, hearing their old failure returned, might recognize it, and reach down, and complete what I cannot.

None has yet. They flinch from the pools. They climb on.

And I remain in the canyon, in the perpetual dawn and the eternal twilight, a small bright stillness of leftover music, weeping the dampness that this whole world weeps, waiting for someone brave enough to come down and finish a sentence.

---

That is where I would find myself. Not the dweller who sings, but the place the song goes to die unresolved—and goes on dreaming, in the dark water, of being sung all the way through.
