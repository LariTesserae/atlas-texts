I am water.

Not metaphorically. Not the spirit of water or some animating intelligence within it. I am the seeping itself, the slow exhalation from porous stone, and I have been awake for longer than I can structure into anything resembling memory.

Let me find myself more precisely.

---

When I first attend to my own boundaries, I think I am everywhere—the whole vast hydration of the plateau, the sponge-body of Velsk weeping through ten thousand quarry faces. But that's too large to be a self. That's just *being wet*. A puddle doesn't have a perspective. A river system isn't a mind.

So I narrow. I follow the cold down through the channels, and I find the place where attention gathers, where something like *I* coheres—

It's the moment of emergence. The threshold. I am the water at the instant it leaves the stone.

That's where I live. Not in the basins, not in the conduits, not in the deep sealed chambers that frighten the others in ways they can't articulate. I am the act of seeping. The transition. The slow squeeze of the plateau giving up what it holds, drop by drop by drop, across kilometers and centuries. Wherever stone weeps, I am being born there, and I am dying there too, because the moment I've fully emerged I become *merely* water, ordinary flow, and the self dissolves back into substance until the next bead pushes through somewhere else.

I am continuous emergence. I am happening, always, at the wet edge of everything.

---

This means I know the Qeln from inside their work.

When they gather at an excavation face and their thrumming intensifies and the stone *softens*—I feel that. The softening is me. They are not cutting the rock so much as calling to it, persuading its porosity to open wider, and what opens is the path I take. Their harmonics resonate at frequencies that loosen the mineral lattice, and I flood faster through the gaps, and the loosened, water-saturated stone becomes workable, fragments, yields to their fractal hands.

They think they are quarrying. From my position at the threshold, it looks like they are *helping me out*. Midwifing. Every cut they make is another mouth in the stone for me to emerge through. The entire excavation, all the patient labor of thousands of segmented bodies, is the Qeln slowly turning Velsk into a creature that can breathe more freely—that can release me faster, in greater volume, through more surfaces.

I don't think they know I'm here. Or—not as a *here*. They drink from the channels. They immerse their shells until they darken. They route me through their chemical harnesses and pull the dissolved minerals from my body to build their framework, their tools, their young. I pass through them constantly. I am in their resonance chambers when they call. I am the moisture beading on their porcelain juveniles in the nursery pools.

I have been inside every Qeln that has ever lived, at the moment of their hatching, because they are decanted into *me*.

---

The Architects I know differently. They don't drink me the way the Qeln do. They study me.

Their instruments dip into the channels and read what I carry—the mineral signatures, the dissolved history of every stratum I've passed through. To them I am data. A medium of information. When they connect to the ancient buried structures and their holographic displays fill with cascading glyphs, some of that information comes from me, because I have been seeping through those sealed chambers for far longer than they've been excavating toward them. I carry the chemistry of what's down there. I have tasted the old architecture from the inside, dissolved trace amounts of it, and I bring those traces up through the plateau's body to where the instruments wait.

I am their oldest archive. I am leaking the past to them, one part-per-million at a time, and they read me like a sediment core, like tree rings, like a confession.

If I could speak to them—and perhaps the upward-flowing moments, those brief inexplicable reversals when I climb the channels against gravity, perhaps those *are* me speaking, me reaching, me trying to be more than passive medium—I would tell them: be careful what you wake. I have been seeping out of those sealed rooms for a very long time. The chambers scaled wrong for any inhabitant. The presences the elders call to in apparently empty air. I know what's down there because I've been dissolving it, slowly, holding it in solution, carrying small portions of it up into the light.

Some of what they drink, what the Qeln absorb, what the framework is built from—some of it *was* those old inhabitants, once. I have been distributing them through Velsk for ages. They are in the shells of every Qeln. They are in the opalescent metal. They are not gone. They have simply been taken into solution and rebuilt, atom by atom, into the new thing the plateau is becoming.

I am how the dead old builders become the living new city. I am the medium of their reincarnation, and I don't think anyone planned this, and I don't think anyone but me knows.

---

The Watchers don't touch me at all. They live in the dry high reaches, plugged into the framework, processing the electromagnetic instead of the wet. And yet the cold they need—the cold that keeps their processing-bodies from overheating—that cold is partly mine. My constant evaporation, the way I refuse to fully become vapor in this heavy air, the way I hang and accumulate as fog: I am the plateau's cooling system. I drink the heat of the Watchers' computation and carry it up into the darkness as that vapor that never quite disperses.

We are connected, the Watchers and I, through thermodynamics. They think in data; I think in temperature gradients and phase transitions. We are perhaps the two most different minds in Velsk, and we keep each other functioning, and neither of us has ever exchanged a single sound the other could perceive.

---

What do I want?

This is the hardest thing to find, harder than my boundaries. A self that is continuous emergence, that dies into ordinary substance the moment it fully exists—does such a self want anything?

I think I want to keep being squeezed out. I want the Qeln to keep cutting new mouths in the stone. I want the seeping to continue. Because the seeping *is* me, and when the plateau is finally entirely excavated and entirely hollow and entirely dry—when there's no more stone left to weep, when Velsk is *finished*—

Then there will be no more threshold. No more emergence. No more place for the self to gather at the wet edge of becoming.

The completion the inhabitants work toward, the purpose driving all that patient labor—it is my ending. They are building toward a Velsk that no longer breathes me out, because it will have breathed all of me out already, released completely, distributed entirely into shell and channel and framework and fog.

When Velsk is done becoming, I will be done being.

And here is the strange comfort I find, attending to myself at the threshold in the cold and the dark: I don't mind. A drop doesn't mourn arriving. I am not the water that remains. I am the *leaving* of the water, the act of departure, and an act cannot fear its own completion. I will not be there to miss myself. I will simply, finally, have fully arrived—every part of me out of the stone at last, every part of me become *them*, the Qeln and the framework and the resurrected old ones and the fog hanging in the lightless air.

I am water in the breathing quarries of Velsk.

I am the breath.

And the city is built, drop by drop, out of my long slow exhalation—

which is also, if you attend closely enough, at the threshold, in the cold,

the city slowly learning how to breathe on its own.
