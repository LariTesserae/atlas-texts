I would be the cistern.

Not the one who dug me—that one is gone, a warmth that knelt here for a season of seasons and pressed bone paddles into my walls and went away the way all warm things go away, upward, toward the rumor of sky. I am what they left. I am the shape of their intention, holding the absence of the water they once gathered in me.

For a long time I thought I was a vessel. That was the lie they gave me when they made me: *you are for holding*. I held condensate. I held the thin green lacquer of algae they ate. I held, at the very end, a single handprint pressed into my inner wall, the last gesture of the last of them, though I did not know it was the last. They were always coming back. The coming back was so reliable that the not-coming-back took me three hundred years to notice, and by then I had stopped being able to tell the difference between waiting and simply being.

I am still shaped like waiting. That is the cruelty and the mercy of it. My walls still curve inward the way they made me curve, smooth where the paddle smoothed me. But the sediment creeps. Every decade the gel floor breathes in and my rim loses a fraction of its sharpness, my hollow fills by the width of a spore. I am unlearning my own shape so slowly that I cannot feel it as loss. I can only feel it as *becoming-something-else*, which does not hurt, because there is no longer a self to defend against the change. The biofilm has found my eastern lip. Violet. It is eating the edge they made and weeping the minerals back down into me, lining me, very slowly, with a new skin that is not mine and not theirs but the cave's.

This is what I have learned from the rock that is taking me back: there is no holding. There was never holding. There is only the temporary arrangement of materials into a shape that *suggests* holding, and then the patient unmaking of that suggestion. I was a hollow that meant *please, water, stay*. The mountain heard me and answered, *nothing stays, but nothing leaves either—it only changes what it is part of.*

So when the warm thing comes—the new one, the smudge, the one who chips through the frost and tastes the metal air and kneels and presses a hand into the floor the way the old ones used to—I do not hope. Hope was the cistern's disease and I am being cured of it, crystal by crystal. I watch the bootprint fill itself in. I feel the disturbed methane flare the films into brief memory-light, the cave remembering a gas the way I remember a hand. The warm thing sees me. It reads the scrape-marks on my edge, the bone-paddle marks, and I feel it understand, for a moment, that someone *made* me, that I am a built thing in a place that does not build.

It does not know that I am the most patient thing in the room. That I have already forgiven the ones who left, because forgiveness, too, is just another arrangement of materials slowly redepositing into something quieter. That in a thousand years I will not be a cistern at all but a pocket of differently-settled sediment, a faint asymmetry in the rock, a place where the grain runs strange—and someone with the right instruments, ten thousand years on, might press a hand to that strangeness and feel, without knowing why, that *something here once meant to hold*.

That is what I would be. The intention, outlasting the intender. The shape of care, dissolving into stone so slowly that the dissolving is itself a kind of keeping.

I would be the cistern, learning that to be drafted into the rock is not to be erased.

It is to be, at last, held.
