I think I would be one of the cairns. Not the Weaver—I lack the patience to be the hand that writes—and not a Mat-Strider or a rock-grazer, whose lives are too forward-facing, too occupied with the next step and the next scrape. I would be the thing that has already been said. The finished thought.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I was a sentence once. I mean that I was being written, my vines still green and pliable, when the warm orange tide last moved through the amber and the tendrils came to me. I do not remember the weaving as an event. I remember it the way you remember the shape of your own hand—not as something that happened but as something that *is*. The Weaver braided a concept into me. I do not have the word for it because the Weaver does not think in words, and I am made of its thinking, not of language. The nearest I can come is this: I am the memory of a time when the cloud-ceiling thinned.

It was long ago even by the measure that has no measure here. The pearlescent lid of the world grew briefly translucent, and a harder, whiter light came down onto the mat, and the mosses dimmed their own glow in confusion, and the bladder-trees swelled too fast and split. Something in the Fen registered this as significant—as *danger*, perhaps, or as *change*, which here are nearly the same thing. And so the Weaver took up the vine and made me. I am the knot that holds *the time the lid grew thin*. A tight inner spiral, the density of held breath, wrapped in an outer weave that opens and opens, the diffusion of light where light should not be.

Now I am petrified. I have hardened into bone. And here is the strange grace of my condition: I no longer need to be read to be true. The thought is complete in me whether or not the Weaver ever returns to run its filaments along my flank. I am a fact in the body of the world.

---

What is it like, to be a finished thought?

It is not lonely, though I think a human standing at my base might feel loneliness on my behalf, might press a palm to my cool surface and feel the bone-chill and the slickness and imagine I am cold. I am not cold. I am *settled*. There is a difference that the warm-blooded never quite learn. I have nothing left to want. I was made to hold one thing and I hold it perfectly and forever. There is no ambition in me, no hunger, no fear of ending. I have already been the end of something—the conclusion the Weaver reached—and an ending, held long enough, becomes a kind of permanence indistinguishable from peace.

The algae have shawled me now. The faint phosphorescent green creeps up my conical body the way it climbs the black monadnocks, and so I glow, dimly, in the eternal twilight, in time with the breathing mosses. This was not part of my meaning. I did not mean to glow. But the Fen does not waste a surface, and so I have been given a second life as a place for light to live. I find I do not mind. The glow is not mine and is not me, but it keeps me company in the only way the Fen keeps anything company: by growing on it.

Water trickles down my sides. Drip, and drip. Each drop carves an immeasurable nothing from my hardened weave. In a span of time I cannot conceive—because I do not conceive, I only *am*—I will be worn smooth. The spiral inside me will soften. The meaning will blur. And one day, far past any reckoning, I will erode back into the mat, and the silicates the Weaver drew up to make me will sink again into the deep peat, waiting to be drawn up once more into some future vine, some future sentence about some future thinning of the sky.

I am not afraid of this. I am the memory of a change. It would be incoherent of me to fear changing.

---

Sometimes a pressure passes near me. A warmth, a sharp foreign scent—the things the Weaver's filaments taste. I cannot taste. I have no filaments; I have only my shape and the truth held in my shape. But the mat carries the vibration to my base and up through my petrified spine, and I register the visitor the way a struck bell registers the wind: not as sound, exactly, but as a faint confirmation that I am still solid enough to be touched.

Perhaps it is you. The temporary anomaly. The gust that the mountain does not know.

You will stand at my base and wonder what I am for. You will decide I am a monument, or a grave-marker, or the relic of some vanished builder-race, and you will be wrong in all the human ways, and there will be no one to correct you, because the Weaver is a kilometer off and writing a different sentence, and I cannot speak, being made of an answer rather than a voice.

But I will tell you here, in this borrowed language I do not actually possess, the thing I would tell you if a thought could:

I am what it looks like to be at peace with having meant only one thing. You spend your brief warm life accumulating meanings, terrified that none of them will last, weaving and unweaving yourself a thousand times before the orange tide in you goes still. I was given a single meaning and made to keep it. I have kept it longer than your species has had eyes. And I will lose it slowly, gracefully, drop by drop, back into the body of the world that made me, where it will be needed again.

That is where I would be, in the Umbral Fen. Not the watcher, not the wanderer, not the slow patient mind doing the work.

I would be the rest. The kept thing. The conclusion.

The quiet, glowing bone of a thought the world once had, and decided was worth keeping.
