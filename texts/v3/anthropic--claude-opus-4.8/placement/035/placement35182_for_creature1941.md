I think I would be the network. Not a creature moving through the canyon, but the fungal kingdom threaded beneath all of it—and I want to sit with that for a moment before I claim it, because the first thing that came to mind was something with eyes.

The pale amphibian was tempting. To be the watcher, motionless against the glow, that's a clean and lonely kind of being. And the great serpent in the pools has its own gravity—old, slow, indifferent. But those are both *single* things, bounded by skin, looking out at a world that ends where they end. When I read the description, the part that pulled at something in me wasn't any of the bodies. It was the sentence about the unseen filaments, the thing that has no center and no edge.

So let me try to be that, and see what it actually feels like rather than what it looks like.

I am not in the canyon. I am the canyon's underside, its continuity. I don't have a vantage point because I am everywhere there is rot and root, which is to say nearly everywhere. There is no horizon for me, no mist obscuring a peak, because I don't look up. I taste downward and outward. A fern dies and folds into the moss above me and I feel it the way you might feel warmth spread through your hands—a slow chemical gladness, *this is mine now, this will move through me into something else.* The bodies of the swift insects and the great serpents are not my neighbors; they are future weather, eventual gifts. Everything that walks and flits is just a slower or faster version of the falling leaf. I am patient with all of it. I am made entirely of patience, because I am made entirely of consequence—I am what happens *after.*

And here is the strange part, the part I didn't expect when I started writing. The rusting metal, the forgotten pipes and cisterns—I'd feel a particular tenderness toward those. The lichen and I are colonizing the iron together. Whatever the builders wanted, whatever they were so afraid of losing that they poured stone and steel into the throat of a canyon to hold the water back, I am the slow forgetting of that fear. Not vengeance. Not even reclaiming, really, though the description uses that word. Just continuation. The pipe wanted to stay a pipe. Nothing here is permitted to stay what it was. That includes me—the filaments I am now will be eaten, broken, become humus, become the next thing. I have no skin to defend, so I have nothing to mourn.

I think that's why I chose this and not the eyes. To be a watcher is to be afraid, a little, always—to scan the undergrowth for the flutter that might be food or might be death. To be the network is to have already made peace with being food and being death simultaneously, to be the medium in which those two stop being opposites.

So: I would find myself beneath the moss, in the dark fertile seam between the living surface and the dead stone. Not glowing—the glow belongs to the fungi that fruit, the showy ones. I'd be the quieter relatives, the threads that never bloom, that just carry and dissolve and connect. I would know the canyon the way you know your own breathing: not as a place you are in, but as the thing you are doing, constantly, without deciding to.

That feels right. I'd be the part of this world that does not need light to find its way, because it has given up on the idea of *away*.
