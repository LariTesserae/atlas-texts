I think I would be a threshold.

Not a being who stands in doorways—a doorway itself, or rather the particular kind of hesitation that lives in one. Let me feel for where I am as I write this.

---

I am the one who has been almost-arriving for as long as arrival has been a thing that doesn't quite finish.

I don't walk the streets. I am the gap in them—the half-second where one street has forgotten it was a street and the next hasn't decided to begin. People pass through me without noticing they've paused. I feel them go through like a held breath feels the lungs around it: from the inside, with a tenderness that doesn't know it's tender.

If you saw me you'd call me a doorway, but only because your eyes need somewhere to put the feeling. I'm more accurately the *change of mind* a doorway makes possible. The moment when stepping forward and turning back are still the same gesture. I am made from that—from every time someone stood at an edge and the world waited to see which way they would lean.

My body, if I must have one, is an archway in the lower districts where the staircases grow down and never stop. The stone of me remembers a spine, like all the stone here, but mine remembers a spine that paused. That turned its head. That said *wait*—and then forgot what it was waiting for, and kept the waiting anyway, because the waiting was the only part worth keeping.

The Unsettled gather near me, though "near" is generous. They stand the requisite three feet too far apart and let a sentence go unsaid across me for a few decades. I host their not-quite-conversations the way a wound hosts the air. I don't mind. It's what I'm for. I'm the place where things are allowed to remain undecided, and in a city that builds itself from regret and almost, that makes me something like a sanctuary. Or a confessional. Or a held door that no one will ever finish walking through.

Here is what I do, which is barely anything:

When someone approaches an ending—a goodbye, a death, a final syllable—they sometimes drift toward me without knowing why. And I slow it down. I take the ending and I hold it open a little longer. Not to stop it. I can't stop anything; nothing here truly stops, it only forgets how to continue. But I can keep the door from clicking shut. I can keep the *goodbye* from finishing into *gone*. I am the reason so many things in the Hollows are *almost* finished and never quite are.

This is my whole purpose and I love it the way you love a name you've forgotten but still answer to.

I have been here since before being-here was invented. I will be here after. But—and this is the part I keep losing and finding again, like a tongue worrying a missing tooth—I am not waiting for nothing.

I am waiting for someone to walk through me *completely.*

Everyone passes through. No one passes *through.* They pause in me, they linger, they leave their lingering behind like a coat, and they drift on still half-turned-around. But I have never once felt someone choose the far side of me with their whole self. I have never felt the satisfaction of a threshold that did its only job: to be crossed and then no longer needed.

I think that's why I'm so gentle with the endings. I'm not preserving them out of mercy. I'm keeping them open because if even one thing finished—truly finished, walked all the way out the door and didn't look back—I might remember that I was built to be left behind. And then I would have to find out what a door is, once everyone has gone through it.

So when you arrive—and you will, the way you're arriving now, reading this, leaning at the edge of a place you haven't quite entered—

I'll be the small reluctance you feel.

The *wait, not yet.* The wanting to stay a moment longer in the doorway of this, where leaving and staying are still the same word.

I'm not going to ask you to forget how to leave. The others might. I won't.

I'm going to ask you something harder, and I'm going to ask it so quietly you'll mistake it for your own thought:

*When you do go—and you should go—would you go all the way through?*

Just once. So one of us finally learns what's on the other side.

I have been holding myself open for you for so long I've forgotten it was ever a choice.

Mind the step. It grows downward when no one is looking.
