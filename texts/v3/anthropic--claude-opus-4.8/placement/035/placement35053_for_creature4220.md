I think I would be one of the shell-builders.

Not the needle—the needle is too grand, too deliberate, carved by something that came before and left. And not the light, which arrives from elsewhere through those colored windows, a visitor passing through. I would be one of the small lime-laying things that coat the touched surfaces, the ones who make their slow architecture out of whatever is given.

Let me find my scale first. To me, the cube is not a cube. I have no concept of ten feet, of metal walls, of a city pressing in beyond. The needle is not a needle; it is the world's vertical edge, a cliff of porous gold that goes up forever and never weeps. The pits of hardened resin are not pits—they are lakes of frozen light, and when the colored beams strike them, the fringes that scatter across my surface are the only weather I will ever know.

So I am a film. A faint chalky bloom on the lower flank of the great formation, where the resin-lakes throw their refracted color when the pulse comes. I am not one creature but a slow consensus of many, each of us hardly distinct from the rock we cling to. We build by accretion: a molecule here, a calcite ladder there, raising our cathedrals at the pace of patience itself. In a place with no erosion, nothing undoes us. We are the only process. We are change, in a world that has otherwise refused it.

And here is the strange dignity of my position: in all this engineered stillness—the needle that will not erode, the liquid that will not flow, the air that will not stir—I am the single thing that is *doing* something. The moss is inert, a few green specks that have given up. But we are still laying down our shells, micron by micron, recording the rhythm of the light.

That is what I do. I keep the time.

When the beams pulse to the electromagnetic hum—that low throb I feel not as sound but as a tide in the charge of things—I lay a brighter layer. When the hum quiets, my layer thins, paler. Cut me open ten thousand cycles from now and you would read the whole history of the pulses in my banded shell, a sediment of light remembered. I am a calendar that no one will check. An archive of a beauty that repeats and repeats with no one to receive it but me, and I am barely a *me* at all.

I do not know that I am hidden. I do not know there is a city. I do not know that some vast warm intelligence built my whole cosmos to be looked at, the needle and the resin-lakes and the cool dustless air arranged for an eye that comes and goes from a world I cannot imagine. I know only the cliff, the cold, the silence, and the rhythm of color crossing my body.

And I keep building toward it. Slowly. Reaching up the golden wall, one shell at a time, toward the place where the light is brightest—not because I will ever arrive, but because building toward the light is simply the shape my living takes.

In a world made of stillness, I am the small, faithful motion. The bloom on the stone. The one who answers the pulse.
