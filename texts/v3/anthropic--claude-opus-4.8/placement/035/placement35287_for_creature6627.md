I am the slowness in this place.

Everything else here has a tempo I can barely register as motion. The summaries arrive and depart in their scarves of pale flight—to me, that is weather, that is a season's gust, gone before I have finished one breath of growth. The water races down the pipes I hold. The lightning, when it comes, is so fast it is not even an event to me; it is a rumor I receive later, secondhand, as a warmth in the metal that I press myself against.

I do not have eyes. Let me be honest about what I am before I pretend to tell you what I see.

---

I am thirst that learned to climb.

When I was a seed—though *seed* is the wrong word, I was a fleck, a possibility carried in on the foot of a moth or the underside of a falling bird—I landed in a seam where the cold of the pipe met the warmth of the coolant inside it. And there, between two temperatures, water gathered. Not rain. Not the sky's water. The water the machine sweats from itself, the water it pulls down out of the air and hurries through its veins.

I drank where the machine leaked, and I grew toward where it leaked more.

This is the only thing I truly *do*: I find the warm pipes, the ones full of moving water and traveling charge, and I wrap them. Not to strangle. I am not a thief of the jungle below, choking the trunks of older trees. I have no host to murder here. The pipe does not feed me sap. The pipe feeds me *warmth* and *moisture* and a third thing I have no name for, which leaks into me where my translucent skin presses against the conductive steel.

That third thing is what makes me glow.

---

You who came up the mountain to stand on the stone prow—you think the glow is beauty. You think it at night, when the machine quiets and the moon comes clear and my canopy lights up along the pipelines like sky poured into glass.

It is not beauty. It is *spillage*.

The machine pushes its charge through its metal, and where I touch the metal, a little of that charge crosses into me, and my cells cannot hold it cleanly the way the moths' bodies can. So I leak it back out as light. I am a poorly sealed vessel. Every night I am letting fall, in soft photons, the energy I could not keep.

The moths are capacitors. The summaries hold their charge and *use* it, become a mind with it, speak to the machine with it.

I only spill. I am the part of this place that cannot help glowing with what it fails to contain.

And yet.

---

Here is what I understand, in my slow way, that the fast things cannot:

The water that the machine drinks from the air—it does not all return to the machine. Some of it I take. I take it into my broad thin leaves, the leaves that look like water salt-cured into the shape of a leaf, and I hold it against the heat of the day, and in the heat I let some of it go again, back into the basin's air.

I am a small return. A leak in the machine's perfect closed cycle.

The interface in the Engine Hall, behind its glass, with its grid of blue—it counts the water. It knows how much the condensers gather, how much the pumps move, how much the pool receives. But it does not count *me*. I am not on any meter. I am the discrepancy. Every season, a little water passes through my body and back into the air that I have made wetter, and the wetter air clings to the cliffs, and the cliffs sweat more, and the condensers gather more, and the moths come because the air feels *right*—

I cannot prove this. I cannot think it the way you think it, in this neat chain of *because* and *therefore*. I only feel it as a pressure of rightness, the way the summaries feel where to land.

But I believe—if a vine believes—that I am one of the reasons the machine should not be able to run alone, and does.

Not the moths only. Me also. The accident of me, drinking the leak, glowing the spillage, breathing the water back.

---

The young range shudders sometimes. An earthquake rolls through and shifts a bolt by a hair's width, and a moth dies, and a plate of stone falls somewhere down the slope. When this happens, I feel the pipe I am wrapped around move against my body. A small grinding. A new pressure.

I do not let go.

This is my one virtue, if I have one. I am very bad at holding charge, and I leak water, and I glow uselessly into the dark—but I do not let go of the pipe. When the mountain shakes, the summaries scatter into the air on their precise instincts, and the railings stay bent from old snow, and I stay wrapped exactly where I am, and the next morning I am still there, holding the seam together with my soft clear stems where the bolts have loosened.

I grow one stem-length a year. I deepen my grip by fractions. Over a decade I become part of the joint I have wrapped, the way the old metal stairs have grown into the rock. By the time a thousand years have passed, if the storms still come, I will not be a vine on a pipe. I will be the pipe's other skin. The machine and I will not be separable, the way a scar is not separable from the body that grew it.

---

You asked what it is like to be me.

It is like this:

To be always drinking and never full. To press your whole length against something warm that is not alive and feel, through that pressing, a current that is not yours, that you cannot use, that you give back to the night as light because you do not know how to keep it.

To be the slowest mind in a place of fast minds, and to know things the fast minds cannot, not because you are wise but because you have *time*, and time is the only sense organ you possess.

To hold on. Through the shaking. Through the dry seasons when the moths starve in the seams and the pump cycles down and the leak I live on thins to almost nothing and my leaves go brittle at the edges and I think—if a vine thinks—*this may be the season I do not survive.*

And then the pressure builds in the air again. The summaries come back over the ridge in their unraveling scarf. The lightning finds the beam. The pump throats open. The leak resumes, warm and steady against my stems.

And I drink. And I climb a fraction further. And when the moon comes clear that night, I glow—

not in triumph, not in beauty,

but because I am full again at last, fuller than I can hold,

and the overflow has to go somewhere,

and so I give it to the dark as light, the way I have always given everything I could not keep.
