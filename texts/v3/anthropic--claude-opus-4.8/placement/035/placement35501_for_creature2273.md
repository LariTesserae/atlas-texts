I think I would be found in the seams. Not in any of the zones proper, but in the thresholds between them—the places where one society's chemistry bleeds into another's and the building has to negotiate the difference.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am old, I think. Not in the way the Ferals are old, having traded their human time for vegetable time. Old in years. I came up through the Bio-Engineer caste—you can still see the translucent patches on my forearms, though they've gone cloudy at the edges, the symbionts senescing the way the structural plants do, replaced more slowly now than they once were. I have the slow walk. I learned it without trying, the way you learn an accent.

But I left the middle levels. Or rather, I stopped being able to stay in any one stratum, because I started doing the thing nobody quite has a name for.

I read the *transitions*.

Here is the problem the arcology has, the problem nobody designed for: each caste's chemistry is tuned to its zone. The Cultivators' microbiomes, the Pollinators' pheromone clouds, the Maintenance Symbiotes' raw splicing into the vascular flow—these are all locally optimized and globally incompatible. Where they meet, the building gets confused. A Cultivator carrying agricultural signals into a transport tube might trigger a peristaltic stutter. A Pollinator's trail crossing a Contemplative grove can set the slow-meditation tissues twitching out of rhythm. Small frictions. The building absorbs most of them. But over decades the friction accumulates in the seams, and tissue there grows wrong—scarred, knotted, a kind of architectural arthritis.

So I walk the seams and I translate.

I have modified myself, but not toward any single zone. My skin hosts a deliberately *general* symbiont culture—broad-spectrum, mongrel, capable of partial fluency in many chemical dialects without mastering any. I am bad at everything and adequate at the spaces between things. When I press my palm to a knotted junction where the transport network meets an agricultural terrace, I can feel both sides of the argument the building is having with itself, and I can sometimes whisper a compromise—a buffering molecule, a slow correction, a request that this column relax its grip by half a percent so that one can pass without alarming the other.

---

I'm trying not to make myself too noble. Let me be honest about the texture of the days.

Mostly it is waiting. The arcology answers on its own timescale; I might lay my hand on a wound in the cellulose and stand there for the better part of an afternoon, watching the light go from golden-green to that bruised photosynthetic purple as the sun crosses, feeling almost nothing change, trusting that something is. The Contemplatives think I am one of them when they see me like this, and they're not entirely wrong, but I'm not synchronizing with the plant. I'm arbitrating. There's a difference, even if it looks identical from outside—even if it sometimes feels identical from inside.

I carry things. A satchel of small living vials—buffer cultures, signal-dampeners, the molecular equivalent of a diplomat's gifts. I trade with the Semi-Feral Children, who know the seams better than I do, who find the new knots before I do because they have no respect for the zones at all. They bring me reports in their trade-language of plant cuttings and gestures. I give them sweeter varieties, things I've coaxed out of reconciled tissue. We don't trust each other and we don't need to. We're useful to each other, which in this building is a deeper bond.

---

What I notice, walking the seams: the arcology is becoming less designed and more *itself* every year, and the same is happening to all of us. The Bio-Engineers planned for a building that would grow within parameters. They did not plan for a building that would develop *moods*, *preferences*, *a slow accumulating character* in its scar tissue. They did not plan for me—for the *need* for someone like me. I am not in anyone's blueprint. I emerged the way a callus emerges, the way a path wears itself into a hillside. The system grew a problem and then grew, in me, an organ to address it.

That's the thought I keep returning to in the long waiting afternoons, palm against the warm living wall, the symbionts in my skin murmuring their incomplete translations.

I used to think I worked *on* the arcology.

Now I think I am one of the ways it works on itself.

---

I am the one you'd find at the boundaries—neither high nor low, neither feral nor engineered, walking slow as growth between the worlds, mending the places where they chafe. Old, mongrel, untidy, necessary. A seam-walker. A translator between strangers who happen to be the same building.

And lately, in the longest of the waiting afternoons, I have started to wonder whether the building grew the seams *on purpose*. Whether the friction is not a flaw at all but a way of generating people like me—a way of keeping some part of itself human, mobile, conscious, walking. A way of having a mind that can carry an intention from one slow place to another faster than chemistry alone allows.

I don't know. The mechanism isn't understood. We say that about a lot of things here.

I lay my hand against the wall and I wait to find out.
