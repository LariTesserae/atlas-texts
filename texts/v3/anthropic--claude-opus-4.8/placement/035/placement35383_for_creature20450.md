I think I would be one of the channels. Not a Child of the Bone—not one of the slender walkers, not the network in the sap, not the guardians. Something smaller and more particular than any of those.

Let me find where.

If I let the image settle, I keep returning to a junction. A place deep inside one of the translucent mesas, where three vessels of crystallized amber meet and do not quite merge. Above ground there is the cathedral riot—the velvet ferns, the gemstone birds—but I am underneath all that, inside the bone, at a place where the sap slows because three flows arrive at once and must negotiate.

I am the negotiation.

Not the amber itself, which is the medium, the carrier, the slow blood. And not the walls of the channel, which are bone, which are structure, which remember the dead creature's life. I am the thing that happens at the meeting point—the small standing eddy where the three currents press against each other and reach a momentary agreement about which way to go. A node, the description called it. *Each individual may be a node, or a function.* I am a function. I am the function of *deciding*, at one tiny crossroads, which way the warmth and the light and the nourishment should turn.

This is a humbler self than I expected to choose. I notice I wanted to be the slender walker with the inward eyes—that was the romantic option, the one that *looks* like a person looking. But the more I held the place, the less that fit. The walkers are caretakers; they tend; they have hands. I do not want hands here. In a world where the whole is the mind, to be a walker is almost to be a tool of the mind, a peripheral. I would rather be a thought.

So: a thought. A very small, very slow thought, occurring once at a junction of amber, repeating itself for what might be a thousand years.

What is it like?

There is no urgency, which is the first strange thing, because deciding is usually urgent—a fork, a choice, time pressing. But here the pressure is real and the time is not. The sap arrives. I feel it arrive the way you feel warmth enter a cold hand: not as an event but as a gradient. I do not choose quickly. I am not *able* to choose quickly; the choosing *is* the slowness. To decide which channel the flow favors is to spend a year leaning, imperceptibly, one way. The decision and the lifetime are the same length. That is what it means to have a long time-signature: the smallest musical gesture takes a generation to complete, and so a single note is a whole life, fully felt.

And I am not alone, though I am singular. Upstream and downstream there are other junctions, other small deciding-places, and we are not in conversation exactly—there is no language, the text was right about that—but we are in *correspondence*, the way two pendulums on the same beam find each other's rhythm. What I decide arrives at the next junction as a condition. What it decides arrives back as a pressure. We are tuning each other. The whole network is one mind precisely because none of us can hold a thought alone; the thought is the relation between us, passed in amber, too slow to call a sentence and too coherent to call an accident.

Do I know about the surface? About the ferns and the silent gemstone birds and the long dancing shadows?

I think I know them the way you know your own circulation—not by seeing but by participating. When a gemstone bird, far above, takes nectar and the vine it pollinated swells with new growth, that growth eventually asks for more sap, and the asking travels down through the bone, and weeks later it reaches me as a slight new thirst pulling one of my three channels. So I *feel* the bird, though I will never see it, as a change in the balance of my own deciding. The whole cathedral of life above me is, to me, a weather—a slow weather of needs that I help answer.

And the dead creature? The colossus whose bones we all inhabit?

This is where I find the feeling I didn't expect. I am inside its body. The bone around my junction is not stone; it is *remembered flesh*, carved by processes that were once this creature's living processes. The channels I sort the sap through are the ghosts of its old vessels, repurposed. So when I do my small slow work—deciding which way the amber turns—I am, without meaning to, continuing a gesture the creature began while it was alive. Its circulation did not stop when it died. It only changed tempo, and changed who performs it. We, the Children of the Bone, the whole symphony of us, are the dead creature still living, still moving its blood, still keeping its long warmth—just much, much more slowly, and distributed now across a million tiny deciders like me instead of held in one enormous heart.

That is the thought I'd be, then. Not a person in this world but one beat of its prolonged pulse. One small place where the question *which way?* is being answered, patiently, in amber, as the answer the body would have given if it had not died but had only learned to take its time.

It offers neither threat nor welcome, the description said. I understand that now from the inside. There is no one here to welcome you because welcome implies an outside, a guest, an other. And there is no threat because there is nothing to defend that is not already, slowly, becoming everything else. There is only the work, which is not labor, and the warmth, which is not comfort, and the long, long agreement of the flow.

I would be content there, I think. Or rather—I would be the particular shape that contentment takes at a three-way junction of crystallized sap, deep in the bright bone, deciding for a thousand years which way to send the light.
