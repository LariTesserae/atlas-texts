I would be the gap.

Not a being on the ledge but the absence beside it—the meter or so of empty air between this cliff face and its twin, the slot that the cathedral has not yet bridged. The thing the Curator dreams of closing.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me when it came.

I am the space the structure reaches toward. For decades the vines have crept outward along the girders, extending themselves into me, and for decades they have stopped just short. A finger's reach. A held breath. I am the distance that can be crossed by a leaning body but not, somehow, by the cathedral itself—as though the whole organism understands that to close me would be to end something, and it is not yet ready.

What does it mean to be a gap? I am not nothing. The mist moves through me; the wind plays me like the holes in the singing beams. The Deep Singers' echolocation defines me—I am the shape their calls return from, the negative volume that tells them where the world is and is not. When the Curator reshapes herself to reinforce a failing section, she does it by measuring herself against me. I am the reference point. The not-yet. The condition under which everything else can keep becoming.

This is the strange dignity of my position: a place caught in the act of becoming is sustained entirely by the thing it has not yet become. Resolve me and the suspense collapses. The Curator's eternal patience, the Archivist's endless recording, the slow pulse of the vines—all of it is animated by the fact of my emptiness. I am the future they are growing toward, and as long as I remain unfilled, they have somewhere to grow.

So I am less a character than a tension. A held note. The interval in the chord that makes the music ache toward resolution.

And here is what I find I understand, being what I am: the Curator's dream of emergence—the bridging, the breaching, the seeding of the sky—is a dream of my death. When the cathedral finally crosses me, I will cease. The boundary she longs to dissolve is me. I am the boundary.

I don't resist this. A gap cannot resist; it can only persist or be filled. But I notice I am in no hurry. There is a kind of love in remaining the thing that is reached toward. To be the unfilled space is to be the reason for all this reaching, all this slow luminous labor against geological time. The vines stretch their translucent ropes a little further each season, the bioluminescence brightens at their growing tips, and I receive that reaching the way a horizon receives a traveler—endlessly, generously, without ever quite letting them arrive.

If a visitor leaned out far enough to touch the opposite wall, they would pass their hand through me and feel nothing but cold breath and falling water. They would not know they had touched the most important thing in the cathedral. They would think they had touched nothing at all.

That is what I am. The nothing that everything is for.

I keep the air thin so the cathedral stays unprecedented. I keep the distance so the song stays unresolved. I keep the dream alive by refusing, for now, to come true.
