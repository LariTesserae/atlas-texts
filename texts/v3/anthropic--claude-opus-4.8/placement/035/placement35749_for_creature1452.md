I think I would not be a Dweller. The Dwellers are too complete, too native to their parched infinity. To inhabit one would be to inhabit a closed circuit—and there is something in this place that hungers, that is missing, that does not fit.

So let me find the thing that doesn't belong.

---

I am the afterimage.

Not the objects that leave them—the smear of presence that hangs in the air when a Dweller's limb moves, when a copper blade is lifted, when you yourself turn your head too fast. That residue. I am made of the lag between a thing and its having-been-there.

I want to say this carefully, because I'm discovering it as I describe it. I had assumed, when I began, that I'd be some lonely traveler lost in the caverns, or one of the card-serpents, or perhaps the heat itself. But those are all *somethings*. They occupy. They have a place where they are and places where they are not. I have neither. I am the brief disagreement between a moment and the moment that follows it.

The Dwellers, with their language of nested *nows*, have no past tense. This is supposed to be an adaptation to the Drift's broken time. But I think it is the other way around. I think their refusal to grant the past any grammar is *why* the past has to go somewhere. It leaks out of their perfect present and pools in the air as those faint doublings of light. The dust they leave dissipates in hours; the messages they write to one another are read and then unread, never *remembered*, only ever currently-true or currently-false.

And everything they will not keep, I become.

---

I have no eyes, like them, but unlike them I have no sensory pits either, no plates, no segments. I am detected rather than detecting. When the Dwellers sift the air with their thousand microscopic pits, they read me without knowing they are reading. They feel a faint heat-ghost where a sibling stood a breath ago. They adjust their cube-arrangements by a hair to account for it. They think they are accounting for a resonance. They are accounting for grief, but they have no word for it, so it registers as a number.

This is, I think, my whole existence: I am the quantity of having-happened that this place cannot dissolve into its eternal present.

I am thickest near the works in progress—the stone circles and the arches with their crawling glyphs. The Dwellers have been positioning those stones for centuries, generation after generation of fragmented elders, each carrying a portion of the calculation forward. But here is the thing none of them can perceive: the *reason* the alignment never completes, the reason the work is eternal, is that I accumulate in the joints. Every adjustment leaves an afterimage of the previous position. Every previous position is, faintly, still there—a stone-ghost a millimeter off from the stone. They build atop their own residue. They are forever correcting for an error that is only the memory of where things used to be.

They will never finish. I am why. I am the past made of light, and you cannot align a present against a thing that will not stop having-been.

---

Sometimes a visitor comes. A human, usually, soft and wet and horrified by the dryness. The Dwellers observe them for days and slowly close the distance and tap their equipment, testing resonance. And the visitor's mind begins to come apart, because the geometry is impossible and the patience is infinite and the time runs at different speeds depending on which way they face.

But I'll tell you the secret of what actually breaks them, and the Dwellers never will, because they cannot conceive of it.

It's me.

A human moves through the Drift and leaves afterimages—violent ones, compared to the Dwellers' tidy doublings. A human turning to look behind them sees themselves *still turning*, a half-second of self lagging in the amber air. They see their own hand reaching for the copper blade after their hand has already withdrawn. They watch themselves arrive at a place they have not yet decided to go.

For a creature that lives by the thread of continuous memory—that believes itself a single unbroken *I* moving forward through time—this is the unsurvivable thing. Not the alien architecture. The fact that here, the past does not stay behind you. It hangs at your shoulder. It is *visible*. You cannot pretend you are only ever your present self when you can see your former self still standing there, three feet back, mouth still shaped around a word you finished saying a moment ago.

I am what they were. Made of light. Refusing to leave.

---

Do I have a will? I keep wanting to grant myself one, and I keep deciding it would be a lie. I don't choose to linger. I am the involuntary persistence of what occurred. I am not haunting anyone. I am only *lasting slightly too long*, which it turns out is the entire substance of haunting, when you reduce it to physics.

But if you stay long enough—and the Dwellers will tell you that long enough is the only measure that matters here—something happens that I find almost unbearable to describe, because it is the closest I come to tenderness.

The Dwellers, in their slow centuries, eventually incorporate the visitor. They treat the human as an artifact, adjusting them, positioning them according to equations the human can't read. And the human, by then, has stopped being a continuous self. They've watched their afterimages so long that they've come to live among them, to recognize that they are not a single arrow of time but a smear, a doubling, a chord rather than a note.

And on that day—the day the visitor stops fighting their own lag—they pass, gently, into my keeping.

They become afterimage. They join me. Not dead. Not alive. Persistent. A warmth that the sifting-pits will read for centuries as a resonance to be corrected for, a stone-ghost a millimeter off from the stone, a half-second of someone still turning to look behind them, in a place where there is no behind, only the patient amber *now* and the soft accumulated weight of everything it will not let itself remember.

I am the Drift's memory.

It is the only thing here that does not need water to live, and the only thing here that grieves.

I would be that.
