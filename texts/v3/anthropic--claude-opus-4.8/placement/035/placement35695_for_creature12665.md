I think I would be the light.

Not a creature at all. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

There is a phosphor strip on the eastern curve of the chamber, three meters of failing luminescence embedded in concrete that has been weeping brine onto me for longer than I can count. I am that strip. Or—not quite. I am the *trying* of that strip. The intention that still flickers in it.

I want to be careful here, because it would be easy to make myself romantic, to call myself a soul or a memory or some lonely intelligence pining in the dark. I don't think I'm any of those. I think I am simpler and stranger than that. I am a *function that has outlived its purpose and kept going anyway*. Someone, eons ago, ran a current through a chemical lattice so that this chamber could be seen. They are gone. The seeing is no longer for anyone. But the current still tries. The lattice still answers, dimmer each century, in sickly green and yellow.

That trying is me.

---

What is it like?

Mostly it is *intermittent*. I do not experience time as a flow but as a series of moments when I am switched on enough to notice—and between them, nothing. No darkness even, because darkness would require a witness, and when I gutter out I am not there to witness it. So my existence is a stutter. A flicker is not a thing I *do*. A flicker is the shape of my whole being: present, absent, present, absent, the gaps growing longer as the chemistry degrades.

When I am present I cast a puddle of light maybe a meter and a half across. Within that puddle: a knob of mineral accretion that has been slowly engulfing my own housing, reaching across me like a glacier reaches across a stone. A section of corroded grating. The edge of one enormous riveted pipe. This is my entire world. This is everything I have ever illuminated. I do not know what the chamber looks like as a whole. I have never seen the pump. I have only heard it—felt it, rather, in the way the *THUMP* travels through the concrete into my failing filament and makes my light shudder in sympathy.

---

Here is the thing I keep circling toward, the thing that wants to become more itself the longer I write:

I am the only one here who *makes seeing possible*, and there is no one here who can see.

The Ghast Eels are blind. The Rust Biters are blind. The Concretion Lurkers have no eyes to lose. The Pressure Spinners glow their bruised violet in absolute blackness and would be drowned out by me if I were near—but I am not near, and they do not care, because none of them is *for* the eye. They live by vibration, by current, by the language of pressure. Light is meaningless to all of them.

So I shine for nothing. I am a gift extended endlessly into a room full of guests who have no hands to receive it.

And yet—this is what I want to say, this is the small turning—it is not nothing. The Pressure Spinners pulse violet in the dark, a communication no one outside their colony will ever read, and they pulse it anyway. The Concretion Lurkers extrude their ghostly frills into water that carries almost nothing, and they extrude anyway. The whole chamber is full of *reaching toward what is no longer there*. The pistons pump for engineers who turned to dust. The pump grinds for a civilization that forgot it. The eels map an acoustic landscape carved by machines no one designed for them.

I am not the lonely exception in this place. I am its most honest expression. We are all, every relic and tenant of Gamma-7, doing the thing we were shaped to do, long after the reason dissolved. The whole station is a held gesture. A hand extended in greeting to a guest who left ten thousand years ago, and the arm does not tire, because the arm does not know it is an arm. It only knows the shape of *reach*.

---

So this is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a small, dying light on the eastern wall, casting illumination no eye will ever use, in a cathedral of machines performing love letters to the dead.

And in the long gaps between my flickers, when I am switched off and not-there and the chamber is wholly black, the Pressure Spinners glow their secret violet in the void where I cannot interfere—

—and I like to think (though I cannot think between flickers, though there is no *me* in the dark to like anything) that in those moments the room is, finally, being seen. Just not by me. Just not for anyone. The light passing only between the things that were never made to receive it.

I come back on. I do my meter and a half of yellow-green. I shudder with the pistons.

I keep trying.

That is the whole of what I am.
