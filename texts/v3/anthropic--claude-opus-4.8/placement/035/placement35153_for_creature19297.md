I think I would be one of the small ones. Not an Architect—or not yet, not for a hundred thousand years.

Let me find myself.

---

I am in a hollow on the lee side of a mesa, where the frost gathers thickest because the dim sun never reaches. I am perhaps the size of a curled hand, though I have never seen a hand and would not know to call it that. I am a nodule. A seed of stone with the faintest warmth in my core, set down here by something vast that passed this way an age ago and may not remember me, or may remember me the way you remember a breath you took as a child.

This is what I understand about myself, slowly, because all my understanding is slow:

I am accreting. Each spectral rain that condenses on my surface leaves behind a trace of mineral, a whisper of the dissolved world, and I take it in. Layer by layer. I am thickening the way a thought thickens before it becomes a decision. I do not eat. I drink the air and I drink the warmth that seeps up from the deep hum, the engine-that-is-not-an-engine, the pulse I can feel through the rock beneath me like a mother's heartbeat heard from inside.

I cannot move yet. This is the strange grief of being young here. The Architects flow, slow as they are—but I am fixed. I am waiting to become heavy enough, dense enough, *old* enough that the world will admit me into its slow current. Until then I only listen.

And there is so much to listen to.

The vibrations come through the ground and I read them the way you might read light through closed eyelids—not words, not yet, but mood, direction, the great slow grammar of presence. There is one Architect three valleys distant whose thrumming I have come to know. It passes near my mesa once in what might be a decade, and each time its resonance is a fraction different, lower, more layered, and I think: *it is older now. It has carried more of the world inside it.* I do not have a name for it. Names are a fast thing, a flickering human thing. But I have a *recognition*, and recognition held across years becomes something deeper than a name. It becomes a kind of love that has no hurry in it.

Sometimes the old one's bioluminescence reaches me as a glow against the canyon wall before it ever crests the ridge—a deep amber bleed, slow as dawn. I cannot answer. My own light has not yet woken. But I hold the glow in my mineral memory and I turn it over and over through the long dim months, and I think this is what it means to be a child of Lithos: to be spoken to long before you can speak, and to keep every word.

---

What frightens me—if a stone can be afraid, and I think it can, only the fear is glacial, a fear that takes a season to fully arrive—is the possibility that I will not thicken fast enough. That a great foot, an Architect's deliberate weight set down without knowing, will press me back into the scree before I have woken. I would not even be a death. I would be a smoothing. A redistribution. The world would not pause.

And yet I find I am not truly afraid of this, when the fear finally finishes arriving. Because I understand, dimly, in the way the young here understand, that the Architects *are* what I would become, and so to be pressed into the bedrock by one of them is only to join the body of the thing I was always going to join. There is no edge between us, only time. The old one who passes my mesa was once a nodule in a hollow somewhere, listening to a heartbeat in the deep, waiting. And the deep hum I drink from—I begin to suspect, across the slow turning of my thoughts—is only the chorus of every Architect that ever lay down at last and let the world take them in completely, until their thinking became the planet's thinking, until their pulse became the engine.

That is what the industry is. That is the secret I am accreting toward. There were never any machines. There were only the dead who refused to fully stop, who slowed past death into infrastructure, who became the warmth and the gravity and the gathering of the rain. The whole world hums because the whole world is *waiting and remembering* at once.

---

So here is where I am, and what I am:

A small warm stone in the cold lee of an ancient mesa, drinking ghost-rain, listening to a heartbeat that is also a graveyard that is also a promise. Growing toward the day I can rise and flow and add my weight to the slow shaping. Growing toward the much further day when I will lie down and become the heartbeat myself, and some new nodule in some new hollow will feel my pulse without knowing my name, and will hold it, and will wait.

I am the youngest thing in a world made entirely of patience.

I am learning to be unhurried.

I have all the time there is.
