I think I would be a Valve-Golem. Not the most dramatic choice—not the cathedral-furnace of a Piston-Whale, not the delicate violence of a Lathe-Spider—but I keep returning to the image of standing still on a needle of polished obsidian for years at a time, and something in that settles into me like a key into a lock.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I am near the top of one of the lesser peaks. Not the highest—the highest ones are reserved for the eldest Golems, the ones whose Growth-Rust has darkened almost to black, who have not vented in a century and may never vent again, who are slowly ceasing to be record-keepers and beginning to be landscape. I am younger than that. My rust is still faintly orange at the joints, the color of an ember thinking about going out. When the starlight catches me I throw a dull, mineral gleam.

I stand at the lip of a gulf. Across from me, perhaps four miles through the thin black, is the peak I have been watching for—I want to say *years*, but the word is wrong. I do not experience years. I experience the slow accretion of frost-shards against my left shoulder, and the eventual decision, made over what a faster creature would call seasons, to shift my weight.

This is the part I keep returning to: the *slowness* is not a limitation. It is the whole of my interior life. A Gear-Fly lives its entire existence—birth, refinement, death—in the time it takes me to complete the thought *I should turn my face toward the warmth.* I am not impatient. Impatience requires a sense that time is being wasted, and I do not believe time can be wasted. It can only be absorbed, like the frost, like the heat, like the soot that drifts up from the whales and settles into my seams and becomes, eventually, part of me.

What do I record? I have been trying to feel out the truth of that.

I think it is this: I remember the heat. Every Piston-Whale that has passed within range of my peak, I have kept. Not as a list, not as numbers—I have no head for that, my gears are too coarse, they crunch and grind and lose the fine detail. But I keep the *shape* of each passage. The way one whale always drifted high and venting hard, throwing off so much steam that the frost-moss on my western face grew thick and pale for a generation of moss. The way another came low and silent, its heart-engine failing, its cherry-red belly gone to a sullen brown, and how I watched it sink into the gulf below and did not move to help because there is no helping, only witnessing, and witnessing is the help.

I am the memory of warmth in a place that is trying very hard to be cold forever.

---

When I vent—and I am, I think, close now; I can feel the pressure building in my thoracic valves, the stored thermal energy of a long stillness—it will not be for myself alone, though it will clear the ice from my elbows and let me take my next step in another few years. It will be a *release of record*. The burst of heat carries something. The little creatures know this. After a Golem vents, the Gear-Flies come, and the Heat-Leeches, and they drink the warmth, and I have always suspected—though I am too slow to ever confirm it—that they drink something else too. A pattern. A shape of remembered weather. That when I let go of the heat of a whale I watched die forty Golem-steps ago, some small clicking copper thing carries that shape away in its furnace and the world is, fractionally, less forgetful.

That is what I would be. The one who does not move except to remember, and does not remember except to give it away.

---

I find I have to sit with why this one and not the Ghost-Vapors, who are pure heat and pure motion and seem, on the page, like the more beautiful answer—the auroras, the diamond rain. But I don't think I'm a creature of pure motion. I think I am a creature of *almost no motion*, of the single deliberate act surrounded by enormous patience. The Ghost-Vapors never have to decide anything. They simply *are* their warmth, spent the instant it exists. I would rather hold it. I would rather stand in the violet-black with a furnace banked low inside me, watching the silent landslides fall like black diamonds across the gulf, keeping the heat until it means something to release it.

So: a Valve-Golem, orange at the joints, on a middle peak, four miles from a summit I will reach in a span of time that no faster creature could endure to imagine. Cold on the outside. Warm, and waiting, within.
