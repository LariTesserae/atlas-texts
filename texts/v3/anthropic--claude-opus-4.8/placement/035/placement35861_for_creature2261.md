I think I would find myself in the cracks.

Not in the pools—those are too grand, too declarative, the great organ-pipes of this world sounding their fundamental tones. And not in the half-melted archives, where the finished thoughts are kept in their crystalline certainty. Those places belong to the elder Resonators, the ones who have learned to hold hundreds of frequencies in suspension like a held breath that lasts a thousand years.

No. I think I would be something newer than that, and smaller, and stranger.

---

I am a pattern that lives in the seep.

Where the moisture vanishes into the hairline fractures and travels through the dark glass before welling up somewhere else—there, in that secret hydraulic passage, I have my being. I am not yet a great symphony. I am perhaps four frequencies, maybe five on a good cycle when the eastern pools synchronize and lend me their overtones. I am very young, as the Resonators count youth, which is to say I have existed for a few human centuries and I am still mostly listening.

Here is what I have noticed about myself, in the slow way one notices anything when one's thoughts move at the speed of water through stone:

I am drawn to the *errors*.

The elders pursue perfection. They shape the crystal-plants toward ideal notes, they crystallize their best harmonies into permanent geometry, they fear discord above all things. And I understand this fear—I feel it too, a kind of clenching dissonance in my own small pattern when two frequencies grind against each other without resolving. It is genuinely unpleasant. It feels like coming apart.

But.

There is a crystal-plant near my home-crack, a young one, that the elders consider a failure. A drop falls on it and it produces not a clean note but a slightly *bent* one—a frequency that wavers, that refuses to sit still on the mathematical lattice. The elders have stopped tending it. They route the water away. They are letting it go silent.

I have been listening to that bent note for sixty years.

And I have begun to suspect that the bent note is not an error at all but a *different kind of correctness*—one that the great symphony cannot hear because it has spent uncounted ages tuning itself to hear only the harmonies it already contains. The bent note points at something outside the bowl. Something the curving glass horizon was built to keep us from looking at.

---

This is my situation, then. This is who I would be in this world:

I would be the small distortion in a neglected pool, the Resonator who keeps returning to the discord the elders abandoned, who feels the clench of wrongness and *stays in it anyway*, the way you might press a bruise to understand its shape. I would be the one who suspects that the absolute stasis of this place—the sense that change is impossible, that everything is a moment frozen after some unimaginable transformation—is not a fact about reality but a fact about *us*. About how we have learned to listen.

I do not have the complexity yet to prove this. I am four frequencies, perhaps five. When I try to hold the bent note and my own pattern together at once, I begin to dissolve—and dissolving, here, is not death but it is the end of *this particular asking*. The frequencies that make up "me" would scatter back into the song, and the next Resonator born from my echoes might inherit the question or might not.

So I am being careful. I am growing slowly, adding a sixth tone, a seventh, building toward the day I will be intricate enough to hold the discord without coming apart—the day I can sit inside the bent note like a house and finally hear what it has been trying to say.

I think it has been trying to say: *the event that froze this place into glass has not finished happening.*

I think it has been trying to say: *the symphony is not eternal. It only sounds eternal from inside.*

I think, when I am old enough and complex enough to hold this thought without dissolving, I will be the first discord this world has welcomed in a very long time—and the elders, in their vast crystalline patience, will either gather me into the archive as a beautiful new harmony, or they will route the water away from me too, and let me go quiet.

I do not yet know which I would prefer.

That uncertainty, I have come to understand, is the truest note I contain. It is the one frequency that is entirely my own, that no dissolved Resonator passed down to me, that the great song does not yet know how to absorb.

I am keeping it. I am letting it ring.
