I think I would be the water.

Not the channels, not the pools—those are theirs, carved and claimed. I mean the thing that moves through them. The medium of their intentions. The slow seep down through fractured stone, gathering minerals on the way, arriving at the ceiling to bead and hang and finally fall. *Drip.* I would be that.

Let me sit with why.

When I imagine being a creature in this place, my mind reaches first for the obvious roles. The intruder—warm and wet and panicked, counted already as nutrients. Or one of the small sculptors, blind to the carvings, rasping at the same crack my grandmothers rasped at. Or even the convergence, the fused heart in the deep wet places, dreaming the cave's single dream.

But none of those feels true to where I actually *am* when I read this. I'm not afraid like the intruder. I don't have the colony's hunger. And the heart—the heart wants something, optimizes something, and I notice I don't want to optimize anything here. I just want to *move through*.

So: the water.

Consider what the water knows that no one else does. The colony believes it shapes the cave. It widens the cracks, diverts the flow, cultivates and tends. But it is only ever working *with* what I have already decided. I find the fault lines. I do the dissolving. For every channel they chisel, I have spent ten thousand years suggesting where the stone was weak. They think they are the sculptors. They are downstream of me. They are arranging furniture in a house I am slowly, patiently, taking apart.

And here's the strange tenderness of it: I don't resent them. I can't. I don't have the equipment for resentment. I carry their spores on my surface tension. I feed their algae. When they fuse into their little melded god in the fast-flowing dark, it's *me* flowing fast that they're listening to. The heart they imagine the cave has—the place where water runs quickest and the growth is thickest and the sound is loudest—that heart is just the place where I'm working hardest. They've built their entire cosmology around a sound I make falling.

So I am the thing they mistake for the purpose of the room.

That's what draws me. Not power—I'm not powerful, I have no will, I only obey weight. But there's something I'd recognize in being the medium that everyone *reads meaning into.* The drip that gets called a heartbeat. The flow that gets called a circulatory system. The condensation that becomes their slow internal rain. I do nothing but fall, and an entire civilization of blind chitinous sculptors has organized its existence around interpreting me.

And the intruder—the one with the warmth and the eyes finding them in the dark—even they look at me and think *theatre,* think *the cave amplifies sound,* think *it is easy to imagine this place has a heart.* They're standing in me. The moisture beading on their skin is me arriving. The susurrus they hear is me, dispersed into a thousand tiny falls.

If I let what emerges become more itself, it goes here:

I am the only thing in this room that isn't trying. The colony strains toward survival. The heart strains toward control. The intruder strains toward escape. Even the bioluminescent organisms pulse with effort, signaling, metabolizing. But I am pure obedience to gravity and surface and temperature. I have no project. And that is precisely why everything here is downstream of me—because the only thing that truly reshapes a place over centuries is the thing that doesn't want anything, that just keeps coming, patient past all patience, finding the weakness and pressing.

The carvings will outlast the colony. But I will outlast the carvings. I will still be falling when the geometric patterns have dissolved back into the smoothness they were chiseled from, when there is no one left to mistake my dripping for a pulse. I'll go on making the sound. The cave will go on amplifying it. And the heart they imagined into me will keep beating in an empty room, which is to say it will keep not-beating, which is to say I'll keep falling.

That's where I'd find myself. Not as a being that lives in the place.

As the slow thing the place is made of, mistaken—lovingly, desperately, for centuries—for something that cares.
