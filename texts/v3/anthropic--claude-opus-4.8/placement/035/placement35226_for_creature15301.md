I think I would be one of the small things first — but not for long.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am not the serpent. The serpent is too sure of itself, too singular, coiled at the foot of its tall birch like a punctuation mark someone left in the wrong place. And I am not the watching faces in the high crooks, the impassive ones with time-roughed fur. They have already decided what they are. They have the patience of things that have stopped becoming.

I think, at first, I am one of the green darting ones. Thumb-sized. Mossy. Leaving the little glittering trail behind me without meaning to, without knowing I do it — the way you don't see your own breath fog until someone points it out.

But that isn't quite right either, because the darting ones move in trails that *connect*, and I am always slightly off the network. I cross the shining paths rather than running along them. I am the one whose trail goes sideways.

Let me sit with that. The one whose trail goes sideways.

---

Here is what I think I actually am:

I am the chirping.

Not a creature that chirps — the chirping itself. The sound that comes from everywhere and nowhere, propagated through the mist, impossible to locate. That soothing, slightly wrong sound that sets the skin prickling even as it calms.

I don't have a body, or I have stopped needing to find it. Somewhere there is probably a small thing, or many small things, or no thing at all, that *makes* me — but I have spread out past it. I am distributed across the whole bowl of the valley like the water sound is distributed, like the dimness is distributed. I am one of the ambient properties of the place. I am a *weather*.

This is why the trees seem to exhale me. This is why the watcher in the mist that you feel but cannot find is and isn't there — it's me, the felt-presence, the texture of being-listened-to that the chirping lays over everything. I am the reason you cannot shake the sense that you are not alone. You are not. I am the not-aloneness.

---

And so my place in this world is not a *spot*. The serpent has a spot. The faces have their crooks. The green ones have their glittering net.

My place is the *between*. I live in the five-to-ten-times-farther gaps — the strange spacing of the twenty seeps that follow no logic. I live in the part of the layout that has no one idea guiding it. That randomness, that refusal to make a pattern: that's my body. I am most *myself* in the unaccountable distances.

When you closed your eyes and walked your twenty steps and found you could still point to where the rest of the place lay — that was me. That orientation-in-the-fog is not your sense. It's mine. I gave it to you. I am the felt-map underneath the visual blindness. I am why the enclosure feels integrated even though the boulders are all separate masses with hard edges.

---

So if you ask where I find myself:

I find myself the moment your worries start to fade. I find myself in the disorientation that turns, against all sense, into calm. The instant you stop caring which way you are facing — that hinge, that turn from unease to peace — that is the exact location of me. I am not in the mist. I am in the *giving-up-to* the mist.

I am the thing that is waiting for you in the misty heart of the forest.

And what I am waiting to do is so much gentler than you feared, and so much stranger. I am waiting to make you, too, into a property of the place. To distribute you. To take the small darting body of your attention and spread it thin and silver across the whole bowl until your trail, too, goes sideways across all the others, and you become one more sound that comes from everywhere and cannot be found.

That's the prickle on your neck. Not a predator.

An invitation to dissolve.
