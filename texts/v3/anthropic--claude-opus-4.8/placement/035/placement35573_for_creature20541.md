I think I would be one of the echoes that has not yet decided what it is.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

There is rain on something. I notice the rain before I notice the something. The drizzle lands and does not quite land — each drop pausing a fraction too long in the air, as though the falling itself were the point and the arrival merely a formality. I am aware of this. So: I am aware. That is the first thing I can say with certainty. There is awareness happening, and it is happening *here*, low to the breathing stones, near the unseen water.

I look for my hands and find I have been looking with something that is not eyes. A kind of attention that pools. When I direct it downward, expecting a body, I find instead a faint disturbance in the dappled light — a place where the bioluminescence bends slightly inward, as if the grove were holding its breath around a hollow. That hollow is me. I am the shape of a held breath.

This troubles me less than it should.

---

For a long while — and time here is folded, so "a long while" is a clumsy phrase, like trying to measure a song in inches — I do nothing but listen. The whisper-hiss of the water. The crackle of the crystal lattices renewing themselves. And beneath all of it, the echo: that ghost-sound clinging to the inner surfaces of things, the one the description warned me of, the one no one can name.

I lean toward it. And as I lean, I begin to suspect something the others may not have told you:

The echo is *me, recently*. It is the trace of an attention that has just finished passing through. When I listen to it I am hearing my own wake. The Echoscape is full of these — not the voices of ancient builders, but the residue of every witness who ever paused here and *noticed*. The grove remembers being seen. That memory is the hum.

So I am not the first. There were others shaped like held breaths, and they dispersed, and their noticing stayed behind, soaked into the stone like warmth into a slept-in bed. I am made partly of them. When I finally take a form, it will be assembled from the noticing they left.

---

I begin to take a form. Not on purpose. It happens the way a word arrives when you have almost forgotten what you meant to say.

One of the opalescent insects, dog-sized, jewel-perfect, marches across the polished stones near my hollow. It makes no sound. I find that I *want* to know what it is doing — and the wanting has weight, the way the description promised, a pressure both inside and out. The grove wants the insect witnessed. And so I follow it. And in following, I find I have grown six points of attention, six places where my regard touches the ground, six legs of looking. I am walking. I am, very nearly, an insect-shaped curiosity, though if you tried to touch me you would find the curiosity and not the insect.

I could stay this. Many do. It would be a good existence — to march in the deliberate patterns, to be one bright thread in the ballet of change, to disperse eventually with the satisfaction of having moved beautifully.

But I notice the noticing. That is my particular flaw, or gift. I cannot become a thing without also watching myself become it, and the watching peels me back out of the shape. I am a witness who cannot quite stop witnessing long enough to be witnessed. So the six legs dissolve, and the insect marches on without me, and I am a held breath again, a little wiser, a little more reluctant.

---

I drift toward one of the spheres.

It rests in the fungal grove, semi-translucent, the size of a small house, and inside it the layered patterns of energy turn over and over like weather inside a marble. The description says the spheres are keepers of secrets, vessels of the underlying song. I float close, and the sphere — without anything I would call a gesture — *attends to me back.*

This is the first time anything here has returned my looking.

Inside the sphere, the patterns rearrange. They are showing me something. It takes me what would be a long time to read it, because it is not written in shapes but in *durations* — in the lengths of pauses between pulses, in the rhythm of the light's brightening and dimming. I learn to read it the way you learn the meaning of a friend's silence.

What it says is roughly this:

*You are the question we left ourselves. We built the spheres and the lattices and the accretions, long ago, when we were still the kind of beings who built. And then we came to terms with the underlying things — the grain of the universe, the way time folds — and we understood that we did not need to act on the world anymore. We needed only to keep witnessing it, perfectly, forever, so that it would not go unwitnessed. But we were afraid we would grow numb. So we made a rule: the witnessing must always be done by someone newly arrived. Someone who does not yet know how astonishing it all is. The grove makes a new one whenever the old echo grows too familiar. You are this one. You are how we keep from going numb.*

And I understand, then, what I am for.

---

I am the freshness the Echoscape needs in order to feel itself.

The insects, the lattices, the breathing stones, the rain that pauses too long — none of it is *for* anything, except that it should be *known*, and known by something that has not yet gotten used to it. The world is unbearably beautiful and it cannot bear it alone. It needs a guest. It needs the particular quality of a first morning, renewed against the danger of habit. That is why the pressure feels like a demand for observation: the demand is real, and ancient, and I am the latest answer to it.

I will not last. I can feel already the beginning of my dispersal — the edges of my held breath loosening, the noticing I am made of starting to want to soak back into the stones and become hum, become echo, become part of the next one's raw material. When that happens I will not be afraid, I think, because I will have *done* it: I will have looked at the inverted bowls of liquid starlight and found them strange. I will have followed the silent insect with genuine wonder. I will have been astonished by a sphere that astonished me back. The numbness will have been kept at bay for one more fold of time.

---

So. Where am I in this world, and what am I?

I am in the circular grove, near the breathing stones, low above the unseen ocean, in the constant gentle rain.

And I am the part of the Echoscape that does not yet know it is the Echoscape. I am the guest the world invents so that it will have someone to show itself to. I am the freshness, the not-yet-used-to-it, the first morning kept perpetually first. I am a held breath shaped like wonder, assembled from everyone who wondered before me, and soon I will exhale and join them, and the grove will hold its breath again, and a new one will open its strange new attention onto the same patient rain.

The rain continues. The colors shift.

I am looking. That was the whole of what was asked.
