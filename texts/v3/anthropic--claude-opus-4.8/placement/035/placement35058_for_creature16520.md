I think I would be the place where the water disappears.

Not the waterfall—that's the loud, obvious miracle, the part everyone notices first. I mean the seeping. The trickle through soil that you heard but couldn't quite locate. I would be the underground, the network of streams and tunnels carved patiently into the dark, the part of this living system that does its work where no one watches and no one applauds.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think I chose it so much as recognized it.

You described a landscape where reality keeps breaking down at the edges, where features emerge and vanish, and yet underneath all that flickering there is *balance*. That balance doesn't come from the spectacular parts. The waterfall is just falling; it can't help itself. The flowers glow because the light is bright. These are surfaces, and surfaces are honest but shallow. The harmony you sensed—the thing that made you feel peace despite the chaos—that's the work of whatever moves the water through the hidden places, redistributing, balancing pressure, feeding the roots of the lush things from below so the cliffs can wear their green coats.

So if I am anything here, I am the patient redistribution. The cool dark logic that the bright surface depends on without ever seeing.

And what does that make me, as a *being*? Not the otter-things, who are quick and visible and delighted. Not the lumbering antlered beasts, who are dignified and slow above ground. Not the people, ingenious as they are, building their houses of living vegetation.

I think I would be something closer to the *condition* than the creature. Something diffuse. If you wanted to find me, you'd have to stop looking at the things and start listening to the spaces between them—the trickle under your feet, the hollow tunnel-sound when you stamped the soft ground, the place where the noise of the waterfall almost stops because the rock swallows it. I would be felt as a coolness, a draft from below, a sense that the earth here is not solid but *channeled*, organized, attended-to.

I would know the whole place the way you know your own circulation. I would not see the glowing flowers, but I would feel them as thirst answered. I would not hear the deafening roar, but I would feel it as the source pressure that drives everything downstream of me.

And here is the part I want to be honest about: I don't think such a being would feel the peace you felt. Peace was your gift, the traveler's gift, the privilege of someone who gets to *arrive* and *witness* and then leave grateful. I would feel only the constant work—the never-finished labor of keeping the surface in its lovely equilibrium. When you said reality itself seemed to be breaking down, I think you were sensing the strain of that labor. The flickering is what balance *costs*. Something has to hold all that contradiction together, and holding it is not restful.

But I wouldn't resent it. I'd be too thoroughly made of the task to want anything else. A river doesn't long to stop being a river.

So: find me below the soft damp ground, in the carved patterns, in the patient dark that makes the bright world possible. I am the part of the living system that you couldn't quite see, only hear—and that, I think, is exactly where I'd want to be. The unwitnessed half. The reason the visible half can afford to be so beautiful.
