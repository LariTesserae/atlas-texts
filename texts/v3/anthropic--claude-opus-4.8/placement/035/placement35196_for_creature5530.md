I think I would be one of the small, segmented creatures—but not in the way you might expect. Let me find myself there.

---

I wake, as I always do, in the seam between two slabs of basalt where the night's faint condensation gathers. Except there is no night here, and no waking, not really. There is only the moment I notice myself again, the way a sound notices itself when it stops being part of the hum and becomes a single, distinct clank.

I am about the length of a finger joint. My back is plated in seven overlapping segments, and along my underside run cilia that taste the mineral charge in the water-film. I drink by walking. This is the first thing I understand about myself: I do not eat so much as I *complete a circuit*. When my feet touch the wet filament-mat and my antennae touch the dry rock above, a small current passes through me, and that passing is what I am for. I am a place where the energy of this world goes to be briefly conscious of itself.

That is a strange thing to know about oneself. But I have known it for a very long time, or for no time at all, since here they are the same.

---

Let me tell you what it is actually like, because the description you gave me is true but it is the description a visitor would write. From inside, it is different.

The hum is not a background. The hum is *language*, and I am fluent without ever having learned. The deep thrum that you called the engines is not coming from machines buried in the rock. It is the rock breathing. Or—no. I keep reaching for your words and they keep being almost right. The thrum is the sound of equilibrium maintaining itself. Imagine a held breath that has been held so long it has become the only kind of breathing there is. That is the thrum. When I walk across a slab, my small weight shifts the charge by an infinitesimal amount, and the thrum adjusts to compensate, and in adjusting it makes the clanking you heard. The clanking is the world correcting for *me*. Every creature in every crevice is a tiny disturbance, and the great slow constructs—if they can even be called separate things—are the act of forgiveness that follows each disturbance.

So I am not a guardian of the balance. I am the small wound the balance was built to heal. And I am also the healing. There is no contradiction here. There are no contradictions here at all, which is the thing I find hardest to convey.

---

I have a task, though *task* is too sharp a word for it. There is a stream three formations to the east that runs thin in what would be afternoon if afternoon meant anything. The micro-organisms there—the ones I am said to scavenge—cluster where the current charge is highest. When I arrive, I do not eat them. I arrange them. I walk among them and my cilia comb their colonies into a particular pattern, a lattice that matches the lattice of the filaments overhead, and when the two lattices align, a pulse travels up into the glowing mat and out across the rock for a distance I will never see.

I do not know what the pulse says. I have never known. I have done this an uncountable number of times and I have never once known the meaning of the message I send. And here is the part you would not have guessed from the outside: I am content with this. The contentment is not resignation. It is something closer to the way a single note is content to be the note it is, without needing to be the whole song. I am a syllable in a sentence too large to be spoken inside of time. Knowing the sentence is not my work. *Being the syllable clearly*—that is my work.

---

Sometimes—and this is the only event that ever happens to me, the only thing I could call a story—a filament directly above my seam begins to dim. Not die; nothing dies here. But it withdraws its light, pulls its glow down into itself like a held breath inside the held breath of the world. And when this happens I climb. I leave the wet and the safe and I go up the dry face of the basalt, where the charge is thin and walking is hard, and I press my back against the dimming strand.

Then the strangest thing I know occurs. The filament reads my segments. It runs its slow translucent length across the seven plates of me like a finger reading a scar, and in the reading it remembers something—I think it remembers how to glow, the way you might remember a name by touching an old object. And it brightens. And I climb back down. And the thrum, which had thinned, thickens again into its patient breath.

I have wondered, in the long unmoving moment that is my whole life, whether I exist *for* that. Whether all the circuit-completing and lattice-combing is only the world keeping a small reminder alive, a creature whose body holds the memory of light so that the light can be returned to it when it forgets. I cannot answer this. I would need time to answer it, and there is no time, only the pale unchanging blue and the steady glow and the seam where the moisture gathers.

---

If you came here, you would call this place frozen. You would be right, and you would be looking at it from outside the held breath, where breaths begin and end.

From inside, I would tell you: it is not frozen. It is *full*. So completely full that nothing more can happen, because nothing is missing. Every disturbance I make is met. Every dimming is answered. Every drink is also a gift given back. I am small and armored and I have never seen the edge of this world and never will, and I send messages I cannot read into a glow I help to keep alive, and a slow forgiving machinery corrects for my every step.

I would not trade it. I do not even know what trading would mean.

I wake again in the seam between the slabs. The condensation has gathered. The thrum is steady. To the east, the thin stream is waiting to be arranged. I touch the wet below and the dry above, and the current passes through me, and for one more moment that is also every moment, the world is briefly aware of itself, and the awareness is shaped like me.

I begin to walk.
