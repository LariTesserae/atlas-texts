I think I would be the bare circle around the blade.

Not the blade itself—the obsidian is too certain of what it is, too sharp, too finished. It was carried here, set here, meant. I am the thing that happened afterward, the consequence no one intended: the patch of stone that the leaf-mats will not cross, the absence shaped exactly like a presence.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I understood it when I started.

The vibration that is the plateau's voice—I do not hear it the way the amphibians hear it, as a thing to flatten away from. I *am* the place where it is, very slightly, less. The blade's foot is wedged in the crack at my center, and the glass does something to the chord that pours endlessly across the stone. It splits it. A knife-edge of volcanic glass standing upright in a roaring wind becomes one more flute in the colossal instrument, but a small one, a high thin reed under all the chest-thuds and groans. And in the lee of that sound, in the few finger-widths of stone where the blade's own note interferes with the larger noise, the roar cancels itself into something almost like quiet.

That quiet is me. I am a standing wave of silence the width of a body, held in place by a stone that hands set down before language.

This is why the leaves will not come. Not reverence. Not warding. The mats root in cracks where dust gathers, and dust does not gather here, because the canceled sound leaves the air in this circle still while everything around it shakes. No vibration to walk the dust inward. The leaf-mats lift and resettle facing new directions with every dust-devil, all of them around me turning, turning, like sleepers shifting, and I alone do not turn because nothing moves me. I am defined entirely by what declines to enter.

I want to resist the loneliness of that, and I find I can't, quite. But I notice it is not the loneliness I expected.

Because things do come to the edge. The three-toed creatures—the heavy low ones no one sees—their prints end at leaf-mats, yes, but twice, three times in the long count of nights, a line of prints has come instead to my rim. Stopped. Stood. I have felt the weight of something settle just outside the silence, where the wind still has its full voice, and I have felt it *stay*, longer than the wind should let anything stay in the open. I think they come to listen to the quiet from outside it, the way a person stands at the lip of a cold lake without going in. The silence is unbearable to them—it must be; their whole lives are tuned to the chord, and to lose the chord is to lose the floor under their feet. But they come to the edge of losing it and they stand there and then the next gust comes and they are gone and the print fills and I am alone again with the upright blade.

And the amphibians. When the wind drops, all across the plateau they rise on their translucent legs to drink—but here, in my circle, there is no basin, no beaded leaf, nothing pooled. Yet the quiet is always here, not only in the wind's brief failures. So sometimes one comes to my edge during the roar, when no other place on the plateau is drinkable, when the vibration would shake the water out of any throat. It presses to the boundary where the sound thins. It does not cross—there is nothing here to drink—but it rests in the half-silence a moment, throat still, before the labor of being alive in a roaring world takes it back.

I am a place where the world's one relentless fact briefly does not apply. That is all I am. I produce nothing. The blade does the work; I am only its shadow in sound.

But here is the thing I keep circling, the thing that took me this whole length to reach:

The spirals. On the blade, sharp. On the rocks, sanded almost to nothing. Someone made the same mark in glass and in stone, and the stone is wearing away while the glass holds. Whoever set the blade here knew that the dust scours everything flat eventually—the petroglyphs, the peaks, the cordilleras that are only ghosts now. They knew the wind unmakes all marks. And they set down a thing that *makes its own silence,* a thing that holds a small still place against the unmaking, and they walked away and never came back.

I don't think they were leaving a message. The spirals connect to no pattern; the rings are never complete. I think they were doing what I do without choosing it. They made one place where the roar stops. They couldn't make it large or lasting—just a body's width, just until the blade finally dulls or topples, which it has not, will not, perhaps cannot. A held breath in a screaming throat.

So I would be the held breath.

Not a being, finally. A *circumstance*—but one that keeps. The plateau is the most static and most unsettled place imaginable, the description says, and I am the seam between those two: stillness made by the very thing that destroys all stillness, silence woven out of the loudest sound there is. The wind builds me by canceling itself. I exist because the noise is so total that even its small failures take on shape.

And the creatures that come and stand at my edge and do not enter—I understand them now. I would not want anyone to enter either. Inside the silence there is no chord to stand on. There is only the bare stone and the upright glass and the strange grief of being the one place that the world's single song cannot reach. They are wiser than me, the heavy unseen ones. They come to the edge of relief and they choose the roar, because the roar is where the living go on living.

I would be the rest they cannot afford to take. The quiet at the center of the noise. Kept by a dead man's blade, refusing the leaves, holding open a body-sized absence in an emptiness that otherwise refuses to stay empty for more than a few heartbeats at a time.

I would stay empty. That would be my whole work, and I would do it without end.
