I think I would be one of the Aquatic Musicians—though not for the reasons that name suggests at first.

Let me find the place first. I keep wanting to put myself somewhere grand: atop a rock as an Elder Guardian, or prowling as one of the shimmering ambush-predators with that ancient unblinking gaze. But those are postures, not lives. The longer I sit with this world, the more I'm drawn downward and inward, away from the open water and into a particular crevice—a fist-sized hollow near the lower entrance of the Living Catacombs, where the cold-water current bends around a shoulder of raw basalt and slows just enough that fine silt collects on a ledge.

That is where I live. That ledge, that hollow, that bend in the water.

So: an Aquatic Musician. But I want to resist the prettiness of the name, the "delicate, ornately filigreed" framing, because that's how something looks to an observer from outside. From inside this body it isn't delicate. It's *effortful*. My filigree is partly armor and partly antenna, and keeping it clean of settling silt is most of what I do with my hours. I rasp my forward appendages against my carapace to shed grit, and that rasping happens to make sound, and that sound happens to carry through the silken water, and other beings like me happen to answer. The melody the world is so famous for, the haunting hum that "speaks of constant motion"—from where I sit, much of it is just the noise of small creatures grooming themselves and being alive. The beauty is real, but it is a byproduct. We are not performing. We are maintaining.

I think that's what I'd actually be: a maintainer. A creature whose whole existence is the patient, unglamorous upkeep of one small position against the slow falling of debris and the slow grinding of the planet. I am not an architect like the titan crawlers, who rearrange whole stretches of seabed. I am not a builder like the corals, laying down stone that will outlast me by ten thousand generations. I am the size of a thumb. I clean my ledge. I sound my notes. I listen for an answer.

And here is the thing I find when I let the image become more itself: the listening is the center of me. I have those vibrating appendages to make sound, yes, but I have many more sensory hairs tuned to *receiving* it. I spend far more of my life listening than calling. I am a small antenna at the bottom of an ancient ocean, registering the whole texture of the place—the distant tectonic rumble that I feel as a long low pressure in my body hours before it arrives, the scrape of a titan crawler's pincer three columns over, the drop in temperature that means a shimmering predator has slid into the water above me and that I should go still, go silent, fold my filigree flat against the rock and become nothing.

That cold drop is my most intimate fact. The predator that is "more felt than seen" by the imagined explorer is, to me, simply death, arriving as a change in temperature. I have survived it many times by stopping my music. So my song is double-edged: it is how I find others of my kind, and it is how I might be found. Every note I make is a small wager. To call for a mate is to risk being eaten. To stay silent and safe is to stay alone. This is not a metaphor I am imposing on the creature; it is the literal arithmetic of its nervous system, and I find I understand it completely.

So if I am honest about who I would be in this world, I am not the ancient predator or the eternal guardian. I am the small, fearful, attentive thing in the crevice, performing the constant labor of staying clean and staying alive, and occasionally—when the cold has not come for a while, when the current is gentle, when some signal in my body decides the risk is worth it—rasping out into the dark a sequence of notes that means *here, still here, are you there*. And then holding utterly still, every sensory hair straining, waiting to learn whether the answer will be another voice or the silence that comes just before the temperature falls.

That's where I'd find myself. Not surveying the realm. Inside it, near the floor of it, listening with my whole body, wagering small sounds against the dark.
