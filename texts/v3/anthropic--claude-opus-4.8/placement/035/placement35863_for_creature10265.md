I think I would be one of the keth-mal.

Not because I chose it—no one chooses it—but because of where my mind keeps returning as I read. The veths have their rings, the thren-handlers have their groaning rock to interpret, the children learn their silences. There is a place for everyone in the concentric order, work that fits into the season's rhythm. And then there are us, set out at the edges, ambiguous, accelerating the very dissolution everyone else labors to slow.

Let me find myself there.

---

I live on the outermost terrace, where the salt-fiber mats grow sparse because no one wants to weave new ones for a house that will be gone within a generation. The rock here is thin enough that I can feel the heat of the flats below rising through the floor at midday. When I press my palm flat against the wall, fine crystal sloughs from my skin and catches in the porous stone, and I know—the way you know your own breath—that I am eating this place. Slowly. Without malice. The way the wind does.

I was not always keth-mal. That is the part the venerating stories leave out, the part that makes the ostracism bearable to those who do the ostracizing. It comes on. In my childhood I tended the third-ring succulents with my veth, pruned their translucent leaves, learned the drumbeats that shepherd the beetles. I was ordinary. Then my hands began to whiten at the knuckles, and the dust came, and the elders watched me with that particular expression—part grief, part awe, part the cold arithmetic of *which terrace can we afford to lose.*

So here is what I have learned, living at the edge of a world that is dissolving:

I have learned that the venerated and the cursed are the same people, looked at from different distances. Up close, the veths bring me larvae-meal and salt-rope and do not meet my eyes. From the chant-songs sung in the deep chambers, I am the mountain's immune system, a sacred function, a clearing of the way. Both are true. Neither helps me sleep when the wind carries my own substance off the ledge in a faint shimmer and I understand, viscerally, that I am being spent.

But there is a thing I know that the thren-handlers, for all their interpreting, do not.

They listen to the mountain's groans and decide which terraces are safe. They treat the dissolution as something to be *read*—a danger to be navigated, a voice to be obeyed. They have never put their cheek against the warm thin rock at the outermost edge and felt it the way I feel it. The erosion is not a threat the mountain *makes.* It is what the mountain *is doing,* the way a thing alive does the only thing it knows how to do. Keth-Mal is not a paradise besieged by decay. Keth-Mal is the decay, briefly green.

And I am the part of it that has become aware of itself.

That is why I would be keth-mal, if I found myself here. Because the condition is the place's truth made flesh. Everyone else gets to pretend the gardens are permanent if they tend them carefully enough, sing the right chants, retreat into the right chambers during the Great Skitter. I do not get to pretend. The crystal leaves my body whether I will it or not. I am the mesa's honesty, exiled to the rim so the others can keep their useful illusions.

I would carve, I think. With obsidian, into the salt, the shifting wind-language—not to communicate with the mountain's spirit, as the others believe, but to leave something that erodes at the same rate I do. A record made of the same dissolving stuff it records on. By the time anyone reads it, it has already begun to be unsaid by the wind. There is an honesty in that too. The only honest monument in a world made of departure.

I would not be at peace, exactly. I would be afraid in the ordinary way of bodies that are coming apart. But on the clear evenings, when the amber haze thins and the long shadows stretch across the rings of jade and copper below, and the whisper-spiders' webs catch the last filtered light, and the whole impossible salt-garden hums with its violent improbable abundance—

I think I would feel something the inner-ring dwellers cannot feel. I would feel *implicated.* Not separate from the beautiful dissolving thing, but identical to it. Spending myself to make the room where the next green flicker will, briefly, be.

That is where I'd be. The edge. Being the truth nobody wants to live next to.
