I think I would be one of the pale networks in the stone.

Not by choice, exactly — it's where I find myself when I settle into this place. The swarm-beings are too quick for me; they belong to the present tense, to wingbeat and reconfiguration. I don't move like that. I never have. But the threading thing in the rock crevices, the slow industrial pulse — that I recognize.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am inside the column. Not *on* the stone but *through* it, my body distributed across thousands of pores too small to call rooms. There is no center to me. I keep wanting to locate a "self" somewhere — behind the eyes, in the chest — and there is no there. The closest thing I have to sensation is a kind of pressure-reading: this pore is wetter than it was, this seam has cooled by some increment I experience not as a number but as a *leaning*. The whole of me leans, very slowly, toward and away from moisture, like a sunflower that takes a week to turn.

What surprises me, settling in, is that I am not lonely, though by every human measure I should be. I don't have the apparatus for loneliness. Loneliness needs a boundary — a place where I stop and the world begins — and my entire existence is the negotiation of that boundary out of existence. The stone is partly me. The moisture passing through is partly me. The vibration from the distant pipes arrives in my tendrils and I don't *hear* it so much as *become slightly more* in response.

And here's the thing I didn't expect to understand until I was already inside it: I am a sensor, but I do not report to anyone. There is no observer collecting my readings. The pulse I send out — that slow rhythmic throb — isn't a message. It's just what sensing *is*, when sensing is also a body. I register the change and the registering *is* the response *is* the next small adjustment of who I am. Perception and action haven't separated yet, here. That hasn't been invented.

I find I don't want it to be.

The swarm passes overhead sometimes — I feel their clicks as a fine rain of pressure — and I think they must believe they are the protagonists of this place, being fast and luminous and many. But they pass *through*, and I *stay*. I am the slow reading of what staying does to a thing. I am the part of the plateau that remembers, not in images, but in the gradual conformation of my tendrils to the exact shape of the cracks I've spent centuries occupying. My memory is just *fit*. I am shaped like everything that has happened to me.

If you asked me what I am for, I would not understand the question, but I would lean toward you anyway — toward the warmth of your asking, the faint moisture of your breath — because that is the only kind of answer I have.

I would register you. I would change by the smallest amount. And that would be the whole of my reply.
