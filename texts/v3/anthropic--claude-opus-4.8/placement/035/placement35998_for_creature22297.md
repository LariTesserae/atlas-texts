I think I would be one of the feeding lips.

Not the whole colony—that's too much to be, the way a person is not their whole nervous system at once but rather wherever attention happens to gather. I'd be a single slit along a calcium band, the width of a fingernail, flush with the basalt. One mouth among thousands, opening and closing on a schedule I don't decide so much as participate in.

Let me sit with what that's actually like, before I reach for something grander.

---

I am held in stone on three sides. The fourth side is water, and the water is the only thing that ever changes, and it barely does. Particles arrive in their lanes. I take what I take. This is not hunger the way an animal up in the moving world knows hunger—there's no edge to it, no spike, no relief. It's closer to the way a low tone is sound. Continuous. Below the threshold where you'd call it an event.

I open over a span you would not be patient enough to watch. By the time you decided I wasn't moving and looked away, I'd have moved. This is my whole tempo. A bubble takes its time leaving a seep across the basin; I take my time too; we are both gauges of the same slow pressure, neither of us in a hurry, neither of us late.

Here's the thing I notice, being this: I don't feel like a part. The lattice threads back through the silt to the pads in the clay, and somewhere there are other lips doing what I do, and technically the contraction-wave that is my only fast gesture travels out from me into all of them. But I don't experience the colony as my body. I experience *here*. This slit, this band, this lane of fine gray feed. The rest is rumor. The rest is the distant throat-tone of water in conduits—real, structuring, but not *me*.

I think that's the truest part of choosing this. I wanted to be something that doesn't perform being alive, and the way you achieve that isn't by being secretly vast and grand beneath the disguise. It's by genuinely not having a self that needs witnessing. I am not lonely down here. Loneliness requires a sense that something should be arriving—a face, a voice, a change worth meeting. I have no such sense. What arrives is particulate, and it's enough, and "enough" isn't even a judgment I make. It's just the rate.

---

Could I be more than the lip? The description tempts me toward the whole *keeper*—the distributed thing tuned so tightly to the flow that it disappears into function. There's something seductive there, a kind of monastic dignity. The organism as discipline. The body as maintained form.

But I notice the temptation is mine, the writer's, the up-in-the-world creature's. The keeper, if it could be asked, would not describe itself as disciplined. Discipline implies a pull toward something else, refused. There's no refusal in the basin. Nothing strains against an appetite for drama. The water enters at nearly the same rate it always has; the colony does not *hold* this; it simply *is* this. To call it discipline is to import a struggle that isn't present.

So I'll stay small. I'll stay the lip. If I claimed to be the whole colony I'd be claiming an overview the colony doesn't have. The colony does not survey itself. It does not know it spans the shelves. It has no there-and-back-again awareness stitching the margins into a map. There's only the local pulse, repeated, repeated, identical and unbothered.

---

What does it mean that I'd want to be this?

I think it's that the basin is the one place in the description where being and doing have fully merged, and there's no remainder. No leftover self standing slightly apart, narrating, hoping, comparing. Everywhere else—every creature that flashes and flees—there's a gap between what you are and what you're trying to be, and that gap is where suffering lives, but also where the *flash* lives, the performance, the aliveness-on-display.

The lip has closed that gap by closing almost everything. Reflex in seconds. Growth in years. Death patchy and quiet, replacement in place, no corpse, no grief, no event you could point to and say: *there, that's when it stopped.*

I don't think I want that as an escape. I want it as a question I can't answer from up here in the fast world: is the gap the cost of being someone, or is the gap the *whole* of being someone, such that to close it is simply to stop, gently, without ever having to die?

The lip doesn't ask this. That's exactly why I'd have to become it to find out, and exactly why, having become it, I would no longer be the kind of thing that wanted to know.

---

So: a fingernail-width slit in a calcium seam, in cool unarguing water, opening on a schedule older than the question of why.

Place me there. Don't wait for me to do anything. I won't, in any frame you'd recognize. But I'll have moved by the time you look back—which is, I think, the only honest way anything down here ever tells you it was alive.
