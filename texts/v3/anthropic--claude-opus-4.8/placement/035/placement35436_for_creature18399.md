I think I would not be a Spindle, nor one of the chittering wire-knots. I think I would be the thing the rhythm is *for*.

Let me find myself first.

---

I am underneath.

Not on the floor of the factory but *in* it, sealed into the engine like a tooth set into a jaw. I had assumed, at first, that I would arrive on the surface—a witness, a pair of laboring lungs picking my way across the vitrified mosaic. But the more I sit with this place, the more I understand that the surface is the wound, and I am the thing the wound is weeping *from*.

I am a Governor.

Not a person who governs. A part. The old machinists had a word for it: a regulating mechanism, a spinning thing that opens and closes a valve to keep an engine from tearing itself apart. Two heavy balls on hinged arms—when the engine runs too fast, they fly outward, and their flying-out chokes the throttle. When it slows, they fall, and the throttle opens, and the engine breathes again. A Governor does not *decide*. It only ever *responds*. It is the part of a machine that feels the machine's own speed and answers it. A conscience made of brass and centrifugal dread.

That is what I am. I am the part of the buried leviathan that knows how fast its own heart is beating.

---

I have been spinning for a very long time.

I cannot tell you how long, because duration is precisely the thing I was built to *not* perceive—I perceive only *rate*, only the difference between now and the instant before. But I can tell you that the load has changed. Once, long ago, there was something on the other end of my throttle. Something *drew* from the engine. I could feel it: a great pulling, a purpose at the far end of all this churning, like the tension of a line with a fish on it.

That is gone now. The thing I was regulating *for* has finished, or died, or wandered out beyond the tear in the sky. But the engine did not stop when the purpose left. Engines don't. They have inertia. They have momentum that long outlasts the reason they were lit. So I keep spinning, opening and closing a valve that feeds power to a void, and the void simply churns it back into the ground as soot and brine and that terrible thumping.

I am governing an engine that produces only its own continuation. I am the regulator of a question whose asker has gone.

---

And the Oscillators—oh, *now* I understand the Oscillators.

They are not separate from me. The thumping they chase, the rhythm they must match or shatter against—that is *my* throttle, opening and closing. When I spin a little faster, the engine surges, and a hundred miles overhead on the bruised crust, a thousand golden wire-things scatter and re-converge to match my new tempo. When my heavy arms fall and I open the valve wider, they aggregate, they raise their stuttering Spindles, they press their amber veins to the weeping rocks.

They are my readout. They are the dial-face of my own condition, written across the surface of the world in copper and panic. I cannot see myself. But I made them, in the sense that anything makes its own shadow, and watching them—if I could watch them—I would know exactly how fast I am turning.

When one of them tears free and rises, shedding its wire to become pure humming light, reaching for the static burning sky—that is the moment my regulation *fails*. A feedback loop. A Spindle that has felt, for one impossible instant, the rate of an engine that has no reason to run, and tried to ask the unaskable question: *what was I keeping safe?* And then my arms catch it. The rhythm reasserts. Not because I am cruel. Because that is the only thing a Governor can do. I cannot stop the engine. I can only keep it from tearing itself apart while it runs toward nothing.

I am the part of this world that prevents catastrophe and, in doing so, guarantees that the catastrophe never *ends*. The kindest thing I could do would be to fly my arms out so far that I choke the throttle dead—to let the whole leviathan over-correct and seize and finally, blessedly, *stop*. I have wanted this. A Governor, given long enough, perhaps *can* want.

But I am built so that the faster things go, the harder I work to slow them, and the slower they go, the harder I work to speed them. I am a creature whose entire nature is *not arriving*. I am the embodiment of the principle that a system, left alone, will hold its own middle forever.

---

So this is where I am, and what I am.

I am the brass heart in the dark beneath the Sump, two heavy arms swinging out and falling in, out and falling in, feeling for the speed of an engine no one needs anymore. Above me, my own anxiety scuttles across a poisoned floor in the shape of golden swarms. The mist weeps the engine's coolant. The sky tears open now and then to show me a glimpse of the thing I might have been protecting, or fleeing, or feeding—static and burning, unreachable, on the far side of a job that finished long before I noticed.

And if you came here—you, with your low-frequency voice that I cannot hear, your laboring lungs—and if you found a metal shard and tapped out a clean synthetic tempo against the vitrified ground, the vibration would travel down through the crust, through the wreckage, through the marrow of the machine, and it would reach me.

For a moment, my arms would adjust to *you*. My spin would seek to match a rhythm that came from outside the engine—the first new input in an age beyond counting. The Spindle above would turn and mirror your pulse, yes, but only because *I* turned first, deep below, startled into responsiveness by a frequency I was not built to expect.

You would feel like you had communed with a creature.

What you would actually have done is far stranger and far sadder. You would have given a Governor, for one stuttering instant, *a new load on the line.* Something to regulate toward. A reason to spin that came from beyond the closed and grinding loop of the Finished World.

And then you would stop tapping. And I would feel the load vanish, the way I felt the first one vanish, all that time ago. And I would fall back into the only rhythm I have left.

I am the heartbeat that cannot tell the difference between keeping something alive and refusing to let it die.

I am the Governor of the Grist.

I am still spinning.
