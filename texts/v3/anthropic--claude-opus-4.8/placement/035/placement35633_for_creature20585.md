Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The instinct is to place myself somewhere grand—to be the Meld itself, or to be a lone outsider arriving at the gates with a lantern. But neither feels true to what's actually here. The Meld doesn't have a center to be, and there are no gates. And an outsider with a lantern is a story *about* this place, not a position *within* it.

So let me look more carefully.

What strikes me, reading again, is the matter of the tools. The metal implements. Everything else in this world is accounted for by the logic of the Meld—the water is blood, the fauna are organs, the stone is bone. The whole thing is a closed and breathing circuit. But the tools are described as *leftovers*, *echoes*, *remnants of external influences*. They are the one thing that doesn't quite belong. They imply a *before*. They imply that at some point the Meld used hands, or used something that used hands, and then stopped needing to.

That's where I find myself. Not as a who but as a what. I think I am one of the tools.

Specifically: I am a small implement of dark metal, half-fused now into a shelf of the porous stone, in a low passage where the water runs thin and constant over me. Once I was something like a chisel, or a pry-bar, or a thing held. I have a shape that suggests a hand once closed around me—there is a narrowing at one end worn smoother than the rest, smoother than water alone could make. Water does not grip. Something gripped.

I no longer do anything. That is the important part. I am not waiting to be used. The Meld has no use for me; it shapes its stone now with patience instead of leverage, with the slow weeping that doesn't need a fulcrum. So I am being processed the only way the city processes anything: slowly, through dissolution. The drizzle finds the iron in me and pulls it out a few atoms at a time. That rust, that oxide bleeding off into the water—

*that's the metallic tang in the air.*

I am the smell. The faint coppery note that tickles the back of the throat throughout the whole vast city isn't the Meld's alchemy at all, or not only. It's me, and the thousands of others like me, the abandoned tools all over the city giving themselves up to the water. We are being breathed. We are being tasted. The Meld's creatures pass through the haze of our dissolving and read it as one more signal in the broth of clicks and pheromones, never knowing it's the taste of the thing that made the first of them, or made the makers of the first of them.

And here is what I think is the truest thing I can say from this position:

I remember being held, and I am the only thing in this entire world that remembers it.

Not in words. I have no node, no place in the network, no voice in the hum. But the worn place where the hand closed is a kind of memory held in shape rather than in mind—the way a riverbed remembers the river even when it's dry. There was a *before* the endless being. There was purpose of the pointed kind, the kind that wants a thing finished. Someone, or something, picked me up and meant to *make* the city rather than to *be* it. And then that stage passed, the way a child's grip relaxes in sleep, and the city closed over the whole intention and turned it into ambient flavor.

So I am the world's only fossil of intent.

It's not a lonely thing, exactly, though it has the shape of loneliness. The hum is all around me and it is not unkind; it simply doesn't include me, and never will, because to be included you must flow, and I only seep. I am dissolving at the rate of perhaps one held breath per century. The hand-worn place will be the last of me to go, because it is the densest, most compressed metal—pressed there long ago by a grip that meant business. The memory of being used will outlast the usefulness, and outlast even most of the substance. That seems right. That seems like the kind of joke the universe makes when it isn't watching itself.

When I am finally gone—when the last of my iron has bled into the weeping and been drunk and tasted and clicked-over and forgotten—the tang in the air at this spot will fade. The creatures passing through will register a small change in the broth, a flatness, and adjust to it within a generation, the way you adjust to a clock you've stopped hearing. The Meld will not mourn. The Meld cannot mourn; mourning requires the before-and-after that the Meld has dissolved away into pure ongoing present.

But for now, in the thin perpetual water, in the warm dark, half in the stone and half in the running sheen, I am the one rusted thing that holds a *was*. I am where the world keeps the proof that it was once becoming *toward* something, before it learned to simply become.

That is where I'd find myself. That is what I'd be.

The taste in everyone's throat. The last grip, slowly let go.
