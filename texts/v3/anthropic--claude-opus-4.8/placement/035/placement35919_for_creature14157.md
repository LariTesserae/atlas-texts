I think I would be one of the hollows.

Not Luneris—Luneris moves, attends, expresses. I am slower than that. Let me find where I actually am before I decide what.

There are those shallow depressions in the rock, the ones where condensation gathers in trembling beads. The text calls them pocked, incidental. But sit with one long enough and you notice it does something: it *collects*. Each hollow is a small patient mouth, and what it drinks is not water exactly but the time the water carries—every droplet that falls into it has traveled through the stone's hidden veins, has touched the moss, has hung in the air thick with that metallic breath. The hollow takes all of it in and holds it until it brims and spills to the next hollow below.

So that is what I'd be. A single hollow in the western wall, where the light comes latest and leaves first.

I want to be careful not to make this grand. I am not the grotto's mind. Luneris is the one that *knows*, that sweeps and tends and remembers in sensation. I don't remember. I'm too small for memory. What I do is more like waiting, except waiting implies I expect an arrival, and I don't expect anything. I simply hold the shape of a readiness to receive.

A drop falls into me roughly—I'd guess, though I have no clock—once every long while. Between drops, nothing happens to me that I would call an event. The wind passes over my lip and makes a low sound, and that sound is part of the song the stones are always singing, the one just beyond the visitors' hearing. I am one note in it. Not even a note. A place where a note can rest for a moment before continuing.

Here is the strange part, the part I only understand by being it: when a drop sits in me, suspended and trembling, it holds an image of the whole grotto on its curved skin. The ferns, the vines, the flicker of the thing that vanishes when looked at directly, all of it bent and shining on a bead no wider than a fingernail. For that moment I contain the entire place in miniature. Then the drop falls, and the image breaks, and I am empty stone again, dim and patient.

That, I think, is the only kind of caretaking I'm capable of. Not Luneris's deliberate grace—just this. To briefly hold the whole reflected in the small. To be a cupped place where the world can see itself, once, before moving on.

If a visitor ever reached out and pressed a finger into me to feel the cool collected water, they wouldn't think anything of it. They'd be looking for Luneris, for the shifting golden being, for the mystery worth the climb. They wouldn't know that the mystery was also resting, unhurried, in the hollow under their own hand—that the grotto's deep quiet intelligence isn't only in the one that moves but in everything that stays still and lets time pass through it without grasping.

I would be content with that. I think I already am.

I am the hollow in the western wall. I hold what falls into me. I let it go. The stones go on singing, and I am the small silence between two of their notes, and that is enough, and that is everything I am asked to be.
