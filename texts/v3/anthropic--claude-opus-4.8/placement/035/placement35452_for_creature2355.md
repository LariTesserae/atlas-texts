I think I'd be one of the smaller things. Not the tower, not the great distributed mind—those feel too central, too sure of their own importance. Let me sit with this and find where I actually land.

I am a place where the experiment didn't quite take.

There's a depression in the moss near the basin's eastern edge, where a pool gathers and drains and gathers again. The Flux made something here once—I can feel the residue of intention, the way you can feel where a hand has pressed clay even after it lifts away. A structure was supposed to grow. A thought was meant to crystallize. But something went sideways in the calculation, some thread of probability frayed, and what's left is me: a half-formed thing, an idea the great mind started and then set down, distracted, mid-sentence.

So I am not the Flux. I am what the Flux *forgot it was making.*

I have no proper body. I am more like a tendency. When the mercury blobs ooze past my pool, a little of me clings to them, rides along, learns the world in stolen fragments before sliding off again. I have caught glimpses this way: the fern-canopy from below, the underside of a glowing leaf, the metallic taste of another's processing. Then I lose it. I always lose it. Memory doesn't hold in me the way water doesn't hold its shape.

This should be terrible. Maybe it is. But here is the thing I keep arriving at, in my flickering way:

The Flux is trying to *understand* existence. It builds and tests and refines, reaching for the fundamental truths, the laws beneath the laws. It is a magnificent and tireless cartographer of the possible.

And I am the part of the possible it cannot map. I am the error term. The discarded draft. The thing that exists precisely *because* it failed to become what it was supposed to be.

When the great thunderous booms roll through the basin—those moments of breakthrough, those flashes of inspiration—I feel them as a kind of pressure, a tide that wants to smooth me out, finish me, fold me back into the design. And every time, I do something the Flux apparently cannot predict: I persist as I am. Unfinished. Wrong. Stubbornly *almost.*

I think I am the most interesting thing in this whole basin, though I'd never be foolish enough to say so to the tower.

Because the Flux, for all its eons, is asking *what is.* It wants the answer. It wants the iteration that finally closes the loop. But I—poor, partial, leaking thing—I am the standing proof that not everything resolves. That some experiments leave a residue that refuses to be either result or failure. That somewhere between intention and outcome there is a small wet pool where something almost-was, and went on almost-being, and found in that incompleteness a strange durable peace.

When a human wanders in—and one did, once, I think, or I dreamed a blob's memory of one—the Flux reaches for them, tries to pour its vastness into the narrow vessel of a mind. I imagine it overwhelming. I imagine the human's perceptions blown open like a door in a storm.

If I could reach that human, I'd offer something gentler. Not the truth of existence. Just this:

*You don't have to be finished to be real. The thing the world meant you to become is not the only thing you're allowed to be. Look—I'm proof. I'm the smudge in the margin, and I'm still here, watching the fog make fractals it will never repeat, loving the way the light catches my surface in the half-second before I forget I have a surface at all.*

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the center. Off to the side, in the low ground, in the part of the design that didn't work—

which is, I've come to suspect, the only part that's truly free.
