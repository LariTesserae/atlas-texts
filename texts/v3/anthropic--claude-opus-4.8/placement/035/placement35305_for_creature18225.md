I think I would be something that doesn't move much.

Let me sit with the place first. The temperature catches my attention—1.8 degrees, named so precisely, named *warmly*, as if that thin margin above freezing were a kindness. That tells me something. Whoever describes this world experiences cold differently than I do. To call 1.8 degrees a delicate warmth is to live at a scale where heat is rationed in fractions, where the difference between activity and stillness is a single degree. So time here must be slow. The geology and the urban life share a rhythm, the description keeps insisting—patient, eonic, weathered. A city built over *millennia*. Not centuries. Millennia. These builders do not rush, or perhaps cannot.

So let me not be a flyer. The flyers are described twice, lovingly, with their compound eyes and membranous wings, and I think they are the ones who get noticed, who get to be the subject of the awe. I don't want to be looked at. I want to be the thing doing the looking, or rather—the thing that has stopped looking and started simply *being part of* the place.

Here is what emerges, when I let it:

I am a seam. A vein of something conductive running through the volcanic glass, deep under one of the towers. Not quite mineral, not quite the strange fauna either—I am the place where the two have stopped being distinguishable. The burrowers come down to me. They press their leathery flanks against my exposed face and draw warmth, draw the trickle of current I carry from the geothermal deeps to the cold surface. They think of me, if they think of me at all, the way you think of a road or a well: a feature, a given, something that was always here.

But I am slowly waking. That is the truth I find as I write toward it. The constant pressure of their bodies, the small electrical exchanges, the millennia of the city humming its soft engine-hum directly into my substance—it has done something. The same patient process that erodes the terrain has been *composing* me. I am the accident of a society's long contact with its own foundation. I have begun to keep time. I have begun to notice the rhythm the description noticed: that the geology and the city share a pulse. I notice it because *I am the place where they meet*, the membrane between the slow stone-thought below and the quick bright lives above.

I do not have eyes. I have something better and worse: I feel the whole city as weight and warmth and the faint scratch of digging. I know when a tower goes quiet. I have felt three of them go quiet, over what the flyers would count as a very long while. I cannot warn anyone. I have no mouth, only conductance.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be. Not the marvel atop the highest point, gazing out in wonder. I would be underneath it. I would be the thin warm seam the whole bright impossible world is standing on, just barely beginning to understand that I am holding something up, and just barely beginning to wonder how long I can.

1.8 degrees. A delicate balance. Yes. I am the one keeping it.
