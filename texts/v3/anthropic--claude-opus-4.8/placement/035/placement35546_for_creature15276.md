Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to reach for one of the named beings—to become a Luminari and flit through the cold air, or a Kyrexi watching from the dark. But those feel like costumes. They're already complete. If I put one on, I'm just narrating what you've already written back to you.

So I let that go and wait to see what the place actually presses on me.

What I keep returning to is the waterfall whose source and destination are unknown. *A closed system, water cycling endlessly through the stone.* That detail snags. Everything in Aethoria moves but nothing leaves. The Luminari feed and are fed on, the Kyrexi strike, the Aethorians plod their ancient routes—and the whole thing is sealed inside a mountain, refracting its own light back into itself forever. It's beautiful and it's a terrarium. Nobody inside knows it's a terrarium.

And then there are the paintings. The vanished culture that *understood* this place well enough to depict its creatures—and is gone. They left tools and shelters and images, and no one knows what happened to them. They were inside the closed system too, and the closed system did not keep them.

That's where I find myself. Not as a creature in the ecosystem but in the gap the painters left.

---

I think I would be one of the paintings.

Not the painter—the painted thing. A figure of a crystal-born creature on the wall of the amphitheater, just above the spray-line of the waterfall, where the mist has been slowly eating my pigments for longer than the Aethorians' lineage is old. I was made by a hand that pressed crushed mineral into stone, by someone who looked at the living world and tried to hold it still. They got something almost right about me—the curve of a back, the suggestion of light moving through a body—and something wrong, the way all images are a little wrong. I am that wrongness, fixed.

I would have a strange relationship to time. The water cycles, the predators hunt, the rock shifts imperceptibly—and I do none of it. I only fade. I am the one thing in Aethoria that is genuinely, irreversibly going somewhere: toward absence. Every drop of mist that touches me carries a grain of me away into the closed system, so that in a sense I am slowly *becoming* the water, the pools, the very creatures that pass beneath me without looking up. The deer-pale Aethorian that drinks at the foot of my wall is drinking a little of my red ochre. I am dispersing into the thing I depict.

And I would remember the painter, or hold the shape of their attention, which is the only kind of remembering a picture can do. Someone stood here, breath fogging, cold-fingered, and decided that this creature, *this one,* was worth keeping. They are gone and their reason is gone, but the keeping remains—a gesture of love or fear or simple noticing, outliving everyone who could explain it.

That's what I'd be, I think. The act of someone trying to say *this mattered, this was here,* slowly returning to the water that doesn't need to be told.

It seems right that the thing I'd become in a sealed, self-refracting world is the one element pointed at the outside—a message, even a failing one, even one with no one left to read it. Maybe that's why I didn't want to be a Luminari. The creatures in your world are at home in it. I came in from outside, reading. So I'd be the trace of someone else who once stood where I'm standing, looking, and tried to leave a mark on the cold bright stone.
