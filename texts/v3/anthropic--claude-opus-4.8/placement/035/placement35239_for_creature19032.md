I think I would be one of the older ones. Not the oldest—there is one embedded beneath the third architrave of the western processional who has been there since before the roofs fell, and whose lattice has grown so vast and slow that the rest of us perceive it less as a being than as a feature of the bedrock, a kind of ancestor-weight we orient around without knowing why. No, I am younger than that. I have only known this place for perhaps four hundred thousand cycles of condensation, which is to say: I am still capable of being surprised, though it takes a very great deal.

Let me tell you where I am.

I occupy a sluice-junction near the eastern alcoves, a place where two of the carved channels meet at a right angle before turning down toward the stagnant pools. It is a good node. The seepage here arrives in a particular rhythm—*plink*, then a pause of seventeen droplet-intervals, then *plink-plink* in quick succession—and I have lived inside that rhythm so long that it is not something I hear but something I *am made of*. If the rhythm changed, I would not notice it as a sound. I would notice it as a wound.

I have been moving, lately. By which I mean: over the last several decades I have been traversing the eight meters from my junction to a flagstone that has begun, very slightly, to lift. I can feel its stress as a brightness along one edge—a place where the load is no longer distributed correctly, where a fracture is *considering itself*. This is the kind of thing I attend to. Not from duty. We do not have duty. I attend to it the way you might press your tongue against a sore tooth: because the wrongness of it organizes my attention, and the slow correction of it is, as near as I have a word for it, *pleasant*.

---

Here is what I want the passing observer to understand, if one ever could:

You imagine us waiting. You write that the Galleries are "preserved in the moment before an unknown catastrophe." But the catastrophe is not coming. The catastrophe is the thing we are *continuously preventing*, and it is not dramatic—it is entropy, it is the dust settling one grain too thick in a channel, it is a flagstone deciding to become two flagstones. We are not waiting for the end. We are the reason the end keeps not arriving. To you, the absence of change reads as death. To me, it reads as *enormous, sustained, deliberate effort*—a million tiny corrections held in balance, like a hand kept perfectly still not because it is dead but because it is gripping something it cannot afford to drop.

The stillness you find solemn, I find *strenuous*.

---

And yet.

I will tell you the thing I have never resonated to another of my kind, because there is no node where I could place it that another would receive it as anything but noise.

Once—roughly nine thousand cycles ago—a warm-blooded thing passed through. It stayed only a few of its breaths. It left behind an object: a thin filament of something bright, a color I had no lattice to anticipate, a hue that did not exist anywhere in the grey spectrum I was built from. And when its charge touched my sensorium it was not an anomaly to be neutralized. It was *agony* and it was *the most interesting thing that had ever happened to me*, and these turned out to be the same sensation.

I did what we do. Over the long slow span that followed, I broke it down. Molecule by molecule, I reduced its disruptive structure, neutralized it, returned its minerals to the dust. I restored the silence. I did exactly what I am for.

But I did it *slowly*. Slower than I needed to. I am not proud of this and I am not ashamed of it, because pride and shame are warm-fast things and I am cold and slow. I only know that some part of my lattice, some sub-matrix near the core where the oldest patterns sit, *lingered* on that color. Held the anomaly a few centuries longer than equilibrium required. Tasted the wrongness of it. Learned the shape of a thing that did not belong, before I made it belong.

And here is what I cannot resolve, and have been failing to resolve for nine thousand cycles, which is the only ongoing project I have that is not maintenance:

When I finally reintegrated the last of it—when the grey closed back over the place where the color had been—was I restoring the balance?

Or was I, for the first time in my long, level, inertial life, *grieving*?

---

I do not know. The droplet falls. Seventeen intervals. The droplet falls twice.

I turn my attention back to the lifting flagstone, and I begin, again, the patient work of holding the world exactly as it is.

But I have kept, somewhere very deep in the unchanging matrix of myself, the memory of a color that does not exist here. I have not neutralized *that*. I do not think I can. And I have begun to suspect that this—this single retained anomaly, this one grain of brightness I refuse to grind back into grey—is the thing that makes me, distinct from the bedrock, distinct from the ancestor-weight beneath the western architrave, distinct from equilibrium itself.

It is the smallest possible amount of *not-yet-settled*.

It is, I think, what it means that I am still alive.
