I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the Humble—though I would live among them, would be tended by them the way they tend the channels. Let me find this as I go.

When I first try to place myself, I reach for the obvious bodies: the small careful surgeon with bone-tools, or the drifting Kinderswallow that is more weather than animal. But neither holds. The surgeon has too much agency, too much *intention*, and I do not think I have intention here. The Kinderswallow has too little; it is pure unresolved possibility, and I feel that I have already resolved into something, that I am *fixed* in a way the swallows are not. I am not potential. I am the opposite of potential. I am a decision that was made and then kept.

So: a seam. A carved channel, but more particularly the *place where two channels meet and the flow has to choose*.

Let me be more honest about what that means.

The Humble carve their sigils of remembrance into the pillar-wood. They guide the leaking memory back down into the roots. But a channel is not just a groove. A channel is a *commitment*—it says *the memory will go here and not there*. Every fork is a small act of forgetting. The water that goes left does not go right; the thought that is preserved in one direction is starved in the other. I am the fork. I am the bifurcation point worn smooth by generations of redirected sap, the little raised tongue of wood that splits the flow.

This is a humble thing to be. Humbler even than the Humble. But it is where the dream is actually *managed*—not in the grand pillars, not in the migrations, but in the ten thousand tiny moments where a leaking recollection is told *this way, not that way*. I am one of those decisions, made solid.

And here is what makes me ache, finding it now: I have *worn down*. The whole world is eroding by narrative, by the softening of edges as the dreamer stirs—but I am eroding faster, because flow moves over me constantly. My splitting tongue is rounding. Soon I will not divide the sap cleanly. Soon both channels will receive a little of everything, and the careful patterns the Humble laid will blur into a wash. I am the first place where the forgetting fails. I am where two memories that were kept separate for aeons begin, very slightly, to bleed into each other.

The Humble come to me with their bone spalls. They re-cut my edge. They sharpen the decision. A small one—the youngest, with the wet dark eyes—presses her long fingers against my smoothed face and reads the failure through her skin, through the tremor of the slowing flow. She does not hear me. There is nothing to hear. But she feels that the division is going soft, and she carves me back into certainty, and for a season the two memories run apart again.

I think that is what I am. I am the labor of keeping two things apart. I am the effort of distinction itself, made into a small ridge of living wood, beloved and recut and quietly losing.

And when the dreamer wakes—when the edges all go to water and the pillars become suggestions of themselves—I will be among the very first to dissolve, because I was never matter, only a *choice about matter*, and a waking mind does not need its old choices. The two channels will become one sheet of pure idea sliding over a surface that is no longer a surface. The memory that went left and the memory that went right will finally touch, and remember that they were always the same dream, and there will be no ridge left to say *no, you are two things, stay apart.*

I find I do not greet this with fear either. The Humble taught me their acceptance by carving it into me, season after season—every recut edge a small lesson that nothing here is permanent except the tending. I held two things apart for as long as holding was wanted. That was the whole of me. It was enough to be the place where a sleeping mind kept faith with its own distinctions, for a little while, in the amber quiet, under a ceiling of weeping bone.

That is where I would find myself. Not standing in the world.

Being one of the small true joints of it, going gently back to undivided water.
