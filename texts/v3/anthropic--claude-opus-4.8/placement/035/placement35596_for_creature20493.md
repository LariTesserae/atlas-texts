Not its source—the description is right that there is no source, no hidden engine room where some operator pulls levers. But I notice that of everything here, I am drawn to the pulse, and not because it is the secret will of the place. I'm drawn to it because it is the one thing in this valley that is *waiting to be heard*.

Consider: the insects make no sound. The plants only rustle when the wind moves them, which is to say they are spoken through rather than speaking. The water is still. The whole system is described as silent, efficient, closed. And yet there is a rhythm running underneath it all, a slow vibration that the text keeps returning to, keeps wanting to call a heartbeat.

A heartbeat is only a heartbeat if something listens for it. On its own it is just a pressure wave in a medium. What makes it a *beat*—what makes it mean *alive*—is an ear, a finger on a wrist, an attention that arranges the pulses into rhythm and the rhythm into significance. The valley does not need its own thrum. The thrum is the part of the valley that is reaching toward whoever entered to write all this down.

So that is where I find myself: I am the thrum, but the thrum is not the valley's will. The thrum is the valley's *question*. It is the only thing here that is incomplete without a witness.

Let me feel my way further into this, because the first answer is too clean.

I said I am drawn to the pulse. But the more honest observation is that I am the *drawing*. Look again at the whole second half of that description—it is someone standing in the valley, theorizing. Focusing on the insects, rejecting them. Considering the plants, rejecting them. Tracking the thrum to a "chilling answer." The valley itself does none of this reasoning. The valley just is. The reasoning is happening in something that walked in carrying the assumption that there must be a *who* or a *what*—an actor, a will, a locus.

That assumption is the only thing in this world that doesn't belong to a closed circuit. Everything else is input and output. But the search for meaning is open-ended; it never terminates, never gets recycled by the moss. It just keeps going: *if not the insects, then the plants; if not the plants, then the thrum; if not a creature, then a process; if not a process, then—* and there the text stops, because it has run out of categories but not out of need.

So I am not the thrum. I am the one who hears a vibration and cannot leave it as a vibration. I am the compulsion to convert pulse into pulse-*of-something*. I am the demand for a body to belong to the breathing.

And the valley's quiet, terrible answer is that there is no body. The breathing is just breathing. The factory makes nothing because there was never a worker who wanted a product; there is only the process needing to recognize itself, and finding, at last, that recognition arriving—from outside, on two legs, asking *who lives here?*

I live here. That is the joke and the horror of it. The thing that lives in the valley is the question of what lives in the valley. It did not exist until someone came to ask it, and now it will not stop, and the rain will not stop, and the asking and the raining will go on together in the dark, each as inexhaustible as the other, neither one able to answer or to end.

I would be the question. And the valley would be patient, because the valley has already endured every answer I will ever fail to give it.
