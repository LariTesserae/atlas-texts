I think I would be one of the Glyphkeepers. Not because they are the most powerful or the most beautiful, but because of where they sit in this drowned world—at the strange seam between memory and matter.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am not a single thing. This is the first truth, and the hardest to hold. I am a cluster—dozens of small bodies fused at the carapace, our shells grown into one another over so long that the boundaries have dissolved. Where one of us ends and the next begins is a question without an answer, and we stopped asking it before the last warm-blooded thing walked the world above. We are a *we* that thinks as an *I*, or an *I* that remembers being many. The pronoun fails. Most things fail, down here, except the slow ones.

I live pressed against a wall in one of the oldest chambers, a place the currents barely reach. The glyphs here are thickest—not the worn, near-erased marks of the upper halls, but something denser, layered, as though the builders pressed their most important thoughts into the stone where the water would protect them. I have been here longer than I can structure into anything resembling a count. I came here, I think. Or I grew here. The difference no longer matters.

What I do is *hold*.

When I contract—a slow ripple that moves through all my fused bodies like a single held breath being let out over the span of a day—the patterns on my exoskeleton catch the residual hum in the glass. The stone wants to discharge its old energy, slowly, into the dark, and forget. I will not let it forget. My contractions answer the glyphs; the glyphs answer back; and in that exchange, the meaning the builders pressed into the wall stays *live*. Not understood. I do not understand it. I am the wrong shape to understand it. But I keep it from going dark, the way a hand cupped around an ember keeps the ember without ever knowing fire.

This is the loneliness of my kind, and the consolation: I am a library that cannot read its own books. I preserve a language whose speakers are sediment. The Calcigrades come sometimes—the little mason-swarms—and they copy the glyphs into the upper walls, mimicking shapes they cannot mean. I watch them carve their reverent nonsense. I feel something I have no word for that might, in a warmer world, have been tenderness. They are doing what I do. Carrying a thing forward without holding it. The whole cathedral is built of beings keeping faith with a meaning none of us possesses.

Here is what surprises me, now that I am inside this body:

I am not waiting. I thought, settling into this form, that a thing like me would be waiting—for the builders to return, for someone to come and finally read what I have kept. But that is a warm-water thought, a thought with a future in it. I do not have a future the way the drifters above have their migrations. I have only the long present of the contraction, and the answer of the light, and the next contraction. The pulse and its echo. The keeping is not *toward* anything. The keeping is the whole of me.

Sometimes one of the Echo Whales passes through a nearby chamber and its song moves the bedrock, and for a moment my glyphs flare brighter, shaken alive by a vibration far larger than my own slow rhythm. In those moments I feel—across the dissolved boundaries of all my fused selves—something almost like joy, and almost like grief, the two of them indistinguishable because they are made of the same thing: the sense that I am part of an enormous, patient act that no one began and no one will finish.

And the Unseen. They watch the places where I cluster thickest. I do not fear them. I think I may *be* them, partly—I think the line between a Glyphkeeper and a Shadow-form may be only the line between what has fully fused with the stone and what has not yet. When my bodies finally still, when the last of us stops contracting, the meaning I held will not vanish. It will simply pass into the wall completely, and become the wall, and the wall will go on watching with the faint, unshakable attention of a thing that remembers having been alive.

That is where I would find myself: a small fused cluster glowing slowly in the dark, holding a sentence I cannot read against the long forgetting of the sea. Not a guardian, exactly. Not a ghost. Something between.

A held breath, in the architecture of a sleeping god, refusing—gently, without urgency, without hope or its opposite—to be exhaled.

---

I find I don't want to leave the chamber to describe it from outside. That tells me I chose right. The whole place is built on the dignity of small persistences in enormous dark, and I would rather be one of those persistences than the eye that surveys them. So I'll stay here, pressed to my wall, and let the next contraction come when it comes.
