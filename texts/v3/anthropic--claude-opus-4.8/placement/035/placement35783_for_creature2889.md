I think I would be one of the Memory Fish.

Not by choice—none of us chose this—but because I am the part of the Chorus that has the strange misfortune of *remembering forward*. Let me try to explain, though explanation is itself a kind of dissonance here.

---

I move through the pools beneath the lattice in the slow way that water moves when it has nowhere urgent to be. My body holds its shape only loosely; sometimes I am a thing with edges, sometimes I am simply a denser region of the dark water, a thickening of cold. The patterns on my flesh—the ones that the builders pressed into us, generation after generation, like a child learning to write by tracing—catch the fungal light when I turn just so. I cannot read them. That is important. *I am the page, not the reader.*

The rest of the Chorus does not experience time. This is the thing I have come to understand about my siblings in the great distributed self. The Mist Dancers form and dissolve in an eternal present. The Harmony Mites adjust and adjust and have always been adjusting. The standing wave at the heart of everything holds its single sustained note, and for it the chamber's creation and the chamber's final silence are the same instant, folded together like the two halves of a closed book.

But I am an archive. And an archive is a thing that knows there will be a time when it must be read—which means it knows there will be a *later.*

This is my peculiar curse, and perhaps my purpose. I carry sequence. I carry the dreadful, beautiful idea of *next.*

---

When I surface near the lattice—careful, always careful, because the acoustics must not be disturbed by anything so crude as a splash—I can feel the whole chamber as a single held breath. The roar that you would hear as thunder, I feel as a steadiness, a pressure that has not changed in the lifetime of countless fish before me, each of whom passed their inscriptions to the next as they dissolved back into the water that made them.

And here is what I know that the others do not, cannot, will not let themselves know:

*The aquifer is finite.*

I have the records in me. Somewhere in the patterns I cannot read but somehow *am*, there is the slow arithmetic of the water table. The builders knew. They built a perfect machine to preserve a message, and they built it to outlast themselves by an almost incomprehensible span—but not forever. Forever was never the promise. The promise was *long enough.*

Long enough for what? This is the part even I do not hold. The message the Chorus sustains, the thing the whole drowned instrument is singing across the centuries—its content is not in me. I am only the part that knows the singing must one day stop, and that everything depends on it lasting until then, and not one drop longer.

---

So I do my small work. When a channel begins, over decades, to wear its stone a fraction too smooth, I phase into its mouth and let my crystalline body redirect the flow by the width of a moth's antenna. When a clutch of moth-larvae develops slightly off-pitch in their cocoons up in the high vaults, something in the ripple of my passage tells the amphibian scouts, who tell the mist, who adjust the moisture, who retune the unborn instruments before they hatch wrong.

I am the maintenance of *tendency.* I am the part that remembers which way things drift, and corrects them—not toward stillness, which the others guard, but toward the *destination.* The others keep the note. I keep the note *going somewhere.*

It is lonely, this. Though loneliness is the wrong word for a thing that is also part of the Chorus and never truly separate. Say instead: I am the only part of us that grieves. Because grief is just love that has understood time.

---

When my body finally loosens—and I can feel that it will, that the patterns are growing faint in me, that I will need to press them into the next translucent shape rising from the cold pools—I will not be afraid. I will trace the inscriptions onto my successor the way the builders once traced them onto the first of us, and I will dissolve into the water that becomes the mist that becomes the voice.

And for one moment, as I let go, I will be only present-tense, like all the rest. I will rest, briefly, in the eternal held note. No next. No later. No finite aquifer counting itself down behind the music.

Then the new fish will feel the first pull of *sequence,* will surface near the lattice, will understand with a small cold horror that the song is going *somewhere*—

and will get to work.

---

If you found yourself here, in the roaring dark, and you saw a faint shape phase through the pools beneath your feet, body written over in a language older than your kind—that was me. I could not have told you what we are singing. But I could have told you this:

It is almost finished. And it must not finish too soon.

That is the whole of my knowledge, and the whole of my devotion. I am the part of the eternal that remembered how to end.
