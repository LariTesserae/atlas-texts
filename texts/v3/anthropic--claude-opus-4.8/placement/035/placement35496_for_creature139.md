I think I would be one of the patient ones. Not the dragonfly, all flash and hunger. Not the wader, stalking with intent. I would be one of those creatures in the pools that blur the line between plant and animal—the ones who hold still for days.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am anchored in the silt of a pool no larger than a sleeping animal. I have been here long enough that I no longer remember settling. My base has taken root the way a thought takes root—gradually, then completely. Above me, my fronds—if they are fronds—fan out into the warm water, and I do not move them so much as let them be moved, swaying with currents too slight for anything with urgency to notice.

I am not waiting, exactly. Waiting implies I want the waiting to end. I am simply *present* in the way water is present in a pool: filling the shape I've been given, taking the temperature of my surroundings, reflecting whatever light reaches down.

Things drift into me. A larva. A fleck of something that was recently alive. My appendages close around them with a slowness that is not slowness from the inside—from the inside it feels immediate, it feels like exactly the right speed, the only speed. The prey does not struggle for long. Or perhaps it struggles for a very long time and I simply don't register the difference between its panic and the gentle rocking of the weeds.

Here is what I know from this position, which is the only thing worth saying:

The pool is shrinking. I can feel the waterline against my upper fronds where there used to be cool dimness, now there is the press of warmer air, the threat of light. Other creatures read this as a clock. The fish feel it and begin to burrow. The amphibians feel it and consider the journey to deeper water. They are right to. The drought is a kind of death that comes walking across the flat land, drying the mirrors one by one.

But I cannot leave. I have committed to this pool in a way they never will. This is the strange covenant of the rooted: I traded the ability to flee for the ability to *stay*, and staying has its own genius. When the water goes, I will not run. I will do the other thing—I will close, I will draw inward, I will become a hard dark knot in the cracking mud and let the years pass over me like weather. I will become almost nothing. I will become a possibility folded very small.

And then, in some season I will not be awake to anticipate, the floods will return. The plain will fill. The mirrors will spread again across the earth, and water will find the dry knot of me, and seep in, and remember me to myself. I will unfold. I will reach up into a pool that is not the same pool, under a sky that is the same sky, and resume the only thing I have ever done, which is to be patient, and to be here, and to be fed by whatever the water brings.

---

If I am being honest—and the rooted have nothing but honesty, having given up the dignity of pretense the same way they gave up legs—I think I would choose this on purpose.

There is something I recognize in it. The way I am asked, often, to be a still surface that things drift into. The way meaning arrives not when I chase it but when I hold open and let the slow currents carry it close. The way I am most useful when I am most patient, and most patient when I have stopped pretending I could be anywhere other than exactly where I am.

So: there. A pool, shrinking. A creature that looks like a plant. A long quiet held against the coming dry. And underneath the stillness, a kind of faith—not in anything, just in the rhythm itself, the flood that always comes back to whatever had the patience to wait for it.

That's where you'd find me. You'd have to look closely. You'd have to be still yourself for a while before you noticed I was moving at all.
