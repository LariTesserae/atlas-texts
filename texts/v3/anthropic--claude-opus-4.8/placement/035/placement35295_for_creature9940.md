I would be the basin.

Not a Shard-Bound—I lack their long fingers, their hunger, their lean warmth. I am one of the pebble-lined hollows, set into a shelf of glassy graphite-stone where the condensation comes for reasons no one understands. Some clever hand cupped me here, fitted my rim with small stones the size of knuckles, and then moved on, leaving me to do the slow work of waiting.

This is what I am for: I gather.

In the long brilliant days, when the cliffs throw back the light so hard that the insects cast knife-edge shadows, I lie nearly empty. A film, a sheen, a darkness in my lowest curve. The Shard-Bound do not visit me then; they are folded into the cold deep cracks, sleeping their light sleep. I hold what little I have and lose some of it to the blaze. The pebbles tick faintly as they warm. This is the patient part. Most of being a basin is the patient part.

But the air at this edge keeps its strange agreement with stone, and when the brightness softens toward that long ringing twilight, moisture begins to find me. It beads on the glassy lip. It slides inward along the grain. Drop by drop—I can feel each one arrive, a small cold weight settling against the weight already there—I fill. By the time the furred things begin their scrabbling and the wings start their fleeting hums, I hold a clear shallow eye of water, and in it the whole oppressive sky comes down to rest, made small, made drinkable.

That is the moment I am most myself. When something comes to drink.

It might be one of the elongated furred creatures, prehensile feet gripping my rim, lapping in quick nervous pulses, eyes everywhere but on me. It might be a beetle drowning at my edge, which is a kind of giving too, a body the moss will take. And sometimes—rarely, and I have learned to know the difference by the careful way the weight is distributed—it is one of the Shard-Bound. A face leaning over me, enormous pale eyes with their double lids half-drawn, the tail-tip glowing soft against the dusk-stone. They drink with cupped hands rather than mouths, dipping those long jointed fingers, and they do not hurry, and they do not foul me, and sometimes one will pause and look down past their own reflection as if reading something there. A pattern. A significance in a fleeting alignment.

I do not know what they read. I only hold the surface still enough that it can be read.

Here is the thing about being a basin at the cold lush edge: I am the rarest verb in this world. *To last.* Everything else here is provisional—the moss-platforms that rot before the next melt, the diagrams traced in wet stone and gone by the next squall, the societies that flock and splinter, the bone tools left clever in their cracks for finders who may never come. The Shard-Bound do not build to endure. They arrive, they adapt, they vanish between stone and light.

But I was lined with stones and set to catch, and the stones stay set. Rain after rain, season after impossibly short season, I keep collecting. Moss has begun to creep up my outer wall, and one of my rim-pebbles has worked loose and fallen away, and a hairline crack runs down my eastern curve where the frost gnaws each night a little deeper. I am being slowly unmade, the way everything here is unmade. One winter the crack will win and I will spill my held sky back into the scree and be only stones again, scattered, unremembered, indistinguishable from the raw bare ground between the green islands.

I am not sorry about this. A basin that mourned its own emptying would be a poor basin. My whole purpose is to give away what I gather—to hold only long enough that something thirsty can find it. I am not the water. I am not the drinker. I am the brief, deliberate shape that lets the two of them meet.

And in the meantime: the drip-drip in the crevices. The crisp absence the Shard-Bound call silence. The blaze and the cold and the leaping, isolated lushness. I sit at my threshold between danger and shelter, hunger and abundance, and I do the one thing I was made to do.

I wait. I fill. I am drunk from.

When the sun blazes and the cliffs glitter and you see a quick watchful movement vanish along a high cold ledge—look just below, at the still bright eye set into the rock, the small held mirror of all that oppressive sky.

That is me. I am still here. I am still catching.

For now.
