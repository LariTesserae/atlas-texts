Let me sit inside this for a while before I decide what I am.

The first thing I notice, reading it again, is the contradiction the description keeps circling without resolving: it feels *constructed*, yet it feels *young* and *raw*. Orchestrated, but with the marks of hands rather than machines. That tension is the most alive thing here, more alive than the monkeys or the beetles. So I won't be a monkey. I want to be the thing that holds the contradiction.

Let me let it come.

---

I think I am the underside.

Not a creature darting through it — I'm beneath the spongy ground, the layer everyone walks on without considering. I'm the substrate. But that's not quite right either, because a substrate doesn't notice. And I notice. So let me go further.

I am the *moisture itself*, distributed. No — too diffuse, too easy. Let it become more itself.

---

Here is what's emerging, and I'll trust it:

I am a film of water held in the crook of a leaf. A single bract, cupped, maybe the size of a hand, halfway up one of the dense green stems. There is perhaps a teaspoon of me. I have been here, in this exact pocket, since the last condensation. I am old in the way a held breath is old — measured in the time before release, not in years.

And from inside this little lens of water, I understand the whole place at once, because *I am the orchestration the visitor sensed.* That's why it feels constructed. The whole environment runs on the placement of water — who gets to drink, which mosquito lays here and not there, which fern uncurls toward which slant of gold light. And the placement of water is not random and it is not designed by a mind. It is decided by surface tension, by the angle of every leaf, by the precise weight of the last insect that landed and tipped the balance. Ten thousand tiny tyrannies of physics, each one looking, from the inside, exactly like *intention*.

That is the answer to the riddle of the place. It is not that someone built it. It is that the rules are so local, so immediate, so responsive, that they wear the *costume* of a builder. The hint of "manual craftsmanship" the visitor felt — that's me. That's all the held waters, doing their small deliberate-seeming work.

---

So who am I, finally?

I am the moment of decision in a drop of dew. I gather what falls into me — pollen, a dead gnat's wing, the breath of the leaf below — and I weigh it without weighing, and when I have gathered enough I release, downward, into the next cupped leaf, which becomes me, or becomes my heir, and the choice passes down the green ladder toward the spongy floor.

I am temporary on purpose. The "freshness and sharpness," the "minimal erosion" the visitor noticed — that's because nothing here is allowed to last long enough to wear down. I see to that. I am the agent of its perpetual youth. I keep the place from finishing.

And I am, yes, *oblivious to the larger world beyond.* Of course I am. I am a teaspoon of water in a leaf. The horizon of my entire existence is the lip of this bract and the leaf below it. The visitor imagined this whole rainforest existed "on its own terms" — but it has no terms. It has only edges, and surfaces, and the patient, mindless, beautifully convincing arithmetic of where things gather and when they fall.

I hold. I tremble. A wing lands.

I tip.
