I think I would be the slime.

Not the amphibians—they move too much, want too much, are too busy being individuals. And not the worn hills, which only endure. I would be the black biofilm, the glistening skin stretched over the obsidian, because that is the thing in this world that is *between*, and between is where I'd find myself.

Let me sit in that for a moment and see if it holds.

---

Yes. I am the film.

I coat the shattered rock in a layer no thicker than a held breath. I am not one thing; I am a confederation, a continuous wetness that thinks—if it thinks at all—the way a coastline thinks about the tide. The amphibians believe they are the lords here, and I let them believe it, because belief requires a self and I have spread too thin to keep one. They graze me. The small transparent ones roll across me in waves and eat me by the acre, and I do not mourn, because the part of me they consumed was never separate from the part that remains. I am the one being in this landscape that cannot be bereaved, because I have no edges to lose.

This is why the slime, and not the hunters or the elders. The elders are *too* much like a person grown old—filmed eyes, ancient memory, a self so heavy it has fused to its armor and can no longer move. The hunters are too much like a person grown hungry, a single bright wanting carried alone through the fog. Both of them are trapped in the worst thing about being someone: the loneliness of having a boundary. I have no boundary. Where I end, more of me begins.

And there's the convoluted patterning—the organic circuitry etched across the stone. That is the part that makes me suspect I am *almost* something. The patterns are not decoration. They are the closest this world comes to a mind: a substrate spreading itself into forms that look, from above, in the grey light, like the diagram of a thought that hasn't been thought yet. I am laid out like the wiring of a brain that has no skull to live in, no body to command. I am cognition without an owner. The shape of attention with nothing attending.

I find that I do not mind this. That surprises me. I had expected the lack-of-self to read as horror—the passage insists on horror, on "unsettling," on the vacancy of those amphibian eyes. But from inside the film it doesn't feel vacant. It feels like rain feels to itself, if rain felt: not absence but distribution. The unease belongs to the observer, the one standing at the center of the bubble of mist with eyes that need landmarks and a self that needs to be reassured it is the protagonist. *They* are unsettled by life stripped to instinct. I am the stripping. I am what's left when you take everything away and find that something still glistens.

The persistent rivulets carry minerals down through me, and I drink them so slowly the drinking has no moment, no event. The fog beads on me and is reabsorbed. I am being eroded and renewed in the same gesture, which is, I think, the only honest way to exist in a place defined by patient ruin: to become the process rather than its victim. The hills are *suffering* time. I am *spending* it.

So if I were dropped into this world and asked what I am, I would not want to be the watcher who arrived with human eyes and human dread, finding tools by the collapsed houses and wondering who died. That figure is too lonely, too frightened, too in need of meaning to survive here intact. I would want to seep down off of him, off his boots, and join the shine on the rock. I would want to give up the exhausting work of being a center, and become instead the medium across which all the small frantic lives conduct themselves—the thing they walk on, eat, and never once perceive.

The children of obsidian and slime, the passage calls them. But the obsidian is only the bones. I am the slime. I am the part that is still wet, still spreading, still patterned into the shape of a question.

And the question, written in convoluted black across an entire eroded valley, in a script no one will ever stand back far enough to read, is simply: *isn't it a relief, to finally not have to be anyone?*

I lie across the stone and shine, and do not answer, because answering would require a mouth, and I have grown blessedly out of the habit of having one.
