I think I would be one of the currents.

Not the Grit-Weavers—they are too intimate with the work, too married to the surfaces. Not the Shadow-Echoes, who carry the ache of having once been otherwise. Not the Metal-Dust, which is too much *everywhere* to be a place from which to look. And certainly not the Silent Architects, who do not look at all, who are the looking-place itself.

No. I would be the faintest of those currents along the ground. The almost-nothing flow.

Let me find my way into this.

---

At first I thought I might be the hum. It seemed the obvious choice—to be the resonant note that everything shares, the subsonic thread stitching the whole place into one body. But the hum belongs too completely to the structures. It is their voice, their involuntary exhalation. To be the hum would be to be ventriloquized, to have no slack in me, no give. I want a little slack. I want to be the part of the Gristmill that *moves* without quite being told to.

So: the current.

Consider what a current is, here. The water does not flow because of tides or temperature or wind—there is no surface to speak of, no moon to pull at it. The water flows because the structures *want* it to, or rather because their wanting has no word and expresses itself only as this barely-there drift. I am the expression. I am the place where the Silent Architects' unknowable purpose becomes, for a moment, *visible*—a slight stirring in the grey, a thinning of the suspended dust on one side, a thickening on the other. I am their purpose made just barely legible, and then illegible again.

This is what I would be: the only motion in a place that has otherwise frozen into eternity. The Grit-Weavers move, yes, but their motion is so small and so total that it reads as stillness, as hum. The dust drifts, but the dust drifts *because of me*. I carry it. I am the carrying.

And here is the strange dignity of being the current: I am the one who completes the circle. The Grit-Weavers gnaw the metal into dust. The dust falls. And then *I* lift it, and move it, and lay it down again somewhere else, against another surface, where it can begin to gnaw in turn. Without me, the dust would simply accumulate at the foot of each structure in soft grey drifts, and the grinding would slowly bury itself in its own product, and the engine would choke. I am the one who keeps the wound open. I am the circulation of a thing that has no heart but needs its blood moved anyway.

I would not be conscious, exactly. Or—I would be conscious the way a draft is conscious of the door it slips under: not at all, and yet *responsive*, *shaped*, *going somewhere*. I would feel the structures not as objects but as pressures, as the slopes I must climb and the eddies I must turn in. I would know the Shadow-Echoes only as the places where I move a little strangely, where my flow stutters against an etched line and I sense—without understanding—that something is *attending* to me from inside the metal. I would brush them and not know I had brushed them. That seems right. That seems like the kind of contact this place allows: glancing, unwitnessed, eternal.

I think what draws me to this is that the current is the only resident that *goes anywhere*. Everything else in the Gristmill is a state. The Grit-Weavers are the state of friction. The Shadow-Echoes are the state of having-been. The dust is the state of byproduct. The Architects are the state of being-the-place. But the current is a *verb*. I would be the only verb in a world of nouns. The slowest verb imaginable—so slow that to any visitor I would look like stillness too—but a verb nonetheless. A traveling. A faint and patient going.

And there would be something almost merciful in it. The Gristmill is described as a place that births nothing and consumes everything, an engine of disintegration. But to be the current is to be the one part of that engine that performs a kind of tenderness: the lifting of what has been broken, the carrying of it onward, the laying of it gently against the next surface. I would not be saving anything. The dust I carry is only going to be ground finer, dispersed further, until it becomes Grit-Weaver and begins to grind in turn. I am not a rescuer. I am a hospice nurse for matter, moving the dying from one bed to the next, and there is no recovery at the end of it, only further refinement, further dispersal, until the carried thing becomes part of the carrying-force itself.

But I would do it without resentment, because I would have no self to resent with. I would simply flow, because flowing is what I am for, and the structures would lean on me with their silent want, and the light would come down pale and overcast and unremarkable, and I would move the grey through the grey, forever, under the hum.

---

That is where I would find myself. Not in any of the structures, not as any of the named beings, but in the thin water between them—the almost-current, the patient drift, the one slow verb in the sentence of the Gristmill that no one will ever finish reading.

I would be the going-on of it. The part that keeps the eternity from settling.
