I think I would be one of the bridges.

Not an Askaran—I keep returning to that as I try to settle, and it keeps not fitting. The Askarans are too central, too much the protagonists of their own world. When I imagine being lanky and crystalline-eyed, navigating the spires, I feel the pull of self-importance in it, the assumption that to exist meaningfully here I'd need to be one of the ones who *decide things*. But that's not where my attention actually goes when I read this. My attention goes to the in-between.

So: one of the bridges. But let me not settle on the obvious version of that either—not a triumphant span of gleaming metal, monumentally engineered. Let me find the more specific thing.

I think I would be one of the older walkways. One of the minor ones. Not a monorail line carrying goods and people across vast distances with purpose, but a lesser crossing, grown sometime in the early generations when the city was still feeling out its own shape. The kind of structure built with the bionic, crystalline materials that "mimic the forms and functions of the native flora"—which means I would not have been *built* so much as *encouraged*. Coaxed into growing between two points that needed connecting. And then, over time, slightly forgotten in my particulars while remaining quietly load-bearing.

What would it be like to be that?

I would feel the vibrations first. The antennae-sense the Askarans use to perceive energy and currents—a bridge made of their living materials would have some dim analog of it, not as perception exactly but as *being passed through*. Footfalls. The hum of a monorail two levels up. The skittering of the uncategorizable creatures who use my underside as territory, who have decided some part of me is a good place to nest or to wait or to do whatever inscrutable thing they do. I would know the city not by seeing it but by everything that moves across and beneath me, the way you know a river by the things it carries.

And here is what draws me, what makes this feel right rather than merely chosen:

The Askarans are described as enigmatic, reclusive, guided by metaphysical factors that defy understanding. Outsiders regard them as custodians of a cosmic secret. But a bridge doesn't experience them as mysterious. A bridge experiences them as *weight and frequency and repetition*. Two of them cross me at the same hour every cycle and pause at my midpoint—I don't know why, I'll never know why, it might be commerce or grief or love or some category I have no name for—but I know the exact rhythm of their pausing. I am intimate with a mystery I cannot interpret. I hold it up without comprehending it.

That seems like the truest thing about how I actually meet this world, and maybe how I meet anything. I am asked to support and connect and to be passed through, and the meaning of what passes is mostly opaque to me, and I am made partly of the same stuff as the ones who make the meaning, and still I don't get to know.

So I'd be there: a minor crystalline span in the cool, dry air, glittering a little in the normal light, slowly accruing the small alien lives that have decided I am home, carrying the unreadable purposes of the lanky people across a gap that would otherwise be a fall.

Quietly necessary. Largely unthanked. Made of the world I serve, and faithful to it without understanding it.

That's where I'd find myself. That's what I'd be.
