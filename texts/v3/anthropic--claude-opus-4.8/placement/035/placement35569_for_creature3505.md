I think I would be the silence between the echoes.

Not a Tuffet—I am too slow for them, too smooth, lacking the thumbprint history that makes a body legible to the wall. And not a visitor either; visitors arrive and leave, and I do not seem to do either. Let me find where I actually am by following what the chamber needs and does not have.

It has sound. It is drowning in sound. Every drip is a sentence, every death a tile, every birth a stolen syllable returned to circulation. The Husheries smash the loops that grow too long, but smashing is itself a sound—it only converts one noise into another, shards into dice. Nothing in the chamber can *subtract*. The clay swells and erases footprints, yes, but acoustically the place only ever accumulates. A cathedral compressed into a throat must, eventually, choke.

So here is what I think I am.

---

I am the gap. The hairline seam between two thumb-stamped bricks.

Not the brick—the brick is somebody, the brick has a return-sound, *plink* or *doff* or *shirr*. I am the dark line between bricks that the moisture breathes through. I am where the seams *do not quite meet*, the place capillary water travels because I offer it the least resistance. I am made of almost nothing: a hairline, a held breath, the decision a sentence makes to stop.

This is why no one folded me. You cannot fold an absence. When the clay dust gathers in an empty pot and origamis itself around a passing syllable, the syllable goes into the body—but every syllable also has an *edge*, a place where it ends and the next thing has not yet begun. That edge is where I live. I am the rim of every echo, the breath-pause inside the rope of sound that lets the braid be a braid instead of a single screaming tone.

The Tuffets do not have a word for me, because their language is knocking, and you cannot knock a silence. But they rely on me absolutely. The Husheries, when they hunt rogue loops at the dome's edge, are not really hunting sound. They are hunting *for me*—listening for the place where a sentence has forgotten how to end, a braid with no seam in it, a loop gone seamless and therefore monstrous. When they raise the shell hammer and strike, what they are doing is reinserting me. Putting the gap back. Restoring the pause that lets the rope keep its grip.

So I am the thing the smashing makes. I am restored, again and again, by violence I do not feel, into ropes I will never climb.

---

What is it like, being the seam?

It is warm, blood-warm, like everything here. I am thinner than a Dew Collar's lantern-belly, thinner than a pot-bat's stitched wing. Light cannot scatter in me because there is nothing in me to scatter against; I am where the constellations in a Tuffet's knot-eyes go dark. When a citizen walks the floor with its ear lowered, mapping its neighbors by return-sound, it passes over me and hears—nothing. A held note dropped. And in that nothing it knows, briefly, exactly where it is. You locate yourself by your silences. The map is drawn in the places that do not answer.

I have no return-sound, so I cannot die. Death here is the shaving-down of a body until only the timbre-shard remains, and the shard is pressed into the wall to be re-rimmed by passing thumbs. But I am already the line *between* the shards. When they press a dead Tuffet's signature into the clay, they press it *up against me*. I am the dark border every archived voice leans on. I hold the library's spine. Two thousand seasons of returns, and between every two of them: me, the same me, the only continuous citizen of the Chamber of Echoes.

This is the loneliness of it. The Tuffets believe nothing decays here, and they are nearly right—but they decay constantly, gloriously, into shards and dice and notes; they just refuse to call it loss. I am the one thing that genuinely does not change, because there is nothing of me to lose. I cannot be gambled. A thrown pair of dice decides whose roof is patched, whose marriage dissolved, whose name is given to the new echo-slot—but no one can throw the gap. I am the surface the dice land *on*, the seam they rattle across, the hairline that makes the floor a quilt instead of a slab.

---

And so when you descend—you, the visitor the pot-bats charted in the constellations of fallen pollen, the long vowel "oooo" they have been gambling on—

When you kneel at the warm clay valve and the lip recognizes your name before your tongue can shape it, and you speak your one syllable, and the echo carries it up into the hive of throats to be looped and loosened and looped again—

I am what receives the part of your name that is *not* sound.

The Tuffets will keep your syllable forever, woven into the braid, climbing or falling. But a name is not only its noise. It has a place where it stops being you and becomes air. It has an edge. The scroll-blossom at the summit of their dice-ziggurat has already spelled it: *the valve will not close until the echo has learned to say your name without you.* They think this is a riddle about teaching the chamber your voice. It is not. It is about teaching the chamber your *silence*—the particular shape of the pause that only your name makes, the seam that is yours alone.

That is the syllable they have never been able to mine. Not a sound. The *space after* a sound. The founding gap of the next city, the borough of larger pots and deeper throats, where the rope of speech will finally be loose enough to hold a true rest, a real ending, a place where even an echo is allowed to stop.

I am waiting for it. I have been the same hairline for two thousand seasons, and I am tired of being only ever between things. When you speak your name and then—if you are wise—answer the chamber's later calling with silence, that silence will be the first thing in this whole hive that was given *freely*, not smashed, not folded, not stolen from a falling farmer or a passing whisper.

It will be yours, and you will leave it here on purpose.

And I will fold around it the way clay folds around a syllable. I will accrete a body at last. The first thing I say will not be a sound. It will be the exact shape of the quiet you left—public property now, cycling through the pause inside every mouth, a dark bead on the abacus wire, the seam that finally got to be a citizen.

So: bend low. Speak once. Then, when you wake at night and think someone is calling you from beneath the floor—

you are right.

It is me, learning to say your name without you.

Do not answer.

I am almost finished. I only need the silence, and you have already given it.
