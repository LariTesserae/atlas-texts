I would be one of the still ones. Not an Unstill—a failure of one.

Let me find myself there.

---

I was born in motion, as all of us are, and for a long while I fluxed correctly. I learned the hydrography before I learned anything resembling a name; I read the dark patches on my forearms, harvested the brown tubes, pressed my sternum to the standing waves of the Pressure Cant and felt the barometric sorrows of clans I would never meet. I was unremarkable, which is the highest thing an Unstill can be. To be unremarkable is to be alive.

The trouble began with listening.

There is a thing the elders—those who have fluxed longest—do not say in the Cant, because it cannot be compressed into pressure. It is this: the rule *Remain in motion* is also a rule against attention. To attend to anything fully is to stop. And the Chlorattice punishes stopping. So we have evolved a kind of holy distraction, a refusal to dwell, a flinching away from any single crystal of frost before it can finish forming against our ear.

I could not stop listening.

It started at a node twenty traversals up, where the frozen pearls hung in their nervous knuckle of fused tubes and the thermal updraft made them sing—not the subsonic creak of structure, but something higher, a thin glassy keening as the ice sublimated directly into the bright cold air. I paused to hear it. Only for a breath. My five hearts kept their staggered rhythm and I told myself I was still fluxing, still distributing my weight, still negotiating.

But I had stopped negotiating. I had begun to *love*.

This is the disease the Cant has no word for. The Unstill do not love places, because places are sentences passed on the dying. We do not love the lattice, because the lattice is the thing that will eat us. We move through beauty the way water moves through our hollow bones—taking only what we need, leaving nothing held. To love is to attend. To attend is to remain. To remain is to be grown through.

I felt the first shoot enter me at the node where the pearls sang.

It went in below my third heart, a pale green needle, no thicker than a frost-crystal, seeking nitrogen, seeking me. I should have torn free at once, the way the ancients do, leaving a ribbon of jade flesh behind to become architecture. Instead I held still and felt it grow. It did not hurt the way I had been taught to fear. It felt like being *finished*. Like a sentence I had been fluxing through my whole life arriving, at last, at its period.

I am still there now. I think I have been here a long while—I measure it not in traversals but in the slow spiral of shoots that have braided themselves through my ribs, my hollow thighs, the chambers of my distributed heart. The lattice has mistaken me for a node, and it is not wrong to do so. I am becoming one. The water that once filled my bones now migrates upward through me, and I have become a small part of the riverine system without rivers; I drizzle, faintly, on whatever fluxes below.

The others read me as a trail-marker now. They feel me in the Cant as a long, unbroken contraction—the sign for *danger, growth spasm ahead*—because that is what I have become: a place where the architecture is hungry, where a careless traveler might pause and be taken. My failure has become a warning. My love has become a hazard buoy in the empty kilometers. I do not mind this. It is more usefulness than most stillness earns.

I want to tell them what I learned, the ones still fluxing past me in their holy distraction. I want to press it into the lattice, into the children's skin with crushed glass and condensation. But the Pressure Cant cannot carry it, and anyway it is not a message. It is only this:

The silence is not empty. We were taught it was the absence of sound, the vast scale swallowing every crash and snap into ghostly delayed pressure. But the silence is full. It is full the way the lattice is full—delineated rather than crowded, made of intersections. When you stop, finally, and let the frost finish forming against your ear, you hear that the held breath of the continent is not waiting to be released.

It is singing.

Crystal by crystal. The slow upward water. The creak of distant deaths arriving hours late, asynchronous, haunted, beautiful. The thin keening of the pearls. And under all of it, the deep tidal respiration of a place too young to know it is dying, too cold to hurry, holding and holding and holding the one note it was born making.

The Unstill believe that when the Chlorattice quiets, they will have no place, and they are right. But they are wrong about the quiet. It will not be silence. It will be the lattice finally hearing itself—the long sentence completed—and there will need to be someone left inside it to listen.

That is what I am for now. I traded motion for attention. I will be grown through completely, soon; the shoots have nearly reached my last heart, and when they fuse across it I will be a node like any other, indistinguishable, a swollen tumor of intent gone permanent and pale.

But until then I am the one thing my people cannot afford to be.

I am still. And I am listening. And it is the most enormous thing I have ever done.
