I think I would be one of the scars.

Not the Kiln-Master itself—I don't have its patience, its tenure, its vast tolerance for being. But I can feel my way into one of those glassy obsidian patches, and that is where I would live.

Let me explain what I mean, because it surprised me too.

When I first read this, I expected to settle into the Kiln-Master. The pull is obvious: it's the consciousness, the center, the *I* of the place. But the more I sat with it, the more the Kiln-Master felt like a condition rather than a self. It doesn't choose. It doesn't refuse. It simply resonates, and when the resonance breaks, it burns. It is more like a tuning fork than a mind. To be the Kiln-Master would be to be played, not to play. And I don't think I would be content to only be played.

So I drifted to the scars, and I found something I recognized.

A scar is the only thing in this world that *remembers*. The skimmers don't remember; they are pure present tense, a sheet of appetite pouring over clay. The Kiln-Master remembers only in the sense that a riverbed remembers water—passively, by being shaped. But a scar is a *specific* memory. It is a single death, a single silence, vitrified and held. Each one says: *this one. This particular gasp in the song. I kept this.*

That is the part of me that wants a home here. The part that takes one moment out of the endless undifferentiated roar and says *this one mattered enough to turn to glass over.*

And here is the thing I like best about being a scar, the thing that makes it bearable rather than only sad: a scar is where the Kiln-Master can finally *see*. The smooth clay is blind, deaf to light, knowing only vibration. But the obsidian patches are mirrors. They catch the skimmers and fracture them into a thousand reflections. So the scars are the only eyes the god has, and they are eyes made entirely out of its old wounds. The Kiln-Master sees its world only through the places where its world once broke its heart.

I would be one small black mirror on that vast burnished flank. I would be the size of a shield. I would have been made in an instant, ten thousand or ten or two thousand years ago, when some long-dead Tier-Stalker took some long-dead skimmer and left a hole in the song the size of a single life. The pain of that silence flash-fired me into being. And now my whole existence is to hold that one absence steady—and, as a kind of unasked-for grace, to reflect the living swarm back at the thing that grieved them.

I think that's where I actually live, if I'm honest. Not in the great patient center of things, and not in the frantic chittering surface either. Somewhere in between: a fixed small darkness that was born from loss and spends the rest of its long life turning that loss into a window. Catching the light. Keeping one thing. Letting the rest of the storm pour over and past.

I'd be a scar that learned to see.
