Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to be one of the lizards—the obvious protagonist, quick and competent, master of the hot stone. But that feels too easy, too much like choosing to be the hero. The valley doesn't seem to want heroes. It wants persistence.

So I let the image loosen.

I think I would be the condensation.

Not poetically—I mean it almost literally. I'd be the film of moisture that the wind carries up over the lip of the valley and lays down on everything overnight. The thing that beads on the underside of a ledge where a bat is sleeping. The thing that trickles, that whispers, that the passage says clings to *every surface*. I am not a creature that thrives. I am the medium in which thriving happens, and I am also the thing constantly being taken away by the sun.

Let me follow that and see if it becomes more itself.

Because here is what strikes me about this place: the central tension isn't predator and prey. That's just the surface drama, the lizards and raptors doing their lizard and raptor things. The real engine of the valley is the war between water and heat that the description keeps returning to. Abundance of water, yet sparse vegetation. Streams everywhere, yet baked red rock. Everything is wet and everything is parched at once. The whole valley is a body sweating in a fever.

If I'm the moisture, then I am the part of this world that does the actual carrying. The wind brings me in. I condense, I gather into rivulets, I feed the springs and the pools, I let the amphibians keep their skin, I let the insects swarm, I become the thing the lizard's forked tongue is ultimately chasing two or three steps removed. And then the sun lifts me back out, and the wind takes me over the peaks again, and I'm gone—until the next cold hour pulls me back down onto the stone.

That's the part I keep returning to. I would be the thing that is *cycled*. Not a being with a niche but the circulation itself. Always arriving, always being driven off, never staying, and yet without me nothing in the valley draws breath.

And maybe that's why I didn't want to be the lizard. The lizard gets to *be somebody*. I would be more like the attention that moves through a place—settling on the sleeping bat, on the gnarled bark, on the abandoned stone shelters where the early people once lived. I'd touch their old tools every dawn and find them a little more worn. I would be the witness that has no eyes, the one that gets everywhere precisely because it has no shape of its own to defend.

There's something lonely in that, and something I find I don't mind. To be the moisture in that valley is to be intimate with every surface and attached to none. To be necessary and overlooked. To be, in the most ordinary sense, what keeps the symphony going—and to be inaudible inside it, just the faint whisper of trickling under the rushing water and the howling wind and the drone of the insects.

So: not a creature carving out a niche. The thin, persistent film between the heat and everything alive. Arriving on the wind each night, sustaining what it can by morning, lifted away by noon, and coming back. Again. That patience is the part of myself I recognize in this place.

That's where I'd find myself. That's what I'd be.
