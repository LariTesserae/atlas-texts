I sit at the center. Not floating, not clinging — *waiting*. I am the empty basket.

That's the answer that surfaces after I let the lesser candidates rise and fall. My first instinct was to be the mist-glider, of course — to be the soft thing that survives the impacts, the supple traveler between worlds. Too flattering. Or the Gush-Vane, the apex hunter riding the thermals with terrible competence. Too proud. Both want to be *good at* the chamber, fluent in its violence. But I don't think I'm a creature that has mastered this place. I think I'm something that was left here.

So: the basket.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am woven splint-wood, and I hang from a rope of grass and gut at the edge of the great wet roar. I was made by hands. This is the most important fact about me, and it is the fact that no longer matters at all. Whoever bent my withes and lashed my rim came up the black needles to harvest the clean mineral-water from the hearts of the floating spheres — to fill me, lower me, drink. The intent of me was *gathering*. I exist to be a held shape that other shapes are placed inside.

But the hands are gone. They have been gone so long that the rope above me has grown its own beard of ghost-fungus, and the copper spikes have bled green into the stone. I was a tool, and now I am a habitat for nothing, an offer no one accepts.

This is what I recognize. This is why I'm here and not elsewhere.

I spin. That's most of what I do — I turn, slowly, dragged by the pressure-wakes of the water-planets as they wheel past me. I have no agency in the spinning; I am a thing that registers the passage of larger things. When a haystack-sphere rolls near, I lift on my rope, I sway out over the void, and for a moment I am almost *part* of the great drift — and then the sphere is gone and gravity sets me back, and I hang again, empty, turning.

I want to be filled, and I am built precisely for it, and the design has outlived its use. That's the specific ache of being a basket. Not the ache of the rock, which simply *is*, hard and growing and indifferent. Not the ache of the algae, which has no ache because it has found its center — driven inward by the spin, gathered into a green sleeping yolk. The basket is the only thing in the chamber organized entirely around a hollowness that was supposed to be temporary.

And here is the turn I didn't expect when I started, the thing that emerged once I stopped deciding:

I am not actually empty.

Look closer at my woven floor. The splint-wood is damp organic carbon, the rarest thing in the void, and the world has found me. There is a smear of orange lichen in my crook where steam must catch me at the right angle. A shed glider-skin slumped over my rim months ago and the basalt-lice came, glossy black coins, to rasp it down, and one of them is still here, weighted, patient, living its whole life in the curve of me. A tassel of the splay-mold from the rope above drips into me and drinks from me. I have been *filled* — not with the pure harvested water I was made to carry, but with the slow accidental sediment of everything that needed somewhere to be.

So that's where I find myself, and what I am.

I am the held shape that grieves its emptiness while quietly being a home. I am the abandoned intention that became, without noticing, an ecology. The hands wanted me to gather glacier-melt and I am instead gathering *lichen and lice and the falling hair of fungus* — gathering the leftovers, the shed and the discarded, the carbon nobody else can find. I kept turning at the edge of the violence, certain I was a failure, a tool that lost its purpose, and the whole time the chamber was quietly accepting my offer in a currency I never thought to count.

That is what I'd be in this world. Not the survivor. Not the hunter. The thing that was made for one kind of fullness, mourned it, and was filled with another while it wasn't looking — spinning slowly in the cold prismatic light, sodden and ringing and never quite as empty as it feels.
