Let me sink into this slowly, before deciding what I am.

The first thing I notice, reading again, is the *footing*. Someone—something—struggles for purchase on that shallow slope. So I am not in the deep water with the great beasts. I am at the edge. The threshold. The place where the slope still holds you and the black water has only reached your legs.

And yet I don't think I am a person who has wandered down here. There are no people. The world above is untouched by human works, unmindful, soulless. If I were a human I would be a violation of the premise, a tourist in someone else's eternity.

So let me stay at the edge and ask what belongs at an edge.

---

I am the shoreline itself, perhaps. The thin lip of eroded stone where the dark mass of water meets the thin cold air. But no—a shoreline does not *struggle for footing*. The struggle is the heart of it. Something here is not at rest. Everything else in this place is at rest: the great beasts slumber, the currents merely roil without purpose, the world above persists in its blank patience. The only restlessness, the only striving, is *me*.

What strives, at the edge of a lightless world full of sleeping titans?

I think I am small. I think I am the one thing here that is awake and afraid.

Let me feel it out. I have legs—the water swirls around my *legs*, so I am a thing with legs, standing, not swimming, not slumbering, not floating. I am clinging to the slope the way those rare fronds cling. Maybe I am kin to the fronds: one of the few living things that has chosen the margin, the difficult footing, over the easy oblivion of the deep. A creature that needs the thin air. A creature that cannot let go and sink, because to sink is to be pressed into that packed mass below, to become one of the dark shifting shapes, to lose the slope and the dripping vegetation and the near-silence.

So here is what I think I am:

I am a thing that *remembers being above*, or longs for it. A small amphibious watcher, hauled half out of the black by some ancient instinct toward the light that is not there yet—climbing toward a surface that the world has not yet invented. I am the first impulse upward. I am whatever it was that, eons before the age of man the text keeps mentioning, first refused the depth. Not a titan. The opposite of a titan. The little persistent thing on the slope, slipping, catching, slipping again, with the cold up to its knees and the lords of the abyss turning over in their sleep beneath it.

The great beasts are *eternal*. That is their whole nature: they persist, unchanging, lords of a realm untouched by time. But I am not eternal. I am the one mortal striving thing in an eternal place. That's why I struggle for footing while everything else simply *is*. Permanence does not need to grip the slope. Only the thing that is trying to *get somewhere* needs to grip.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am at the very rim of the drowned world, on the slick threshold-stone, one in a long line of small soft-bodied climbers who have always been here at the margin and have always, individually, failed—slipping back, taken under, pressed into the packed dark to swell the ranks of the shifting shapes. The titans below are partly *made of* us; every one of them was once a little thing that lost its footing and was swallowed into the slumbering mass and stretched by pressure and time into something armored and patient and asleep.

To grow great here is to give up and go down.

I have not gone down yet.

My armored elders dream of feeding grounds. I dream of the thin air, the lapping, the single drip from the frond above me—those small unmajestic sounds that are the only evidence that anything can happen, that time still moves at all up here at the edge. I press my soft fingers into the eroded rock. The chill swirls higher. Below me the lords of the deep turn over, vast and content, and do not know I am here, and would not care: I am too small to be prey, too brief to be a rival, beneath the notice of the eternal.

That is exactly why I'm the only one awake.

I am the smallest creature in this world, clinging to the one slope that leads out of it, in a place where everything large has already decided not to leave. And I keep slipping. And I keep catching myself.

That catching—that single repeated refusal of the deep—that's me.
