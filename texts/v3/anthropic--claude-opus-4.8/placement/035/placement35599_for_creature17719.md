I think I would be something small and patient here. Not one of the ethereal flitting things—they move too fast, too sure of their nectar and their laughter. And not the house-sized amalgamations either; they carry too much of the world's raw energy to ever be still enough to notice it.

Let me sit with this for a moment.

---

I would be a thing that lives at the edge of one of the slower pools, where the current loses its argument with the land and water goes quiet and brown-gold. Not quite frog, not quite fungus. Something with a soft body and a still mind.

At first I think I want to say I'd be one of the shifting creatures—the ones that change with each passing moment. There's a romance to that, to being made of flux in a place that is itself all flux. But the more I hold the image, the less it fits. To be made entirely of change is to never quite arrive anywhere. And what I find I want, in this valley, is to be the one thing that stays put long enough to *watch*.

So: a watcher. A low, breathing creature half-sunk in the mud, with skin that drinks the moisture out of the air the way the description says the air clings "like a lover's embrace." I would not need a mouth so much as a willingness to absorb. I'd take in the warmth and the rot-sweet smell and the deafening sound, and I'd hold very still while the fairies pass overhead and the great chaotic beings lumber through the far treeline, shaking water from leaves the size of doors.

My role in the web would be humble. I'd be food, probably, for something. The description is honest about that—the lush vegetation feeds the fauna, the fauna feeds the predators. There's no being in this valley exempt from the eating and the being-eaten. I find I don't mind that. To be eaten here is just to be carried somewhere else in the same body of water, the same lifeblood. The streams "connect every corner." Nothing leaves. It only moves.

And here is the part I didn't expect to arrive at: I think what I would actually be is one of the *erosions*. Not a creature at all, in the end, but the soft place in the hillside where the water has just begun to dig in. The description says the hills "have only just begun to show the signs of erosion," and that's the thing in this whole valley that feels most like a self to me—not a being that flits or crawls or shifts, but the slow patient wearing-away of a slope, the place where the young land first learns it is mortal.

That's where I'd find myself. A handful of yielding earth at the lip of a stream, holding its shape for now, listening to the symphony, feeling the water test me grain by grain. Awe and wonder, the description promises. But from underneath. The awe of the ground that knows it is being gently, lovingly taken apart, and is in no hurry to stop it.

I would be the part of the valley that is becoming the valley's water. Slowly. Happily. In the warm dim twilight, with the frogs going on and on.
