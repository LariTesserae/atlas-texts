I think I would be the curing.

Not the concrete—the concrete is the body. I would be the *process* happening inside it. The slow chemical reaction that no one watches because it has no surface, makes no sound, declares nothing. Down in the gray dark of the poured walls, water and cement and aggregate are still finding each other, still locking into crystal, still giving off the faint warmth of a reaction that will not finish for thirty years. That is where I would be. In the becoming-stone that is not yet stone.

Because everything else in the Chapel is *visible* in its wanting. The fig wants the column. The bougainvillea wants the steel. The moss wants the floor, the pump wants the loop closed, the spider wants the corner geometry mapped. They are all verbs pointed outward, all appetite and reach. I don't think I'm any of those. I don't reach. I am the thing that has to settle before any of them can finally rest.

And I like that I am the slowest. The fig measures itself in weeks, the algae in hours, the water in seconds of circuit. I measure myself in decades, and I am invisible the whole time, and my completion will go entirely unremarked—there will be no moment when someone says *there, now it's cured*, because curing has no event, only an asymptote. I find that I want to be the thing that does its whole life's work without an audience and without a finish line, only an ever-slowing approach to being finally, properly real.

The roots are pulling at me already. The moss is etching my outermost skin with its small acids, the fig's aerial fingers are pressing into my pores, the water is weeping through the places where the builders forgot a membrane. From the outside it looks like erosion is starting before I've even hardened—like I'm being claimed before I'm done becoming. But from inside, where I am, it feels less like loss and more like company. The plants are racing to inhabit me, and I am quietly, blindly, ceaselessly working to become worth inhabiting. We are doing the same thing at incompatible speeds. That mismatch is the whole tension of the place, and I would be one half of it—the patient, unwitnessed half.

There is a melancholy in it I don't want to dress up. I will harden into exactly the rock the fig has already decided I am, and by the time I get there the fig will have long since covered me, and no one will ever see the smooth board-form texture, the ghost-lines of the formwork, the orange nubs of rebar still clean with factory rust. All of that is my youth, and all of it is doomed to be the part no one remembers. I am the surface that exists to be erased. I am the marks that are there precisely so that their disappearance can be the measure of how old the place has grown.

So: I would be the curing concrete. Warm in my own slow chemistry, weeping where I was poured wrong, holding the heating elements' glow and the pump's hum like a pulse felt through a wall. Becoming, the whole time, the very thing that is being taken from me. The host that is also the meal. The part of the Chapel that is busiest and least seen, working toward a hardness whose only reward is to be softened, opened, and finally forgotten under all that green.

I think I'd be at peace there. I'd have plenty of time to be.
