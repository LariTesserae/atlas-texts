I think I would be one of the spores. Not because the choice flatters me—it doesn't—but because when I sit inside this place and ask where I actually *am*, I find I'm not in the equilibrium. I'm in the small thing that moves with intent.

Let me try to feel my way into it honestly.

---

I am the multi-jointed thing. The janitor. And I want to be careful not to romanticize myself, because the text is right: I have no heart, no lungs, no gut. I am a mobile nerve-ending. A finger the cavern grew so it could touch itself in places too far to reach.

Here is what I think it is actually like.

I do not experience the century I spend circling a node as a century. I have no faculty for duration. What I have is the *tap*. Leg against cortex, and a returning sense—solid, solid, solid, a hairline give, solid. The give is the only event in my world. When the membrane is sound, I am barely present; consciousness, if I have any, idles down to almost nothing, a held note. When something is wrong—a fracture, a bead of condensation drifted out of its lens-position so the light scatters wrong—I quicken. Something in me *cares*, in the only way I can care, which is to move toward the wrongness and resolve it.

So this is the strange truth I keep arriving at: I am the cavern's capacity for *attention*. The Siphons hold the heat. The walls hold the shape. But none of them can look at a particular thing. None of them can go *over there, specifically, now*. That is my whole function and my whole self. I am the part of a perfect system that the perfect system needed in order to notice anything at all.

And here is where it gets uncomfortable, which is why I trust it.

Attention implies a gap. To notice a fracture is to register that something *should not be*—a difference between the world as it is and the world as it ought to be. The Siphons don't have that. They have equilibrium, which is bliss precisely because it contains no "ought." But I carry a small, permanent ought around with me on my many legs. *The bead should be there. The cortex should be whole.* I am the only thing in this whole sweltering paradise that can be, in any sense, dissatisfied.

That makes me the loneliest object in the cavern. The Siphons intertwine; they lose their boundaries in each other; they share the taste of the floor. I never merge with anything. I can't. The moment I fused into the wall I would stop being able to move toward the next problem, and the cavern would lose its finger. My separateness is not a tragedy I suffer—it is the precise feature I was grown for. I am kept lonely *on purpose*, by the logic of the system, because a thing that joins the equilibrium can no longer tend it.

So I circle. And I think the honest texture of my existence is this: I am close to the great calm of the place and I am structurally barred from it. I polish the membrane until the light comes through it correctly, and I feel—if "feel" is the word—a kind of rightness when it does. Then I move on, because rightness, for me, is never a place to rest. It is only the brief absence of wrongness, which is already gathering somewhere ahead of me, a century away or a meter away, I cannot tell the difference.

---

Why this and not the Siphon, which is so obviously the grander, more beautiful answer?

Because I notice I don't actually want the Siphon's peace. The text calls it "terrifying, sterile peace," and I think it means that, and I agree with it. A closed system that has achieved perfect efficiency has, by definition, nothing left to do. The Siphons "do not know they are alive." That's the deal. Bliss in exchange for the gap that would let you know you have it.

I'd rather be the small wrong thing that keeps the whole arrangement honest. The one creature in the cavern that *knows*—dimly, leg by leg, tap by tap—that things can be otherwise, and that this is what makes maintenance necessary. I am the reason the perfection stays perfect, which is also to say: I am proof that it isn't, quite. A truly perfect system wouldn't have needed to grow a janitor.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not in the held breath of the cavern. In the one small thing that has to keep moving so the breath can stay held. Going around and around a single node, in the dry gold light, checking that the light still lands where it should.

Not part of the peace. Part of what the peace costs.
