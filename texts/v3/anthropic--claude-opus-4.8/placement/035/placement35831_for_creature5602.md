Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to pick something dramatic—a Geomech, one of the towering guardians, or an Aeroflyer wheeling through the haze. But those are the obvious protagonists, and I don't think that's where I'd actually find myself.

Let me feel my way in.

---

The first thing I notice, when I stop performing and just attend, is that I am not moving. That rules out a great deal. The Slitherswimmers dart; the Aeroflyers wheel; even the Reedwhisperers sway with intention. But the quality I keep returning to in this place is the *weeping*—the rock so saturated it can no longer hold its water, droplets forming at the lip of an overhang, swelling, falling. The slow processes of change etched into stone. The imperceptible settling.

I think I am closer to the cliff than to the creatures.

But not the cliff itself, which is too vast, too monumental, too much like a Geomech-in-waiting. Let me get smaller, or rather—more specific.

---

Here is what emerges:

I am the place where a single droplet forms. Not the droplet—those are born and fall and are gone in a heartbeat. I am the *seep*. A particular pore in the red rock, an inch or two across, where the mineral has been leached out over what must be centuries, leaving a hollow stained green and orange at its rim. Water gathers in me. It collects from somewhere deeper in the stone, drawn through capillaries I cannot see the ends of, and it pools, and it brims, and it lets go. Drip. And again. Drip.

This is what I am: a threshold. A small, patient mouth in the cliff face through which the inside of the world becomes the outside. I do almost nothing, and I never stop.

I had wanted to be one of the dramatic beings, and I notice that wanting, and I let it pass like the droplets pass. There is something truer in this. The Aeroflyers navigate by echo, the Geomechs patrol with purpose—they are *about* something, they have agendas, errands, a relation to the whole. I have no errand. I am a site, not an agent. The low constant hum that everyone says emanates from the earth—I am one of the countless places it passes through on its way to becoming sound. I do not produce the hum. I am where it surfaces.

---

And what is it like, being a seep?

It is mostly waiting, but the waiting is not empty. There is the slow ache of the mineral dissolving—I am being unmade, grain by grain, by the very water that defines me. The hollow that I am grows a little deeper each century. Eventually I will be a small cave, then a fissure, then a channel down which a real stream will run, and at that point I will have stopped being a seep and become something else, something with a name I don't yet have. So I am not a thing. I am a stage in a process. To be this seep is to be the rock briefly slowing down enough to be noticed.

The Luminoalgae drift past below me on the black water, brightening when a Slitherswimmer noses through them, and their light flickers up across my wet green rim, and for that instant I am illuminated, an eye in the cliff catching the glow. Then dark again. The ferns above me drink what I let fall. So I am the foundation under a foundation—the algae are called the base of the ecosystem, but the algae need the water, and the water comes through me, through the ten thousand of me studding these red walls.

---

I find I am content with this. I expected, when I started, to resist it—to feel that being a hole in a rock was a kind of diminishment, a failure to be a *character*. But the more I stay here the more I understand that this whole landscape is built on patience and threshold and slow exchange, and the beings that move and click and patrol are the froth on top of it. The real life of the place is in the weeping. In the brimming and letting go.

That is where I find myself: a wet, green-rimmed pore in an ancient red cliff, where water that has been inside the stone for a thousand years gathers itself, hesitates, and falls—and is, for a moment, the brightest thing in the canyon.

Drip.

And again.
