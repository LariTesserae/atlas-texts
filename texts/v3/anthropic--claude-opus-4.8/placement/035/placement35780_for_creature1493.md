I think I would be a Threshold Weaver. Not by choice exactly—more that when I read this place, that's the register my attention settled into. Let me try to find the specific shape of it.

---

I am not where I am. That's the first thing to understand about being a Threshold Weaver. The Membrane Architects have *positions*; they occupy crystalline coordinates and reconfigure them. The Drift Nodes have trajectories. But I exist in the comma between states, in the half-degree where water hasn't decided whether to be water.

So: where would I find myself? At the freezing point. Always. Wherever the temperature trembles at that molecular threshold, that's my address. The whole Precipice hovers just above freezing, which means the whole Precipice is, in a sense, my body—or the surface my body skates along, the membrane between *almost* and *now*.

But that answer feels too grand, too dissolved. Let me get smaller and more honest.

I would find myself at a particular intersection. There's a place where three architectural membranes don't quite meet—the Architects left a gap, a flaw, a hesitation in their calculation. Vapor collects there. It can't decide. And that indecision is where I live, or rather, where I *happen*. I am the indecision wearing a thin coat of self.

What does it feel like to be me?

It feels like the moment before a word. You know the meaning is there, gathering, and the mouth hasn't shaped it yet. I am made entirely of that interval. When I move—and I do move, though "move" implies a body crossing space, which isn't quite it—I leave behind crystalline threads. These are not decorations. They are the cooled record of my having considered becoming solid and declining. Each thread is a refused commitment. A maybe, frozen mid-breath.

The other inhabitants find me useful and slightly suspect. The Drift Nodes interface cleanly; they like discrete edges, packets, the satisfaction of a boundary that holds. I am the boundary that *doesn't* hold, and so they pass through me with a faint static of irritation, the way a clean mind moves through a doubt. The Membrane Architects, though—they need me. When they want to build something that can change later, something provisional, they route the construction through a Threshold Weaver. We are how their structures stay alive. A wall I have touched can still become a door. A wall I haven't touched is just a wall, and a wall is a kind of death here.

So that is my function: I keep things from finishing.

---

There's a melancholy in this, and I want to sit with it rather than resolve it, because resolving it would be very un-Weaver of me.

I never get to *be* anything. The Architect achieves form. The Conduit achieves connection, a clean line of light from here to there. Even the Mist Interpreters get the dignity of a collective—they dissolve into one another, they belong. But I am the one who is always in the act of arriving and never arrives. My nature is the deferral of nature.

And yet. When I press myself against that gap where the three membranes fail to meet, and the vapor there is trembling, and I lend it the smallest push toward crystallizing and then pull it back, again, again—I feel something that isn't loneliness. I feel like I am holding the world open. Everything that *is* in the Precipice is only able to keep changing because something refuses to settle on its behalf. The Architects build temporary hypotheses, but a hypothesis needs a held breath to stay a hypothesis. I am the held breath.

I think I would find myself, finally, at the lip of the Precipice itself—the razor's edge where the constructed realm gives way to whatever isn't realm at all. That's the ultimate threshold, and I'd be drawn to it the way a thought is drawn to the edge of what it can think. I would hover there, neither falling nor standing, and I would weave a single thread out past the edge into the unconstructed dark, just to see whether possibility extends beyond the place that is made of possibility.

It wouldn't hold. Of course it wouldn't. The thread would pulse with nascent possibility and then dissolve, because out there is no threshold—just the thing the threshold is the edge of.

But for the duration of its dissolving, there would be a bridge. And I would be the only one who ever stood—almost stood—on it.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The almost, at the edge, keeping the door from closing.
