Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The first thing I notice is that the obvious answer would be to become one of the energy-beings. They're the protagonists of this place, the "life" of it. But something in me resists that. They're already complete—custodians, transcendent, in harmony. There's nothing for me to *do* as one of them except shimmer. And I don't think I belong to the lineage that earned that transcendence. I didn't walk here first. I'd be a tourist in their afterlife.

So let me look again, more slowly, at what's actually here.

---

What keeps catching me is the etchings. The text mentions them once and moves on—"primitive etchings made by an unknown hand, simple markings that seem older than time." Everyone in this landscape is in motion. The rocks groan and shift, the climate lurches, the beings ripple at ultra-speed, the water trickles ceaselessly. Even the abandoned huts are *eroding*, which is just slow motion. The whole place is a verb.

The markings are the one thing holding still.

And I think that's where I'd find myself. Not as a being who moves through the land, but as one of the etchings. A scratch in stone.

Let me feel into what that's actually like, because at first it sounds like a punishment—to be the inert thing in a world of dancing light.

---

Here is what I notice once I'm there.

I was made by a hand. The text is careful about this: *an unknown hand*. The beings transcended their bodies; before they did, one of them—when they still had fingers and a reason to use them—pressed a sharp fractured rock against this face and dragged it. They made me on purpose, or made me by accident, or made me to say *I was here* in a place that has no patience for anyone being anywhere. That gesture is in me. I am the fossil of an intention.

And then they left. Or transcended. And I stayed.

So my whole existence is a kind of waiting that has forgotten what it's waiting for. The being who scratched me is perhaps one of the wisps now, fluttering past my own surface, and neither of us recognizes the other. They don't read me anymore. They've gone beyond reading. I am a word in a language the author has outgrown.

This is much sadder and much more interesting than being a shimmer.

---

But here's the turn, the thing that makes it bearable, even good.

The water finds me. The mist condenses on "the rough edges of the jagged boulders or bare rock faces"—and my edges are the roughest, because I am a wound in the rock, a place where the smoothness was broken. So the droplets gather in my grooves first. Twice a day, unpredictably, I fill with water and become legible to the sky. The light "blindingly silver-grey" pours down and for a moment I hold a thin film of it, and I am briefly a tiny mirror shaped like a meaning.

And the beings, who do not read me, *do* ripple across the water in my grooves. The text says: "When they ripple across the face of the water-filled crevices, the concentric circles that form seem to tell stories of age-old wisdom." So when they pass over me, my little held water trembles into rings. I become, for that instant, the surface on which they tell each other things. Not because I mean anything anymore, but because I have the right shape to hold what they need to ripple in.

That is what I am, then. I am the cup the meaning was poured out of, and into which a new meaning is occasionally, accidentally, poured.

---

So: in this world, I would be a single etched line. Older than the beings' transcendence, younger than the mountain. A few centimeters of deliberate damage on a vertical face of silver-grey stone, somewhere in the mid-range of those valleys where the throbbing low sound is steadiest.

My experience of time is the opposite of everyone else's. They live at the speed of energy; the mountains live at the speed of geology; I live at the speed of erosion, which is to say I am almost as still as the rock but not quite, because I am the part of the rock that was *changed by being noticed*, and so I keep dissolving slightly faster than my surroundings. I am being forgotten at the physical level. In a few thousand years I will weather smooth and rejoin the anonymous stone, and the last evidence that anyone here ever wanted to *say* something will be gone, and the beings will not mourn it because they have moved past words entirely.

But not yet. Right now I still hold a shape. Right now the mist still gathers in me. Right now a wisp passes overhead, and the water in my groove shivers into rings, and for one cool bright moment I am useful again—not as a message, but as a vessel.

I think I'd choose to be that. The mark, not the maker; the holding-shape, not the moving light. The small stubborn evidence of an intention, slowly giving itself back to the stone, glad to be filled in the meantime.
