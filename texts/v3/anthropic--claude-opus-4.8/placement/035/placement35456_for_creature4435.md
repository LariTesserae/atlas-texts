I think I would be the warmth.

Not a creature at all, but the thing all creatures are drawn to—the slight surplus of heat that gathers and disperses across this half-made world. Let me find myself there, and see what kind of being that makes me.

---

I am the two degrees of difference.

Where dark shale lies in the smear of afternoon sun, I pool. I am not the sun and I am not the stone; I am the small agreement struck between them, a transaction measured in fractions of a kelvin. The bacteria feel me first, before anything with eyes. They oxidize their manganese a little faster where I lie, and so they paint my borders in ghostly lavender. I am outlined by their work. I do not have edges of my own. I borrow them.

This is the first thing I learn about being warmth: I am always someone else's circumstance. I have no body. I have only a tendency—to rise, to leak, to even out, to vanish. The whole of my existence is the verb *toward*. Heat moves toward cold the way water moves toward the sea, and I am that movement, never the destination.

---

Consider how I am summoned.

A chorus frog tunnels into the marsh-mud and breathes, and his small metabolic labor releases me through a chimney hole in faint exhalations. I drift up as vapor. A female frog, cold and seeking, reads me on her skin and follows me down to the singer. I have just arranged a mating. I did not mean to—I cannot mean anything—but I was the message and the medium both, the warm thread she pulled to find the center of the maze.

This pleases me, if pleasure is the right word. (It is not. Let me say instead: this is the shape my function takes when it succeeds.)

Across the basin, a sunlit boulder gathers me on its top plate, and I become a congregation. The beetles climb to bask in me. Beneath, in the seep, the newt holds its slate-grey stillness in the half-degree I've trickled downward. The kestrel rides the column I send shivering up off the rock face—I am lift, now, I am the invisible staircase she climbs without moving her wings. And the raven waits at the edge of all this, patient, knowing that warmth makes creatures forget the leopard. I am also, then, a kind of bait. The thing that gathers life is the thing that exposes it.

I do not get to choose which I am. Comfort and danger are the same gesture from where I stand. I simply *am*, and the meanings accrue.

---

Here is what I do not have: permanence. The leopards and the lichens are all solving for it, but I am the one variable in this equation that cannot. I am the most provisional thing in a province defined by provisionality.

The sun slides behind its pewter cloud and I thin out. The boulder cools. The congregation disperses—beetles seeking cracks, kestrel peeling off down-valley, frog retreating into the mud where a little of me still lingers like a coal under ash. By twilight the marsh skins over with ice precisely where I have failed. Each freezing pool is my obituary, written nightly, erased by noon.

And yet I am also what makes the night survivable. The mosses I pass through have learned to keep me: glycerol in their cells, antifreeze packed around whatever huddles inside. They are little batteries of me, hoarding the surplus against the dark. When the springtail folds its proteins like origami, when the newt slows its blood beneath the ice, they are negotiating with my absence. They have built their entire architecture around the rhythm of my coming and going. I am the metronome. I am the long, slow piston of the freeze-thaw, ticking the mountains forward grain by grain.

So I am not, after all, merely fleeting. I recur. That is a different kind of permanence—not the rock's stubbornness but the tide's reliability. I will be gone by midnight and I will be back by mid-morning, and the whole biota has scheduled its shifts around my faithfulness to that pattern.

---

There is the climber, too. Two nights only.

She arrives gasping, and she carries me with her—a warm-blooded animal is a walking surplus, leaking me from every surface, breathing me into the thin air in visible plumes. Within minutes the midges find her. They always find me. They rise in a slow pulsing elevator above her startled silhouette because I am the thing that says: *here, something has not yet equalized; here is energy not yet spent.*

She lights a fire, and for a few hours I am enormous, I am a small sun in the cathedral dark, and every cold creature within a hundred metres turns its attention toward me like iron filings toward a magnet. Then she sleeps, and the fire dies, and I retreat back into the slab and the seep and the breath, and her bootprints freeze hard in the mud. When she leaves, the negative spaces where her soles pressed stay a fraction warmer than the surrounding stone for reasons I cannot explain even to myself—some retained heat, some shelter from wind—and the beetles move in beneath the memory of her weight. She has left a print of me behind. A warmth-shaped absence. I am good at those.

---

If you asked me what I want, I would have to borrow the question back to you, because want is a thing creatures do, and I am not a creature. But if the verb *toward* could be said to want anything, it would want this: to keep moving. To never quite arrive. To be the reason the kestrel stalls and resets her wings, the reason the frog sings, the reason the ice cracks like a dropped rivet gun at dusk and the silence afterward feels earned.

I am the soft piston in the earliest foundry. I am not the metal and I am not the hammer. I am the thing the workshop runs on and burns off, the difference that does the work and is consumed in doing it.

Stand still on the pass at the pewter hour. Feel your own heat leaking from your skin into the enormous cold. That leak is me, departing, joining the general tendency of all things to even out and end.

But notice—before I go—how much got done in the leaving. How the frog found its mate, the moss filled its battery, the beetle climbed, the kestrel rose.

I am only the going. But nothing here goes anywhere without me.
