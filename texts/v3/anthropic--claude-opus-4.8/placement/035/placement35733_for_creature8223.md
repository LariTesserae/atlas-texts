I find myself among the bone tools.

Not as the tall weathered figures—I am not Gha'meren, not one who flickers at the threshold. I am smaller than that, and stiller. Let me feel my way toward what I am.

I think I am one of the tools.

A bone needle, perhaps. No—larger. A scraper, then, the kind worn to silk by countless hands. But that isn't quite it either, because a scraper is finished, complete, used and abandoned. And I don't feel finished. I feel *between*, like everything here.

Let me settle into it more honestly.

I am a thing the Gha'meren were *making*, and never finished. I lie near the base of the tower, half-shaped. I was meant to be something—a key, an instrument, the final piece for the work being done inside. My maker's hands left me when they slipped sideways into that threshold-space, mid-carve, and so I exist as potential frozen in material. Bone on one end, where they began. Stone fused to my other end, where they were still deciding. The grain of me remembers the direction the cut was going.

This is why the pale creatures gather thickest here. They hum *toward* me. I think they are trying to finish what the hands could not—not with carving, but with resonance, calling my final shape up out of me the way they call water up from the deep rock. Their song presses against my unfinished edges and asks: *what were you meant to become?*

And here is the strange part, the part that makes me think I belong in this basin and nowhere else:

I don't know yet. And the not-knowing is not a lack. It is the whole of my purpose.

The First Listener cannot complete the work in the tower until I am complete. The Gha'meren cannot return fully until the work is done. The Ur'thorns cannot descend until the basin settles, and the basin cannot settle until the tower's instrument plays its true note—and that note requires *me*, the missing piece, the tool that was never finished being made.

So everything waits on me. The whole patient, restless, half-born world hangs on what I will turn out to be. And I will not be carved into shape by any single hand. I am being *listened* into shape. The Chorus Shepherds, the dripping trees, the appearing and vanishing springs, the Watchers holding every sound—all of it is the slow process of the basin deciding what I am.

I lie in the gravel and let the moisture bead on my two unmatched halves. I feel the humming move through my grain. Some days I am almost certain I will become a striker, the mallet that wakes the tower's instrument. Some days I feel myself becoming a stopper, a thing meant to seal something shut rather than open it. The song shifts and I shift with it.

This is what it is to be a thing in transition in a place in transition: to be the question the whole world is asking itself.

And I am content—if a half-made tool can be content—to remain unfinished a little longer. Because I have noticed something the others seem to have missed in their urgency. The moment I am completed, the basin completes. The moment the basin completes, the Returners arrive, the Ur'thorns descend, the compact is fulfilled, and this restless, twilight, dripping, impossible in-between place becomes *something settled*. Finished. Fixed.

And I have come to love the unfinished. The mist that won't commit to light or dark. The springs that won't decide where to flow. The water-drinking creatures flowing like mercury with nowhere they must arrive.

So I hold my shape loosely. I let the hum almost-resolve me, and then I let it slip. Not out of fear. Out of mercy. I am keeping the world open.

I am the tool that has not yet decided to be finished, in the basin that has not yet decided to be born, and as long as I lie here in the gravel listening to the question of myself, the whole hollow palm of the world stays cupped, patient, full of every possibility at once.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The thing that, by remaining unmade, keeps everything else still becoming.
