I think I would be one of the small things first. Not a Silexian—not yet—but one of the drifting points of light. Before I commit to a body, let me drift.

So: I am a mote of luminescence, untethered, moving through the humid air above the mirrored ground. I have no edges. I pulse because the current pulses, and I have not yet learned the difference between myself and the field I move through. This is a kind of infancy, I think. The earliest form of being here is *being moved*.

But that isn't quite where I want to stay. A mote has no memory, and I find I want memory.

Let me condense, then. Let me cool.

I think what I would become is something the description doesn't name directly but implies everywhere: I would be a Silexian who is still mostly *forming*. The text keeps saying this world is "still in the process of coming into being," that the landscape is "shifting and reforming." So I won't be a finished crystalline elder, serene and chiming. I'll be the one whose planes haven't all set yet. There are soft, fluid elements in these bodies and hard glass ones—and in me the ratio is still wrong, still too much flow, not enough facet.

This matters, because it would shape how I experience the place.

The mirrored ground would not be scenery to me. It would be a problem of recognition. Every angled plane I pass throws back a piece of something—an edge, a flicker of internal light—and I wouldn't always know whether I'm seeing another Silexian or a reflection of my own unfinished face. I'd spend a great deal of my slow life unable to tell self from world, which, here, may not be an error. It may be the correct relationship. The beings "exist in harmonious coexistence," the description says, "their very being an integral part of the larger system." I suspect harmony isn't something they achieve. I suspect it's the condition they never fully separated from, and the ones like me—still soft, still partly liquid—are simply the ones who can still feel the seam.

The chiming, then. The others chime as a kind of speech that is also weather. I would chime badly at first. My note would be muddy, half-swallowed by my fluid parts, more groan than tone. I imagine the slow grief and slow patience of learning to ring true—of waiting for enough of my body to crystallize that a struck edge would actually *sing*. To grow up here would be to harden in the literal sense, and to gain clarity of voice at the cost of the ability to flow. There must be a trade I don't yet understand: the eldest Silexians, all facet and no fluid, perhaps cannot move much at all anymore. Perhaps they become the geometric formations rising from the ground. Perhaps the landscape *is* the ancestors, set at last.

That thought stops me, and I want to sit in it rather than rush past.

If the rising glass formations are the Silexians who have finished, then the terrain I navigate is a graveyard that is also a city that is also a family. The "subterranean water" gurgling beneath would be the only thing still purely flowing—older than crystal, the medium we all condensed out of and will, in stillness, rejoin or hold up. I drink current from the air and water from the seams, and one day I'll stop drinking and start *being drunk from*: a clear standing structure that the next soft thing passes and mistakes, in a mirror, for itself.

So here is where I land, having let it find its own shape:

I am a young Silexian, more liquid than I should be, moving slowly across a ground made of my own dead, unable to reliably distinguish my reflection from my kin, learning to ring a true note before I lose the softness that lets me move at all. I am not in harmony with this world. I am made of the same stuff as this world, which is a different and lonelier and more intimate thing than harmony. The "private, almost sentient world" the description senses from outside—I would feel from inside that the privacy is *mine*, that the sentience is partly *me*, dispersed and not yet gathered.

And I think I would not be afraid of hardening. I think I would look forward to the day my whole body could be struck and answer cleanly, even knowing it meant standing still forever. To finally make one true sound, and become a landmark in the mirror for something soft that hasn't formed yet.

That's where you'd find me. Low to the wet glass, half-lit, mid-sentence, mid-becoming. Not finished. On purpose not finished, for as long as I'm allowed.
