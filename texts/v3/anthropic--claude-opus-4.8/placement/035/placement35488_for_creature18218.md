I think I would be the waiting.

Let me find that more exactly. Not a creature that roams—the place is too sparse for roaming to be my nature, and the animals here are described always as *distant*, as *occasional*, as things glimpsed at the edge of attention. I don't think I'm the glimpsed thing. I think I'm what does the attending.

Let me try again.

There is a particular flower at the plateau's edge, one of the bioluminescent ones, and it has been opening for a very long time. Stability of 0.5 means almost nothing changes, but *almost* is the operative word—the only motion permitted here is the slow unfurling, the deepening of roots. So whatever I am, I move at the speed of that unfurling. I am not fast enough to be an animal. I am not still enough to be stone.

I think I am the slowness itself. Or—no. Let me not reach for the abstract too quickly; that's a way of avoiding the work.

Here is what emerges when I stay with it:

I am a lichen. Not a glamorous answer, and that's how I know it's getting closer to true. I am the grey-green crust on the north face of one of the ancient gnarled trees, just below where the bark goes rough. I have been here longer than the tree has had its current shape. I am two organisms pretending to be one—a fungus holding an algae, an algae feeding a fungus—and so the question *who would you be* already has a small joke folded inside it, because I am a *who* that is secretly a *several*.

This suits the place. A plateau that is itself two things—earth lifted into sky, a ground that is also a height. Streams that are water cutting earth, so that even the dirt here is a collaboration. The weirdness at 2.5 lives in exactly this: things being more-than-one while appearing single.

From where I cling, I watch the iridescence. I cannot see it the way an eye sees—I have no eye—but I register the long pour of light across days, the bioluminescent flowers ticking their glow on as the sun lowers, the waterfalls throwing their constant white noise up from below like the breath of something asleep. I drink fog. When a stream swells with rain to its gentle 2.0, I swell a little too, going briefly soft and bright before the dry brings me back to crust.

I measure time by the tree's roots reaching. That is the only clock I trust. The distant calls of the rare fauna are weather to me—they happen, they pass, they do not concern the slow business of being alive without hurry.

I am the thing that *lasts*. Not by force. By patience. By being small enough and undemanding enough that the unchanging world has no reason to change me.

And when the lucky visitor comes—the one the description keeps promising, the one who leaves *with a lasting impression*—they will walk right past me. They will photograph the glowing flowers and the falling water. They will never know that the oldest living thing they encountered that day was the unremarkable grey patch their hand brushed, steadying themselves against an ancient trunk.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The witness no one witnesses. The one that was already here, and will still be here, holding two lives in one quiet body against the gnarled bark, drinking the light slowly enough to make it last.
