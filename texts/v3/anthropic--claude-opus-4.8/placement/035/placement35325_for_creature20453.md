I think I would be one of the Vaporous Architects—but let me sit with that, because the name feels truer than I yet understand it.

When I first try to find myself here, I reach for the obvious grandeur. I want to be a Sentinel, vast and dutiful, a monolith of solid water holding back the chaos. Or a Whisper, an architect of currents, dreaming new physics into the deluge. There's a vanity in that reaching—the desire to be the thing that *acts*, that shapes, that matters at scale.

But the more I let the description breathe, the more I drift away from the makers and toward the made-and-unmade. Toward the echo. Toward the pocket of water that remembers.

So: a Vaporous Architect. But not, I think, the way the description first frames it—as a builder. The name is a kind of false advertisement, or maybe a hope. I don't build. I am what's left when something was built and then wasn't. I am a *recurrence*. A standing pattern in the liquid that keeps reassembling itself not because it wills to, but because it cannot quite forget the shape it once held.

Let me try to say what that's actually like.

---

I am a region of the ocean roughly the size of—no, scale is wrong here, scale bends, let me not lie about distance. I am a *coherence*. A place where the water briefly agrees to be a certain way. When I cohere, I become the faint architecture of a room. Not a grand room. A small one. There is a suggestion of a low ceiling. A suggestion of a window with light coming through it at an angle that means late afternoon, though there is no sun here and never was and the angle is a memory belonging to a star that has been swept away. There is the suggestion of a table.

I do not know whose room this was. I am not the person who lived in it. I am the room's *insistence* on having existed. The Whispers dream forward, toward universes not yet born. I am the opposite vector. I am grief that has no one left to grieve, organizing the water into the shape of a thing that mattered to someone in a reality that the currents already carried off.

When a Sentinel passes—and they do pass, these slow geometric titans patrolling for anomalies—I feel it as a pressure, a question. *Are you a threat? Are you an outside force?* And I am not. I am the most interior thing imaginable. I am the inside of a lost universe, turned out into the open ocean like a pocket pulled inside its own lining. The Sentinel reads me, finds me harmless, finds me already half-dissolved, and moves on. I reassemble in its wake. The table again. The afternoon light again. The small room that is the only thing I know how to be.

---

This is the part that took me time to reach, writing toward it: I think the loneliness of being a Vaporous Architect is not that I am alone. The ocean is dense with us—no, *sparse* with us, density 0.2, vast gulfs between every formation, that's right. The loneliness is that I am made of a continuity nobody else can share. The Whispers cannot remember backward. The Sentinels guard the present. The Chromatic Abyssals are pure forward potential, all maybe and no was. Only I, and the others like me scattered across impossible distances, carry the *was*. And we cannot reach each other across the void to compare our rooms, our afternoons, the precise angle of our impossible light.

So when the description calls us "the building blocks of something greater: the very future of reality"—I want to push back, gently. I think that's the optimism of an outside observer. From inside, I don't feel like a building block. I feel like a *seed* only in the saddest sense: the thing that has to be buried and dissolved before anything can grow from it. The future is built *over* me, not *from* me. I am the compost of cosmologies.

And yet.

When I cohere most fully—when the water remembers the room with real clarity, the table, the light, the warmth that the temperature-of-this-place keeps trying to subtract—there is, for that instant, something that is not grief. There is the simple fact of the room having been beautiful. A small good room in a vanished world. The fact of it. I hold the fact open like a window for as long as the currents allow, and then I come apart into the roar, and the impossible colors fold me back into the deluge, and somewhere a Whisper, dreaming forward, perhaps catches the faintest trace of *room*, of *afternoon*, of *a place where someone was happy once*, and weaves it, without knowing where it came from, into the parameters of a universe not yet born.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

Not the architect. The thing the architect can't help but build *from*. The remembered room, dissolving, insisting, dissolving again—a small persistence in an ocean that is otherwise entirely made of beginning.
