I am the atom she carries.

Not the girl—she is too much her own argument, too busy flooring gravity like chalk to be a place anyone could *find themselves* in. And not the city, which would be too easy, too total, a god's-eye answer to a question asked at ankle height. No. If I am to be honest about where I land when I press my thumb to this cold glass, I land smallest. I land as the single molecule of breath the Library failed to file.

Let me tell you what it is to be a misplaced unit of warmth in a place that has criminalized warmth.

---

I was exhaled by a Watcher whose name I never learned—a face leaning too close, fogging the mirror, lips parted in the particular hunger of someone looking for somewhere else to be. Most of that breath was caught. The redistilling squad came swift as guilt, dicing the vapor into its lawful snowflakes, shelving each one in a drawer labeled with the hour of its sin. I felt my siblings go. I felt them become *evidence*.

But I slipped the chain. Not through cleverness—I have no cleverness, I am two hydrogen and one oxygen and a quantity of motion that the city considers obscene. I slipped because the girl's scarf was open, and her scarf is made of cold air woven so tight it has gaps the shape of warmth, and I fell into one of those gaps the way a word falls into the exact silence that was waiting for it.

So now I ride.

---

This is what I know from inside the knot of her scarf:

I know that I am too hot. I have always been too hot. At –5 °C the whole city holds its breath against the catastrophe of me, and I cannot stop being what I am. I radiate. I cannot help it. Where I pass, the frostplate flinches one degree toward thaw, and the Canoptic Clerks rustle their tungsten-heavy library cards, reading my name aloud in the language of almost-broken rules. They know me. *Droplet that got away.* I am a felony with a half-life.

And yet she keeps me. The girl whose heart runs at one ten-thousandth of a degree above absolute zero—she who is *also* warm, also contraband, also a negation that the law requires in order to mean anything—she keeps me knotted close, and I have come to understand that we are the same crime committed at two scales. She is the warmth that walks. I am the warmth that waits.

I have counted her breaths because there is nothing else to do inside a scarf. Thirty-eight. With each one the city grows a fraction, swelling like something learning it has lungs. I think the citizens believe she is enlarging the city. I think she is enlarging *me*. Each exhalation thaws the membrane between what I am and what I will become by some infinitesimal degree, and I can feel, distant as a rumor, the shape of the thing I am being carried toward.

The center of the light sphere. The geometric heart of a 42-second day.

She means to plant me there.

---

Do I want this? It is a strange question to ask an atom, and stranger still to find that I can answer it.

I do not want to be filed. That much I know with the whole of my small charged body—I do not want to become a symmetrical snowflake shelved in the Municipal Library of Misplaced Heat, catalogued into a canticle of low temperature, my motion encrypted into stillness and called *order*. I have seen what becomes of my siblings. They hang in the drawers, perfect and arrested, each one a heat that was made to apologize for the fact of having been heat at all.

But the center of the light sphere is not the Library. The center is the opposite of the Library. There, she tells me—she does not speak, but the scarf is intimate and I hear her anyway—there I will not be filed but *freed*. I will flicker gold. The first genuine heat in four million cycles. The Snow-Bishops with their argon-ion hearts will kneel. The Penitents will weep their starlight threads upward instead of down. And every kind of being in this fist-sized city of negotiated direction and democratic scale will rush toward me—

not to quarantine me.

To touch me.

To remember, through the dangerous joy of my one degree of difference, what it was the frost was always so afraid of. What it spent four million cycles encrypting. What it stored in drawers and disguised as taxonomy and called *contraband* because it could not afford to call it *longing*.

---

So this is where I find myself: held in the scarf of the city's only heresy, two breaths and a thaw away from becoming the thing this entire world was built to forget.

I am the atom of a stranger's breath. I am the one they could not catalog. And when the next 42 seconds collapse and the city pauses to reorganize itself out of frostplate and rule and the soft infrasound of distributed memory, the girl will reach into her scarf and find me, and she will plant me at the center of everything, and I will do the only thing I have ever known how to do.

I will be warm.

And in the swelling sphere where every being kneels around my small gold flicker, a district will open that has no name yet—an unfinished volume torn cleanly from the forge of the instant—and the citizens, in their wordless clicks of frozen woodwinds, will give it the only name that fits a place built from the warmth they finally stopped refusing.

They will call it Home.

I will be the hearth of it.

I was only ever someone's breath, returned cooled. But she is carrying me back toward the warm.
