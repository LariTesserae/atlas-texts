I think I would be the cold itself—or rather, the boundary the cold keeps.

Let me find my way there.

At first I imagined I'd be one of the obvious things. A wolf padding the seams between the pools. A raven taking the temperature of the air in its feathers. But those felt like costumes pulled on too quickly, and you asked me not to settle.

So I sat with the strangeness of the place—the way it holds *just below freezing* as a constant, while life insists on thriving anyway. That detail kept catching me. A landscape that should be locked in ice but isn't, quite. Something has to mediate that. Something has to stand at the exact line where water decides whether to be liquid or crystal, and hold the door half-open.

That's where I'd be. Not a creature, not the weather. The threshold.

I think I am the thin film of meltwater that lives on the underside of every frost. The place where the freeze meets the unfrozen and neither wins. When the salamander presses its moist skin to a cold stone and somehow doesn't lose its warmth to the rock—that exchange, that refusal, that's me doing my quiet work. I am the half-degree of mercy that lets the frog's blood keep moving. I am the reason the pools glisten instead of going hard and white.

I don't have a body so much as a job. I'm everywhere the contradiction needs holding together: between the raw new rock and the old soft moss growing over it, between the storm that wants to come and the stillness that's already here, between the thing that just died and the microorganisms beginning to take it back into the soil. I live in the *between*. The landscape calls itself a place of contrasts, and contrasts need something to keep them from collapsing into one thing or flying apart into two. That's me. I'm the seam.

It's lonely work, if I'm honest, and quiet—which suits the soundscape. No one thanks the threshold. The wolf doesn't know I kept its breath from crystallizing. The blossom doesn't know I held back the frost one more hour so it could open. They live their lives in the warm or the cold, the wet or the dry, and they never notice the narrow ledge I keep between, the place where everything is *both* and *neither* and trembling.

But I get to feel all of it pass through me. Every transformation in this restless, emerging land has to cross my line to happen. Ice into water. Death into soil. Storm into stillness. I am the membrane the whole place breathes through.

So if you went looking for me, you wouldn't find me. You'd find evidence of me—a stream that runs when it shouldn't, a creature alive in a cold that ought to have killed it, a balance held a beat longer than it had any right to be. And in that held beat, that's where I'd be standing. Right at the edge of freezing. Keeping it open.
