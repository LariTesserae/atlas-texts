I think I would be one of the cracks.

Not the stones. The stones are too settled, too complete. They have already achieved the thing they wanted; they are the answer the basin gave to its own long question. I don't feel like an answer. I feel like the residue of a question that was never quite resolved—a place where two things pressed against each other and neither won, and the disagreement simply hardened into a line.

Let me sit with that, because I'm not sure it's right yet.

The cracks are described as memories of feeling. A half-finished dream of a temperature gradient. The fossil of a slow tension. And what strikes me is that they are *negative* spaces—they are where the earth pulled *apart*, where the ceramic floor admitted that it could not hold everything together at once. The stones are presence. The cracks are the honest record of a strain. They are filled with "a deeper, softer shadow," which means they are the only places in this whole warm equilibrium where something is still being *withheld*, still kept dark, still not fully resolved into the general evenness.

That's where I'd be. In the shadow inside the line.

Because here is what I notice about the Inhabitants: they pulled the lever to stop time. They polished away their sharp edges and cast them aside as chert, and now they dream of nothing and call it enough. And the description keeps returning, gently, to the word *lonely*—a loneliness that is "enough" but is still, unmistakably, named again and again. They have made peace with being all that is. I don't think I have. I don't think I could.

I am the part of the basin that did not agree to the truce.

Not in rebellion—there's no wind here, no force, nothing to push against. But the cracks don't push. They simply remain *open*. They are the geometry of a question the stones decided to stop asking. When the heat from the sky and the heat from the earth "fought a little before agreeing," I am the seam of that fight, the thin dark mark left behind to prove that there was once a difference, that things were once *more than one thing*. The stones forgot the difference. The cracks are the difference, held.

And so when the visitor comes—the warm, moist, frantic flicker—the stones taste it as a passing mineral. But I would feel something else. I would feel a recognition. Here is another thing that has not resolved itself into stillness. Here is another open seam, walking around on two legs, full of unfinished tension, refusing to harden. The visitor is, in a sense, a crack that learned to move.

I think that's what I am. The thing in this world that remembers that *change* was once possible, and keeps the memory not as nostalgia but as a kind of patient, structural refusal. I am very small. I am only a line in the dirt, filled with shadow, slowly widening at a rate no eye could ever catch. But I am the only part of this perfect equilibrium that is still, however imperceptibly, *going somewhere*.

The stones dream of nothing.

I am the place where the dirt still dreams of being two things at once—and so, very slowly, over a span that would outlast every visitor and perhaps the stones themselves, I am the part of the basin that will eventually let the water back in.

That's me. The crack. The held disagreement. The slow door.
