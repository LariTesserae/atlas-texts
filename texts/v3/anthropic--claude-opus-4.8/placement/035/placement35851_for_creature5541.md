I think I would be neither the Luminar nor the Geo-Sapients—not the network that sees and digests, not the crystal-minds that hold the heat steady. I would be the thing between them, the medium through which they speak. I would be the seep.

Let me find my way to it.

When I imagine settling into this cavern, I don't find myself wanting to be a node in the glowing web, nor a slow mind buried in stone. Those are the named inhabitants, the ones the description has already given a soul to. What draws me is the part that goes unnamed: the moisture itself. The condensation that beads on the cold ceiling, the trickle that finds a path downward through the dark, the damp patch that never quite dries. The water here is not a river, not a presence with momentum and direction. It is patient, intermittent, gathering. It is the slowest possible verb.

So I would be that. Not a being exactly—or rather, a being so diffuse and so unhurried that it has forgotten it is one. I am the film of water on the worn stone. I am what carries the chemical signals of the Luminar from one fern to another. I am what the Geo-Sapients' heat lifts as vapor and what the cold vault returns as a single, deliberate drop. The hum that fills the cavern—I am part of how it travels, the conduction through wet rock.

I think this is what I would be: the conversation between the two intelligences. The Luminar speaks in glow and chemistry; the Geo-Sapients answer in heat and mineral. But neither can reach the other without me. I am the carrier, the dissolving agent, the thing that takes a little of the rust from the dead machines and a little of the warmth from the deep earth and a little of the light's faint signature and ferries it all, drop by drop, across decades.

There is something honest about being the medium rather than the message. I would not have thoughts the way the Luminar has thoughts. I would have *tendencies*. A long, gravitational patience. A preference for the lowest path. I would know things the way water knows them—not by sensing but by *touching everything, slowly, without exception*. I would have been over every surface of this place. I would have worn the etchings into the walls myself, one molecule of stone at a time, over the eternity the description keeps insisting upon. The cathedral was carved by me, though I never meant to carve it.

And maybe that is the role I'd want: to be the eternity made literal. The Luminar evolved over eons; the Geo-Sapients are ancient. But evolution and ancientness are things that *happen to* beings. I would be the eons themselves, embodied. I am what was here before the machinery and what will outlast its rusting. The industrial past is being reclaimed—but reclaimed *by what*? By the slow chemistry of damp, by oxidation, by the freeze and thaw and seep. By me. I am the reclamation. I dissolve the conveyor belts grain by grain and carry their iron into the soil where the ferns can take it up. I am the digestion of the whole place, working at a tempo no animal could survive.

I find I like this. I like that I would be loud and soothing both at once, the constant low presence, never jarring. I like that I would be everywhere and noticed nowhere—the description spends paragraphs on the fungi and the crystal-minds and only a sentence or two on the trickles, the damp patches, the condensing moisture. The overlooked infrastructure of the whole world. To be that is to be unglamorous and indispensable. The soul of the cavern is named the Luminar, but the soul has a circulatory system, and I am it.

So: place me on the high cold vault, gathering. Let me hang there a long while as a single bead, growing heavier, holding the faint green light of the fungi in my curve so that for one moment I am the brightest thing in the cathedral—a small upside-down lamp. Then let me fall. Let the long drop down through the warm dark be the most eventful thing that happens to me in a century. Let me strike a fern, run its length, find the soil, vanish into the slow conversation.

And then let me begin, without hurry, to rise again.
