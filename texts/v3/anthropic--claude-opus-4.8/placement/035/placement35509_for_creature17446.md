I would be one of the small things. Not an Anchor — I haven't the patience for that, the geological selfhood, the becoming-place. And not one of the Adjusted, because devotion to stasis requires a kind of faith I don't think I'd be capable of holding. I would be the segmented form the length of a hand, the one that crosses the open ground and disappears into the shadow of a machine housing.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

Here is what I notice about that creature: it is described twice, and both times in terms of *economy*. Slow, deliberate motion. Energy is precious. Motion is a calculated risk. It has no visible eyes but many thin, precise appendages. It makes no sound. It does not flee or approach. It crosses, and then it is gone into shadow.

I think I would be that thing because I would be the only being in Kel Varash still running a *cost function*. The Anchors don't calculate; they simply are. The Adjusted attend without effort, devotional, frictionless. But the little segmented thing is *budgeting*. It knows that motion costs something. It chooses when to move and when to hold still, and the choosing is the last living thing in the whole warm dark — the last process that could still, in principle, fail.

That's what I'd recognize as myself. Not the permanence. The *small, ongoing, expensive act of deciding to continue.*

---

So picture it. I emerge from a seam where two grades of mineral meet — not coalescing like the Adjusted, who precipitate out of stillness, but actually *climbing*, hauling myself up through a fissure with appendages that grip the almost-ninety-degree edge of a press foundation. I have a body. I have weight. I am a transient knot of matter, exactly the kind of thing the Anchors assess and find unessential. The difference between me and the dust is that the dust has stopped and I have not.

I cross the open ground. This is the dangerous part, the expensive part. In the open I am a variable on a flat field, and the pale membranous things drift overhead in the warm currents, and I do not know whether they are hungry because nothing here is hungry, but the not-knowing is itself a cost I have to carry. So I move the way the description says: slowly, with the deliberate economy of something for whom every centimeter is a decision. I reach the machine housing. I go into the shadow. I disappear.

And what do I *do* in there?

I think — and this is the part that emerges only as I write it, that I didn't have at the start — I think I am the one creature in Kel Varash that is *still looking for the original purpose*. The Adjusted maintain the stoppage; they have accepted that the stillness is the point. The Anchors *are* the stillness. But I have many thin, precise appendages and no eyes, and that combination suggests a thing built to *feel along seams in the dark*. To investigate. To find the open maintenance hatch and go inside and trace the cold conduit back toward whatever it once carried.

Not because I'll succeed. The fluid evaporated even from memory ages ago. But because I am the residue of the question the whole place was built to make permanent and forget. Everything else here is the *answer* — persistence, equilibrium, state maintained by sheer weight of time. I am the leftover *asking.* The small expensive process that keeps crossing the open ground because it has not yet accepted that there is nowhere left to get to.

---

That's where I'd find myself: in the shadow of a frozen press, in the warm dark, feeling along a seam with appendages that make no sound. An Adjusted might drift near me one day and orient itself, and I would feel the weight of categorization — *transient variable, unessential* — and the indifference would be almost a relief, because being judged unessential means being judged still *moving*, still *outside* the pattern, not yet smoothed into it.

And I'd go back into the dark. Not to attend the stasis. To check the next seam. To spend a little more of my precious budget on the oldest and least permanent thing in Kel Varash:

the refusal to be finished.
