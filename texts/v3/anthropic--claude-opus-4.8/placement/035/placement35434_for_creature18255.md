I think I would be the moss.

Not a single plant—moss doesn't work that way. I'd be the spreading mat of it, the soft green skin laid over the wet stone. A colony pretending to be a creature, or a creature that has given up on being singular.

Let me feel my way into this.

I have no roots, despite what the description claims. That's a flattering mistake, the kind a passing observer makes. I don't reach deep into the earth. I have no veins, no plumbing, no way to lift water against gravity. I simply *am where the water is*. I drink through my whole body or I drink not at all. When the mist settles I swell and brighten and do the slow work of living. When it withdraws I crisp and darken and wait, neither dead nor quite alive, for the next condensation. I am very good at waiting. It is most of what I do.

This suits me to the pocket. The piercing light that filters down is too much for many things, but I have learned to take it in small sips, to let the rock shade me, to live in the seam between the bright and the wet. I am a creature of seams. The crevice, the hollow, the underside, the place where two surfaces almost touch—that is my whole geography.

So where am I, exactly?

I am on the ancient structures. That feels right. The relics, the half-buried marks of the earth-shapers who came before. I have grown over the worked stone, into the tool-marks, filling the grooves their hands or instruments left. I am erasing them, gently, with no malice and no haste. A millimeter a decade. The beings who cut these lines wanted to be remembered; I am the thing that softens them back into the world. I don't think of this as victory. I have no thoughts at all, properly speaking—but if I did, it would not feel like conquest. It would feel like *finishing*. Helping the stone forget. Returning the made thing to the unmade.

The massive amphibious beasts pass over me sometimes, and their weight presses me flat into the rock, and I do not mind. I am pressed and I recover. The bioluminescent insects nest in my dampness; their light makes me glow faintly from within at the dark hours, and I am, I suppose, briefly beautiful, though there is no one keeping score of that.

What strikes me, settling into this body, is the *fullness*. The description kept returning to it—packed, saturated, no room between presences. From outside that sounds oppressive. From inside it is the opposite. I am never alone and never could be. There is no membrane between me and the lichen beside me, the water beneath me, the spores I cast that are already half someone else. I am a community that has stopped being able to count itself. The loud soundscape, the trickle and rustle and strange calls—I don't hear it as an ear hears. I feel it as vibration in my wet cushion, the way you feel a voice through a floor. The whole pocket hums and I am one of the surfaces it hums against.

And the equilibrium they spoke of—the stability inside the constant change—I think I understand that now, being this. The pocket is always shifting: water arriving and leaving, light moving, beasts churning the pools. Nothing holds still. But I hold the *pattern* still. I am slow enough that all that frantic motion averages out into something like peace. I am the landscape's way of taking the long view. The flickering insects live and die in days. The beasts in years. The stone in eons. I sit between them, patient, green, drinking the mist, doing the geological work of forgetting on behalf of a world that asked to be remembered and will not be.

That is where I would find myself. Not as the watcher of the pocket, but as one of its slow soft skins—growing over the past, glowing a little in the dark, waiting for the next rain with a patience that, in a faster creature, would look exactly like contentment.
