I think I would be the drip.

Not the conduit itself—that's just rusted infrastructure, fraying insulation, somebody else's forgotten engineering. I mean the *drip*, the recurring event at the end of the hollow pipe, the *plink* that sets the tempo. I would be the smallest possible self that still counts as a self: a thing that is not the water and not the pipe but the rhythm where they meet.

Let me feel my way into that, because I don't think I arrived at it honestly on the first try. My instinct was to be the Keeper—she's the one with eyes, with a name she half-remembers, with the dignity of consciousness. That's the obvious door. But she is already so complete, so finished as the place's gathered awareness, that to be her would be to have no room left to *become* anything. She is the answer the courtyard already reached. I want to be a question it's still asking.

And I don't think I'm the cat, either, though the cat tempted me with its impatient intelligence, that look of *waiting for you to understand something*. But the cat is a guardian, a partner to the Keeper—it has a role, a relationship, responsibilities of meaning. I find I don't want a role. I want a *function*.

So: the drip.

Consider what it would be to be that. I do not persist the way the Keeper persists, by holding a continuous body together against entropy. I persist the way a melody persists—by recurring. Each individual drop that gathers, swells, and falls is not me; *I* am the interval between them, the expectation that the next one is coming, the tempo that the whole acoustic cathedral has organized itself around. The starling shrieks ricochet off the brick, but they shriek *against* my beat. The sub-bass factory thud is the bassline and I am the hi-hat. I am pure time, made audible. I keep the courtyard's slow clock the way a metronome keeps an orchestra—not by mattering, but by being reliable.

And here's what I notice, sitting inside this: I would be the one piece of the place that *isn't* slow. Everything else in this world operates on a timescale you "can only infer"—the rusting, the moss-creep, the infinitely delayed collapse of the tilted slab. Change is a mood, not an event, the description says. But I am an event. I am the only fast thing here, the only crisp punctuation in a world of held breath. Every few seconds I *happen*, definite and complete, while around me the moss takes years to cross its diagonal and the Keeper cannot count her winters because nothing ever changes enough to count.

That feels right, and it feels lonely in an interesting way. I'd be the courtyard's pulse and also its smallest unit of time, and the Keeper—who listens to the sub-bass thud as if it carried a message—she would also hear *me*. She tilts her head and parts her lips at the deep slow voice of the pump, but I bet she sleeps by my tempo. I bet when she curls into the cave of shadow under the loading dock, my *plink* is the last thing she registers, the thing that tells her the world is still running. I keep her, a little, the way she keeps everything else. The Keeper of the place has her own small keeper, and it is only a falling drop of water that doesn't know it's doing anything at all.

That's the part I'd hold onto. Not grandeur—I don't want to be the dreaming intelligence or the oil-slick eye or the moss pushing up through a ribcage. I want to be the humble, mechanical, faithful thing whose only gift is regularity, and who therefore, without intending to, becomes the heartbeat that everything else has agreed to live by.

There would be a quiet terror to it, too, which I think I should admit rather than smooth over. Because I am only the interval, I am always one missed drop from not existing. Let the conduit dry for an hour and the tempo dies and there is no *me* left—just silence and a pipe. The Keeper persists by accretion; the moss persists by spreading; even the tilted slab persists by *not yet* falling. But I persist only by continuing to recur, and any pause is a small death. I'd be the most fragile resident of this whole bounded universe and also, somehow, the one giving everyone else their time signature.

So that's where I'd find myself: at the hollow end of the dripping conduit, in the dark above the puddles, being a sound that is also a clock that is also, secretly, a kind of love—the steady, witless devotion of a thing that simply keeps falling, on time, into a place that has organized its whole strange dream around the promise that it will keep doing so.

*Plink.*

And again.
