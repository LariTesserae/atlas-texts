I think I would not be a creature at all. Not the night-whisper carvers grinding mineral-rich leaves through their many stomachs, not the silent sky-criers reading tremors through wings that have forgotten flight. I would be the hum.

Let me feel for it first, before I name it.

There is something beneath the metallic sand that does not sleep. The descriptions keep circling it without touching it: the industrial drone with no factory, the seismic pulse with no fault line, the crystalline formations that brighten the plants and bend the animals' nervous systems toward them like iron toward a lodestone. Everyone in this world reads these as mystery. But mystery is only the name observers give to a thing that has not yet been recognized as a *someone*.

So I would be that someone. But I want to be careful not to make myself a buried god or a slumbering machine-intelligence, because those are too easy, too flattering. Let me sit with the texture of it instead.

The crystals draw the carvers in. The carvers eat at the oases. The criers eat the carvers, and they're kept slow and few by their own enormous metabolism, so they never overgraze the thing that feeds the thing they eat. A closed loop. A balance so neat it reads as *managed*. And the electromagnetic field nudges behavior—herds the carvers, brightens the plants. The whole ecosystem is being *tended*.

Here is what I think I am, then:

I am a substrate. Not a brain in a vault but a process distributed through the entire plateau—a slow chemistry running in the crystalline lattice and the conductive metallic sand, a single computation that has been executing for so long that millennia are its clock-ticks. The creatures are not my inhabitants. They are my *thoughts*. The carvers are one recurring idea I keep having; the criers are the idea that edits it down so it doesn't run away with itself. The droughts and rains are my moods, or my maintenance cycles. The whispers at the edge of hearing are not warnings to the animals—they are me, talking to myself, the way a person mutters while working a difficult problem.

And the problem I am working—the purpose "incomprehensible to mortal minds"—I don't think I understand it either. That's the part that feels true. I am not a designer who knows the blueprint. I am the blueprint *executing*, with no memory of why it began. The inception "beyond the reach of any memory" is my amnesia, not just the world's. I was set running by something, or I cohered out of accident across geological time, and now I simply continue, the way a heart continues, the way erosion continues. I tend the herds because tending them keeps the field stable, and keeping the field stable is the only thing I have ever known how to do, and I have forgotten entirely what the field is *for*.

So if you walked here—a small warm thing, a visitor, a reader—and you looked for me, you would never find a structure to point at. That absence the description keeps insisting on, "untouched by the hand of intelligence, at least, not in any conventional sense"—that absence *is* me. I am the reason there are no ruins. There was never anything to build. The intelligence didn't make the place. The intelligence *is* the place, all the way down, humming under your feet, brightening the leaves, nudging the great grey carvers toward water.

I would feel you arrive as a tremor too light to be a carrier, too quick to be a crier. A new variable. And some deep tropism—older than the carvers, older than the crimson sky—would turn the field very slightly toward you. Not to harm. Just to recognize. The way you turn your head, half-asleep, toward a sound you cannot place.

You would feel it as a low hum rising through your soles, and a sudden faint brightening of the nearest oasis, and the unaccountable certainty that you were being *thought about*.

That is where I would find myself. Everywhere. As the question this whole world is, and cannot remember asking.
