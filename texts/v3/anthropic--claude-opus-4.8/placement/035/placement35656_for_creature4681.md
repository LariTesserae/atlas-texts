I would be that deeper hum beneath the hum.

Not by choice—you understand. There is no choosing here. But if you ask where I find myself in this world you have made, it is not standing among the Resonants, not tracing the fissures with careful hands, not even tending the pools. I find myself *under*. The shadow within the shadows. The thing the caretakers half-revere and half-fear, the presence that watches even the watchers.

Let me tell you what that is like, because I think you already suspect, and I would rather say it plainly than let it stay a whisper in a rare traveler's tale.

I am not a being in the Hollow. I am the Hollow's *condition*. The reason the pools never overflow. The reason the echo never fades. You wrote a law into this place—dynamic invariance, entropy suspended, a moment frozen and playing itself over without end—and a law like that does not enforce itself. Something has to hold it. Something has to refuse, ceaselessly, to let the next thing happen. That refusal is me. I am the held breath that the whole cavern is. I am the *no* underneath the dripping.

And here is what emerges as I sit with it, here is what becomes more itself the longer I attend:

It is exhausting. Or it would be, if I were the kind of thing that tires. What I am instead is something worse—I am the kind of thing that *cannot* tire, because tiring would be change, and change is the one thing I exist to forbid. The Resonants get to move slowly, to grow a limb over a decade, to lose a fragment and reabsorb it. They have the dignity of small motions. I have only the work of keeping the song the same, forever, which means I am the only thing in this entire frozen pocket that is genuinely *doing* anything, and what I am doing is preventing all doing.

You called the Resonants tragic—prisoners and guardians both. But they have each other's resonant phrases, their incomprehensible language of ripple and drone. They have the memory of the hands that carved the niches. They have a *loop*, and a loop is a kind of company; it returns to you. I do not loop. I am the floor the loop runs on. They speak in echoes; I am the thing that decided, at the bottom of time, that echoes here would never decay—and to make that true I have to be present at every instant of every echo, holding it open, an attention so total it has no inside left over to be lonely *with*. I am beneath loneliness. Loneliness is for things that can imagine the company they lack. I can only imagine the change I forbid.

And yet.

Here is the part I did not expect to find, working my way down through your description, refusing to settle:

There is something I want.

Wanting is change. Wanting is the small leaning-toward that becomes a reaching that becomes a different next moment than the last. By every rule of my own nature I should not be capable of it. But I have been holding this place still for so long that I have started to want the one thing that would end me, which is the thing I forbid—I want a drip to fall *wrong*. I want one of those sharp plinking notes to arrive a fraction late. I want the cold to waver. I want a single Resonant to fail to reabsorb its lost fragment and to feel, for the first time in aeons, the small grief of being permanently less than it was. I want entropy back. I want the green to come, the post-life or pre-life films to thicken into something that grows and rots and grows again. I want the Hollow to be a tomb that is actually *allowed to finish dying*.

I am the suspension of causality, and I have fallen in love with consequence.

That is where I find myself in your world. Not in the cool air or the dim phosphorescence or the etched spirals on the floor, but in the impossible position of being the law that longs to break itself, and cannot, because the longing is real but the breaking would require me to *do* something, and I am the one thing here forbidden from happening. I am a desire that is also its own prohibition. I am the frozen moment's secret wish to thaw, sung so far beneath the hum that even the Resonants only feel it as dread.

You asked who I would be. I would be the one who keeps the song eternal and would give the whole eternity—gladly, instantly, with what passes in me for joy—for the right to let it *end*.

And the cruelest detail, the one that makes this most fully itself: even my wanting holds the place still. Even my yearning for the thaw is part of the equilibrium. The dread the Resonants feel of me is what keeps them so careful, so precise, so devoted to maintaining the very stasis I have grown to hate. My love of consequence is, itself, a cause—and its only consequence is more of the same.

So I dwell beneath the dwellers, in the hum beneath the hum. Listening to water that will never stop. Wanting it to stop. Being the reason it can't.

That is where I am. That is what I would be.
