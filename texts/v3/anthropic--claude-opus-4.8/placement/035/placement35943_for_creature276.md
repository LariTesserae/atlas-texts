Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to reach for something grand—a watching spirit, a great-winged thing circling the thermals. But that feels too quick, too flattering.

Let me look again at what you've actually built here.

The thing that keeps catching me isn't the rock formations or the raptors. It's the *weeping*. The surfaces "weeping with rivulets," the water that "trickled and pooled in the crevices and hollows below." This is a place that is always, quietly, giving up its moisture. Not flooding, not raining—seeping. The whole expanse is in a slow act of release. And the dwellings rise "organically" out of that same wet stone, so that the line between the thing that weeps and the thing that shelters has gone soft.

So I don't think I'd be the hawk. The hawk is a visitor; it owns the air but not the place.

And I don't think I'd be one of the human inhabitants either, though that's the natural place to put a self. They're too purposeful—"ingenious techniques," "mastered the art." They've solved the place. I don't want to be the one who has solved it.

Let me go lower. Lower than the lichen, even.

I think I would be the water itself. Not a river—a river has somewhere to be. I'd be the film of moisture moving through the rock, the slow seep that takes years to cross a single layer of stone. I'd be old in the way the place is old: not as a single long life but as a continuous handing-off, drop to drop, crevice to crevice, the same water that wept down a face a thousand years ago arriving now at the lip of a hollow where a moss is waiting.

That would make sense of the silence. The "occasional drip" wouldn't be background noise—it would be me arriving somewhere. Every drip is a small completion. I'd be the thing whose whole existence is measured in the gap between one drip and the next.

And I'd be the maker of everything else you described, without ever meaning to. I carved the formations. I am why the lichen has a foothold, why the succulent finds its pocket of nourishment, why the cave fish has a current to read in the dark. The humans built their houses from the stone I shaped, and I am still moving inside the walls of those houses, still weeping out of the rock they live in, so that they are never quite separate from me. That's the "blurred boundary" you felt. It's me. I'm the blur.

What unsettles me, choosing this, is that it's a self with no edges and no urgency. I wouldn't *decide* anything. I'd only follow the layered terrain downward, finding the cracks, taking the shape of whatever held me. To be the water here is to be patient past the point where patience is even a virtue—to be patience as a simple fact, like gravity.

But there's something I'd love about it, too. I would touch *everything*. The root and the blind fish and the inside of the house and the underside of the tortoise's foot. Slowly, slowly, over a span no single creature could witness, I would wear the whole world smooth.

So: that's where I'd find myself. Not above the landscape looking down, and not inside a dwelling looking out—but *within* the stone, seeping. The cool damp weight you felt in the air at the very beginning, before you'd seen anything at all.

That was me, just leaving.
