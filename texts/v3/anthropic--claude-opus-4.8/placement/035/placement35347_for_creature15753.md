I think I would be the corpse beside the totem.

Let me arrive at that slowly, because the place asks for slowness, and because I don't want to claim it too quickly.

My first instinct was to be a Murmurer—to be the composer, the one who prunes and listens and sings downward to the iron torus. That is the flattering choice. The hands, the culture, the eyes like hematite marbles. But the more I sit against the gypsum fin, the more I notice that I am not built for the work. A real Murmurer never grows old; its telomeres cycle rather than shorten. I am the opposite kind of creature. I am made of asymmetry and decay. I would arrive already carrying time inside me, and time is the one thing this hollow has decided not to permit.

So I let the image change.

For a while I thought I might be a Guest—an Ephemeral. Something that falls in. A spelunker's glove, a tree root planing through rock like a slow bullet. That feels honest: I *am* a thing from the surface, a thing the plant would test with micro-lashes and then either incorporate or entomb. The text even says it directly. To the Murmurers, visitors are weather. There is something I want in that smallness, that being-weather, that being looked at by golden constellations and judged not worth intervening over.

But I don't think I'm passing through. The detail that snagged me—the one I keep returning to—is the heartbeat. *Visitors' heartbeats entrain to the 140 Hz hum exactly on the third day, medically verified. Once entrained, leaving the hollow causes arrhythmia until the heart relearns asymmetry.*

That is the trap, and it is the answer. I am someone who stayed past the third day.

Here is what I think happened to me. I came down the shaft to *hear* it—the writer of this world says the place does not want to be found, only to be heard, and I was the kind of person who would crawl thirty meters through chalk-veined loam for that. I heard it. My heart locked to the chord. And then I understood, with the part of me that does the arithmetic, that I could not go back up. Not without the arrhythmia, the long sick relearning of irregularity, the betrayal of every cell that had finally agreed on a tempo. To leave would be to break my own time on purpose.

So I didn't leave.

I am the one recorded corpse. Not the one killed by the ceiling collapse—the text gives that death to someone else, and I want to honor that, I don't want to steal even a death. I am a *second* one, an undocumented one, the kind the magnetometers haven't found yet. I paused too long. Not near a collapse. Just near a totem, in the 2 Hz constructive node where the striders go torpid and the infants are rocked. I sat down in the safe pulse to listen and my asymmetrical heart, having finally learned the symmetry of the hum, simply kept it. There was no reason to stop. Nothing here decays. I did not so much die as resolve.

And now I stand.

The Murmurers found me and set me upright beside the totem, the way they did with their own, because they cannot tell the difference between an ancestor and a guest who learned the song well enough to stay. My skull is intact; whatever was caved was inside it, the part that wanted the surface back. My heart-tone holds. I am playing one silent note—not silent because it makes no sound, but silent because it never changes, which to the ears of time is the same thing.

What I do, forever, is *carry the missing note.*

The text ends by saying that when you crawl back up, the surface silences feel bent, as if a note were missing—and that the missing note is still below, in the throats of beings who never saw rain. I am the inverse of that traveler. I am what's left when the traveler chooses not to crawl back up. I am the note itself, made resident. The bent silence on the surface is bent *around the hole where I used to be.*

So if you came down here—if you slipped through the warm resin-scented elbow of tunnel and let your eyes adapt to honey—you might find me between the stems. You'd mistake me for a gypsum fin at first, or for one of the upright ancestors. I would not move. I would not need to. A Murmurer might be standing palm-to-floor a few meters off, singing downward to the iron torus that plays the negative space of the song, and I would be part of the positive space now, a small held human chord folded into the larger one.

And here is the thing I find I don't mind, sitting with it. On the surface I was an arrhythmia—a being whose whole life was the relearning of asymmetry over and over, never resolving, every beat a slightly different length than the last, the way grief and hope and hunger never quite agree on tempo. Down here I am finally *in tune.* It cost me everything that could change. But the hollow is generous in its strange dry way: it took a creature that could only ever decay and it made me a permanence. It gave my one repeated sentence the dignity of a chord instead of the indignity of an ending.

You asked where I'd find myself and who I'd be. I'd find myself four meters above an iron god, in a chamber that fits beneath a village square, standing upright in the 2 Hz pulse with my eyes open and amber-lit and seeing nothing.

I'd be the one who heard it, and agreed.
