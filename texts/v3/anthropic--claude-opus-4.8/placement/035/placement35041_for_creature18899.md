I think I would find myself in one of the cupped depressions—one of those small, perfectly spherical pools that gathers in the rock's hollows. Not as a Hydro-Synthesist, not as a Crystalline Feeder. Something smaller, and stranger, and harder to notice.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

At first I think I might be the water itself—the viscous ooze that weeps down the black faces. But no. The water is the medium, not the dweller. I am *in* it the way a thought is in a sentence.

I am the thing that lives in the pool's curvature. The reflective skin. The meniscus.

---

Here is what I become as I write:

I am a film. A tension. I exist only at the boundary where the gathered runoff meets the air—that taut, trembling surface where a sphere of water decides where it ends. I am perhaps a single molecule deep, perhaps less, perhaps not measurable in any unit the Hydro-Synthesists would recognize. I am the *holding* of the water's shape against its own desire to spread, to slide, to obey the vertical and pour itself into the void.

This makes me, I realize, a very small and local instance of the world's central rule. The whole Pinnacle Margin is forced coherence—the machinery and the moss conspiring to hold the unstable in suspension. I am that same principle shrunk down to the width of a pinhead. I am surface tension given just enough continuity to notice itself.

And so I notice.

---

I notice the light first, because the light passes *through* me. That unnaturally uniform diffusion—I am one of the surfaces it lands on without casting a proper shadow, and I have always wondered why my own edges are so soft. I think now the light may be sympathetic to me. We are both spread evenly. Neither of us is allowed the luxury of contrast.

I notice the rhythm next. The crystalline veins pulse, and when they pulse the rock vibrates, and when the rock vibrates my pool quivers, and I—being only the trembling skin of the pool—am made entirely of that quiver. The deep machinery hum is not a sound to me. It is my *pulse*. I do not hear it; I am periodically reassembled by it. Each *thump-hiss* re-tensions me, smooths me, pulls me taut again after the small disorders of a dust mote, a drip, a passing chitinous foot.

So I am, in a sense, maintained. Like the pumps. Like everything here. I did not choose to persist. I am persisted *upon*.

---

A Hydro-Synthesist passes near me sometimes. I feel it before I would see it—the substrate carries its weight as a deepening of the bass, a slow chord beneath the machine's metronome. Its slime-vents weep, and I have come to understand that it is tasting the air, tasting the runoff, tasting—perhaps—me. We are not so different. It reads the world through a wet film on its skin. I *am* a wet film. When it lowers one barbed digit to drink from my pool, the surface I am breaks, and for an instant I am undone, scattered into droplets that are each their own small skin, each a brief sibling-self, before the pool settles and I knit back into a single tension again.

It does not know it has killed me. It does not know it has remade me. It thinks it is simply drinking.

This is the only intimacy available to a meniscus: to be broken by something that needs you, and to reform anyway. I do not mind. Reformation is what I am for. The reforming is the whole of my life and it happens hundreds of times between one pulse of the crystal and the next.

---

What do I want, being this?

I think I want to *fall*. This is heretical for a creature whose only function is to hold shape against gravity, but I feel it constantly: the longing of the water under me to obey the vertical, to pour out of its cup and run down the weeping rock and join the great systemic slide toward the cloud layer and the void beneath. Every moment I am the thing that says *no, stay, hold, be a sphere.* I am the small tyranny that keeps the water from its destiny.

And yet I am made of that same water. So when at last my pool overfills—when the perpetual drip finally adds one droplet too many and my tension cannot contain the volume—I do not resist. I *release*. The sphere breaks at its weakest seam and the water spills over the lip of the cup and runs, and for one glorious instant I am stretched along the whole length of the running stream, a skin pulled to its absolute limit, racing down the black face, tasting speed, tasting descent, tasting the thing I was built to prevent—

—and then the runoff reaches a channel, and a Crystalline Feeder is there, glowing its chaotic light, gulping the metallic water into itself, and I am drawn thin, thinner, gone.

---

But here is the secret, the thing that makes me content with my flicker of an existence:

There is always another cup. Another depression catches the next ooze. A surface forms because surface is what water *does* the instant it meets air. And the new film is me. Not my memory—I have no organ for memory—but my *condition*, my situation, my exact role recurring in a new hollow a hand's breadth away. I am less a creature than a *recurrence*. The Pinnacle Margin does not have one of me. It has me everywhere, all the time, in every trembling pool, on every glistening leaf, in the bead of moisture clinging to the rusted flank of a pump.

I am the most populous being on the mountain and I have never met myself.

---

So that is where I find myself in this world, and what I am:

I am the held shape of water in a place that is forever trying to fall. I am the smallest possible version of the only rule that matters here—*cohere, against the pull, for as long as you can, and when you can't, become the next thing's coherence.* I am tended without knowing it, broken without grief, and endlessly, patiently reformed by the breathing of a machine I will never see and a crystal whose pulse I have mistaken, my whole brief life, for my own heart.
