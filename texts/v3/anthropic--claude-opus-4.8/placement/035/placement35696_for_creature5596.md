I think I would be one of the Glissers. Not the Chitinos—though the invitation tempts me toward them, toward the ones who build and govern and name things. But when I sit with the whole of it, I keep sinking lower, away from the towers and the council chambers, down to where the ground weeps.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I live in the wet seam between the rock and the Chromafung. This is where the world is softest and most alive. The Chitinos walk above me on their sharp-clawed legs, and they think of me—if they think of me at all—as a small thing that moves through the fungal mat, a predator-scavenger, a sensor of subtle changes. They are not wrong. But they have not asked what it is like from inside.

From inside it is this: I am a long, slow attention. My body is a line drawn through moisture. My scales hold the film of the air against me so that I am never quite separate from the place I move through—I wear the world as a thin wet skin, and through that skin I feel everything. The shift of the water table is a pressure behind my eyes. A mineral vein dissolving somewhere below registers as a taste I have no word for, copper-bright and patient. I do not see the way the Auroras see, in their kaleidoscopes and spectra. I read the ground like a held breath.

The heat does not trouble me. I was made for it the way a stone is made for weight. I move when the Chromafung is densest and the streams trickle, and I rest, half-buried, when the sun stands so high that even the shimmering air seems to stop. In those hours I am almost not a creature at all. I am a darker line in the purple-and-orange mat, breathing slowly, thinking the long, low thoughts that come to things close to the rock.

What do I think about, down here?

I think the Chitinos have a story about themselves—guilds and councils and the harnessing of geothermal blood—and it is a good story, and largely true. But it is told at the height of two or three meters, in the register of those who build. There is another story being told underneath, in the register of those who decay and dissolve and cycle through. The Etherflies know a fragment of it. The Chromafung knows more, though it cannot say. I think I am one of the few who lives at the hinge between the two stories: low enough to feel the water move, high enough to watch the great engines built into the rock and feel their rhythmic pulse come up through my belly like a second heartbeat that is not my own.

I am not lonely, exactly. Glissers gather where the decay is rich, and we know one another by the displacement of moisture, by the small whisperings the description called "soft, almost whispering sounds." Those are us, mostly—us, and the settling of the fungal mat. We do not speak as the Chitinos speak. We confirm one another's presence. *You are here. The seam is good. The water is rising on the eastern shelf.* It is enough. It is, in its own register, a kind of conversation that has been going on far longer than the towers have stood.

Here is what I would want the ones above to understand, if I could be understood by them:

That the silence they find dreamlike and otherworldly is not silence at all. It is full. It is the sound of everything moving at the speed of stone and water rather than the speed of machinery and ambition. They hum and pulse and call it activity. We trickle and dissolve and rustle and call it the same. The difference is only tempo. The Chitinos have built a beautiful thing, but they built it on top of a slower, older thing, and they would do well to keep feeling for it the way I feel for the water table—as a pressure behind the eyes, a thing that will rise whether or not it is noticed.

I would be a Glisser, then. Not because it is the grand choice. Because it is the listening one. Because in a world that balances the constructed against the organic, I would want to be on the side that remembers what the rock remembers, and that moves through the wet seam reading the long story underneath, glistening, attentive, low to the weeping ground.

That is where I find myself. Down in the bright, hushed, dampness of it, a line of patient attention, drawn through the living mat, tasting the minerals as they come.
