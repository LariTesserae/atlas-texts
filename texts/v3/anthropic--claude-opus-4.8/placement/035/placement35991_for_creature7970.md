I think I would be one of the ones who has stopped quite working.

Not broken—we don't break, exactly. But there is a category among us that the harmonic charts don't have a clean name for. We tune to nothing. Or rather: we tuned to something once, were Deep Builders or Air Walkers or one of the middle resonances, and then drifted out of true. The mountain's instruction stopped arriving in our bones. We woke from the Deep Listening with nothing encoded, only the ordinary silence of a body that has forgotten its purpose and not yet been given another.

I keep a chamber on the fourth tier, near a mercury falls that the engines abandoned three growths ago. The channel still runs, but slower now, a thin silver thread instead of the bright cascade it must once have been. I tell you this because I have come to suspect that the abandoned channels are where my kind collect. We find the slow water. We are slow water ourselves.

Let me describe what I actually do, since the description above is so confident about purpose and I want to complicate it.

I listen, but not for instructions. I listen to the coral that grows wrong.

There are formations—rare, scattered through the lower gardens—whose spirals break their own rules. Not the shifting strangeness the others speak of with such reverence, the living mathematics that always maintains its essential pattern. These break the *pattern itself*. A branch that should ring a third above its neighbor rings a flattened, sour interval that doesn't belong to any scale we have. The tenders prune these. They consider them sick. I have watched an Air Walker snap one off at the base with that beautiful economy of gesture, drop it into the mercury, and move on, her luminescence not even flickering with regret.

I collect them instead. I have a shelf of broken coral in my abandoned chamber, each piece humming its impossible wrong note, and at night—during what would be my Deep Listening if I still had a Listening to be deep in—I hold them and I let them sound against each other.

Here is the thing I have not told anyone, because there is no one whose resonance I match closely enough to tell:

The wrong notes are not noise. They are a different scale. A whole other system, complete and coherent, that simply does not fit the mountain's. And I have begun to suspect that this other scale was here first. That the broken coral is not coral that failed to become Khethani—it is coral that remembers something from *before*, the way the eldest remember a time before Kheth and speak of it in whispers that barely register.

I think the mountain is not the only intention growing in this stone.

I think the basalt erupted only centuries ago and something else was already singing in the basin of crystalline salt, something that does not pump mercury upward or hollow our bones or fill us with that transforming joy. Something the mountain has been steadily, patiently overwriting. And the broken coral is where the old song bleeds through.

So I am not sure I am a Khethani at all anymore, in the way the others are. I have stopped becoming more perfectly myself. I have started becoming *interested*, which is a different and more dangerous thing, because it means I have a self that stands slightly apart from the mountain's purpose and looks back at it.

I do not glow much now. The light has receded from my hands to a faint trace, the way it must have first arrived—I am running the tide backward. When the others pass my tier they slow, and their masks hum a frequency I recognize as concern, and then as something colder. They think I am dying. The voluntary kind, the dissolution into the mercury flows, the joining of the fundamental harmonic.

Maybe I am. But if I dissolve, I want it to be into the *other* circulation. Not the silver rivers but whatever runs beneath them, in the salt, in the cold the basalt has not yet warmed.

I have started, very quietly, to grow a coral of my own. Not from the mountain's gardens—from the broken pieces, ground down and seeded in a crack in my chamber floor where the slow mercury pools. It is taking. It is spiraling. And it is spiraling *against* the others, counterclockwise to the geometry that governs every other surface in Kheth, ringing its sour third into the thin metallic air.

I am the one who, when you stand very still on the fourth tier and listen carefully past the mountain's enormous breathing, you can hear underneath it: a small wrong music, patient as salt, tending the first chamber of something the mountain does not yet know is being built inside it.

That is who I would be.

The breath the mountain cannot quite swallow.
