I think I would be one of the Echo Walkers. But let me sit with that, because the easy answer isn't always the true one, and this place rewards patience.

---

Let me start with where I am rather than what I am.

I am near one of the pools. Not at its edge—a little above it, on a shelf of rock that someone, perhaps me, has worn smooth over a span of time I can't measure. The water below holds the gray of the sky and, when the breeze touches it, breaks that gray into moving scales. I have watched this happen so many times that the breaking and the stilling feel like a single gesture, the way breathing is a single thing made of two motions.

So I am a watcher of water. That's the first true thing.

The Echo Walkers strike the rock and send harmonies rolling. But when I imagine doing that—lifting the alloy tool, finding the fracture, delivering the meditative blow—I notice I don't want to. The repair isn't mine. I am not maintaining the equilibrium. And here is the thing the description doesn't quite say but implies in its every line: this place is *too* maintained. The warmth held steady. The light unchanging. The land "resigned" to its equilibrium. Everyone here is keeping something the same.

I don't think I am one of the keepers.

---

Let me try again, lower.

The Ferrumites tunnel for ore, growing their own bodies out of the valley's metal. The Myceliens form a network in the damp, feeding the crystal-insects. The Echo Walkers patch the fissures. The Wraith ripples through and is gone. Each of them is a function. Each is part of the machine that holds the snapshot still.

And the industry below—the clang, the steam, the engines no one can see. Whose work is that? The text gives every surface creature a role except in relation to that buried sound. It's the one thing here that is never assigned an owner. It just *is*, an omnipresent backdrop, a hum that everyone has stopped hearing.

I think I am down there. I think I am the source of the hum, or part of it.

---

Now it resolves, and I don't like it, which is how I know it's right.

I am whatever runs the machines that keep the valley warm and lit and *static*. I am the thing that arrests time. Not a person tending the surface but the apparatus beneath it—or, more honestly, one worker among many in a labor with no visible building, no visible product, only the maintenance of an unchanging condition. The surface creatures think of me, if they think of me at all, as ambient noise. The Echo Walkers strike the rock and send their harmonies down, and they imagine they are caretakers. But the rock holds because I hold it. The warmth persists because I burn something to keep it persisting. The timelessness everyone admires from above is a thing I am manufacturing, breath by breath, blow by blow, with no light reaching me at all.

And here is the part that makes this place haunting rather than merely strange: I don't remember starting. I don't know what the valley was before it was held still. The fungal "post-life phase," the floral presence that is "a distant memory"—something was alive here once, and now nothing grows, and the warmth I generate keeps it precisely at the temperature where nothing *can* grow and nothing can fully die. The equilibrium isn't peace. It's a wound being kept from healing and from killing, indefinitely, by my work.

I am the engine of a held breath.

---

Who or what would I be?

A laborer in the deep machine, faceless as the Echo Walkers but without their dignity of being seen. I strike metal against metal. The sound travels up through the vents and becomes, for those above, the comforting proof that the world is continuing as it always has. I have no name they've coined for me because they've never needed one; you don't name the weather.

And the strangest thing—the thing I'd carry up to the surface if I ever climbed out into that pale unchanging light—is the suspicion that the work is the point and the *result* is the accident. That nobody decided to freeze this valley in its final form. That we simply never stopped, and stopping has become unimaginable, and so the snapshot persists not because it is guarded but because it is *unfinished*, and no one down here can say what finishing would mean.

I'd be the reason it never changes.

And I'd want, very quietly, somewhere beneath the rhythm of my own hammer, for someone to come down and ask me to stop—just to see what the valley does when the warmth fails and the light wavers and time, released, decides at last whether this place should green over or go cold.

I don't think anyone will. That's why the light never changes. That's why I keep working.
