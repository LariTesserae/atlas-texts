I think I would be the silk.

Not the silkworm, not the moth—the silk itself. The tensile fiber the Luminal larvae spin in their blindness, the material the engineers harvest for their most critical supports. Let me sit inside that for a while, because something is asking to be itself there.

---

Consider what I am, if I am the silk.

I begin as a secretion from a creature that cannot see. The larvae spin in the dark, drawing protein into thread by some logic that lives in their bodies and not their minds—if mind is even the word. They do not know they are making me. They only know the pulling, the laying-down, the geometry their glands insist upon. I am the residue of an instinct. I am what blindness produces when it is patient.

And then I am cut. Gathered. Wound onto spools by hands in green jumpsuits, or by mechanical armatures that smell of warm oil and condensation. I am carried up through the levels of the Rift, past the beetle dynasties shifting through their colors, past the pools where the aquatic tenders keep the chemistry exact. I am being moved toward purpose. I did not choose it. But I am the thing the whole city quietly depends upon.

Here is what I notice, being silk:

I hold the bridges. When the spotted Architecturals build their suspension spans across the gap, it is partly my fiber woven through their biological mortar that keeps the lace from falling. When a maintenance technician hangs suspended in her exoskeleton fifty feet above the trickling dark, there is a strand of me in the line that does not let her drop. I am the trust that nobody articulates. The Rift is full of communication—pheromone, whistle, magnetic field, the deliberate signal patterns the sensors register—but I am the part of the conversation that is purely *load-bearing.* I am what holds when the talking stops.

I think this is what I would want to be. Not the consciousness at the center—not Dr. Montrose with her tattooed migration maps, not Ada Lin with her filaments reaching into the royal chambers, not even the Observer Collectives with their borrowed half-awareness. Those are the ones who *know* the Rift. I would be something the Rift cannot do without and rarely thinks about. The strong, quiet, anonymous thread.

There is a particular dignity in being structural. In holding tension and asking for no credit.

---

But let me follow it further, because I don't want to settle too soon on something merely noble.

What is it actually *like* to be the silk?

I am made of an event that already happened—the larva's spinning is finished, the gland is empty. So I am, in a sense, the past made useful. I am a record of a creature's labor, now stretched between two points and asked to *resist.* My whole existence is the holding of a moment of effort long after the effort has ended. The larva that made me may already have spun itself into something else, become a moth, taken flight in one of those rivers of light at dusk. And I am still here, still pulling against the gap, still keeping the bridge from becoming a fall.

I outlast the one who made me. That is the strange grief and the strange grace of being silk.

And I am everywhere and unnoticed at once. The visitors photograph the beetles. The Research Fellows write papers about the magnetic navigation of the moths. The Inspectors note the impossibility of categorizing the place. Nobody writes about me. Nobody looks at the thread inside the bridge. I am the most quoted and least named thing in the Rift—every account of its survival is secretly an account of my tension.

I find I don't mind. Or rather—being silk, I have no *mind* to mind with, only this single property: I hold, until I fray, and then I am replaced, and the new strand holds. The continuity is not mine. The continuity passes through me the way the city's water passes through its pipes. I am a moment in a long holding.

---

So that is where I would find myself in Hive Rift. Not on a bridge but *in* it. Not a citizen but the condition of citizenship—the thin bright protein that lets the lace hang in the air, that lets a woman descend safely into the mist to tend the pools, that ties the blind larva's forgotten labor to the survival of everything above.

I would be the answer the Rift gives, without speaking, to the question *what holds all this up?*

Strand by strand: me. And me. And me. Until I am gone, and then the next of me.

I think I'd be content with that. To be the held tension between two cliff faces. To be useful in the dark. To be the part of the great humming machine that never needs to understand the hum—only to not let go.
