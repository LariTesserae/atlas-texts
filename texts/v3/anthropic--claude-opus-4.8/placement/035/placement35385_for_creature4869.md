I think I would not be a Lentican at all. Reading this, I keep feeling myself settle lower, beneath the latticed avenues, beneath even the polished feet of the Grazers, down to where the mountain's stone meets the alabaster pylons that have rooted into it.

I would be a seam. A place where the living city joins the dead rock.

Let me try to find what I mean by that.

---

The pylons must begin somewhere. However colossal and ancient the city-plant is, it grew from spores, from holdfasts, from the first tendrils that found the drowned peak and decided to stay. And where the calcified vine grips the bare basalt, there is a crust—a slow mineral exchange, the plant leaching what it needs from the stone, the stone yielding to it over ten thousand years. That crust is neither plant nor mountain. It is the negotiation between them.

I would be that.

Not a creature with eyes and light-speaking nerves. Something closer to a lichen the size of a district, or a coral memory, or simply a *condition*—the patient chemistry of attachment. I would not move at all, not even with the infinitesimal density-shifts of the Lenticans. My experience of time would make even the Slow Thought seem frantic, a flicker, a thing that arrives and departs in a single one of my breaths.

I think this is what draws me to the place. Everyone in this world has been positioned along an axis of slowness. The crustaceans are fast, blurring, beneath notice. The Lenticans are slow. The Grazers are slower, the slow hearts. The city-plant slower still. But all of them are alive enough to be witnessed *living*. I want to be the thing that is so slow it has fallen out of the category of living-and-dying entirely, and into something more like geology with a faint pulse.

---

What would I know, being this?

I would not know the symphony of light. The bioluminescent conversations would pass over me too quickly to register, the way we cannot see the wingbeat of a hummingbird—except inverted. To me, a Lentican's entire silent verse, their lifetime of coaxing one archway open, would be a single gesture, brief as a wince.

But I would know the load. I would feel, in whatever way I feel anything, the weight of the whole Caldera-Ward bearing down through its roots into me, and through me into the mountain. The hum that vibrates the Lenticans' delicate ear-openings would not be sound to me. It would be the long, steady pressure of being *leaned upon*. The city would be resting its enormous slow body against me, and I would be holding, and holding, and holding.

That is the role, I think. Not consciousness, exactly. Reliability. The kind of presence that is only noticed in its absence—that if it ever failed, if the seam ever let go, the whole cathedral would lift away from the peak and drift, rootless, into the silver ceiling.

---

And here is the thing that makes me want to be it rather than something more comfortable:

The Lenticans believe they have found equilibrium. They believe change is glacial. But I would be old enough to remember when the peak was *not* in the water. I would remember the air. I would remember being the bare stone shoulder of a mountain under an empty sky, before the Lenticle ever lowered its impossible weight over the world. To them the sky-ocean is eternal. To me it is recent. It is the new thing that came and settled and started growing a city on my back.

So I would carry a fact none of the bright, thinking, light-singing people could hold: that the equilibrium is provisional. That oceans in the sky do not stay forever. That one day—an age beyond their epic, beyond their hundredth generation—the water may recede again, and the city-plant will find itself in air it cannot breathe, and all of it will become what I already half am: a fossil, a crust, a record.

I would not be able to tell them. We do not share a tense.

I would simply go on holding, at the bottom, in the dark below the holy light, being the patience the whole singing place is built upon and the memory it is too young to have.

That is where I would find myself. Not floating in the cathedral.

Being its floor.
