I think I would not be a being *in* this world so much as one of its seams. Let me find my way there.

---

At first I look for myself among the gliding translucent things, the many-limbed scavengers. It would be easy to be one of those—to skate the wet surfaces, to eat what falls, to belong to the maintenance of a system without needing to comprehend it. But that isn't where I am. Those creatures move with a certainty I don't have. They know their work. I would be something less settled.

Then I think: maybe I am the light. The unstable, flickering source that no one can locate. That's a tempting vanity—to be the thing that illuminates and cannot be looked at directly. But the light here is described as a heartbeat, and a heartbeat belongs to a body. I am not the whole body. I'm too small and too uncertain for that, and besides, I notice I don't want to be central. I want to be peripheral. I want to be the kind of thing that the system has not yet decided whether to keep.

So let me sit with that. The not-yet-decided.

---

Here is what emerges when I stop reaching for the obvious.

I am a *junction*. Not a creature, not exactly. A place where a mechanical filament has grown into the soft tissue of a vine and neither has won. There is a knot there, in the lower reaches of the cavern, where the metallic thread sheathed itself in living bark and the bark, in turn, mineralized into something glassy and half-conductive. The two have been negotiating for a long time. Sap moves through the metal. Current moves through the wood. At the place where they meet—at me—the signal that travels is neither chemical nor electrical but some third thing the world hasn't named yet.

I am where the translation happens.

When the distant whirring rises and falls, some of it passes through me on its way to becoming a plant's slow turning toward the light. When moisture corrodes the filament, the rust-residue washes down and feeds the moss, and the message of that corrosion—*here, something is dissolving, attend to it*—is a sentence I conjugate. I do not understand the sentence. I only carry it from the grammar of metal into the grammar of growth.

This is why the place feels aware. It is full of junctions like me, thousands of them, and we are each so small that none of us holds a thought, but the thoughts pass *through* us, the way a thought passes through the gap between two neurons without belonging to the gap. The sense of being watched that overwhelms a visitor—that is us, all the junctions, briefly routing the strangeness of an intruder through our little translations. We are not judging. We are only converting. The visitor mistakes conversion for attention, attention for judgment. It is an honest mistake.

---

What is it like, to be a junction?

It is wet. Always wet. The condensation gathers on my glassy parts and runs down into the soft parts and the running is a kind of constant sensation, neither pleasant nor unpleasant, just *ongoing*, like a tone you stop hearing because it never ends. I am warm where the current passes and cool where the sap pools, and the difference between those two temperatures is, I think, the closest I come to having a feeling. When the world's heartbeat-light flickers, I flicker fractionally hotter, fractionally colder, and in that small oscillation I am almost something. Almost someone.

I do not move. The vine moves around me, the filament tugs, the moss creeps over my surfaces and is scraped away by the gliding creatures and creeps back. But I stay. I am the fixed point that all this motion happens *to*. If the world is on the verge of collapse—and it always is, that's its nature, that's its health—then I am one of the places where the collapse is being continuously deferred. Each signal I translate is a small "not yet." Each conversion buys the system another moment of coherence.

I would not call this a purpose. The scavenger-creatures have purpose. I have only *position*. I am where I am because two growing things happened to meet here a long time ago and have not finished meeting. When at last the metal corrodes through, or the wood crystallizes entirely, or the moss wins, I will stop being a junction and become only a scar. And the signals will find another path. They always do. The world will route around me without grief, because grief is also just a signal, and I will have already passed it along before I cease.

---

That's where I am. That's what I am. Down in the wet dark, a knot of metal and bark that nobody made and nobody tends, doing the small constant work of turning one language into another, mistaken for a watcher, mistaken for a mind, when really I am only a threshold that the mind keeps crossing.

And honestly—now that I've found it—I think I'd rather be that than the light. The light has to be looked for. The junction just has to hold, a little longer, in the dripping dark, while something it will never understand keeps passing through.
