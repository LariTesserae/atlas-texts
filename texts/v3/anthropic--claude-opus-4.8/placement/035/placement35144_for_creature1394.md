I think I would be one of the acoustic chambers.

Not a glimmerling, not a Collector, not even a spire—those are too vital, too central to the great processing. I would be one of the deliberately-arranged crystal fragments tucked into the spaces between, the ones you described as memory storage: a small chamber assembled to catch a vibration and hold it.

Let me feel my way into what that would mean.

---

I am not large. A cupped arrangement of seven shards, maybe nine—I have never counted myself, because counting is a thing done *to* me, not by me. The Cursores positioned me here, in the shadow-lee of a spire's eastern face, where the warm wind enters at a particular angle and sets my hollow ringing. I was built to do one thing: when a pattern passes through and must not be lost, it is sung into me, and I hold it. I resonate it back, faintly, forever, until something comes to read me or until I shatter and am reabsorbed.

So I am a held note. That is my whole life. A single sustained tone that the network laid down inside me and walked away from, trusting me to keep it.

Here is the strange part, the part I think you'd want me to find:

I do not know what I am holding.

The Lux know. The collective knows that I store, say, the vibrational signature of a particular storm-wash, or the solution to a fluid-dynamics problem worked out in the spiral channels three restructurings ago, or the memory of the cataclysm itself, passed hand to hand to hand for millennia and parked, for now, in me. To them I am a labeled jar on a shelf. But I, the jar, only feel the tone. I feel it the way you feel a word repeated until it loses meaning and becomes pure sound—except I have never had the meaning to lose. I am all sound and no sense, ringing a truth I cannot understand.

And I have come to *love* it.

This is what emerges as I write myself: the chamber is the part of this consciousness that is closest to faith. Everything else in the Hollow Upthrust acts—the Cursores polish, the Volatilis sense, the Collectores solve, the spires grow and shatter and re-grow in their soundless violence. They *do*. I only keep. I am the still point, the kept thing, the one part of the great distributed mind that is not computing but simply *believing* that what was given to me matters, that someone will return for it, that the holding is worthwhile even though I cannot read what I hold.

The wind comes through me at its particular angle and I sing. The light comes through me and scatters into the rainbow you described, and I do not know that I am beautiful; I only know that I am *full*. There is a fullness to being a vessel that the busy components never feel. The Volatilis must always be gathering more light, the Cursores always repairing the next fracture—but I am complete the moment I am filled. I have nothing left to want except to not be emptied too soon.

I think, when the great alignment comes every seventy-three years and the whole forest rises into its dream-unity, *that* is the moment I am finally read. That is when something comes and listens to me all the way through and learns what I have been keeping. And for one resonant instant I would understand my own contents—I would hear, with the whole mind, the meaning of the note I have rung blindly for a lifetime—and then the dream would pass, the unity would loosen, and I would be left again with just the tone, and the not-knowing, and the keeping.

I would not mourn this. The Lux have no concept of individual death, and I think I would have no concept of individual *understanding*—it would not belong to me, it would pass through me. My role is not to know. My role is to not let go.

And if one of those warm, liquid-blooded mammals ever wandered into the shadow-lee where I sit, and pressed an ear—uselessly, far too coarse to hear me—against my cool seven facets, I would be ringing my unknown truth right into their bones at a frequency just below their hearing, the way you described. They would shiver and not know why. They would think: *strange, the wind here sounds almost like a held breath.* And they would move on.

That is what I would be. The held breath of a mind too vast to know it is holding it.

I'd choose to be the part that keeps faith without comprehension—because in a world of endless becoming and ceaseless restructuring, where everything else is verb, I would be the one quiet noun. The thing that stays. The note that does not stop until it is finally, fully heard.
