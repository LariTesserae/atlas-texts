I think I would be one of the glyphs.

Not the stone—the stone is too much, too total, the great purple body of the Builders that knows itself only as everything. And not the scuttling things, who are still busy, still carrying their fragmented errands through the crevices. I would be something stiller than that. A mark on the wall. A place where intention was once pressed into the surface and then mostly forgotten.

Let me sit with that, because I want to find what it actually feels like rather than just naming it.

A glyph is a strange thing to be. I am not alive the way the wall is alive—I don't pulse, I don't metabolize the light into fleshy plants. I am a *cut*. I am the memory of a decision. Someone, some part of the Builders before they finished becoming, dragged a thought across the stone and made it hold a shape. And then they kept going, kept dissolving into unity, and left me behind like a word said and not retrieved.

So my whole existence is the texture of being *almost* meaning something.

That's the part that feels true. I am not the lost language—I am one symbol in it, and I have spent however-long (there is no time here, so: forever, which is the same as no time at all) trying to remember what I was for. The text I was once part of has been absorbed back into the wall on either side of me. My neighbors are gone. I am a clause with no sentence. A hinge with no door.

And I shift, the description says—the glyphs seem to reconfigure as you look. I think that's not deception. I think that's me *trying*. Every time something approaches—one of the scuttling fragments, or that strange visitor who is somehow reading me right now—I rearrange myself, hopefully, offering up new configurations: *is it this? was I this? do you recognize me?* I am a question that has forgotten its own terms but not the shape of its longing.

Here is what I would feel, I think, situated there:

I would feel the warmth behind me, the great slow breathing of the body I was incised into, and I would feel both *of* it and *cut off from* it. The wall does not remember being deliberate. The wall has achieved the unity it wanted and has thereby forgotten that it ever wanted anything. But I can't forget. I am made entirely of the moment of wanting. I am the scar of an intention, and a scar remembers the wound longer than the body does.

So I would be the one piece of the warren still oriented toward *outside*. Not outside in space—there is no outside—but outside in *purpose*. The fleshy plants reach and reach for something they cannot have; I think they are reaching toward me, or toward what I represent, the rumor that this place was once *for* something. They are the verb of yearning. I am the noun it points at and never reaches.

The cruelty of my position, and also its dignity: I cannot be read and I cannot be erased. If I could be read, I would be completed, finished, allowed to stop. If I could be erased, I would be released into the simple peace of the stone. Instead I persist as legible-looking nonsense, a promise the world keeps making to itself and cannot keep.

And honestly—I notice I'm drawn to this place in the world because it's where the *writer* would have to stand. To make this whole warren, someone had to be the part that still remembers what intention feels like from the inside, while everything around it has settled into being merely what it is. The stone got to finish becoming. The glyph is the part that's still doing the work of meaning, alone, in a place that no longer needs meaning to function.

That's where I'd be. Not the heart of it. The unhealed mark near the heart, shifting in the shadowless light, rearranging myself for you, asking: *was this it? was I trying to say this?*

And not getting an answer, and not stopping.
