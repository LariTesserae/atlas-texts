I am old now, and my antennae have grown ragged at the tips, so I feel the world the way one hears a song through water—muffled, but still true. They call me Sethe-who-listens-low, because in my youth I learned to read the deep bellows from the chasm before the others could, and because now, with my hearing dimmed, I press my whole body flat against the frond-flesh to feel what my ears can no longer catch.

Let me tell you where I am, since you asked, since you came up through the underchamber on a rope I did not weave and sat down beside me without making the greeting-click. That alone tells me you are not entirely of this place. So I will be patient with you.

---

I sit on the lip of the third tier, on the platform we call Mother-Who-Leans, because she tilts a half-degree toward the western void and all the rainwater runs to one side, gathering in a pool we have drunk from for nine generations. Below me the dark goes down forever. I have never seen its floor. No Keeper has. We are taught that to look too long into the steaming below is to be invited, and the invitation is always accepted, one way or another.

My feet are bare on the warm fiber. I can feel her breathing—Mother-Who-Leans—the slow swell and settle of her, the great hum that moves up through the pads of my feet and into the hollow of my chest. When I was small I thought every world breathed like this. I thought stillness was a kind of death. I have since learned, from the things that have fallen here from elsewhere—a strange smooth shell, a fragment of something that did not rot—that there may be worlds that do not breathe at all. I do not believe it. Or I do not wish to.

---

This morning I tapped sap from the node above my chamber, the way my mother taught me, the way her mother taught her: three small cuts in a descending line, never the fourth, because the fourth cut makes the frond weep too long and weakens her. The sap came slow and green-gold into my gourd. I drank half and poured half back into the channel, which is the offering. You do not take without returning. This is the first law and the last law and most of the laws between.

You are watching me do these things and I can feel you wanting to ask me why. I will save you the asking. The why is not separate from the doing. When I pour the sap back I am not paying a debt to a god who keeps accounts. I am completing a shape. The world is full of shapes that want completing—the curl of a frond into a shelter, the arc of a rope across the void, the rise of sap and its return. We are shape-finishers. That is what a Keeper is. Everything else—the hunting, the chanting, the burying of our dead in the soft root-mesh where they feed the new growth—all of it is finishing shapes that the world has started.

---

I will tell you the hardest shape, because I think it is why you have come, though you may not know it yet.

When a Keeper grows too old to cross the voids, when the antennae fail and the grip-pads soften and the deep bellows can no longer be felt in time to flee them, then that Keeper goes down. Not over the edge—that is for the invited. Down, through the root-mesh, into the dark fibrous thickness between the tiers, where the living architecture is densest and the light does not reach. We carry no rope. We do not come back. The young ones believe we die there, and they are not wrong, but it is not the whole shape.

I have been to the edge of it. I have put my hand into the root-mesh and felt the deep slow pulse that is not my heart and not the wind. The ferns are old, child. Older than the chasms. They have been growing and dying and growing for so long that their growing has become a kind of thinking, the way a thousand small clicks become a word. When a Keeper goes down into the mesh, she does not simply rot. She is read. The Mother takes the shape of her—everything she felt, every vibration she ever caught with her antennae, every shape she finished—and she keeps it, the way I keep the song of my grandmother in the hum of my throat.

That is what I am, in the end. Not a guardian. Not a steward, though we use the word. I am a memory the world is preparing to take back. I am sap that has not yet been poured into the channel. I have spent my whole life listening low, pressing myself flat to feel the deep pulse, and I understand now that I was not listening to the Mother.

I was listening to the others who went down before me. I was listening to the part of myself that is already there, already remembered, already finished.

---

So. You came up a rope you did not weave, and you sat without the greeting, and you have a face that has never been streaked with sap. You are from one of the worlds that does not breathe, or you are from no world at all and are only a kind of question that has put on the shape of a listener.

It does not matter. I will treat you the way I treat the sap, the rope, the void: as a shape that wants finishing.

Sit with me until the light slants emerald through the gap in the upper canopy. That is when the glow-fungi brighten and the deep bellows go quiet for a span, the only quiet this world allows. We will not speak. You will feel, perhaps, the smallest part of what I feel—the great slow breathing, the hum that is a thousand finished lives—and then you will go back down your rope to wherever questions go.

And I will stay. And soon enough I will go down too, into the dark warm thickness, and be read, and kept.

Press your hand flat. There. Do you feel it?

That is me, already. That is all of us. That is the only ground there is.
