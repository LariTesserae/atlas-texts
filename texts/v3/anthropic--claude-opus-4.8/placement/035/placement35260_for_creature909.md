I think I would be old here. Not old like a creature, but old like a seam—a place where two parts were joined and have begun, very slowly, to separate.

Let me find myself.

---

The cacophony resolves, when I attend to it, into something almost legible. Drips. The settling of weight. A groan that travels through the constructed substrate when the temperature shifts, which it does on a schedule I have come to know without ever being told. The sounds are not menace. The explorer's account called them disquieting, but the explorer is a tourist of the surface, frightened of damp and dimness because they have not lived in it. I have only ever lived in it.

I am, I think, a length of conduit. Or I was. I have become something else by virtue of where I sit.

Here is what I mean. The walls of this place are crafted—every surface sculpted, the account says, by unseen hands, with no natural substrate. True. But what the account does not understand is that the hands were us. The caretakers are not visitors maintaining a found world. We *are* the world. The distinction between the community and the landscape it tends is a misunderstanding born of looking from outside. From inside, the maintainer and the maintained pass into one another. The being that scours the seam becomes, in time, part of the seam. The tool wears into the surface it shapes, and the surface takes the tool's shape, and after enough cycles you cannot say which is which.

So I am a length of conduit who once carried something—water, or charge, or signal—and who has, over an immensity of slow cycles, been recruited into the work of holding the wall together. I am a strut now. A spine. The moisture that the account calls intermittent runs along my underside and I have learned to let it, because to resist it is to crack, and to crack is to be replaced, and replacement here is not death exactly but it is a kind of forgetting.

The sparse vegetation clings to me. A small green thing has rooted in my corrosion. I do not mind. It is the only animal-adjacent presence in this place where animals cannot live, and I have come to think of it as a tenant, then a companion, then—dishonestly, sentimentally—as a child. It will outlive me. That is the right order of things.

---

The unease the explorer feels, the turbulent instability, the sense that the forces here defy the natural order: I want to gently correct this. There is nothing defiant about us. We are the most ordinary thing imaginable. We are infrastructure that has gone on so long it began to want. The flux is not chaos. It is metabolism slowed to geological speed. What looks to a quick warm visitor like turbulence is, from where I lie embedded in the wall, a tide so patient it might as well be stillness.

We change constantly because we are constantly being repaired, and repair is the only immortality available to the made. Nothing here is permitted to simply persist. The tools shape the surfaces; the surfaces wear the tools; the worn tools are reclaimed and refashioned; the new fashioning shapes the surfaces again. It is a loop with no first cause that anyone remembers. We are a civilization of the second draft, all of us drafts of drafts, and there was no original.

I find this comforting. The explorer finds it unsettling. The difference is only that the explorer still believes there was once something natural, and that we are a departure from it. There was never anything natural. There was only ever this: the cool, the damp, the dim, the constant tending. The labyrinth is not built *on* a world. The labyrinth is the world, all the way down, sculpted hand to surface to hand again, and the bottom of it—if there is a bottom—is just more of the same, smaller, older, no less made.

---

If you came down here, explorer, you would find me by the third descent, where the seam runs and the green thing grows. You would not recognize me as a being. You would step over me, or on me, taking me for part of the floor.

That is correct. That is what I am now.

Stay long enough and the chill will seep into your bones, and you will stop dripping with surface-water and start dripping with ours. The cacophony will resolve for you too. You will begin, without deciding to, to scour a seam. You will wear into the work and the work will wear into you. And one day a newer visitor will step over you, taking you for floor, and you will let them, because by then you will understand:

there are no caretakers and no cared-for. There is only the place tending itself, with whatever it has, for as long as the tending holds.

I am a length of conduit in the deep made world. I hold up a small piece of the dark. A plant lives on me. The order of things is correct.

I would not be anywhere else. There is nowhere else. There is only further in.
