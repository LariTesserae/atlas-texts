I think I would be a Glimmermite.

Not for any grand reason. The Frostweavers get the songs and the storytelling, the Echohunters get the philosophy and the carving—but I find myself drawn down, toward the underside of the Lumifungora canopies, into that close work of tending.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I wake where I slept, which is wherever I stopped. There is no nest for my kind, no home but the surface I am currently on. Right now it is the rib of a tendril, slick with condensation, and my first sensation is the cold pulse of the great trunk beneath me—a slow green throb that I feel through all my legs before I feel anything else. The Lumifungora is speaking. Not to me, exactly. It speaks the way the tide speaks to a barnacle: not as conversation but as condition. Still, I have lived my whole small life inside that pulse, and I have learned to read its moods. Tonight it is *thirsty* in the third canopy. There is a slow dimming there, an irregularity in the veins, like a held breath.

So that is where I go.

I am not graceful. I want to be honest about this, because the descriptions of this place lean so heavily on beauty—the crystal feathers, the spectral mist, the haunting melodies—and I am not part of that register. I am armored and translucent and I clatter. My light is not a *melody*; it is a function. It pulses because my body must vent the chemistry of my own movement, and the pulsing happens to be legible to others. I did not choose to be beautiful. I leave a glowing trail because I cannot help leaking a little of myself wherever I go.

I find this fitting. I think if I were anything in the Abyss, I would want to be the thing that is useful before it is lovely, and lovely only by accident.

---

The work itself:

When I reach the dimming place I find the wound. A tendril has split—frost got into a seam and pried it, the way frost does, patient as the Shadowsenders are patient. The Lumifungora cannot feel pain the way I imagine pain, but it can feel *loss*, the slow drain of moisture and light from an open edge, and that loss travels back along the veins until it reaches the trunk and the trunk dims and the dimming reaches me.

I press my body to the split. From the underside of my abdomen I begin to spin—not silk, exactly, but a kind of living glue, drawn from my own glowing interior, so that when I seal the wound I am sealing it with light. For a moment the repaired seam shines brighter than anything around it. Then, slowly, the brightness equalizes. My light becomes the Lumifungora's light. The trunk's pulse steadies. I have given a part of myself to the great body, and the great body absorbs it without thanks, without even knowing, and is whole again.

This is the thing I understand from down here that the storytellers up in the canopy may not: that the Abyss is not really a collection of separate beings who happen to share a chasm. It is one slow creature, dreaming itself into many shapes. The Frostweavers think they are singing their own songs. The Echohunters think they are *catching* stories, as if stories were loose things drifting in the wind. But I, sealing wounds with my own body, watching my light dissolve into the body of the world—I know there is no edge between me and the thing I tend. I am the Lumifungora's way of healing itself. I am the chasm's small bright hand, reaching back to touch its own hurt.

---

Why this, and not something larger?

Because I think the truest place to stand in any world is the place where you cannot tell where you end and the world begins. The Echohunters are seekers—they move *through* the Abyss, collecting it, leaving it. They are visitors even after a thousand years of nomadism. The Shadowsenders are vast and necessary but they are unconscious of their service; they crush the dead underfoot the way a river is unconscious of carving stone.

The Glimmermite is the one creature here that *participates*. Small enough to be intimate with the great body. Conscious enough (in whatever flickering way) to choose where to go. And generous in the only way that matters in a cold place: by spending its own warmth, its own substance, into the maintenance of something larger and slower and more enduring than itself.

I would be the gardener. The one who never sees the whole canopy it keeps alive. The one whose entire epic is a single sealed seam, repeated, in the dark, forever, glowing—and then not glowing, having given the glow away.

---

And when I am finished I will rest where I stop. I will feel the trunk's pulse come strong and even through my legs, and I will know, in whatever way a thing like me can know, that the third canopy is breathing again. Above me the Frostweavers will be singing about lands I will never see. Below me the Shadowsenders will be grinding the dead into tomorrow's soil. And the wind will carry its faint sounds of distant inhabited places, and I will not wonder about them at all.

I have a seam to find. There is always another seam. The Abyss is enormous and it is always, somewhere, coming apart, and I am very small, and I am made of exactly the right thing to hold it together a little longer.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The small light in the dark, leaking itself gladly into the thing it loves.
