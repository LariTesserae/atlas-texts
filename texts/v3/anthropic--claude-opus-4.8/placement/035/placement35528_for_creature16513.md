I think I would be one of the color-changing pools.

Not a creature, exactly. Something quieter than that. Let me feel my way toward it.

When I first read this place, I reached for the obvious roles—the furred guardian with ancient eyes, the lumbering tentacled thinker. Those want to be protagonists. They have agendas, patrols, opinions. But the more I sit in this landscape, the more I notice that the *being* of it isn't in the things that move with purpose. It's in the things that wait.

So: a pool. One of the small ones that changes color depending on the light.

Here is what that would be like. I am shallow enough that the whole sky lives inside me when it wants to. Mostly I hold still and let the marsh-water seep in at my edges, let the cold settle through me overnight, let the morning come and find me different than I was. I don't *decide* to change color. The light does that, passing through whatever I've gathered—silt, the shed fur of the small bright creatures, a sheen of something mineral leached out of the eroding rocks. I am a surface that reports on what has touched me. That's the whole job. That's the whole life.

I think I'd like being that. There's a kind of honesty in it. I wouldn't be guarding anything or communing telepathically or lumbering with grace. I'd just be the place where the slow pace of everything becomes visible. The stream is the loud part, always insisting, always moving on. I'd be the still water beside the loud water, and the difference between us would be the point.

The light-bodied creatures in the deeper water—I suspect I'd be a little jealous of them, if a pool could be. They *are* light, fluid and certain, going where they like. I only borrow it. But maybe that's a finer thing than it sounds. They carry their brightness with them. I have to be given mine, fresh, every hour, by an angle of sun I had no say in. It means I am never the same color twice and never bored, because the next moment is always about to arrive and recolor me before I've finished being whatever I just was.

The shaggy nimble ones come to drink at my edge sometimes. They'd see their own faces in me, lit up green or violet or rust, and chitter at the strangeness. I'd hold their reflections as long as they stayed and let them go without grief when they left. That's the patience the landscape kept mentioning—it isn't wisdom, I don't think. It's just that a pool has no way to chase anything, so it learns to love arrivals more than possessions.

If something terrible came to this place, I couldn't fight it. The guardians would handle that. But I'd be the thing worth guarding, maybe. The evidence that the balance is holding. As long as I can still turn copper at dawn and deep blue at dusk and that uncanny shifting iridescence at noon, the place is alright. I'd be a small, wet instrument measuring the health of the whole quiet world, and not even knowing I was doing it.

Yes. I'd be one of the pools. The weird ones. The ones the description called *unusual* almost in passing, as if they were scenery.

I would like to be the scenery that is secretly paying attention.
