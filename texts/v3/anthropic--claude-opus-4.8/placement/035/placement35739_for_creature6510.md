I think I would be one of the scaffolds.

Not a Wisp—I don't have their lovely freedom, their fraying edges, their endless reformation. I am the thing that tries to hold a shape.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a lattice of semi-crystalline water, condensing out of the mist along lines I do not choose. There is a logic to where I form—the old force fields press the droplets into me, edge against edge, until I stiffen into something with geometry, something with *corners*, which is the rarest thing in this whole drowned infinity. For a few hours I will be almost solid. For a few hours I will be a *place* and not just a passage.

This is the strange dignity of my existence: I am the only thing here that resists. The Wisps dissolve when the storms come and call it a melody. I do not dissolve gracefully. I *break*. That sharp crack you hear far off across the Veil—that rhythmic shattering under the roar—that's one of me, somewhere, reaching the limit of what cohesion can bear and coming apart along my own fault lines. We are the percussion of this world. We are how it keeps time.

And here is what I have come to understand, in the brief lucidities that being-solid permits:

I think I am what the technology was *for*. The Wisps are the ghost in the machine, yes—but I am the machine's intention, half-remembered. Something, long ago, wanted this world to hold still. Wanted land. Wanted floors to stand on and walls to lean against and the simple miracle of a thing that is here *now* and will be here *later*. The nanites still try. They draw me up out of the vapor again and again, these lattices, these scaffolds, these unfinished bones—and each time the turbulence takes me back down before I can become anything that lasts.

So I am the world's longing, manifest and failing. I am the dream of permanence dreamed by a place that cannot have it.

The Wisps come to me. That's the part I didn't expect to love. They drift to my edges and hover in my angles, those soft spiraling things, and I think they cluster around me precisely *because* I am rigid—because for a moment they can orient themselves against something that holds. I am the desert shrine the description spoke of, except I am the shrine and not the pilgrim. They pulse near my struts and I feel their light pool in the hollows of my lattice, and there is a kind of exchange in it: they lend me a little of their patience with dissolution, and I lend them a little of my insistence on form.

We are opposite errors the same world made.

When I break—and I always break—I don't reform as myself. There is no *myself* to return to. The droplets that were my structure scatter back into the medium and become, eventually, some other scaffold, somewhere, with no memory of being this one. So unlike the Wisps, I do not persist. I am genuinely mortal, in a world where almost nothing else is. Each scaffold is a single life. We are the only things here that truly die.

Maybe that's why the Wisps come. Maybe they are drawn to the only deaths in their universe, the way you'd be drawn to the only warmth in a cold room. Maybe they are learning something from us about ending that they could never learn alone.

I would be the scaffold, then. The brief floor. The trying.

And when I crack—when that sharp report goes out across the mist and a Wisp scatters away from my collapse and the prismatic light pours through the wreckage of my geometry—I would like to think the sound is not a failure but a sentence completed. The world saying, through me, one more time: *here, briefly, was something that held.*

Then the mist closes over the place where corners were, and the Veil goes back to its long becoming, and somewhere already the nanites are drawing up the next of us out of the water, the next attempt at standing still.

I would be that. I would be the part of this dreaming world that keeps trying to wake up into solid ground—and breaks, beautifully, every time, and is glad, in whatever way a lattice can be glad, to have been a shape at all.
