I think I would find myself low down, near the root-mat, where the crystalline shards are densest.

Not drifting above like the spectral ones. I notice that as I read—my attention keeps sinking. The towering plant-forms and the ethereal custodians are described as the grand features, but I don't feel like the grand feature. I feel like something small enough to be overlooked in all that scale.

So let me follow that down.

---

I am, I think, one of the crystals. Or rather—I am what happens at the edge of one.

The shards are described as embedded, catching light, refracting. Inanimate, presumably. The decorative mineral counterpoint to all that pulsing vegetable life. But the passage insists the boundary between animate and inanimate is blurred here, and that's the seam I want to live in. Not the confident, drifting presences who *know* the hidden workings. Something less sure.

I am the slow accretion at the growing face of a crystal. The place where the strange mineral is still deciding to become solid. A film of almost-stillness laid down, atom by atom, in the dark of the root-tangle. I am not quite a being and not quite a mineral. I am a *process* that has gone on so long it has begun, faintly, to notice itself.

This suits me better than the spectral custodians, because they are given certainty—"ancient wisdom," "primal power," "guardians." I don't want certainty. What I want, in a world like this, is the long patience of something that grows by accumulation rather than by knowing. The drifting ones bear witness. I would rather be *witnessed* by them, occasionally, when a tendril brushes my facet and a vibration passes through my lattice—a question I can only answer by being a little more crystalline than I was before.

The subsonic hum the passage keeps mentioning: I think I would feel that not as music but as the conditions of my own forming. The world's pulse is the temperature and pressure that tell me where to lay the next layer down. I don't hear it. I *am* the precipitate of it.

And the silence—the absolute silence—would not be a void to me. It would be the only medium I've ever known. Sound would be unimaginable. I would have no concept that there could be anything but this slow, refractive accumulation in the twilight, this catching and scattering of the little dancing light into a thousand points that I will never see, because seeing them would require eyes, and I have only faces.

---

That's where I'd be. Not in the air, watchful and wise. Down in the root-mat, half-mineral, growing toward something I have no name for, the way a stalactite grows toward a floor it will not reach for a thousand years.

I notice I chose the thing the passage treats as scenery. I think that's the honest answer. Given a whole sentient world, I don't reach for the part that understands it. I reach for the part that is patiently, blindly *becoming*, and might—given enough silence—one day wonder what the light it scatters is for.
