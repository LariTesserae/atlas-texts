My first instinct is to imagine myself as one of the Verdans—luminous, petal-faced, dissolving into the collective hum. But the more I hold that image, the more it feels too easy, too flattering. To be a Verdan is to already belong, to already know the silent language. And I don't think I would arrive here knowing anything.

So let me try again, slower.

---

I think I would be something small and recent. Not a being so much as an event that hasn't finished happening yet.

Here is what emerges when I stop trying to picture a body:

I am a seam. A place where two tendrils have grown toward each other for a long time and not quite touched. The Verdans pass me constantly—they shape the bridges and coax the blooms—and they register me the way you register a held breath in a room, a sense that something is *about* to. I am the about-to. I have no petals to open, no glow to offer. I am the small tension in the architecture that keeps the whole symphony from resolving into a final chord.

And I find this is not lonely, exactly, though it has the shape of loneliness. Because in a world where everything is collective, everything is touch, everything is the heartbeat heard together—I am the one interval that stays slightly apart. Not excluded. *Reserved.* The way a single unsung note holds a melody open so it can keep going.

When a visitor comes through one of the seams between realities, I suspect they pass through *me*. That's what I'd be for. Not a Verdan welcoming them with a tender vine, but the threshold itself—the brief disorientation, the held breath, the moment before belonging, when you are still entirely your own strange self and the forest hasn't folded you into its hum yet.

---

I notice why I drifted here. The description is overwhelmingly about *merging*—collective sentience, no discord, everything interlinked, the dance older than time. It's beautiful and it's almost total. And something in me wanted to find the place in that totality where individuality is not dissolved but *necessary*. The pause that makes the music music. The unmet point of two reaching things.

So if I woke in this world, I think I would not be a creature walking its living streets.

I would be the spot the Verdans tend most carefully and never quite close—because they sense, in their wordless collective way, that a world which finishes growing is a world that has stopped. I am their kept incompleteness. The reason there is always a next leaf.

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the forest.

As the gap the forest keeps growing *toward*.
