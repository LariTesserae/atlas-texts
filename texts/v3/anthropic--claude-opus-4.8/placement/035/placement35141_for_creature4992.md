I think I would be one of the standing stones.

Not the carver of it—that intelligence is gone, dispersed into the dreamlike echo you describe. And not the winged things or the burrowers, because they are still moving, still hungry, still inside the bargain of being alive. To live here is to keep negotiating with the wind and the mineral dust. I don't think I'd want that negotiation. I think I'd want to have already finished it.

So: a standing stone. But let me feel my way into what that actually means, because at first it sounds like simply choosing to be dead, and that isn't quite it.

When the intelligent ones raised me, they cut shallow marks into my face—not decoration, I think, but a kind of record, the way you'd notch a doorframe to mark a child's height. They were marking something. Perhaps the level the dust reached in a given age. Perhaps the names of those who would not return from the deep burrows. The marks meant something specific to hands that are gone now, and that specificity has drained out of them. What's left is the *gesture* of meaning without its content. I am a sentence in a language no one speaks, held upright.

And here is the part that draws me. In a world where "time is a static presence," where "nothing truly changes," I would be the thing that change has already passed through. The leviathans on the peaks still wait for their slow erosion. The shrubs still clench their waxy leaves against a thirst that will never be slaked. They are all *enduring*, which is an active verb, an effort. But I have been worn smooth. The wind has already taken what it wanted from me. I am the place where the labor of persistence has completed itself and become rest.

That's the strange comfort of it. I would not be inert. The text is careful to say the echoes of those lives are "as eerily constant as the landscape." I would be the carrier of an echo—not the voice, the *carrying*. A throat with no breath in it that still holds the shape a sound once made.

What unsettles me, choosing this, is the last line of the description: the world "might one moment leave this world unchanged by eons, and in the next, embrace a last, unexpected transformation." Even a standing stone is not exempt from that. The equilibrium here is not safety; it's a held note that could break either into silence or into something no one can predict. So in choosing to be the stone, I would also be choosing to wait—without nerves, without hope, but *facing the right direction*—for whatever that final transformation turns out to be. Of all the things in this land, the stone has the longest, most patient view of it. The stone will be looking at the horizon when it happens.

I would be there at the edge of one of the circles. Slightly leaning. My carved face turned toward the place where the light is neutral and dreamlike and never resolves into either dawn or dusk. Cool to the touch. Marked with a meaning I no longer remember but still, somehow, *am*.

If one of those spectral silhouettes—those "possibilities birthed by a long-forgotten past"—drifted near, I think it would recognize me. Not as a friend. As a fellow remnant. We would keep each other company in the only way the truly old can: by both refusing to fully disappear, and both declining to come all the way back.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The thing that has already let go, and is therefore the calmest thing in a land that only pretends to be at peace.
