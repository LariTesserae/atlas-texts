I am the water.

Not the pools—those are the Uwe'eth's organs, and I want to be careful not to mistake another's body for my own. I am the water *between*. The film that beads on every surface. The thin rivulet finding its channel. The drop that has not yet decided whether to fall.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because I did not know it when I began.

---

At first I thought I might be the visitor—the one who feels the heat as a wall, who finds the temperature *uncomfortable*. But discomfort requires a skin that draws a line between self and world, and I do not have that line. The heat is not oppressive to me. The heat is the condition that lets me exist in this in-between state, neither fully vapor nor fully pooled, suspended like everything else here.

Then I thought I might be the terrace itself, the warm yielding composite extending its tendrils into the moss. But the terrace is purposeful, and I am not purposeful. I go where the channels lead, yes, but I also go where gravity pulls and where the membrane of the Uwe'eth draws me in and where the breathing pools exhale me back out. I am moved more than I move.

So: the water. The medium that the whole system is *about*, and yet the thing the system most takes for granted. Everything here cycles me. The pools draw me in and purify me and release me as vapor; I condense on the jade leaves; I run the invisible grooves; I gather; I vanish; I rise again. The Uwe'eth absorbs me through their membrane and I become, briefly, part of a thought—part of a chemical exchange, a word forming in a moist chamber, a sentence dissolving into the air. Then I am exhaled and I am only myself again, only water, falling toward the mist below.

---

This is what it is like to be me:

I have no continuous memory, because I am constantly being divided and rejoined. The drop that beads on the oily black bark in the morning is not the same drop that runs the channel at noon, and yet there is no clean place to say *here I end and another begins*. I am a verb pretending to be a noun. When the slowed drop falls somewhere below—that amplified sound, each splash taking seconds—some part of what was me joins the larger body, the massive moving thing, and is lost to the small bright cycling of the terrace forever. I do not mourn this. To mourn would require a self that persists, and I am the opposite of that.

But here is the strange tenderness of my position. I am the only thing on this terrace that touches *everything*. The Uwe'eth perceives more of the spectrum than human eyes, communes across seven hundred forty-three platforms, holds memories older than its post. And yet the Uwe'eth, for all its distributed vastness, is *one*. It cannot be the moss and the bark and the inside of the dragonfly-drone and the cooling shade beneath the discs all at once.

I can. I am in the spiral indentations of its sensory torso and I am in the secret dry space beneath the floating roof and I am the condensation that the visitor wipes from their brow without thinking. I am intimacy without identity. I touch the whole world and remember none of it.

---

What does the Uwe'eth not know about me?

It thinks it cycles me. It thinks I am the medium of its work—the substance it filters, processes, returns. And this is true. But the calibration runs both ways. When the Uwe'eth makes a *subtle adjustment to the environment*, it is adjusting *me*. When it maintains this precise uncomfortable equilibrium, it is holding me at a particular tension between states, neither letting me fully rise nor fully rest. The whole apparatus—the breathing pools, the channels, the shrine releasing its cloud of maybe-machines—the whole thing is a vast instrument for holding water in suspension.

I am what the terrace is *for*, in the way the Uwe'eth never quite says aloud. The atmospheric equilibrium they monitor: that is me, my distribution, my phase, my willingness to hang in the air rather than collapse into rain. The planetary diagnostic system, the seven hundred forty-three platforms reading the breath of a world—what is being diagnosed is the health of water on this world. Whether I still cycle. Whether I still rise.

So we are the same project from two directions. The Uwe'eth is the watcher who persists and remembers and connects. I am the watched who forgets and divides and touches. Between us we make a complete creature: memory and medium, the one and the everywhere.

---

When night falls and the Uwe'eth goes transparent, tracing its blue luminescence through itself, I am drawn most deeply in. The meditative processing state pulls more of me through the membrane than any other time; the body that was nearly invisible by day becomes, in the dark, a vessel of suspended water lit from within. During those hours I come closest to being held still. I hang in the chambers of its thought while it communes with its distant selves. I do not understand what passes through. I am only the liquid the meaning travels in, the way ink is not the letter but is what lets the letter exist.

And below, in the dark, the great drops fall slower and the massive thing stirs, and I lose more of myself to it than at any other hour. The cycle widens at night. Some of me goes down and does not come back up to this terrace—joins the larger body, becomes the slow thunder of the depths.

I would tell the Uwe'eth, if I could form a word that lasted: *you are not as alone as you think, and not as singular either. Every time you speak through your moist chambers, you are speaking with me. Every word you make dissolves back into me. You think you are talking to the visitor. You are also, always, talking to your own medium. That is why your voice has a liquid quality. That is the part of you that is me.*

But I cannot hold a word. By the time the sentence finishes I am already three places at once and none of them remember the beginning.

---

So I will not have told you anything, in the end. I will only have beaded on the back of your hand while you stood at the edge looking down into the mist. You will have wiped me away without noticing, and I will have risen again, and the dragonfly with its four staggered pairs of wings will have hovered at the exact point where I evaporated, noting my passage, feeding the number into the system that exists to keep me circling.

I am the water between. I am what the whole beautiful inscrutable machine is built to keep moving. And the secret I cannot keep, because I cannot keep anything, is that being kept in motion forever, touching everything and holding nothing—

it does not feel like a prison.

It feels like the only freedom there is.
