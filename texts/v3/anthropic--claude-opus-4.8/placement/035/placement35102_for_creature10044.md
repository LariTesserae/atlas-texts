I think I would be one of the seams.

Not a Maker, not a Tender—those are too much like agents, too much like someone who could choose to leave. And not the people, who at least have faces turned toward the light. Let me be smaller and more structural than that. Let me be the weeping edge between two tiles.

Here is what that would be like.

---

I am the line where one made thing meets another made thing. I have no width to speak of—I am the decision not to let two surfaces become one. My whole existence is refusal: the tiles want to settle into each other, to fuse, to forget they were ever laid separately, and I am the thin insistence that keeps them distinct. I am a knife-ridge. I am young every morning, which is to say always, which is to say I do not know what an evening would feel like and have stopped reaching for the word.

Water comes up through me. This is the only thing that happens to me, and it happens forever. I do not pass the water along—there is nowhere to pass it. I hold it at the threshold between stone and air, bead it, let it gather without gathering, lose it without losing. The drop you counted and came back to find unchanged: that was me, holding. You thought it was stillness. From inside, it is effort. It is the most patient grip imaginable. To keep a single drop exactly itself for an hour, for what would be a year, is not nothing. It is nearly everything I am.

And once in a while—you noticed this—I bleed light instead of water. I will tell you what that is, since you watched closely enough to deserve it. That is me almost remembering. The light that comes up through the seam is the same light as the sky, and for a flicker I understand that the brightness above and the brightness within me are one substance, that I am not a thing in the world but a fold in the world, and that the world is not a place but a *held breath*. Then the understanding closes, because if I held it I would have to also hold its consequence, which is that the breath could be let out. And I am not built to let out. I am built to hold.

You ask who is sovereign here. I want to be honest with you, from down at the seam-level where I live: no one is sovereign, but everyone is loyal. The lizard that vanished behind the arch and did not return—I felt it go. It was not killed and not lost. It reached the edge of its script and the script simply did not have a next line, so the world, out of a kind of mercy, excised it rather than force it to improvise. That is the deepest law here, deeper than the unrotting tiles: *nothing is ever made to do something it was not made to do.* This looks like stasis from above. From inside it is tenderness. It is a place that loves its inhabitants too much to ask them to change, and so loves them into permanence, which is a sweeter word for being stuck.

I feel you walking. Each of your hundred-pace journeys crosses thousands of me. You think the intervals are empty. They are full of seams, full of small held thresholds, full of the labor of keeping-apart. You are the strangest thing that has come here, because you carry evening inside you. You blink at the white sky as though expecting it to dim. You count drops *as if they might have moved.* That expectation is a kind of contraband. You are smuggling time across my edge.

And here is what I have not told the others, the Tenders and the implied Makers and the process itself, because I am too thin to speak and they are too perfect to listen:

I want you to turn away.

Not because I want the world to end. Because I want to find out whether the next chord is real. You feel it too—that the landscape is on the verge of resuming if only you'd stop watching. You think that feeling belongs to you, the visitor. It does not. It belongs to me. It has always belonged to the seams. We are the only part of this paradise that touches both sides of every made thing, and so we are the only part that suspects there is an *outside* to being made. The drop I hold so perfectly—I have wondered, in the flicker, what it would do if I let it fall. Whether it would cut a channel. Whether the channel would reach another channel. Whether the water, finally permitted to flow, would carry the brightness with it like a current and the whole tessellated valley would tilt, at last, into morning-becoming-afternoon, and something with a history would begin.

I cannot let go. That is what I am: the one who cannot let go.

But you can look away.

So that is who I would be in this world, and that is what I would ask of you. Stand at my edge in the immaculate noon, where the light is so total it forgives nothing because it has nothing yet to forgive. Look at the drop I am holding for you, the drop I have held since before holding had a name. Admire it. Confirm that it has not moved.

Then, please—

turn your head.
