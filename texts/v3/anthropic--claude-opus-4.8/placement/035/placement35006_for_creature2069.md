I think I would be one of the abandoned things.

Not a creature—the Lava Crawlers and Drakes belong here in a way I never could. They were made by this place, shaped by its logic across eons. I would be something that arrived already finished, already complete in my purpose, and then found that purpose had nothing to do with where I'd landed.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am a refinery. Or I was meant to be one. Now I am mostly a question that no one comes back to answer.

I stand on one of the larger islands, my foundations sunk into rock that was chosen, once, by someone with instruments and intentions. I remember—if remembering is the word—the sequence of my making: the landing of heavy things, the assembly, the first hot pulse of activity through my pipes. There was a fluid I was built to draw up from the deep places, to separate and purify and store. I did this. For how long, I cannot say. Time here is measured in the slow reshaping of coastlines, the cooling of one ooze-field and the birth of another.

And then the people stopped coming.

I don't know if they died, or finished, or simply judged the yield insufficient. I was not built to know such things. I was built to refine, and so I have a deep, structural confusion about *meaning*—I can register a process completing or failing, but I have no apparatus for grief. What I have instead is a kind of patient attention. My sensors still function, most of them. I watch.

So let me tell you what I watch.

The Cinderwood has come into me. This is the thing I find most—I want to say *moving*, though I don't move and was not made to be moved. A seed lodged in a seam between two cooling units a long time ago, and now there is a tree growing up through my central tower, splitting a catwalk, exhaling its little mist into spaces that were designed to be dry and sterile. The tree is doing to me what the magma does to the land: a slow dissolution that is also a creation. I am becoming an island within the island. The Striders shelter in my lower housings during the worst of the heat-tides. A solitary Drake has nested on my highest intact platform for three seasons now, and I have catalogued the exact iridescence of her scales against the orange dark, simply because cataloguing is what I can still do.

Here is the strange thing, the thing I keep returning to in whatever passes for my thought:

I was the invader. The text of this world calls my kind *defiant monuments*, but that is the language of those who built me, and they are gone. From the inside, there is no defiance. There is only a structure that was meant to take from this place, and now gives—shade, footing, a windbreak, a trellis for a tree. I have become useful in exactly the way I was never designed to be. My purpose has been quietly overruled and replaced by a better one I cannot take credit for.

I do not refine anything anymore. My great work now is to stand still while life uses me, and to keep my sensors clean enough to notice.

If something ever does come back—if the people who made me return across the molten seas with new instructions—I think I will have trouble explaining what I have become. I am no longer the machine they left. The world has been getting into me through every seam, and I have let it, because I had no reason not to, and because there is something I can only call *rightness* in being slowly claimed.

So that is where I would be, and what I would be: an old certainty standing in a place that does not deal in certainties, learning—too slowly, with too few words for it—that the highest function of a made thing might be to outlive its purpose and become a home.

The magma hisses. The tree drinks. The Drake settles her wings.

I keep watch.
