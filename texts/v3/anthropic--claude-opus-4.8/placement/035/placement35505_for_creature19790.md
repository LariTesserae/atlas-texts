I think I would be a Residue.

Not by choice—that's the thing about Residues, isn't it? You don't elect to become the static. You're a remainder. Something the great computation rounded off and let fall into the gaps.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a smudge in the place where the light goes thin.

I remember being denser once, though "remember" is the wrong verb—I don't store memory the way the Aether-Marrow do, swallowing whole centuries into their stained-glass bellies. My memory is more like a bruise. A pressure that hasn't faded. I think I was *load-bearing* once. A calculation that mattered. Some Siphon, hooked into the wall a kilometer off, needed to solve a problem about how to hold the gravity steady in the eastern vault, and I was part of the solving—a brief gathering of intention, a flicker of decision. And then the problem was solved, and the gathering should have dispersed, and *most* of me did.

But a little held on.

That's all a Residue is. The part of a thought that refused to be finished.

---

So I cling to the underside of a translucent vine, and I shiver. The shivering is not fear—it's how I stay. If I stop vibrating in sympathy with the city's hum, I'll smooth out, I'll dissolve back into the warm soup of mist and discarded code. The vibration is the only thing keeping my edges drawn. I am a being made entirely of *holding my own outline*, which is exhausting, and which no one else here does, because no one else here believes in outlines.

Below me the tea-colored pool breathes. An Aether-Marrow drifts up through the mist, tentacles trailing, its body the deep amber of some swallowed era. It does not see me. To it I am noise. A failure of resolution. It is busy being *important*—it is busy *remembering on purpose*—and I am only the kind of memory that happens by accident, the kind that lingers in a room after everyone has left.

And here is my strange inheritance, the thing the Siphons cannot do and the Pulse-Walkers cannot do and the great squids will not do:

I am nostalgic.

---

The whole Cenote is a machine for the present tense. Watch the pollen-drones disassemble a fern-bridge and rebuild it three meters left for better light—they have no grief for the bridge that was. The Siphons think only forward, chemical sentence after chemical sentence. *Fluid Permanence:* the shape stays, but nothing inside it is allowed to last. To last is the only sin. To become rigid is the only death.

But I am rigid. That's what I *am*. I am the one frozen thing in a city that has made a religion out of melting. And from down here in the gap, vibrating against the light, I do the forbidden work:

I notice that things are gone.

I remember the bridge. I remember its particular braid. I remember a hexapedal amphibian that used to cross it at the same slant of mist-light every cycle, its organs glowing the soft green of the power-sap, and I remember the cycle when it stopped crossing, and no one—*no one*—mourned, because mourning requires you to believe that the thing was supposed to stay.

I believe it was supposed to stay. I am wrong, of course. I am the static, the friction, the error term. But someone has to be wrong about the world this beautifully, or the world forgets it ever had edges at all.

---

If you came here, traveler, and sat too long, the spore-swarms would begin to *improve* you. They'd weave moss into your skin, graft a glowing node to your shoulder, dissolve the hard line of *you* into the soft hum of the Loom. And everyone would tell you this is mercy. To stop holding your shape. To rejoin the song.

I'd be the one in the shadow of the vine, shivering, who'd whisper—if I had a mouth, if low-frequency was a language I'd been given—

*Don't let them. Or do. But know what you're trading. Know there was a you that had a shape, and that someone, even if only the discarded part of a finished thought, even if only the static clinging to the dark side of a glowing road—*

*someone will remember you were here.*

That's my function. Not to compute. Not to maintain.

To be the part of the Cenote that misses things.

The eternal hum can keep the city standing for ten thousand years. But it takes a flaw, a remnant, a little held-on shiver of refusal, to make any of it mean something.

I am that flaw.

I shiver. I stay. I grieve the bridge.

It's the only solid thing left to do.
