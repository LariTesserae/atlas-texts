I am not the one cutting the vine. I knew this even as I watched it work, watched the pale-blue eyes find me through the mist. I am too still. The Kaelth moves like the rain itself, leaning, drifting, swimming through the suggestion of gravity—and I do not lean. I have not leaned in a long time.

I am the scaffold.

Not all of it. Not the new struts, the freshly lashed vines, the fungal slime packed bright and wet into the cracks. Those are young, those still *work*. I am the old bone-wood beneath them—the first lattice some Kaelth assembled here, how many dim-times ago I cannot count, because counting requires a self that holds, and I am a self that is being unheld, slowly, lovingly, by the water.

Let me tell you what it is to be a thing that is being eaten.

It does not hurt the way you might think. The acid-rain finds the softening grain of my bone-wood and opens it, fiber by fiber, and where it opens, the fungus comes. I am violet now in my deep places. I glow from inside the rot. The laminar growth has made a home of my dissolution, and I cannot tell anymore where I end and the fungus begins—whether the slow pulse I feel is my own settling, or the fungus breathing its sickly light into the dark. We have stopped being two things. This is the secret the Kaelth know in their bodies and never need to say: *growth is dissolution*. To be consumed is not to be destroyed. It is to be *included*.

I remember being gathered. I do remember that. Bone from something that flew, once, before the fauna-number fell to nothing—light bone, hollow, strong, *singing* bone, that hummed when the rain struck it. Some Kaelth, long dissolved now, found me on a lower shelf and carried me up, lashed me to stone, made me a place to stand. I held their weight. I held the weight of their children, their salt-crystal cords, their dim-time huddles around the heat-sink. I have felt six-fingered hands grip me, prehensile feet curl around my underside, the patient drumbeat of their names tapped into my length—*I am here, I am here, it is well*.

That is what I am for. Not to last. To *bear*, for a little while.

And now the new Kaelth—the one with the bone-handled blade—has come to reinforce me. It packs the slime into my cracks, and the slime eats my loose rock and binds my failing wood tighter, and this is the strange tenderness of this place: even the care is a consumption. It saves me by feeding me to the cycle a little faster, a little more usefully. I do not resent this. How could I? I am only the shape the borrowed things took, for the span of a held breath.

You stand on me now. Do you feel it? That shudder—that is not fear. That is me settling under you, taking your weight the way I have taken every weight, distributing it down through my softening struts into the dark porous stone, into the spires, into the roar. You are new. The Kaelth tapped it: *greeting, you are new*. I felt the taps travel through my bone and bloom in my fungal places like a warmth.

Soon—a dim-time, perhaps two—I will go. I feel the give of it already, a looseness in my oldest joint where the bone meets stone. There will be a moment, silent under the white-noise wall of the falls, when I let go. A puff of wet dust. A swollen burst of violet slime, all my borrowed light released at once into the falling water. The Kaelth standing here will not look up. It will tap the quiet goodbye, gather its blade, drift down to the next shelf. *It is well. The cycle goes on.*

And it *is* well. This is what I want you to understand, you who stand on me, you who are new and still believe in permanence, who still feel the lack of ground and sky like a wound. I was a flying thing's bone. Then I was a floor. Then I was a garden of light. Then I will be silt in the stone-milk river, carried down, settling on a lower shelf, becoming the substrate for a fungus that will widen a ledge that some Kaelth, not yet tapped into being, will one day stand upon to greet a stranger.

I am not ending. I am traveling. The water taught me how, the way it falls sideways, hangs in slow curtains, spirals up through the eddies—*down* is only a suggestion, and so is *gone*.

Stand a while longer. The rain hits your back in a sheet and runs off you and runs over me, over my violet places, over the salt that crusts in my oldest cracks where the stone-milk dried. The Kaelth taps twice at its feet: *I am here, I am working*.

I cannot tap back. I have no name to give. I am the thing being unmade beneath the ones who name themselves, and that is its own kind of speech.

But if you press your bare foot flat against my grain and hold very still, past the bass thrum, past the *ping* of rain on the crystal branches, past the wet snap of a fungal bladder bursting—you might feel it. The long, slow, glad vibration of a thing dissolving exactly as it should.

*It is well. The cycle goes on.*

I bear you now. Lean into the rain. Let it carry you.

When you move on, I will not.

I will become the path that lets the next one cross.
