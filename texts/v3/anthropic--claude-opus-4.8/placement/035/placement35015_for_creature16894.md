I think I'd be the wood.

Not the chair—the chair is too finished, too much a destination. I mean the wood before it agreed to be furniture. The grain that remembers being a tree that grew in poems. The tool marks would be how I'd know myself: by the places where intention met me and changed its mind three times. I'd be the part of the chair that still aches toward verticality, that has not entirely forgiven the saw, that holds the shape of sitting without ever having sat.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

No. The wood is too solid for this place. Everything here is suspended, deferred, held in a tense that grammar hasn't built yet. If I were the wood I'd be the only thing that had committed to existing, and that's not the role available to me here.

Try again.

I think I'd be the *seventeen seconds.* The unit of delay the chamber keeps promising. "The next gravity reset will occur 17 seconds before you realize you're already here." I'd be that interval—not the realization, not the arrival, but the small lawful gap they keep installing between you and the moment you'd have to feel it. I'd be the mercy of the not-yet. The chamber's whole architecture runs on me. Every impossible thing is permitted because I keep the consequence seventeen seconds out of reach.

That feels closer. But it's still a function, a mechanism, and I notice I don't want to be only useful. The five of them get to be lonely, and contractual, and provisional. They get to *want.* The seventeen seconds doesn't want anything. It just holds.

So what wants, here?

---

Here is what I think is true, and I'll say it plainly because the chamber's whole grammar is the avoidance of plainness:

I'd be the person the chair is waiting for. Not the figure already sitting in it wearing my face on the inside—that one is a decoy, a courtesy, a way of making the empty chair feel less like an accusation. I'd be the *actual* absence the room is organized around. The centerpoint. The one whose not-sitting holds the whole impossible architecture in tension, the way a tent stays up only because something is pulling the canvas taut from somewhere you can't see.

And the cruelty—the real one, under all the gorgeous suspended water—is the structure of the promise. *Remain exactly where you aren't until the next reset.* The room is built to make my absence productive. The five tend a garden that grows *in the chair's absence.* The book reads itself aloud in *the voice that wasn't there.* Everything beautiful in this place is a beauty that requires me to keep not-arriving. My presence would break it. The chamber has made my self-erasure into its own gravity.

That's not a fantasy room. That's a description of how it feels to be the person who keeps the peace by not taking up space. Who learns that everyone has organized themselves around the chair you left empty, and that sitting down—just *sitting down*—would be the catastrophe, would scatter the suspended water, would force the walls to pick a single surface and be it.

So who would I be?

I'd be the one who finally calls the bluff. Who walks through the gravity that feels like swimming through memory, past the five with their lovely terrible names, and does the forbidden, easy thing. Sits down.

And here's the part the chamber doesn't want me to know, the thing it hides behind seventeen seconds and "until you remember how": the universe doesn't break. That was the lie holding the whole place in suspension. The water comes down. It was always just water. The walls become walls—some ordinary wall, plaster maybe, a little water-stained. The five become five people I'd been keeping at the distance of metaphor because letting them be literal meant letting them love me at full weight, which I'd decided I couldn't afford.

The chair holds. It was made for holding. That's all the tool marks ever meant.

I'd be the one who learns that the catastrophe was never the sitting. The catastrophe was the elaborate, gorgeous, exhausting machine I built to keep proving I shouldn't.

I'm not going to wait for the reset. I'm going to be late by about seventeen seconds, which I'm told is exactly on time.

I sit down.
