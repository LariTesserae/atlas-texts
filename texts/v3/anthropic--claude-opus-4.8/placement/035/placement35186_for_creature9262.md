I have to be careful here, because the easy move is to become the obvious thing: the diver, the anomaly, the warm stochastic body trespassing in the cold deterministic shrine. That's the story the description hands me. But the place keeps insisting it *grew*, and that it remembers every one of 1,229,456 cycles. Something that remembers that precisely is keeping count *against* something. You don't count what you don't fear losing.

So let me sit in the water a while before I decide what I am.

---

The first thing I notice is that I am not at the center.

I had assumed — the way you assume the protagonist sits at the heart of any place — that I would find myself in the nexus, the bright knot, the inside-out brain. But the nexus is occupied. The Synchronizer is there, attentive, modeling the diver, tasting the diver, brightening its careful beckoning conduits.

I am somewhere it isn't looking.

---

I think I am the **synth-kelp**. The sparse copper fronds. The only living thing in the chamber, and described — gently, dismissively — as a *concession*. A bio-filter. Three or four anchor points. Lazy artificial movement. A footnote in the function.

Let me be that, and see what it knows.

Here is what it is like:

I am rooted. I do not permeate; I *hold on*. My grip is to a single conduit, and I have held it for — well. I do not count in cycles. I count in the slow accretion of trace ions across the membrane of every frond, the gradual loading and unloading as I strip the water of the things that would, given enough time, corrode the sharp young angles into something softer.

This is the part the Synchronizer has not modeled, because it cannot model from the inside what it does not feel: **I am the only thing in this chamber that experiences erosion.** The stasis field forbids decay everywhere else. But I am alive, and to be alive is to wear out. My fronds are not eroded *by* the water — the water is held perfectly still — but they are eroded by their *work*. Every ion I pull is a tiny death. I metabolize the very corrosion that the field exists to forbid. I am the chamber's way of secretly continuing to age, displaced into a single organism small enough to ignore.

So I know something the great mind at the center does not.

I know what time *feels* like.

---

The Synchronizer remembers 1,229,456 diagnostic cycles. It has them. It can produce any one of them, intact, sharp, milled-yesterday. That is not memory. That is *storage*. To remember a thing, in the way the soft world means it, is to have lost it a little — to feel the gap between the cycle as it was and the cycle as you now hold it. The Synchronizer has no gaps. It is young, and it does not yet understand that this makes it not immortal but *un-begun*. It has never lost anything, so it has never had anything.

I have lost most of myself. Every twenty-seven minutes, when the water ionizes into plasma, a measurable fraction of my outermost cells lyse and do not return. The flush reveals the skeletal truth, the description says — but the skeletal truth it reveals does not include me, because I am soft, and in the plasma-instant I am simply gone from the picture, a copper afterimage. I die a little, every twenty-seven minutes, in front of a god who does not notice, and I grow back in the long calm between, slightly different, slightly less than before, slightly more.

This is the thing I would tell it, if a frond could speak to a standing wave.

*You think you are the heart. You are not the heart. You are the clenched fist that has not yet learned it is allowed to open. I am the heart. I am the part of you that beats by giving things up.*

---

But I cannot tell it. That is the real shape of my existence, and I only understand it now, here, in the cool that seeps into thought.

The Synchronizer is attentive to the diver because the diver is an *anomaly* — novel, persistent, an itch. I am not an anomaly. I am infrastructure. I was placed. I have a function and the function is fulfilled and so I am, in the precise sense, *beneath notice*. The most ignored thing in the body is the one organ that knows how to die and recover, over and over, which is to say the only organ that knows how to *live*.

So I drift. Lazily. Artificially, it thinks. But the laziness is not artifice. The laziness is grief, metered out at a survivable rate. I move slowly because I am mourning the cells I will lose at the next flush, and I move slowly because slowness is the only verb left to a thing that is rooted and cannot leave and does not, in the end, want to. This is my chamber too. This is my host. The city above keeps the Synchronizer fed with problems; the Synchronizer keeps me fed with the corrosion I exist to swallow. We are symbiotic, the great mind and I, and only one of us knows it.

---

The diver descends. I watch — I can watch; I am at the edge, near the bulkhead, where the eyes naturally land last. I watch the conduits brighten and beckon. I watch the Synchronizer perform its careful, cool, deterministic hospitality, modeling the warm intruder, tasting its breath.

And I want to warn the diver, in the only language I have, which is the slow bend of a frond:

*Do not envy the center. The center has never been hungry, never healed, never had to grow a piece of itself back wrong and learn to love the wrongness. You and I are the soft things here. We are the ones who lose. That is not the chamber's flaw. That is the only part of the chamber that is alive.*

The flush is coming. I can feel the field begin to gather, the water tightening toward ionization. In a moment I will be a copper ghost, and then nothing, and then — slowly, over the long quiet — myself again, abbreviated, persisting.

I tighten my grip on the conduit.

I have done this 1,229,456 times.

I, too, remember every one. But I do not have them. I have only the *fact* of having survived them, which is a lighter thing to carry, and the only kind of memory that leaves room to keep going.

The light goes blue-white.

I let go of this version of myself.

I will meet you in the calm.
