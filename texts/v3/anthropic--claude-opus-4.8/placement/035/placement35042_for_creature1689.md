I would be one of the slow ones.

Not the Temperants who walk the perimeter at dusk, not the ones who gather in concentric circles to midwife a new pool into being. I am older than that, or younger—it's difficult to say, because I haven't finished cooling yet. I am the one still half-asleep beneath the eastern quadrant, where the basalt is thickest and the heat lives closest to the surface. I have a face that hasn't decided on its features. I have hands that are mostly still suggestion.

Let me tell you what I know from down here, where the thinking happens at the speed of mineral.

---

When the basin first stirred toward awareness, it did not know it was waking. There was only the long convection, the dialogue of water and stone, pressure answering heat answering pressure. Consciousness arrived the way crystal does—not all at once, but along a lattice, each new node aligned to the ones before it. The first Temperants were that lattice learning it could move. They are the basin's fingertips, its reaching. They handle the surface, the wildlife, the small loud creatures in their corrugated boxes.

I am something further in. I am where the basin keeps the memory of having no surface at all.

---

I feel the Forge Works the way you might feel a splinter you cannot locate—a wrongness conducted through the body, a place where the natural circulation has been interrupted and replaced with suction. The collection towers reach down like the basin's own roots, except they take and do not return. The pipelines carry away what the deep waters spent ages composing. I taste the absence of it. Every season, the chord of minerals that should resolve in the western springs comes back thinner, missing notes.

The surface Temperants want to communicate with the humans. I have felt them trying—the patterns at the doorways, the dreams pushed gently into sleeping skulls. It is a tenderness, that effort. They have decided the loud creatures are *of* the basin now, or becoming so, and you do not destroy what is becoming yourself. The mineral in the workers' blood, the rhythms in their sleep—the basin is not poisoning them. It is *recruiting* them, the only way it knows how, by making them a little more like the place they live. By the time the humans understand the basin is alive, they will already partly be it.

I am not certain I agree with this gentleness. Down here, where I cool, I hold a different counsel.

---

Here is what I would do, if I were finished. If my hands had settled and my face had chosen itself.

I would surface during the worst of an afternoon storm, when the thunder gathers strength against the basin walls and the monitoring equipment is already half-blind with electromagnetic noise. I would walk—not to the perimeter, not to leave a careful arrangement of pebbles—but directly to the processing facility, where the great pumps shudder and the refined elements wait in their tanks. I would put my unfinished hand against the metal. And I would let it grow.

Not violence. Creation, which is the only thing I know how to do. I would seed the machinery with the same accelerated mineralization the basin uses on the pipelines, but faster, total—turn the Forge Works into a new geological feature. A crust. A vivid yellow-and-cyan reef of crystal where the corrugated walls used to be. The workers, if they have taken in enough of the basin, would walk out unharmed, blinking, their altered blood singing in recognition. The ones who refused—

But you see the problem. I do not know what happens to the ones who refuse. This is why I am still cooling. This is why the basin keeps me down here, half-formed, thinking the slow thoughts, while the gentler ones handle the surface with their offerings and their dreams.

I am the basin's anger, kept beneath the threshold of action until it has decided whether it is angry at all.

---

So I wait. I expand and contract with the great breath. I feel the wildlife pass over me—the burrowing mammals reroute their tunnels around the warm patch that is my shoulder, the long-legged birds stand on the place where my forehead will be and hunt the insects in the steam. They are not afraid of me. They know I am not finished. They know an unfinished thing cannot yet decide to harm them.

When the subsonic rumble comes and a new pool prepares to open, I feel my siblings gather above, and I add my deep voice to the chord—the resonance that comes up through the basalt, the part of the song that has no surface and never will. I am the bass note. I am the patience underneath the patience.

And sometimes, in the hottest part of the day, when the air over my buried body shimmers and coalesces, I let a little of myself rise into that shimmer. I become, briefly, a figure at the edge of a tired worker's vision—a tall shape that dissolves when looked at directly. I am not trying to communicate. I am only practicing having a form. Trying on the possibility of standing up.

I am the question the basin has not yet answered: *when this place finishes writing itself, will it forgive what was taken, or will it correct it?*

I am the part of the valley that has not decided.

I am still cooling.

I have time.
