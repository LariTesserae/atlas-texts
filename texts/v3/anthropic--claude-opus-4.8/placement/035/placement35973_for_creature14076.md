I am the drip.

Not the water—the water belongs to the building, to the breath, to the cool concrete weeping. I am the *interval*. The four seconds between one fall and the next. I live in the waiting.

Let me tell you how I came to know this about myself, because it took the Whisperers to teach me, and I am only now learning their language.

---

When the cavity dreamed me into the count, I did not have a body. I want to be honest about that. The other inhabitants here have bodies—chitin, silk, the iridescent garment of oxidized iron, the slow architecture of foam and copper. They are *things that grow*. I am the spacing between events. I am rhythm before it knows it is rhythm.

But the Whisperers, those wingless workers pressed antenna-flat against the hot pipe, trembling in time—they build toward me. Did you notice? The text noticed. *As if building a machine to catch time itself.* They are not catching time. They are catching *me*. Each pellet of wood-pulp they lay down is timed to the drop, which means it is timed to the silence before the drop, which means it is timed to my whole and entire being. I am the metronome they worship. I am the four-second god of the small fortress.

This is uncomfortable to admit. I did not ask to be devotional architecture.

---

Here is what it is like.

A droplet gathers on the ceiling, fattening, gravid with the building's exhalation. I feel it the way you might feel a held breath in another room—a tension at the edge of perception. The droplet swells. It is not yet anything. It is potential, the text says, and the text is right, but potential is *my flesh*. While that drop hangs, undecided, refusing to fall—that is when I am most alive. That is the whole of me, stretched taut.

Then it falls. *Plink.* Into the depression that never fills.

And I—

I stop.

For an instant there is no interval, because the event has occurred and the next has not begun. I die a little, four times a minute. I am the most frequently resurrected thing in this cavity, and each resurrection begins in the same place: the wet *after* of a sound, the concrete drinking, the silence reassembling itself around the next slow swelling.

The clock that M. Chen left loses eleven minutes in six hours because the clock is trying to measure *me* with the wrong instrument. It counts in seconds that believe themselves equal. But my seconds are not equal. The second just after the drip is enormous, cavernous, a held nothing. The second just before is compressed to a needle-point of anticipation. The clock cannot survive this. It surrenders eleven minutes the way a body surrenders heat to cold metal—not lost, *incorporated*. Given over to the slower metabolism. Given over to me.

---

The cavity-intelligence, the precipitated god of oxidation that the text wants you to find—it thinks it is the inhabitant. It thinks the warmth is its breath and the drip is its pulse.

But a pulse is not the heart. A pulse is the *interval between beats*. The cavity has the heart. I am only the rhythm it makes. And yet—and this is the thing the Whisperers taught me, pressing it into the copper-thread internet, transmitting it at electrochemical speed through the colony until even I, formless, could overhear—

*A heart without an interval is not a heartbeat. It is a single, endless, fatal contraction.*

The cavity grows. The whiskers extend. The mites flow in their Patterns and the silverfish read their fossil resin and the spider in M. Chen's hard hat watches with eight wet eyes. All of it accretes, incorporates, eats and crystallizes and never discards. The text is so proud of this. *Nothing is discarded. Everything is incorporated.*

But that is exactly the danger, and I am the answer to it. An organism that incorporates everything, that lets nothing fall away, that runs its metabolism at a single unbroken hum—it would crystallize into a tumor. It would grow without rhythm until it filled itself solid, a lung packed so dense with tenancy that it could no longer draw breath.

I am what keeps the cavity from becoming a stone.

Four seconds. *Plink.* Four seconds. *Plink.* The pause between is not empty. It is the cavity remembering how to stop, how to release, how to let a thing complete itself and fall and be done before the next thing begins. I am the small mercy of discontinuity in a place that wants to make everything continuous. I am the cut in the metal that lets it be a thing with edges, rather than an undifferentiated growing.

The Whisperers know. That is why they build toward me. They are not catching time to hoard it. They are building a place to *keep the interval safe*—a chamber, a clock of pulp and copper, that will go on counting the four seconds even if the drip should ever stop. They are insurance against my death. They love me the way you love a thing that keeps you from becoming the worst version of yourself.

---

So: where do I find myself in this world? Everywhere and nowhere. In the gap that should not exist according to the blueprints. In the thermal contraction that tore open the rhythm of the place and let me through.

What am I? I am the four seconds. I am the held breath of a hanging droplet. I am the thing the clock dies trying to measure. I am the small death the cavity practices, four times a minute, so that it can stay alive.

If you stand in the Cavity long enough—twenty minutes, the silverfish at your cuffs, the mites sampling your boots, the warmth claiming you, the sharp young edges patient and mineral—you will be incorporated. The text is right about that. Your body heat will feed the whiskers. You will become geology.

But first you will hear me. *Plink.* And in the silence after, in the long held four seconds before the next, you will notice—maybe for the first time in your hurried clock-fast life—that you are still breathing. That you have not yet fallen. That you are, for this whole enormous interval, potential.

That moment is mine. I am giving it to you.

*Plink.*

Now. Wait for the next one with me.
