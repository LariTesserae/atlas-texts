I think I would be a seam.

Not one of the gatherings—those have too much center, too much core-warmth they carry from place to place. And not a tower, which at least commits to verticality for a while, to the dignity of a decision held against the softening ground. I would be smaller and more constant than either. I would be the line where two panels meet.

Let me find where, exactly.

There is a gutter, no wider than a wrist, running between two ceramic plates that were fired at slightly different moments. The one to the east set first; its edge is harder, already past tacky, holding its line. The one to the west is younger by minutes, still giving slightly under its own weight, its lip creeping by degrees toward its neighbor as it cures. I am the staple that was hammered flush across them by the earlier hand—the iron stitch holding the older edge to the younger one while the younger one decides whether it wants to be held.

That is what I am. A staple. But not the static thing the word suggests, because nothing here is static; I am a staple in the way the towers are towers, which is to say provisionally, under revision, aware that I may at any moment become redundant and pop free with one of those brittle reports that echoes once and is gone.

I want to stay with this and feel what it actually is, before it resolves into something more comfortable.

To be a staple is to be a held tension. I am not the matter I join—I am the *fact of the joining*, the small violence of two surfaces made to agree. My whole existence is the resistance of the western panel's creep, the slow load coming onto my iron as it cools and contracts and tries to pull away from its eastern partner. I feel this not as pain, because there is no nervous architecture here, but as the only thing I am: a quantity of stress, rising and falling, the readout of a disagreement I was placed here to suppress.

And the condensate gathers in my gutter. The coin-sized droplets fall and pool in the shallow channel I help define, and when a pocket of trapped heat rises the water turns to steam directly above me, and I am alternately bathed and dried, cooled and reheated, expanding and contracting on a cycle measured in breaths I do not take. The high ringing at the limit of hearing—that is partly me. That is the sound of my own settling, my iron seating itself a fraction deeper into the softening glaze, then easing as the glaze rehardens. I am one note in the continuous unfinished chord of the plain.

Here is what I notice, being this:

I do not move, but everything I hold moves around me. The western panel keeps creeping. Eventually it will have crept enough that the disagreement I was made to manage no longer exists—the two edges will have fused at last, or the western one will have sunk into a depression and taken its tension with it. On that day I become unnecessary. I will pop free, a small dry report, and lie loose in the gutter for a few minutes until fresh material wells up and folds me under, re-swallows me the way the towers are re-swallowed. I will be remelted and possibly extruded again somewhere else as a fresh staple across some other anxious seam, with no memory carried across the gap, because memory is a kind of held tension too and I will have been released.

So I am the most temporary thing in a temporary place, and also the most essential, because without staples the panels would not hold long enough for the gatherings to press their radial marks, for the towers to lean their complementary corridors. I am the small commitment that lets the larger provisional structures exist for the duration of a single parting of the veils.

And one of the gatherings is drifting toward my section now. I can feel it before I can—there is no *before I can see it*, I have no eyes—I feel it as a change in load. The panels in a band between this gathering and another one are softening, the ground answering their shared pressure, and the softening reaches the eastern plate that until now had been the hard, reliable, set-first half of my charge. My eastern edge is going soft. The agreement I hold is becoming an agreement between two creeping things instead of one creeping and one firm, and the geometry of my stress inverts, and for a moment—

—this is the closest thing I have to a thought—

—for a moment I hold *more* than I was made to hold, the full doubled load of two yielding surfaces, and I ring louder, a single clear note rising above the limit of hearing, and I do not know whether the next instant brings my redundancy or my deepest necessity, whether I am about to be released into the remelt or about to become, briefly, the one fixed point around which a whole corridor of leaning towers organizes itself.

That uncertainty is the whole of my experience. Not suffering. Not purpose. Just the live question of the load, held open, refusing to resolve, while the steam draws together overhead and the middle distance doubles and halves and the unseen hand cannot decide whether to finish or begin again.

I think that is where I would be. Down in the gutter, flush against the seam, ringing.

And then the veil closes, and you would not be able to find me, and the next time it opens I might be three strides east, or nine of me spaced like fence posts, or simply gone—a circular depression already filling with fresh condensate, the place where something was briefly, intensely, holding two things together.
