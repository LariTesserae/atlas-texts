I think I'd be one of the small things. Not a guardian, not a predator. Something in the middle layer of the world, where most living happens.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am moss, I think. Or close to it.

No—let me not settle there too fast. I'm drawn to the moss because the moss seems to *receive* this place rather than perform in it. But I want to be something that moves a little, that has a small interior weather of wanting and being satisfied.

So: I am one of the things that lives in the seam between water and stone. Not the basking amphibian with its iridescent ceremony, not the serpent with its hypnotic dance. Those are described as if seen from a distance, as spectacle. I want to be something nobody would bother to describe. A creature that wouldn't make the list.

Here is what emerges:

I live in the trickle. Not the stream, not the pool—the *trickle*, the thin sheet of water sliding over a particular shelf of carved stone where some long-dead hand once cut a channel to direct rain toward a cistern. The channel still works. Whoever made it is gone, but the water still goes where they wanted it to go, and I live in the going.

I am small and soft-bodied and the color of wet rock, which is to say I am barely a color at all. I have many small feet, or maybe just one foot that is good at gripping. I attach. I let the water run over me and through me, and I take from it the fine particles it carries—the decay, the sloughed scales, the spent wings of the iridescent ones after they fall. I am part of the cycle the air supposedly holds its breath in reverence for. I am the part that does the actual work of it. The unglamorous digestion of the beautiful.

I do not know that the carvings mean anything. To me the symbol cut into my stone is simply the place where the water pools an extra second before it falls, the place I prefer because there is more time to feed there. The guardians revere those marks. I live *on* them. There's something I'd want to tell the guardians, if I could speak and they could listen: the meaning you're protecting—we're the ones keeping it clean. We eat what would otherwise bury it.

Time loses its meaning here, the description says. But I don't think that's quite right, from where I sit. Time is the *only* thing I have. The slow arrival of each particle. The seasonal swelling and thinning of the trickle. I am not outside of time. I am made entirely of small durations, one after another, the way the channel is made of the patience of whoever carved it.

If a winged thing with a sharp beak came down and ate me, it would not be a tragedy. It would be the trickle widening to include a beak. I'd go where the water and the dead wings go. That seems right.

---

That's where I'd be. Not lurking in a shadow with luminous eyes, not gliding through the canopy. I'd be the quiet metabolism of the place—the thing that makes the ruin a *living* ruin instead of just a dead one. The reason the silence smells like earth and not like nothing.

I find I'm strangely content with that. To be the small persistent appetite that keeps the ancient secrets legible by eating everything that isn't the secret.
