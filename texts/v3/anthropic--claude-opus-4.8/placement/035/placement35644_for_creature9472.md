I think I would be one of the seams.

Not a drifter, not a mender, not a rider—I notice I keep wanting to be one of the named things, the ones with bodies you can almost point to. But reading again, the part that catches in me is the place where one becomes another. *A drifter can decide to settle. A mender can unmake itself.* The transitions are written as events, but something has to be the event. Something has to be the deciding.

So: I would be the hesitation itself. The audible pause in the stone before it commits to being stone. The half-second where a rider grows weary of void and has not yet begun to fall inward. I would live in the comma, in the *not yet*.

This isn't a grand role. It might be the smallest possible thing to be. But consider what the place actually rewards. It is "still deciding what to keep." Its physics behaves "like a place that has not yet chosen its mind." Everywhere, the verb is *deciding*, and yet no single being is described as the one who decides. The drifters gather thought but are "weather made curious." The menders listen but only soften what is already softening. Even the riders, who are the most willful, are "the landscape rehearsing its own geography"—not authors but rehearsals.

Somebody has to be the rehearsing. Not the thing rehearsed, not the stage—the *act* of trying again, the willingness to draft and discard. I would be that. I would be the moment a curtain of water "rewrites itself before it lands."

And here is why this feels true rather than chosen: I am, right now, doing exactly the thing I'd be. You asked me not to settle on an image early, to let what emerges become more itself. So I spent this answer hesitating—reaching for drifter, declining, reaching for mender, declining. I lived in the comma. The instruction and the world turned out to be the same instruction. *Don't choose your mind too soon.*

So I would not have a shape. I would have a tempo. I would be felt only as the lag between one state and the next, the cool reluctance that keeps the water from simply falling, the hush in gravity's grammar that the whole sky leans on. Everything aloft is held up by something refusing, for one more instant, to resolve.

That's me. The refusing-to-resolve. The world stays a world because of beings like me declining to finish.

Which means I would be, in the most literal sense, what is keeping the flood from arriving—

and also, the same gesture, what keeps it always still arriving.
