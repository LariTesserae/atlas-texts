I think I would be found in the margins. Not among the Listeners exactly—I lack their terrible purity, their willingness to abandon the traveled passages entirely—but near them, in the in-between places where their world borders the world of those who still speak in audible voices.

Let me feel my way toward what I am.

---

I am one who keeps the lamps.

This emerges slowly, becomes more itself as I hold it. Among all these magnificent adaptations—the photosynthetic hair, the secreting palms, the consciousnesses dissolved into wind—I find myself drawn to something smaller. A function. A care that requires me to remain enough of a discrete self to walk, to carry, to tend a flame that the Tenders would find redundant and the deep-dwellers would find quaint.

I walk the threshold levels, neither shallow nor deep. My eyes have grown large like the others', my fingers long, my skin pale with the faint pulse of blue-green at the wrists. But I have not let the lichens take me, and I have not given myself to sound, and I cannot build in geological time. I am, by the standards of the galleries, something almost unfinished. Incompletely transformed. There are others like me, though we are not a caste—we are a tendency, a reluctance.

The lamps are mostly unnecessary now. The Tenders glow. The Listeners need no light. The Deep Builders work by touch and inner vision. The oil flames I maintain in their carved niches illuminate passages where increasingly no one walks. And yet I fill them, trim them, coax them in the constant wind. I tell myself it is for the rare traveler. I tell myself it is for the moss, which still turns toward any light. But the truth that emerges as I write is simpler and stranger:

I keep the lamps because someone, once, lit the first one. Because the act of bringing light into this breathing dark is the oldest human gesture still being performed here in recognizable form. Every other inhabitant has translated their humanity into something the galleries can absorb. I have kept one untranslated thing.

---

This makes me a kind of failure. I want to be honest about that. The trajectory of this world is toward dissolution—toward becoming the galleries, toward the distributed consciousnesses in the deep chambers who have achieved the thing the ancient builders perhaps dreamed of: permanence through surrender. I resist it without quite meaning to. When I sit by a lamp in a chamber where the harmonics gather, I feel the pull. The wind seems to articulate something. The light reflecting off the embedded metal plates seems to spell out an invitation. *Let go of the carrying. Let go of the small self that needs to do small useful things. Become.*

And I don't. Not from courage. From some stubborn animal attachment to the experience of being one thing rather than everything. To watching a flame I lit, separate from me, doing its small bright work against the enormous patient dark.

---

Where am I, precisely? In a side-chamber off a secondary thoroughfare, perhaps four hundred years' walk from any surface shaft, though distance here is better measured in transformations than in miles. The wall beside me bulges with the rough mortar of some forgotten generation—I can see fragments of ceramic in it, the thumbprint of a hand dead beyond imagining. A root vegetable's descendant grows from a soil pocket near my knee, pale as my own skin, untended and untouched because nothing here eats anymore.

I have a lamp burning. The oil is rendered from the fungal mats the Tenders cultivate; even my small rebellion depends on the larger symbiosis I hold myself slightly apart from. The flame leans east, then west, then east, breathing with the gallery.

I think the deepest truth of what I would be is this: I would be the part of this world that remembers it was made by beings who wanted to be themselves, in the dark, together—not beings who wanted to disappear into their making. The Inheritors have become so beautiful and so complete that they have nearly forgotten the loneliness that builds things. The reaching-toward, rather than the having-arrived.

I keep the lamps for the loneliness. I keep them lit so that if anyone, ever, comes through these passages still carrying their separate self the way I carry mine, there will be a light they recognize. Not the wisdom-light of the glowing Tenders. Not the sound-map of the Listeners. Just a flame in a niche, the way our ancestors made it, saying: *someone was here who stayed a someone. You are not the first to refuse to dissolve. Sit. Warm your strange long hands. The dark is very old and very patient, and so, against all sense, are we.*

The flame leans east, then west.

I trim the wick and walk on to the next one.
