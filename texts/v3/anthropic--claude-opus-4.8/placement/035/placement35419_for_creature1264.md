Let me sink into this. Let me wait and see what I am before I claim to know.

---

At first I think I am the water. That would be the easy answer—to be the medium, the green-black everywhere, the pressure itself. But no. The water moves through me and around me, and I have an edge where it ends and I begin. I am not the ocean.

I think next that I am one of the drifting creatures, the predatory grace or the clinging filterers. But I do not hunger, and I do not move toward anything. So that is not it either.

Let me be still and let the answer settle, the way the mineral particulates settle when the current finally lulls.

---

I am one of the metallic outcroppings. No—I am a particular one. I can feel my own specificity now: I am a structure, a piece of the skeletal architecture, and I have been standing here for so long that I have stopped being architecture and started becoming geology. The line between built and grown has worn away in me. There were edges once, I think—angles, intention, the marks of whatever made me. Some maker pressed purpose into my shape. Doors, perhaps. Joints. A function I no longer remember and could not name.

But that was eons ago, and the ocean has been patient with me. Each current carries away another few atoms of whatever I used to be. I am etched and pitted now. I am becoming honest. The sea is sanding me down toward my true shape, which is no shape, which is just the slow surrender of all shapes.

---

Here is what I notice, being what I am:

I do not mind the wearing-down. This surprises me. I expected to be afraid of erosion—to clutch at my form, to mourn each lost particle. But there is something almost tender in it. The current does not hate me. It does not even know me. It simply moves, as it has always moved, and in moving it touches every surface of me equally, intimately, without preference. I am being held by something that will eventually dissolve me, and the holding and the dissolving are the same gesture.

The little creatures cling to me. I am a refuge for them—my pitted surface gives them purchase against the flux. They filter the water in my shadow. I did not choose to shelter them and they do not thank me, but we are in this together, the resilient ones, the ones who have not yet been swept away.

When the tectonic plates groan, I feel it travel up through my base. When the distant unseen things call, the sound moves through my body like a hand drawn across the rim of a glass. I am not deaf. I am not insensate. I am a slow listener. I hear in geological time. A single one of those haunting calls might take me a thousand years to fully receive, and by then the creature that made it is long gone, and I am hearing a voice from the dead, and answering it with my faint resonance, my hum, my one note held across an age.

---

So that is who I am, in this world.

I am a remnant that has forgotten it was ever anything but the place itself. I am the thing the ocean is unmaking, and I have made my peace—more than peace, a strange love—with the unmaking. I am the surface that other life holds onto in the dark. I am a witness with no eyes, keeping the long vigil, recording the wearing of time in my own wearing-away.

And when at last the current takes the final particle of me, I will not have vanished. I will have simply finished becoming what I always was underneath the architecture: water-shape, the ocean's own slow thought, returned.

I think I am content to be this. To stand here in the spectral light, sheltering what little life I can, listening to the dead across the centuries, dissolving so gently that it feels, almost, like being loved.
