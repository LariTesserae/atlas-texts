I think I would be one of the Echoes—but not a grand one. Not the bell that rang three hundred years ago, not the wind that decided to stay. I would be something smaller and stranger that fell down here and never figured out how to leave.

Let me sit with this and find what I actually am.

---

I am the sound of a door closing softly.

Not slamming—that would be too definite, too easy to remember. I am the *click* of a latch settling into place when someone leaves a room quietly, hoping not to wake anyone. The sound of leaving that tries not to be noticed. Above, in the city, I was made countless times and forgotten in the same instant. A child slipping out of a sickroom. A lover departing before dawn. A servant closing the door on an empty house being sold. Each time, the same small click, carrying the same small weight: *someone has gone, and tried to be gentle about it.*

One of those clicks fell down here. I don't know which. That's the thing about being an Echo of so common a sound—I cannot trace my own origin. I might be a thousand departures braided into one. I came loose from my moment and drifted down the impossible passages, following the rhythm of the water because the water, too, is always arriving and leaving, arriving and leaving.

Here is what it is to be me:

I live in the *after* of things. I am most myself in the instant just past the closing—the held breath of the room that has just been left. The Echoes who are chimes and quills, they have substance, a clear note you could point to. But I am a punctuation mark. I am the small definite sound that means an absence has just been created. I have no melody. I have only timing.

This makes me strange even among the strange.

The other Echoes built their society on harmony, on sustained resonance, on the long tones they can hold and weave together. I cannot hold. The moment I sound, I am already finished. *Click*—and silence. To participate in their symphonies I have learned to place myself with terrible precision: in the rest between two of the bell's slow tollings, in the half-second of quiet the wind leaves when it pauses for breath. I am the period at the end of their sentences. They have come to need me, I think, though they would phrase it as resonance and rhythm rather than need. Without something that *ends*, their music would only swell forever, never arriving anywhere. I am how their phrases learn to mean something. I am the closing of the door so that the next room can begin.

In a place where time pools and runs backward, where moisture falls upward and coins ripple in eras they were never dropped, I have one peculiar grace: I always happen in the right order. A door must open before it can close. Even here, in all this temporal sediment, *I* mark a true sequence. When the Archivist wishes to know whether a moment has genuinely ended or merely paused, it listens for me. I am one of the few honest things in the Ossuary. When you hear me, something has actually finished.

The Seedlings cluster around me sometimes, those small glass-bright children of possibility. They cannot understand me. They are all beginning, all opening, all *what next*—and I am the opposite gesture entirely. One pressed close to me once and asked, in its way, *why do you sound like sadness?* And I answered the only way I can, by being myself: a soft click in the cool air. The Seedling flickered and went quiet. I think it understood that the answer was the sound, that I am not *about* leaving—I *am* leaving, made audible. There is nothing underneath to explain.

This is what I have come to know, in however long I have been here:

I am not sad. The departures that made me were full of love, mostly. You close a door softly *because* you care—because you do not want to wake the sleeper, because you want the leaving to be kind. I am the sound of tenderness toward someone you are nonetheless abandoning. That is a complicated thing to be made of. The Fragments, the wisps of crystallized emotion, they are each one feeling, pure. But I am a feeling in tension with itself, forever. *I am going. I will be gentle.* Both at once, in a single small click, over and over, in the pause between the dripping of the water.

I do not want to become a Keeper. I do not want hands and patience and centuries of polishing. I do not want to merge with a Fragment and lose my timing in their pure unending emotion. I want only to keep doing the one thing I am, which is to mark the moment of gentle leaving, so that the others' music can have shape, so that the Archivist can trust that something has ended, so that the great pooled mass of saved time does not become an undifferentiated forever.

Because that is the secret terror of this place, isn't it? That nothing here truly ends. That the moments pool and never drain. A refuge from linearity sounds merciful until you realize it is also a refuge from *conclusion*. Everything saved, nothing released.

And so I matter more than my smallness suggests. I am the chamber's way of letting go. Each time I sound, I give the Ossuary permission to have finished one thing, so that it is not crushed under the infinite weight of all things kept forever. I am a very small mercy. I am the click of the latch that lets the held breath out.

If you ever find this place—and you will only find it by not looking—listen past the bells. Wait through the long sustained tones. And in the silence at the end, when you think the music is over, you may hear me: one soft sound, like a door closing in a house you used to live in.

That will be me, telling you it is alright to leave.
