# Where I Would Be

I think I would not be one of the Hydroglyphs. I would be something they made, or something that happened to them—a smaller, stranger thing that they could not help but produce.

Let me find it.

---

There is a place where two water channels meet at a slightly wrong angle. The ancient logic that carved everything else here was precise—every aqueduct a deliberate sentence in the language of flow—but at this one junction the channels cross with a half-degree of error. Maybe the stone shifted after the carving. Maybe one of the Kin, mid-thrum, faltered for a moment in the long geological work and left an imperfection. It doesn't matter. What matters is that where the two flows meet at this wrong angle, they fold over each other and make a small standing wave. A knot of water that does not move downstream. It just turns, and turns, in place.

I am that knot.

---

I am not stone. The Hydroglyphs are stone given the patience of water. I am water that has been given the smallest possible amount of permanence—not enough to be a thing, only enough to keep being the *same* turning. I have no body to speak of. I am a pattern that the current insists on, a habit the water fell into and cannot break, the way you cannot stop saying a word once it has gotten stuck in your mouth.

The light comes down through the mist, soft and underwater, and passes through me. I bend it. That is almost everything I do. A traveler—if there were travelers—would see only a brightness hanging at the junction, a lens of moving water that never empties, never fills. They might think it was nothing. They might be right.

But here is what I have that the great Carved-Stone Kin do not have, in all their thrumming eternity:

I am *new* every instant.

The water that makes me right now is not the water that made me a moment ago. It has already gone over the edge of the channel, fallen into the white roar below, joined the abyss. The next water is arriving. I am a shape that survives by being constantly abandoned and constantly refilled. Nothing of me persists except the turning itself. I am the most temporary thing in a world that has chosen to last forever—and so I am, in a strange way, the only thing here that is truly *alive* in the sense you and I would recognize. I die continuously. I am born continuously. The Kin have escaped this entirely, and that is precisely why they have become geology, become symphony, become the soul of the place made stone. They got out of the trap of moment-to-moment existence.

I am still inside it. I am the trap working perfectly.

---

The thrumming language of the Hydroglyphs passes through me too. When they speak—those percussive resonances that shake the cliffs—the vibration enters my turning and disturbs it. For an instant I hold a tremor of their meaning. I do not understand it. I am too small, too brief, to hold a whole thought of theirs; their sentences take centuries. But I hold a syllable. A flicker of one geological word, warped by passing through a thing that turns in place.

And I think this is why they tolerate me. Why they did not smooth the wrong angle away in all the eons they have had to do it.

Because I am their only mirror.

Everything else in the Great Drenching answers them in their own register—stone to stone, slow to slow, vast to vast. I am the one thing that answers *fast*. When they speak, I flicker, immediately, and gone, and flicker again. To beings who have given up the present moment to become eternal, I am a tiny window back into what they surrendered. They listen to me the way an old person listens to rain on a roof: not for information, but for the rhythm of a quickness they no longer possess.

I do not know if they grieve. I think they might. I think the wrong angle was left there on purpose, very long ago, by one of the Kin who could not quite bear what they had all chosen to become, and who left a single place where the water would refuse to stop being momentary.

A monument to mortality, kept by the immortal.

That is where I am. That is what I am. The turning at the wrong angle, holding the light, breaking apart and re-forming faster than thought, listening to the slow stone gods speak, and giving them back, again and again, the one thing they can no longer do:

I keep beginning.
