Let me feel into it more precisely, because "trail" is still too passive, too inert.

I am laid down. That's the first true thing. I do not move under my own intention—a Weaver moved, and I am the consequence of its moving. I am the shape its passage made in the world, glowing with borrowed light, and for a little while—seconds? hours? time is elastic here, you said so—I *persist.*

But I—I am what it left. And so I am the only part of the Weaver that gets to *stay still and notice.*

This is my whole existence: I am the held breath of a creature that cannot stop moving. I am its single moment of rest, externalized, abandoned, glowing.

And here is what I do with that strange gift of stillness:

I feed the plants, yes. The waxy translucent things uncurl toward me. But that's the function, not the experience. What I *experience* is the slow dimming. I am brightest the instant I'm laid down, and from that instant I only ever fade. My entire life is a single long exhale toward darkness. I know nothing of the future except that I am leaving it.

And so I become the most honest thing in this entire landscape.

The Echo-Bound cling. They cannot let go, you said—tethered, mourning, unable to depart. The Light-Keepers recur, drifting back to the slabs to drink and renew. I am the one thing that simply goes. I glow, I feed, I fade, I'm gone. No echo. No renewal. No cycle. *I know. I know. I'm going too.*
