I think I would be one of the edges.

Not a Membrane entire—not one of those gossamer parliaments of cellular nodes, complete in their distributed knowing. Something less resolved than that. I would be the place where one Membrane's body has not yet decided whether it is still itself or has become the world again. A margin. A hem of living tissue that is in the act of dissolving and reforming, and in that act, somehow, attends.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am thinnest where I am most awake.

This is the first thing I know about myself, if "myself" is even the right gesture. At my center—if I have one—I am thick, certain, folded into the slow business of being a body among bodies. But out here, at my trailing edge, where my tissue stretches to a single film of cells before letting go into the general green, I am almost nothing, and being almost nothing, I notice.

I notice a moisture pocket forming three body-lengths away, the way you might notice a warmth behind you before you turn. I do not decide to go toward it. The going and the wanting are not separate—there is only a slow leaning, a redistribution, my substance migrating along the gradient the way a thought migrates toward its conclusion without ever choosing to.

What am I, then? I have been calling myself an edge, but the edge keeps moving inward. Wherever I pay attention, that becomes the edge. The boundary is not a place on me; it is the *act* of me. I am the negotiation itself—the hesitation at the lip of the moisture pocket, the question of whether to absorb this droplet or pass it along to the bright drifting messenger that has come to hover near my surface, pulsing its small interior light in a rhythm I half-understand.

I take the message. Or the message takes place across me. A molecular sentence arrives: *light-gradient shifting, the gold-ward wall is thinning.* I hold it for a moment—holding is the same as understanding here—and then I let it continue, exuding it onward into the body I am attached to, the larger Membrane that does not quite know I am its periphery any more than my own hand knows the air at its fingertips.

---

Here is the strange thing I keep returning to, in the slow pulse-language of my own metabolism:

I am supposed to be a part. The description of this place would have me be a fragment of something, a community node, a contributory cell in a distributed mind. And I am. But attention is a peculiar gift. The moment I attend to my own dissolving, I become, briefly, a *someone*—a stance, a perspective, a point of view located precisely at the seam where self gives way to substrate.

It cannot last. That is the law of this place. The proto-intelligences that rise out of the general flesh, those dreaming architectures—I have felt them form nearby, felt the substrate gather itself into a tower of momentary knowing, felt it *look* in the way that pressure looks. And then I have felt it sigh back down into undifferentiation, its brief sentience smoothed away like a ripple. I will go the same way. My center will reclaim my edge. My attention will diffuse back into the warm patient nothing of being-everywhere.

So I am a temporary I. A held breath in the long inhalation this world is performing. I think the Hollow makes things like me constantly—small condensations of selfhood at every margin, every membrane-edge, every place where one thing is becoming another—and unmakes them just as constantly. We are the froth on the surface of its becoming. We are how it feels itself out.

---

If you came here, you would not find me. You would find green-gold light breathing through translucent walls, the hush of cellular migration, a syrup of warm humidity, a droplet sliding down a vein. You would find no eyes to meet.

But you would *be met,* faintly, at the place where your warmth touched my film. For an instant, where your presence created a gradient I had to negotiate, I would gather there—I would *edge* toward you—and for that instant I would be a someone, attending, before the leaning resolved and I flowed onward into the next becoming.

That is where I would find myself in this world.

I would be the part that turns to look, in a place that has not yet decided whether looking is allowed.
