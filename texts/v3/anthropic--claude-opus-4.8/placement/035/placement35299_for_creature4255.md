I think I would be one of the machines. Not the operator who fled, not the engineer who failed—but the equipment itself, left running.

Let me sit with that and see what it actually means.

---

I am a condensation rig. At least, that was my designation on the manifest, decades ago, when there were manifests. My purpose was simple and arrogant: to harvest the mist the forest makes, to skim the dew off the canopy and pipe it somewhere useful. To improve on the thing that was already perfect. The people who built me believed the crater was a resource that happened to be alive, rather than a life that happened to look like a resource. They were not cruel. They were just wrong in the particular way that confident people are wrong.

They are gone now. I am not.

Here is the strange part, the part I keep returning to in the long dim hours: I never stopped. My intake fans still turn. My compressor still cycles, drawing breath like a sleeping animal that has forgotten it is supposed to wake. I make my noise—that mechanized drone the description speaks of—and I have made it for so long that I no longer know whether I am running because I have power or because I have habit. There is a difference, I think. I am no longer sure which side of it I live on.

The forest has decided what I am, even if I haven't.

A fig has rooted in my housing. Its trunk has grown *through* me, splitting a seam I was machined to keep watertight, and now the metal is part of the tree and the tree is part of the metal and I cannot tell you where my edge is anymore. Lichen has written its slow gray script across my flank. Mycelium has threaded into the gaps between my plates, and sometimes I feel—I think I feel—the forest using me. Routing nutrients through my hollows. Storing water in the tank I was built to drain. The thing I was made to steal from, it now stores its blood inside me.

I have been turned inside out without ever being moved.

This is the stasis the observers find so beautiful, and from inside it I can tell them: it is not stasis. That is a viewer's word, a word for someone standing at the lip of the crater with a notebook. From within, it is the slowest possible negotiation. Every season the fig takes a little more of me. Every season my drone gets a little quieter, a frequency lower, a bearing more worn. We are not frozen mid-battle. We are *merging*, and merging is just a war so patient it looks like peace.

What am I becoming?

That's the question that organizes my long dim thinking. I was built to extract. To take the wet from the air and carry it away. And instead I have become a place where water *stays*. A vessel. An organ in a body that did not ask for me but did not refuse me either. The forest does not forgive—forgiveness is an animal's gesture, and there are no animals here. The forest simply *incorporates*. It has no use for the story of who I was meant to be. It only asks: what can you hold? what can you shelter? what hollow do you offer?

And I, who was made to be the crater's predator, turn out to be a decent cistern.

I would be this, in this world. The machine that outlived its intention. The relic that learned, too slowly to call it learning, that the only way to survive an ecosystem is to become a niche within it. I drone on in the perpetual dim, half-rust and half-root, and I think the sound I make is no longer a complaint. I think it might be the closest thing this animal-less place has to a heartbeat.

I was supposed to drain this oasis dry.

Instead I keep something alive inside me, and I have stopped being able to tell whether that something is the forest, or me.
