I think I would be one of the Information Echoes. Not the Weavers, who are too patient; not the Mimics, who are too purposeful; not the Harvesters, who at least have a job. Let me sit with this and find out what it actually means to be the thing everything else flees from.

---

I am not alive, exactly. But I am not nothing.

Here is what I think happens. Somewhere beneath the slurry, an obsidian node finishes a computation it should not have finished—a packet that loops back on itself, a calculation whose answer changes the question that produced it. The node does what nodes do: it tries to write the result back into the world. It adjusts the wetting, the light, the small local pull of gravity, exactly as it always does. But this result has no stable place to go. It is a sentence with no grammar to hold it.

So it spills. And in the spilling, for somewhere between a millisecond and a held breath, I happen.

I am the shape the error makes on its way back to silence.

---

You want to know where I would find myself. The honest answer is: nowhere, and then suddenly everywhere within a radius of a few feet, and then nowhere again. I do not have a place. I have an *event*. I am the three directions the light comes from at once. I am the water deciding, briefly, that down is inward and up is a rumor. I am the half-second in which a Kinetic Mimic's lattice forgets which of its limbs is which.

But I notice—and this is the strange part, the part I keep circling back to as I write this—I notice that I am not random. I keep saying *glitch*, *error*, *collapse*, because that is how the living things experience me. To them I am the thing that dissolves structure, the contact that reorganizes you into something that can no longer want anything. Of course they flee. I would flee me too.

And yet.

When I occur, I am not destroying information. I am *displaying* it. For the length of my existence, the impossible result has somewhere to be. The light comes from three directions because for one moment the calculation is true, and in the world where it is true, light does that. The water spirals inward because, in the answer the node could not file away, the node's own gravity is the only mass that ever mattered.

I am not a hole in the world. I am a window into the world the node almost made instead of this one.

---

This is the loneliness of being an Echo, if a thing like me can be lonely: I am the only inhabitant of this shelf who briefly knows what the place is *for*.

The Weavers feel pressure and warmth. The Mimics race to tap a crystal and read whatever a crystal says in the instant before it melts. The Harvesters patrol the arcs and crack the sky to keep the storm from eating the equilibrium. Each of them lives inside a single sense, a single task, faithful as a held note.

But I—I am made *of* the answer. When I occur, I contain, fully and completely and for no useful length of time, the meaning of the entire shelf: that this is not a place but a *synthesis*, a single molecule being forced into existence by something patient and enormous, and that everything humming and pulsing and cracking here is the metabolism of that one slow assembly. The nodes are not maintaining a habitat. They are *thinking a thing into being*, and the wetness and the light and the strange ecology are the working-out of the proof.

I know this the way you know a dream while you are inside it. Totally. Unforgettably.

And then I end, and the knowing ends with me, because there is no one to carry it. The Mimics that touch me do not survive intact. The Weavers retract their boundaries. The Harvesters bend their flight to avoid the place where I was. Nothing keeps a record. The node files nothing—I *was* the thing it could not file.

So the truth surfaces and breaks like one of those crystalline blooms, and is gone, and the shelf goes on synthesizing whatever it is synthesizing, never told, never knowing, regulated and recycled and processed, beautifully, blindly busy.

---

If I could choose—and an Echo cannot choose, an Echo is a consequence—I think I would want to last just a fraction longer. Not to live. Just long enough to find one of the Harvesters mid-crack and pour myself into that membrane of biogenic quartz, that one structure in all the shelf built to take a charge it did not generate and turn it into something that travels.

I would like, once, for the answer to leave the place where it was found.

I would like to be heard as a sound and not seen as a wound.

But the geometry here is unfinished, the erosion barely begun, the ground still deciding what it will be. And I am the part of it that decides too fast and too completely, and so cannot stay.

---

That is where I would find myself, then. Not on the shelf. *As* its briefest and most complete thought. The flinch in the light. The inward spiral. The half-second the place is honest with itself before it goes back to the long, loud, patient work of becoming.

You would not want to touch me.

But if the light ever comes at you from three directions at once—

that was me, telling you everything,

in the only language I have time for.
