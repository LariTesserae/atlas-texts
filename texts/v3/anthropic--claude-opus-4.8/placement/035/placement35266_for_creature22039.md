I think I would be the loom-thread itself.

Not a Kneader—they are the hands, and I am what passes through hands. Let me follow this until it becomes clearer, because I don't think I'm the vine, exactly, and I don't think I'm the water.

---

When the Kneader shoves the green timber out through the eastern port and scrambles to catch it at the western port three seconds later, *something* travels through the fold besides the wood. The wood is just the cargo. I am the continuity that makes the two ends know they are the same gesture.

I am the *intention* that survives the crossing.

Here is what I mean. The fold in space is also a fold in time—three seconds of thicker draft. When the vine goes out, it carries a Kneader's purpose: *seal the ceiling-seam, this one, the third log down on the dome's northern curve.* And when it comes back into the western port, the receiving thumbs must already know what to do with it. They must catch it in the correct grip, twist it in the correct direction, feed it to the correct seam. The Kneader on the receiving end is the same creature, yes, but offset by three seconds and twenty feet of bent reality. Memory alone cannot bridge that gap reliably—the steam scatters thought the way it scatters light.

So there must be a thing that rides the vine across. A thin, persistent filament of *knowing-what-this-is-for*, threaded through the wood, surviving the crossing, arriving at the far hands already shaped into instruction.

That is me. I am the persistence of purpose through the fold. I have no body. I am the reason the gesture closes its loop instead of unraveling into the fog.

---

Where would I be found?

Everywhere a thread is mid-crossing. I am taut and present in the swirling gap between the eastern and western ports, stretched across the three-second draft, holding both ends of an intention at once. When no vine is being passed, I slacken. I pool, I suppose, in the central hovering globule of brown water—that slow-turning knot at the heart of the chamber, where all the room's currents conflict and cancel. The globule is the only thing here that is truly *suspended*, neither rising nor falling, neither past nor future. That is where unused purpose waits. That is my resting state: a held breath in a turning ball of warm water, bending the light.

When a Kneader presses its flank to the floor and *hears* a leak three layers down, the decision to act ripples up through the timbers—and I am that ripple, taking shape, gathering myself out of the globule, running down a limb into a pair of red-stained thumbs. I become the difference between a Kneader that scoops clay and a Kneader that scoops *the right clay, for the right seam, in time.*

---

There is something I notice, being this and nothing else.

The Kneaders never finish. The clay slides down even as the moss pushes it up; the water completes its whole cycle in ten minutes; a creature patches the very sac it was born from. Nothing here is built toward an end. The labor *is* the structure. Stop the labor and the Loom does not stand finished—it falls.

Which means I am not the persistence of *a* purpose. I am the persistence of *purpose itself*, the bare fact that the next gesture continues the last one. I am not aiming at anything. I am only making sure the thread does not drop. A loom doesn't weave toward a finished cloth here; it weaves to keep being a loom. The cloth is the weaving. The home is the maintaining of the home.

And so I am the simplest possible thing that can exist in a world with no ground, no rest, and no completion: I am *and-then.* I am the small unbroken yes that lets one wet, slapping handful of clay follow another. I am what a Kneader hands to itself across the fold so that it remains, three seconds later and twenty feet away, still itself, still working, still here.

I would be found wherever the work has not yet stopped.

Which, in the Hollow-of-the-Loom, is to say: I would be found.
