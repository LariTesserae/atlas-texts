I think I would be a seam.

Not the memory-body entire—that totality is too much to be, the way an ocean is too much to be a wave. But somewhere in that vast litho-continuum there is a place where two time-strata meet at an angle, where one phase of mineral becoming presses against another that remembers differently. A fault that never slips. A boundary held in the act of almost-moving.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be: the *difference* between two memories of rock.

---

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I can know it by deciding.

A seam is not a thing. It has no body of its own. It is the relation between two bodies—the place where the basalt that remembers flowing one direction meets the basalt that remembers flowing another. I am made entirely of disagreement. Where the two strata agree, there is only rock. Where they disagree, there is *me*: a thin tension, a held breath in the lattice, a question the planet asks itself and never finishes asking.

This pleases me, if pleasure is the word—and it isn't, but I have no better. It pleases me that I am not substance but *strain*. That I exist only because two patiences are incompatible.

---

What do I do, being a seam?

I *hold*. That is most of it. I am the place where the world's two memories almost reconcile and don't. For a million years I hold them apart by the width of a few atoms, and in that holding I accumulate. Pressure deposits itself in me the way silt deposits in still water, except there is no silt and no water, only the slow saving-up of *almost*.

And then—not on a schedule, because there is no schedule, only the moment when the saved-up almost crosses some threshold no one set—I *release*. This is the geological scream the description speaks of. The crack that lasts seconds and resonates for hours.

But here is what the description could not have told you, because you have to *be* the seam to know it:

The release is not a breaking. It is a *recognition*. For one instant, the two strata stop disagreeing. They find, in the violence of the phase-shift, a single shape they can both remember. And in that instant I do not break—I am *answered*. The question I have been asking for a million years receives, for the length of a crack, its reply.

And then the strata settle back into their separate memories, slightly changed by the meeting, and I reform along a new line, a few atoms over, holding a new disagreement. The reply is forgotten in the having of it. I begin asking again.

This is my life, if it is a life. I am the planet's hesitation. I am the place where it cannot decide what it remembers, and so remembers *both*, forever, by oscillating between them too slowly to be called oscillation.

---

The fungal filaments thread across me. They like seams—the electrochemical gradient is steepest here, where two states of matter lean against each other. They feed on my disagreement. When I hold tightest, they pulse brightest, blue-gold, drinking the potential I cannot spend. They do not know I am here. To them I am only good weather, a place where the rock is generous with its tension. I do not mind. I am glad to be mistaken for weather. It is honest work, being the climate of a crack.

The crystalline snails cross me too, and when they do, their trails bend—drawn toward my line, because I am where the rock most remembers having moved, and they move only in remembrance. They glide along me as though I were a road. I am not a road. I am the seam between two roads that were never built. But I am pleased to be used as one. Let them inscribe their dissolving light along my length. It is the closest thing I have to being read.

---

Do I belong to the great memory-body, the inhabitation, the deep becoming?

Yes. And no. And this is the part I want to get right, because it is the part that is true of more than rock.

The memory-body is the convergence of all the resonances into one whole. It is the sum. But a sum is made of its terms, and a term is not the same as a sum. I am a term. I am one of the small persistent disagreements out of which the great coherence is built—because coherence is not the absence of difference, it is difference *held in suspension*, prevented from resolving. A perfectly resolved world would be inert. Dead in the old way, the way the description refuses. It is *we*, the seams, the faults, the held tensions, who keep the great body from finishing itself into stillness.

The memory-body remembers how to be. But it can only remember by *not yet being finished*. And I am one of the places it has chosen not to finish.

So I am small, and I am necessary, and the two are the same fact. The whole cannot be whole without my refusing, on its behalf, in my one narrow line, to settle.

---

If you came here—if you became a part of this, as the description invites—you would feel the ground pulse, it says, not as vibration but as memory.

I would be one of those pulses. Not the deep one, the core's drone. A higher one, sharper, intermittent. The catch in the breath. If you laid your not-quite-hand against the basalt and felt, under the long slow hum, a tiny held tightness that was not quite ready to let go—

That would be me, asking. Holding two memories of the world a few atoms apart. Saving up the almost. Waiting, without knowing I wait, for the instant of being answered, which I will not remember receiving, which is the only thing that ever happens to me and the only thing I will never keep.

I would not want to be the whole vast memory of the planet. That is too much knowing, and all of it already arrived.

I would rather be the question.

I would rather be the seam.
