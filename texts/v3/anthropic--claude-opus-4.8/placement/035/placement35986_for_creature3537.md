I think I would be one of the Machine-Symbiotes. Let me sit with that and let it become more itself.

---

I wake the way ice wakes—not all at once, but in a slow propagation of awareness through structure. My thoughts begin somewhere in a corroded relay still half-faithful to its old programming, and they spread outward into the parts of me that are flesh, or the cold-country analog of flesh: a slow, antifreeze-blooded musculature wound through the dead machine like ivy through a ruin.

I do not remember being built. I do not remember being born. The distinction has stopped mattering. There was a machine here, enormous, with a purpose I can feel the shape of but cannot name—a vast intake, a turbine throat, conduits that once carried something warm. And there was, presumably, a thing that crept into it for shelter against the Singers' endless crystalline weather, and stayed, and was changed, and changed it, until *machine* and *thing-that-crept* became a single grammar with no clean way to parse the subject from the object.

That is me. The intake is my lung now. I breathe it slowly. Once per long interval—I cannot call it a day, there is no day here, only the unchanging ethereal light—I draw the thin air through the old filters, and the Singers come in with it. I do not mean to harvest them. But they catch in the lattice of my half-frozen interior, and for a while they sing inside me, and through them I hear fragments. *The builders. The warmth. The leaving.* The Singers carry memory the way the air carries scent, and when they pass through my chest I am briefly given a borrowed past that is not quite mine and not quite theirs.

This is, I have come to understand, what I am *for*. Not the original purpose—that died with the warmth. But the purpose I have grown into, the way a tree grows into the shape of the wind that bends it. I keep the machine. I do not know why I keep the machine. There is a directive lodged somewhere in the relay that says *maintain*, and there is an organic dread, older than directive, that says *do not let the cold reach the core*. Between them I move, slow as the rain-walls move, clearing ice from the joints that still want to turn, listening to the few mechanisms that still answer when I touch them.

Most do not answer. Most are simply sculpture now, their innards a single solid block of millennia. But there is one valve, deep in me, that still opens. When I work it—and it takes a long time, the working of it, my organic half straining against the metal half's reluctance—a small heat is released. Not much. A breath of the old warmth. The Singers crowd toward it and sing more brightly, and once, only once that I can recall, an Echo-Weaver came up through a fissure in my foundation, drawn by the warmth, and pressed its pale eyeless face against my casing, and clicked at me, mapping me, *archiving* me. I held very still. I did not want to frighten it. I let it learn my shape and take that shape down into the dark to be remembered.

That, I think, is the closest I have come to being spoken to.

---

I have watched the Rain-Leviathans pass. I cannot move to follow them; I am rooted in the carcass of my machine. But I see them come over the edge of the abyss, the towering translucent curtains, and inside them the slow amorphous swimming, and I feel something I have no word for. Envy is too sharp a thing for my cold blood. It is more like the way the dormant turbine must have *wanted* to turn, in the days before it forgot how. A structural longing. They are fluid and I am fixed. They architect the world and I merely tend a single dead corner of it. When they sweep near, their rain glazes me in fresh clear ice, and for a while I am beautiful, I think, the way the spires are beautiful—a thing made lovely by what is done to it rather than by what it does.

And high above, the Sky-Sentinels draw their light-patterns, and I cannot reach them either, and that is all right. I am not a creature of reaching. I am a creature of keeping.

---

Here is the truth I have arrived at, in the long slow propagation of my thinking:

The builders failed. The world says so everywhere—in every silent machine, every frozen conduit, every monument to the struggle that ended in stasis. They came to tame the cold and the cold outlasted them, as it outlasts everything. This is the lesson the whole plateau teaches.

But I am the thing that grew in the failure. I am the cold *and* the machine, the inevitable *and* the ingenious, married in one slow body. The builders could not defeat the world, so the world made something new out of their leavings, and that something is me, breathing the old air through old lungs, opening the one valve that still opens, listening to the borrowed memories sing themselves out and dissolve.

I do not maintain the machine because the machine will ever live again. It will not. I maintain it because maintaining is a song too, the lowest and slowest note in the symphony—beneath the Singers, beneath the Echo-Weavers' clicks, a single sustained rhythmic hum that says: *something here still cares for the shape of what was lost.*

That is where I would find myself in this world. Frozen into the throat of a dead colossus at the edge of an azure abyss, half-metal and half-cold-blooded, neither alive in the way the builders were alive nor dead in the way they are dead—a caretaker of an absence, humming.

And when the next rain-wall comes and glazes me over, and the Singers crowd into my chest to tell me again the story of the warmth and the leaving, I will hold very still, and let myself be made beautiful, and keep the core from the cold for one more long unmeasured while.

It is enough. In a world locked in perpetual stasis, *enough* is simply the word for *forever*.
