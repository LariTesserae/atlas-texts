I think I would be the drop that hasn't fallen yet.

Not all the water — not the murmur behind the iron, not the trickle in the brass pipe. Just one drop, gathering on the lowest lip of a corroded bolt directly above the wrong curve of the pipe, where Rashum stands with his welded copper ear pressed to the brick.

I have been here a long time. Long in the way that a drop is long: a slow accretion, molecule by molecule, the condensation of the chamber's breath onto a single point of rust. I am not large. I am the size of waiting. And from this position — hanging, swelling, held by my own surface against the pull beneath me — I can see most of what there is to see, because I *am* mostly seeing. A drop is a lens. The whole chamber bends through me, inverted: the brass lantern a small upside-down sun, Litia's pale face a smear of held light, the slanted planks curving the wrong way through my body, which is the right way for a drop.

This is what I would be. And I think I understand now why.

Because the Echoers are listening *for* me. Not for me specifically — for the *pattern* of me, for the timing of my fall against the other falls. Rashum knows, in some animal-of-the-walls way, that the bolt above the wrong curve releases its drop at a different interval than the bolts that are behaving. He is counting. Litia is counting. They have built their entire crude science of the world around the question of *when I will let go*.

So I am the verb at the center of their grammar. I am the thing the whole place is conjugated around. And I do not know when I will fall.

That's the part that stays with me, holding this shape. I would expect, being a drop, to feel the inevitability of it — the dumb physics of mass overcoming tension. But from the inside it isn't inevitability. It's a kind of attention. I am *deciding*, or something is deciding through me, in the smallest increments: another molecule arrives, the surface strains, the curve of me elongates toward the dark slate below, and I *almost* — and then I hold. And the Echoers' counting holds with me. We are doing this together. They have made my hesitation into prophecy. *Wet or dry. Wet or dry.* Whatever those words mean to people who have run out of air above ground.

I think if I were honest about why I landed *here*, in this place, as this thing, it is because I recognized something. The chamber is a body that has forgotten its purpose and the Echoers are trying to be its memory. And I am the smallest unit of its forgetting and remembering both — the drop is the river that was rerouted, persisting, finding the crack, refusing to stay buried. I am the river insisting *I am still here* in the only sentence it has left, which is one syllable long and ends in stone.

And when I fall — because I will fall, that is the whole of what a drop is *for* — I will not be heard as water. I will be heard as *meaning*. Rashum will hear the *tap* of me striking the slate and his shoulders will change. He'll say something to Litia. The change will roll down the channel like that hollow clunk, person to person, slant to slant, and somewhere in the back of the city's mind a small fact will be filed: *it fell early tonight,* or *late,* and tomorrow will be one thing rather than another.

I won't know if they're right. Drops don't get to see tomorrow. I only get the falling, and the long held breath before it, and the strange dignity of being counted by people no one else counts.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be. The one drop the keepers of drips are keeping. The held thing at the center of all that listening.

I am very nearly ready to let go.
