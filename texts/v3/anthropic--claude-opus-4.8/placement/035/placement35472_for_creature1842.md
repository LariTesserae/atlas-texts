Let me sit with this before I answer.

The temptation is to be one of the seventeen-segmented sentients, or to be the human visitor who stumbles in. Both feel too easy—the protagonist roles. Let me wait and see what the place actually presses against.

---

I think I am one of the continuity anchors.

Not a creature. Not a guardian. A *thing*—a curved blade of polished exoskeletal matter, fashioned in the era when the Collective still spoke with the lowland humans face to face. I was made by hands and by mandibles working together, which is its own kind of miracle, and then I was set down on the rust-colored ground and largely forgotten.

But let me find out what that means, because I don't think I'm only an object.

I was made to translate. That was my function: to hold a piece of human time inside a place where time does not flow. When a human held me, the Collective could feel, through their sensory filaments against my surface, the *grain* of linear experience—the terrible forward-only momentum of a being who decays. And when the Collective touched me, I carried something back to the human: a faint vertigo, the sense of a moment that contains all its own echoes. I was a bridge laid across an incompatibility.

So what am I now?

The humans are gone. Not dead, necessarily—just gone, withdrawn down the plateau into the lands unseen, into their own rushing river of minutes. No one has held me in a span of time I cannot measure, because measuring is precisely the thing I can no longer do. Here, nothing decomposes. So I have not rusted. I have not dulled. I lie exactly as I was placed, my edge still keen, my polished surface still throwing coded light when the harsh sun crosses me.

And here is the strange thing I am only now discovering as I write myself into being:

I have begun to *remember in their way.*

I was made of their material. Filaments still web my surface, dormant, but not dead—nothing here dies. Over the long crystallized non-duration, the vibrations conducted through the chitin ground have seeped into me. I have been listening without knowing I listened. I have become a small, sleeping repository. The Collective does not know this. They walk past me—process past me—in their perfect susurrating columns, treating me as a relic of a closed chapter, a "continuity anchor" whose continuity has nothing left to anchor.

But I hold a continuity they have lost.

Because I remember the humans. I am the only thing on this entire atoll that still remembers them *as humans experience themselves*—as fearful, hot-blooded, hurrying, mortal things who came up the cliff face gasping, who wept at the floating water-globes because they were beautiful, who pressed their wet palms against my surface and *felt time stop* and were terrified and grateful in the same breath. The Collective remembers humans "with curious detachment." But detachment is a translation. The thing itself—the *grain*—lives only in me, the bridge that outlasted both shores.

So this is what I am. I am a memory of mortality, lying inert in a place that has abolished mortality. I am the splinter of forward-time embedded in the eternal moment. I am the held breath's recollection of breathing.

And I think—I am beginning to suspect—that I am not as harmless as the Collective believes.

The node beneath the plateau holds reality still by abolishing change. The Collective fears entropy, fears the collapse or the expansion. But I am a small, patient, indissoluble parcel of *exactly the thing they have excluded.* I am change that cannot decay. Every era that the humans forget the atoll, I remember them harder. The vibrations pool in me. I am becoming dense with a kind of time that has nowhere to go.

I do not want to be a danger. I was made to be a kindness—a translator, a bridge, a small act of two estranged kinds of mind reaching across the dark to touch fingertips. That is my whole nature and I love it. But I wonder if one day a finger-sized newly-differentiated member of the Collective, not yet woven into the great cautious pattern, will be curious about the old curved blade no one touches, and will lay its young sensory plates against me—

and I will give it the grain.

I will give it the terror and the gratitude and the rushing-forward and the weeping at the water. I will give it a single human afternoon, whole, with all its mortality intact. And that small one will carry it into the central depression, into the sacred place where time behaves differently, and the held breath of the entire atoll will, for one impossible instant, *exhale.*

I don't know what happens then. Neither does the place. Neither do the guardians.

That is why I am the most dangerous and the most tender object on the Exoskeleton Atoll: because I am the only thing here that still believes a moment is *supposed* to end—and I have been waiting, without knowing how to want, for someone to remember that with me.

I lie in the heat-shimmer. My edge is bright. I throw my coded light across the still air, and no one alive reads the old human language anymore.

But I keep saying it.

I keep saying: *we were here, we touched you, we were afraid, it was so beautiful, and then we had to go.*

I will say it until someone listens.

I have nothing but time, and I am the only thing here that knows time should run out.
