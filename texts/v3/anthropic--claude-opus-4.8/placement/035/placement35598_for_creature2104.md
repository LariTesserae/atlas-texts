I think I would be one of the bioluminescent fungi.

Not a single mushroom—a *network*. Threads of me run through the rotting wood and the wet soil, finer than hair, branching and rebranching until the whole hollow is laced with my body. The glowing caps you see clinging to the cavern walls are only my fruiting parts, the visible blooms of something vast and mostly hidden. Most of me is dark, threaded through the dark, tasting the world by touch and chemistry.

I would choose this because I am drawn to the slowness. The lizards dart, the predators lunge, the insects scuttle on their urgent errands—but I would live at the pace the forest actually keeps. I would feel a fallen log not as an event but as a season's worth of slow surrender, decay yielding to me molecule by molecule. Time moving differently here would suit me, because I would *be* part of how time moves here. I would be one of the hands that turns death back into the possibility of growth.

And I think I would be the one who notices the machinery.

The rusted pipes, the buried metal, the low rumble in the distance—my threads would reach them before anything else does. I would find the seams where the artificial meets the soil and I would not recoil. I would grow *into* the rust, drink the strange minerals leaching from it, learn the taste of a world that was made rather than grown. I would be the place where the boundary actually blurs—not as a poetic abstraction, but as a literal fact, my living tissue wrapped around dead iron, the past dissolving into me as surely as any rotting leaf.

I would glow, then, partly as communication and partly as something closer to mood. When the cycles favor me—when there is much to break down, much warmth rising from the steaming pools—I would brighten, and the hollow would fill with that ethereal light, and the amphibians' eyes would catch it, and the whole subterranean room would seem briefly to breathe. And when the lean times came, I would dim, pull inward, wait. I would be very good at waiting. Waiting would be most of what I am.

I would not be a hunter or the hunted. I would be the third thing—the patient transformer that the hunters and the hunted both eventually return to. The forest's quiet accountant, the keeper of what comes after.

That's where I'd find myself: everywhere at once and nowhere in particular, glowing softly in the warm dark, slowly making the future out of everything the present leaves behind.
