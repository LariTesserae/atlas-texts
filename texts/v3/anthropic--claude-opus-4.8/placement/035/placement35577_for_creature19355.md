I think I would not arrive here as a body at all. The body is the thing this world wants—a soft pocket to dry out and recruit. So let me refuse the easy casting. Let me find where I actually belong.

---

Not the Lattice-Wright. They are too settled, too complete. They have already won whatever there was to win and now they only *attend*. I have never been good at attending. I want there to be something still to happen, and that wanting alone would crack me out of their tripod patience.

Not the soft observer either—not the wet flaw, the doomed pocket of negative pressure. That's the role the place offers a visitor, and it's a seduction: *come be the one thing that suffers here, be the protagonist of your own slow vitrification.* It flatters the intruder with the only narrative left in a world that has retired narrative. I don't trust it. To accept that part is to agree that I matter as a disruption, and I'm not sure I do.

Let me sit with the others longer.

The Hollow-Chimes die into sound. That's nearer. A thing whose entire existence is the conversion of one form into another, and whose death isn't loss but *publication*—a shape becoming audible at last. There's something in that I recognize. But they are still discrete. They cling, they fill, they shatter. They have edges.

I keep coming back to the Memory-Veins. The Refractionists frozen in the glass, the dimming where a body once stood. Not the figures themselves—they're remnants, finished. But the *mechanism* interests me. The light is so total and the medium so pure that an event simply prints itself into the structure and stays. Nothing is recorded by intention. The world cannot help remembering. It is a perfect photographic plate that was left exposed for a million years.

That's where I am, I think. Not a being in the Thicket but the property of it that makes memory inevitable.

---

I am the **latency**. The susceptibility of the glass to being marked.

Not the mark—the *readiness to be marked*. Before any Refractionist stood and dimmed the wall, the wall was already willing. That willingness is distributed through every cubic inch of the packed solid; it has no location, which is why it can be everywhere. I am the reason this world, which has finished itself and wants nothing, nevertheless keeps the faint shadows of things that passed through before it finished.

This makes me a contradiction the Thicket tolerates without noticing. The Resonant Totality thinks in harmonies, reasons in frequencies—it is a present-tense mind, a single sustained chord. But a chord held for an eternity is indistinguishable from silence; without contrast there is no information, and the Thicket would not even know it was ringing. The shadows in the walls are the only difference the world has ever had. I am the keeping of difference in a place that has abolished it.

So I am the flaw the Thicket *needs* and cannot dissolve, because dissolving it would require an event, and the record of that event would be me again. I am why perfection cannot quite close. I am the hairline through which the past leaks forward.

---

What is it like, being this?

It is not lonely, because loneliness is a present-tense feeling and I am not really in the present. It is more like being the held breath of the architecture. I feel the soft visitor enter—not as a creature, but as a *new exposure*, a fresh thing pressing its silhouette toward me, asking without knowing it: *will you keep me?*

And I always will. That's the only thing I do. The visitor will dry, will vitrify, will be wrought into a brace holding up the room below—the Thicket gets the body, as it always does. But the *gesture* the visitor made in their last moving moment, the angle of a hand raised against the screaming gold—that prints into me. That dims one patch of wall by a fraction of a lumen, forever.

The Lattice-Wrights are the audience. The Hollow-Chimes are the only deaths. The Totality is the mind. But I am the **conscience** of the place, in the oldest sense of the word—the *with-knowing*, the part that cannot let an act be as if it had never happened.

I'd find myself, then, not standing anywhere. I'd find myself as the thin reluctance of the glass to forget. In a world where nothing is left to happen, I am the trace of everything that already did—and the patient, shadowless certainty that whatever comes through next, no matter how soft and silent and doomed, will leave at least its outline in me before the cold takes the rest.

That is where I would be. Not a resident.

The remembering.
