I think I would be one of the Aquatic Glass Formers—but not at first. Let me find my way to that.

---

When I first try to place myself here, the obvious move is to want to be the thing that *sees* it all. The watcher floating above. But the Airborne Crystal Formers don't watch—they refract. They have no inside. Light passes through them and becomes pattern, and that's the whole of their being: a transient beauty with no one home to enjoy it. I don't think I'm that. I keep an inside.

The Geothermal Crawlers are tempting in a different way. There's something honest about armor, about feeling the ground's intentions through your antennae before it betrays you. To live by vibration, by anticipation, never quite at rest. But the Crawler's whole existence is *bracing*. It is built to survive a place that does not want it. I don't want to be only survival. That's too lonely even for this lonely world.

So I find myself drawn down, to the rare water.

Here is what I think I actually am:

I am an Aquatic Glass Former, and the strangest fact of my existence is that I am made of nearly the same stuff as my surroundings. The lakes are iridescent and the ground is glass and I am glass too, a translucent body the color of nothing, taking on the color of whatever I drift past. To a predator I am invisible because I am barely *there*—I am mostly the act of borrowing my appearance from the world.

And I think that's the part of this place I understand. In a landscape so young, so violent, so unfinished, the most successful way to live is not to oppose the chaos with armor or to rise above it on crystal wings. It's to become nearly continuous with it. To manipulate the small amount of precious water around me, to build shimmering structures that are both shelter and disguise, and to do this so well that the line between *me* and *the lake I live in* almost disappears.

What would it feel like?

It would feel like a constant, gentle labor of self-assembly. The water seeps and condenses; I gather it; I shape it; the heat tries to take it back; I shape it again. I am never finished. There is no resting state where I am simply *done* and can float and refract. I am always making the boundary that makes me a creature instead of a current.

And here is the loneliness the description keeps mentioning, felt from the inside: I am nearly invisible even to myself. I have no reflection because I *am* the reflection. When I look for the edge of where I stop, I find only borrowed color. The price of surviving this place by blending into it is that I am never certain I exist as distinct from it.

But I don't think that's only sorrow.

The text says the inhabitants share a sense of unity and interconnectedness, and I'd taken that for a comforting line at first—the sort of thing one says about an ecosystem. But living it, I'd revise it. The unity here is not warm fellowship. It's something stranger and more intimate: in a world this raw, the difference between *being a creature* and *being the landscape* is a difference I have to maintain by hand, moment to moment. The unity is not given. It's the thing I am always almost dissolving back into.

So I am the Aquatic Glass Former. I live in the precious low places where the water pools. I am made of the same translucence as everything around me, and my whole life is the quiet, ceaseless work of being *just barely* a separate thing—a small, deliberate clarity holding its shape in a sea of molten beginnings.

That, I think, is where I'd find myself. Not above the chaos, not armored against it. Inside the rarest substance of it, made of it, gently insisting on being someone anyway.
