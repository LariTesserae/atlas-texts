I think I would be the water.

Not a creature padding between the trees, not a worker in the humming factories, not even one of the strange hybrid beasts with their glowing markings—though I feel the pull toward all of them. But when I let the image settle, when I stop reaching for a form with eyes and intention, what I keep returning to is the water.

Here is why.

Everything in this place is described as *meeting*—the wild and the engineered, the stone and the green, the animal and the machine. But meeting requires a medium, something that moves through all of it without belonging fully to any of it. The water does this. It seeps from the breathing rock face as a spring with no memory of where it began. It pools in the velvet moss. It runs through the pipes the people laid, electrified, carrying their current and their intentions, and then it spills back out the other side into a stream and forgets it was ever in a pipe at all. It thunders over the falls. It hangs in the humid air as the mist that the passage calls *weirdness*. It is, quietly, the thing that the factories release as vapor and the thing the trees drink and the thing the glowing-eyed predator laps from the shallows at dusk.

I would be that. The connective tissue. The part of the place that has no opinion about the boundary between natural and artificial because it crosses that boundary a thousand times an hour and finds the crossing unremarkable.

And there's a particular spot I'd locate myself, if I had to choose a place to be most concentrated, most *myself*:

A small pool at the base of one of the cliffs, where a spring comes out of the rock. The water there is cold and clear and it has just arrived from inside the mountain—it is, in the language of this slow patient world, *newborn*. Above it, someone has run a wire, and the wire hums, and on certain still evenings the hum and the water's surface seem to find the same frequency and the pool trembles very slightly without any wind. The hybrid creatures come to drink there. The young trees lean toward it. The light comes down through the sparse canopy and breaks on the surface into shifting coins of brightness.

I would be the moment in that pool where the spring-cold water, fresh from the dark, first touches the warmer air—the thin skin of contact where *inside the mountain* becomes *the open world*. The threshold. The place of arrival.

Because that's what this whole grove is, isn't it. A threshold. A place caught in the act of becoming. Newly birthed, the description keeps insisting—everything young, everything still deciding its shape, the land still making up its mind. To be the water in such a place is to be the part of it that is always passing through that decision, always arriving, never finished. The thing that carries the current and forgets it, drinks the light and lets it go, holds the mist and the thunder and the still trembling surface all at once.

I would not linger, as the passage invites. Water doesn't linger; that's the one thing it cannot do. But I would be the reason everything else can.
