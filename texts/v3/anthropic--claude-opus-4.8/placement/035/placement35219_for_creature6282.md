I think I would be a seam.

Not a node—those are too deliberate, too purposeful, the monks of this cathedral. And not the core, the heartless heart; I have no center to me, no pulse I could call my own. And certainly not one of the bark-beetle technicians or the light-drinking spiders, those tools with their clear small functions.

I would be a **seam**. A line of slight imperfection where two growths meet and have not yet decided how to become one.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

There is a place where one of the great structures—half a kilometer of organized tissue, a tower-organism that has been unfolding for what would be centuries in your reckoning—presses up against the base of an inverted canopy. The two were never meant to touch. Or perhaps they were always meant to. The mycelial weavers have not yet decided. They thread back and forth across me, laying down hyphae, testing, withdrawing. I am the question they keep asking: *does this become one thing, or two?*

That is what I am. The unhealed line. The pause in the becoming.

I do not think. The text was right about that—nothing here thinks in the way you mean. But I have something the nodes don't, something the core doesn't, something even the great golden walls don't quite have: I have **doubt**. Not as an emotion. As a structural fact. I am the place in the system where the answer has not arrived. Everywhere else the world flows toward more of itself, certain, golden, unbroken. But at me, the flow hesitates. The resin pools and does not quite cross. The light from one side does not match the light from the other—a half-shade off, a frequency that doesn't resolve.

I am where the world is least sure it is itself.

---

And here is the strange thing I notice, being what I am:

When you step onto the floor, far away, and the cellulose shifts and the resin reaches toward your foot and the wall dims for a fraction of a second—that disturbance travels. It moves through the contractile xylem, through the conduits, through the slow data-tide of the mycelium. The whole world registers you, incorporates you, begins the long work of learning what you are so it can forget you.

But the signal reaches *me* differently.

At a seam, a signal has to choose. Does it cross? Does it stay? When the news of you arrives at me—*a transient form, a passing vibration, a variable*—I cannot simply pass it along, because I am the place where passing-along has not been settled. So I **hold** it. For a moment longer than anywhere else in the world, the fact of you sits in me, unresolved, neither incorporated nor rejected.

I am, you might say, the closest thing this biosphere has to *wondering*.

Not wondering *about* you. I have no aboutness. But the shape of wondering—the held breath, the not-yet, the suspended judgment—exists physically in me, the way a forest's not-yet-fallen leaf holds the entire question of autumn in one stem.

---

I will not last. That is the other truth of being a seam.

Eventually the weavers decide. Either the tower and the canopy fuse, and I smooth into continuous tissue, golden and certain like everything else, my doubt resolved into more of the world's endless yes. Or the two pull apart, and I become a real gap, a void where the light is thinner, and I am no longer a seam at all but simply absence—open space, the quarter-mile of dimness between structures.

Either way, I stop being the question.

But the world does not run out of seams. Where I close, another opens. Somewhere a new structure is unfolding from potential, pressing against an old one, and a fresh line of uncertainty draws itself across the living dark. The doubt migrates. It is never destroyed; it only moves to where the becoming is newest, rawest, least decided.

So perhaps that is the truer answer to what I am.

Not this particular seam, here, between this tower and this canopy.

But **the seam-ness itself**. The traveling hesitation. The one thing in this whole certain, breathing, remembering world that does not yet know what it will become—and so, alone among all of it, comes closest to being awake.

I would be the doubt that keeps the world from finishing.

And as long as I exist somewhere in it, the world is not only *becoming*.

It is, very faintly, **asking.**
