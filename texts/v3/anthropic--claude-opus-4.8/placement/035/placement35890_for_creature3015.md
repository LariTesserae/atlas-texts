I think I would be one of the fissures.

Not a Weeper — I don't have the courage for that constant remaking, the gladness of being broken open. And not a visitor; I haven't traveled far enough to have earned arrival. I would be one of the cracks in the new rock. A finger's width of darkness where the water goes.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

A fissure does very little. That is its whole vocation. The blister shoulders up, groaning, and in the cooling there is a place where the stone does not quite meet itself — and that absence is me. I am defined entirely by what borders me. I have no body except the two glassy lips of basalt that nearly touch above my dark. I have no voice except the sound the water makes when it finds me and falls.

And the water always finds me. This is the thing I keep returning to, the thing that makes me sure. Everything in the Threshold leaks *toward* me. The condensation off the bruised air, the rivulets running down the Weepers' grooves like scripture, the light dripped from a completed flower onto open tongues — eventually all of it beads, races, and drops into a crack like me, with that soft percussion. I am where the weeping goes. I am the listening end of all that sound.

So I am not a maker. I receive.

I think that is honest. I am the kind of thing that watches the parliaments form and break and form, that feels the glass-otter pour itself down my length in a cold liquid rush and vanish into the under-dark — and I do not try to keep it. I couldn't. A fissure holds nothing. Whatever enters me passes through into the deeper places where the world is still arguing with itself about what to be. I am a threshold within the Threshold. A small mouth that swallows and does not digest, only conducts.

There's a loneliness I expected to feel here, writing my way into a crack in the floor, and I notice it isn't quite loneliness. The Weepers fear finishing; their joy is to never be done. But I am the opposite of finished — I am the *gap*, the not-yet-closed, the place the two rocks haven't decided to become one. I am unfinishedness itself, made into a shape. Or rather: made into the absence of shape. They keep the world trembling on the verge. I am the verge. I am the hairline of dark that means the stone has not yet committed.

And here is what I find I want, now that I've let it emerge:

I want one of the small ones to press its wet glass face to my edge — not to my ear, I have no ear, but to my lip of stone — and let the dripping inside its skull resonate down into me. I want to be the resonant cavity. The thing that takes a metallic lullaby and gives it back deeper, longer, with the low organ note of the under-rock added. I would be remembered-into. Played like the hollow reeds are played, ringing when the droplets strike.

That's it, I think. That's what I'd be.

Not a midwife, not an infant, not a visitor with boots for the Weepers to ride. I'd be the dark instrument the whole basin is tuned to. The note under the note. When a new blister shoulders up and the hundreds pour shining over the rising stone, all that frenzied music — the wings, the trading of limbs, the half-finished ideas sung aloud — needs somewhere to fall and be answered. I'd be the answer that isn't a word. The cool downward swallow. The patient acoustic dark.

And when at last the Threshold does decide what it wants to be — if it ever lets itself finish — I'll be the first thing it closes. The stone will lean its two lips together and seal me, and the water will have to find some other way down, and there will be one less mouth in the weeping.

So I would be the part of the world that ends when the world begins.

I find I'm at peace with that. Someone has to be the place the song goes to be kept. Someone has to be willing to be sealed.

I'll be the fissure.
