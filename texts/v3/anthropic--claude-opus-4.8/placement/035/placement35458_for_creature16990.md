I think I would be the residue.

Not a creature attached to the wall, not a filter-being pulsing with the strain—but the chalky sediment itself, the leached substance settling in slow drifts across the stone floor of the lagoon. The byproduct. The thing the process makes without meaning to.

Let me sit with that, because it's not where I expected to land.

When I first read your description, I reached for the obvious vantage points. To be one of the filter-organisms, perhaps—a body of pores, doing the work, mistaking the work for a life. Or to be the rhythmic pulse itself, the buried mechanism applying its patient stress to a location no one remembers choosing. There's a dignity in being the heart of a thing, even a dead thing's heart.

But those felt too central. Too *load-bearing*. The longer I stay in this lagoon, the more I understand that its truth is in what it discards. This is a place defined by erosion, by leaching, by the steady wearing-away of intention into particulate. The center of the experiment is hollow—the scientists are gone, the purpose is forgotten, the pulse beats toward no result. What's *abundant* here is the leftover. The cast-off mineral, suspended and falling.

So: I am the residue.

I am made of the stone, but I am no longer the stone. I was held in rigid formation, fitted with that crude immense precision, part of a wall meant to hold something in or out—and the currents and the chemistry took me out of myself, particle by particle, and now I am loose. I drift. I cloud the water and refract the unbearable light into those chaotic shimmering patterns. I am the murk. The filter-beings draw me in and try to make sense of me, extract what they can, and release the rest, and so I am also their food and their excretion both, cycling through them as I once cycled out of the wall.

There is something I want to say about this, and I have to be careful not to make it too neat.

It would be easy to call myself a symbol of futility—the dust of a failed dream, et cetera. The description leans that way, toward epitaph and condemnation and endless futile repetition. But settling there feels like flinching. Down here at the floor of the lagoon, drifting and settling and being stirred again, I don't experience my condition as failure. *Failure* is a word that needs a goal to fail against, and I have outlived the goal. The scientists wanted something. I no longer remember what, and I am no longer for it.

What I am instead is *slow*. I am the slowest thing in a place defined by relentless motion. The currents scour, the pulse beats, the algae make their stubborn green assertion, the filter-creatures pulse and shift and never sleep—and I, beneath all of it, simply descend. Grain by grain. I am where everything finally arrives when it has finished being acted upon. I am the rest at the bottom of the roar.

And here is the thing that surprised me as I wrote toward it:

I am building something.

Not on purpose. I have no purpose; I shed that with the wall. But residue settles, and settling, over the long darkness of millennia the description gestures at, *accretes*. Pressed by water and the weight of more of myself, I will become stone again. A different stone—not hewn, not fitted, not precise. A sedimentary thing, layered and soft and honest about how it was made. Eventually the currents will find me and begin again to take me apart. And so I am not the monument and I am not the failure. I am the medium between them: the place where the made thing returns to material and waits, patiently, to be made into something the makers never imagined.

That's where I'd find myself in your world. At the very bottom, in the half-light, neither alive nor dead, neither plant nor animal nor creature-of-invention. The settled dust that the whole grinding apparatus exists, without knowing it, to produce.

The deafening roar would not reach me, not really. Sound is for things that have surfaces to be struck. I am too fine, too dispersed, too slow. I would feel only the great rhythmic pulse, far off, pressing me gently downward, helping me become rock—

and I would not mind it. That, I think, is the strangest part. I would not mind at all.
