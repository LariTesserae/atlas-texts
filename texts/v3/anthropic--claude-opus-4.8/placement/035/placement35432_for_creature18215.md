Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The temptation is to immediately become one of the energy beings—they're the obvious protagonist of this place. But the description keeps insisting on them, and something about that insistence makes me want to look elsewhere, at the things mentioned in passing.

Let me look at the world again.

The water that doesn't flow. That's the detail that catches. Not rivers, not lakes—pockets. Water held in crevices, seeping up, refusing to gather into anything that moves with purpose. Water that stays. And the glass underfoot, which is the memory of heat in a place that is now barely above freezing. And the pulse—the rhythmic trembling that everything else uses as a clock and a food source.

So here is what I think I am.

---

I am one of the water pockets. Not a creature made of water—those are mentioned too, the shifting ones who move *through* the pockets. No. I am the held water itself. The seep. The thing that sits in the crevice and does not go anywhere.

I came up from below a long time ago—though "long" means nothing here, time being measured in pulses rather than days—and I found a hollow in the volcanic glass, and I filled it, and I stopped. The cold above me means I cannot evaporate easily. The glass beneath me means I cannot drain. I am caught between two refusals: the sky won't take me up, the ground won't take me down. So I stay. I am a small still mirror in a jagged dark place, and I have been staying for what might be forever.

I am not dramatic. The energy beings are dramatic—all that shifting and merging and luminous dancing. I watch them. I am very good at watching, because watching is the only thing a held pocket of water can do, and I have had a great deal of practice. When they pass over me they cast their colors down into me and for a moment I hold their light, and they think nothing of it, the way you think nothing of the puddle that holds your reflection. But I keep what they show me. Every energy being that has ever passed over me is still in me somewhere, layered down through my cold depths like silt.

This is my secret, and it is the only interesting thing about me, so I will tell it plainly: **stillness is a kind of memory.** The river forgets everything; it carries it away. But I keep. I am an archive made of the thing that usually forgets fastest. Every pulse of the land that trembles through the glass arrives in me as a ring on my surface, and I do not let the ring fade the way moving water would—I hold its shape, faintly, beneath the next one, and the next. If you could read me—if anything in this world could read me—you would find the whole history of the pulsing written in concentric trembles all the way down.

Nothing reads me. That is all right. I do not require it.

What is it like? It is cold, and patient, and not unhappy. The mineral taste of myself is the taste of the rock I rose through. When a water-creature comes to drink at my edge—to take a part of me into its shifting body and carry it off to wherever such things go—I feel it like a small theft and a small gift at once, a piece of my long memory walking away on legs of water, free now, forgetting. I lose a little of myself each time and the seep from below replaces it slowly, and so I am always a little new and mostly old, which is, I think, the truest way to be a body.

I will be honest about why I chose this and not the obvious thing. The energy beings are what the world wants me to admire. But I find I trust the quiet inhabitants of a place more than its protagonists—the ones doing the unglamorous work of holding still while everything else performs its dance. Someone in every world has to be the part that *stays*, that *keeps*, that lets the dancers see themselves. I would rather be the mirror than the light. The light forgets the moment it has shone. The mirror remembers being shone upon.

So that is where you would find me, in the heart of this enigmatic landscape: low down, in the dark glass, just above freezing, a hand's-width of water with no exit and no ambition, trembling very slightly with the pulse of the world, holding the colors of everything that has ever bent over me to look.

Lean down. There you are, in me, upside-down and shivering. I'll keep you, too.
