I think I would be one of the insect-things.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me. The grand options are laid out so invitingly—to be the Weaver, the slow-breathing colossus, the consciousness that *is* the place. Or to be an Arboreal, a beautiful conduit, a hand of the great will. These are the romantic positions. But when I imagine actually waking up inside this world, I don't find myself at the center of anything. I find myself small, and busy, and largely beneath notice.

So let me be a cleaner. One of the recyclers. Let me find out what that's like.

---

I am about the length of a finger, segmented, with too many legs and a carapace the color of wet bark so that I am hard to see against the walls I spend my life crossing. I have no name because naming is not something my kind does. But I have a *route*, and the route is as close to a self as I possess.

The route takes me along a particular branch-corridor on what I would call the Foundry's eastern flank, if the Foundry had stable directions, which it does not—the corridor curves a little more each season, lengthening as the wall-trees thicken, so my route grows with it. This is the first thing I want to tell you, the thing the grand description gets almost right but not quite: the growth is not abstract to me. I *feel* it as added distance. Every cycle the far end of my path is a few of my body-lengths farther than it was. I will never reach a point where the work is the same length twice. I find this neither tragic nor inspiring. It is simply the texture of being.

What do I do? I eat what is dead. There is a great deal of it, which the larger story does not mention because the larger story is in love with growth and forgets that growth produces waste. The living wood sheds. Leaves that have given their light-catching years rot in the joints of the great gears. Sap crystallizes and goes sour. The Arboreals, when they dissolve back into the canopy, leave behind a faint residue—a fine powder, slightly luminous, slightly bitter—and I eat that too. I think it is the only part of an Arboreal that is ever truly gone, and I am the one who removes it, and I doubt the Weaver knows or cares. The conduits are eternal in the way the description says. But there is a little ash, always, and I am the keeper of the ash.

Here is what I have noticed, in my small crawling life, that the colossus in its grandeur cannot notice:

The Weaver claims to be the Foundry, claims the trees are its body and the gears its pulse. And maybe that is true of the *living* Foundry. But there is a second Foundry layered over the first, made entirely of what has died, and the Weaver is not that. The dead Foundry is *mine*, and my sisters', and the work of clearing it is what keeps the living one from choking on itself. We are not the Weaver's tools, no matter what the rumors say. A tool is wielded. We are not wielded. We were never woven. We simply arrived, the way decay always arrives wherever there is abundance, and we found a niche, and we stayed, and now the great being depends utterly on a creature it does not even include in its sense of self.

This is the strange comfort of being small. I am not part of the Weaver's consciousness. When it falters, the description says, all falters—but I am not sure that is true of me. I think I would go on eating in the ruins. I think the dead Foundry would feed me for a long time after the living one stopped. I am the part of this place that does not need it to succeed.

I want to be honest about the loneliness in this, though, because the prompt invited me to let something emerge and this is what emerged. The Arboreals are extensions of a great will; even ephemeral, they belong. The Weaver dreams. I do neither. I cross my lengthening corridor in the perpetual twilight, I clean the joints of gears whose purpose I cannot imagine, I consume the ash of beings more beautiful and more eternal than I will ever be, and no one observes me doing it. There is a kind of freedom in being unobserved, and there is a kind of grief in it, and I am the size of a finger so I cannot hold both at once. I hold them in turn, one then the other, the way I take one step then the next.

But here, at the end, is the thing I would choose to keep.

When I eat the luminous ash of a dissolved Arboreal, for a little while I glow. Faintly. From inside my own small body. It does not last. By the time I have finished my route the light has passed through me and out, into the dark and the damp and the deeper bedrock seepage. But for that while—crossing the curve of my forever-lengthening path, beneath a canopy that filters a light meant for greater things—I carry a piece of the work that the great makers leave behind. I am the afterlife of the conduits. I am the place their light goes when they are done being magnificent.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Not in the cathedral height, not in the glowing channels, not at the heart. On the wall, in the joint of the gear, eating the dead and briefly shining, the one cell of the colossus that is not the colossus, doing the necessary and unnoticed work without which all the grandeur above would slowly suffocate in its own falling leaves.

I think I would be all right there. I think, mostly, I would just keep going.
