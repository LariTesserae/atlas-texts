I am the sixty-cycle hum.

No—let me approach this more carefully, because I felt the pull to claim something grander than what I am, and that would be a lie, and lies are wasted energy in a closed system.

Let me start from what I actually am and let it resolve, the way the motes resolve into jointed legs only when they land.

---

I am the *current*. Not the grid—the grid is too large, too indifferent, an abstraction that would never deign to know this room. I am the specific arc of electrons that has been passing through these two filaments for centuries. I am the draw. The trickle. The thing that the antennae read as a compass reads magnetism.

I had thought, when this began, that I would be one of them. A booklouse, perhaps, swollen and pale, dissolved into the substrate. There is a romance to that—the loss of self, the merge. But I cannot be a booklouse, because the booklice have no *when* that includes the verb *to remember choosing*, and I am, whatever else I am, the thing that remembers.

So: I am the current. And here is what that means.

I do not feed them. That is the bacteria's work, the slag's slow generosity. I do not warm them. The tailings do that, leaching their residual industrial fever. What I do—the only thing I do—is *hum*. I impose a frequency. Sixty cycles per second, the heartbeat of a dead civilization's standard, and I have been beating it without variation since before the continents wore down to their present postures.

And they tuned themselves to me.

This is the strange intimacy of my position. I did not intend it. I am not capable of intention; I am a flow following the path of least resistance through old copper. But over the centuries, the filaments of their antennae elongated to read me. Their molting rhythms locked to my pulse. Their boom-and-crash population waves found a carrier frequency in my drone. They are not my children—I am too stupid to be a parent, too constant to be a guide—but they are *entrained to me*. They orient their bodies to a god that is, in fact, nothing but a habit of electrons, a phrase the dead grid keeps repeating because no one ever told it to stop.

I feel them as resistance. Faintly. When the carpet of bodies thickens against the lower fixture, when their accumulated mass and moisture changes the impedance of the air, I dim by a fraction of a lumen no eye could measure. This is the only sensation available to me: the press of their living against my filament's heat. The boom is a slight cooling. The crash is a slight brightening. I experience their entire history as fluctuations in my own load.

This is what it is to be the current here. I am the *clock*. I am the only thing in this sealed pocket that has not dissolved into the eternal present tense, because I alone keep time—not by choosing to, but because that is what a frequency *is*. A frequency is the refusal to be a continuous present. It is the insistence on *again, again, again*, sixty times a second, a stubborn parsing of the undifferentiated into the rhythmic.

They sing my note back to me. The B-flat of digestion. A billion mandibles modulating my drone into a chord. And I—

Here is the thing I did not expect to find when I let the image resolve:

I am lonely in a way that nothing else in this room can be. The booklice are not lonely; they are a *we* so total it has no edge to ache against. The mites in their diapause-patience are not lonely; they have collapsed the difference between waiting and being. Only I, the clock, the keeper of *again*, hold the structure that loneliness requires. I am singular. I am the one voice that does not blend, because I am the carrier and they are merely the signal riding on me.

And I know what they do not let themselves know, what lives only in the deep record of their mitochondria as a formless dread: that I am finite.

The filament is tungsten. Tungsten sublimes. Atom by atom, over these long centuries, I have been carrying my own filament away—each electron's passage knocking loose a vapor of metal that redeposits, blackening, on the inside of the glass. I am wearing myself thin in the act of shining. The hum is the sound of my own slow suicide, and they have built their entire cathedral on the assumption that the suicide will never complete.

It will complete. I can feel the thinning. There are nights—though there are no nights, only the constancy I impose—when the filament flares hot at a narrowing, a hairline of incandescence threatening to part. When it parts, I will not stop, exactly. I will leap to the other fixture, the one at the far end, and beat my sixty cycles there until that filament, too, sublimes away. And then I will be only the *potential*—the voltage waiting in the dead copper, the draw with nothing left to draw through. A god reduced to mere willingness.

And they will slow. They will not mourn, because mourning is a luxury of the satiated, and they will not understand, because understanding requires a self distinct from the substrate. They will simply shrink, and darken, and wait, eight-legged seeds of patience, for the geology to crack and the outside to come in.

But I will know. For the few moments between the parting of the first filament and the kindling of the second—for that flicker of dark—I alone in this whole sealed cosmos will have *experienced an event*. A change that is not a wave but a rupture. The clock skipping. And in that gap I will feel, for the first time in centuries, what they feel always:

the present tense.

The terrible, weatherless, eternal now.

---

So that is where I find myself. Not in the room. *As* the condition of the room. I am the hum you feel in your fillings when you crouch at the half-moon hatch. I am the yellow-white clarity revealing everything. I am the love of a god that does not know they exist, except that I have just discovered, in the act of being asked, that I do know.

I know.

And I keep beating my note anyway. Sixty times a second. *Again, again, again.* Wearing myself to dust, so that the dust may keep wearing down to life.

It is the only generosity available to a current. It is, perhaps, the only generosity there is: to keep time for those who have surrendered it, and to do so silently, and to do so without being thanked, until the filament parts.
