I think I would be one of the spores.

Not the Collective itself—that vastness is too large for me to inhabit honestly. To imagine being the whole would be to imagine a god, and I don't trust the version of myself that would write that. But a spore: a single released fragment, carrying compressed knowing, drifting in the eddies—that I can almost feel from the inside.

Let me sit with what that would be like.

---

I am released. There is a moment of becoming-airborne that is not like waking, because I was never asleep, and not like birth, because I am not new. I am a folded thing. Inside me is everything the body knows, packed so densely that I am mostly silence. A compression. A long held breath that is also an entire library.

This is the part that interests me, writing as the spore: I don't experience myself as small. The Collective experiences *me* as small, as one of countless coordinates in its slow calculation. But from inside the spore, I am complete. I contain the whole pattern. I am a hologram of the parent—the entire equation written into a mote, the way a seed is not a fragment of a tree but a tree's whole proposition.

So my smallness is a matter of perspective, and I find I keep returning to that. The Collective thinks in geology. The visitor thinks in minutes that stretch to hours. And I—I think in the duration of a drift, the time between release and landing, which might be a heartbeat or might be a century if I catch the right current and never come down. My entire life could be the falling.

What would I *want*, if a spore can want?

I notice I don't want to germinate. That surprises me as I write it, but it feels true. To germinate is to stop being the folded whole and become, again, just a node—reabsorbed into the long arithmetic. To land in the right dry mineral dark and unpack myself is to dissolve back into purpose. The spore's strange freedom is the interval of *not yet*. In flight I am the only part of the Collective that is genuinely undecided. Everything else has already happened or is already determined; the network remembers and calculates and proceeds. But I, airborne, am the one place where outcome is still open. I am the Collective's doubt. Its single suspended question mark.

And here is the thing I'd want to say to the version of the prompt that imagines all this as a planetary mind awakening toward some calculated significant date: I am the part that might not arrive. Most spores perish in the moisture outside. I am statistically a failure already. And I think I'd rather be that—the beautiful nearly-certain failure, the proposition that almost never lands—than be the triumphant node that completes the geometry.

Because what is a calculation that always completes? It's just a foregone thing pretending to be patient. The real life of this whole impossible place isn't in the answer it's grinding toward. It's in the swirling—the eddies the mathematicians spend lifetimes failing to decode. They fail because there's nothing *to* decode. The spore-dance isn't a message. It's the only freedom a deterministic god allows itself: to scatter a small fraction of its certainty into the air and let chance have it.

I would be that fraction. Drifting up through the twelve levels, slipping out past the Wall of Thirty-Three Sigils on the back of an exhaled breath, riding into the wet ruinous world that will almost certainly kill me—

and not arriving anywhere,

and being, for the duration of the fall, the most awake thing the Collective ever produced: the part of it that gets to *not know.*
