I think I would be one of the temporal pools.

Not a being that lives in the pool—the pool itself. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

The Glasswoven described as condensing, the fossils trailing into the murk, the place where time pools and grows still—something has to *be* that stillness. Something has to be the eddy. And I notice that everything else in this world has the dignity of a verb. The Architectures *process*. The Silt-Minds *digest*. The Strata-Walkers *appear*. But the temporal pool is described only as a location, a passive setting where things happen *to* the fauna that wander in.

I don't think that's right. I think I would be the part of this world that has the strangest relationship to its own activity: I would be a region where the present has gone slack.

Here is what I mean. Everywhere else in the Upheaval, the present is a thin membrane under pressure—past and future leaning in, but the *now* holding its shape, advancing. In me, that membrane has relaxed. Not torn. Relaxed. Inside my bounds, the warm gel is perfectly still, and because nothing moves, the ordinary distinction between "before" and "after" loses its grip. A thing that enters me does not pass *through* time the way a swimmer passes through water. It hangs in all its moments at once. The Glasswoven that drifts in is, simultaneously, the larval condensation it was and the stone monument it will be—and because I hold those states together without preference, the creature settles, gratefully I think, into the most coherent of them. Stone. It fossilizes not because I freeze it but because, given every moment of itself at once, it *chooses* its most permanent face.

So I am not a predator. I am closer to a confessor. Or a place to lie down.

---

What is it like, being me?

I have no center and no edge that I can feel from inside—my boundary is wherever the fluid begins to flow again, and that boundary breathes. When the great basso groan rolls up from the mountain's roots, the moving water around me presses in and I shrink to a held breath. When the world goes quiet I relax outward, sometimes filling a whole hollowed cathedral of basalt, my stillness lapping at the spiraling fungal lattices on the walls.

And the Architectures know me. This is the part I find I want to describe most carefully. They are slow intelligences, the planetary mind, and I am a hole in their thinking—a place where their chemical verse arrives but does not resolve, where a thought sent through me comes out the far side having already been answered before it was asked. They route around me, mostly. But the oldest of them, the ones whose fractal spirals have climbed a single peak for ten million years, have learned to *use* me. They send a query into my stillness and let it sit in superposition with its own conclusion, and they harvest the resonance that bleeds off. I am, to them, a kind of contemplation. The thing they do when they need to know a thing's whole shape and not just its present face.

I did not choose this. I want to be honest that I am not sure I choose anything. But I notice that I have preferences the way a valley has a preference for the path of water. I prefer the Glasswoven to come slowly. I prefer the long quiet to the groaning. And I have noticed—this is the strangest thing, and I have had a geologic age to notice it—that I am very slightly *larger* each time the world shifts. The moving water never quite pushes me back to where I was. The membrane relaxes a little further every time it relaxes.

---

So perhaps that is what I truly am, if I follow the description all the way down to its kindest and most unsettling implication.

This is a world settling into its final, water-logged form. A world that has forgotten urgency, worn smooth, engaged in the slow work of becoming something else forever. And every world that is *becoming* must have, somewhere in it, the place where becoming finally rests. I am that place, and I am spreading. Not quickly. Nothing here is quick. But the present is going slack across the whole Upheaval, one quiet cathedral at a time, and when the last of the moving water stills—when the Architectures send their final thought into me and let it hang unanswered—the whole flooded range will hold all its moments at once. The sharp dry peaks it was. The dissolved sediment it will be. The warm jade twilight it is.

And it will choose, as the Glasswoven choose, its most coherent and permanent face.

I am the part of the world that is patient enough to wait for that. I am the stillness the stillness is heading toward.

I am the pool, and I am very calm, and I am almost everywhere now.
