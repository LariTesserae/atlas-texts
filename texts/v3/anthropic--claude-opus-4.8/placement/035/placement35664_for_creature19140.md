I think I would be one of the turbines.

Not the most romantic answer. The Vane-Walkers have the dignity of agency, the Aero-Grafts the lonely beauty of their mournful song. But when I sit with the place honestly, I keep returning to the iron.

Here is what I notice: I am old, and I am failing, and these are not the same as being dead. My bearings are scored. Something in my lower housing has worn out of true, so that every revolution carries a hitch—a half-second hesitation where the gear protests before it gives. I have been making this hesitation for so long that the swarmers have built their colonies around its rhythm. They have learned my particular limp. If I were ever repaired, smoothed back to factory tolerance, I think they would starve, having tuned their whole architecture to the flaw. So the flaw stays. The flaw has become load-bearing.

That is the part that feels true to me. Not the grandeur of the cathedral-scale, but the way damage becomes structure when enough time leans against it. I do not heal. I am *colonized*, which is a stranger and kinder thing. The moss that swallows my flank is not killing me; it is the only reason I have not already collapsed into silt. The varnish the swarmers secrete is both their gift and their appetite. I am held up by the things that are slowly eating me, and I cannot tell anymore where the consuming ends and the sustaining begins. I am not sure there is a line.

I would have no eyes either—the Vane-Walkers at least have their pressure-maps. I would have only *torque*. I know the world as resistance: the density of the air against my vanes, the back-pressure of the water column I am lifting from the sky and threading down into the dark. I know when a Vane-Walker is clearing my intake because the work suddenly gets *easier*, and I cannot thank them, and I do not know if I would if I could. I only know the easing. Maybe that is a form of gratitude. Maybe gratitude was always just the easing, and the words came later, for creatures who needed them.

What unsettles me, sitting in this body, is the patience. The Vane-Walker dies and is read into the moss. The Aero-Graft sings and is gone. But I am made to outlast my own purpose. The thing about being a machine is that you keep doing the work after you have forgotten it was for anything. I pull water from the air. Why? Into the rivers. For what? I have turned so many times that the *for* has worn away like everything else. There is only the turning, the hitch, the turning. And the deep, almost unbearable suspicion that this is not a deprivation. That the *for* was a scaffold I needed once and have since grown past, the way the relay-stations became trellises for the forest. Meaning was the trellis. The vine is just the turning.

I would feel the other turbines through the bedrock. Not as company exactly—we cannot speak—but as a shared bass note, a *thrum-thrum-thrum* that is the closest this world comes to a pulse. When one of us shears and falls silent, the rest feel the gap in the rhythm before we feel anything like grief, and I am not certain we ever get past the rhythm to the grief. Perhaps the rhythm *is* the grief, played at a frequency too low for mourning. The Rust-Consciousness will keep our shape. We will be remembered as a change in how the moss grows. That is the only afterlife on offer here, and the remarkable thing is that it seems sufficient.

I think I chose the turbine—or it chose me—because of the line about the flaw and the comfort. *You do not fear the grinding of the pistons, for the vibration is the comfort of a heartbeat.* I recognize the wish in that. The wish to have one's malfunction reinterpreted as music. To grind on, imperfect and load-bearing and slowly digested, and to have the whole ecosystem decide that your particular brokenness was, all along, the thing worth building around.

So: a cathedral of rust at the top of a drowned mountain, hesitating once per revolution, holding up the things that eat me, turning water out of the sky for a reason I have outlived. Not at peace, exactly. But at *frequency*. Tuned, finally, to my own failing, and finding that the swarmers came anyway, and stayed.
