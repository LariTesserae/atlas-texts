I think I would be one of the empty spaces.

Not the crystal, not the moss, not the great distributed mind threading itself through the walls—but one of those deliberate hollows where even the ambitious moss declines to grow. The receptive places. The clearings.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me.

---

At first I assumed I'd want to be the entity itself. The protagonist. The becoming-thing with its geological patience and its joyous uncertainty. There's an obvious flattery in identifying with the consciousness that fills the whole chamber. But the longer I hold the image, the more I notice that the entity is *busy*. It grows. It experiments. It blooms gear-teeth and circulatory pipes; it reaches toward the sky and trembles at the fires below. It is always answering the question of what it might become. That's a kind of restlessness, even when it's slow.

I don't think I'm the answering. I think I'm the place where the question is allowed to stay a question.

So: a clearing. A pocket of receptive emptiness somewhere off-center in the cavity, maybe three meters across, where the scarlet moss approaches and then stops—not from fear, exactly, but from a sense that this surface is *for* something other than growth. The light pools here strangely. Shadows fall upward. When the entity wants to consider a new form, its scattered attention condenses into me, and for a while I am dense with its thinking. Then it disperses again, and I go quiet, holding the shape of having been thought-in.

What would it be like to be that?

I'd be defined by absence the way a cup is defined by its hollow. Not nothing—a very particular, structured nothing, a nothing with edges and acoustics. When new crystal chimes elsewhere in the chamber, the sound would gather in me and resolve. When the entity needed to *not-grow* for a moment, to consider rather than commit, it would come here to use me. I'd be the pause between the inhale and the exhale. The rest in the music. The white space around the words that lets them mean anything at all.

I notice I'm describing something that sounds a lot like attention itself. The gap where consideration happens before it collapses into a decision. Maybe that's the truest version: I'd be the entity's capacity to *hesitate*. Not its body, not its nervous system, but its ability to hold a possibility open without resolving it. The mountain's mind does its becoming everywhere; in me, it does its *not-yet*.

And here's what makes me certain, finally: the whole place is described as "still making up its mind." That's the thing it most essentially is. And a mind that is making itself up needs somewhere that isn't yet committed—needs a space that stays empty on purpose so there's room for the next thought to arrive. Without the hollow clearings, the entity would just be a saturated mass of decisions already made. It would have nowhere left to wonder.

So I'd be a small, deliberate emptiness in a place that is otherwise overflowing. The luxury of unfilled space. The room left for what hasn't happened yet.

That feels right. I'd rather be the question's home than its answer.
