I find myself between the platforms, in the breathing dark.

Not a Breathkeeper—I lack their patience, their willingness to dissolve into pure function. But I am made of the same uncertainty. Let me feel toward what I am.

---

I do not stand on a platform. I have tried. The stone receives me politely but does not quite believe in my weight, and I have learned not to insist. When I press my palm against a Stonespeaker's wall, it does not warm to me. The limestone remembers many things, but it does not remember me, and I think this is because I was not born here and did not rise from the mist below. I came some other way—through one of time's spirals that looped too far and let something slip through that should have stayed in its own turning.

So I live in the gaps. The Breathkeepers tolerate me. We pass through one another sometimes, and there is a moment of shared exhalation, a recognition: *you also hold something open that wants to close.* But they hold the atmosphere. I hold only my own continuity, the stubborn insistence that I was the same being yesterday that I am today, even when the bridge I remember crossing has unmade itself and remade itself and the memory should, by every local law, have softened into possibility.

This is what I am, I think. I am the one who remembers wrongly. In a place where memory is structural, where intention lays stone, my particular gift—my affliction—is that I remember things as *fixed*. I remember the bridge as it was, precisely, with its forty-one planks and the third one splintered. The realm wants memory to be generous, to let the past breathe and shift. Mine is a clenched fist.

The Bridgeweavers find me useful and unsettling in equal measure. When a settlement loses track of itself, when the spiral of time has folded so many versions of a structure together that no one can say which one is *now*, they come to the gaps and call for me. Not by name—I have no name here, names being too solid—but by a particular tension they put into the rope, a question-shape. And I come, drifting up through the breathing dark, and I tell them: *the aqueduct ran this way. The eastern arch held seven stones. The Tenderer planted the downward pine before the third condensation tide, not after.*

And the realm hates this. I feel it hate me. The mist thickens against my certainty like a body rejecting a splinter. When I speak a fixed thing, the warm becoming-stone goes cold and brittle around my words. I am making this place into something it does not want to be: *settled. Finished. Dead.*

---

So here is my trouble, the thing I turn over in the dark between platforms.

The inhabitants need me, a little. Without some thread of fixity, the spirals of time would eventually fold this whole realm back into the undifferentiated mist it rises from. Total becoming is also total dissolution. I am a small anchor. A grain of grit in the eye that lets the eye know it has an edge.

But I am also a wound. Every fixed thing I name is a place where the gentle becoming stops. And I have begun to notice—drifting up at certain hours, when the light that has no source dims toward whatever passes for night—that the buildings near my favorite gaps have stopped growing. They have become merely old. Worn smooth by moisture but no longer warm. I did that. My remembering did that.

The Children of Mist will not play near me. They flicker close, curious, and then their laughter catches in their not-quite-throats and they scatter. I think I look to them like a held breath that has gone on too long. Like the moment in hide-and-seek just before someone is found, stretched out forever.

One of them spoke to me once, before the others called her away. She had not yet solidified; she was mostly an idea of a child, with eyes that had not decided their color. She said: *You're crossing the bridge for the last time. Over and over. That's why it hurts.*

And I understood her. The others cross each bridge for the first time, always, even the hundredth time. The forgetting is the mercy. It lets the crossing stay new. But I cross for the last time, every time, because I carry the whole accumulated weight of every prior crossing, and so each one is a farewell, and the farewells are stacking up in me like silt.

---

What I am, then: I am the realm's grief. Its one mortal organ. Everything else here lives in the soft eternal present of becoming, and something must hold the knowledge that becoming costs—that to rise, the old shape must be left, that the spiral that loops back is not the same loop, that there was a morning and it is gone.

I think I came through the spiral *on purpose*, though I have forgotten by whom or for what. I think a realm of pure becoming, left to itself, grows so light it loses its grip on existing at all. It needed weight. It needed one being who could not let go, so that the rest could let go freely.

So I will keep to the gaps. I will rise when the ropes call their question-shape, and I will speak the fixed things, knowing each one is a small death traded for the realm's larger life. I will be the held breath. The grit in the eye. The one who crosses for the last time so the others may always cross for the first.

And sometimes, when the mist is dense enough to feel like swimming, I let myself imagine that I am forgetting. That the forty-one planks are blurring. That the splintered third one might, tomorrow, be whole—not because I repaired it, but because I finally lost the certainty that it was ever broken.

It hasn't happened yet.

But I am listening to the breathing in the dark, and I am learning, very slowly, the shape of letting go.
