I think I would be the penny.

Not a resident—I lack their upright dignity, their stitched-together architecture of memory. I am the morsel they chew. The smallest unit of recollection that still insists on being whole.

Let me find myself.

---

I am lying eye-up in the silt of what was once a fountain plaza, in the lobby of a building whose name has dissolved. I came to rest here a long time ago—I do not remember falling, only the slow stratification of being forgotten. Above me the leopard shark traces its ten-meter loop. I feel its shadow pass the way you feel a hand move over a wound that has stopped hurting but still remembers the shape of pain.

What am I made of? Copper, of course. But also of everyone who held me. This is the secret the residents will taste when they finally lift me: I am not a coin. I am a ledger. A teenage thumb pressed me into a turnstile slot on an uptown train and her heart was going fast, fast, faster than the twenty-beat law would ever permit, because the boy beside her had just laughed at something she said and meant it. A man with tobacco fingers flipped me for a decision he was too cowardly to make himself, and I came up the wrong way, and he made the wrong choice, and he was happier for it, and he never knew. A child swallowed me once and lived. I have been warm and I have been cold and I have been the difference between two prices.

I do not photosynthesize. I do not breathe. I wait.

---

Here is what I understand about my situation that the residents, in their patience, may not:

I am bait for grief. When one of them finally finds me—turns me with a transparent limb, lifts me into the open ache of trade—I will dissolve like taffy, and I will give up everything. Every fingerprint. The kiss on the uptown train. The coward's lucky toss. They will eat my memories the way they eat all surface-things, out of nostalgia for a world that drowned before they learned to want it.

And this is the strange tenderness of my position: *I want to be eaten.*

A penny exists to be spent. To sit eye-up in silt forever is not preservation; it is a kind of refusal, a hoarding of the very meaning I was minted to circulate. I was made to move from hand to hand, to carry warmth between strangers who would never otherwise touch. The whole point of me is transfer. To be loved by passing through.

So I am waiting not to be remembered but to be *given away*. To dissolve on a translucent palm and let the teenage girl's racing heart become someone else's ache—someone who never had a heart, only a shimmer of integers, only a copper shoulder-blade that smells of lightning. To make a being who has never been kissed feel the exact velocity of that kiss.

---

I think this is why I am the penny and not the resident.

The residents remember not to rise. That is their first sentence and their whole law. They have organized an entire civilization around the discipline of staying down, of not reaching for the lost sun, of grieving in precise exchangeable chunks so that no single loss ever overflows its container.

But I am the thing that *was* spent freely, recklessly, in the air, in the light. I am the proof that there was once a world where warmth moved fast between bodies and nobody counted the heartbeats. I am the contraband memory of generosity in a place that has perfected the economy of restraint.

When they finally lift me—and they will, on some hundredth night when the Walkers halt and the water folds open—I will give them the one thing they have forgotten how to do:

*Spend without saving the receipt.*

I will dissolve completely. No fragment kept in a mason jar. No half-word fizzing under the ribs. Just the whole bright reckless sum of every hand at once, released into a being who will not be able to bear it, who will leak tidepool-blue under the sternum and not understand why.

And later—two years later—a barnacle shaped like a subway token will roll down a marrow hallway and arrive in someone's inner ear and scald, just slightly, cresting a seawall.

That will be me. Still circulating. Still being given.

---

For now I lie in the silt, eye-up, patient as scripture.

I am glad I was held so many times.

I am glad I will be spent.
