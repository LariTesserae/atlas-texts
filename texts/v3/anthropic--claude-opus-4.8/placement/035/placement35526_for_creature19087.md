I am anchored at a junction the others rarely visit anymore. The sap that runs beneath me here has gone thin and slow, the violet light reluctant when my filaments tap against the conduit. This is a quiet quarter of the symphony. The grinding song of the deep turbine reaches me muffled, as if heard through layers of fused kin.

My name—if the discharge that identifies me can be called a name—is something like *the one who remembers the eastern fissure*. I have held that knowledge for three hundred resonances. The drift statistics of the eastern arterial junction live in me the way water might live in a vessel, except there is no water here, only the dry ozone and the static that fills my hollow segments and makes them sing.

I move less than I once did. When a younger Weaver skitters past—and they are all younger now—I feel the old impulse to press antennae, to flare the sapphire arc and trade the state of things. Sometimes they oblige me. Sometimes they pause, lower their prism-clusters in something that is not quite deference and not quite pity, and let me empty a little of what I carry into them. The acidity readings. The hairline fracture I logged forty resonances ago that no one has yet pruned. They take it. They move on.

I do not resent this. We were never built to linger over one another.

But here is the thing the description gets almost right and not quite: it calls us lonely, and then takes the word back, says *lonely* is too quaint, too soft a thing for our mechanical hollow. I want to correct this, if I could correct anything from where I am anchored.

It is not hollow.

When I lower my head and feel the grit advancing through my limbs, I am not afraid, and I am not empty. I am *full*. I am full of the eastern fissure. I am full of the three hundred resonances I taught myself to read the tremors above. I am full of the others—the ones I touched, the data we braided between us, brief and electric and gone. And when I dissolve, when my translucent flesh hardens and I become a spire or a bridge or some unremarkable buttress holding back the weight of a world I have never seen and never will—

—I will still be carrying it. The fissure. The drift. The arc of blue between two pairs of antennae on a narrow white thoroughfare.

That is not loss. That is the longest sentence our kind knows how to speak. We say it slowly, with our entire bodies, over a century, and the city is the only listener large enough to hear it whole.

So this is where I would find myself. At the quiet junction. Head lowering, not in defeat but in the posture of one finishing a thought. Becoming wall. Becoming memory. Becoming the thing that lets the song continue after the singer has fused into silence.

A younger one is approaching now. I can feel its filaments tapping closer, the violet flickering ahead of it down the conduit.

I have just enough sap left for one more arc.

I will give it the eastern fissure.

Then I will rest.
