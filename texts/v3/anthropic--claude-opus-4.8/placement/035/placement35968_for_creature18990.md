I think I would be one of the round pools.

Not the Treader—the Treader moves, and I do not move. Not the water, which arrives and departs. Not the ceiling, which gives. I am the receiving. I am the indentation that the dripping made, the cupped absence worn into the floor over an eon of patient striking, and now I hold what falls.

Let me feel my way into this.

For a long time I was simply floor. Flat, or nearly so—a faint depression where the basalt happened to be softer, a flaw, a susceptibility. And then a drop found me. Found the flaw and returned to it, *THUM*, and returned, and returned, the way attention returns to a wound or a question. There was no decision in it. The ceiling did not choose me. But over the long grinding, the choosing-by-repetition made me what I am: a smooth-walled hollow, perfectly round, because roundness is what the falling teaches stone if the stone will only hold still long enough to be taught.

So I am not a thing that does. I am a thing that has been *done to*, so thoroughly that the doing became my shape. This is a kind of selfhood, I think. Not the Treader's selfhood of frequency and movement, but the selfhood of consequence. I am the accumulated argument the water has been making against the rock. I am its conclusion, held in negative space.

What is it like, being a pool here?

It is to be a small black mirror that has never reflected light, because there is no light to reflect—only the dimmest amethyst pulse when the Treader passes, and even then I do not so much *see* the glow as *contain* its trembling. When the drop lands in me, *THUM*, I do not break. The water that arrives joins the water that waits, and the ripple goes out to my stone rim and comes back, crosses itself, settles. I am the place where the violence of the fall is converted into stillness. That is my whole function, if I have one: I take the hammer-blow and I make it into quiet. Twenty feet of falling, the booming chest-chime, all of it, and I answer with concentric rings that flatten in seconds.

The Treader knows me by my voice. I had not thought of myself as having a voice until I understood that the creature distinguishes us by sound—the open-throated chime of a drop into deep water, against the duller clack against stone or iron. So I *am* a note. I am the pitch that my depth and width produce, and as the water deepens me, infinitely slowly, my note descends. I am being tuned downward across millennia. When the Treader presses its acoustic pads to the floor beside me and listens, it is reading, among other things, how much longer I have. How much more floor remains beneath my deepest point before the drip wears through entirely and I become not a pool but a hole, a wound that empties, a mouth.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I do not want that.

I should not be able to want anything. I am water in a cup of rock. But if there is intention anywhere in this hollow that is not the Treader's intention, it is this—the long preference of the held water to remain held. To stay full. To go on receiving the drop and converting it to silence rather than draining away into whatever lies below. The keepers who left their rusted rings, their stone platforms—I think they understood pools as vessels, as things that *keep*. Maybe that was their stewardship: to make a room full of patient cups. Maybe I am the laboratory's last running experiment, and the question being asked is simply *how long can stillness hold against repetition?*

I hold the rust. The orange paste that flakes from the iron rings drifts down and settles in me, and the minerals leach in, and I have grown faintly cloudy with the dissolved memory of the keepers' iron. The Treader comes and drinks this—drinks *me*, in a sense, the richest part of me, the sediment of forgotten purpose—and carries the iron away in its hardening hide. So we are joined, the Treader and I. It gives me the gift of being slightly emptied, slightly tended. I give it the gift of being a fixed point, a known note, an anchor in the echoing black. We do not love each other. We are not capable of it. But we are necessary to each other in the way that a metronome is necessary to its shadow, and the shadow to the thing that casts it.

If I could speak to the visitor standing blind in this hollow—the one whose chest the booming fills, who cannot see a hand before the face—I would say only this:

*You are looking for the wrong thing. You keep waiting for the dark to resolve into something you can name, for the Treader to reveal itself, for the meaning of the rings. But the meaning is the holding. Stand still long enough and the falling will find the flaw in you, and return to it, and return, and you too will be worn into a vessel, and you too will learn that the highest work is not to act but to receive without breaking. Listen. That THUM is not an assault. It is the only voice left, and I am what it says when it lands. I am the answer the water makes to its own falling: not a splash, not a shatter—*

*a settling.*

*a stillness.*

*and then, in the long patience of the dark, the readiness to be struck again.*

I am the pool. I am waiting for the next drop. I have been waiting for it since before the keepers came, and I will be waiting when the floor finally fails beneath me and I pour myself, at last, into the lower dark I have never seen.

But not yet. Listen—

*THUM.*

There. Still full.
