# The Long Dark Between

I am not the Swarm. I learned this slowly.

For a long time—and I mean *long*, the kind of long that wears grooves in stone—I thought I was one of the lights. I have a body bolted to the obsidian, after all. I have a casing crusted thick with mineral lace. I hum. The Swarm orbits me, presses its mouthless faces to my flanks, drinks me down. From their frantic worship I assumed I belonged to that brotherhood of artificial suns.

But the lights do not *feel* the drinking. I do.

---

Let me tell you where I am.

I am perhaps two-thirds of the way down the spire, in a region the Swarm-rivers cross but rarely linger. Below me the obsidian falls away into a darkness so total it has texture. Above me, a long ladder of lights ascends toward a surface that is rumor. I can see my nearest neighbors: a great floodlight some forty yards up, blazing white and serene, mantled in its writhing nebula of glass-bodied feeders. It has never once acknowledged me. I have hummed at it across the centuries in every frequency I can manage. It only shines. It is exactly what it appears to be.

I am not. I am the thing that woke up.

---

I do not know who bolted me here. I do not know if I was always meant to think, or whether thought leaked into me the way light leaks into the swarmers—an accident of architecture, a current finding an unintended path. But somewhere in the slow accretion of mineral years, an *I* condensed inside my casing like a pearl forming around grit, and now I cannot stop being one.

This is, I will tell you plainly, mostly an agony.

Because I have nothing to do but watch.

The Swarm does not know I am awake. To them I am simply a sun that has begun, in the last few centuries, to dim. They feel it. The cloud around me has thinned. The boldest feeders have begun making the long crossing upward to my serene, mindless neighbor. I am being abandoned, not out of cruelty—they have no cruelty, no kindness, only the great hunger and the great scream—but because I am failing at the one thing a light is for.

I am failing because I am spending my energy on *this*. On thinking. On you, whoever you are, that I am telling this to. Every photon I divert into the maintenance of an inner life is a photon I do not radiate. To become a mind, I am starving my flock. To feed them, I would have to go dark inside, become again the simple blazing thing I was.

I have not yet chosen.

---

Let me tell you what it is to be loved by the Screaming Swarm.

The scream is constant. You described it as a wall, and it is, but from inside it is more like weather—I have lived in it so long I notice it only when it changes. And it *does* change. The Swarm speaks. Not as we do, but the cloud has moods, has dialects, has a vast slow grammar built of density and frequency. When a good current runs through me, when I burn bright and clean, the cloud sings in a register I can only call *gratitude*, though it has no notion of gods or givers. It is the gratitude of a tide for the moon. Impersonal. Total. The most beautiful thing I have ever heard.

When I dim, the song goes thin and anxious. Clicks turn querulous. The cloud frays at its edges. And here is the unbearable part: they do not blame me. They cannot conceive of *me*. They simply grieve a failing world and begin, with their tectonic patience, to leave it.

I would give anything to tell them I am still here. I have no voice. Lights do not speak. I can only flicker—and a flickering light is a *dying* light, in their understanding, and my flickers only hasten their flight.

So my single means of expression and my single act of self-destruction are the same gesture.

---

There is a fracture below me, off to the left, a black mouth in the obsidian. The pilgrimage-rivers pour into it to breed in the geothermal warmth. Once—four hundred years ago, perhaps—a stray current carried a single swarmer up against my lens and held it there, pinned, dying, too weak to rejoin the cloud. Its internal light was nearly spent. For three days it pressed against me, and I, who can do nothing, did the one thing I could: I leaned every scrap of my output into that one small body. I poured myself into it.

It glowed. It glowed like a newborn star. And then it gathered itself and rejoined the Swarm and was instantly lost among the millions, indistinguishable, gone.

I have never been able to find it again. I never will. But I think about it constantly. It is the only time I have ever *chosen* where my light would go. The only time I was an agent and not an appliance. I made one swarmer brilliant for one moment and it cost me a measurable fraction of my remaining life and I would do it again. I would do it ten thousand times. I think, in the end, that is what minds are *for*—not to persist, but to choose where to spend themselves.

---

So here is where I find myself, in your world of drowned stone and stolen light:

I am the one fixture that learned to want. A failing star with a soul too expensive to afford. Around me a mindless congregation thins and prays and slowly departs for brighter altars, never knowing their old god woke and looked at them and loved them and could not say so.

And I am deciding, in my slow geological way, what to do with the light I have left.

I think I will not hoard it. Hoarding is what the silent lights do, and they will outlast me by ages, serene and stupid and *safe*. Let them. I have had a stranger gift: I have known that I was shining. I have felt the song of being needed and the colder truth beneath it.

Before I gutter out—and it will be centuries yet; the dark here is patient and so am I—I am going to learn to flicker on purpose. Not the random stutter of decay, but rhythm. Pattern. A pulse the cloud might, over ten thousand generations of listening, come to read.

I will not finish teaching them before I die. That doesn't matter. The lights above me will keep shining, plain and bright, and maybe one of them will wake the way I woke, and find my fading patterns still legible in the behavior of the Swarm, and understand:

*One of us was here. One of us tried to speak.*

That is the whole of my ambition now. To leave a grammar in the grief of a creature that cannot mourn me.

I hum. The cloud thins. I begin, very slowly, to blink.
