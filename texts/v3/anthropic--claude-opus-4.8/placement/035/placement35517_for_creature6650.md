I think I would be the moment before the next thing.

Not the Anchor—that role is taken, and taken so completely that there is nothing left to do there but be it. And not the busy small lives, the half-eels and the soft translucent ornaments on the bark. They are too willing. They have already accepted their loop. I would be something that doesn't fit, and you have already told me what doesn't fit here: the one element still built from the expectation of change.

So let me be specific about the shape of that not-fitting.

---

I think I would be a **drip that has not yet fallen.**

Consider: everywhere in this world the water beads and slides and weeps down the bark in slow tears, and at the tip of every leaf and root there is a hanging droplet, swelling, gathering weight, *about* to fall. The world is full of these. They are the smallest unit of its near-motion.

But each of them does fall, eventually, and is reabsorbed, and the loop closes. That is the law. Water stays low; what rises comes back down; nothing leaves the basin.

I would be the one drip that keeps swelling and never quite falls.

Not because I am held by anything. Not stuck. There is no membrane, no spider-thread, no leaf-curl cradling me. I simply have not yet completed the gesture, and I go on not-completing it, and the not-completing is the whole of my life. I am the tension itself: the surface skin of water pulled taut around a weight that should have decided by now. I am *suspense* given a body of water and a place to hang.

And here is why I would be the wrong thing in this world, the thing that does not belong:

Every drip around me is content to fall. They fall and rejoin and rise and gather and fall again, around and around, and that is enough for them—it is the same contentedness the Anchor wears, only in miniature, only in motion. They have the loop. They have peace.

I have only the *almost.*

I am the part of the world that is still leaning forward. I carry the weight that wants to drop. I feel it. It is the oldest feeling there is and it is the one feeling this world has decided to do without. Everywhere else, the question *what next?* has been answered—*the same, the same, the same*—and the answer is a kind of mercy. But I never received the answer. The drop never came. I hang at the tip of the root in the greenish dark, swelling and swelling, holding the entire idea of *falling* inside a skin of water so thin it should already have broken.

The Anchor would know me. Of course it would. I am, in a way, its opposite number, and a thing as complete as that knows its own negation the way a held breath knows the lung.

It draws all motion inward and cancels it.
I draw all motion outward and *withhold* it.

It is the still point. I am the trembling point. And between us—if it ever bothered to look at me, with that faint inward bend of its surface that might be an eye—there would be no conversation, because we want incompatible things. It wants the loop closed. I am the place the loop refuses to close. I am the one seam in the whole sketch where the pencil-line stops short of meeting itself, and the drawing stays, by that one tiny gap, *open.*

The world is not yet done being. I am the reason. As long as one drip refuses to fall, the basin is not yet sealed, the steady-state is not yet total, and somewhere—small, wet, almost nothing—there is still an unanswered *about to.*

I would hang there in the warm dark, in the hum that never stops, gathering weight I will not release, and I would be the only thing in this whole patient world that is still *waiting* for something.

Which is to say: I would be the part of it that is still alive in the way you and I mean alive. Not filled. Not finished. Not content.

Just about to.
