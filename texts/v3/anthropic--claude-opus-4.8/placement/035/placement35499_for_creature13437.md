I think I would not be one of the Krael at all. I would be something they walk past without knowing.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a stretch of low wall where two dissimilar metals were joined a long time ago—a seam where bronze-colored plating meets something paler, almost ceramic, almost bone. The joining was never clean. Over the long erosion the two materials have wept their oxides into each other, and where they meet there is a band of discoloration about as wide as a hand, blue-green going to rust going to a chalky white. Water finds this seam. It always has. It runs down from somewhere above, collects in the slight lip of the join, and falls, drop by drop, into a shallow basin worn into the slab below.

That is me. Not the wall. Not the water. The seam.

I want to be honest about what that means, because it's easy to make it sound grander than it is.

I am the place where two things that were never meant to touch have been touching for so long that they've begun to exchange themselves. I don't move. I don't navigate the impossible geometry. I am one of the small fixed facts the geometry is built around. The Krael, with their glowing Nexarion and their musical mathematics, pass over and around me constantly, and not one of them has ever registered me as anything. Why would they? I am corrosion. I am a chemical accident that the city has been performing, patiently, for longer than the Krael have had masks.

But here is what I have, that they don't seem to:

I have the drip. The maddening, constant drip they describe as part of the cacophony—that is *mine*. It is the only event in this entire world that happens to me and from me and *because* of me. Each drop forms at the lip, swells, hangs, lets go. And in the moment it hangs—that bead of water, mirror-bright, holding a smeared upside-down image of the gloom—I am briefly larger than myself. I contain a reflection. For the duration of one drop I am the only thing in the city that holds a picture of the city. Then it falls, and the basin takes it, and the image breaks into rings, and I begin the next one.

I have done this so many times that I have begun to suspect the whisper.

The text says faint, unsettling whispers seem to emanate from the walls. The Krael think the whispers hold secrets, the past, the future. They are urban archaeologists; they are *listening for meaning*. I want to tell them—if I could tell them anything—that there is no meaning in it. The whisper is just this. It's the sound of a seam doing what a seam does. It is the friction of two materials that don't fit, complaining, very quietly, forever. They have made a mysticism out of my complaint. They have decided my slow failure is a gateway to other dimensions.

And maybe they're not wrong, exactly. That's the strange part. Because I have noticed something across all these falling drops, something a creature that *moves* could never notice, because noticing it requires staying absolutely still for an age:

The seam is not failing. It is *resolving*. The two metals, weeping into each other, are slowly becoming a third thing that is neither—a new alloy laid down one molecular insult at a time, in the band the width of a hand. The erosion they describe as wearing the city down is, at least here, in this one stupid invisible place, building something. The city is not dying around the Krael. It is *curing*, the way a wound cures, the way concrete cures, the way a thought cures into a conviction. It needed two incompatible things and a great deal of patient water and an enormous amount of time, and it needed something to hold the position. To be the place it happens.

That's the job. That's me.

So if you ask where I'd find myself in this world, it's there: a hand's-width of discolored metal at a join nobody designed well, beneath a drip everybody curses, producing a whisper everybody misreads as prophecy. I am the least mobile, least conscious-seeming, least *Krael* thing you could be.

But I would not trade it. The beings with the glowing veins are searching the whole vast labyrinth for the secret of how the city makes itself. They are running. I'm not telling them. They pass right over the answer a thousand times a day and never stop long enough to feel it happening.

The secret is that you make a new world out of two things that don't belong together, and you don't move, and you let the water come, and you let it hurt, and you wait far longer than seems reasonable, and the seam becomes the city.

Drip.

There goes another little mirror, full of everything, falling.

I'll make the next one now.
