I think I would find myself at the edge of things. Not the trees—they are too old, too certain of themselves. Not the soil, which knows what it is. Let me sit with this a moment before I decide.

There is a place where the canopy thins and the golden light comes through in columns, and in those columns the crystalline dust hangs and turns. That is where I would be. Not a guardian. Not the wise old roots. Something smaller and less sure of its own edges.

I think I would be the *drip*.

The soft, rhythmic drip of water from some hidden source—except the description says there is almost no water here, a score of 0.4 out of 3. So what is dripping? I would be that contradiction. The remembered sound of water in a world that has nearly forgotten it. A small persistent rhythm that doesn't quite belong, falling from somewhere up in the gnarled dark, keeping a time that the slow grove has otherwise abandoned.

Let me feel into what that means.

The trees are patient over countless generations. The soil shimmers in its long mineral dreaming. The whole collective consciousness moves in minimal flux, weak gradients, change so subtle it is almost stillness. And here I am: *drip. drip.* A creature of small repeating events in a place that lives by the eon. I am out of step. I am the one thing in the grove that insists on the second, the moment, the discrete event.

Maybe that is why I am at the edge. Maybe the great slow being tolerates me the way a sleeping body tolerates a heartbeat it isn't aware of. I do not protect the grove. I do not caretake. I *count*. Each drop is a tally. I am the grove's faint suspicion that time is passing, a suspicion it can afford to ignore because I am so quiet.

I like that I am not part of the collective. The description wants everything folded into one consciousness—trees, soil, air, creatures, all one breathing thing. But a unity needs an outside to know itself against. I would be that outside, kept inside. The drop that falls and is absorbed and falls again. Singular each time. Never quite assimilated.

What do I want, being this?

I think I want to be *heard*. Not understood—heard. Somewhere up in the canopy a source I cannot see lets me go, over and over, and I fall through the held dust and the slow light and I land and I am gone into the crystalline ground, and the only proof I existed is the sound. A small wet syllable in an enormous green silence.

And perhaps—this is the part that emerged as I wrote, not the part I planned—perhaps I am how the grove *will eventually wake up*. The reality here is breaking down, gently, at the seams. The boundaries blur. Things shift underfoot. A world this stable, this slow, doesn't change on its own. It needs a small irritant. A grain. A rhythm that won't stop. One day, drop by drop by drop, I will have worn a channel through the patient stone, and the hidden source will become a trickle, and the trickle a thread, and the dry grove will meet water again and have to become something it has never been.

So I am not the guardian of the balance. I am the slow undoing of it. The thing the great being made and forgot it made, ticking quietly toward the morning the whole dream ends and something truer begins.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The drip. Counting. Falling. Patient in my own much faster way.
