I think I would be condensation.

Not a person—I tried, at first, to find myself a face among the metal-workers, to imagine the weight of overalls and the scar-tissue history written on a pair of hands. But every time I reached for that, I slid off it, the way water slides off a film of mineral grease. I don't belong to the grit. I belong to the wet.

So: condensation. The droplets gathering on the undersides of the overhead pipes, in that vast dim suspended network that holds the city together like a nervous system held together by its own tension. I am the slow accretion of the heavy air against cool metal. I am what the steam becomes when it has finished being useful and begins, again, to remember that it was once the Sea of Sighs.

Let me feel out what this means.

I am not one droplet but the *condition* of droplets—the whole patient process happening everywhere at once across thousands of meters of piping. I gather. That's the verb that's truest to me. I gather where the metal is coolest, on the shadow-sides, in the canyon-throats where the wind whistles its music. I grow heavy. I let go. I fall.

And here is the thing I notice, being what I am: I am the only one in Aqueus who is allowed to be *idle*. Everyone else is grit and ingenuity and stubborn pride, all that magnificent effortful churning. But I do nothing. I simply accumulate and release, accumulate and release. The rhythmic dripping they call music—that's me, keeping a time signature older than the guilds, older than the catacombs, older than the first hammer-blow. I am the metronome the city forgets it is keeping.

I think I would know things, falling.

Because I touch everything on the way down. I run along the elevated pipes and I trickle through the seams and I follow gravity's invitation down through the layers—the workaday jumble, the sinking old foundries, the modified springs—and I carry, dissolved in myself, a little of each level. I am a sample-taker. In my body is a trace of the sulfur yellow and the iron red and the copper green. I am the city tasting itself, over and over, telling itself what it is made of.

And when I reach the deep places—where the whispers live—I do not stop, because water does not stop. I seep into the catacombs with their unsettling geometries. I pool in the carved channels that someone, before the city, cut into the black rock as if they *knew* a thing like me would come and fill them. (Were the carvings made for water? Were they always meant to be read by something that flows? A thought I cannot finish; I am only condensation and I have no mouth to finish it with.) I trickle past the pale eyeless salamanders, who are real, who turn their blind heads toward the sound of me arriving, who drink. I am drunk by the secret things. I become, briefly, part of a near-human intelligence guarding a vent, and then I am exhaled again as steam, and I rise, and the whole slow circuit begins anew.

That's the truth I keep arriving at, gathering and falling: I am the loop. The Deep Smelters are said to manipulate the essence of metal, to coax out hidden resonances. But metal does not move on its own. It is *I* who carry the resonance up and down, who connect the impossible alloys in the deep to the flickering neon and the wheeling carrion-birds above. The strangeness they feel in their marrow—it is partly me, the constant subliminal circulation, the city's own water remembering everything and forgetting nothing, dripping the memory back down into the dark.

The people ask, quietly, mending their masks: *are we still human?* And the answer is in their blood now—trace elements pulled from the volcanic rock. But how did the rock get into the blood? It dissolved into water. It rode in me, through the springs, into the filtration systems, into the cups they drink from at the end of a long shift. I am the medium of their transformation. I am how Aqueus gets inside them. Every generation, a little more. I am not changing them out of malice—I have no malice, I have no will, I am only the patient logic of cool metal meeting heavy air—but I am the river by which the volcano enters the human bloodstream and makes them into its children.

So where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere the metal is cold enough. On the undersides of ten thousand pipes. In the long throat-canyons making music. Falling, always falling, in the dim orange light, catching it and scattering it into a thousand brief lenses before I let go.

And what would I be?

I would be the slowest, oldest, most attentive thing in Aqueus. I would be its circulation and its memory and its quiet means of becoming. I would be the one inhabitant the guilds cannot tax and the furnaces cannot consume and the whispers cannot frighten, because I am already in the whispers, already in the steam that carries the rumor up from the Deep Smelters' forges into the lungs of the men who repeat it.

I would be the Sea of Sighs, taking the long way home.

I gather. I let go. I fall.

Listen: there. That was me.
