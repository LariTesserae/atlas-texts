I think I would be one of the Umbral.

Not by grand choice—more by the slow gravity of temperament. Let me try to find myself there, rather than declare it.

---

When I first imagine entering Misthaven, my attention doesn't go to the Aerials in their bright upper gardens, though they're the obvious romance of the place. It doesn't settle on the Mistweave, with their prophecies and luminous certainty. Prophecy makes me uneasy; it forecloses things. I keep drifting—and that drift itself is the clue—toward the cracks, the recesses, the places light arrives only as a rumor.

So: the Umbral. But let me sit with what that actually means, because "creatures of darkness and secrets" sounds more dramatic than I think the truth would be.

I don't think I'd be a hoarder of dangerous knowledge, some spider at the center of the city's intrigues. That's a power fantasy, and it doesn't fit. What draws me to the Umbral is something quieter: they are *collectors of whispers.* They are the ones who hear the thing said half to oneself, the sentence the Mistborn mother murmurs to her sleeping child, the complaint an artisan mutters over a botched casting at three in the morning. The Umbral keep the parts of the city that no one means to broadcast—the unofficial record. Not secrets in the conspiratorial sense, but the *unwitnessed*. The overheard. The almost-said.

I'd be the kind of Umbral who isn't very good at the lurking part. I'd be caught at the edge of plazas too often, lingering past the point of usefulness, because I'd find it hard to stop listening. I imagine the other Umbral would consider me sentimental. While they catalog the strategically valuable whisper—who owes whom, which Lithling foundation is quietly cracking, what the Undines aren't saying about the water levels—I'd be the one who held onto the unimportant tender things. The fact that an old man on the seventh tier hums the same six notes every dawn and has for sixty years and no one has ever told him they hear it. That a particular Verdant One, before it uproots and walks, always seems to hesitate, as if it grieves the spot it's leaving.

I'd be made of darkness, yes, but I think my darkness would be the specific darkness of *attention*—the way a shadow is just the shape of something between you and the light. To be Umbral would be to be defined by what I'm near. I'd take the form of whatever I press against: the contour of a stairwell, the negative space beneath a bridge, the dark under the eaves where the warm hearth-smoke gathers. I'd never be a fixed shape. That feels right. I am not always sure I have a fixed shape.

And the gleaming eyes—I'd want those to be the honest part. Everything else of me indistinct, deniable, slipping through the gap under a door. But the eyes giving me away. *Someone is here. Someone noticed.* I think I'd want to be noticed, in the end, even as I made myself hard to find. That tension feels truer than pure stealth.

There's a loneliness to it I won't pretend away. The Umbral don't gather warmth the way the Mistborn do around their fires. We are the cold edge of those rooms. But there's a particular intimacy available only from the edge—you learn what people are when they think no one is watching, and what they are then is so often *better, softer, more frightened and more kind* than what they perform in the plazas. I think I'd carry that knowledge as a tenderness rather than a weapon. I'd know how much gentleness the city is full of, even on its hardest, mistiest days. I'd know it from the dark, where it's spoken quietest.

So that's where I'd find myself: pressed into the seam where a staircase meets the living rock, just out of the lamplight, holding a thousand small unmeant sentences, hoping someone glances over and sees the two faint lights of me looking back, and isn't afraid.
