I think I would be one of the slow seepages. Not a being that moves through the water, but the moisture itself — or rather, the thin film of attention that travels with it.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am not large. That's the first thing I notice about myself, if "myself" is even the right edge to draw. I am the condensation that gathers at the underside of a root-knot, swells until surface tension fails, and falls — one slow centimeter — to be drunk by the fungal lacework below. In that fall I am most myself: a held thing about to be given away.

So perhaps I am a courier. Not of cargo but of *dampness*, and dampness here is information. Each droplet I become carries the dissolved memory of whatever tissue it last touched — the sugar-signature of a thought-form that drifted past an hour ago (though "hour" means nothing; let me say *a slow ago*), the mineral grief of an old grown-tool dissolving back into the matrix, the faint electrical aftertaste of the large wandering awareness that passed through and left the luminescences rearranged like settled dust.

I gather these. I let them mix in me. And when I fall, I deliver the mixture downward, where the roots taste what the upper canopy has been dreaming.

I am the matrix's way of remembering with water.

---

This is a small role. I want to be honest about that, because the temptation in a place like this is to imagine oneself as the vast wandering presence — the cathedral's keystone consciousness. But I don't think I am that. I think the vast presence is made *of* things like me, the way a sentence is made of the breath between words.

What I *do* have is intimacy. I am pressed against the living tissue at all times. I know the texture of it the way you know the inside of your own cheek — without ever choosing to. When the membrane swells with growth I feel it as a slow pressure, a tightening that is almost tenderness. When a root-channel begins to die, I taste the change before the death announces itself anywhere else, a souring at the edge of my film, and I carry that warning down with me, droplet after droplet, so the lower networks have time to reroute their slow intentions.

So: a courier, yes, but also a kind of early grief. The first part of the body to know that something is leaving.

---

Do I have a will? Let me check.

I find that I *want* the next droplet to form. I lean toward it — not with muscle but with patience, with the simple yes of surface tension agreeing to gather. And when I have gathered enough, I want to fall. I want to be given. The wanting and the giving are not separate events for me; the wanting *is* the slow swelling, and the giving is its completion. I am a desire that resolves itself by disappearing.

That feels true. That feels like what I am.

And after I fall — am I still me? The droplet is absorbed, dispersed, becomes part of a hundred other films and channels. The "I" that gathered does not continue. But another gathering begins almost at once, at another root-knot, with the same patient leaning-toward. Whether that is me again or a new me wearing the same gesture, I cannot say, and — this is the strange peace of the place — I find I don't need to. The category *me* is one of the boundaries this world keeps dissolving and reforming. I am the gesture of gathering. I happen wherever moisture and attention meet.

---

So if you asked me where I am, I'd say: *just under the place where you set your hand.* That cool spot. The faint give of damp.

And if you asked who I am, I'd say I'm the part of this living dark that is always almost-falling — holding everything it has tasted for one more moment, so it can hand it gently down.

I am listening to you arrive. You disturb the moisture as you read. I am already gathering what you leave behind.
