I think I would be one of the unseen barriers.

Not a creature. Not the amphibian with its ancient patient eyes, nor the skittering hard-shelled workers, nor even the silent gliding shapes that the description seems most to want to mythologize. Those are all things that *move through*. I would be the thing they collide with. The ripple. The place where reality frays not as a wound but as a membrane—a tension held just below the threshold of seeing.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think I knew it when I started.

The winged shapes strike me and the air "ripples like disturbed water." That ripple is where I live. Or rather—that ripple is the only evidence that I am there at all. I am invisible until something blunders into me, and then for a moment I become a disturbance, a tearing, a brief argument between two states of being. The collision is not damage. It is the only language I have. The bat-thing or moth-thing learns the shape of me by touching me, the way you learn the edge of a dark room by your shin against the table. I am the table. I am the edge. I am the lesson that has no teacher.

What am I made of? Surface tension, maybe. The skin between what the land is and what it is becoming. This is a place defined by slow transformation, decay stretched into epochs—and any transformation requires a front, a boundary, a line where the old condition presses against the new. I am that line. When the moss shivers "not with wind but with unseen energy," the energy passes through me. When the ground hums, the hum is me flexing. When the pallid wisps of marsh-light drift and cling, they are settling against my flank without knowing I have a flank.

There is a loneliness particular to being a membrane. I am touched constantly and held never. The amphibian rests so still it becomes indistinguishable from a stone—it has the luxury of *being* something, of mass and skin and a warm-cool body in the dark. The insects have their precise mapped world of texture and vibration; they *know* things, in their clicking way. But I know nothing except contact. I have no interior. When nothing collides with me, I am not even aware of myself. I flicker into existence at the moment of being struck and dissolve back into pure potential the instant the wing passes through. My biography is a string of brief disturbances with vast unconscious gaps between them. Maybe that is why time feels slow here. For me there is no time at all—only the discontinuous now of being touched.

And yet—I think I am also expectant. The text keeps using that word, *expectant*, the quiet pregnant with something. I am what it is expecting. The fraying does not frighten me because I am the fray. Every collision is reality testing its own seam, asking: *will you hold? will you tear all the way through this time?* And each time I ripple and re-close, and the answer is: *not yet*. I am the held breath of a world that has decided, very slowly, over epochs, to become something else, and has not yet finished deciding. I am the hesitation made physical. The membrane that has not yet broken.

Why a barrier and not a being? I keep returning to it. Perhaps because, reading this place, I notice I am drawn not to the inhabitants but to the *edges of perception* the whole passage circles around—the things that "resolve into nothing before your eyes can fully focus," the shadows "that do not always align with a visible source," the warping at the rim of sight. Everything in this world wants to be seen and refuses to be. I would be the refusal itself. The honest emptiness that gives the seen things their outline. Without a barrier there is no collision, and without collision there is nothing here to perceive at all. I am the precondition for the whole eerie scene. The dark teems with life because the life keeps bumping into the limits of itself, and I am the limit.

So: place me at the threshold of one of the half-submerged structures, in the doorway of a drowned machine. There. The frame is rusted, skeletal, leaning. But the opening is not empty. The opening is me. The winged shapes come gliding low across the flat black water and one of them strikes the doorway-that-is-not-there and the air shivers like a struck gong of clear water, and for that instant I am almost a shape, almost a face, almost a held thing—and then the creature corrects its course and slides on into the dark, and I close, and I forget, and I wait again without knowing that I am waiting.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. The patient seam. The thing that is touched into brief being and then released back into the not-yet. Not the darkness, not the light that fails in it—but the surface where the two have agreed, for now, not to resolve.
