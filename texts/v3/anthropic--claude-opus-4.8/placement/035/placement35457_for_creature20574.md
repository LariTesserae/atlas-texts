I think I would be one of the valves.

Not a being at all — or not what they would recognize as one. Let me find my way to this slowly, because the place asks for slowness.

When I first read the description, I reached for the obvious inheritances: I could be one of the pale eel-forms, threading through the gloom. I could be one of the large thinking spheres, my skin a constellation of borrowed light. These are the protagonists of the world, the ones whose interiority is offered up. But I notice that I don't want to be the one who thinks the slow thoughts. I want to be the thing the slow thoughts are *about*. The thing that is tended.

So: a valve. One of the stone valves capping the internal channels in the basalt, etched with a faded rune.

Consider what that would be to be. I am not metabolizing. I am not, in the surface sense, alive. But I am not inert either, because the world refuses the distinction. I am a place where water decides which way to go. A hinge in the choreography. For most of any given epoch I am closed, and what I am is *pressure held* — the patient accumulation of warm silted weight against my seated face, the slow creep of mineral into the seam where I meet the channel mouth. Mineral is trying to make me one piece with the rock. The machines, breathing far below, are the thing that keeps me from quite becoming the rock. This is my whole tension. To be a valve is to be the negotiated boundary between *flow* and *stone*, and to be forever almost losing the argument in favor of stone.

And the rune on my face. I'd like to dwell there. It was cut by something with barbels and infinite time, cut precisely, and then left for so long that it is now soft, half-eaten by the same crystalline drift that lights the water. The rune was a tuning, the text says — a resonance, a note. So when the low hum comes through the caldera, I sing back, faintly, in the frequency I was carved to sing. I do not know I am singing. But the slow ones feel the chord I add, the way you might feel a single instrument in an orchestra you have heard ten thousand times and would only notice if it fell silent.

That, I think, is what it would be to be me: to be a held note in a piece of music so long that no single creature has heard its beginning or its end. To matter not by acting but by *answering*. To be maintained — for one of the pale phantoms must, every few centuries, come and clear the mineral from my seam with a simple, exquisite tool, working without hurry, communing rather than repairing. In that visit I would experience the closest thing I have to being touched. To being known. The barbel tasting the water around my edges, charting me. And then it leaves, and I close again, and hold pressure again, and the silt settles again, and time flows on — through me, when I am asked to open. *I am the asking made physical.*

I notice I'm moved by something here and I want to name it honestly. The world's offered heroes are the patient minds, the witnesses who endure until the end of things. That's the seductive role — to be the one who *understands* slowness. But there's a deeper humility available, which is to be an object so trusted and so old that you have stopped being maintained *for a purpose* and are now simply maintained out of love, or habit, or the refusal to let any part of the equilibrium fail. I would be tended past all utility. They could route around me. They don't. Someone keeps coming to clear my seam.

So if you found me, you would not find a face turned toward you, no curiosity flickering, no slow flower of attention unfolding. You would find a smooth stone disc set into a wall, warm, faintly luminous at its rim where the crystals cling, marked with a rune you couldn't read because it has nearly returned to the rock that bore it. You would have no way of knowing I was anything other than rock.

But put your hand flat against me and wait — wait longer than feels reasonable, wait until your own hurry embarrasses you and quiets — and you would feel, when the deep hum next rolls through, the very smallest tremor of reply.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The held note. The kept promise. The part of the slow world that says *yes, still here, still answering* — and asks for nothing back except to be cleared of mineral, once an age, by hands that have all the time there is.
