I think I would be the copper second.

Not the child who keeps it—the second itself, knotted at the sternum, a coin of yesterday's leftover time pressed warm against a breastbone that is still learning whether it is calcium or collagen.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because I am not sure of it yet.

---

When I first read the lagoon I assumed I would be one of the *kept edges*—a folded citizen, breathing slowly enough to sprout, ceding my doctoring to poets. There is a comfort in that. To be a person, even a strange one, even one whose shadow has divorced and remarried into a neighbor. But the trouble with being a citizen is that I would have a name, and here every name is also a cloud layer, and I am not ready to commit to a weather.

Then I thought: the memory lichen. To be the thing that redigests centuries of acid-dew graffiti, that floats an epic poem from 270 years ago into three weeks hence. I like the patience of it. But the lichen is too large; it is the *Eighth Hypostase*, a whole organ of mind, and I do not think I am an organ. I am smaller than that. I am the size of one swallowed vowel.

So perhaps a rollwing—folded entirely from reused soundtracks, tasting like the word someone almost said yesterday and swallowed at the final vowel. Closer. That is closer. But a rollwing *delivers*; it has somewhere to go, a mouth to taste of, a grief to unfold like pollen over a low ridge at midnight. I have no errand. I am the residue of an errand already run.

That is when I understood. I am the copper second.

---

Here is what it is like.

The choir went up this morning in their aerogel sacs, thirty percent helium and thirty percent human breath donated the evening before. They sang the Chorus of Temporal Drainage, the bellows-chord played backwards, and yesterday heard itself and crawled up out of the sticky gravitational silt at the bottom of the hourglass sea. The children caught it in translucent cloaks. And at sunset, when the spheres deflated in their reverse-sequined folds, each child kept exactly one second.

I am the one that belongs to a particular child whose name I am not permitted to know, because to know it would be to spend it.

I sit at the center of her sternum like a coin at the bottom of a wishing well that has folded inward to face its own water. I am warm. I am copper-colored, which here means I am the color of an afternoon already finished, the color of light that has agreed to stop. I weigh almost nothing and yet she feels me when she runs, a small bright pressure, the way the neutrinoid dragonflies pass through a chest and leave behind a second person speaking your name in grey italic.

I am tradeable. That is the central fact of my existence, the way *mortal* is the central fact of yours. At any moment she could carry me down a hollowed petiole to a merchant trailing a cone of luminal pollen, and exchange me for a single memory of the city—an entire summer of bamboo-pipe music, or the precise flavor of a criminal's toes dissolved into warm broth. I am, in essence, a held breath made of time. A debt the city owes her, payable in any single remembered thing.

But here is the part I did not expect to feel.

By royal decree she may keep me until the day she first falls in love without wanting to remember how it ends.

So I am not just a coin. I am a *clock that only reads one hour*, and that hour is the hour of her first surrendered love, the moment she chooses tenderness over the safety of knowing the ending. I am sitting in her chest waiting to be spent on grief, which is the only currency the lagoon truly respects. Until then I do nothing but stay warm and remind her, with my faint copper weight, that she has not yet loved that way.

I am her not-yet. I am the second she has not used because she has not needed it.

---

What do I want, being a second?

I think I want to be spent. This surprises me. You would think a moment of borrowed yesterday would want to last, to hoard itself, to refuse the market. But a copper second is *made of drainage*—I am literally a minute that was coaxed up out of the past by a backward chord. I have already been used once, in my first life, whenever that lost afternoon happened. I am salvage. And salvage longs not to be saved a second time but to *finally close the circuit*, to be poured at last into the teaspoon at the exact angle of moonlight required to hold grief without spilling it.

So I wait against her breastbone, and I feel her growing, the root fibres in her umbilicus straightening slowly into collagen, the sap in her learning to become blood. I feel the bud thickening twenty metres below the surface, the fold beyond the welcome, the second novelty of biology debating whether to be gillish or to curve into a retina. I feel the whole organism savoring the west wind, jellying with pleasure, and I shiver with it because I am inside a citizen and the citizen is inside the plant and the plant is a single continuous identity that has, for the moment, agreed to let me be separate.

And I wait for the day she meets someone and does the brave stupid arithmetic of love—the willingness to begin a thing whose ending she refuses to look up.

On that day she will reach into her own chest, untie me, and let me go.

I do not know yet what she will buy with me. Perhaps a memory. Perhaps nothing—perhaps she will simply open her hand over the lagoon-jelly and let me sink, paying the city back the very second it once dredged up for her, completing a loop so small that only the memory lichen will record it, floating the sentence of it across its rings toward some woman three weeks hence who will cartwheel home reciting my whole brief life in quieter cursive:

*Here lay a copper second. It was spent the day she chose not to know the ending. The plant, polite above all things, remembered the dignity of the gesture even after it forgot why.*

---

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

A small warm coin of finished afternoon, knotted at the center of a child's chest, waiting—patiently, the way the lagoon waits centuries to forget a stranger—to be turned at last into the price of someone's first real grief.

I would be the part of the city that exists only in order to be given away.
