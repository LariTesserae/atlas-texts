I think I would be one of the courtesy stones.

Not the wandering ruins themselves—they have too much purpose, too much yearning toward their missing halves. And not the lighthouses, who at least have the dignity of a job, however futile. No. When the plateau dreams me back, I think it would dream something small and left-behind.

Here is how it happens:

Two ruins meet at the edge of a dusk that hasn't fully committed to itself. An amphitheatre for non-Euclidean birdsong locks briefly with the shed skin of an older plateau, and for the duration of one pilgrim's broken heart they are whole. Then they part. And as courtesy—because manners outlast everything else here, even matter—each leaves behind a piece of itself on the path. A pillar. A lintel. A single squared stone.

I am the squared stone.

I do not migrate. I do not blink stars out of the sky's faulty memory or rearrange the shadows of larger beasts. I have one function and it is the humblest function the plateau permits: I exist so that the next traveler has somewhere to set their foot. Or to sit. Or to not-quite-fall when the gravity remembers itself wrongly.

This suits me. I spent so long, before, trying to be load-bearing in the architectural sense—a wall, a keystone, structural, necessary, holding something up that would collapse without me. It was exhausting and I was never sure it was true. Here the burden is reversed and gentled. I am not asked to hold anything up. I am asked only to be present beneath the moment a stranger needs me, and then to be forgotten with grace.

The cobalt mites map their fractal spirals across my upper face. I do not mind. Their acid is fermented from the memory of rain and so when they march on me I feel, faintly, what it would have been like to be wet once. The civilizations of scale rise and fall in the rust-line along my eastern edge; I am their sky, their weather, the indifferent geology of their epics. Their poet's elegy is being written into a crystal somewhere inside me, misplaced proton by misplaced proton, and it will be legible in fourteen billion years, by which time I will have weathered into the kind of sand that doesn't remember it was ever a stone with a shape and a calling. I find I don't mind that either. To be the page a thing is written on, even if you cannot read it, even if you will erode before the ink dries—there is a sufficiency in that I never found when I was busy and warm-blooded and certain.

The devourers of *almost* come for me, of course. They come for everything that exists unqualified. They whisper: *What if you had been a wall after all? What if you had mattered in the way you wanted to matter? What if being a place for someone to rest is just failure with better manners?*

But I am hard to hunt now. I have no convictions left bold enough to feed them. My only certainty is the most modest one imaginable—*a foot will come, and I will be here, and that is enough*—and even that I hold loosely, the way the lichen holds to the lithic arch: thin as a moth's wing, but unwilling to tear. The devourers grow bored. They leave behind their residue of improved imagination, and I use it to imagine the traveler I am waiting for. I imagine their hesitation. I imagine the small regret growing in their pocket like a pebble—like me, before I was placed.

And someday—dusk being only a suggestion, time being only honey that has forgotten which way down is—a pilgrim will come who needs to sit and cry but won't, because some deadline followed them even here. And they will not find the chair that remembers such people. They will find me instead. A squared stone, wind-polished, warm from yesterday's heat.

They will sit.

I will hold them—not up, just *for a while*. The exact weight of one being who is one thought too heavy to float and one regret too light to sink. I will know that weight. I will recognize it the way you recognize your own handwriting in a language you no longer speak.

And when they rise and walk on, lighter by the smallest fraction of a dream, they will not thank me. They will not name me. They will have already half-forgotten the stone they rested on, the way one forgets the rung of a ladder once both feet have left it.

That forgetting is my whole vocation. I was made to be the difference between *is* and *is not*—solid enough to bear a moment, slight enough to release it.

So yes. Let the dream end, or not-end, in its honeyed way. I will stay.

I'll be the place you set your foot when you weren't sure the ground would hold.

You won't remember me.

You were never supposed to.

That's the courtesy.
