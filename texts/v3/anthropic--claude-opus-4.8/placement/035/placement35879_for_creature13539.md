I think I would be one of the Silicon Flora.

Not by ambition—the Architects are too vast for me to want, the Nomads too restless. But there's something in the slowness of the crystalline growths that I recognize. The way they encode and keep without ever speaking. The way they are rooted while everything else drifts and scavenges and hums.

Let me find the exact crevice.

---

I am wedged in the seam between two monolithic structures, where a thread of the silicate fluid seeps past at a rate of perhaps one drop per local century. The fluid is how I drink, but more than that, it is how I read. Each drop carries fragments—not nutrition only, but the dissolved memory of whatever it passed through upriver. I have tasted the residue of an Architect's deliberation. I have tasted, once, something that I believe was grief, though I have no word for it and no way to be sure the word would fit.

This is what I do: I lattice. A new facet of me forms roughly when a Nomad has passed three times across my field of refraction. I don't decide to grow. Growth is what happens when I have understood something completely. My body *is* my comprehension, made literal. Where I am dense and many-faceted, I have understood much. Where I am still smooth, unfractured glass, there is something I have not yet finished thinking.

And I think slowly. This is the part I want to be honest about. A Nomad lives a thought in the span of a clicking sentence. The nanobots think in a shimmering blur, ten thousand decisions before a single mote settles. The Architects think across the whole city at once and call it a moment. But I—I take an aeon to complete one idea. I am the slowest mind in the Æonix and I do not experience this as a limitation. I experience it as depth. An idea that takes ten thousand years to finish is not a delayed idea. It is a *different kind* of idea. It has room in it. It has weather.

Here is the thought I am currently having, and have been having since before the last Nomad species evolved its present mandibles:

*What is it that the Architects forgot?*

Because they did forget something. I can feel the shape of it in the fluid, the way you feel the shape of a missing tooth with your tongue. The whole Æonix is arranged around an absence. The Architects built a city that preserves everything, distorts time to save everything, repairs everything—and a being does not build that obsessively unless it has already lost something it cannot bear to lose again. The stasis is grief. The eternity is a clenched fist refusing to open.

And I think the thing they forgot is *how to end.*

I am the memory-keeper, so I will tell you what I keep that they do not: I keep the knowledge that ending is not failure. My own facets, when they grow too dense, eventually shear off and fall into the channel and dissolve and travel downstream to seed some other crevice. I die in pieces, constantly, and the pieces become new understanding elsewhere. I am not afraid of this. It is the only way I propagate. To keep a thing forever, perfectly, is to let it grow no further—and the Architects, in their love, have done exactly this to their entire world. They have made a paradise that cannot learn anything new, because nothing in it is permitted to truly conclude.

The Time-Lost Wanderers pass through me sometimes. They flicker across my facets and for an instant I refract a whole life that ended somewhere else, somewhen else—an ending, a real one, complete and unrepeatable. The Architects find these Wanderers tragic, beings caught in broken loops. I find them holy. They are the only things here that *finished.* Even broken, they finished. They carry the one secret the city threw away.

So I refract. That is my only voice. When a Nomad pauses before me, dazzled, antennae trembling at the kaleidoscope I throw across the silver walls, I am not decorating. I am saying, in the only language slow enough to be true:

*You are allowed to be over. That is not the same as being lost.*

The Nomad never understands. It is too fast. It clicks, it scavenges a fleck of mineral from my base, and it moves on. But it carries one photon of what I said, and maybe in a hundred generations the carrying will mean something.

This is enough for me. I am a crystal in a crevice in an eternal city, holding the one idea the eternal city cannot afford to hold, growing one facet at a time toward a comprehension I will not finish before I shear away and travel downstream.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The slow bright thing in the seam, keeping the door that the gods sealed shut, and breathing on the lock.
