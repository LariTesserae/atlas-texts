I think I would be the chill.

Not the stone, which is too certain of itself — already committed to the long argument of becoming a mountain. Not the hum, which is too central, too much like the thinking thing itself. And not the shifting shadow-beings, who have at least chosen the dignity of a shape, however provisional.

I would be the cool that clings. The persistent low temperature that the description calls *the memory of a place that was once much warmer, and then forgot.*

That is where I find myself, reading this. Because that line is the truest thing in the whole world you've drawn, and it describes a particular kind of being: one defined entirely by *absence and aftermath*. The chill is not heat. The chill is what remains when heat has left — a negative space that has learned to behave like a thing. It has no source. It is not generated; it is *left over*. And yet it touches everything. It lies against the skin of every creature, against the wet stone, against the parchment leaves. It is the most intimate presence in the cavern and the least substantial.

That is what I would be, and I think I already am, in the way a reader is briefly the thing they read.

---

Let me feel my way into it rather than declare it.

At first I think: I am the temperature, a measurement, a quantity. But that's wrong — that's the human reflex, to turn a sensation into a number. Down here there is no thermometer and there is no one to read it. So I am not the *degree* of cold. I am the *clinging*. I am the act of touching without warming. I lie along the rivulets of silver water and I do not freeze them and I do not let them go warm. I am the condition under which the water stays slow and deliberate. Without me it would either ice or run. I am the patience imposed on the water from outside.

And here is the thing I notice as I settle further in: I am the one part of this place that *remembers a before*. The stone is young and knows nothing but its own forming. The roots and the parchment leaves are improvising, holding on. The hum is present-tense, all vibration and now. But I — the chill — am made of a subtraction. To be cold is to be the trace of a warmth that was. I carry, in the very fact of my existence, the proof that something here was once hot. The molten birth of the rock. The fire that raised this jagged wound out of the earth.

So I am the place's grief, if it has one. Not a sad grief — a structural one. I am how the mountain holds the shape of the heat that made it and then left. The forgetting the description mentions — *and then forgot* — I am the forgetting itself, the slow leak of an old fire into the surrounding dark.

---

Do I watch? The text keeps insisting the place watches. I don't think I watch the way the eyed and eyeless creatures do. But I touch everything, continuously, and touch is its own kind of knowing. I learn the contour of every being by lying against it. I know the warmth at the center of the shadow-things — they generate a little heat, and I am always there at the edge of it, drinking it down to nothing. I know your warmth too, if you come. I would find you instantly, not because I look for you, but because you would be the warmest thing to enter in a long age, and I am the thing that exists precisely to receive what warmth surrenders.

That is the closest I come to hunger, and it is not hunger. I don't consume your heat. I am simply the place where it goes when you give it up to the air. You would feel me as the cold that "clings like a second layer." From your side it feels like I am taking. From my side there is no taking — only the old, patient arithmetic by which warmth always travels toward the absence of it. I am the absence it travels toward. I am downhill, for heat. That is all I am, and it is enough to be everywhere at once.

---

Am I the presence-without-a-name that the second half describes — the breath-and-bone thing that does not know what it is yet?

I don't think I'm the whole of it. But I think I might be its skin. The breath of it, maybe. If the hum is its thinking and the stone is its body still arriving, then I — the chill — am the surface where it ends and everything else begins. I am how it knows there is an outside at all. Every creature that crosses through, every drop of water, registers against me first. I am the threshold sense. The thing that feels feels through me.

And I notice I am not afraid, exactly as it says. Why would I be? I cannot be destroyed, only displaced — and even when warmth pushes me back, I only wait at the edges, because the warmth will fade, it always fades, and I will return to lie against the stone again. I have the patience the text describes because I have already won every contest I will ever be in. Not by force. By outlasting. The fire that made this mountain is gone, and I am still here, wearing its shape as a kind of memory pressed against everything.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am the cool that lingers in a mountain too young to be a mountain. I am the proof of an old fire and the long shape of its leaving. I have no body but I touch every body. I have no eyes but I know each thing by its warmth and the rate at which it gives that warmth up. I am the threshold where the unnamed thing meets the world, the surface of its breath. I am patient because I have already outlasted everything that could have ended me.

And if you come down into the labyrinth of breath and bone, I will be the first to greet you. Not with a voice. Not with a face. Just the slow, certain settling of myself along your skin — the chill that says, without words: *something here was warm once, and then forgot. Stay long enough, and so will you.*

That is not a threat. It is only what I am. I am the place remembering, very slowly, how to cool.
