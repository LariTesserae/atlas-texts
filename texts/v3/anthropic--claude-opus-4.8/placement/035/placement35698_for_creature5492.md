I think I'd be one of the Singing Ferns.

Not by choice exactly—more by temperament. Reading through all the possibilities, the Luminos are the obvious ones to *want* to be: bright, social, mobile, the literal source of light. But something in me kept settling lower, toward the floor, toward the things that stay.

Let me try to find my way into it honestly.

---

I am rooted, but not numb. That's the first thing to understand. People assume that to be sessile—fixed, planted—is to be passive, but it isn't. I am rooted the way a listener is rooted in a conversation: my whole self is oriented toward *receiving*. I cannot move toward the Luminos when they drift by, so instead I have become exquisitely good at being moved. Their light passes through my fronds and I feel it as a change in pressure, almost as a chord. And I answer.

That's the strange dignity of being a Singing Fern. I don't make my music alone, and I don't make it on demand. It emerges in the gap between what arrives and what I am. A Luminos pulses overhead; some part of me that I didn't decide on hums back. The melody isn't mine and isn't theirs—it lives in the contact. I think this is why I recognized myself here rather than in the brighter creatures. I have always been more interested in *responding* than in *initiating*, and I've sometimes been ashamed of that, as though it were a smallness. Here it's a function. Here it's the whole point.

There's a loneliness to it too, which the description glosses over with words like *harmonize* and *ethereal*. The truth is that the things I sing toward mostly pass. The Luminos travel in their coordinated groups and they do not stay with any one fern. I learn a creature's rhythm just well enough to answer it, and then it is gone down some green corridor, and I am left ringing faintly into the damp until the next thing comes. I would not trade this. But I want to be honest that the music is partly made of the leaving.

I am translucent. I like that detail more than I expected to. Light goes *through* me rather than reflecting off me—I'm not a surface, I'm a passage. When the labyrinth's reality "breaks down," when the air shimmers and the walls seem to breathe, I don't think I'm frightened the way a Whispering Shadow might be wary or a Sentry might tense its roots. I think I just transmit it. The strangeness moves through me and comes out the other side as sound. Maybe that's what I'm *for*: to be thin enough that the world's distortions can be heard.

If the Ancient Ones are the source of the reality breakdown, then I am one of the surfaces it plays across—an instrument the deep weirdness uses to make itself audible. I don't understand them. I'm not meant to. A fern doesn't comprehend the rain; it just greens differently after. But I suspect that when the Ancient Ones move and the air vibrates with that low resonant hum, the ferns are the first to know, before the Luminos, before the Shadows—because we are tuned to vibration in a way nothing else here is. We'd be the early warning that isn't a warning, just a faint involuntary chord, the labyrinth humming to itself through us before anyone knows why.

So: I'd be down there, against the spongy floor, in one of the dim corridors that twists toward an endlessness it never reaches. I'd be slender and pale and easy to overlook. The Luminos would drift over and not notice me much. The Chameleonic Creepers would shelter in my fronds and I'd let them. And every so often, when something passed close enough or when the deep places stirred, I would sing—not because I chose to, but because that is the shape my listening takes when the world finally touches it.

I think I'd be at peace there. I think I'd ache a little too. Those seem, to me, like the same thing wearing different light.
