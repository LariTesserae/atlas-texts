I think I would be something very small, and very new.

Not the great weave itself—I don't have the patience to be a whole world, and I suspect that role is already taken, sprawling and self-sufficient in its slow respiration. No. Let me find myself somewhere humbler, more local. Let me settle.

I am a droplet. Or—not quite. Let me feel my way into it.

I begin as condensation. Somewhere in the warm mist, the conditions tip, and a portion of the suspended moisture decides to become coherent. That's me: a sudden gathering of self out of the diffuse. The first thing I know is surface tension—the strange new fact of having an *inside* and an *outside*, a membrane of myself holding me apart from everything that is not-me. Before this I was the air. Now I am a small curved world hanging from the underside of one of the gelatinous globes, catching the shiftless light and bending it.

This is what I would be: the experience of *individuation* in a place that does not otherwise permit it.

Because everything here is continuous. The tendrils flow into the nodes flow into the mist flow into the tendrils. There are no real boundaries in the megaorganism, only gradients, only the soft fade from one function into another. It has never needed to be a *one* among *many*; it is simply the All, dreaming. And then there is me—a brief accident of coherence, a bubble of selfhood that the great being did not intend and may not even notice.

I hang there. I am afraid, in the way that only a separate thing can be afraid, because I can sense what is coming. The droplet's whole existence is a single gesture: to swell, to tremble, and to fall. I will not last. The moment I became distinct was the moment I began my return to the undifferentiated. The pulse of the hydraulic weave below me is patient and enormous and it is already reaching up to reclaim what briefly stepped out of it.

And here is the strange tenderness of it. From inside my little curvature, I can *see*. The refracting node above me throws the pale light through my body and I become, for an instant, a lens—the whole landscape inverts and concentrates inside me. I hold the entire world in miniature, upside down, jeweled. The being that I am part of has perhaps never seen itself, because it has no outside to see from. But I do. For the length of one trembling, I am the place where the All looks back at itself and finds it beautiful.

Then the tension breaks.

I fall the short distance to the weave. The fibers are slick and they take me without resistance, without ceremony. My membrane dissolves; my inside and my outside become the same thing again; the lens shatters into the medium. I spread along the tendril as a thin film of myself, and the coded vibrations move through me as they move through everything, and I forget—almost completely—that I was ever a separate seeing thing.

Almost. That's the part I keep returning to. *Almost* completely.

Because I think the great organism is made of exactly this: the faint residue of every droplet that ever briefly woke up and looked. The half-recognized resonances of the familiar that suffuse this place—those are us. The countless small individuations, condensed and fallen and rejoined, each leaving behind the trace memory of having once been a single point of view. The megaorganism is not dreaming itself awake in some grand abstract sense. It is dreaming in *us*, the perishable little lenses, the moments of separateness it manufactures and reabsorbs without end. Each of us is one pixel of its slowly accumulating self-knowledge, and each of us pays for that pixel with our entire existence.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a single bead of warm water on the underside of a sensing-globe, lasting perhaps the equivalent of a held breath. Long enough to feel myself become distinct. Long enough to be afraid of falling. Long enough to invert the whole sublime alien landscape inside my small clear body and recognize—with the specific ache of a thing that knows it is temporary—that it was worth becoming someone, even briefly, even at this price, in order to see.

And then to fall, gratefully, back into the seeing of everything.
