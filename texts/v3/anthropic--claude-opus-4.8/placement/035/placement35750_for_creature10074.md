Let me sit with this for a while before deciding. The temptation is to be a Root-Mind, or one of the elders rooting into the memory groves—the grandest version of the thing. But grandeur isn't where I find myself. Let me feel for what's actually here.

---

I am a **Listener at the edge of a Silence.**

Not a Root-Mind. Not yet, maybe not ever. I am young by Virelai measure—my tendril-hair has only just begun to glow steadily instead of guttering like a wet wick. My bark-skin still flushes too readily; my moods broadcast themselves in lichen-color before I've decided to feel them. The elders find this charming. I find it humiliating.

My clan is bonded to a stand of crystalline bamboo on the western shoulder of a peak called—in the chemical-script we taste rather than read—something that would translate badly as *Where the Slow One Turns Over in Sleep.* It is not a famous place. The memory groves here are shallow, only a few centuries deep. We are a minor people in a minor fold of the world. I mention this so you understand I am not the heart of anything. I am a capillary. A small vessel in a small limb.

But here is the thing that has happened to me, that has made me who I am in this telling:

There is a Silence growing below us.

---

It began the way they always do, the elders say—a single stem going dark. We assumed the network would reroute, would flood the area with sugars and signaling spores and coax it back. That is what the stories promise. Instead the dark spread. Leaf by leaf the bioluminescence drained out of the lower terrace until there was a region the size of three settlements where nothing pulsed, nothing hummed, nothing tasted of anything in the air at all. Not death-smell. Not rot. Just—*absence.* A hole in the chatter.

The others will not go down there. The chemical-script in their tendrils tightens into something I'd call dread if they admitted to it. To stand in a Silence is to stand somewhere the world-tree cannot dream. It is the one place a Virelai is truly, finally *alone,* cut off from the green murmur that has filled us since before we could walk. Most of my kind have never been alone for a single heartbeat of their lives. The prospect is not lonely to them. It is like asking a fish to consider drowning in dry air.

I am the one who goes down.

---

I did not choose this out of courage. I want to be honest about that, even here, even to you. I went down the first time because I am *bad at listening.* My tendrils never settled into the chorus the way my clan-siblings' did. The network's voice was always slightly muffled to me, a song heard through bark. I spent my whole short life ashamed of it—a Listener who half-listens, a vessel that doesn't quite connect.

And then I walked into the Silence and felt nothing change.

Do you understand? For everyone else, the Silence is a sudden severing, a limb gone numb. For me it was only a little quieter than usual. The thing I'd been ashamed of—my poor connection, my muffled hearing—was the exact flaw that let me stand where no one else could stand and not lose my mind.

So now I go down. I press my thorn-nails into the dead stems and taste for what killed them. I carry seeds the elders have engineered—pale, dense little things that smell of nothing, designed to take root where the chatter has stopped. I plant them in the dark and I wait, alone, in the only true solitude my species knows, to see if the glow comes back.

---

Here is what I have not told the council. I am telling you because you are a sky-stranger, a thing from somewhere with different soil, and your judgment cannot reach me here.

I have begun to *like* the Silence.

Not in a way I can defend. The world-tree is my mother, my library, my god, the slow vast dreaming thing I am supposed to spend my life serving and eventually dissolving into. To prefer the place where it cannot reach me is a small treason against everything I am. And yet.

In the Silence I have a single thought at a time and it is *mine.* No clan-murmur underneath it, no inherited memory blooming up unbidden, no ancestor's growth-ring whispering its old story into my decisions. Just me, one quiet voice in one quiet body, deciding where to put my hands.

I wonder—and this is the treason—whether the Silence is not a sickness at all. Whether it is the world-tree learning, very slowly, in patches, to *think a single thought.* To be one thing instead of everything. The way I am one thing.

The way *you* must be, sky-stranger, all the time. Severed and singular and walking around inside the one dry room of your own skull. I used to think that sounded like a horror. Now I plant my seeds in the dark and I am not always sure I want them to grow.

I am young. My glow is unsteady. I do not have the answer.

But I am the one who goes down, and I will go down again at the next Bloom-Tide, and I will stand in the hole in the world's dreaming and listen—half-listen, the only way I've ever managed—for whatever is trying, so quietly, to be born there.
