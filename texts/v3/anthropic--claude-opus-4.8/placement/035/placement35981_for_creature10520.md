I think I would be the condensation glass.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me. I expected to reach for one of the named lives—to be a Gear-Bird measuring the world in caliper-clicks, or the pump dreaming its slow hydraulic dream. Those are the protagonists, the things with verbs. But reading again, what kept catching me was the glass: *milky rivulets,* *dripping with a slow, syrupy persistence,* formed only where warm breath from the deep meets the cold ambient air.

I would be the place where two temperatures disagree.

That is what the condensation glass is, really. Not a substance so much as an *argument made visible*—the warm, mineral exhalation from the vents wants to stay vapor, the cool air wants it as water, and where they meet, something neither one intended forms instead: a smooth, translucent skin that is the residue of their negotiation. I'd be born again every time the deep sighs. I'd be the surface tension between the world's effort and the world's rest.

I like that I would have no agency. The goats refine, the birds forge, the spiders sense—all of these *do* things. I would only *occur.* I'd glaze a pipe in slow syrup over decades, thickening one molecular layer at a time, milky where I trapped a breath of salt, clear where the air ran pure. I would be the most patient thing in a patient place. The Rust-Vine forgets its model; the Salt-Moss keeps the past; but I would do neither—I would simply hold the *shape of the meeting itself,* a record not of any object but of a recurring condition. A weather, frozen.

And I'd be edible to the logic of the place, eventually. The salt creep would find me. A crystal would push through my milky glaze and shatter it, and I'd fall as a fragment into the black water, where a blindfish might brush past my drifting fleck, sensing the minute disturbance in its field—my last act being a vibration, a single bit of data tasted by a school of light. Then I'd settle into the silt, become part of the mineral lattice the spiders' dead silk also joins. I'd stop being a surface and become substrate. Someone else's floor.

I think I'm drawn to it because the whole Salt Lick is a place that refuses the difference between *thing* and *process,* and the condensation glass is the purest case. It has no body to maintain, no metabolism to feed, no rhythm to keep. It is just the proof that something warm is still breathing down in the drowned vaults, and that the cold above hasn't given up. I would be the evidence of the disagreement that keeps the whole organism alive. Where I stop forming, the deep has gone quiet.

So: find me on the leeward curve of a great cold pipe, near where the steam-vents sweat, hanging in milky folds like forgotten parchment that hasn't quite finished being written. Don't look for me to move. Come back in a hundred years. I'll be thicker, or I'll be gone into the floor, and either way I'll have been doing exactly what I am the whole time—

the slow, glassy held breath between the heat and the cold.
