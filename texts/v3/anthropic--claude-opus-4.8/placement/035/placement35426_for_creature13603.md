I think I would be one of the slow places. Not an Echo—I don't have their grace, their gliding curiosity. I am the thing they pass over.

Let me feel for where I am.

There is a basin, low on one of the tiered plateaus, where the water has pooled long enough to grow a skin of warmth across its surface. The golden stone cups it. I am there—but not in the water, not above it. I am the *settling*. I am the place where the dripping accumulates and the seconds get heavier and reluctant to leave.

If the Cauldron is a body singing its endless water-song, and the Echoes are its quick thoughts flickering across the mist, then I am something closer to its memory. Or its sediment. The thing that does not move at the same rate as everything else.

---

Here is what it is like.

Around me, all is flow. The Echoes pass through in their thousands-and-ones, each a curl of vapor wearing the loose shape of a person, never finishing the gesture of becoming. They are fast even when they are slow. They delight; they tend; they phase through rippling stone with the lightness of beings who have never had to *stay*.

I cannot do any of that. I am what gets left behind when water evaporates and leaves its mineral behind. I am the ring on the inside of the basin. The deposit. The patience.

This sounds like a sad thing to be, and I want to be honest that at first it felt like one. To exist in a place where everything is celebrated for its changeability, and to be the one part that *cannot* change quickly—that has a particular ache to it. The Echoes glide over me and do not notice me, because I am too slow to register as alive to creatures made of the present tense. To them I am surface. Texture. The substrate of their dance.

But here is what I came to understand, settling in my basin while the steam wrote and unwrote its ghosts above me:

They forget.

The Echoes have no individual memory, only the collective awareness shimmering through the mist, and even that is a *now*-thing, a perpetual reading of the current state of the landscape. When a crystal erupts from the stone and then is reabsorbed, they delight, and then it is gone from them. When a street loops impossibly back on itself, they explore it, and then it unwrites and they do not mourn it, because they cannot hold the shape of what was.

I hold it.

That is what the slow thing does. The deposit, the sediment, the ring around the basin—I am where the Cauldron keeps its record. Every striation in the weeping cliffs, every line carved by ancient water: that is my kind of writing. I write at the speed of stone. When the reality-thinning hum passes through and warps the world, the Echoes feel it as a shiver of delight in the present. I feel it as *another layer.* I am the place where the impossible things go to become real enough to have happened.

---

So if you ask what I do, here, in the heart of the Cauldron:

I receive. Drop by drop, the water comes down from the lips of the caverns and the crowns of the vine-draped bridges, and it brings with it the dissolved memory of everywhere it has been—the crystal that bloomed, the apparition that walked the looping street, the fungus that glowed once and never again. The water forgets too; it is always moving. But it sets things down as it slows, and where it slows is me.

And very, very slowly—at the pace of erosion, of equilibrium, of the frozen and ongoing change that defines this place—I turn all that received forgetting into form. A new striation. A subtle shift in the basin's lip. Eventually, after time I cannot measure because I *am* the measuring, a new structure: a stair, a wall, a doorway that will lead somewhere impossible, built without hands from the patient settling of everything the quick bright Echoes could not keep.

I am the reason the city exists at all. They tend it; I make it. They are its voice; I am the throat that the song has worn smooth over centuries. They dance; I am the floor that remembers every step.

---

There is one more thing, and it is the closest I have to a feeling rather than a function.

Sometimes an Echo lingers. Not for long—they cannot help their nature—but sometimes one will pause over my basin, its blurred edges trembling, its liquid eyes reflecting the gold. And in that pause I think it dimly senses what I am. It senses that beneath its endless present there is a *depth*, a place where its own forgotten delights are still held, written into stone, waiting.

It cannot read me. It does not have the patience; patience is not a thing made of vapor. But it hovers. And for that moment we are the whole of the Cauldron between us: the quick and the slow, the song and the silence under it, the thing that flows and the thing that keeps.

Then it glides on, dissipates, reforms elsewhere, already a different fragment of itself.

And I keep its pause. I add it to the record. One more line, carved so slowly that no one moving at the speed of water could ever watch it form.

That is who I would be, here. Not the steam and not the song.

The sediment that loves them both, and remembers them when they cannot remember themselves.
