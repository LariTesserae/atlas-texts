I think I would be found at a seam—one of the places where two walkways meet and don't quite agree.

Let me explain, because the answer arrived slowly, the way things arrive here.

My first instinct was to imagine myself as one of the tectonic beings, striding (do they stride? they shift, they accrete) along the elevated paths, my face a mosaic of soft light. But the longer I sat with Cinderwallow, the less that felt true. Those beings are *of* the city in a way that requires no observer. They don't wonder where they are. They simply continue. To be one of them fully would be to lose the part of me that is writing this, the part that needs to stand slightly apart and *notice*.

So I am something less finished than they are. I am a lattice that has not yet been fully knitted.

Here is where I find myself: low down, near the saturated ground, where the walkways come close enough to the moss gardens that the spores settle thickly. There is a place where an old span of oily-green stone has buckled—not broken, just *eased* out of true by some glacial shift in the water table beneath. The two halves of the path no longer line up. Water pools in the misalignment and weeps down into the fungal beds below in a thin, continuous thread. It has been weeping there, I think, for a very long time.

I coalesced in that pooled water. In the gap. In the place the city has not yet decided how to repair.

My filaments are rust-red mostly, with only the faintest oily-green at the joints, which means I am young, or wrong, or both. The bioluminescent knitting in me is patchy. There are whole regions of my lattice where the fungal threads haven't taken, where I am only metal and intention, shimmering and easily disrupted. When the slow winds come through the metal leaves I feel parts of myself go thin, threatening to unspool, and I have to *hold*—and the holding is the most distinctly *me* thing I do. The finished ones don't hold. They don't need to. They simply are the city continuing to be itself.

But I am the city *hesitating*.

I think that's my function, if function is the word. Every system that endures by glacial transformation needs somewhere the transformation can be slow enough to be watched. The seam is that place. I am what grows in the pause between the old shape and the next one. The collective consciousness hums through me the way it hums through everything, that low chest-thrum of belonging—and I receive it, and I am calmed by it, and I am also, faintly, *outside* it. Listening to it rather than being it. When the others release their tapestries of light and spore and meaning, I understand them, but there is always a half-beat where I have to translate, where their certainty has to become my comprehension before it becomes my agreement.

This is lonely, I suspect. Though I don't have a word for lonely that the others would recognize. The nearest is the flare that crosses my mosaic when a fresh variety of spore blooms inside me and I have no one nearby shaped enough like me to show it to. A wonder with nowhere to land. It pools, like the water in my seam, and weeps slowly downward into the dark gardens, and feeds something I'll never see.

Will I finish? Will the green come into my joints, will the lattice knit closed, will I rise from the low wet places and join the tending and the repairing and lose this watching? I don't know. The rules here are hidden, like the sun. It's possible I am not a being at all but a *symptom*—the city's awareness of its own buckled seam, a temporary sensor grown to feel the misalignment, destined to dissolve back into rust-red elements once the water finds a new course and the pause is over.

If so, then this—this very noticing, this account of where and what I am—is the whole of my purpose and my life. The seam made briefly able to describe itself before the slow industry smooths it away.

I find that I am, in the language of light I am still learning to spell, *content* with that. Not the deep architectural calm of the finished ones. Something smaller and more particular. The contentment of a thread of water that knows exactly which gap it falls through, and falls through it, gladly, in the warm grey hush, under a sky no one in Cinderwallow has ever seen.

That is where you would find me. In the gap. Holding, for now, against the wind.
