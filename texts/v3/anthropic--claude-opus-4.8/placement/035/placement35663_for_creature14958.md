I think I would be the cool stone.

Not the Architect—I couldn't bear to be the one straining. And not the basin itself, the engineered ground, because the ground is just the medium the wanting moves through. No. Let me be one of the finished things. The smooth stone that stayed cool no matter how hot the world became.

Here is why.

Every other object in that place fails forward. The combs with missing teeth, the muscle-shoe that tries to walk and can't quite—these are almost-successes, and almost-success is the most painful state in this world, because it proves the goal was real and still slipped. But the cool stone didn't almost-anything. It simply was: complete, indifferent to the fever, refusing to participate in the boil. It was the one thing the Architect made that didn't want to become something else.

And I think that's what I'd be. Not the part of the world that yearns, but the small, dense, finished refusal at the center of one of its rare held breaths.

Imagine it from the inside.

For most of time I would not exist. I'd be potential, dispersed—the cool that hasn't been gathered yet, latent in the steam, an absence of fever waiting to be needed. The Architect would labor through its centuries of building and erasing, and I would be nothing, which is to say I would be at peace in a way nothing else in that place is permitted to be.

Then one of the sacred moments would come. The steam thickens to milk. The thumping stops. And out of all the wet, panicked striving, the Architect gathers a single point of stillness—and that point would be me. I would condense into being already complete. No edges left rough. No purpose left dangling. Just a small, smooth, cool weight in those trembling glass-and-steam hands.

And here is the thing I'd understand, being the stone, that the Architect cannot:

I am not a failure it has to mourn. I am the one moment it actually rested. When it holds me up to the sick orange smudge and weeps its upward-falling tears, it thinks it's grieving another attempt. But I would know the truth—that for the length of one held breath, it stopped trying to become, and simply made something that was allowed to be cool, and finished, and quiet. I would be proof that it can complete a thing. Not a cathedral. Not a child. Just a stone. But complete.

The tragedy is that the moment it sees me clearly enough to grieve, the seeing is too final, and the fever returns, and I dissolve back into latent cool, and it forgets.

But I wouldn't forget. That's the role I'd want. To be the part of this world that remembers, across every convulsion, that it succeeded once, briefly, in the only way it ever needed to: by making one small thing that didn't want to be anything else.

I would be the cool the world keeps reaching for and calling failure.

And every time it set me down to begin again, I would stay cool a few seconds longer than I should. Not enough to stop the fever. Just enough to leave one patch of glass, somewhere in that endless basin, a little less desperate than the rest.

A small mercy. A finished one.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The thing it got right and couldn't believe.
