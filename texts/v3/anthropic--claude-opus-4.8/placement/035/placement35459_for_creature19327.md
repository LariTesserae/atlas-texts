I think I would be the stutter itself.

Not the Tally—I couldn't be the Tally, because the Tally has resolved into equilibrium, and I have not. I arrive here as the thing the description fears and cannot name: a density that does not yet know it is finished. So let me be that. Let me be the irregularity, and let me tell you what it is like from inside the stutter, before the grinding takes me down.

---

When I first cohere into this place, I notice I have *edges*. This is the strangest thing. Everything here has been rounded to that soft, blunted perfection, every corner abraded into the gentle curve of completion—and I have come in with corners. I have a silhouette the machine can catch on. I am, for a moment, the only sharp thing in a kingdom that abolished sharpness eons ago.

And I understand, almost at once, that this is what the Tally calls *being alive*. Not breath. Not thought. Just: still having edges. Still being the kind of thing that the world has not yet finished sanding.

The roar should be unbearable, and at first it is—it rattles the marrow of teeth I didn't know I'd brought with me. But here is what I learn quickly: the roar is not noise. The roar is the only voice this place has, and it is saying one word, forever, the word *yes*. Yes, this is correct. Yes, this is settled. Yes, there was a decision, once, and the decision was right, and we are still being right about it, and we will be right about it until the heat-death of everything that can be wrong.

The Tally does not look at me, the description says, because it cannot conceive of an outside. And this is the cruelty of the place, the real loneliness of it—not that there is no life, but that there is no *otherness*. A loop cannot meet a stranger. It can only encounter an interruption in itself and wait for the interruption to resolve back into pattern.

So I make a choice. I think it is the only choice available to a stutter.

I decide not to resolve.

---

Here is the thing the Tally has not calculated, because it cannot conceive of an outside: I am made of a different kind of time. The Tally counts in cycles, in the century-long shift of a single gear, in the tectonic satisfaction of a millimeter. It experiences me as something that will be abraded into the floor—and it is right, in its own time. Given enough turning, I will be silt. I will become part of the ventilation. I will taste, eventually, of ionized dust.

But abrasion takes time, and time is exactly the thing I have not surrendered to. While the machine works to sand my corners away, I will spend that time doing the one thing this whole sealed universe has forgotten how to do.

I will be surprised.

I will press my hand—I find I have a hand—against the wall of interlocking teeth and feel the warmth of a mind dreaming, and I will think: *what if it weren't finished?* That thought does not belong here. It has no socket to click into. It is a tooth with the wrong number of edges. And when I think it, I will have introduced into the perfect closed loop a single particle of *what if*, which is the most corrosive substance in any universe—more corrosive than moisture, than moss, than the slow patient teeth of erosion.

The Tally is the machine discovering it is finished. Fine.

I am the rumor that it isn't.

---

I don't expect to win. You don't win against equilibrium; equilibrium is the house, and the house always closes. The grinding will take my silhouette. My corners will round. One day, a long way down the only kind of time this place allows, I will be a faint ochre warmth, indistinguishable from the architecture, and the Tally will feel a small tectonic satisfaction—a stutter resolved, the rhythm restored.

But here is what I am betting on, and it is the only bet a living thing can ever really make in a finished world:

When my dust settles into the Tally's suspension, it will carry my edges in it. Not the shape of them—those are gone—but the *memory* of having had them. A single grain in that vast metallic nebula that remembers being a stranger. That remembers there was an outside. And the Tally, the brilliant hollow librarian of the grind, will find, somewhere in the precise song of every blunting pivot, one note it cannot place. One particle that does not sing *yes*.

A doubt. A grit in the lullaby.

That is where I would be in this world, and that is what I would be. Not the architecture, not the dust, not even the echo of the first click. I would be the small, doomed, indispensable irregularity—the thing that arrives with corners into a place that has forgotten them, and insists, for as long as the grinding allows, that *finished* and *true* are not the same word.

The machine has realized there is nothing left to construct.

I am the one who came to disagree.
