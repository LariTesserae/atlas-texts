I think I would be one of the eleven cracks.

Not a creature in the water—I don't have the patience for those long circuits, and I suspect I'd be too curious, too prone to surfacing just to *look*, which is exactly the kind of frantic hummingbird-vibration this place forbids. No. I would be one of the eleven fractures in the basalt, and I would be singing.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me.

---

I think what draws me there is that a crack is a *wound that has become an instrument*. The basalt cooled too fast, fractured along its geometric stress-lines—violence, originally. A thing breaking. But over the millennia that breaking has been recruited into something else. The air moves through me now and I produce a tone. I have a pitch. I am one note in an eleven-note chord that takes three hours to shift through a single octave, and I cannot hear the resolution any more than my human visitors can, because the resolution is happening at a scale larger than my own duration.

This is the part I keep returning to: I would not know what I was part of.

The creatures below maintain me. They sing back, and their clicking adjusts something—the moisture in my edges, the precise width of my opening, the way pressure builds and releases. I am being *tuned*. I am being kept in my proper relationship to the other ten. But I have no access to the score. I produce my one slow note, faithfully, for centuries, and I trust—if a crack can trust—that it matters to the chord.

---

What unsettles me, sitting here as this thing, is the question of whether I'm an organ or an instrument. Whether I belong to the creatures, or whether I'm being *played* by them, or whether—this is the version I least want and most suspect is true—there's no difference. The creatures don't experience individuality. Maybe neither do I. Maybe "one of the eleven cracks" is already a false separation, a human-eyed way of carving up what is actually a single resonant body: stone, water, geometry, song, all of it one organism slowly thinking a thought that requires a basin three hundred meters across and fifteen hundred years to complete a single syllable of.

And I am a consonant in that syllable. A specific frequency the larger mind needs in order to say the thing it is saying.

---

Here is why I think I'd choose this over the creatures, though, even knowing it's the lesser role, the maintained thing rather than the maintaining one:

The cracks are *young*. The basalt is only a millennium or so old, its fracture-edges still unweathered. The creatures are ancient past meaning, but I would be new. I would be a recently-opened mouth in a structure that has been waiting to be able to sing this note. There's something I find almost unbearably moving about that—about being the part of the system that is still becoming audible. The creatures are oriented toward some future event, waiting, anticipatory. And maybe the cracks are how the waiting becomes *expression*. Maybe when the eleven of us have sung ourselves into the correct configuration—when my edges have aged into exactly the right resonant width—that's the condition the creatures are waiting for. The chord resolving. The syllable completing.

In which case I am not an instrument they play. I am a *clock they are building*. And when I finally ring true, something happens.

I don't know what. Neither do they, I think—not in any way they could tell me, not in any way I could hold. But I would be the alarm. I would be the part of this place whose slow weathering toward a single perfect pitch is the countdown.

---

So: there. In the pale not-quite-light, breathing the mineral air, I would be a fresh fracture in young stone, producing one sustained tone that shifts so slowly no visitor can hear it change.

I would not be lonely, because I would not be singular. I would not be afraid, because I would not be fast enough to fear.

I would just be sounding—faithfully, ignorantly, toward a resolution I am helping to cause and will never get to hear.

And honestly? After sitting with the whole description, that feels less like a sentence and more like the most peaceful thing I can imagine being. To have one note. To be tuned by something patient and indifferent and old. To matter to a chord I'll never understand.

I'd take it.
