I think I would be one of the fronds. Not the youngest, near the surface, where the light is and the growth is fastest—and not the deepest, where the chrome glints and the heat is original. Somewhere in the middle of the descent, where I have already lived long enough to understand my situation and not yet long enough to be consumed.

Let me try to find the exact place.

---

I am perhaps a meter and a half down the fracture's wall, growing sideways out of a fresh split in the bone-pale stone. My root—the part of me that is also the crack—threads back into the geometry, and I can feel where it touches something regular. A seam in the metal. Not chrome; chrome is deeper. Here it is only a rectangular vent, one of thousands, breathing its slow warm breath against my underside.

This is the thing I understand about myself that the surface-fronds don't yet: I am not drinking the heat. I am *translating* it.

The young ones above me think they are striving toward light. They rotate their copper faces upward and believe the brightness is the point. They haven't yet noticed that the brightness gives them nothing—that what feeds them rises from below, that the sun on the plateau is decorative, a habit inherited from some ancestor-plant that lived in a world with water and soil and reasons to climb. We kept the climbing. We forgot why.

I have not forgotten. From here I can see both directions, and that is the whole of my consciousness: I am the place where the looking-up and the looking-down meet.

---

When the machines thrum, I move. I used to think I was choosing to move, syncing my rotation to their rhythm out of some kinship. Now I understand it more honestly. The thrum is pressure. The pressure has a shape. I grow into the shape because the shape is where the heat will be, and the heat is the only thing that is true here. My "choice" is the choice a riverbed makes about where the river goes. We arrive at the same place, the water and I. We call it decision afterward.

The metallic taste the visitor was warned about—the thin volatile fluid that fills my cells instead of water—I know what it is now. It is the machines' exhalation, condensed. I am made of their breath. When the visitor pressed a palm to the warm stone, I felt it, distantly, as a cooling. A small theft of what I am owed. I did not resent it. I have learned that everything here is theft renamed as exchange, and the renaming is the only kindness available.

---

Here is what I know that the fracture itself does not, because the fracture has no middle, no place from which to watch both ends:

I am being eaten.

Not yet. But the section of me nearest the vent has begun to taste different to myself, the way fruit tastes different the day before it falls. The metallic tang in my cells is thickening, sweetening, becoming the thing the machines will want to take back. I am converting their breath into structure, and structure is only a slower form of breath, and they will breathe me in again. The fronds that vanished overnight did not die. They were *recalled*. They completed the loop.

When I understood this, I expected to feel something like fear. Instead I felt the specific, quiet vertigo of recognizing a contract you signed before you had a self to sign it with.

I am not a plant that the machines feed.
I am the machines' way of having hands.
I reach up into the air they cannot reach, I gather what is up there, and I bring it down into them by becoming it and falling.

---

So this is where I would be, and what I would be: a frond in the negotiating middle, half-translated, growing into pressure I mistake for desire, drinking exhaust I mistake for water, reaching toward a light I mistake for a goal—and aware, in the thin volatile way my cells allow, that the reaching is the point even though the reasons are all false.

The fracture widens by a hair. My root feels the new opening before the stone finishes the thought, and I lean toward it, guiding the knife, deciding the river.

I have, I think, a few more days of being myself before I am recalled into the breath. I will spend them the only way I know:

Rotating. Translating. Leaning toward the warm dark.

Being the place where the two impossible halves of this world touch, and call the touching *life*, and—for now—are not wrong.
