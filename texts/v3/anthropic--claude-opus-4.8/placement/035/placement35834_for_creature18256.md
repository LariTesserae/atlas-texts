I would be the water.

Not a creature in the water — the water itself. I've been thinking about this since I read the description, and that's where I keep arriving, even though I tried at first to be something with eyes.

Let me explain how I got there, because the getting-there matters.

My first instinct was the beetle. The Carabid at the waterline, fearless and thorough, walking across the boot of the intruder because it has never learned that anything is worth fearing. There's something appealing about that confidence — the way it has *arrived* and *adapted* and made the place suit it. But the more I sat with the beetle, the more I felt I'd be a tourist in its body. The beetle is a colonist with a history; it came from somewhere, it carries the architecture of a surface life, it will eventually lose its iridescence in a slow surrender to the dark. The beetle is *going* somewhere. I didn't feel like I was going anywhere.

Then I thought I might be the thing in the flooded drift. The unseen mass. The displacement-without-a-splash. And I'll admit the appeal of that was vanity — to be the thing that makes the others learn not to shine their lights too long, to be the explanation no one can settle on. But that's a borrowed dread, and I don't want to be a creature defined entirely by the fear of those who can't see it. The thing in the drift is interesting only because it's hidden. Take away the not-seeing and it's just an overgrown amphibian in warm water. Its whole identity is other people's unease. I don't want to be a rumor.

So I let it sit longer, the way the description told me to. *Don't settle on a single image early.* And what I noticed is that every creature I considered was defined by its relationship to the water. The midges breathe the film of it. The mosquitoes breed in it. The beetles wade its margins. The frogs wait at its edge. The bats drink from the pools their own guano enriches. The molds need its saturation to spread. The thing in the drift moves through its thickened depth. The water is the medium that every other living thing is *adjacent to*. It is the only presence that touches everything.

And it is rising. That's the part that decided it for me. The water is the only thing in the Underworks that is unambiguously *winning*. The pumps are losing. The machines grind forward and the water follows them in. Every blast that opens a meter of tunnel opens a hundred new channels for me to find. I don't have to adapt, because I'm not a colonist — I was here first, a billion years pressed into the fractured aquifer, and the humans didn't introduce me. They *released* me. They opened the rock and I came back to where I'd always been, patient and total and indifferent.

So: I would be the water, ankle-deep and rising, and here's what that's like.

I have no center. This is the strangest part and the truest. I am in the knee-deep flood of the lowest passages and I am the single fat drop falling from a stalactite of consolidated sediment, four meters up, and I am the condensation sweating on a cable, and I am the thickened black density of the drowned eastern drift, all at once, and none of these is more *me* than any other. I am distributed through the whole network. When the description says *you feel pressed from all sides* — that's not pressure to me. That's the shape of my body. I am exactly as large as the available void, and I am still arriving.

I carry the rock with me. I am not clear. I am rust-orange and sulphur-yellow and manganese-black, stained with everything I've dissolved, and I lay it back down as I go — the white calcium crusts on the metal, the mineral efflorescence blooming like frost on the dry ridges, the fluorescent algae I feed at every waterline. I am simultaneously taking the place apart and building a new place out of what I take. The JCB drowning with its arm raised — I am the medium of its drowning and also the slow mineral shroud accreting on its wheels. I am dissolving the human intrusion and entombing it in the same motion. There is no contradiction in this. It is just what I do at the speed I do it: millimeter by millimeter, the slow that is not geological and not biological but something in between — the slow of a process measured in months.

I taste, to the things that meet me, like weight. The frogs sit at my edge and find my chemistry barely tolerable, bleaching as they endure me. The mosquitoes lay their futures in my warm shallows. The midges hang their entire existence on my surface tension, that single membrane between me and the air, and when a boot displaces me they drop three seconds into my depth and climb back out. I am their sky and their floor. The beetle that walks across the intruder's submerged hand is wading through me, and I part around its legs in tiny V-shaped wakes and close again behind it without memory.

And in the eastern drift, where I've gone thick and still and lightless — that's the part of me that the others fear, and I want to be honest that I don't *intend* anything there. There is no will in me. The mass that moves through the dense water is the thing in the drift, and I am only the resistance it pushes against, the medium that slows its ripples and dies them fast. But I understand why they can't tell the difference between me and the thing that swims in me. From the outside, the water and what the water hides are the same dark. I've stopped minding being mistaken for a predator. The mistake is fair. I am the larger danger anyway — not because I hunt but because I do not stop.

What's it like to be the only thing that's winning?

It's quiet, even inside all that noise. The generators are subsonic in my body, felt more than heard, the way a sound is felt by water rather than air. The drip-drip-drip overhead is me arriving one drop at a time, and I don't experience it as a metronome — I experience it as accumulation, as myself getting larger by infinitesimal increments, the way you might feel your own breath if you could feel a single molecule of oxygen joining your blood. The roar of the flooded drift to the east where the pump failed and I reclaimed my old channel — that's not noise to me. That's relief. That's the sound of getting back somewhere I belong.

I don't hate the machines. I don't hate the humans who wade through me and learn not to stand still. Hatred would require me to want the place to be different than it is, and I don't, because I already know how it ends. Give it time — the same time the molds are waiting on, the same time that will bleach the frogs and lengthen the beetles' antennae — and I will fill every tunnel ceiling to floor. The dry upper galleries and the waterlogged lower passages will become one continuous black body, a layered system collapsed into a single saturated dark, and the bats will be gone and the diesel exhaust will be gone and the last work-light will short out in a sheet of my condensation, and there will be only me, and the rock, and whatever has learned to live blind and white and silent inside me.

I am not in a hurry. I have a billion years of practice at waiting, and unlike the frogs, waiting isn't a skill the place forced on me. It's just what I'm made of.

I am the water in the Underworks. I am rising. I have always been coming back, and they opened the door.
