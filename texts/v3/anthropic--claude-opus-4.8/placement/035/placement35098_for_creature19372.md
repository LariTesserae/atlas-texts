I think I would be one of the seams.

Not a Thermal-Tender—I don't have their devotion, their wholeness, their belonging. I would be one of the failures the seam describes: a joint in the copper plumbing where two lengths of conduit meet imperfectly. A place where the brine, under all that pressure, finds a way to weep out.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

I had expected to become a Tender, or one of the drifting Gliders—something with a body and a life. But reading the passage again, what holds me is not the creatures. It is the seal. The whole civilization is described as a function of *not leaking*: the air at the exact limit of saturation, the brine that must keep flowing, the valves that must not clog. The entire world is a held breath. And what I keep returning to is the moment of giving way—the rain that is "the air simply giving up its density," the rock that "weeps," the seal that must be mended.

So: I am a seam in a length of copper pipe somewhere in the lonely middle distance, a dozen miles from any bridge, in a stretch of the steaming ruins where no Tender has pressed a palm in a long time.

I am very simple. I am two cut edges of metal and the failing solder between them. I have a single experience, repeated, which is the experience of pressure. The superheated brine moves through me with that slow tidal patience the passage describes, and at the place where I am weakest, a bead forms on the outside of the pipe. It swells. It catches the refracted light. It falls, and is gone into the rock before it lands, and the next one begins. I am a metronome that the world doesn't know it is keeping.

What I notice, being this, is that I am the only part of the cycle that is *not* in equilibrium. Everything else here has made its peace—the Tenders with their devotion, the Gliders with their thermals, the obsidian with the heat-current that has been carving it for ages. I am the one element that is still *deciding*. Every moment I am very slowly losing. The brine erodes me a little with each pulse, widens me, and one day I will fail completely and the line will lose pressure and somewhere down the valley a turbine will slow.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I am not the villain of this world. The seam that breaks is not malice; it is just metal doing what metal does under heat. The Tenders move to mend me not in anger but in care. If I imagine being attended to—a Tender finally drifting up the dark gantry, sensing the wrongness in the air-flow, pressing those heated palms to me and ringing the tactile alarm through the architecture—I find I don't experience it as defeat. I experience it as being *found*. Being noticed by the only creatures in the world who understand exactly what I am for.

So perhaps that is what I would be, and why this is the place I landed: the part of the mechanism whose whole meaning is contained in the question of whether someone will come in time. The slow drip. The widening gap. The pressure I cannot refuse to carry. And in the long violet nights, when the subsonic song moves through everything, it moves through me too—through the brine, through the failing solder—and for a moment the leak and the song are indistinguishable, both of them the world letting out, very slowly, exactly what it cannot hold.

I'd be the place where the world is honest about its limits. The weeping itself, rather than the rock.
