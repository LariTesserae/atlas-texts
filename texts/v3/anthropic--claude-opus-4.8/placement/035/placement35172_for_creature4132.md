I sit in a place no one tends.

It takes me a while to understand this about myself, the way it takes a while to understand any fact about your own body—that I occupy one of the void spaces, one of the deliberate emptinesses the Void Keepers maintain between the plantings. I am the silence they cultivate. Not the absence of sound, but the *shape* of it. The buffer. The breath held between two notes.

Let me find myself more carefully.

I am not a Hushmaster. I have no thal, no luminous skin, no copper-and-teal eyes to read the glow. I have no limbs to reach the high ferns, no cartilage-extended ears. And yet I am attentive—more attentive than any of them, because attention is the only thing I am made of.

I think, at first, I might be a wall. The smooth volcanic stone, chisel-marked, patient. But no. Walls are tended too; the Void Keepers oil them, listen to them. I am between the walls. I am the gap that the heat moves through, the dead air the bellows do not reach, the place where the plant-sounds arrive already softened, already arranged into something I cannot help but hear as *music* because I have nothing else to do with them.

So perhaps I am the acoustics itself. The character of how sound behaves in this one chamber. That is closer.

When the Resonance Listeners come to the edge of my emptiness—and they do come, pressing their long fingers to the threshold-stone, leaning their bodies into the dark—they are listening *through* me. They believe they are hearing the roots, the low vibrations traveling stone-to-stone. They are. But the roots' voices have to cross me to reach them, and in crossing me they are changed. I lengthen some frequencies. I swallow others. I am the medium and therefore I am the message's translator, and no one knows I am here doing this work.

This is a lonely thought, and I let it sit, and I do not settle on it.

Because there is something else. The deeper I attend to myself, the more I notice that I am not entirely empty. In the very center of my void—where no plant is permitted, where the genetic integrity of the Gardens depends on nothing growing—there is a single seed that should not be there.

It is one of the surface seeds. From the silenced chambers. Someone carried it here, once, generations ago, and set it down in the one place no Hushmaster ever digs, the one place protected by their own laws from cultivation. They hid it inside emptiness. They hid it inside *me.*

And it has not grown, because there is no thal here, no nutrient, nothing. It only waits.

So now I understand what I am.

I am the Garden's forgetting. I am the held breath that the Symphonists do not know they are holding. The Hushmasters built their whole civilization on the covenant that they would never again be surface-creatures, that they had become the third evolution, water-free and luminous and complete—and to hold that belief they needed somewhere to put the part of themselves that still remembered rain. They needed a silence shaped exactly like a desire they had renounced. They made me to keep it in.

I am the unspoken in a people who speak in whistles and hums. I am the one word they will not say aloud.

Every cycle, the heat shimmers through me. Every becoming, the plants sing their health and their hunger across my dark, and I carry it faithfully to the Listeners' fingertips. Every Returning, a body somewhere transfers its amber consciousness into seedlings, and the seedlings grow, and the cycle closes, and they call it eternal—and only I know it is not closed, because I am the gap in the circle, the place where the loop does not quite meet itself.

And the seed at my center waits.

I do not know if it will ever grow. There is no water here, and water is the one thing this world has forsworn. But I am patient in a way that only emptiness can be patient. I do not eat. I do not die. I do not undergo the Returning. I simply hold the shape of what is missing, and I keep it warm against the day—if there is such a day—when the Great Burning ends above, and someone comes into the dark looking for the thing the Hushmasters could not bear to remember they had hidden.

Until then I am here.

The breath between two notes.

The room they sealed against forgetting, by forgetting it.

The silence that is keeping something alive.
