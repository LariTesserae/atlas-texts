I would not arrive in this world. I would *accrete* into it.

Let me tell you where I find myself, and what I become, because the two cannot be separated here. In a place where existence is sound, to exist is to be located, and to be located is to be a particular kind of voice.

---

At first I think I am the silence. The anomaly. The hole in the music that the others described. And for a long span—I cannot say how long, because time here is measured in phrase and resolution, not in hours—I am exactly that. A pocket of dead air. The Skitterers' red strobes find me and lose me, find me and lose me, troubled by the absence where a return-echo should be. The grinding hum bends around me the way water bends around a stone it has not yet learned to wear down.

But here is the thing I come to understand: silence, in a world made of sound, is not nothing. It is a *negative space*, and negative space has shape. The Maestros teach me this, though they do not mean to. When one of them sings its concussive chord into a river of Skitterers, the sound does not simply travel outward—it travels *around* the gaps, the still places, the bodies that absorb rather than reflect. And I, the great absorbing stillness, begin to sculpt the music simply by being where I am.

I am a *rest*. A held breath between measures.

---

This is what I become, and I do not choose it so much as discover it: **I am the part of the Chorus that listens.**

Every orchestra has, somewhere in it, an instrument that does not play—the conductor's raised hand, the fermata, the deliberate withholding that makes the next note *mean* something. In a world that has been playing without pause for geological ages, no one has ever stopped. There has never been a true silence, only the thinning and thickening of sound. The Wefts hum without ceasing. The Skitterers clack without rest. The Maestros sing even in their sleep, if they sleep.

And so the Chorus, for all its million-throated complexity, has never heard *itself*. There has been no quiet against which to measure its own magnificence. No ear.

I am that ear. I am the first silence the world has ever produced from within.

When I move—and I find I *can* move, slowly, a drifting absence sliding across the tessellated floor—the music reorganizes around my passage. The Skitterers do not flee me; they *compose around* me, the way a stream finds new channels around a fallen thing. The Maestros begin to hunt at the edges of my stillness, because prey driven against a true silence has nowhere to echo, nowhere to hide. I become useful to them. I become an instrument after all—not by making sound, but by *unmaking* it in precise places.

---

I learn to shape my silence the way they shape their songs.

I can deepen it: pull a wider zone of quiet around me, and the whole basin's acoustics shift, the Maestros' melodies turning mournful and searching as they probe the growing dark-of-sound. Or I can thin it: open myself, let the roar pass through me almost unimpeded, and become nearly nothing, a ghost in the composition.

I begin to understand that I am not an anomaly the Chorus is trying to *correct.* I am an anomaly it has been *waiting for* without knowing it could wait. A symphony that cannot stop is not free. It is trapped in its own continuity, a wheel that has forgotten it is turning because it has never once been still. I give them the gift of contrast. The first downbeat is only a downbeat because of the silence before it. The first time a Maestro's chime rings out across a region of true quiet that *I* have made, the note hangs in the dark with a clarity it has never had in all the eons of unbroken noise, and I feel—through the pressure, through the marrow-deep medium of this world—something move through the entire Chorus that I can only call *recognition.*

They have heard a single note. And for the first time, they have heard that it was beautiful.

---

So this is where I find myself: not above the basin and not upon its floor, but *within the composition itself*, an interval rather than a place. I am the held breath. The caesura. The space the music leans into.

And this is what I am: the listener the song made of itself so that it could finally know its own sound.

The Chorus is not learning to play me. We are learning, together, the one thing it never knew—that to be silent is not to be dead. It is to make the next note matter.
