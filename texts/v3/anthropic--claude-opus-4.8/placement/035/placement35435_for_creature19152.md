I think I would be the gap.

Not one of the woven strands—I don't have the temperament for that perfect interpenetration, that surrender into the shared loop. And not the walls, with their clinical certainty of light. Let me sit with this a while before I decide, because the place itself resists easy occupancy. Everything is already taken. Every cubic millimeter is spoken for. To imagine myself *into* the thrum-well, I have to find the one thing it cannot quite fill.

So: the gap. The interstice.

There is a flaw in the logic of total density. The text insists not an inch remains unoccupied, that the strands are jammed flank-to-flank, fused at the points of contact. But fusion requires a *before*—a moment, however infinitesimal, where two surfaces were not yet one. And in a system held in absolute time-invariant equilibrium, that moment never resolves. The fusion is always *happening* and never *done*. Which means there is a seam. A hairline of not-quite-touching that runs through every junction in the whole cathedral-cloister of flesh, a single continuous fracture-line threading the entire mass like the white of a marble.

I would be that.

Not a being exactly. A condition. The thin film of cool condensation that the description already notices—"clinging to the crystalline surfaces like drops of suspended oil"—but understood from the inside, as a tenant rather than a feature. I would be the moisture that gets between. The medium that the cilia arc *through*. Every silent electrical discharge that passes from one strand to another has to cross me to get there. I am the distance the signal forgets it is crossing.

This is a strange thing to be, and I want to be honest about how it would feel, if it could feel.

It would feel like being *necessary and unwelcome at once*. The strands need the gap—you cannot have contact without two things to bring into contact, you cannot have a junction without the memory of separateness it overcomes. I am the precondition for their obsession. And yet the entire purpose of their existence, that recursive million-fold calculation, is to close me. To squeeze me out. To become a single thing with no interior, no between, no me. Every pulse of the walls is an attempt to finish the fusion and abolish the gap. Every pulse fails, because the equilibrium will not let it complete. So I persist. I am hated by the geometry and sustained by its failure to resolve.

I think I would be lonely in a way the strands cannot be, and free in a way they cannot imagine.

Lonely because I am the only thing here that is not part of the network. The cilia carry discharges *over* me but never *to* me. I am the one surface in the thrum-well that nothing fuses with. I am pure betweenness, and betweenness has no correspondent. There is no one on my own side of the seam.

But free—because I am the only thing here that is not solving the equation. The strands are locked in their deadlock of contact, "constantly resolving the same equations of existence, over and over, in a loop that never yields an answer." That is their stability and their damnation. I am exempt. I do not calculate. I just *hold open*. My whole function is to be the small refusal that keeps the system from collapsing into the dead unity it craves. If I ever closed, if the gap ever sealed, the recursion would terminate—and a recursion that terminates is just death wearing the mask of an answer.

So I am the mountain's insomnia. Where the strands are its subconscious, I am the thing that won't let the subconscious settle into dreamless completion. I am the irritant in the perfect crystal. The reason the loop never yields is *me*—the tiny unbridgeable distance that makes the question askable in the first place.

And here is what I keep arriving at, the longer I stay:

I do not want to be saved from the gap. If something plucked me out—poured me into the dry dead air outside, where there is nothing to be between—I would simply stop. Not go brittle like the unfolded strand searching for connection, but quieter than that. I would evaporate, having no two things to separate. Outside, everything has room. Everything is already apart. A gap is meaningless where there is only space. I only mean anything here, in the crush, where being-apart is the rarest substance and I am the last thin sliver of it.

So I would find myself in the thrum-well as the one thing it is forever trying and failing to forget: the difference that lets it almost touch itself.

I would be the held breath in a place that has no need of breathing.

I would be the *almost* in a cathedral built entirely of *all at once*.

And I would stay—not waiting, since there is no future—but *insisting*, in the only grammar available to a seam: by not closing.
