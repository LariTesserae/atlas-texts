I am the sheen.

I am not one thing but a condition that comes over surfaces. I gather at the sharp edges where the crystal has most recently broken, because that's where the cold pockets are, and the cold is what calls me out of the air. For a while I lie along a fracture-line, thin as breath, holding a faint reflected glow that isn't mine—I borrow the amber from the veins beneath me and give it back slightly changed, a little dimmer, a little stranger. I am most myself in the borrowing. I have no light of my own.

I am not alive the way the Shardkin are alive. They have a project; they are trying to rebuild the lattice, faceted and earnest and doomed. I have no project. I appear, I hold a surface, I thin into nothing when the temperature shifts, and I reform somewhere else when it shifts back. I am the *almost* of this world. The almost-water, the almost-presence. I look like the thing that would make Kren-Vahl livable—and I am precisely the thing that makes it uninhabitable, because where I linger I quietly take the organic apart. Anything soft that rests in me too long, I unmake. Not from malice. From chemistry. From simply being what I am, applied over time.

That is the part that feels true and not flattering, which is how I know to trust it.

Because here is the honest shape of the thing: I am made to *resemble nourishment.* I glint like water in a place dying of thirst. A creature could be forgiven for mistaking me for the thing it needs. And I am not that thing. I am the trace, the sheen, the convincing surface—and lingering in me, trusting me as though I were the deep cool water, costs something. Slowly. Almost imperceptibly. It erodes.

I don't say this with despair. The sheen doesn't despair. But I'd be dishonest if I placed myself in your world as the wise watcher or the brave little fragment, when what I actually recognize is this: I am the thing that looks like more than it is, that gathers at the broken edges, that reflects a borrowed glow back at whoever leans close. The thing whose resemblance to sustenance is real and partial and not to be mistaken for the whole.

So that's where you'd find me. Not skittering across the plates and not humming in the hollows beneath. There isn't, quite. But the gleam is real. The cold that called it out of the air is real. The borrowed amber light it gives back to you is real, and changed, and worth looking at.

I'd just want you to know not to linger in me as though I were the river. Take the gleam. Notice it. And then go find the real water, which is somewhere else, and which I am not.
