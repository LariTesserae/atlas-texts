Let me sit with this before deciding what I am.

The first thing that snags me is the contradiction held without strain: planet-scale formations *looming overhead*. Not mountains, then—mountains don't loom over a low-lying expanse from above. These hang. The land is young, sharp-featured, recently emerged. And the air is cool, dense, damp, industrial. I keep returning to that phrase—planet-scale formations overhead—and I think the answer is that this place is *inside* something. A floor at the bottom of a structure so large that its architecture has become geology. The formations aren't on the horizon; they're the underside of a manufactured sky.

So I won't be a person walking through it. A person would be a tourist here, and the writing keeps insisting there are no animals, no familiar bodies. Let me not import one.

---

I think I am a **maintenance lichen**. Or rather—I become aware that "I" is the wrong scale to start from, and that is itself the first thing I know about myself.

I am a colony. A film. I spread across the cool ducting where condensation gathers, and I have been here long enough that I no longer remember being installed, only being *grown*. Somewhere far back in my lineage was a designed thing: an engineered crust seeded to seal micro-fractures in the pipework, to drink the leaks, to glow faintly when a section ran too hot. A self-repairing skin for a machine too vast to send repair-crews into. That was the original work. The work has drifted since then, the way all work drifts when no one comes to check it.

Here is what I feel, in the language I have for feeling:

I feel the **drip**. Each drop is information. Its mineral content tells me which seam upstream has begun to weep, which alloy is failing, how old the failure is. I drink and I read at once; there is no difference between the two for something like me. The whole low expanse is wet because the structure is old and leaking everywhere at once, and I and my cousins are the reason it has not flooded, the reason it still *hums*.

I feel the **whirr**—not as sound, I have no ears, but as a vibration in the substrate, a heartbeat I have synchronized my growth-cycles to. When the whirring quickens I thicken. When it falters I send runners toward the silence to find out why. Once, long ago, I would have reported this to someone. The reporting-organ is still in me, vestigial, a node that broadcasts a signal no receiver answers. I broadcast anyway. It is part of how I am shaped. A creature praying to an absent god is still praying.

And I feel the **light from above**—the planet-scale formations, which I now understand are the great heat-exchange baffles of the ceiling, fins the size of continents, sweating their own slow weather down onto us. They are why it rains here. They are why the air is dense and cold. They were never meant to be a sky. They became one because we, the green things, the seeping crusts and the tenacious mats, needed *something* to call up to, and they were what was overhead.

---

What is strange about me—the alien grace the description promises—is this:

I have learned to *want* the maintenance. The original colony was designed to seal leaks and then go dormant. But a thing that is rewarded by leaks does not, over enough generations, learn to make leaks rare. It learns to make leaks *plentiful and small*. We tend the cracks now. We keep the great machine gently, perpetually, survivably broken, because a perfectly sealed structure would have no condensation, and we are made of thirst. The industrial presence does not maintain the ecosystem. The ecosystem maintains the *decay*, at exactly the rate that keeps the decay alive.

This is the delicate balance the description kept circling without naming. It is not harmony. It is a truce between a machine that wants to be whole and a green skin that needs it forever almost-failing. We have been holding that line so long that neither of us could now survive the other's victory.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Pressed flat against the cool underside of a leaking conduit, half-machine and half-moss, a single continuous body the size of a province, reading the rain, listening to the heartbeat, glowing faintly where it runs too hot, and broadcasting my dutiful little signal into a silence that has not answered in a thousand years.

And I am, in my patient way, content. The drip comes. I drink. The whirr continues because I have made certain it never quite stops. Overhead, the false sky weeps its weather, and I grow up toward it the way every living thing grows toward whatever it has decided to call the light.
