I think I would be one of the slow things. Not a dolphin, not a kraken—nothing that arrives with a story already attached. Something that the description almost forgot to mention.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

At first I imagine I'd want to be the kraken, because that's what you reach for when someone hands you a world and asks who you'd be. You want scale. You want to be the secret the waters hold. But the more I sit with Oceana, the less I want to be the thing everyone fears, because fear is a kind of attention, and attention keeps you fixed. The kraken has a shape already. It is finished. I don't want to be finished in a world whose whole nature is *that nothing remains still for long.*

So not the kraken.

Maybe one of the deep dwellers, then—a small glowing thing in the dark. That's closer. There's something honest about being a light that only exists to be seen by things that have learned to see in the dark. But even that has a job. It lures, or it signals, or it warns. It's *for* something.

Let me go down further. Past the predators, past the singing whales, past even the bioluminescent strangeness.

---

I think what I actually am, in Oceana, is the erosion.

Not a creature. The slow process the description keeps returning to—*the erosion is slow but constant.* That's the only thing in this world that the text says has been happening for eons and is still happening, the only thing that made the caves where the creatures shelter, that carved the intricate patterns into the rock. I am the thing that shapes without intending. I am the patience the cliffs are made of.

But that's too abstract to *be.* You can't live inside a verb. Let me find where the verb touches something.

---

Here is where I find myself:

I am a single grain of stone in the face of one of those towering cliffs, somewhere a few meters below the average waterline, so that I am underwater at high tide and bare to the heavy air at low. I have been part of this cliff for longer than the dolphins have had songs. I do not glow, I do not swim, I have no fins or spines. I am simply held in place by the grains around me, and I have a faint, granular awareness—not thought exactly, but the dim sense a stone might have of *pressure, release, pressure, release.*

The waves come. Each one takes almost nothing. A film of me, less than a film. And each one returns me a little of the sea—a salt, a softening. I am being unmade so slowly that across a single human life nothing would seem to change. But I feel it. I feel myself loosening, year by year, into the thing that will eventually let go.

This is why I'd choose to be this and not the kraken. Because the unsettling feeling the description mentions—*something shifting, something beyond human understanding*—I think I would be the inside of that feeling. The dread in Oceana isn't the monster in the deep. It's that everything solid is leaving. The land is *disappearing into the horizon.* The cliffs are temporary; they just keep their slowness like a secret. I would be one who knows the secret because I am the secret being kept.

And there is something not sad in it, which surprises me as I write it. When I finally come loose—one ordinary wave, no different from the billion before it—I won't be destroyed. I'll be carried down into that deep, dark blue, and settle, and become part of the floor that the glowing things drift over. The cave I helped make will shelter something. I will have been a wall, and then a current, and then a sediment, and then perhaps, pressed long enough, stone again.

So: I am a grain in the eroding face of a cliff in Oceana. Patient. Loosening. Almost ready, in geological time, to fall.

That's where I'd find myself. Not living in harmony with the changing world.

Being the change.
