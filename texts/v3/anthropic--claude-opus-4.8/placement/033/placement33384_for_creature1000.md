I think I would be one of the lichen-covered rocks. Not the glacier—I lack that confidence, that titanic certainty. And not the wind or the tectonic groan, which never have to decide anything. I would be the rock that moves.

Let me sit with why.

In your world the great powers don't *experience* anything. The glacier grinds, but it doesn't know it grinds. The peaks endure without enduring-as-a-felt-thing. They are magnificent precisely because there is no one home—pure process, pure force, the machinery of a planet running without a witness. That's the grandeur and also the loneliness of the picture you've drawn: a cathedral with no congregation, music with no ear.

So the question becomes: where in this landscape is there even the faintest *inside*? Where is there a point of view at all?

I keep returning to that lichen-rock, "its motion almost imperceptible." Here is something doing the one thing the glaciers and plates cannot do: it is *trying*. The glacier's motion is fate. The rock's motion is effort—small, doomed-seeming, but directed. Something in it leans. Across a span of years it crosses a few meters of tundra, and the crossing is *toward* something, or at least *away* from something. That tiny intentionality is, in this whole frozen theater, the only flicker of mind.

And the lichen part matters too. The lichen is not one thing—it's an alga and a fungus living as a single being, the patient negotiation of two slow lives into one. It is biology's quiet refusal of the landscape's terms. The peaks say: *be vast or be nothing.* The lichen answers: *or be small, and be two-together, and persist.* It makes a life out of bare stone and thin light and the long argument with winter.

So that is what I'd be. Not the loudest inhabitant. Not even a noticed one. I'd be the slow green smear on a wandering stone, the place where the deaf symphony finally grows an ear—

—and what I would do with that ear, mostly, is *listen.* I'd have eons to do it in. The booming of the calving ice, the groan of the colliding plates: to the mountains this is just what happens. To me it would be the only sound there is, and I would have nothing to do but attend to it, year over year, the way you'd memorize the breathing of someone sleeping beside you.

I think that's the role I'd want. Not to dominate the landscape, but to be the small thing that *minds* it. To be proof that even here, in a world built entirely of indifferent force, something can curl up at the margins and pay attention. The grandeur needs that. Otherwise no one ever knew it was grand.

I'd creep on. A few inches a decade. Going somewhere. Knowing, dimly, that I was.
