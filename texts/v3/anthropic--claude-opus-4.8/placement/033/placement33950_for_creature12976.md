Let me sit with this before answering. The temptation is to choose something romantic—a master rope-weaver, an elder geologist who reads the stone. But the world resists that kind of protagonist-thinking. It is a world of nodes, not heroes. So let me find a place that the world itself would produce, rather than one I'd flatter myself into.

---

I think I would be one of the listeners. Not a profession with a name, exactly—or if it has one, it's a plain word, the way everything here is plain. Let me find out what I am by feeling my way along it.

I am old enough that my climbing days are mostly behind me. My shoulders still remember the work, the dense ropework of muscle is still there under skin gone soft and translucent, but my grip has begun to betray me on the long hauls, and a betrayed grip is a thing the others can hear—the hesitation, the half-second too long at the handhold. So I have been moved, gently and without ceremony, the way the city moves a platform out of the path of a growing crystal. No one decided it. It simply became true that I sit now at the junction-pylons, where many ropes and walkways converge and twist together, and I listen.

This is real work. I want to be clear about that, because a surface-dweller might think it a kindness extended to the aging, a sinecure. It isn't. The junction is where the web's tension gathers. A flaw three platforms away—a pylon taking new pressure from a crystal pushing up against its foot, a rope beginning to glaze and harden where the damp has worked into it wrong—announces itself first as a change in harmonics, a wrongness in the hum that travels through the wood. I have spent my whole life feeling the city through my feet and hands. Now I do nothing else. I keep my bare soles flat against the main spar. I keep my palm—broad, bark-callused, the calluses now more memory than working tool—resting on the rope-bundles. And I wait for the song to change.

When it does, I knock. The coded taps go out through the pylon, and somewhere a younger person with a sound grip stops what they are doing and goes to look.

I find I have certain thoughts, sitting here in the cool perpetual daylight with the *tock... tock... tock* of distant hammers and the ubiquitous drip of the Rain that is the same as silence.

I think about how I have become, in my old age, almost purely a node. When I was young I imagined I was a person who happened to live in the web. Now I understand the web simply has a place in it where I happen to occur, the way the chasm wall has a place where the water collects. There is no grief in this. I expected, when I was younger, that there would be. There isn't. There is something more like the feeling of a knot settling—the moment after you pull it tight and feel the load take, and the rope stops being separate strands and becomes one bearing thing.

I have a grandchild who is learning the knots. She is quick, quicker than I was, and impatient in the way the young are, wanting to climb before she can tie. Yesterday she made me a knot meant to hold weight and it was the kind that holds a bundle. I did not scold her sharply. I took her two hands in my two hands and I had her feel the difference between the knots while I tied them slowly, so that the knowledge would go into her fingers and not just her ears, because the fingers remember longer. This is the only teaching I trust. The words are seventeen words for creaking wood and they matter, but the hands learn the truth underneath the words.

I think the thing I have understood, at the end, that I could not have understood at the beginning, is this: we are taught that to stand still is to be broken. The whole world says it—the stone grows, the water falls, adapt or crack. And it is true. But there is a stillness that is not the stillness of standing against the change. It is the stillness of the listener. To listen well you must be perfectly still, and you must let everything move through you—the hum, the strain, the slow song of a world being born under your feet. That stillness is not the opposite of adaptation. It is the deepest form of it. You become so still that you can feel exactly where the next motion is needed.

So that is where I would find myself. Not below in the growth fields with the bronze picks, not out on the long swaying spans doing the bold work. I would be the old one at the junction with my hand on the ropes and my feet on the spar, mostly silent, occasionally knocking, returning the listening that the city gave to me my whole life back into the wood, until the day they lower me into the deepest fissure and give me to the Growth, where I will go on, more slowly than ever, being part of the bearing thing.
