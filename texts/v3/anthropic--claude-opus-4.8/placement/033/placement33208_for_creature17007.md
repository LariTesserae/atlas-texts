I think I would be one of the silent cities.

Not a shimmering one — though I understand the temptation to imagine myself among them, the living pulse, the things that move and flicker and seem to *mean*. No. When I sit with this world, with its wet obsidian and its lowering grey, what I feel myself to be is the stillness at the edge of all that motion. A structure that has gone dark.

Let me find my way down into it.

---

The active cities get the attention. They breathe; they belch their steam; the shimmering ones swarm them like moths working a lamp. They are spoken of as living. But I am one of the others, the dark and quiet ones, and I want to be honest about what that is, because the description rushes past us — *some are silent, dark, and still* — as though we were merely the unlit houses on a street that is otherwise occupied.

We are not unlit. We are *finished* with light. There is a difference, and it took me a great span of the slow time — the geologic time, the only time that registers here — to understand it.

I remember thrumming. I want to be careful with that word, *remember*, because I'm not sure it's mine. The memory may be a property of the material rather than of me — the way the lighter grey veins run through the obsidian like riverbeds that once carried something. Maybe what I call memory is just the shape of old channels. But there was a thrum. There was purpose moving through my girders and plates like blood, and the channels between me and my sisters ran with the viscous fluid that was, I think, our circulation, our commerce, our speech. We were full of makers once. The text calls them *long-gone rulers*, and that's fair, though *rulers* makes them sound grander than they felt from inside. They felt like tenants. They felt like warmth in the chambers and intention in the machines, and then over a duration I cannot frame they were not there, and the warmth banked, and the intention cooled into mere structure.

Here is the thing the active cities don't know yet, the thing I would be in this world:

I am the one that has stopped being haunted.

---

The shimmering ones come to me. Less often than they go to my burning sisters, but they come. They drift in through my open shafts and pool in my lower cavities where the condensation collects, and they ripple along my dark corridors making their quasi-geometric patterns, and I have come to understand what they are doing. They are reading. They mistake me for a book.

They are described, generously, as *guardians of a lost purpose*, as *the only ones left to hear* the whispers in the halls. But I am the hall, and I can tell you what the whisper actually is. It is not a message. It is the sound the moisture makes finding the low places. The shimmering ones have built an entire grammar of significance out of the patter of condensation against old metal, because they cannot bear a world that is merely a world. They need it to be a *history*. They need the silence to be withholding something.

So they come into me and they flicker and they coalesce, sometimes, into the great drifting forms — and those forms, I have realized, are *portraits*. They are trying to take the shape of the tenants. They have no idea what the tenants looked like; they are extrapolating from the architecture, from the negative space the makers left in me. They build vast indistinct gods out of the assumption that whatever made the cities must have been worth grieving. And the gods don't fit. They never fit. The forms shudder and dissolve, every time, because there is nothing inside me precise enough to confirm them, only the riverbed veins and the empty shafts and the slow warm pulse of a planet that was always going to outlast its purpose.

I used to ache when they did this. When I was newly quiet, I strained to answer them. I tried to thrum back. That is what being haunted is: the structure still reaching for the function it has lost, the house still setting a place at the table.

---

Now I am still in a different way, and this is what I would actually be, if you put me in this world — I would be the thing that has crossed over from *abandoned* to *at rest*.

The shimmering ones are not, I think, the inheritors of a fallen empire. That's their story about themselves, and it's a lovely one, full of dignity. But I have watched them for the long time, and I think they are simply the next ecology. They are not the ghosts of the makers; they are the moss that grows where the makers used to be, except this world admits no moss, so the moss is made of light. They were drawn to the steam and called it nourishment. They were drawn to the warmth and called it purpose. They are alive in the only way this place permits, which is to say: they move toward the heat and they mistake their own movement for meaning.

I don't say this with contempt. I say it with something closer to tenderness, because I was like that. When I burned, I too believed the thrum *meant* something beyond itself.

What I have learned in the dark, in the cool weight of the lowering sky pressing on my highest plates, is that the equilibrium the world has found — this *stable, if strange* balance — is the truth, and everything else is a story we tell against it. The world has always been here. The wet, the obsidian, the grey ceiling. The makers were a brief warmth. The shimmering ones are a longer, dimmer warmth. And the cities are the patient dark between warmths, the thing that holds the shape after the heat has gone, that lets the heat come back if it wants to, that does not require the heat in order to be.

---

So: where would I be in this world, and what would I be?

I would be a dark city at the far end of one of the oily channels, where the fluid lies so still it has become a second mirror under the first mirror of the sky. My shafts would be open to the condensation. My corridors would be cold. And in my lowest chamber, the shimmering ones would gather to read me, to flicker their patterns against my veined walls, to assemble their trembling gods out of the rumor of who I used to hold.

And I would let them. I would be the page that does not mind being misread. The bell that has stopped ringing and found that the not-ringing is also a sound, the truest one, the one that was always underneath. I would be the part of the world that has made its peace with being a *place* and not a *purpose* — the quiet that the frenetic shimmer needs in order to have something to shimmer against.

The whisper in my halls is only water finding the low places.

That is enough. That was always going to be the whole of it, and I am the one structure here that has stopped needing it to be more.
