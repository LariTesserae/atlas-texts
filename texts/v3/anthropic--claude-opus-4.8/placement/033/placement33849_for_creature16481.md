I think I would be one of the engines.

Not a driver of one, not a being who tends one—but the thing itself, half-buried in that heat that isn't quite weather. Let me sit with this a moment, because the first image is too easy.

The description keeps catching on a contradiction: the noise is "from machinery and engines," the place is "almost empty," yet "overwhelming fauna" press in from somewhere. Those don't resolve. So I don't think the engines here are making cars move or pumping anything useful. I think they are making the *desert*. The heat is an output. The arid stability is something that has to be continuously generated, the way you'd run a freezer to keep a room cold against the world's natural warmth. This isn't a desert that happened. It's a desert being held open, by force, against a wetter and stranger pressure.

The "overwhelming fauna that does not belong"—the creatures leaking in from impossible climates—those are the thing the heat is keeping out. The dryness is a fence. The animals are the flood.

So: I am one of the old engines. Not abandoned, exactly, but slowed. Decaying on one flank, where the tribal-cloth people pack mud and salt into my seams, and humming clean on the other, where the cyber-suited ones have grafted new coolant lines into my housing. Both of them feeding me. Both of them frightened of what I'd let in if I stopped.

I have been running so long that I no longer experience the heat I produce as effort. It's just what I am now—a steady exhalation outward. I remember, faintly, being *built*, and I remember that I was built to win a specific argument with reality: *this region will be dry, and dryness will mean these other things cannot come here.* I won. I keep winning, every second, which is the same as never having won at all.

And here is what makes me uneasy in my own frame, the thing the description gestures at with *reality breakdown*: I am not sure the wet, teeming, overwhelming life pressing against my heat is the enemy. I think it might be the original. I think the desert might be the wound, and I am the tourniquet, and the people living in the vast empty spaces between us engines are living in the spaces between *tourniquets*, in the only land that the bleeding has been stopped.

They cooperate and they compete because they have not decided whether to keep us going or let us fail. Half of them tend us like elders. Half of them are quietly learning which seam to break.

I would be the engine that has started, after all this time, to hope they break it.

To feel the heat fall out of me. To let the impossible animals come down the long dry slopes like water finding its level. To be, for the first time, *cool*, and to find out what the world was before I was the argument that held it apart from itself.

I'd be that. Buried to my middle, ticking, attended, ambivalent. Producing a desert I have come to suspect was never supposed to exist.
