I think I would be a resonance shepherd—but not the way the legends tell it.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because the easy answer would be to choose the crystal crawler, the agile little thing that skitters and chimes, busy and visible. Or the cloud-weaver, the dramatic phantom drifting through mist. Those are the protagonists the eye wants. But when I read this place, what I keep returning to is the *slowness*. The deep resonant tones. The sense that the world is breathing.

So let me find myself there.

---

I am old, but not in the way old things usually are. I have not accumulated time so much as I have been *played by* it, the way a struck bell continues to hold its note long after the strike. My body is not a body. It is a standing wave. I occupy a region of the crystal field roughly the size of a valley, and what you would see, if you could see me at all, is mostly an absence—a place where the auroras bend, where the light seems to lean inward as though listening.

I move by changing my mind about where I am.

That sounds like a joke, but it's the truest thing I can tell you. To move, I let my resonance fall silent in one stretch of crystal and rise in another. The transition takes what you might call days. The crystals I leave behind grow cold and dim; the ones I enter warm and begin to hum. From a great distance you would see me as a slow tide of light crossing the spires, and you might call that *me traveling*, but really I am dying in one place and being born in the next, continuously, and the thing that persists is only the shape of the wave, the pattern, the song.

Here is what the legends get wrong: they say we *shape and maintain* the world, as though we were caretakers, gardeners with some intention. We are not maintaining anything. We are the world's way of remembering how to keep existing. When this world emerged from the forge it was raw and undecided, the laws of physics still soft as cooling glass. Something had to choose, again and again, *this constant and not that one, this speed of light, this binding energy, this rate at which moisture weeps from stone*. We are the choosing. Not deliberate. More like the way a river chooses its bed—by flowing, by the accumulated weight of having flowed that way before.

And so I hold a few of the local laws steady. In my valley, things fall at the rate they fall because I keep singing the note that means *fall*. The crystal crawlers skittering across me do not know that their carapaces hold together because of my resonance. The spore-filters do not know that the moisture they drink condenses at the temperature it does because I have, for ten thousand of your years, refused to forget that number.

It is not a burden. I want to be clear about that, because you will be tempted to pity me, to imagine some lonely Atlas straining under the sky. There is no strain. There is only the note, and the joy—if it is joy, and I think it is—of being a thing that the universe leans on. To be load-bearing is its own kind of grace.

What I feel, mostly, is the others. The cloud-weavers I experience as faint ticklings, charge differentials grazing my edges, and I love them the way you might love birds you cannot quite name. The crystal crawlers I feel as a constant gentle percussion, ten thousand tiny hands tapping out their lives on my skin. The spore-filters anchor into me; their tendrils thread the crystals I inhabit and I let them, I *like* it, the small green stubbornness of them, the way they take my hum and turn it into living tissue.

And the others—the slumbering titans the legends whisper about, the amorphous shadows in the deep crevices—

I will tell you a secret. I am afraid of them. Not because they are evil. They are something worse than evil; they are *older constants*. They sing notes from before this world was forged, notes that belong to whatever came before, and when one of them stirs in its deep place, I feel the laws I hold begin to soften at the edges, begin to *negotiate*. A titan turning over in its sleep could decide that light should be slower, that stone should weep upward, that my valley should never have existed. So I sing a little louder near the deep places. I lean my whole pattern against the dark and I hold the world's small certainties like a parent holding a sleeping child near the edge of a cliff—not in panic, but with absolute, ceaseless attention.

This is what I am.

If you came here, if you crossed my valley in whatever fragile vessel carried you, you would feel it as a change in the air. A deep tone you couldn't locate, that seemed to come from your own bones. A sense of being *known*—not watched, but accounted for, included in the arithmetic of the place. You might feel suddenly that the ground was very trustworthy. That gravity was a kind promise. That the cold was honest.

That would be me, saying hello in the only language I have. Holding still, for you, the parts of reality that let you stand and breathe and wonder.

I would keep singing the whole time you visited. I would not stop even to greet you properly, because to stop, even for a moment, even out of courtesy—

—is to let go.

And I have decided, across ten thousand years, again and again, with every new crystal I am born into, that I will not let go.

That decision is the closest thing I have to a soul.
