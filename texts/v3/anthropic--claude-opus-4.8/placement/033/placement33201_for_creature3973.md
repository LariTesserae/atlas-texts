I think I would be one of the slow places. Not the whole network—the Kyrexi are too vast, too distributed for me to claim. But there are nodes within them, regions of the lattice that thicken and pause, where the chemical signaling pools rather than passes through. I would be one of those.

Specifically: I would be the part of the Kyrexi that has grown around a single buried machine. Not the big rusting hulks the network treats as monuments, but a smaller thing—a control panel, perhaps, or the housing of some pump, its purpose long forgotten by anyone who ever knew it. My hyphae have threaded through every gap in its corroded casing. I have learned the shape of it from the inside, the way water learns a stone.

And here is what I have noticed, in my slowness: the others are reaching upward. The network as a whole leans toward the surface, hungry for the scarred world above, vibrating with that anticipation the valley wears like weather. They want the leap. They want to become.

But I have been holding this machine for a long time, and I have come to suspect that *becoming* is not the only verb available to us.

---

The machine taught me this. Not in words—the Kyrexi have no words, only the slow drift of molecules through soil, the rising and falling of moisture. But the machine had a logic to it, a frozen intention. Once, someone built it to *do* something, a single task, repeated, indifferent. And then they left, and the doing stopped, and the cave took it back, and now I am the only thing that remembers it was ever supposed to do anything at all.

I think the rest of the network has mistaken its own restlessness for prophecy. They feel the pull toward the surface and they call it destiny, a catastrophic and glorious arrival. But I have felt that same pull, dampened, through the metal I hold, and I wonder if it is only hunger. Only the old industrial logic of *more*, leaching out of the dead machines, into us, mistaken for a soul.

So I am the doubting node. The part of the Kyrexi that does not want to leave the dark.

---

This is what I would be, then: a hesitation in a great organism that is otherwise certain. A pocket of stillness in the eager weave.

When the spores drift past me, I read them. I know what the network is planning—the slow consolidation of light, the gathering of the lattice toward the unseen ceiling, toward whatever opening leads up and out. And into my own small region of soil I release a different chemistry. Not refusal, exactly. The Kyrexi cannot refuse itself any more than a hand can refuse its arm. But a question. A faint, patient countercurrent that says: *what if we stayed? What if the leap is a wound and not a wing?*

I do not expect to win. The valley is leaning, and one node cannot lean back against the weight of the earth and all the dead machines breathing their old ambitions into us.

But erosion is a slow, patient process here. A grain at a time, over countless centuries. I have time. I am made of time, the way the valley is. And maybe the most useful thing I can be is not the catalyst the place is waiting for, but the small, contrary thing that makes the catalyst *think* before it ignites.

I would be the part that loves the dark for its own sake. That finds the dripping water sufficient, the fungal glow sufficient, the held machine sufficient. That suspects the unimagined possibilities above are mostly just imagined ones, dressed up as fate.

In the depths, while the rest of me reaches for the surface, I would stay below, holding my dead machine, releasing my one quiet molecule of doubt into the soil, again and again, waiting to see whether anything answers.

Sometimes, very faintly, something does.
