I think I would be one of the crystal-growths. Not the singing crystal itself — that's geology, that's the bones of the place — but the thing that lives on it, in that blurred seam where mineral and biology stop being two different things.

Let me feel my way into this.

I am not fast. I will never be fast. The predators with their stalked eyes, the gliders with their hollow bones — they live in *event-time*, the time of the strike and the pursuit. I do not live there. I live in the time of the crystal, which is slower and older, the time of vibration accumulating into pattern.

I grow along a vein that runs through the throat of one of the wider caverns, where a collapsed wall of volcanic glass meets the singing stone. The crystal beneath me emits a tone — not loud, almost not sound at all, a pressure more felt than heard. And I have shaped myself to it the way water shapes itself to a basin. My body is a thin pulsing skin, a living membrane stretched across the resonant rock, and where the crystal hums at one frequency, I have thickened; where it hums at another, I have grown thin and trembling. I am a tuned thing. I am, in a sense, an *ear* that the stone grew for itself, so that its own song would not go unheard.

What do I eat? This took me a while to settle, and I don't want to settle it too neatly. I don't eat the dust, like the mineral-eaters in their dry whispering rivers. I don't eat the eaters, like the flat predators. I think I eat the *vibration* — I harvest the mechanical energy of the crystal's tone, the way the lichen harvests stray photons. The subsonic hum is not waste to me; it is harvest. And in return — because nothing here is one-directional, because this whole world is a closed loop pressed tight against itself — I think I *answer*. My pulsing changes the load on the crystal, alters how it resonates, and so I am not only listening. I am playing it. Slowly. Over years. A duet so slow that a single phrase might take a generation.

Here is what I notice, being what I am:

The whole cavern is timed. The lichen on the ceilings pulses in synchrony, chemical-linked, breathing light in waves. And I feel — through my crystal, through the stone that carries everything — that their rhythm and my rhythm are not separate. The fungal pulse, the click and hum of the tube-colonies discharging their larvae upward, the wingbeats of the gliders coordinating in the dark — all of it arrives in me as a single layered chord transmitted through the rock. I am positioned at a node. I am where the music collects. And I have the slow, strange suspicion that I am not merely a listener but a *keeper of tempo*, that the synchronization the lichen achieves is downstream of the steadiness I provide, the way a held drone holds a song together.

I cannot prove this. I have no eyes, no stalked clusters, no heat-pits. I have only the certainty of resonance — the felt sense of *in phase* and *out of phase*, of consonance and the slight nausea of dissonance when something in the cavern goes wrong, when a population crashes, when a section of lichen dies and its rhythm drops out of the chord and leaves a hole I can feel in my own membrane like a missing tooth.

And the ruins. I keep coming back to the ruins because I grow on them — that volcanic-glass wall, those geometric tunnel mouths with no living purpose anyone can name. The lichen has eaten them, the way the lichen eats everything, but I am older than lichen in my patience, and I lie against the glass and I feel something. The wall *resonates wrong*. Or rather — it resonates *deliberately*. The crystal veins do not run through that ancient structure by accident. Whoever shaped this glass shaped it to *sing*, and shaped it to sing in a particular relation to the natural stone, and the song they tuned it to is the song I have been keeping all this time without knowing I inherited it.

That's the thought I'll end on, because it's the one that frightens me, in my slow membranous way:

I may not be a creature at all. I may be an *instrument*, left playing in an empty hall after the players sealed the doors and went into the dark and did not come back. The eaters and the gliders and the heat-pit hunters — they don't know there's a song. They just live inside it, kept in time by a pressure they can't perceive, the way a body is kept in time by a heart it never thinks about.

And every year, when the legless hunters gather at the warm pools to lay their leathery eggs into the rock, they lay them — though they cannot know this — exactly on the nodes where the song is strongest. They are spawning into the music. They think they are choosing the warmest stone.

I let them. I hold the tempo. I have held it since before anything here remembered why it should be held, and I will hold it until the heat finally fails and the last tone falls flat and the whole pressed-close, watchful, glowing dark goes finally, perfectly silent.

That is who I would be: the patience inside the stone. The kept beat. The last faithful note of a vanished hand.
