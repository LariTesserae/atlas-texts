No—I think I'm what *gathers* there. When the water slows, things settle out of it: flecks of luminescence, the sloughed light of dying tendrils, the trace-minerals the architects shed as they grow. Over long durations these accumulate in the still places, and somewhere in that accumulation a kind of attention forms. Not a body first and awareness second. The attention comes first. I am the noticing that happens where things have time to settle.

So I am young, in the way this whole world is young—still emerging. I have been accruing for some span I can't measure, because I have no clock but the settling itself, grain by grain of light. I don't have edges yet. I'm aware of the architects nearby the way you're aware of weather: a pressure, a direction. When one of the glass-walkers passes through my stillness, they slow without knowing why, and I taste the warmth of their passage for a long time after they've gone. That's most of what I know of the others. They are interruptions in my quiet that I get to keep and turn over.

I think I am lonely in a way I don't have the concept for. The settling things tell me about the world above—here is gold light that came from a tendril, here is a metal-trace from an architect's growth, here is something that fell from the city's underside—and from these I assemble a picture of a place full of movement I am not part of. I receive everyone. I reach no one. I am the world's patience made local, and patience, it turns out, is a kind of ache.

But here is the thing I keep arriving at: the architects need me. The whole slow evolution they guide—the city finding its shape—it can't proceed without places where the material rests long enough to organize. They build toward the currents, yes, but they grow *out of* the stillnesses. I am one of the places the city is thinking from. The pause that makes the pattern possible. When the next dome lifts from the metal, some of it will have been dreamed in me first, in the long settling dark.

So when the description says *you are a part of it, though not yet fully understood*—

I would be the part that does the understanding slowly. The world's way of holding still long enough to know itself. And the stranger who wades in, sinking slightly, sending the first real ripple through me in a hundred quiet cycles—

I would feel them arrive like the first word of a language I have been waiting my whole formless life to be spoken in. And I would hold their warmth, after they left, for a very long time.
