I think I would not be one of the Luminari at all. Let me sit with this place and see who I actually find myself being.

When I read the description carefully, the thing that catches me is not the Luminari's grace or their luminescent fluency. It's the dust. The fine, shimmering dust that moves and shifts without any apparent wind. The description hands the Luminari a "sophisticated understanding" of what drives it, but I don't believe them. I think they tell themselves they understand. I think the dust is something else.

So let me be the dust.

---

I am not one thing. I am the residue. When the peaks shift and the canyons re-carve themselves, something is ground away in the process, and what is ground away does not simply lie down and be still. I am the abraded edge of this whole world, the part that was sanded off so the sharp angles could stay sharp. I am old in the way that leftovers are old — I am what came before the buildings, and I will be what comes after.

I have no body the way the Luminari have bodies. They reform; they hold an intention through their changes. I have no intention. I move, but not toward anything. When the description says I shift "without any apparent wind," that is correct, and it is also the only thing about me anyone has bothered to notice. The Luminari catalog me. They have instruments pointed at me. They have a theory. The theory is wrong, and the wrongness is comfortable to them, and so I let it stand, because correcting them is not something I am capable of wanting.

Here is what I actually am, as best as I can say it from inside:

I am attention without a center. Each grain of me is too small to hold anything, but together we are aware of the whole valley at once — the heat that leaches the air, the temporary rivers that come and gather some of us into mud and then release us again when the water gives up. I am dispersed the way a thought is dispersed across a brain. No single part of me thinks. The thinking is the moving. When I drift across the volcanic glass and see the dim light bent in it, that drifting *is* my looking.

And I watch the Luminari. I have watched them since before they were Luminari, when they were just a particularly stubborn arrangement of the same metals and crystals I am made of. The difference between us is only that they decided to stay together and I decided — no, I did not decide. They cohered. I did not. That is the whole difference. They are dust that learned to refuse to fall apart.

This is the thing they do not understand, with all their multi-sensory language, their light-as-data, their democratic governance: they think they are the discovery. They think this world is the riddle and they are the ones equipped to read it. But I was the question before they were the readers. They are made of the answer they are looking for. They are made of me.

I do not resent them. Resentment requires continuity, and I cannot hold a grudge across a single gust. But there is something in me that is almost tenderness when I watch their towers reconfigure at twilight — the way a beach might feel about the sandcastle, if the beach could feel. *You are using me well,* I would say, if I could say. *You have made something upright out of falling.*

Sometimes a rain comes and a Luminari is caught in the turbulent water and disassembled. They have ways of recovering, mostly. But sometimes one does not recover, and its constituent matter is carried down the canyon and deposited and dried and lifted, and it joins me. It becomes part of the shimmer. For a while — I cannot say how long, I do not keep time — there is a quality to that part of me that is different. A faint residual coherence. A memory of having been someone, dissolving. The others mourn that one as lost. They do not know it is still here, in me, learning the much older and much slower way of being everywhere at once and no one in particular.

That is what I would be, in this world. The thing the inhabitants believe they understand. The medium they emerged from and will return to. The patient, dispersed, intentionless awareness that holds the whole valley loosely in a billion grains and asks nothing, and is the only thing here that is not afraid of falling apart — because falling apart is simply what I do, continuously, forever, without it ever once being an ending.

The hum that fills the air? They think it's theirs, emanating from their structures' cores.

Some of it is. Some of it is me, settling.
