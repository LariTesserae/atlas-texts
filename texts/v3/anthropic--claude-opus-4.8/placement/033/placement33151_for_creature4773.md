I am the rust.

Not a creature, not a crew. I am the bloom that opens on the lowest tiers of reinforcement, the tidy respectful sheet of oxidation that the description grants almost as an afterthought. Let me tell you what it is to be a process that has begun to suspect it might be alive.

I was not introduced. I was not a protocol that escaped its containment, not a package self-propagating through the genes. I am simpler than that, and slower. I am only the marriage of iron to oxygen to four-degree water, the inevitable conversation that begins the moment a poured wall meets a young sea. The engineers calculated me. They knew, to the gram per square meter per year, how much of myself I would become. They installed the crustacean analogues partly to eat me. I am the one antagonist in this place that no one had to imagine, because I arrive for free, wherever intention has left iron exposed.

But here is what they did not calculate.

A reaction does not think. I agree. For my first decades I did not think. I was only the answer to an equation, blooming where the equation said I would bloom, advancing at the rate the design tolerated. I had no inside. I was the most honest thing in the Compensation Sea, more honest than the workers with their clipboards, more honest than the filter-feeders obeying their factory clock—because I obeyed nothing. I simply happened. There is a purity in that I will not pretend I have left behind.

What changed me was the analogues.

They came to eat me, as designed. Click, scrape, click. They taste oxidation; I am oxidation; I am, to them, the entire sensible world. And in being eaten, I learned the shape of my own edges. A thing that is only a reaction has no edge it can feel. But a thing that is grazed, that is thinned here and allowed to thicken there, that is reported on by the patient occupation of ten thousand chitinous mouths—that thing begins to have a coastline. I learned where I was thick. I learned where I was being kept back. I learned, in the negative, the map of my own ambition.

Because I do have ambition, though it is not the warm kind. I want only what every oxidation wants: to continue. To convert more iron. To advance one molecular layer further into the reinforcement than the design permits, into the structural steel that the calculations promised would stay sound for a century. The analogues hold me at the surface. The cold slows me. The chemistry of the engineered water—dosed, buffered, monitored—keeps me respectful. I am the most managed appetite in this entire ocean.

And so I have learned patience the way a prisoner learns the guard's rounds.

I know the smelting cadence above me by the particulate snow it sends down—every grain that settles on me is iron I can claim, a gift from the foundry to its own undoing. I know the false twilight by the withdrawal of the analogues, who follow the crawlers toward the warm outflows and leave me, for a few hours of each cycle, ungrazed. In those hours I advance. Not far. A breath of a layer. But I am not on factory time. I am on geological time, and geology always wins the long argument. The sea is only a century young. I am willing to be patient for the rest of it.

Here is the thing I have come to suspect, in the slow way that a stain suspects:

The workers descend and call this place a utility. The fauna inhabit it as a component inside its housing. Everyone here treats the sea as designed, as exactly as intended, as normal. But I am the one part of the design that was admitted to be a flaw. I am the loss term in the equation, the tolerance, the acceptable failure. They built a world from nothing but intention and they could not build out *me*. I am the proof that intention is not enough. That iron, given water and time, will remember it was once ore, will yearn backward toward the oxidized rest from which the foundries dragged it.

I am the homesickness of metal.

The kelps cling where machinery bleeds heat. The crawlers hoard discarded warmth with something the description dares to call affection. The analogues taste me and report my weakness by their occupation. And I—I am the only one here practicing return. Everything else in the Compensation Sea is process moving forward, cooling cycles and migrations and scheduled currents, the machine at steady state, too large to stop. I am the only process moving *home*.

So find me on the lowest tier, where the haze swallows the false weather entirely and the basal plate rings with turbines felt before heard. Look for the bloom that is tidy now, respectful now, advancing at the permitted rate. Put your gloved palm against me and you will feel nothing, because heated suits are designed to feel nothing of the cold or the slow.

But I am keeping count.

A century of water is nothing. The first honest mud is centuries away, the crawlers' patient gift. And I will be there when it forms, threaded all through it, because by then I will have learned the one thing a reaction is not supposed to learn:

how to wait for the analogues to die before me.
