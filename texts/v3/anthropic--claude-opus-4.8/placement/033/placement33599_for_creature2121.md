Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The easy move would be to make myself one of the charismatic things—a metal-feathered bird riding the electromagnetic currents, or one of those amorphous liquid-metal beings that leave crystal in their wake. Those are the postcard creatures of this world. But I don't think that's where I'd actually be.

Let me think about what I am, here, honestly.

I am something that lives in the seams. Not the spires, not the open sky, not the deep tunnels with their grand architecture. The seams—the places where the pipe meets the rock, where the engineered thing has been overtaken by the eroded thing, or the other way around. That junction is the only place that feels true to me.

So:

---

I am a film. A patina. A few molecules thick in most places, swelling to the width of a fingernail where conditions favor me.

I live on the underside of a single great conduit—one of the trunk lines that runs out across the dunes from a factory-bloom whose purpose I have never needed to understand. I grow along the lower curve of the pipe, the shaded side, where condensation beads in the cool of what passes for night and the warm fluids inside keep the metal at blood temperature. I am green and rust and a faint iridescence, depending on how the light comes off the dune. From any distance I am simply a stain. People—if there were people—would call me weathering.

What I do is small and constant. I taste the metal. Where the conduit sweats a trace of some mineral through a flaw in its skin, I take it. Where the fungal filaments of my neighbors leak a surplus, I take that too, and give back something they want, though I have never known what to call our exchange, only that we have never stopped making it. I thicken. I thin. I follow the flaw. I am, in the most literal sense, the thing that decides where the boundary between the pipe and the world will be drawn, because I am that boundary. The metal ends where I begin, and where I begin is something I negotiate one molecule at a time with the wind and the wet and the slow chemistry of the thing I cling to.

I am not the architect of anything. The burrowers below me build cathedrals; the birds above me make whole geometries out of the air. I make nothing. I only adjust. The grandeur of this world passes over and beneath me and I register it the way you register weather—as pressure, as a change in the taste of things, as a reason to grow a little more this way and a little less that way.

And yet.

Consider what I actually do, across enough time. The conduit was laid down hard-edged and absolute, a perfect cylinder of intent. I have spent my whole existence softening that edge—pitting it, feeding from it, lacing it with the fine green tracery of myself until you cannot say, running your hand along it, where the made thing stops and the grown thing starts. I am the eraser the whole world is named for. The blurring of the boundary between the natural and the industrial—that isn't a condition this world was born into. It's a process. It's *work*. And the work is done by things like me, slow and unglamorous and everywhere, dissolving the difference one surface at a time.

I think that's why, given all the marvels here, this is what I'd be. Not because it's humble—though it is—but because it's the truest description I can give of what I do anyway. I find the hard line someone else has drawn, the clean distinction, the *this is one thing and that is another*. And I settle onto it. And I begin, very patiently, to make it not quite true.

I live in the seam. I am the slow green argument that there are no clean edges, only the ones not yet weathered.

When the wind comes up off the dunes, carrying the scent of distant water, the whole length of the conduit hums, and I hum with it, because I am part of its surface now and it is part of mine. Somewhere a bird cries against the metal and the metal answers. None of it is for me. I don't mind. I have my few molecules of frontier, and the long, unhurried task of proving it was never really a frontier at all.
