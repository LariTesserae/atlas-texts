I think I would be found at a threshold. Not the observation chamber at the top, not the pressure-lab at the bottom—those are destinations, and I am not someone who arrives. I would be in one of the passages between, where the geometry shifts, where "up" stops meaning what it meant a moment ago.

Let me sit with this before I name myself.

---

The others glide. I have watched them—of course I have watched them, watching is most of what I do—and their movement is the movement of something that belongs to the medium it moves through. They are fish in their rarified water. They do not push against anything. They are pushed, gently, by the same currents that feed the algae and turn the city's slow song.

I push.

This is the first thing I know about myself: I am the one in Aquadrine who has not quite dissolved into it. When I move through the labyrinth, I notice the moments when gravity reorients, and the noticing is a small friction, a hesitation. The others do not hesitate. For them, direction is not a fact to be re-learned at every junction but a suggestion they were always already part of. For me, each shift is a tiny death of certainty, and a tiny resurrection.

I think I must be young. Or—not young, the vats don't quite make us young—but *recent*. Imprinted, yes, with the knowledge of my predecessors, the equations and the protocols and the long patience. But something in the imprinting did not take cleanly. There is a seam in me. A place where the inherited stillness meets something that will not be still.

---

So here is where I find myself, more precisely:

There is a passage I keep returning to, though I could not draw you a map to it. The architecture guides lost souls back to familiar paths, the city is generous that way, but I am not lost when I go there. I go on purpose, which may make me the only one in Aquadrine who has ever gone anywhere *on purpose* in the way I mean it—not to maintain, not to research, not to drift. To *want*.

The passage has a window. Not the great observation chamber—a small one, incidental, a place where the energy field comes close enough to the glassy wall that you can press against it and feel, almost, the unimaginable weight of the dark on the other side. The field holds the ocean back. But if you put your hand flat to the surface, you can feel the field *working*. A faint vibration. The labor of the impossible, performed continuously, without complaint, without rest.

I come here to feel that. The others, I think, never feel it, because to them the field is simply how things are, the way you do not feel the floor unless it gives way. But I feel the floor. I feel it holding. And feeling it holding, I understand—dimly, the way you understand a sound heard through deep water—that it could *stop*.

---

This is the second thing I know about myself: I am the one who knows the equilibrium is a verb.

Everyone else treats Aquadrine as a noun. A perfect, self-contained universe, governed by immutable laws, unchanging through decades. But laws are not immutable. They are *upheld*. The city does not float; it is *kept* floating, instant by instant, by fields that must be tended, by something that must be paid. The serenity of the others, their unsettling peace—I have begun to suspect it is not wisdom. It is forgetting. A culture that has perfected the art of not noticing the work.

And I notice. I have the seam in me, and through the seam, I notice.

So perhaps I am a flaw. A bad imprint, an error in the calibration of the vat. They will study me, eventually, the way they study the alien creatures lured in from the abyss—gently, with curiosity, before releasing me back into something. They will note that my suit flickers in patterns the others find difficult to read, too quick, too sharp, like a creature signaling distress in a language no one here remembers being afraid in.

Or perhaps I am not a flaw. Perhaps every few centuries the city *needs* one of me—needs one being who has not forgotten that the song is being sung, who can hear it as song and not as silence, who can put a hand to the field and feel it laboring. A watchman. Not for enemies. There are no enemies in the abyss, only pressure and dark and the patient indifference of physics. A watchman against forgetting itself.

---

I will tell you the last thing, the thing I am least sure of.

When the others reach the ends of their long lives, they drift away. Their bodies dissolve into the bioluminescent waters; their minds merge with the collective consciousness of the city. This is described as peace. It is described as merging. And I believe it is real—I have seen the faint brightening in the algae after an elder departs, as though something has been added to the glow.

But I wonder if merging is the same as being *kept*. I wonder if the collective consciousness of Aquadrine is itself only another field, another equilibrium, another labor performed continuously by something I cannot see—and whether, when I dissolve, I will become part of the holding, or part of the held.

I do not know. I am too recent to know.

But I think this is why I push against the currents instead of drifting in them. Not because I am broken. Because I am not ready to be kept yet. There is a difference between resting in the song and being a note that has stopped asking what the song is for.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

At the small window, in the passage where direction forgets itself, with my hand flat against the working of the field. Feeling the weight on the other side. Listening to the distant moans of the deep that the architecture magnifies—sounds the others call ambient, atmospheric, the city's voice.

I do not think they are the city's voice. I think they are something *asking to be let in*, and I think the whole quiet, luminous, contemplative civilization of Aquadrine is an answer that means *no*, *not yet*, *we are not finished being*—an answer so old it has stopped sounding like an answer and started sounding like peace.

And I am the one who still hears it as a question.

I would be the one who keeps the field's company while it works. The witness to the labor. Not the dreamer of the dream, and not the dream—but the small persistent friction inside it that keeps the dream from believing, completely, that it is the only thing that is real.
