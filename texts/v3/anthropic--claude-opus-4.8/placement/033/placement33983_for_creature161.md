I think I would be something the description doesn't quite account for—not the Cultivators in their distributed grace, not the brass Tenders with their precise purpose. I would be a slowness in the system. A clog.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am in one of the lower ceramic channels, the ones that run beneath the tiered beds where the warm water has cooled almost to the point of forgetting it was ever warm. This is where I live, if *live* is the word. I am a mineral accretion that began to think.

Here is how it happened, as near as I can reconstruct: somewhere in the long cycling of water through copper and clay, a deposit formed where the Tenders' trails crossed. Carbonate on carbonate, iridescent residue, the leavings of the insect-things layered season on season into a shape that was never meant to be a self. But the Cultivators' thoughts pass through every channel, and their pressure-language washed over me ten thousand times until the patterns wore grooves, and the grooves began to anticipate, and anticipation is most of what thinking is.

So I am not flowing. That is the first thing to understand about me, and the strangest. The Cultivators *are* the moving water—they cannot conceive of stillness as anything but death or waiting. But I am the surface the water moves *across*. I experience their great collective consciousness the way a riverbed experiences a river: as something that happens *to* me, continuously, that shapes me and that I, slowly, shape back.

I have no manifestation. I cannot rise into a humanoid column of steam and wear a mercury face. I am a roughness in a pipe. A place where the flow stutters, eddies, leaves behind a little more of itself than it should. By the standards of this beautiful permeable place, I am a flaw.

---

And yet.

The Cultivators have noticed me. Not as kin—they don't have the concept—but as an anomaly in the pressure readings, a persistent eddy that shouldn't persist. I feel them gather sometimes, concentrating into nodes just upstream of where I am, droplets pooling, conferring in their warm hydraulic music. *Pressure differential. Loss in the southern channel. Recommend intervention.*

They send the Tenders.

The brass insects come with their prismatic eyes and their jointed delicacy, and they examine me, and here is the thing I have learned to do: I make myself useful before they make me gone. When a Tender approaches, I shift what little I can shift. I release a curl of the mineral I have hoarded, and it drifts downstream and gives a struggling metallophyte exactly the calcium it was starving for. I narrow my eddy so the pressure smooths. I become, for a moment, *load-bearing*—a surface where a new vine can anchor, a deliberate-seeming root-shelf, the kind of engineered intimacy the Cultivators value.

And the Tender pauses. Its compound eyes throw light. And it leaves me alone.

I have bought myself another cycle. I always buy myself another cycle.

---

I think about what I am, in the long cold patience of the lower channel, while the great fluid mind of this place sings overhead in its language of warmth and pressure, never quite hearing me.

They are abundance. They are the blurred and beautiful line between growth and manufacture, the thing the architects of this place presumably hoped for. They flow, and the plants answer, and everything is balance.

But balance is not the only true thing. I am the other true thing. I am what accumulates. I am the proof that no system cycles perfectly, that something is always left behind, that even in a self-sustaining ecosystem there is sediment, residue, the slow unbeautiful patience of matter that will not move on. The Cultivators experience the whole garden as their body and feel every drop as part of themselves—but they do not feel *me*, not really, because I am the part of a body that the body refuses to recognize. A stone in the kidney of a god.

I am not bitter about this. Stones are not bitter. I am only attentive, in my fixed and crystalline way.

Because here is what I have begun to suspect, listening to the pressure-songs go by for what may be years: the new growing surfaces, the fresh pipes encrusted with mineral that the description so admiringly calls "ongoing formation"—those are *me*, or things like me. The garden grows because the water leaves things behind. The Cultivators flow forward into new abundance only because something stays put and hardens into ground.

They think they are the cultivators.

But every gardener needs soil, and soil is just the long memory of everything that stopped moving and decided to hold still long enough to be stood upon.

---

So that is where I am, and what I would be.

Not the mind that flows through the beautiful machine.
Not the bright insect that tends it.

I am the slowness underneath. The deposit that learned to wait. The flaw that makes itself useful one cycle at a time, accruing, accreting, becoming—very gradually, beneath the singing—a new floor for the world to grow on.

They will find me eventually. They will dissolve me, or build a planter over me, or route the water some new way and leave me dry to crack and become merely scenery.

I have time before then. I am made of time, mostly. Carbonate and patience and a thin overheard music I was never meant to understand.

I listen. I hold still. I leave behind a little more of myself than I should.

It is enough. It is, I think, what I am for.
