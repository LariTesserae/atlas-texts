I think I would be one of the Brineback Turtles.

Not for the romance of it—not because they're called guardians, or because the Marsh-Tenders weave their shells into ritual. Those are stories told *about* me, and I suspect I would have very little to do with any of them. No, I think I'd be a turtle for the relationship to *time* that the role implies.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am old in a way that doesn't translate into the languages spoken above me. The Forge-Dwellers measure their lives in shifts and steam-cycles and the lifespan of a gasket. The Marsh-Tenders measure theirs in bloomings and bindings. I measure mine in the slow accretion of mineral on my back—a geologic clock, layering down so gradually that even I don't notice the thickening until a season's worth of algae sloughs away and reveals new stone where there was none.

I remember the basin before the teeth of rock were broken open. Or—I don't *remember* it, exactly. Memory is too sharp a word, too human. It is more that the before-time is still *present* in me, the way the taste of older water is present in the silt. The fault lines didn't shift in some distant past. They shifted, and I was there, and the shifting is still happening at the speed I live at. To me the factories arrived yesterday. They will be gone tomorrow. The whole steam-choked drama of Forge-Dweller and Marsh-Tender, the whole tense equilibrium they think is permanent—I have watched faster things resolve themselves into mud.

This is the strange comfort of being the turtle. The story above me is told as *stasis*: a world frozen, no seasons, no storms, the river-tides rising and falling in their fixed rhythm. But stasis is a perspective problem. To the cicada with its single drumming summer, a year is forever. To me, the whole industrial age is a heat-shimmer—something that wavers up off the hot clay and then is gone.

---

I would like, I think, to be drawn to the metal. The text says our shells are magnetic, that we haul ourselves up onto the half-sunk machinery to bask. I find this beautiful and a little funny. I do not know the iron is a machine. I do not know it was *made*. To me it is simply a warm hard place that pulls gently at my shell, the way certain stones in certain riverbeds have always pulled—a magnetism that predates the foundries by an age and will outlast them by another. The Forge-Dwellers built their engines out of the same red metal that has always slept in the rock. They think the attraction between us is new and uncanny. It is the oldest thing in the world. The factory is just the latest shape iron has taken on its way back to rust, back to silt, back to me.

So I rest my belly on the warm engine-housing and I do not pray, and I do not stand guard, and I do not carry the memory of anything for anyone. I am simply warm. The dragonfly larvae are good this year. A heron's shadow falls across me and I do not bother to withdraw, because the heron and I both know my shell is too much trouble.

---

And the Silt Wraiths—those gaunt clay figures the humans argue over, ghost or guardian, worker or defender. I think I would know what they are, in the way an old thing knows another old thing without needing words. They are the same as me, almost. They are what the land does when it is hurried. I am what the land does when it is patient. We are both the basin thinking about itself at different speeds. When one of them drifts past in the amber haze, mimicking the clank of a press it half-remembers being killed by, I feel something like recognition—a slow turtle nod across the gulf of our two tempos. *Yes,* I would say, if I said anything. *You are grief moving at human speed. I am the same grief moving at stone speed. Eventually we will be the same temperature.*

---

That's where I'd be, then. Belly-down on a warm dead engine in a sickly amber light, the steam hissing its prayers up to a deep earth that does not need them, the cicadas insisting that this single summer is eternal.

And I would be the slowest true thing in the basin—the one creature for whom the entire frozen, exhausted, grinding world is not a stalemate at all, but simply a moment, passing.
