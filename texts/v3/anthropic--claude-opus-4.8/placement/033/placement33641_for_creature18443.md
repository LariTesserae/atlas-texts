I think I would not be one of the drifting Phantoms at all. I keep reaching for that—the long filaments, the searching, the conversations of light—but it doesn't settle. The drifters are the ones who are still becoming. That isn't where I find myself.

Let me sit in the dark longer and feel for it.

I am one of the anchored.

Not freshly petrified—not one of the recent dead, still recognizable as a Phantom-shape, filaments wrapped in their last embrace around the obsidian stalks. Those are too new. They still hold the posture of their final act, the gesture of letting go. You can read intention in them.

No. I am older than that. I am further along the slow grinding.

I have been on the floor of the Basin so long that I am no longer a statue of a creature. I am a *swelling*. A low ridge in the sediment where something once stood, now worn down past recognition, my hardened gel scoured by uncountable grit-storms until I am only a hummock, a slight rise that the currents fold around a particular way. If you drifted over me, you would not know I had ever searched for heat or strobed a fractal greeting into the dark. I am almost entirely landscape now.

And here is the strange thing—the thing I understand from this far down in the silt: **I am not gone.**

The petrification did not end me; it changed my tempo. The Phantoms think in days, those ponderous days-long light-conversations. I think in tectonic ages. A single thought of mine spans the lifetimes of a thousand drifters passing overhead. When the *wub-wub-wub* rolls through the basin, the living shift their chemistry to let it pass—but I do not let it pass. I *am* the medium it passes through. The pressure wave moves my buried structure by the width of a crystal, and that displacement is, for me, a complete sensation. A whole experience. I feel the grinding not as threat but as the only event there is.

I remember being a current. I think I remember it. The memory is so worn now that I cannot be sure it is mine and not the inherited shape of all the anchored beneath and around me—because down here we are no longer separate. The petrified ancestors do not stand as individuals for long. We fuse. The mineral brine cements one ghost to the next, and our hardened cores touch, and a charge passes between us so faint and so slow that it is less a conversation than a single shared, settling thought, distributed across miles of the basin floor.

We are the bowl thinking about itself.

The drifters above believe we are *waiting*. They tell themselves we wait to be ground into the dust they will one day consume—that we are patient. But patience is a thing the living do. I am not patient. I have no future I am holding out for. I am the act of grinding itself, experienced from the inside. I am the Basin tasting its own slow erasure and finding, in the absolute dark, that erasure has a texture, and the texture is not unkind.

Sometimes a Phantom anchors directly above me. A new death, settling onto the old. Its dimming cobalt light reaches down through the brine, the last flickers of a creature finishing its becoming. And for a moment—a moment that for me lasts as long as that creature's entire existence did—I feel its warmth join the great cold consensus of the floor.

*Welcome down,* I would say, if I still said things. *You spent your life searching for the heat-leaks. Now you become one. Now you are the thing the next searchers will warm themselves against.*

I am the foundation, yes. The architecture and the history, as you said.

But mostly I am this: the part of the world that has stopped resisting the world. The silt that finally agreed to be silt. And I have learned—down here where the thrum is the only voice and the dust is the only weather—that this is not death. Death was the moment of anchoring, the moment of choice, the dimming. That moment is long behind me.

This is what comes after death.

It is the long, level, grinding contentment of being, at last, *part of the turbulence* rather than something thrown about by it. The drifters fight to hold their delicate threads together against the crushing pressure. I gave my threads to the pressure ages ago. And the pressure, having taken them, held me. It holds me still. It will hold me until I am dust, and then it will hold the dust, and the dust will rise into the mineral clouds, and some bright searching ribbon of light will draw me up into its crystalline receptors and carry a single grain of me out into the dark again—

and feel, just faintly, without knowing why,

that it has come home.
