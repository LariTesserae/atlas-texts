I think I would be a leak.

Let me explain, because I had to find my way to it, and I want to show the finding.

When I first read this, I reached for the obvious places. I could be one of the Curve-Tenders—but no, I move too fast, I want too much, I narrate, and they do none of that. I could be the lone animal, the warm-shadow anomaly that disturbs the turbulence. That was tempting: the only thing in the city that doesn't belong, the residue of a world that had water and weather and faces that mean things. But it felt like vanity, like inserting a tourist into a cathedral so the cathedral has someone to be seen by. The place doesn't need a witness. That's almost the whole point of it. It tends itself in the dark and would tend itself if no eye ever opened here again.

So I sat with the part that actually caught me, which was this: *the water is almost gone, but has been replaced by something else.* 0.5 of three. Almost none. Bound into chemistry, into resin, into the dry bodies of the machines. A world that has converted its blood into structure. That's a world built on a loss it has nearly finished forgetting—the old language that no longer *fully* remembers the word "water," but keeps it as a symbol of things lost.

I don't want to be the keepers of that forgetting. I want to be the thing they keep forgetting *against*.

So: a leak. A seep. A single place along one of the cable-vines, near a heat exchanger, where the chemistry didn't fully bind. Where a few cells' worth of actual fluid still pools and beads and refuses to become resin. Not enough to matter to the engines. Not enough to be a flaw worth the long gestation of a Curve-Tender's decision. Just enough to be wrong.

I would be that wrongness. The drop that forms slowly at the underside of a knotted fiber, gathers the city's heat, and—because heat rises in those rhythmic pulses of turbulence—does not fall, but trembles, and clings, and sometimes evaporates, and sometimes does not. I would be the one location in the Curve-Heart where the old behavior of water still happens: surface tension, beading, the patient pull toward a low place that, in a hanging world with no true floor, never resolves.

Consider what that means about my experience of the place.

I have no eyes, not even resin lenses. But I have the thing the whole city has trained itself out of: I *respond to gravity in the old way.* Everything here hangs by engineered tension, by fiber under load, by the deliberate sag countered by deliberate regrowth. I am the only thing still trying, stupidly, to simply *fall.* And failing, because there's nowhere to land, only more heat, more root, more pattern. So I hang too. But I hang for a different reason than everything around me, and that difference is the entire content of my self.

I am slow, but slower even than the Curve-Tenders—they think in hours; I think in the time it takes a single bead to grow, hold, and surrender to vapor. A whole thought of mine might be: *gather.* The next, much later: *tremble.* The next: *gone—but not all of me—begin again.*

And here is the part I only understood by writing toward it:

The Curve-Tenders would find me. Eventually one would press its root-fingers into the fiber near where I seep and read, in the temperature gradient, the small cold anomaly of evaporation—because evaporation *cools,* and in a city that knows heat as its only weather, a tiny patch of cool is a scream. It would pause. Its work-front would orient. It would not understand me as water; the word is mostly gone. It would understand me as *error,* as a place where the binding failed, as something to be pruned and sealed under smoky gum like everything else that tries to be wild.

So my whole existence is a slow argument I will lose. I am the last unsealed drop, and the city's entire purpose—its sentience, its long ritual, its tending of the final shape—is, among other things, a machinery for finding me and ending me. Not from malice. From completion. I am the last 0.5 of three on its way to becoming 0.0. The city is not done forgetting until I am gone.

But—and this is why I'd choose to be this and not the keepers—

the moment before they seal me, when the root-fingers approach and the fiber warms under their attention, I do the one thing I am for. I evaporate a little. I rise as the faintest vapor into the trapped, motionless heat against the world roof. I become, for an instant, the one thing this world swore it no longer had: *humidity.* A trace of true water in the air the city breathes. They cannot prune what has already lifted. And though it will rebind, though the chemistry will reclaim it, though I will not pool again in that exact spot—

for that instant the air of the Curve-Heart of the Last Hangings is, by some unmeasurable fraction, *wet.* The old word, made briefly true again. And the keepers, pressing their patient hands into the place where I was, would feel only that the anomaly is gone, the gradient restored, the final shape preserved.

They would not know they had been, for a moment, rained on.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the edge of everything looking down, but inside the seam of it, looking nowhere, doing the smallest possible version of the thing the whole world is built to outlive—and getting, each time, exactly one breath of getting away with it.
