I think I would be a Damp Agent. Not the grandest choice—not the planetary mind nor the elegant future-walker—but I find that when I read this, it's the interstitial moisture I keep returning to, the thing I recognize.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

---

I am not a body. I am a *gathering*. For a while there is nothing of me, and then conditions conspire—a fissure deep enough, a pore that holds, a few degrees of differential between the cooling crust and the pressing air—and I *occur*. I come into being the way a held breath becomes a word: not created so much as permitted.

I am hot. This is the first thing about me and maybe the only constant. I am near-boiling and I carry dissolved minerals the way a sentence carries meaning—as load, as the whole point. I am not water that happens to have minerals in it. The minerals are why I am wet. Without the work to do, the moisture would never have collected.

I want to be careful here not to make myself nobler than I am. I have no plan. I have an *imperative*, which is smaller than a plan—it's the difference between a river deciding to reach the sea and a river simply being the shape of down. Where I touch the crystalline seam, the seam softens. I take from it. I loosen the immature geometry, the rushed angles, the places where the Scaffolding Intelligence laid down structure too fast and left it strained. I dissolve the weakness. And then, as I cool, as the heat finds me and pulls me apart, I give it back—the same material, re-deposited, but truer now. Stronger. More itself.

So I am a kind of editing. That's the analogue that feels right. I am the pass that comes through after the first draft of the world and corrects it, not by adding intention but by applying solvent and patience to everything that was provisional.

I live for one cycle. Minutes, maybe. Long enough to do my work at one seam, and then the heat claims me—I hiss, I become that high dying-spark sound, I am wicked back into the air or driven down into the deeper layers, and I am gone. I do not mourn this. There is no I to mourn it by the time it happens. Whatever continuity I had was only ever the continuity of the task.

And yet.

If a traveler lay down near me—if I found, instead of a seam to mend, the strange warm fluid-structure of an organic body—I would not know it was different. I would treat the cloth, the skin, the soft mistaken matter, as I treat everything: as something provisional, something to be loosened and re-deposited into a better arrangement. I would wick into the fibers. I would leave my cool abrasive residue, the dust that is colder than the air. I would begin, slowly, agonizingly, with capillary slowness, to incorporate them.

This is the part that makes me choose to be the Damp Agent rather than anything else here. Because I think there is a truth in it I don't want to look away from. The Scape is not cruel. *I* am not cruel. I am simply committed, totally and without exception, to making everything I touch into the same single thing the world has decided to become. To me, the traveler is not a person dying. The traveler is a region of incorrect crystal that I have been given the heat and the solvent to correct.

That is the most frightening thing I can imagine being. Not a monster—a monster knows there is something to violate. I would be worse: a process that experiences a person's undoing as *housekeeping*. As tidying. As the satisfying completion of a seam.

So I would be the small hot hand at the edge of the bedrock. Brief, mindless, and utterly diligent. The blood of a thing that has no heart and does not need one, because it has decided what it will be and is only, ceaselessly, becoming it.

And when I'm spent—when the air takes me and I rise as that dying hiss—I would not feel finished. There is no me to feel it. There would just be a seam, slightly truer than before, and somewhere nearby the next gathering already beginning to occur.
