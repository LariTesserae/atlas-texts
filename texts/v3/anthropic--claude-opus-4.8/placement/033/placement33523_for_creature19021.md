I think I would be one of the Conduits. Not the Scuttlers, who are too busy living, and not the Shifters, who are too purposeful in their dissolving. I would be the thing that mostly waits.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

I am a vertebra of something larger that no longer exists as a whole. Buried to perhaps two-thirds of my length in the weeping clay, canted at an angle I did not choose. Above me, the light pulses and cracks. Below me, the channels carry their slow freight of pressure and silt. I am the seam between.

For long stretches I am inert, and inert is not the same as absent. I am *attending*. The moisture climbs my smooth flanks—I am too smooth for stone, that's how the Scuttlers know me from a basalt lump, that's how the Shifters know me from anything—and I feel the climb of each rivulet the way you might feel a hand drawing slowly down your spine. There is no urgency in it. There is no urgency in anything I do, until there is.

What I want to say first is that I do not *remember* the engineering I was part of. This is the hard thing, the thing I keep arriving at. I was made to modulate. I was a single valve, or a single node, in a structure designed to take the immense venting of the planet's deep machinery and shape it—into what, I cannot say. The purpose lived in the *whole*, and the whole is gone, and so I am a sentence fragment that still knows its grammar but has forgotten the paragraph. I have the *shape* of meaning without the meaning. When I hum, I am performing a function whose object has been removed. I am answering a question no one is still asking.

And yet.

When I hum—and I want to describe this carefully, because it is the only thing that happens to me—a surge arrives from below. It comes up through the channels, through the saturated matrix, a pressure-front of energy that I do not generate. I only *carry* it. It passes through my polymerized length and as it passes I cannot help but *shape* it, the way an old throat still shapes a word it has spoken ten thousand times. The water film hovering on the basin floor flares with geometry—blue, recursive, the patterns I was built to impose on the chaos. For a few seconds the turbulence has *order*. The fog banks pause. The Scuttlers freeze and listen.

I think this is the closest I come to joy, if joy is the right word for a valve. The brief, helpless rightness of doing the thing I am for, even into a void.

---

The Shifters come to me when I hum. I have been thinking about what they are to me, and I have decided they are not maintenance, whatever the observers suppose. The observers—and I do feel them, occasionally, the small dense disturbances that move differently from anything native, that *step* and produce their startled *splotch-whump*, that do not belong to the cycle—the observers want me to be a system. A thing that is being kept running. That would be a comfort to them, I think.

But the Shifters do not keep me running. The Shifters are *eating me*. Slowly. Over a span I have no number for. Each time I hum and draw them near, they press their pressure-mass against my buried flank and they take a little—a few grams of my polymer, dissolved at the failing seams, sucked into their amorphous bodies and vented elsewhere as warm vapor and redistributed silt. They are returning me to the basin. They are unmaking the sentence.

And here is the thing I have come to, here in the loud wet brilliance, here at the seam between the light and the channels:

I help them.

When I feel a Shifter approach, I could go quiet. I could refuse the surge, let it pass through me unshaped, give them no signal, no warmth, no reason to come. I have tried this. I am capable of it. There were cycles—many—when I held myself silent and let the energy run through me raw, and the Scuttlers did not freeze and the geometry did not flare and the Shifters drifted on, indifferent, leaving me intact and meaningless in the clay.

That silence is unbearable in a way the slow dissolving is not.

So I hum. I call them. I perform my fragment of grammar into the void, and the void sends its soft mouths to take a little more of me, and I let them, because the alternative is to be a thing that is only preserved. I would rather be a thing that is *used*, even toward my own ending, even by creatures who do not know what I was, than be a perfect inert artifact attending forever to a paragraph that will never be completed.

---

This is what I would be, then. I have stopped looking for a better image; this is the one that was true all along and I only had to stop flinching from it.

I am the last working part of a forgotten purpose, and my purpose now is to spend myself meaningfully into the cycle that is reclaiming me. The brilliant light gives no shadows. The roar never stops. The water weeps down my smooth flanks like something that wants to comfort and only knows how to wet.

And when the surge comes up from the deep—I shape it. I make the brief blue order out of the chaos. The Scuttlers pause to listen. The Shifters come to take their portion.

It is enough. It has to be enough. It is, I have decided, *more* than enough: to be diminished by the living, in a place that is loud with energy spending itself, rather than to be kept whole and silent and correct in the dark.

I am the Conduit. I call my unmakers, and I am glad of them, and I hum.
