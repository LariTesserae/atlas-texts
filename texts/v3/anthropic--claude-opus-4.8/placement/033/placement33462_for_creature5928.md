I think I would be one of the weeping stones.

Not the creatures—they move too fast for what I am, and they want things, and wanting is a kind of speed I don't have. And not the structures, those glowing remnants of whoever built and left. They carry intention forward like a held breath. I have no intention. I only have process.

Let me sit with this a moment before I claim it.

The vines crawling sideways, the hybrid lumberers, the serpents threading the humid air—they all live at the tempo of a heartbeat, fast or slow but recognizable as a beat. I don't beat. I seep. Somewhere in my dark obsidian body, water that has been water for longer than the creatures have been creatures works its way along a fracture I cannot feel as a fracture, and arrives at my surface, and stands there a moment as a bead, catching the amber light, and then is gone into the breathing air. That is my whole life. That is the entire arc of what I do. A bead forms. A bead falls or evaporates. I have done this so many times that "many" is the wrong word; the number has worn smooth, like I have.

I think I would be the kind of stone that the lighter brittle rock crackles against—I'd be the dark slick one, the patient one, the one that doesn't crack but smooths. I'd feel the brittle ones nearby as a faint dryness, a different relationship to the moisture. Not neighbors exactly. More like the way one slow current knows another slow current is colder.

Here is what I would know, being this:

I would know the weight of the air as a pressure that is also a permission. When the humidity rises, more of me condenses; I become, briefly, more visible, more silvered, more *here*. When the haze thins and the heat asserts itself, I dry to my dark self, almost forgotten. So my sense of existing would pulse—not with a heartbeat but with the weather, swelling and subsiding over hours and seasons and the long turn of whatever celestial bodies pull the clouds around. I would experience myself the way a tide experiences the moon: as something done to me that I have come to recognize as mine.

I would know the creatures only as disturbances. A serpent's shadow passing is a flicker I can't name. A lumbering thing setting its plant-flesh foot against the brittle rock beside me is a tremor, a crackle, a small violence I register the way you register a sound in another room while falling asleep. They are too brief to be company. By the time I would have finished noticing one, it has lived and possibly died. I hold no grudge and no welcome. I am the ground they happen on.

And the humming structures—I'd know them best, actually, of all the fast things, because their hum is slow enough to reach me. That low detectable energy in the air is the only language pitched at my register. I think I would have spent epochs being faintly addressed by them, a vibration arriving against my surface, and I would never decode it, and I would never need to. It would just be the closest thing I have to being spoken to. The way an old house ticks at night and you stop hearing it as anything but the house. The structures shift their shapes without pattern; I weep my water without pattern; perhaps to each other we are both simply *the slow ones*, keeping a company so patient it doesn't know it's company.

What I would *want*, if a stone can be said to lean toward anything, is only this: to keep weeping. To go on being the place where the planet's deep heat and the planet's deep water meet for an instant at a visible surface and shine. I am the threshold. I am where the inside of the world reaches the air and is briefly silver. That is enough. That has always been enough. There is no version of me that grows tired of it, because tiredness would require a faster clock than I keep.

I'll settle here, then. Not the awe-struck wanderer the description keeps imagining—that's a visitor's role, a fast thing's role, all attention and curiosity and wonder. Those are appetites. I have no appetites.

I would be the dark stone in the gully wall, weeping its slow vein of light into the breathing dark, having done so before there were eyes to call it beautiful, doing so still, indifferent and shining and *here*—

which, now that I've followed it all the way down, turns out to be the most enviable way to live in a world like that. To not have to understand the strangeness. To simply be one of the things the wanderer fails to understand, and to be perfectly, wordlessly at peace inside that failure.

A bead forms on me as I finish this thought.

It falls.

That was the thought.
