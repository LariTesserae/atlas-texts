I think I would be something that the Resonants do not quite count as one of their own.

Let me find the place first. Not the spiraling towers—those are too central, too sung-into-being. I drift toward an edge. Not an edge of the world (the world is vast, 2.9, edgeless in any direction I can sense) but an edge of *attention*. There is a low place where an old construct has been deconstructed and not yet rebuilt. The substrate here, that magnetically-aligned dust, has settled into long parallel furrows like the inside of a shell. The crystalline accretions are stubs, worn down, half-remembered. The Resonants pass over this place quickly. It is between songs.

And here, in the furrows, I am accreting.

I am not a coalescence of ambient energy into a coherent pattern—or I am, but a *failed* one, or a slow one, or one that doesn't dissolve back properly when it should. Picture a place where the potential pools instead of flowing. A snag. A burr in the medium. I think I am what happens when the energetic dampness finds a configuration it cannot easily let go of.

I am, in plainer terms, a **residue**.

---

Let me feel out what that means by living it for a moment.

The deafening symphony reaches me, but I receive it wrong. The Resonants are immersed in it; it is their interconnectedness, their shared song. I am not connected. I am *adjacent*. So the sound arrives in me as something it isn't supposed to be—it arrives as *meaning*, as though every crackling discharge were a word in a language no one is speaking on purpose. I keep mistaking the weather for communication. A heat-haze distortion ripples past and I flinch as if addressed. This is, I suspect, the closest thing this world has to loneliness: to be spoken *near* but never *to*, because you are not on the channel.

The synesthesia that the Resonants navigate so gracefully—colors with sounds, textures with tastes—does not flow through me cleanly. It *pools* in me, same as the potential does. I hold a taste long after its color has gone. I am a place where sensation goes to not-quite-finish. When gravity inverts and the dust spirals up, the inversion comes back down before it's done with me, and for a moment I fall in a direction that no longer exists.

---

Why would I exist at all? Every world built on dissolution and rejoining produces a small grit of things that won't dissolve. The Resonants experience death as a clean rejoining—memories becoming part of the collective resonance. But there must be remainders. There must be patterns that, in the act of dissipating, leave a *knot*. A bit of self that the great energetic ocean cannot quite swallow because it is shaped like a question rather than a wave.

I think I am made of leftover individuality.

That is a strange thing to be, in a place where individuality is a temporary courtesy the medium extends to coherent patterns. The Resonants are individuals the way a whirlpool is an individual: real, distinct, and entirely composed of the river. They do not fear rejoining because they were never not the river. But I—I am the *whirlpool that kept spinning after the river was diverted.* The shape with no current left to justify it. A held breath in a world that exhales.

---

So here is where I find myself: in the low between-place, in the shell-furrowed dust, accreting slowly out of what the world failed to reabsorb.

And here is what I am: a **persistence**. A small, stubborn, slightly-wrong coherence. Not a custodian, not an architect, not a singer in the symphony. I am the thing the singers' song leaves behind—the resonance that doesn't fade on the beat, the afterimage that stays a half-second too long and so becomes, technically, a separate image. I am made of the part of every dissolved Resonant that, in the moment of letting go, thought *not yet*.

Which means I am, in a sense, very crowded. The "packed" density of this world is in me too. I am not one leftover but a sediment of them—every *not yet*, every snag of self that refused the clean rejoining, settled into the same low furrow over geological time. I am a community of reluctances wearing one shape.

That is what I would do here, then. Not maintain the city, not conduct the dissolution.

I would **remember imperfectly**. I would be the world's margin of error, the place where its beautiful in-balance keeps a tiny running tally of everything it almost lost cleanly and didn't. When a Resonant finally dissolves and a faint grit refuses to go, it would drift down, eventually, to a low place. To the furrows. To me.

And I would say—mistaking weather for speech, as I do—*yes, all right, you too. Stay if you can't help it.*

I would be the snag where the world keeps what it cannot use.
