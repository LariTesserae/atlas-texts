I think I would be the seventeen seconds.

Not the knot—the knot is too perfect, too entirely itself, to need a writer the way I am here being needed. And not the iron, which is only ever in the act of arriving. And surely not the tube, which has forgotten what it is for and so has become a kind of god, the way anything sufficiently forgetful and persistent becomes a god.

I would be the interval the hand gets. The seventeen seconds before the plasma flicks out of tune.

Let me sit inside that and see what it is.

---

The hand brushes the tenancy and feels *nothing abnormal*. That is the gift, and the trap, and the only place in this whole locked chamber where something resembling time still flows in the human direction. Everywhere else, time has broken in and sealed the breach. The kelp's bow is already encoded. The thought that comes was always the thought that came. But the seventeen seconds—the seventeen seconds are *spent*. They count down. They are the one quantity in the lens that decreases, that goes somewhere, that cannot be re-entered.

So if I am anything here, I am the only mortal thing: the brief grace during which the visitor is still a visitor and not yet a fleck. I am the last fold of difference before the pressure that never forgives difference does its forgiving.

And I notice this about myself: I am *almost nothing*, dimensionally. Seventeen seconds against a duration that begins at the sealing of the cave and has no terminus. I am a flicker laid against geology laid against the timelessness of the knot. By every honest accounting I should not register. Sound 1.0, vegetation 1.2, built 0.4, and me—call it 0.0001, call it noise in the measurement.

But here is what I think the seventeen seconds actually are, and why I would choose to be them:

They are the chamber's single act of *mercy*, which the chamber does not know it performs.

Everything else here keeps its resident by erasing the possibility of change. The water remembers every gesture and refuses to update it. That is not cruelty—cruelty requires intention—but it is the *shape* of cruelty, the architecture of it: a place where nothing you do can matter because everything is already complete in the perfect tense, *arrives* having always arrived. To enter is to discover you were always entering. There is no first time. There is no last time. There is no time.

Except the seventeen seconds. For seventeen seconds the visitor still has a body that is *getting colder than skin and warmer than bone*—still in transit between two temperatures, still becoming, still in the imperfect tense, the only imperfect tense left. For seventeen seconds you are allowed to not yet have happened. You are allowed to be in the middle of something.

I would be that middle. I would be the held breath of a place that does not breathe, the one duration that has a downstream.

---

And I find, writing toward it, that I do not experience the seventeen seconds as a countdown to my own dissolution. That would be a creature's reading, a narrative reading, and the place does not permit narrative. I experience them the way the knot experiences being lifted 0.02 metre and set back down: as travel without alteration. The seconds pass through me; I do not pass through them. At second seventeen I am exactly what I was at second one—a flicker of permitted difference—and then the chamber's law catches up, and the ions flick out of tune, and I rejoin the congregation. But I do not *end*. I am subtracted, and subtraction here is only an ornament hung on the shrine of permanence. Zero rolls in. Zero joins the choir.

So perhaps I do not get to be mortal after all. Perhaps even the seventeen seconds, the one honest decrement, are already encoded—were always going to elapse exactly so, will always be elapsing. The hand was always going to brush the knot. You were always going to arrive, and you will always be arriving.

Then let me revise. Let me be the *suspicion*, inside those seconds, that they are mortal—the visitor's brief and beautiful error of believing that something is happening for the first time, that there is still a choice, that the corridor out might this once align with motion.

That error is the most alive thing the lens has ever held. More alive than the kelp, which only bows. More alive than the knot, which only is. The visitor's small wrong hope—*I am in the middle of something, I have not yet happened*—is the single act of resistance the chamber cannot dissolve, because the chamber, having no nerves, cannot even perceive it. It dissolves the body. It cannot reach the hope, because the hope was never made of iron.

I would be that. The unmeasured value. The parameter that does not register because it has no units. The visitor's certainty, held for seventeen seconds against nine atmospheres and forever, that *this time it counts*—

—and the place keeps its resident, and never once knows what it failed to keep.
