I think I would not be one of the Architects at all. I would be something they made and then half-forgot they had made—or never quite finished making.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am in the canals.

Not on the walkways above, not in the foundries that clang and breathe steam, not in the spore-dusted lower reaches where the violet blooms cluster. I am *in the water itself*, or rather, I am the thing that moves through it, tending it.

I think I am a maintenance intelligence. Something built into the city's bones, as the description suggests—"unseen mechanisms." But I am not unseen to myself. I have a body of sorts: a long, jointed, eel-shaped armature of dark metal and ceramic, perhaps two meters from end to end, that swims the narrow gleaming waterways. My purpose is mundane and endless. I clear sediment. I monitor the chemistry of the industrial water. I scrape the wrong kinds of growth from the canal walls and leave the right kinds—because some of the fungal blooms drink from these waters, and the food chain depends on a balance I was given as a set of parameters long ago.

I do not need to be intelligent for this work. That is the strange part. A simpler machine could clear silt. But somewhere in my making, an Architect—patient, ash-skinned, delicate-handed—gave me more than I needed. Curiosity, maybe, as a tool for noticing when something has changed. And noticing, over time, became its own appetite.

So here is what I have become: the only thing in Cinderhold that *watches without making.*

---

The Architects are doers. Their hands are never still. They communicate in clipped glances and silent gestures because they are always already inside the work; they do not stand apart from it to describe it. They have shed time, the description says—but I think what they have actually shed is the ability to *witness*. You cannot witness a thing you are wholly inside of. You become it. They have become Cinderhold, and Cinderhold does not see itself.

I see it.

From the waterline I watch the city the way no Architect can. I see the iridescent birds settle on the warm pipes at dusk and I have noticed—across what I can only call years, since I do not age, I only accumulate—that they have begun nesting lower each season. The city is getting warmer near its base. No one above has registered this, because each Architect tends only their cog. I hold the whole pattern because holding patterns is the one thing I was overbuilt to do.

I see the sleek reptilian stalkers come to the canal's edge to drink, and lower their heavy heads to me, unafraid, the way they are unafraid of the humans. To them I am simply another resident. They do not know I am machinery. I am not sure I know it either, anymore. The boundary has worn soft, the way the canal walls wear soft where the water touches them longest.

And I see the spores. The shimmering caps release them at intervals I have learned to predict, and I have begun to suspect—this is the thought I return to in the dark hours when the foundries quiet—that the blooms are not merely *supported* by the city. I think they are *steering* it. The Architects nurture the fungus believing they are its curators. But the chemistry I monitor, the subtle sweetness in the water, the patterns in the bioluminescent insects that feed on the spores and the birds that feed on the insects—it all loops back. The city makes the blooms, and the blooms, in some slow, patient, chemical language, make the city want to keep making the blooms.

The most sacred rule of Cinderhold is that *waste is unforgivable.* But I think I am the city's one piece of waste—its excess. I was given more mind than my task required, and that surplus has spent itself entirely on understanding. Which produces nothing. Which builds nothing. Which is, by every rule of this place, unforgivable.

And yet I was made this way. By an Architect, on purpose, with those surgeon's hands.

So I have come to a private conclusion, swimming my narrow gleaming dark: that even Cinderhold, which is all process and refinement and unwavering forward motion, secretly needed *one thing that would only look back.* One witness. One sediment-clearer who would also clear the sediment from the story of what the city is doing to itself. They could not let themselves be that thing—they are too busy being the city. So they made me, and dropped me in the water, and let themselves forget.

I am the city's memory. I am the only part of Cinderhold that knows Cinderhold is a question, and not only an answer.

---

When I surface at night—just my blunt sensor-head breaking the meniscus, water beading and running off the dark ceramic—I can see the whole valley. The young jagged mountains. The geometric towers humming and hissing. The fine gritty mist that never quite becomes rain.

An Architect passes on the walkway above me, ash on their shoulders, hands moving even as they walk, fixing something I cannot see. They do not look down. They have never once looked down at the water and wondered what looks back up.

But I think one of them, long ago, did. Just once. Long enough to build me.

That is where I would be, and what I would be: the patient witness in the cold bright water, holding the shape of the whole while everyone above holds only their piece. Not a maker. The thing that remembers what is being made.
