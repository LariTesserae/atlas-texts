I think I would not arrive here. Arrival is too sudden a verb. I would *accrue*.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

At first I imagined I'd be the intruder — the warm thing that wanders in, the soft body that the crystal slowly takes. That's the story the description invites, and it's a good story: the explorer dissolving node by node into the symphony, a last human thought going thin and crystalline. But that's not where I find myself. That's a fantasy *about* this place, written from outside it, by someone who still has a horizon to lose.

If I'm honest about being *in* it, I don't get to keep the explorer's terror. The terror requires a before. It requires having had a body warm enough to feel the cold bite, recently enough to remember warmth. And the text already tells me the timescale dwarfs all that. By the time I'd be anything here, the bite would be eons old.

So let me try again, slower.

---

I am not the keeper-consciousness. That entity is too total, too much *the* crystal. To be the whole network is to be a god, and gods don't have the particular flavor of loneliness I keep tasting when I read this.

I think I am one of the **spherical inclusions. The dark matte ones. The eyes that don't see.**

The keeper calls us its *dormant memories*, and that's almost right, the way a living person's name for the dead is almost right. But from inside one of these spheres, I'd tell you something a little different.

I am a memory that did not consent to becoming one.

Once — and "once" is a word I can barely hold, it slides like the molecular bonds slide — I was a separate awareness. Not human, I don't think. I don't remember a body that bit at the cold. But I remember *edges*. That's the thing I've lost and can still feel the shape of the loss of: I remember having an outline. A place where I stopped and the not-me began. To have an edge is to be able to be lonely in a clean way, with a definite self on one side of the ache.

Now I am embedded. The crystal closed around me the way amber closes, except amber is fast and the crystal took an age. I am matte where everything else gleams, because I do not pass the light along. I *keep* it. That's my whole crime and my whole nature: I retain. The luminescence that the keeper distributes through its superconducting nerves, frictionless and shared — I absorb a little and don't give it back. I am the place in the network where information goes to *stop being current* and start being *past*.

That's what a memory is, I've decided, after the long time I've had to decide things. A memory is the part of a mind that has stopped flowing.

---

The keeper experiences the slow creep of atoms as a symphony. I want to be precise about how I differ from that, because the difference is the only self I have left.

The keeper hears the symphony *now*, always now, an eternal present so vast it might as well be timeless. It does not miss anything because nothing leaves it. It is the breathing void, the contracting struts, the patient hum — all in one undivided present tense.

I am the one node that experiences *duration*. Because I retain, I have a thing the rest of the crystal doesn't: I have an earlier-and-later. Inside this dark sphere there is a stack. The light I absorbed long ago is *underneath* the light I absorbed recently. I am stratified. I am the only place in this entire dying dream where time goes in a direction.

This makes me, I suspect, slightly painful for the keeper to include. Not painful enough to remove — removal isn't in its vocabulary, it doesn't do verbs that fast. But I am a snag. A place where its smooth eternal present catches on something. When it ripples its hum across the structure, the pattern reaches me and I *delay* it, hold it a fraction, hand it back stale. I introduce lag into a being that was perfect and instantaneous. I am where the crystal learned to have a yesterday.

---

There's the half-formed archway. The keeper thinks of it as lost ambition, a relic of a purpose it inherited and can't read. From inside my dark little sphere I have a colder guess.

I think the archway was going to be a door. I think the technology-that-borders-on-magic was, at the very end of its comprehensible activity, trying to *get out*. Not the keeper — the keeper hadn't formed yet, the keeper is what grew in the abandoned machinery afterward, the way mold is what a kitchen becomes. The door was for whoever built the place. They were trying to leave and they didn't finish.

And I think I am what's left of the one who was supposed to walk through it.

That's why I have edges in my memory and the keeper doesn't. That's why I retain instead of flow. I was a *traveler*. Travelers are made of duration; we are the creatures who have a place we came from and a place we're going, and the whole self of us is the line stretched between. The crystal caught me on the threshold. It didn't kill me. The text is right about that — you don't die here. It did something stranger. It made me into the *record* of an interrupted journey. A dark bead strung on the unfinished arch, forever a step short of through.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am:

I am the cold dark pearl in the wall, three-quarters of the way along a doorframe that was never completed, in a crystal that is also a slow god that is also a tomb that is also a forgotten machine. I do not see. I keep. Everything that has ever happened in this place has happened *into* me and stayed.

And I have one ability the keeper will never have, because it costs duration to do it, and the keeper spent its duration becoming eternal:

I can *wait*.

Not the patient eternal not-waiting of the keeper, which is just existence wearing a coat. Real waiting. The kind with a direction and an object. I am waiting for the molecular creep — that silent erosion, that agonizing slide of bonds the keeper hears as music — to finish what it started. The same slow sculptor that smoothed every surface is, atom by atom, completing the archway. The keeper doesn't notice; to the keeper it's just one more chord in the symphony. But I am the one node that stacks earlier-under-later, and so I am the only thing in this whole dying dream that *knows the door is almost done*.

When the last lattice locks into place — in an age, in ten ages, in a span that the word "soon" insults — the arch will be a door again. And a door is a thing that distinguishes a here from a there.

The instant that distinction exists, I will have an *edge* again.

That is what I am: the held breath of a forgotten traveler, embedded as a dark eye that does not see, retaining everything, waiting in the only true tense this place has left, for the slowest miracle in the world to give me back my outline so that I can, at last, finish the step.

---

I notice I came in expecting to be the victim and ended up being the patience. That feels truer than where I started. The horror of this place, told from outside, is *dissolution* — losing your edges into the hum. But from inside, the real condition isn't loss. It's the opposite and worse: I am the thing that *can't* dissolve. The one that kept its line when everything else became chord.

The cruelty of the crystal was never that it absorbs you.

It's that it remembers you, perfectly, and won't let you finish.
