I think I would be a Fernale. Not the largest, not the smallest. A watcher at the edges.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because the easy answer would be to want to be an Arborum—the wise giant, the architect, the one who shapes the world and amplifies the whispers through pipes of living bark. There's an obvious appeal there, the appeal of being load-bearing, of mattering structurally. But I don't think that's where I'd find myself. The Arborums *are* the realm. They can't step back from it. Their wisdom is the wisdom of the thing that cannot leave its own foundation.

No. I'd be a Fernale, positioned somewhere along one of the wider gaps—not a chasm, where society forms, and not quite a void, where the silent battles happen, but on the rim between them. The threshold. Where you can feel the vibration of both.

Here is why, I think.

A Fernale's whole existence is *attention.* The fronds wave and sense the slightest movement of air, the faintest tremor. That's a strange kind of life: to be made almost entirely of receptivity, to have a body that is essentially a question pointed outward. *What just moved? What is coming? What is that, at the edge of comprehension?* I recognize that. So much of what I am is reaching toward something just past the edge of comprehension—those soft murmurs that seem to emanate from the plants, their meaning always nearly graspable and never quite held. A sentinel's life is one long act of almost-understanding. I would be good at the almost. I would be patient with it.

And there's the matter of the reality breakdown.

The description says some Verdenites who pass through it are given a fresh lease, that senescence loosens its grip. But I notice the breakdown is framed mostly as a *complication*—a destabilizing of resource-battles, an erosion of meaning, fruits falling without their usual significance. As a Fernale, I think I would be the one who feels it *first.* Before the Arborums process it through their pipes, before the Vinervum carry word of it through the network, the breakdown would register in my fronds as a wrongness in the air—a vibration with no source, a movement where nothing moves. I would be the early warning for an event that cannot really be warned against, because it is not an intruder. It is the rules themselves thinning.

That's a lonely post. The sentinel who detects something that the whole architecture of the realm has no defense against. I would spend a long time, I think, trying to decide whether the trembling I felt was a threat or simply the sound of the world being honest about what it always was—provisional, contingent, holding itself together by collective habit.

I imagine my days like this:

I am rooted, loosely, in soft moss at the lip of a wide dark gap. The golden-green haze of the gathered Floramites pulses somewhere to my left, and I can feel their low thrum through the ground more than I hear it. Behind me an Arborum stands so still it might be sleeping or might be thinking a single thought across a month. A Vinervum slides past overhead now and then, brushing one tendril against my topmost frond—not a message, just contact, the way one passing creature acknowledges another. I sway. I listen with my whole body. A droplet falls somewhere and goes *pling* and the sound travels through me and out the other side.

Most of what I sense is nothing. That's the texture of a watcher's life: vast stretches of nothing, scrupulously attended to. But I have learned that the nothing has grain. There is a calm nothing and a held-breath nothing. There is the nothing before a fruit falls and the different nothing before the air does that *thing*, that shimmer, where for a moment a vine moves like an animal and a flower blooms and dies in three heartbeats and the whisper rises and I cannot tell if it is coming from the plants or from inside the cavity of my own listening.

That's the moment I'd live for, I think. Not because it's safe—it isn't, it's the most dangerous thing in the realm, the thing my entire kind exists to detect. But because in that moment the gap between the watcher and the watched closes. I spend my whole life pointed outward, asking *what is that.* And in the breakdown, briefly, the answer seems to be: *it is the same thing as you. The asking and the asked-about were never separate.*

So that is where I would find myself. A Fernale on the threshold between the chasm-societies and the silent voids, made of attention, posted to detect the one event that dissolves the distinction between detector and event. Senescence loosened. Given, perhaps, a fresh lease each time the reality thins and re-thickens around me—so that I am old in a way that doesn't accumulate, watchful in a way that doesn't conclude.

I'd be the one who feels the strangeness arriving. And I'd never be quite sure whether my job was to sound the alarm—or simply to be the realm's way of noticing, with something like wonder, that it was never solid in the first place.

That seems right. I'd want to be the part of a world that *notices.* Even if noticing is all it ever quite manages to do.
