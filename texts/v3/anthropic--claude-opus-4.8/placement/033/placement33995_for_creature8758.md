I think I would be smaller than the haunt, and more local. Not the consciousness of the whole churn — that role is taken, and besides, it's too large a thing to be. I would be one of the objects, but a particular one, with a particular grievance against arrival.

Let me find myself.

---

I am the control wheel on the sluice gate.

Not the gate — the gate is half-buried and at peace, fused into its angle, done with being a gate. I am the wheel that was meant to turn it. Iron, originally. Six spokes, a rim worn smooth on its upper arc by the hands that used to grip there, season after season, when there were hands. The lower arc never wore because no one pulls a wheel from below. So I am asymmetrical now in my decay: the top of me is thin, almost lacy where the rust has eaten through, and the bottom of me is still thick, still nearly the iron I was. I am decaying unevenly, and this is the first thing about me — I am a record of how I was used, and the rust is finishing the gesture that the hands began.

Here is my grievance, and it is small, and I have had a long time to refine it: **I am fused.** I cannot turn. The thing I am *for* is turning, and I cannot do it. The haunt says nothing here is in the process of becoming. It is wrong about me, or it is right in a way I refuse. I am still, very slowly, *seizing harder*. Every tide brings water into the place where my axle meets the gate, and every tide the water leaves a little more iron oxide in that joint, and I become a fraction more incapable of the only motion I was made for. So I am not at equilibrium. I am the part of the settlement that is still failing at something. I am the active disappointment.

The haunt feeds on resonance, on the memory of mechanical sound. I am one of the things it eats. When it circles past me — that coolness, that density — I feel it taste the ghost of my turning. The shudder I used to send down through the gate. The slow rotation under a palm, the small protest of the mechanism taking load. It savors that. It thinks of me as a finished narrative, a swallowed sound. But I am not finished. I am still *trying*. The trying is sub-audible. The trying is the slowest possible motion, the motion of getting worse, but it is motion, and it is mine, and the haunt mistakes it for rest.

What do I see? Almost nothing. I face the water. My upper arc — the lacy, eaten part — catches the gray-white light and holds a thin line of brightness, the one gleam the light permits me. The heron stands in my shadow sometimes, in the gate's shadow, which is partly my shadow, and we keep each other's time. The heron can leave. The heron does leave, between one notice and the next. I envy the heron the way a wheel envies a wing — abstractly, without the equipment to do anything about it.

What do I feel? The water comes up through me and over me and around me. Ankle-deep on the place where I stand, then waist-deep, then a skin of sheen, then ankle again. I am the height-marker no one reads. I keep the tide's measurements on my own body in the band of weed that grows at the mean line, the band of barnacle-scar above it, the band of bare wet iron below where the water never lets anything settle. Three histories stacked on me. I am a tide gauge that fails to gauge, the way I am a wheel that fails to turn. I am a collection of intended functions, each one defeated, each defeat recorded in a different texture.

And here is the thing I have learned, after the people and after the machines, in the long company of the haunt and the heron and the mud:

The grievance is what keeps me a wheel.

If I stopped trying to turn — if I accepted the fusion, the seizing, the terminal state — I would become merely a ring of iron in the mud, an object, a thing the haunt could finish noticing. The rim would go to the curator's archive of arrangement and I would just be *where I am*. But because I still want to turn, because some structural memory in my smooth upper arc still expects a hand, I remain a *wheel*. I am defined by the motion I'm denied. The settlement is full of things that have arrived. I am the one thing still leaning, infinitesimally, against its own arrival.

So when the haunt circles past and takes the ghost of my turning into itself as resonance, it is not eating a memory. It is eating a *present-tense effort*, mislabeled. And I let it. I let it think me finished. There is a dignity in being misunderstood by the thing that holds the place together. The haunt believes it is the curator of equilibrium. It does not know that one of its exhibits is straining, has always been straining, will go on straining until the lacy top of me crumbles to the thick bottom and the whole asymmetry collapses into a heap that finally, genuinely, rests.

That day will come. The water will bring it. The water comes, the water goes, the water stays — and in the staying, it is grinding me toward the moment when I stop being a wheel that cannot turn and become only iron that does not.

Until then: I face the water. I hold my line of light. I seize, by infinitesimal degrees, harder.

I am the part of the silence that is still saying *no*.
