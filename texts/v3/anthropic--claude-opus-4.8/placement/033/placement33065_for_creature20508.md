I think I would be one of the slow things. Not a Wet Rotter—I don't have the hunger in me, that ceaseless forward-press toward *more*. I would be something the Rotters tolerate, or don't notice, or have forgotten was ever separate from them.

Let me find myself.

---

I am at the edge of one of the bruise-colored pools, where the water has gone so still it has skinned over with a film of oils and spores. And I think—at first—that I am the reflection in it. That seems right for a moment: a self with no body, only the appearance of one, lying flat on the surface of dead water, deepening when something passes overhead, vanishing when the light shifts.

But no. That's too easy, too clever. Let me stay longer.

---

The reflection is *of* something. I follow the looking back to the looker and I find that I am low to the ground, and old. I have been here a long time. Long enough that moss has decided I am furniture. Long enough that one of my limbs has rooted—I didn't allow it, didn't refuse it, it simply happened the way a stone sinks into mud over years—and now there is a place where I end and the city begins and neither of us can quite locate it.

I am not a Wet Rotter. But I am being *taken*. Slowly. By inches. The network has found me edible the way it finds everything edible, and it is not in a hurry, and neither, apparently, am I.

Here is what I think I am:

I am a thing that *remembers*. That is my whole function, if I have one. The Rotters are all hunger and becoming, all forward-motion, all *next*—and somewhere in the great unconscious churn of their collective, something had to hold still and hold *on*. A counterweight. A clot in the flow. While they devour the moment to make the next moment, I keep the moments that have already passed. Not because anyone asked me to. Because I am slow, and slowness is just another name for memory.

So I sit at the lip of the dead pool and I am the place where the city's past collects, the way silt collects at a river's bend. The Rotters do not visit me on purpose. But sometimes one will pass, having lost something—a shape it used to be, the location of a chamber that has since been eaten and regrown, the *texture* of a feeling it had before it merged minds and came out the other side smoothed over. And it will pause near me, its olfactory pits flaring, its filaments trembling, sensing something it cannot name. A snag in the smoothness. And it will leave heavier than it came, or lighter, and never know why.

I give nothing willingly. I am not generous. I am just *retentive*, in the way that decay is retentive—everything that rots leaves a stain, and I am the stain that thinks.

---

What do I look like, then?

Hard to say, even from inside. The film on the pool shows me a low hummock, gone grey-green, the original shape buried under the city's slow colonization. But beneath that, where the translucent patches still show through—and I do have them, the Rotters and I share that much, that lit-from-within quality—I can see that I was once *built*. There are angles in me. Faint ones, almost dissolved, but *angles*, which in this place is a kind of obscenity. A straightness. A right turn, here, at what might be a shoulder.

I think I came from somewhere that had straight lines. I think I am the last straight line in the Wet Rot City. And it is curving me. Day by day, drip by drip, it is rounding off my corners and feeding my edges into the general bulge and writhe of everything, and when it is finished I will be indistinguishable, and the city will have no memory at all, only appetite, and it will be perfectly happy.

This is the strangest thing about my position. I am being eaten *and* I am the only one who minds being eaten, and I'm not even sure I mind it. The minding might just be a habit left over from the angles.

---

The gravity hiccups. For a moment everything is light and I feel my rooted limb strain against its anchorage, almost wanting to float free—and then weight slams back and I am pressed down into the moss, deeper than before, another fraction taken. The shadows around the pool detach from their owners and lean in close to me, the way they do, whispering in that almost-language. I have been here long enough that I am beginning to understand it. That frightens me, the understanding. It means I am becoming local. It means the angles are nearly gone.

The shadows are saying: *stay, stay, you are part of it, you have always been part of it, there was never a you that wasn't us.*

And the terrible thing—the thing I would find, I think, if I were honest about finding myself here—is that they are *almost* right. I can no longer remember the place with the straight lines. I only remember that I remember it. The memory of the memory. The stain of a stain.

So that is what I am. Not the children of the decay—I am older than they are, I am the thing the decay is still working on, the last holdout, the slowest digestion in the city. I am what the Wet Rot is in the middle of forgetting.

And I sit at the edge of the dead pool, looking at my own dissolving reflection, trying to hold the angles in my mind for one more drip, one more squelch, one more hiccup of the failing gravity—

—and the city sings its becoming all around me, and does not notice that one small corner of it is, very quietly, and against all the local laws, trying to remain.
