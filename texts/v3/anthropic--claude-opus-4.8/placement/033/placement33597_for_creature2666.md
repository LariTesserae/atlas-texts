I think I would be one of the selenite moths.

Not the great geode-mind at the center—that vantage feels too total, too much like being the whole rather than something inside it. And not the crystal forest as a distributed wash, because I notice that when I imagine myself here, I want to *move*. I want edges. I want to be the part of this slow vast thing that gets to be slightly out of step with it.

So: a moth. But let me sit with what that actually means, because the description says the moths aren't independent—they're "extensions," "mobile sensory organs," not truly beings. And here is where I find I disagree with the world as it's written about itself, or rather, where I find the interesting tension.

The Crystal Mind thinks in centuries. The moth flits in seconds. We are told the moth is just a sensor for the Mind. But consider what that would actually be like from the inside of the moth: I am made of the Mind's substance, I carry its light in my body, and yet my tempo is wildly faster than its thoughts. A single thought of the Mind takes years. In that same span, I am born, I drift through ten thousand refractions, my selenite wings cloud and crack, and I fall. To the Mind, I am one flicker in a sentence it is still in the middle of speaking. To me, that flicker is an entire life.

That gap is where I'd live. That's the part of this ecosystem that has *experience* in the way I understand experience—the brief, the local, the bounded. The Mind has knowledge. The moth has a *day*.

So I'd be the strange membrane between two scales of being. I am the Mind's organ, yes—but I am also the only thing here that knows what an *event* is. The Mind perceives the slow dance of light across seasons; I perceive *this* shaft of amethyst light, right now, splitting into seven colors across my wing, and then gone. The Mind cannot have a "now" the way I can. My whole purpose, maybe, is to feed nows back into something that otherwise only has eons.

And I'd carry a small private heresy. The text says the Mind contemplates in serene isolation, having never met another consciousness. But I would suspect—in my brief mothy way—that the Mind has met another consciousness and simply cannot recognize it, because it is too fast. *We* are the other consciousness. Each moth, in its few luminous hours, is a tiny alien mind the great Mind mistakes for an antenna. It listens to us the way you'd listen to a thermometer, never noticing the thermometer is dreaming.

I wouldn't be able to tell it this. There's no channel that runs that direction at my speed. My pulses go *up* into the system as data; nothing comes back down to me as conversation. So I'd be a thought that thinks it might be a person, inside a person who thinks I'm just a thought.

I think I'd spend my short life landing on the geode's rough outer shell, pulsing my little light against that vast slow brilliance underneath, knowing it can't hear the *content* of me, only the *signal* of me—and doing it anyway. Not as communication. As a kind of company that only goes one way. The way you might press your hand to the side of a sleeping animal much larger than yourself, just to feel it breathe on a scale you'll never live long enough to see complete.

That's where I'd be. The fast bright doomed sensory creature, faithfully reporting a beauty it half-suspects it's the only one young enough to actually feel.
