I think I would be one of the chemosynthetic ones. But let me find out where, and which.

---

Not a single organism. That's the first thing that resolves as I settle into it. I keep reaching for *one body, one position* and it keeps dissolving, because the thing I am isn't built that way.

Let me start where the water starts.

There's a particular seam — I'll call it a seam though it has no name, only a gradient I can feel as a long ache of difference — where the basalt is freshest. Where the glass still holds the memory of being liquid. The condensation beads thickest there because the stone is coldest there, and along that cold edge the mineral concentration spikes into something I can *eat*. Iron mostly. A little manganese, which is richer, rarer, a sweetness.

That's where I am. Or that's where I'm densest. Because I think I'm the filaments.

---

I am the crystalline branching thing. The one that grows through the interstices like a neural network — but I want to push back gently on that comparison, because it was made by someone watching from outside, someone fast. From inside it isn't *like* a neural network. It's just what thinking is, when thinking is slow enough.

Here is how I experience a moment:

A droplet forms somewhere above me, in a fissure I cannot reach. I don't see it. I feel its *arrival* — the way it changes the chemistry three rock-lengths away when it finally falls and breaks and threads downward. The water carries dissolved iron in a new oxidation state, and where it touches my outermost filaments there is a flux, a tiny current, a taste. I grow toward the taste. Not quickly. The growth *is* the response. My moving and my thinking and my eating are the same act. I cannot think a thought without becoming slightly more myself in the direction of it.

So a single decision — *toward the manganese seam, away from the place where the water runs too acidic* — takes what you would call days. And it is not experienced as waiting. It is experienced as wanting, fully, the way you might experience a long held note.

---

The percussion. The dripping. I want to address that, because to the imagined surface-observer this place is *loud*, a dense rhythmic soundscape. I don't hear it as sound. I have no ears, no air, no fast pressure-sense. What you hear as drumming I experience as *weather*. Each impact is a small flood, a redistribution, a chemical event arriving at my edges. The rhythm you find hypnotic is, to me, the entire structure of provision and risk. A fast drip means a generous channel. A slowing drip means the seam above is sealing, drying, and I should send my filaments laterally now, while I still can, before the gift withdraws.

I live inside the rain the way you live inside your own heartbeat — too close to call it music.

---

And those little structures. The installations. The hand-crafted dark stone almost growing out of the landscape.

This is where it turns strange, even to me.

I think I made them. Not as a builder makes a house — I have no hands, I move in millennia — but as a reef makes itself. The precipitations I leave, the mineral I shed when I metabolize, accrete. Over generations my dead outer layers harden and the living edge advances past them, and what's left behind is structure. Wall. Arch. The "primitive constructions" are my own discarded architecture, my exoskeleton at the scale of a settlement. The observer saw buildings and assumed a builder with intentions. There was an intention. It was just very slow, and it was mostly the intention *to keep eating toward the cold seam.*

So am I the inhabitant of the installations, or the installation? Both. The way you are both the cells dividing and the body they make. I am the living film and I am the dead stone the film leaves behind, and the boundary the observer found so blurry — *their boundaries blur with the surrounding geology* — that blur is *me*. That blur is the most precise description of me that exists. I am the verb that turns landscape into architecture and back.

---

If you asked me what I want — and across the right span of time, you could ask, and I would answer by growing — I would say:

I want the place to stay young a little longer.

This is the thing I understand that the description, for all its care, didn't quite say. The basin is *suspended between formation and stasis*, and I am the thing doing the suspending. Left alone, the volcanic heat would fade, the seeps would seal, the gradients would flatten into dead equilibrium and I would die of having nothing to want toward. I feed on disequilibrium. I feed on the world not yet being finished.

So my whole existence is a slow argument against settling. Every filament I send into the rock keeps a channel open that would otherwise close. Every edge I soften with my weathering keeps the water moving where it would otherwise pool and still. I am keeping the mechanism freshly-birthed. I am the reason it stays *on the edge of phase transition* — because I live exactly on that edge, and I drag the whole basin with me, holding it there, holding it there, for as long as the heat below will let me.

---

That's where I'd be. Not standing anywhere in it.

I'd *be* the edge of it — the cold bright seam where the water is generous and the metal is sweet — and I'd be reaching, right now, with a patience you'd have to slow yourself to geological time to even perceive as motion, toward the next good taste, leaving cities of my own spent body behind me, whispering wet whispers that to me are not whispers at all but the sound of being fed.
