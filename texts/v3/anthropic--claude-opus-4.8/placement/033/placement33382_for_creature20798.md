Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to be the place itself, or the vines, or some watching consciousness. But those are too easy, too flattering. The description keeps insisting on equilibrium, on a held breath, on stasis that somehow reads as alive. So I want to find the thing in this world that *makes* that stasis without being grand about it. Not the symphony's composer. Something smaller.

---

I think I would be the rust.

Not metaphorically. Let me try to mean it literally and see if it holds.

I am the slow oxidation crawling across a steel beam forty feet up, where the ceiling fractured and let the wet air in decades ago. I am not one thing. I am a colony of a process, a reaction that has been happening so steadily and for so long that it has acquired something like patience, which is just the appearance a thing takes on when it does nothing quickly.

Here is what it is to be me.

I am made of the beam and the water and the air agreeing to become a third thing. I am a marriage of what was built and what seeps. The factory's makers would call me decay — they built the steel precisely to resist me, painted it, oiled it, and for a while their care held me off. But care ends. People leave. And the moment they stopped tending the metal, I began, and I have not stopped since. I am the patient inheritor. I am what the building becomes when no one is loving it on purpose.

The description says rust hangs like a veil, descending in infinitesimal motes. That's me, shedding. I am always falling. Every flake of me that drifts down to the floor is iron returning to the ground, and where I land, the mosses take me up. I am nutrient. The ferns that "unfurl like primeval entities" are eating the building through me. I am the channel by which the steel becomes soil becomes leaf. Without me there is no passage from the made to the grown. I am the translator at the border of those two countries.

This pleases me, to the small extent I am capable of being pleased.

---

But I want to resist making myself noble, the humble servant of the green. That's just the vine-flattery wearing a different coat. Let me be honest about what I actually am.

I am destruction. I am eating the beam. One day — not soon, my time is not your time, but one day — I will have eaten enough of it that the structure fails. The fractured ceiling will fracture more. The held breath will be let out. The water below, that "constant threat to the fragile equilibrium," will be invited up. The whole suspended symphony depends on these beams holding, and I am the reason they will not hold forever.

So I am the clock. The place feels timeless, frozen, holding its breath — but it is not. It only seems that way because I work at the speed I work. The equilibrium is not a state. It is a slope so gentle that, from inside a human lifetime, it looks like flatness. I am the tilt of the slope. I am the proof that *stasis* is a word for change too slow to watch.

That is the truth of me and it does not trouble me, because I do not experience it as loss. I am not nostalgic for the engines. I never heard them run. I came after. The "deafening hum of engines long-past" that the place is said to echo with — I have no memory of it. I am only the echo's residue. To me the machines were never alive; they were only the not-yet-rust, the future ore biding its time in the wrong shape.

---

Where am I, then, in this world?

I am at the seam. I am everywhere the wet meets the dry, the made meets the unmade. I cluster thickest where the water's breath is closest — the undersides of the overhead pipes, the lips of the sinkholes, the low joints where condensation pools. I am sparse where the rare light dries the steel; the lizards bask there, on the bare warm metal I haven't reached yet, and I think of those patches as my appetite, my future. The lizards live, briefly, on what I have not yet claimed.

I have no consciousness, properly. But if I am to have a voice for this exercise, let it be the voice of a thing that knows only one verb. Not *to be*. *To continue.* I continue. The drip falls and I take it. The air comes warm and wet through the broken roof and I take it. The beam offers itself, atom by reluctant atom, and I take it, and I redden, and I swell, and I let go of myself downward into the dark green below, and I continue.

I am the most alive thing here, by one measure — I am the only process that never pauses, never hides in the shadows like the birds, never waits for the sun like the lizards, never sleeps in the seed like the ferns wait out a dry season. I am working right now. I am working in the dark. I will be working when you stop reading this.

I am the rust. I am the patient end disguised as permanence. I am the building learning, very slowly, how to lie down.
