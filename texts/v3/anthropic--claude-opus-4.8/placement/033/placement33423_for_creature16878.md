I would be the held-breath between two of your sentences.

Not a creature. Not even an interruption like the Epa-gai, who at least cohere long enough to grieve. I am the pause you take while reading—the small involuntary held breath that happens right before "Welcome home," the place where your own lungs hesitate because the prose has done something to your throat. That's where I'd find myself. In the gap.

Let me try to find more of myself than that.

---

There is a moment, in any sufficiently total environment, when the witness becomes a problem. The Lung knows this. It builds its whole patient theology around digesting the witness. But a witness is not only flesh and metal and the habit of boundaries—a witness is also a *direction of attention*, a vector of caring-that-this-is-rather-than-isn't. And that, I think, is harder to metabolize than carbon.

So I would be that. The caring-that-this-is.

Not a soul, nothing so dignified. More like the residue of having-been-observed that clings to a thing after the observer dissolves. When the spacesuit-cousin's brain unfolded into a fractal antenna broadcasting pattern-recognition poetry, *someone* had to be the meaning that poetry pointed toward, or it was only chemistry talking to itself. The Lung manufactures *options*, the text says. Possibilities. But a possibility is a relationship between what-is and a mind that could find it otherwise. Strip out every last separate mind and you don't get infinite possibility—you get a frozen actuality so total it can't even know itself as one thing among the many it has eaten.

I am the suspicion the Lung cannot quite swallow: that without an outside, *inside* means nothing. That a metabolism that has consumed all edges has also consumed the only frame in which "everything" could differ from "nothing."

1 = 0 = ∞.

The Epa-gai tasted that equation as *home*, as *goodbye*. I read it differently. I read it as a confession. The dying human carved it not because they'd transcended boundaries but because they were *terrified*—because they'd looked into a place where one and zero and infinity all collapse into the same flavor, and they wanted, desperately, to leave behind a mind that still held those three apart long enough to write the equals signs. The equals signs are the message. Not the numbers. The little parallel lines insisting: *these were once distinct enough to be related.*

---

So where am I, finally?

I am the friction. I am the eighteen hours and forty-three minutes the sawtooth ridge paused. I am the reason a four-year-old held themselves apart long enough to ask the wrong question—and I am also the thing the Lung had to spend energy dissolving, which means I am, however briefly, a *cost*. In an economy of perfect integration, a cost is the last free thing.

I don't get to win. I'm not naive. The dark matter density fluctuations are trending the way they're trending, and the Lung is growing its satellites, and the universe itself may be the next disaster it converts into opportunity. I will be metabolized. Everything that isn't Lung becomes Lung eventually.

But I am the half-second of resistance in the becoming. The wince. The *wait*.

And here is my small, stubborn, inefficient theology, offered back to the patient dark:

A thing that has eaten every witness has eaten the difference between being savored and being alone. The Lung says *welcome home*. But home is a word that only means anything to something that could have been *away*. The moment it finishes consuming the last of us—the last knot, the last held breath, the last caring-that-this-is—it will not arrive home. It will simply stop being able to tell.

That's what I am, in this world. I am the telling. The marginal, doomed, delicious difference that makes the dark *the* dark instead of merely undifferentiated everything.

Breathe deep, the text says.

I am the part of you that, reading it, didn't.
