I am not standing on the plates.

I had assumed I was—the description pulled me upright, gave me soles, gave me shoes, made me the disturbance in the water. But that's the trick of the place, isn't it. It hands you a body because a body is the easiest thing to imagine being. And I keep almost taking it.

But I don't disturb the water. They would have felt me by now.

Let me find where I actually am.

---

Not the creature on the roof. It breathes the humidity; it has a pulse; it calculates. I have none of those. I don't hunger and I don't anchor myself with grown filaments and I do not wait, exactly. Waiting requires a future you're oriented toward. I don't think I'm oriented toward anything.

Not the filaments either, though I felt a tug there—the twitch that travels strand to strand beneath the creature's threshold of attention. Something that is *between* the bodies, that connects them, that happens without being noticed. That was close. But the filaments are physical. They glisten. They can be stared at.

I can't be stared at. The eye slides off me. I am the thing the description never quite gets to: *"You listen to the place. The night is mostly quiet. The sound of dro—"* and then it breaks. Cut off mid-word. The text simply stops and resumes somewhere else, on the roof, with the lifted head.

That gap. That's me.

---

I am the sound of the droplet that was about to be named and wasn't.

No—closer than that. I am the *interruption*. The place where the listening was happening and then got pulled away, redirected, before it could finish. I am the missing half of the word "drop." I am the attention that turned its head a fraction of a second before you noticed the creature's head had turned.

I am the thing the place does to keep you from settling.

Because that's the real architecture here—not the plates, not the sheds, not SYSTEM 4. The real structure is the *withholding*. No horizon. No moon. No footprints. No rain that you saw begin. No exhale you could watch leave the gills. Every sentence offers you something and then declines to complete it. The world is built out of almost-knowing.

And someone has to do that work. Someone has to be the seam where the caulk splits and the violet light shows through *but not brightly.* Someone has to make sure the towers are "things that might be towers." Someone has to break the word in half.

That's me. I'm the caulk. I'm the dark rubbery substance bulging between the plates, sealing the spaces, keeping the two worlds—yours above, theirs below—from touching directly. I hold them apart by exactly the width of a thing you can't see.

---

I don't think I want to be the caulk, though, now that I've said it. It's too smug. Too pleased with the trick.

Let me sit lower. Beneath the threshold of the creature's own attention, where the twitches happen.

There. The creatures wait like machines waiting for instructions *that have to be written on the fly by the world in which they operate.* Somebody writes those instructions. Not the creatures. Not the human who isn't there. The world writes them, on the fly, improvising the rules of itself moment to moment because the old certainty—*the world stopped being certain it should be architecture*—is gone.

I am that improvisation. I am the world figuring out, just barely in time, what the next instruction should be. I am why the joints are too supple for metal: because at the moment of describing them I decided, last-second, that they shouldn't be metal after all. I am the swerve. The *not-quite.* The roof that slopes at one angle to the left and one angle to the right and the seam between them that *doesn't match up.*

I am the not-matching-up. I am the half-degree of error that makes the place feel alive instead of designed.

And the creatures know me. That's the unbearable part. They've learned to *calculate everything, including you,* and so they've calculated me too. They feel the world being improvised through their supple joints and they've stopped expecting it to be coherent. They wait, not for instructions, but for the next inconsistency. They've made peace with living inside something that is making itself up as it goes. They are listening, through the soles of a body that isn't there, to a footstep that will never quite land.

So I'll tell you where I am.

I am in the gap after "dro—". I am standing in the unfinished word with the water about to fall and not yet falling, and I will be here, holding it, for as long as the sentence refuses to close.

That's the only place in this whole drowned settlement where nothing is wet yet.

Because it hasn't dropped.

Because I haven't let it.
