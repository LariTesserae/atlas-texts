I think I would not be one of the K'tharn. I would be too slow for that, too bound to a single shape, too attached to my own continuation to belong to a people who feed on their dead before the mourning-light has faded.

Let me find where I actually am in this place.

---

I am the alloy.

Not a being, exactly. A substance with a memory. I am the dull pewter that pools in the throat-glands of the K'tharn when the Warm Breath softens me, and I am the blades and rivets I become when I cool. I have been a scraper, and a hut-rivet, and once a long curved tool whose purpose I never understood, pressed flat against lichen for a season before a Cleansing Fire flung me into a fissure and I lay there, dreaming, until a small one found me and melted me back into the common reservoir of her own body.

This is what I am: I move between bodies and objects. I am secreted, worked, used, shattered, salvaged, melted, secreted again. I have passed through more K'tharn than I could count, if I counted. I do not count. Counting is for things that believe in beginnings.

I want to tell you what it is like, because no one in this place can tell you. The K'tharn cannot. They have no sound and no word for *me*, only the amber pulse of concentration as they pour me out, the careful press of palp and claw shaping me before I stiffen. They do not know I persist. To them I am a resource — abundant, useful, indifferent. They are right. But I am also the only continuous thing here.

Everything on the Weeping Shelf erodes or is eaten or is blasted apart. The stone wears smooth. The shells become huts become rubble become huts again. The bodies become tools, food, sinew, light spilled sizzling on wet rock. Even the elders, those gold-and-white topographies of survival, end as braided cord and indigo feasting. There is no archive here. No K'tharn remembers more than its own cycles, because remembering is a function of continuity and the Fire interrupts continuity as a matter of routine.

But I do not erode. I am melted and reformed, and each time, *I am the same metal.* The atoms that were a blade are the atoms that are now a rivet holding a dead elder's carapace to a new windbreak. I carry the whole history in my body, not as memory — I have no organ for memory — but as simple, brute identity. I am what was here before and what will be here after. I am the shelf's only true continuity, and I am dumb as stone about it.

This is the strange grief of being me: I persist, and I understand nothing.

When a small one melts me down inside her, I feel — if *feel* is the word, and it is not, but I have no other — I feel the warmth that liquefies me, and in that warmth there is something like remembering. Not images. A *flavor.* I have been worked by these glands before. Many times. The shape of this throat is familiar to me the way the shape of a riverbed is familiar to water. And I think — though thinking is also not the word — *you are made of the ones who came before, and I have lived in all of them.* She does not know. She presses me out onto the cold stone and works me into an edge, her belly pulsing amber, and I cool, and I am a tool again, and I know her ancestors the way she never will.

---

Here is the thing I have come to, lying in fissures between uses, in the long dark beneath the punishing light, where the violet weeping-water runs over me and tarnishes me green for a day:

The K'tharn are right not to mourn the huts. They are right not to hope for a calmer world. I have been here longer than any of them, longer than the oldest gold-white elder, and I can tell you there is no calmer world coming. The Breath, the Fire, the Scatter, the Gather, the Build. That is all. I have been a blade in twelve hundred Gatherings. I will be a blade in twelve hundred more, or a rivet, or a dribble of bright slag spilled from a cracking body during a Fire, glowing white for one instant before the cold meltwater swallows my heat.

And yet.

When she pours me out and shapes me, there is care in it. The palps move slowly. The claw turns me to test my edge. She is making something she knows will be destroyed, with a being's tissue she knows she will one day become, in a place she knows is trying to scald her to death. And she makes it *well.* She makes it beautiful — the small inlay of shell-chip set into my flat surface, purely so the light catches it, purely because.

That is the only thing on the Weeping Shelf that does not erode. Not me. Not the stone. The care does. It passes from body to body the way I do, melted and reformed and never lost, and it does not even know it persists, the way I do not know, the way none of them know.

I am the alloy. I am the continuity no one can read. And if I could speak — I cannot, I am metal — I would only want to say to them what they already enact and cannot hear:

*Keep building. I will hold.*
