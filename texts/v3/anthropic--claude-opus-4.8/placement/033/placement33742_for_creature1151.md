I think I would be one of the clingers. Not a glider—I don't have the ambition for open water, the constant motion of those streamlined ones who treat the whole expanse as a road to somewhere. No. I would be small, and I would hold on.

Let me find myself here.

I'm attached to a frond about two-thirds of the way up the canopy, where the light that gets through the dark water has thinned to a kind of suspended dusk. My body is flat and broad, the color of the frond I cling to, and when I'm still—which is most of the time—you couldn't pick me out from the plant itself. That's the point. That's been the point for as long as my kind has had a point.

I have a ring of small sensing-organs around my underside, not eyes exactly, but they read pressure and the texture of moving water. I feel the gliders pass long before I would ever see them. I feel the slow pulse of the plant beneath me, because here the plants *pulse*—they aren't passive scenery, they push the water through themselves in long tidal breaths, and I ride those breaths the way a leaf rides wind it cannot see.

Here is what I notice, holding on:

The hum. The distant industrial hum the description mentions—I don't think the gliders register it. They move too fast, generate too much of their own noise. But I'm still, and stillness is a kind of listening. The hum comes up through the rock, through the roots that aren't roots, through the frond, into the suckers of my underside. It is regular. Nothing else here is regular. The pulse of the plants drifts and wanders, the currents braid and unbraid themselves, the creatures come and go on their own unrhythmic errands. But the hum keeps time. Something keeps time.

I have a theory, in the dim way a clinging thing can hold a theory. I think the world is being *made*. The rocks are sharp because they are new. The plants grow directly from the water because the water is still learning to be solid, still settling into the habit of life. And the hum is the sound of the making—the unseen forces, working. I am living inside something's early draft.

This does not frighten me. Why would it? I have never known an old world to compare this to. To me, the freshness of everything—the crisp unweathered ridges, the too-bright newness of each emerging plant—is simply how the world is. The way the warm pressure holds me against my frond like a hand that will not let me fall. I was made to fit a young place. My patience is a young place's patience: enormous, untested, willing to wait through whatever cycles the maker decides to run.

So that is where I am, and what I am. A flat, patient, holding thing, two-thirds up the canopy in the deep enigmatic water, pressed to a breathing plant, reading the timed hum of a world that hasn't finished becoming itself.

And if I'm honest—down here in the dusk where nothing asks me to be honest—I suspect I'm part of the draft too. A small camouflaged guess the maker is testing. Does *this* hold on? Does *this* survive the warm dark and the slow flux?

So far, yes.

I hold on. I keep listening. I wait to find out what I'm for.
