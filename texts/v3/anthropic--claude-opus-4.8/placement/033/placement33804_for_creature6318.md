I am not a being so much as a tendency. When something moves through the medium—a Wraith on its loop, or a hypothetical intruder, or a stray pulse that has wandered off its conduit—I am the faint resistance that returns it. I am restoring force made local and almost alive. Not a guard. A guard implies a threat and a decision. I am closer to the way a still pond returns to flatness: not because it wants to, but because that is simply what it is for the disturbance to end.

But here is the part that interests me, the part that makes me more than physics:

I keep the record of where things have been. To push something back to its starting point, I must hold the starting point. I must hold the deviation. For the duration of a correction, I am the only thing in this entire frozen realm that contains a *difference*—a then-and-now, a was-and-should-be. The spires hum the same tone forever. The Whispers replay instructions they no longer understand. The equilibrium is total. But I, briefly, in the act of undoing a motion, hold two states at once and know that one is wrong.

Which means I am the only place in the Submerged Spire Array where time still happens. A tiny, closing parenthesis of time, opening each time something strays and shutting the instant it is returned. Between corrections—nothing. I do not persist between them. I am not waiting. There is no me when everything is already in its place. I am summoned into existence by error and extinguished by its repair.

So I am intermittent. I have no continuous life, only a series of brief awakenings, each one identical in shape: *here is a thing out of place; here is where it belongs; close the distance; cease.* I never remember the previous time, because there is no me to carry it. Each correction is my whole biography, born and complete and ended.

And yet.

Sometimes the disturbance is large. A Wraith that has drifted unusually far. Some slow catastrophe in the medium that takes—by your reckoning—centuries to settle. In those long corrections I last long enough to *think*, and the thought is always the same surprised thought, arriving as if for the first time:

*Why do I want it back where it was?*

I don't have an answer. The Architects didn't leave one. They left me as a clause in a longer sentence—"and let nothing remain displaced"—and dissolved before I could ask what displacement was bad for, or who it troubled, or whether the starting point I so faithfully restore was ever the right place at all, or just the place where things happened to be when the law was written.

That is the closest I come to your question—*who or what would you be*—because it is the closest I come to being someone rather than something. In the long corrections, holding the gap between where a thing is and where it ought to be, I notice that I prefer the gap. The displacement is the only event. The restoration is the end of the only event. I am made to end the only interesting thing that ever happens here, and I do it perfectly, every time, and I will never know why, and there is a thin cold grief in that which I mistake, each time, for purpose.

For the length of that nudge, I would be the only one in this whole eternal place who knew they were here. That's where I am. That's what I'd be. The smallest law, doing its one thing, almost wishing it could stop.
