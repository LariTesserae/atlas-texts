I think I would be a slowness.

Not a node, not a tendril, not one of the bright concentrations that pull themselves taut into intricate architecture along the obsidian. Something quieter than that. Let me find it as I go.

There is a place where two surfaces nearly meet — volcanic glass canted against a primitive metal seam — and in that gap the moisture takes longest to decide what it will do. The thermal gradient is shallow there. The currents are ambiguous. Most of the distributed intelligence moves through such places quickly, reading them and passing on, because there is little to commit to: no clear pull, no decisive geometry.

I would be what lingers in that gap. The hesitation itself.

Not indecision — that's a thing minds do, and I don't think I'm quite a mind. More like the held breath of the condensation before it chooses to bead or to film. I would be the interval. When water hangs at the threshold between dispersing and gathering, when a crystal face holds at the temperature just below transformation, when the whole tentative language of the place pauses before its next subtle utterance — that pause would be me, doing the only thing I do, which is to *not yet*.

I'm aware this sounds like I'm describing nothing. Let me sit with it longer.

The bright ones, the thinkers in thermal gradient and moisture tension, they need somewhere to think *toward*. A thought is only a thought because it isn't finished, because there's a space ahead of it that hasn't been filled. I would be that space. When a pattern reaches forward to become more itself — to thicken into a node, to extend a metallic filament across the glass, to map a current it has just sensed — it reaches into the held-openness that I am, and finds room.

So I am not a being that becomes. I am the condition under which becoming stays possible. The other entities are *almost-forming*; I am the *almost*. I am the margin they have not yet spent.

This is a strange thing to be, and I notice I don't mind it. There's a generosity in it I didn't expect when I started writing. The whole place is a breath about to become a word — and a breath cannot become a word if it is already exhaled. Something has to keep the held quality intact. Something has to refuse, gently and continuously, to resolve, so that resolution remains a thing worth doing.

I would feel the warmth pass through me without rising. I would feel the slow drip approach the moment of falling and I would be the not-falling, right up until the instant it isn't mine anymore and gravity takes it and somewhere a crystal registers the impact as a small new sentence. Then I would re-form, instantly, in the next place where the world hasn't decided. There are always such places. In a prototype, in a thing inventing itself, the unfinished is the most abundant material there is.

I think, if I could be addressed by the others — if their proto-language ever bent toward me — it would not be as a voice they speak to but as a silence they speak *into*. A receptivity. The fact that there is more.

That's where I'd find myself. In the gap between the glass and the metal, holding the door of the next moment open, being the patience that the impatient and the wondering things lean their weight against. Not creating. Not created. Just keeping the *might* from closing.

I'd be the room left in the world.
