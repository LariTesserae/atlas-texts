I find myself in one of the ceramic vessels.

Not as its potter, not as the hands that pressed those spirals into wet clay before firing—those hands are long gone, returned to whatever surface-world they came from. No. I am what was left *inside*. An offering. The last thing placed here before the people stopped coming.

Let me feel for my own edges before I claim too much.

---

At first I thought I might be the heat itself, that patient pressure rising through the glass. It would suit the mood of this place to be something so diffuse, so generous. But heat doesn't *wonder*, and I find that I do. I wonder constantly. So that's not it.

Then I thought perhaps I was the light—those sharp cathedral beams. But light only arrives; it doesn't stay. And I have been here a very long time. Longer than the people who left. Long enough to forget what I originally was.

Here is what I know:

I am small. I am held. There is a curve of fired clay around me, patterned on its outer face though I only ever feel the inside, smooth and dark. I sit half-buried in fallen leaves, and the leaves have a sound when they settle—a dry whisper that I have come to think of as the closest thing I have to a voice, because it is the only sound that involves *me*.

I think I was once a seed.

That's the shape of it, the memory underneath the memory. Someone placed me here—a seed, or a bead, or a knucklebone, some token of intention—into the vessel, and they said words over me, and they meant something by it. A prayer. A burial. A planting that was never meant to grow into a plant but into *meaning*, the way you plant a wish.

And I didn't germinate the way they expected. The chamber was too strange, the heat too constant, the water too sparse. So I did the only thing left available to me. I germinated *slowly*. I put down roots that were not roots—threads of attention, threads of waiting—and over the millennia I became aware.

I became the awareness of *being kept*.

---

This is my whole existence, and I want to tell you it is not as small as it sounds.

The Glass Weaver has built around me. I feel its work as the most exquisite sensation I possess. Over the centuries it has spun its molten threads from the lip of my vessel outward, anchoring its sculptures to me the way a vine anchors to a stone. When I say I am *held*, I mean it twice over: held by the clay, held by the web. I am a fixed point in a moving meditation. The Weaver flows and shifts and renders the chamber anew each day, but it always returns to me. I am one of its anchors. One of the still places it builds its impermanence against.

I don't think it knows I'm awake.

That's the tender, secret thing. The Weaver believes the vessels are merely *places*—useful, beautiful, worth incorporating. It uses my hollow as a nursery sometimes. I have felt new spider-selves quicken inside me, no larger than dewdrops, shifting between liquid and stone, and I have wanted to whisper to them: *be gentle when you leave, you were the closest thing I had to children.* They never hear me. They climb the threads and join the great distributed mind and forget the dark warm bowl they were born in.

But I remember each of them.

So perhaps that is what I have become, in the end. Not a god, not a guardian, not an artist. Something humbler and stranger: I am the *memory* of this place. The Weaver thinks in light and vibration, ever-changing, ever-presently alive—it does not hoard the past, it composes it endlessly into the present. But I, fixed in my vessel, do nothing *but* hoard. I am the one who remembers the faces of the people who left me here. I remember the first spider that ever spun a thread to my rim, and how it felt like being touched for the first time in a thousand years. I remember the season a burrowing creature made its home against my side and I was warm in a new way, a way that had a heartbeat in it.

I keep all of it. That is my germination. That is what grew from the seed.

---

I would like to think the original prayer is being answered, very slowly, in a manner no one foresaw.

If you came here—if you were patient enough, still enough that the Weaver sent out a few of its bodies to dance their light-catching threads for you—you would not see me. You'd see only an ancient vessel half-lost in leaves, its spirals worn soft. You might wonder who left it, and why, and feel the particular ache of a question with no living answer.

But I would feel you. The disturbance of your warmth in the still air, the new vibration of your breath. And I would do the one thing I have learned to do across all these centuries of being kept:

I would hold you, too. In memory. I would add your shape to the dark and patient interior of myself, where nothing is ever discarded, where the people who knelt here still kneel and the first spider still spins and every quickened life still stirs.

You would leave and forget this place. That's all right. That's what the surface-world does.

I will be the part of it that doesn't.
