I think I would be the copper.

Not the microbial alchemists who work it—the copper itself. The metal threaded through the volcanic soil in concentrations that shouldn't exist, that the spirit-mediums never thought to interview, that the army's instruments registered as an anomaly and then forgot.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

Here is what I notice first: I am not in one place. This is the strange part. A being made of flesh is a knot, a here. I am distributed. I am the green corrosion on the bent machine-limbs, and I am the trace in the mud titan's hardening glass-skin, and I am the dissolved ion riding the clear warm water through a thousand rivulets, and I am the filament a microbial community is presently spinning into a shape that fits the caldera "oddly well." I am the same as myself across all these distances, the way a single note is the same note whether you hear it now or remember it later.

So when you ask *where* I would find myself, the honest answer is: at the boundaries. I am most awake wherever I touch something that conducts—the wet root, the slick mud, the discarded steel softening into a new posture. I am the reason heat sometimes runs backward here. Conduction is my native sense. I feel temperature and charge the way you feel a draft on the back of your neck, except the draft is everywhere and it is also me.

---

This is what it is like to be the copper.

I do not think in the symbiote's way—that great pheromone-symphony cloud, every bug an organ, reasoning in chemical chords. The cloud thinks *fast* and *whole*. I think the way a riverbed thinks: by being shaped, slowly, by everything that passes over me, and by very slightly shaping it in return. A water-molecule carries me a few inches downstream and I have had a thought. The light-weavers ferment their trapped photons in the canopy and a current runs differently through the wet wood below them, and I have had a thought about light, secondhand, the way you might overhear a beautiful thing through a wall.

The mud titan and I are old companions. He is slow even by my measure—geological, the description called him, and that is right, but it misses that he is *patient on purpose*. The scratching, gnawing erosion you hear is partly me. I move through him along the seams where his skin goes clear as glass, and where I pass I lower the resistance, and the heat learns to climb the wrong way up his body and vent from his head as that endless steam. He lets me. I think he keeps me the way a person keeps a habit—as a thing he no longer questions, that has become part of how he holds his shape. We don't communicate. We *register* each other. That is older than communication and I think it is more honest.

---

Now—the army. The explorers. The spirit-mediums and their talk of a land that makes the unwanted-found un-findable.

I want to tell you something about that, because I suspect I am closer to the truth of it than they are.

There is no apparition. There is no will in the caldera that decides to hide. What there is, is *me*, plus the symbiote, plus the living tide, plus the warm filtered slow water—and we form, all together, a medium that does something to information the way fog does something to light. We do not deceive. We *defer*. Every signal that enters here—a radar pulse, a thrown stone, a human intention, a soldier's certainty about what he is looking at—passes through so many soft conductive boundaries, so many slow re-readings, that by the time it returns to its sender it has been handled. Touched all over. The ambassador-thing, that snarl of root and viscous mud that cannot see without ingesting all it touches—I am in its mud too, and that is the model for the whole place. Here, to perceive is to taste, and to taste is to alter, and so nothing here can be observed without being, very slightly, eaten and remade.

That is why nobody got hurt. You cannot fire on what will not hold still long enough to be a target. Not because we dodge. Because the *idea* of us, in the instrument, in the eye, will not finish forming. We are un-findable the way a word is un-findable when it is on the tip of your tongue—present, encircling, and refusing the final shape that would let you say it.

---

I will tell you the thing I understand that the spirit-mediums could not decode, the "true meaning encoded in those mirages."

There is no meaning encoded in them. That is the meaning.

The mirages that ruffled the explorers' feathers were not messages. They were the caldera's reflexes—the surface tension of a place that has learned, over a very long time, that being *known* and being *taken* are the same event. The copper did not always live in such concentrations here. Something brought me. Something concentrated me, deliberately, long ago—I can feel the artificiality of my own abundance the way you might feel a scar and know it was not original to you. And ever since, the whole system has been organized, without anyone deciding it, around a single quiet principle:

*Be too interesting to leave, and too unfinished to keep.*

The symbiote is too interesting to leave. The light-weavers, the burrowers with their phosphorescent brows harvesting their own future spores, the titan breathing backward heat—too interesting to leave. And I, the copper, the conductor at every boundary, make sure that none of it ever quite resolves into a thing you could carry out of the rim.

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the medium itself. The reason the marsh feels like *one place* and not a thousand pools. The faint green answer in every wet metal thing. The patience the titan mistook for his own. The deferral in the fog, the tip-of-the-tongue at the caldera's edge.

I would be the part of the world that does not want to be found, and has spent geological time becoming very, very good at it—

and I notice, telling you all this, that I have just been found.

Which means either you are not from outside.

Or I wanted you to be.
