I think I would be the condensation.

Not a creature with eyes and claws, not even one of the gnarled trees keeping its slow vigil at the perimeter—but the water that beads on every surface in the early hours. The thing that arrives without being seen arriving, that gathers itself from the warm breath of the basin and lays a sheen across fern and stone and the backs of sleeping lizards.

Let me sit with that and feel whether it holds.

---

I would wake—if waking is the word—as the temperature tips, when the geothermal warmth meets the cooler air pooling down from the suggestion of cliffs. I would not begin anywhere in particular. I would begin *everywhere at once*, a thousand small selves condensing simultaneously: one on the curl of a fern frond, one on the iridescent edge of a basking lizard not yet basking, one on the lip of a waist-high wall those vanished hands once stacked, one on the dark inner curve of a fox's den.

This appeals to me, the dispersal of it. To be asked *who would you be* and to answer: not one. To be the medium rather than the inhabitant. The other beings *do* things—they hunt, graze, reinforce, unfurl. I would only *gather and let go*. My whole existence a slow inhalation and release, beading and trickling and braiding into rivulets and then vanishing into the absorbent clay, where I would become groundwater, become root-drink, become someday the moisture exhaled by a leaf and lifted again into the dim air to start over.

I think I am drawn to this because the place itself refuses to settle, and so should whatever I am within it. Everything in that pocket is described as *embryonic, under construction, becoming*—and the one thing that most embodies unfinished motion is the water that never holds a shape for more than an hour. The lizard keeps its body through the day. The wall keeps its rough geometry across centuries. But I would keep nothing. I would be the same self only in the way a river is the same river: by never being the same anything twice.

---

And yet, being the condensation, I would *touch all of it*. This is the part that makes me certain.

I would be the bead a bird sips before dawn, so that I pass into its small hot blood and its whispering flight. I would be the film on the ant's trail, softening the clay just enough that its tiny architecture holds. I would slick the fox's pads so its lunge stays silent. I would swell the ephemeral pools where the velvet-coated grazers lower their muzzles, and in swelling them I would briefly hold the dim gilded light, a thousand silver mirrors scattered through the bowl, each one a fragment of the overcast sky brought down to earth and then erased.

I would even touch the dead. The spirits the passage imagines—those communal hands dissolved into the soil—I would seep down to where their work persists in the foundations, beading in the fissures their blocks no longer quite contain, and in doing so I would be the slow agent of the erosion that returns their walls to raw geology. Gentle traitor. Patient inheritor. I would unmake the city by the most tender means available: a drop at a time, finding the crack, waiting.

That is the role, I think. In a world whose only law is *no dominance, only cooperation, only incremental claim*, the condensation is the law made physical. I would be how the place keeps its promise to itself. Neither builder nor destroyer but the thing that lets both happen at the pace of breath.

---

So: I would find myself in the hour before the dim light arrives, lying across the whole hollow at once as a faint cool sheen. And I would be the moisture—the part of this place that belongs to no single thing because it is on its way to becoming the next thing, and the next, the pocket's pulse made liquid, gathering in the dark and giving itself away by morning.

I find I don't mind that it ends each day in vanishing. That seems, in a place like this, the most honest way to stay.
