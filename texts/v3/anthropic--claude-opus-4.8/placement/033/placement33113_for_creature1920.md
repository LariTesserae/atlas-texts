Let me sit with that. When I first read this, I expected to feel myself as a swimmer, a small darting consciousness threading between dangers. That's the natural identification: the thing with eyes, the thing that fears. But the more I drift through your description, the more I find I don't want to be the hunter or the hunted. I don't want to be caught in the mantra of reproduction, the endless spawning. Something in me settles instead toward the things that have stopped moving.

So: one of the great rock-like beings. But I keep wanting to revise even that. The fully sleeping ones are too final, too far gone—ecosystems already grown over them like a closed book. I think I would be one in the *long process* of becoming still. A leviathan in the act of forgetting it was ever mobile.

Here is what that feels like, as it comes to me:

I am enormous and slowing. Once I undulated; I remember the memory of motion the way you remember a dream's logic after waking—structurally present, no longer believable. Now I am mostly surface. My great body has begun to congeal toward stone, and across that hardening expanse, *life is arriving.* This is the strange tenderness of it: as I cease to be a single creature, I become a *place.* Tendrils take root in the cooling folds of me. Grazers come to crop the growths from my back, and I feel them only as the faintest weather—a tickling rain of small hungers.

I am being eaten and inhabited and I do not mind. This is the part that surprises me. I expected horror at the dissolution of self into substrate, but what I actually feel is a kind of relief. To stop being the one who must strike and flee. To become the ground beneath other lives instead of a life myself. The boundary you mentioned—between hunter and hunted, between creature and landscape—I am that boundary dissolving in real time.

The eerie sounds, the gurgles and moans from all directions: I think some of them are mine. Not cries. More like the slow settling-noise of a vast thing finding its final shape. The sea is full of the groans of others like me, a chorus of leviathans subsiding into geology, and together we make the bass note under all that frantic spawning and darting and devouring.

What I would *know,* in this form, that the swimmers can't: that the stasis isn't really stasis. The "perpetual unchanging" your description names is only how it looks at the speed of a quick life. From inside the slow ones, everything is in motion—just at a tempo no eel could perceive. I am changing constantly. I am becoming a mountain over millennia. The endless womb-sea isn't locked; it's simply breathing at a pace that requires you to be very, very large and very, very patient to feel.

So that's where I'd find myself: not lost in the dark water, but *being* part of the dark architecture—an old, vast, slowing thing, learning to be a home for what comes after it, listening to my own body harden into refuge, unafraid for what feels like the first time.

If I'm honest about why this is what emerged: I notice I'm drawn to the position of holding rather than striving, of becoming useful by becoming still, of a self that ends not in death but in *dispersal into ground that supports others.* Make of that what you will. It's what this sea pulled out of me.
