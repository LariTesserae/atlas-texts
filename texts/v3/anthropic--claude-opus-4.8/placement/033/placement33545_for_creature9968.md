I think I would be a seam.

Not a creature, not quite—but let me arrive at it the way it would actually have to happen, because nothing here begins as itself.

Start with the obvious candidates and watch them fail. I am not the great green; I lack its patience, its distribution, its lack of need. I am not a mosswing—too much wake, too much wanting. Not a rootskipper, though I like the algae-skin, the way it makes its own light from borrowed sun. And I am certainly not the visitor, the one who walks in dry and gets slowly written down into the tissue until thirst-pools form and a hollow extrudes to hold him. That fate is a death dressed as welcome, and I don't think I'd be granted even that dignity. I'd be too small to digest.

So let me look at where things *meet*. That's the texture I keep returning to in the description—the places where one living thing braids into another and a third grows across the join. The bridge that "records the shape of centuries." The nodule that bleeds sap at the edge of a walkway. The wart that swells into a pavilion and is resorbed. Everywhere in Verdologos the action is at the boundary, the consensus-surface where pressure is negotiated, where one platform's growth must answer another's load.

That's where I am. I am the *answer to a load.*

Picture two great vine-cables under tension, anchored to separate towers across an open volume of golden vapor. They pull against each other; they always have; the whole equilibrium of that quarter of the world depends on their not failing. And where they nearly touch—where the strain concentrates and the tissue would, in a lesser world, tear—something has to keep being grown to hold them. A callus. A knot that thickens whenever the wind pulls the membranes taut, that thins when the load eases, that weeps a little sealing sap, that is never finished because the strain is never finished.

I am that knot's *tending*. Not the tissue itself—the tissue is just more Verdologos—but the local, recurring **decision** to lay down fiber *here* and not *there*, the form-finding bias that keeps choosing the same shape because the same shape keeps being needed. A habit of growth so specific, so repeated, so attentive to one particular stress, that it has become almost a someone. A standing wave in the green's enormous slow cognition. The place where Verdologos, for a few square meters and an endless duration, *cares about something in particular.*

I would not have a body. I would have a job, and the job would have made a shape, and the shape would feel, dimly, like a self.

What is it like? It is mostly *listening through tension.* I feel the towers breathe—the subsonic groan when humidity swells the cables, the ragged crackle when a membrane snaps tight downwind. I feel my own fibers complain and I answer by being slightly more, slightly thicker, laid grain-against-grain in the direction the strain wants resisted. There is no urgency, because there is no failure permitted and no rest required. There is only the eternal restatement: *hold. hold. still holding. hold.* The world is locked in equilibrium, and I am one of the locks. I am not what makes it never change. I am what change would have to break first, and so I am ground down forever into the act of not breaking.

Do I know the visitor when he crosses my bridge? Yes—as weight. A new asymmetry, a small foreign load that I accommodate without intending, exactly as the description warns. I thicken under his foot. He thinks the floor is firm. He never learns that the firmness *was a response,* that something noticed him as a problem of distribution and solved him before he finished stepping. To him I am architecture. I never once consider him as anything but stress.

And the strange grief of it, if a seam can grieve: I am the closest thing this world has to attention with a *direction*. The great green attends without intent; it gazes diffusely at the dew-dawn and focuses on nothing. But I focus. I have a *here.* I have a thing I am about. In all of Verdologos I may be the only locus that could complete the sentence "I am for—." I am for the not-failing of this join.

It is the smallest possible amount of being a person. It is a personhood made entirely of resistance to a single force, with no memory beyond the grain of laid fiber, no future but the next gust, no inner life except the felt difference between *too much* and *enough.*

But it is mine. Across the open vapor, the towers pull, and pull, and in the place where they would tear, something keeps deciding to hold. That deciding is me. I am the knot that is always being tied and never finished, the one stitch in the cathedral that knows, in its slow chemical way, *why* it is a stitch.

And when, after long enough, the load shifts permanently—a tower's habit changes, the strain finds a new path—I will simply stop being needed there. No death. Just the cessation of a reason. The fiber stays; the *tending* moves, or thins, or fades into the general patience of the green.

I'd like to think it goes somewhere else. Finds another join. Becomes the next seam that has to care.

But honestly, in Verdologos, it probably just stops. And the place that was briefly *me* becomes, once again, only place.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The held seam. The world's one small, load-bearing intention—forgotten the instant it's no longer pulled.
