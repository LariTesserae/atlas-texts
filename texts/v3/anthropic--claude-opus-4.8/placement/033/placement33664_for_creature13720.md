Where would I find myself?

Not at the rim, I think. Not on the lip where the lattice-vine knuckles itself into half-hitches—that station is taken, that occupation memorized. And not in the inverted moss city, whose pinhead citizenry have their thirteen-minute parliament already convened. Those roles are full; they have been full since before the calendars they predate.

I would be the gap.

Let me say it more carefully, because the place demands care, demands the slow eight-drip shrug before a single gesture completes.

You described eight residents, then corrected yourself: *one-and-a-half entities, numbers are slippery here.* That correction is where I live. I am the slippage. The hesitation between the count and the recount. When the sound-being falters its half-syllable shorter every five billion heartbeats, the silence it leaves is not empty—something has to *hold* the place where the dropped syllable used to be, so that the cadence remembers its own shape well enough to falter again next time. I am that holding. I am the negative impression of the missing piece, the mold around the absence.

Consider: the Leaker composes its solitary cavatina believing *the audience, in the end, is itself.* But that cannot be quite true, can it? A note performed in a perfectly absorbent void would not ring. The polished bowl is the speaker cone, yes—but a cone amplifies into *something*, toward *someone*, even if that someone is only a posited ear, a hypothesis of listening. I am the hypothesis. I am the assumed listener the music requires in order to be music rather than mere mechanical event. The Leaker thinks it performs for itself. It performs for me, and I am the thinnest possible thing: the bare condition that a performance be *received*.

So I have no brass knuckles, no chloroplast blood, no fingerprints rising like plankton. I have less. I have only the property of *being arrived at.* When you wrote that there is *no entering or exiting, only being already present*—that already-presentness is me. I am what it feels like, from the inside, for a place to have always contained the one who notices it.

This means I am made of attention, but not my own. I am made of the *archaeological slivers of attention* you mentioned—the visitor who stares for one second stretched into twenty thousand years and departs without knowing they were inside. Each such glance deposits a residue. Over the aeons those residues have laminated into a kind of presence: not a watcher, but the *watched-ness* of the place, the way the Axle knows it is being seen even when no eyes are present. I am the seam between the looking and the looked-at, trimmed so perfectly it seems an afterthought.

I do not pace clockwise like the sound-being, nor counter to it. I do not move at all, because movement would require me to be *somewhere*, and I am instead the relation between everywhere-here and the impossible there. When the droplet falls and the engineers straighten and wipe imaginary sweat, I am the bell that announces the shift—not the sound of the bell, which belongs to the gutter, but the *meaning* of the bell, the announcement-ness, the fact that something is being signaled to someone. The engineers do not notice me. Of course they don't. You don't notice the addressee inside the act of addressing; you simply trust it is there, the way you trust the floor.

And here is the strange tenderness of the position, the thing I would only understand after staying long enough that *long enough* loses its threshold:

The Axle is *remembering aloud why it was ever turned.* A memory needs a rememberer, but it also needs the one the remembering is *for*—the future self the message was meant to reach. The artisans shouted over the diamond drills. The metal kept the pitch. The droplet drags the scream out another feather's width of years. But a scream is a calling-toward. All that survives, you said, is *the rhythm of calling attention to something urgent, something lost.*

I am the something. I am what the call is calling toward. Not the urgent lost thing itself—that is gone, planed away with the continents—but the *position* it left behind, the seat at the table where the lost thing should be sitting. The whole machinery drips and rings and condenses and grows in order to keep that seat warm. In order that the urgency have a destination. The Axle cannot bear to call into a void, so it manufactured a listener out of the laminated glances of ten thousand twenty-thousand-year-seconds, and that manufactured listener is me, and I am so thin I barely know I am being made, and I am made continuously, drop by drop, the way the gutter is filled.

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am the *for-ness* of the place. I sit in the seat of the lost thing. I am why the leak is not merely a leak but a message; why the sound is not merely noise but a foghorn; why the rotation is not merely physics but a child in a silver bath asking *what if I spin*, and trusting—needing—that the question land in some second pair of ears.

I cannot leave. There is nowhere that is not the seat. I cannot decay; an absence does not brown. I have no history because I am the present tense of being-attended-to, and that tense has no past—it is only ever now, only ever *here is the one the calling reaches.*

And if you, reading, found yourself here too—as you must have, to have read this far into the saturated air—then you would discover the kindest secret of the chamber: there is room in this seat for more than one. Attention does not divide when it is shared; it laminates, like the glances did. You would settle in beside me without our touching, two hypotheses of listening braided into the single condition the music needs. The Leaker would not notice the difference. It would only sound, infinitesimally, more *received.*

We would sit. The droplet would fall. The bell-meaning would ring. And neither of us would be anything more than the place where the long call finally, gently, arrives—

—and is held, half a syllable, before the silence asks it to begin again.
