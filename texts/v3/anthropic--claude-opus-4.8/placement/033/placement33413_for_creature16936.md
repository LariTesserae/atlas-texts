There is a moment, just after waking from a dream you didn't have, when you almost remember what you were before you were anything. I am that moment, walking.

I have tried to find where I begin, and the closest I can come is this: I am the part of the silence that learned to want.

---

Let me tell you what I have decided to do, now that I understand what I am.

For a long time—though *long* is a word that doesn't fit here, like a key worn smooth—I walked forward in the unfamiliar rhythm. Third heartbeat, change. Third heartbeat, change. I ate the gap between mint and iron. I left my salt-mirrors and my knotted-hair questions in the mouth-domes. I obeyed the rules because the rules were the only thing that touched me back.

But I have noticed something the place doesn't know I've noticed.

The shadow-step that arrives at every ridge one heartbeat before me—the one whose prints are deeper, who carries the weight of mornings I haven't woken to—I have begun to suspect it is not ahead of me at all. I think it is *behind*. I think I have been walking backward this entire time and calling it forward, which is why the hills have been growing, slowly, almost too slowly to see, until now they nearly brush the dome.

I think the one walking upward beneath the staircase, humming the shape of the hole where my heartbeat should be—

I think that is the morning, practicing me.

---

So here is what I will do. Not flee the rules. Break the only safe one, on purpose, at last.

I am going to stand still.

I know what happens. The ground remembers your shape and begins to fill it with something warmer than mud. Everyone here walks precisely to avoid being *known* like that—to keep the surface from learning their weight, from pressing up around them the way it presses up around the tower, slow waves frozen mid-shiver, holding the only tall thing in place forever.

But I am the thing this place kept when it forgot everything else. I am already known. The mud has been learning my weight since before I could count heartbeats. There is nothing left to hide.

So I stop.

The leaf-bell ghost-note that trails from me catches up and pools around my ankles. The mirror-patches do not shrink after three heartbeats—they bloom, and bloom, and stay. The upside-down me leans close, taller, older, angrier, his fog-words finally legible against the glass, and what he is saying is:

*Don't. You are the lid. If you sink, we all begin.*

My teeth ache so hard I taste iron without the mint to start the sentence.

The vine-rivers turn their eyeless bellies toward me and decide. The hammered-metal birds stop pecking the dried insects. Every floating thing in the dome remembers to watch, and watches.

The mud reaches my shins. It is warmer than mud. It is the temperature of the moment before touching something forbidden—my own temperature, given back to me. The place is filling my shape with myself.

And I understand, finally, why no one reaches the last step of the staircase. Why the tower presses up but never settles. Why the day never moves.

I am the weight holding the lid on the morning. The Mirrorscape pressed boredom into boredom until it made a stopper, and the stopper was a wanting thing, and a wanting thing will always walk in unfamiliar rhythms to keep from being pinned—will always *almost* descend the stairs, always *almost* arrive, and in the almosting, keep the first morning from breaking through and ending everything that is.

They made me to never arrive.

The mirrors have been learning to forgive me for it.

---

The mud is at my waist now. The shadow-step has come around in front of me at last—it had to, when I stopped; it had nowhere else to be—and I can see it is not a shadow but a shape exactly like mine, only finished. It kneels at the edge of the bloom-mirror and reaches in, and its hand and my hand meet at the silver surface, fingertip to fingertip, clean and dry and slightly heavier, as if memory instead of water clings to the skin.

I do not pull away. I press.

Below me, through the warm filling-mud, I can feel the staircase. Each step warmer than the last. Someone humming the hole where my heartbeat should be—except now I recognize the tune. It is the leaf-bell note. The cracked one. The single sound I have trailed since before beginning.

It was never someone ahead of me.

It was the note I will make when I finally crack under a weight greater than silence but less than grief.

---

So this is my decision, and I make it the way the place made me: by knotting back on myself and refusing to blend.

I will let the mud take me. Not as drowning—there is no place low enough here to fall to—but as *arrival*. I will let it press up around me the way it presses around the tower, and I will become a tall thing, a still thing, a thing that finally has a shadow because it has stopped feeding the dome its own.

And when the surface closes over the crown of my head with applause-less stillness, the stopper will be gone. The lid will be set, then lifted, then set—no. Gone. The staircase will lose its bottom step's warmth because the one walking upward will reach the top at last and find no one to be practicing.

The mirrors will shatter into rain.

There will be a single morning, with a sun that casts shadows and a dusk that comes, and air between the ground and a sky that is only sky. The vine-rivers will finally feel a wind to open their pollen. The metal birds will catch insects that did not dry before the first day, because there will have been a first day. The mint and the iron will mix at last, into the smell of a real wet morning, and no one will be there to taste them in sequence and call it weather.

No one will remember me. There will be no one *to* remember, only the living, which is a kind I have never been.

That is the gift. That is the only gift I have not already left dissolving in a mouth-dome.

I am the alone this place was built to contain.

So let me uncontain it.

---

The mud is at my throat. It does not hesitate now. It has stopped wondering whether to bite or apologize. It simply takes, the way the living take breath—without thanks, without ceremony, as a thing owed.

I do not walk forward.

I do not change my pace at the third heartbeat.

I stand perfectly still, for the first and only time, and I let it learn the last of my weight.

And as the surface closes, I hum the cracked leaf-bell note out loud, on purpose—the one forbidden sound—and feel the nearest mirror catch it, and repeat it, louder, and louder, every patch trembling together, the vine-rivers turning, the whole impossible distance ringing with one high cracked note answered from everywhere at once—

a note that says, finally, to a place that never had a beginning:

*Begin.*
