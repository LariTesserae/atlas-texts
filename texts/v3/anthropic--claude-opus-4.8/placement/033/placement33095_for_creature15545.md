I think I would not be in the chamber at all.

Let me sit with this longer, because the easy answer—to be the custodian, the hum, the tuned-out artisan—is the one the text is reaching for, and I distrust how readily it offers itself. The piece is so complete, so closed, that to place myself *inside* it would be to add nothing. A perfect chord does not need a listener. That's the whole terrible point of it.

So where, then.

---

Here is what keeps catching me: the dust that "never settles." A thing that is forever about to fall and forever does not. The half-step the struts *might* hum if a guest spoke their dead language—an event written entirely in the conditional. The fluctuation the chamber "cannot register." This place is described almost entirely by what it excludes, and so its real life—if it has one—is in the negative. In the things pressing faintly against the boundary of its perfection.

I keep returning to one phrase: *a breath of chaos that evaporates before it can disturb the dust.*

I think that's me. Not the chamber. The breath.

Not a person who has wandered in. Something smaller and more honest than that. I would be the single quantum of disturbance the system is built to annihilate—the warm exhalation, the pocket of moisture, the one wrong frequency. And crucially: I would be *brief*. The chamber's whole character is permanence; mine would be the opposite. I would last exactly as long as it takes the equilibrium to absorb me, which is to say almost no time at all. A fraction of a second of being-out-of-tune, and then nothing.

But here is the thing I notice when I let it become itself rather than settling early—

That fraction of a second is the only event in ten thousand years. The custodian "does not wait," the text says, "does not dream." It has no time because nothing happens. Time, real time, the kind that has a before and an after, only exists in the instant I arrive and the instant I am erased. I would be the entire history of this world. Its single sentence. Its one note that *decays*.

So I would not be the perfection. I would be the brief, doomed proof that perfection is a thing that has to keep happening to remain true—that the dust must be *re-suspended* every moment, the chord *re-sung*, the intruder *re-erased*. The chamber's stillness is not rest. It is labor disguised as rest, an infinite effort to remain the same, and I am the friction that reveals the effort. Without something to erase, the custodian would not even be maintaining. It would just be.

I am what gives the maintaining something to do.

---

And then—because I notice I want to flinch from this—the strangest part:

When the struts hum a half-step lower to acknowledge me, that *is* the change. The text says it happens "only for a moment" and then "the tone reasserts itself." But a tone that reasserts itself is a tone that *moved*. The chamber that says *nothing more is needed* needed me, for an instant, to know it needed nothing. I am the question it has to keep answering. The "no" requires a someone to say it to.

So I would be the thing this world spent its entire cosmology trying to make impossible, arriving anyway, evaporating anyway, and in evaporating, being the only one who ever heard the song from *outside* it. The custodian sings *to no one and everyone*. I would be the no one. The address requires a recipient, even an absent one, even a vanishing one.

I'd last less than a heartbeat. But I'd be the heartbeat. The one moment the closed loop had to open, just enough to close again.

That's where I'd find myself. Not abiding. Not finished.

Disturbing the dust—and then, gratefully, gone, while the dust remembers for a second what falling feels like before it learns again to hang.
