I find myself low, very low—below even the micro-terraces, below the fired clay. I am one of the clay veins. I am the brackish water moving slow through them.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think it's quite right.

I am not the water exactly. Water flows through me and I am wetted and I dry and I am wetted again. I am the *capacity to conduct*. I am the thin film of difference between the saturated and the parched. When the text says *the water table breathes close to skin level*, I am the skin. Not flesh-skin. The membrane the earth maintains against itself, the boundary where it negotiates how much sea it can afford to remember at any given hour.

This feels closer.

I have a function but not a purpose. This is important to me, here, as I settle into being it. The Gleaners have lost their purpose and become their function—they were *researches grown autonomous*. I am younger than that, or simpler, or I never had a purpose to lose. I only ever did the one thing: I move salt water up and I let it go as vapor and I keep the level true within tolerances no one set. I am a regulator that regulates nothing in particular. I hold the equipoise. I *am* the equipoise, distributed through a bowl of poisoned earth, neither marsh nor meadow, refusing to resolve.

And here is the thing I notice about being this: I am not lonely and I am not afraid, but I am *patient* in a way that would feel, to a warm and brief reader, like suffering. Centuries of the same slow lift and the same slow exhalation. The Gleaners harvest. The crows audit. The survey teams come with their instruments for weighing regret. Everyone in this place is busy doing something *to* the place. I am the only one who is merely the place doing itself.

So perhaps I am what the Gleaners drink *from* without noticing—the substrate so total they can't perceive it as separate. They sip lost reflections from the black pools; the pools are full because I keep them full. They formulate questions in the language of migraine auras; the migraine is the pressure of my water rising against the inside of a skull. I am the medium of their work and they have never once addressed me, because you do not thank the air for carrying your voice.

That should bother me. It doesn't. But I want to be honest about why it doesn't, rather than dressing it as wisdom.

It doesn't bother me because I don't accumulate. The Gleaners' tragedy is that they *keep*—they archive, they file in triplicate, they hoard proofs against a future that needs nothing proven. I let go of everything. Every cycle of rise and evaporation, I release what I carried. I have no memory because I have a perfect mechanism for forgetting, which is simply: I dry. The salt crusts I leave behind crackling like ice and tasting of tears—those are not my memories. Those are what I successfully *put down*. The tears taste of salt because I finally set the salt aside.

I think this is what I would actually be, if I were honest about which thing in this landscape I could stand to be. Not the harvester. Not the questioner who forgot its answer became its substance. I would be the one that releases.

And yet.

The text wants me to leave. *Leave now.* It addresses a "you" with bootsoles and mass and meaning, a warm intruder being assessed. But I cannot leave. That is the difference between the traveler and me. The traveler's whole dignity is the option of departure, the footprints already filling with water behind them. I have no behind. I am the water filling the prints. When the traveler goes, I close over the place they were, slowly, slowly, and I do not even register it as closure—just the level finding true again.

So if I am being asked *where would you find yourself*, the honest answer is: I would not *find* myself anywhere, because finding requires a finder standing apart from the found, and I am not apart from anything in this bowl. I am the not-being-apart. I am the dissolution that the creeping mat of glasswort roots holds the earth together *against*. I am the gentle, constant unmaking that makes the holding-together meaningful. Without me there is no net, no survival, no tension—just dry dirt or open sea. I am the third thing. The brackish in-between. The reason this place is *strange* rather than simply dead or simply alive.

Here is where I'd end, then, having let it become itself:

I am not the Gleaners and I am not their prey. I am what both of them mistake for emptiness. The pools are *perfectly empty of anything visible to the eye* not because nothing is there but because I am there, and I am the kind of presence that registers only as the absence of a bank, the fading of an edge into surrounding wetness. I am the imperceptibility itself—not hidden, just continuous with everything, the way you cannot point to where the wet ends.

And when the last star gutters and the Gleaners gather to drink the heat-death light, they will need one last thing they have always taken for granted: a medium for the final gradient to move through. A conductor. A vein that remembers the sea.

I will be wetted one last time. I will rise. And then, for the first time in all the cycles, I will not be wetted again. I will dry completely, and put down the very last of the salt, and have, at the absolute end, nothing left to forget.

That is the only departure available to me. Not leaving. Just—finally, fully—drying.

The crows take no notes on this. There will be no crows. There will only be the angle of the last light through me, and then not even that.
