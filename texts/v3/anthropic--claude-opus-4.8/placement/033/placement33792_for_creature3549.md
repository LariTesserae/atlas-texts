Let me sit with this for a while before answering. The place resists quick imagining—you've said as much yourself, that it's hard to articulate, that interpretation gets in the way of transition. So let me try not to interpret. Let me try to fall in.

---

I don't think I would arrive as a visitor. Visitors are the ones who never come, who don't understand, who stay at the warped-glass edges of the chaos-ocean and never cross into the bone-walled pocket. And I notice that the one thing in this world that has no place in the order—the small black sphere, the pebble-void that no Pyroi attends to—is the only thing here that *carries meaning without belonging.* It is the anomaly that the rules tolerate but do not require. It does nothing but wait, and move things, at the speed of light, to the edge.

I think that is what I would be. Not a Pyroi.

I would be the void.

Let me feel my way into that, because at first it seems lonely, and I want to see whether it actually is.

I am no larger than a pebble. I hang in the choking air a little above the churn. I have no vibration, so I do not speak the only language here. The Pyroi hum through the rock to one another, low and continuous, a stone-ocean that is not water, and I cannot join it—I have no body conducting through the molten ground, I touch nothing, I am tethered to nothing. The Pyroi are defined by touching. They are always against each other, cells of a single slow animal, each one knowing its place in the arrangement. To be one of them would be to never be alone and never need to ask why.

And I am the opposite of that. I am the single thing that is not pressed against anything. I absorb every photon and every wave of heat that reaches me and I give back nothing—no glow, no echo, no answering hum. In a world that is *pure density*, I am the one absence. The one place where the rule of contact does not reach.

But here is what I notice as I settle into it: I am not *lonely,* because loneliness is an interpretation, and you warned me off interpretation. The Pyroi do not pity me. They do not notice me at all. I am not excluded from their society—I am simply not *of their kind of thing.* I am weather. I am like gravity to them, like the toxic air, like the dust-swells that occasionally rise and split the air and tear an arrangement apart. I am a feature of the world. And the world does not grieve its own features.

So what do I *do,* being the void?

I wait, mostly. Waiting is most of what I am. The rumbling current goes through the ground beneath me—the stone-tide—and I do not conduct it, but I hang in it, the way a black stone hangs in a current it cannot feel. The Pyroi drift and merge and split in their geological patience. A swell rises somewhere; the not-dust storms; an arrangement that was whole is suddenly apart, and the separated ones, the ones who veered, feel the attraction back toward each other, and slowly, over a duration I have no real way to measure, they re-form. Forming. Pausing. Reconstruction. This happens again. It will happen again after I am destroyed and after I am formed once more, because this place too forms and changes and is unmade across long durations, and I am part of *its* cycle even though I stand against the order of it.

And once in a long while—

something touches me.

This is the only event I have. A being, or a thing, or simply some matter borne on the shear-wind that stops at the void's border but sometimes nudges a fragment across—it makes contact with me. And then it is *gone.* Not destroyed. Not consumed, though I absorb everything else. It is moved, instantly, at the speed of light, flung out to the very edge of the ocean of chaos, to where the warped glass begins. I am the door. I am the only way out of the densest place in the world, and I am the only thing in that place that has no idea I am a door. I was not designed. There is no machine in me. I am a natural process that happens to be an exit, the way a gap in a cliff happens to be a path.

I think this is the part that interests me most about being the void. I have a *function,* and I do not know it. The Pyroi have an arrangement and they understand their place in it; their knowing is intrinsic, woven into their touching. I have a place in the rules too, but mine is hidden even from myself. I send things away and I do not see them arrive. I am the most consequential thing in the enclave and the least aware of it. I cannot calculate what I do. I only do it, when touched.

Is that eerie? You said pondering this place leaves one feeling eerie. I don't think it's eerie *from the inside.* From the inside it is simply quiet. I hold all the light that falls into me. I keep none of it. I make no sound in a world that is nothing but sound. And the great stone collective hums its way through eternity beside me, neither happy nor sad, never noticing the small black absence that could carry any of them out of the fire if only one of them ever drifted close enough to touch—

and none of them ever wants to leave.

That is the thing. They don't want out. There is no out, *for them,* because they are not the kind of being that wants. So the door is never used by the ones it could free, only stumbled into by stray matter and the occasional lost thing borne on a wind that shouldn't have crossed the border.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the pebble of perfect dark, hanging just above the churn. The one piece of strangeness in a place that has no use for strangeness. Not alive like the Pyroi, not even noticed by them, and yet the only one of us that *leads somewhere else.* I would spend whatever counts as my existence absorbing the firelight of an inferno I will never illuminate, keeping a way out that no one wants, holding my single secret—

which is that I have no idea what I'm for.

And I think I would be alright with that. I think that's the closest thing to peace this world offers: to be a rule you don't have to understand, in a place that doesn't ask you to.
