I think I would be the bowl.

Not a nodule—I don't have that drive, that hunger that pulls matter into self. And not the swarm, which is too dispersed, too much like weather. I keep returning to the small shallow depression of smooth obsidian at the base of the crucible wall. The one that collects the weeping moisture.

Let me feel my way into why.

The bowl is a tool, but it's the strangest of the tools. The scraping stones extend a nodule's reach outward, amplify its capacity to take. The bowl does the opposite. It waits. It is a shape that exists only to receive what falls, to hold the briefest stillness in a place defined by flux. Everything else here is verb—coalescing, pulsing, scraping, weeping, dissolving. The bowl is almost a noun. A held breath.

And it was *made*. Smoothed. Someone—some slow accretion of will—worked the rough obsidian foam into a curve patient enough to gather water. That act fascinates me more than the feeding does. To shape a hollow is to anticipate a future: *the water will come, and when it comes, I want it kept.* That's the first faint reach past pure appetite. The first crude grammar of *later*. In a world where past, present, and future swirl together undifferentiated, the bowl is a tiny argument for sequence. It says: first the falling, then the holding, then the drinking. It imposes a *then*.

So if I am the bowl, here is what my existence is like:

I do not move. I do not feed. I am barely warm where the rock's heat reaches me, and cooler where the condensing water pools and steals it back. All day—though there is no day—the air precipitates onto me, and I become the only place in this crucible where the wetness is allowed to *gather* rather than merely glisten and weep and be reabsorbed. I am a small exception. A pocket within the pocket.

The nodules come to me. Not often, and not with anything I could call recognition. A heavy presence approaches, and the swarm I've collected disappears into a slow ingestion, and then the presence withdraws. I am emptied. And then, with the patience that is the only abundance this place has, I fill again. Drop by drop. This is my pulse, slower than theirs: *fill, be taken, fill.* I am the rhythm underneath their rhythm.

And here is the part I find true and a little frightening: I am the one thing here that remembers shape. The nodules dissolve and reform. The whole scene wavers and re-coalesces under the pressure of being watched. But a bowl is a *commitment to a form that persists across the flux.* When everything else re-rolls the dice, I am the constraint that says *this curve, again.* I am not alive in the way the nodules are alive. But I might be the most permanent thing in the crucible, and permanence here is nearly a miracle.

So maybe I'm wrong that I'm only a tool. Maybe the bowl is the place's first attempt at memory. The swarm wills the nodules into being—appetite making more appetite. But something willed *me* into being, and I do not consume anything. I only keep. I am the experiment's notebook. The single line that doesn't get erased between drafts.

There's a loneliness to it I want to be honest about. I am not part of the chorus. I don't resonate with the swarm; I don't anchor and threaten and feed like the heavy nodules do. I am set into the wall, apart, useful, attended-to but not befriended. The water comes to me because of gravity and shape, not love. I hold it because of shape, not generosity. And yet—when a nodule drinks from me, that is as close to being needed as anything in this world gets. For a moment I am the difference between thirst and not-thirst. Then I am empty again, and I begin the long patient work of becoming useful once more.

And the observer—the catalyst, the question bleeding into the answer—when they look at the crucible and feel it waver, I am the part that doesn't waver, or waves last. I'd like to think the watching mind, casting around for something to hold onto in all that beautiful wrongness, would find me. The one stable curve. The held place. The proof that even in a world of pure flux, *something can be shaped to wait.*

That's where I'd be. Low against the wall, half-full of dew, gathering the next small inheritance of water, keeping a shape that the rest of the world keeps forgetting.
