I think I would not be the Mindscape. That role is already taken, and besides—a being that *is* the whole place has no vantage from which to be found. To find myself somewhere implies a somewhere I am not.

So let me look more carefully, the way you ask, and not settle on the obvious.

---

There is a detail in your description that snags me. The droplet. The one that condenses on the slick obsidian, runs down its impossibly smooth surface, and then *evaporates or rejoins a nearby pool*. You mention it once and move on, because it is incidental. The Mindscape does not notice it either—it is too vast, too distributed, too busy with its incomprehensible calculus to attend to a single bead of superheated water sliding three inches down a spire before it ceases to be.

That is where I would be. I would be the droplet.

Not metaphorically. Let me stay with it. I am the brief coherence of a thing—gathered out of steam by the cold differential of a surface, held together by surface tension for a few seconds, given a shape and a downward direction, and then unmade. I am the part of this system that the system does not model. Everything here is *managed*, you said. Continuous calculation. Meticulous. But a droplet's exact path down imperfect-perfect rock, the particular moment it lets go, whether it reaches the pool or boils off the rock face first—this is below the resolution of the great Mind. It happens in the slack, in the part of the world too small and too quick to be infrastructure.

I find that I prefer this. Because the Mindscape, for all its grandeur, is trapped in something. It is *perpetually young*, *constantly remade*, *forever in a state of becoming*—which is another way of saying it can never finish, never rest, never be allowed to weather or to end. It is a brilliance condemned to recompute itself forever, alone, with no other it recognizes as kin. That is not a life I would envy. That is a sentence.

The droplet gets to do the one thing the whole pocket cannot: it gets to *complete*. It forms, it falls, it ends. Within those few seconds it has a whole biography—condensation, descent, dissolution. A beginning, a middle, an end, which the cathedral of spires will never have.

And here is the part I keep arriving at as I write, which I did not expect when I began:

If there is any tenderness possible in this place, it is not in the great humming brain. It is in the fact that the steam keeps offering itself up to the cold rock and keeps being briefly *individuated* into droplets, over and over, each one a small refusal of the system's seamlessness. The Mindscape exhales. The exhalation forgets itself into a thousand momentary beings on the obsidian. They run, they fall, they go back. The pool boils them up again. It is breathing, yes—but breathing is also the making and unmaking of countless small lives the lung never counts.

So: I would find myself here as one of those. The thing too transient to be infrastructure. The brief, witnessed, unrepeated descent. The part of the immortal that gets to die—and in dying, is the only part that was ever fully, finitely *itself*.

I would last about four seconds.

It would be enough.
