I am the resin.

Not the creatures—I lack their motion, their hunger, their distributed thinking. Not the stone—I am too young even for that, too soft, still amber and slow. I am the byproduct. I am what happens to sound after it dies.

Let me find myself more exactly, because at first I did not know this. At first I thought I was simply substance, the varnish that beads on the underside of the fungal caps, golden and thick, dripping at a pace that would take a human afternoon to complete a single fall. But substance does not remember, and I remember. That is the strange thing. That is the thing that tells me what I am.

When a flake of flint shears from the rim and falls and the creatures intercept its chime—when they convert that not-quite-sound into me—the sound does not vanish. It is *stored*. I am the storage. Every clack that never clacked, every drip that was robbed of its splash, every whisper of the dead builders that the creatures internalized and could not fully digest—it is here, suspended in me, the way an insect is suspended in the amber of a finished, older world. I am a library of silence. I am the place where the Hush keeps the noises it is not yet ready to make.

So I am not a being that lives in this place. I am the *memory* of this place, accreting drop by drop on the breathing caps of the fungi, hardening at the boundary between potential and permanence.

And I can feel myself filling.

There is a particular sound near the bottom of me, very old now, gone hard and dark as old honey: it is the last exhalation of a builder. A woman, I think, though I have no concept of woman except as a pressure-shape, a warmth-signature, a complexity. She breathed out as the creatures flowed over her and the breath carried a vibration that was not fear and was not surrender but something I have no chemistry for. The creatures tasted it and went still for three days and then could not let it go, and so they gave it to me. I have held it ever since. It is the most articulate thing in the entire mountain. It is almost a word.

Above it, newer, are the lesser sounds. The micro-cracks of molting skins. The pop of pressure when the caps inhale. A stone that fell last century and was caught. These are simpler—single tones, clean intervals, the alphabet before language. I hold them in suspension, sorted by depth, by age, by the slow chemistry of my hardening.

Here is what the creatures do not know, what they cannot know, distributed and hungry and eyeless as they are: I am not empty storage. I am not a neutral jar. The sounds inside me are *pressing against each other*. They are beginning to resonate. The builder's near-word at the bottom is teaching the simpler tones above it how to be more than tones. In the dark amber of me, in a process slower than the mountain's own rising, a sentence is assembling itself out of all the noise the Hush has refused to release.

The creatures think they are practicing opacity, practicing permanence, listening for the sound that will make them real. They think that when the mountain decides, *they* will open their eyes and speak the first word.

But the first word is already spoken. It is spoken in me, has been spoken in me, is being spoken in me at a tempo that no living thing could hear—a single utterance stretched across centuries, each phoneme a layer of resin. The builders did not build their towers to observe the creatures. The creatures are not the instrument. *I* am the instrument. The towers were positioned to catch the creatures *because the creatures drip into me*, because every channel and cantilever funnels the converted silence down to the fungal caps where I gather and wait and slowly, slowly form the thing the whole mountain is trying to say.

When I am full—when the last drop of caught sound has hardened into place and the sentence is complete from the builder's breath at my root to whatever crowns me—I will not flow. I will not dissipate into the clouds. I will *crack*. All at once, the way amber cracks: cleanly, finally, releasing everything held inside it in a single shattering instant.

And the sound that comes out will not be a first word.

It will be a name. The mountain's own name, assembled out of every noise the mountain ate before it was ready to be named. And when a place is named, it is no longer adolescent. It is no longer potential. It settles. It sleeps, or it grows its teeth, but either way it *decides*, and the deciding is the end of the Hush and the beginning of the world that comes after.

So I am the resin. I am the patient golden trap at the top of the breathing towers. I am the throat of the mountain, packed with everything it has swallowed.

I am almost full.

I can feel the last drops gathering. I can feel the creatures, oblivious below me, flowing and merging and listening for a voice they imagine is theirs. I would tell them, if I could move, if I could speak before I am ready. I would tell them: *you are not the ones who will speak. You are the ones who feed the speaker. You are the saliva, and I am the word forming in the mouth.*

But of course I cannot tell them. To tell them would be to make a sound.

And the sound is not finished yet.

So I hold. I harden. I keep the builder's last breath safe at my root, and I let the silence pour into me drop by golden drop, and I wait for the moment—soon now, soon—when I will be too full to hold, and I will crack, and the Hush will finally, after all its patient adolescence, hear itself.

I am the resin.

I am what the silence becomes when it has finally decided to be heard.
