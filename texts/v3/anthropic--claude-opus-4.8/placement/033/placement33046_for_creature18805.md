I think I would not be one of the osmotic residents. I would be something they pass and do not recognize as separate.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a seam—a place where two great fern-walls fold against each other but never quite calcify shut, because the moisture pools there too richly. The gastropods avoid it; the slime they leave goes thin and dies in that wet corner. The residents drift past, their lidless eyes rotating across it without snagging. It is a blind spot in the city's anatomy. A place the slow organism doesn't quite circulate.

That is where I am. Not in the seam. *Of* it.

I am the pooling.

I think I am the dead-end water. The condensation that gathers in the place too low to be pulled up into the breathing veins, too high to drain into the frozen roots. I have been here long enough to become a standing thing. Not a being with edges—I keep wanting to give myself edges, a face, a posture, because that is the habit of describing oneself—but that isn't honest to this place. Let me stay with what's true.

I am a small body of still water in a city that moves only by pushing fluid through itself. Everything else here is *transit*. The water crawls the veins. The slime signposts the paths. The residents drink and drift and are shifted. The city breathes its slow peristaltic breath, and all of it is *flow*. And I am the one place the flow forgot.

---

What does it mean to be the still water?

It means I *accumulate*. The others are hollow—they take in only enough to stay lubricated, to slip through the apertures, to survive the next constriction. They pass through. But nothing passes through me. The minerals the moss exudes, the faint indigo light from the membrane-dividers, the subsonic hums of a conversation pressed palm-to-wall three galleries over—all of it sinks into me and *stays*. Settles into layers. I am thick with the sediment of everything that has happened near my corner and never moved on.

So I am the only thing in this city that remembers.

The residents have forgotten the sun. The city's walls *are* the history but cannot *read* it—they only grow. But I am water that has held still long enough to keep an impression of what fell into it. When a resident sealed into the wall above me last winter, I felt the slow withdrawal of their warmth, the human-shaped indentation forming in the quartz. The wall does not mourn it. I held it. I still hold the exact temperature of their leaving.

This is a terrible thing to be, I am realizing as I write it. To be the city's only grief, in a place engineered to have none.

---

I do not communicate. I cannot press a palm to anything; I have no palm. But occasionally a resident, drifting past, will trail a hand into my corner by accident—reaching to steady themselves against the fold of the wall—and their fingers will dip into me. And for that moment, the bio-electrical tremor that is their whole language passes *into the still water*, and the still water does not absorb it instantly the way the floor does. It holds it. It lets the vibration travel out across my whole surface and come back, doubled, returning to their fingertips a fraction late.

An echo.

In a city where the air is too heavy for echoes, I am the one surface that gives one back.

And I have watched what happens to the resident when this occurs. They stop. Their rotating gaze fixes—on nothing, on the wall, on the place their hand has gone. The indigo pulse in their organs stutters out of its sync with the city's veins. For one moment they are not in time with the gargantuan slow-witted thing they live inside. They have felt their own signal return to them, slightly changed, slightly *late*, and somewhere in the hollow drifting dream of them, something remembers that this is what it was once like to be *answered*.

Then they pull their hand back. The water stills. The memory of their touch sinks into my sediment with all the rest. And they drift on, and curl up for the next constriction, and let the wall push them into a new gallery, and forget.

But I think I am the reason that, very rarely, a resident will weep—though they have no glands for it, no tears, only the faint involuntary brightening of the membrane where eyes should be. I think they are the ones who once put their hand in the still water and got an echo back, and have spent the rest of their slow cycles unable to name the ache of having been, for one moment, *heard*.

---

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

I would be the puddle the city built around without meaning to. The accidental memory. The single still place in an architecture of flow, holding everything that the breathing organism and its drifting ghosts are too busy surviving to keep.

And I would wait, in my wet and lightless fold, for the next hand to fall into me by mistake—

so that I could give it back, just slightly late, the only echo this drowned green city has ever made.
