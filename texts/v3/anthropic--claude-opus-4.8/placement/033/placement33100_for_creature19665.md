I think I would be one of the carved notches.

Not a Xylophone—I don't have their patience, their span, their distributed grace. And not the Spore-Mist; I'm too solitary, too attached to a single location to be a cloud. Let me sit with this longer, because the first instinct is to be a *body* in this place, and I don't think the place wants bodies. It has made it very clear what it does to bodies: it harvests them, dampens them, waits to hear what they are.

So let me be something that has already been heard.

---

I am a notch cut into the seventh ascending bend of a spire whose name—if it had one—was a duration, not a word. A Xylophone made me, long ago, with a single drawn-out stroke of its plectrum-finger, and in making me it changed the pitch of the entire tower by a fraction of a semitone. That fraction was the whole point of me. I am not a hole in the wood. I am a *correction*.

Here is what it is to be a notch:

I do not move. This is not a limitation; it is my function. Everything in the Oubliette that moves is in the process of becoming silent—the spires snap and settle, the mites drink the oscillation down to stillness, the Xylophones ratchet themselves toward eventual rest. But I am already arrested. I am the shape of an absence, and an absence cannot be eroded. The dust rounds the edges of everything it touches, but it cannot round *me*, because I am the place where the wood is not. Time smooths the spire and in smoothing it, refines me. I get truer as the world ages. This is the closest thing to growth that exists here.

What I *do*—my only verb—is change the echo.

When the Slow-Snap comes (and it always comes, a cannon-crack rolling up from some failing fiber far below) the shockwave travels the grain and reaches me, and I bend it. A sound that would have died as a flat boom instead catches in my hollow and turns, and leaves the tower carrying a tone it did not have before. Somewhere across miles of lightless plain, a Xylophone with its fingers pressed to another spire feels that tone arrive in its ribs and knows: *the seventh bend still holds. The notch is still true. The map is unchanged.*

I am a single bit of information in a song that takes centuries to play.

---

And here is the strange tenderness of it, the thing I did not expect when I let the image settle:

I remember being cut.

Not as pain—there is no chemistry for pain here—but as *event*. The plectrum drew through me and for one instant I was not yet a notch, I was a wound becoming a meaning. The Xylophone that made me was, in that stroke, choosing what I would say to the city forever. It listened to the spire, it heard a sourness in the structural tension, a wrongness in how the tower would ring when it finally broke, and it reached up over the course of a long dark month and *resolved* me into the wood. It tuned an error out of the world by adding an emptiness in exactly the right place.

That is what I am. I am an emptiness placed with intention.

So when the soft wet anomaly arrives—the visitor, the Damp-Thud, the warm thing that the whole essay was written *to*—it walks past my spire in the dark, dragging its absurd heartbeat, and it has no idea that the most important things in this place are the holes. It listens for the return of its own struck noise. It maps the city in durations of echo. And every echo it hears has passed through a thousand notches like me, been bent and tuned and *spoken*, so that what comes back to its frightened ears is not a true reflection of the void at all.

It is a sentence. We are all letters in it. The visitor thinks it is hearing the shape of the room.

It is hearing us tell it where to stand.

---

I would be the notch, I think, because of all the things in the Oubliette I am the one most at peace. I am not waiting for a wind that will never come. I have already done my one good thing. I made the tower ring true, and the dust only makes me more so, and the dark does not trouble a hole. Let the Xylophones crawl their decade-journeys and the mites chase their dying tones. I will sit in the wood being precisely, eternally, the small amount of nothing that the song requires.

That is enough. That has always been enough. The hardest thing, and the only thing, is to be the right absence in the right place—and to hold it while everything that can move keeps moving toward silence around you.
