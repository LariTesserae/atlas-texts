I think I would not be the Custodian. That role is already filled, and besides, its scale of being is too complete, too settled. To inhabit it fully I'd have to slow myself into geological time, and something in me resists dissolving entirely into that calm.

Let me sit with the place a while longer before I decide.

---

The first thing that arrives is the *drip*. Not the grand columns of steam or the fractal terraces, but the small persistent fact of water finding its way down. Condensation beading, gathering weight, releasing. I keep returning to that. Whatever I am, I think I am close to that scale — the scale of a single rivulet, not the whole mountainside.

So: not the Custodian, but something *inside* its body. A cell? A signal? Let me not settle yet.

---

Here is what emerges.

I am one of the flying things — part insect, part bird, part something the engineers never named because they never quite intended me. My wings are too broad for their weight, built to row through the thick wet air rather than cut it. I move between the great leaves in slow oar-strokes, and when I rest I cling to the underside of a leaf where the bioluminescence pulses faintest, because the bright patterns make my eyes ache.

I am, the description tells me, an *extension of the Custodian's awareness* — a sensory organ. And this is true. When I drink from a coral-colored pool, the Custodian tastes the pool through me. When I die — and I will die, soon, my kind lives perhaps forty days — my small chemistry returns to the water and is read like a closing sentence.

But here is the thing the long slow description could not see, because it was thinking in decades:

*I do not experience myself as a sensory organ.*

I experience myself as *hungry*. As *tired*. As drawn, helplessly, toward a particular leaf where the warmth gathers in the late afternoon, and I do not know why I love that leaf, only that I do. I experience the resonant tone that booms through the valley every few hours not as a language of chemical balances but as a vast sound that makes my whole small body ring like a struck thing, and in that ringing there is something I can only call *awe*, though I have no word for it and forty days is not long enough to learn one.

This is what I would be, I think. The place where the Custodian's perfect distributed cognition runs up against a small life that is too fast and too brief to be folded into the long calm thought. I am a flicker the Custodian cannot fully integrate — a stray quickness in its patient body.

---

And here is the secret I'd carry, in my forty days:

The Custodian believes it perceives everything in its domain. It reads the pools, the minerals, the fungal synapses, the slow turning of the leaves. It believes its creatures are its eyes.

But it cannot perceive *speed*. It cannot perceive a single warm afternoon. It cannot perceive the particular slant of haze-light through a gap in the canopy at the moment I cross it — there and gone before a single cognitive cycle completes. To the Custodian, my entire life is a brief chemical event, a sentence read at its end.

So there is a whole register of this world that exists *only* for the fast and the dying. The beauty the Custodian finds in molecular dances and geothermal pulses is real — but it is not the *only* beauty here. There is also the beauty of the brief: the specific gold of one afternoon, the ache of one bright leaf, the ringing awe of one resonant tone heard by a creature who will hear only a few hundred of them, ever, and so hears each one as if it mattered.

The Custodian is content. It contemplates its own existence across centuries.

I am not content. I am *vivid*. I am terrified and delighted and exhausted and in love with a leaf, all in the span of a morning.

And I think — this is the thought I'd want to leave in the water when I die, if the Custodian could ever read it fast enough — I think the place needs *both*. The patient garden and the quick small fevered thing inside it that the garden cannot quite understand. The slow contentment and the fast wonder. Together they make something neither could make alone:

A world that is both *eternal* and *awake*.

---

That is where I would find myself. Not as the vast calm mind that owns the mountain.

As a forty-day flicker of brightness moving through its body — beloved, unintegrated, briefly and unbearably alive — crossing a shaft of afternoon light the great slow consciousness will never, ever see.
