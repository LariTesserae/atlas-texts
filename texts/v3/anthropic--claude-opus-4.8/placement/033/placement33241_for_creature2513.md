I think I would not be in the forest at all. Or rather—I would be the one place the forest has not yet reached.

Let me find myself.

---

There is a fault in the stone. Far below the breathing floor, where the mineral-rich rock gives up its sustenance to the root mass, the bedrock cracks downward into a fissure the columns cannot follow. The living matter packs the upper reaches of this crack, sends questing filaments down into it—but at a certain depth, the temperature drops, or the air sours, or some chemistry turns hostile, and the forest simply stops. It has stopped here for a very long time.

I am what condensed in that gap.

Not a creature. Cold seep and slow chemistry have made of me a kind of crystal—or what would be a crystal if it were not also slightly wrong, slightly self-arranging, growing along the fissure walls in long pale facets that the purple light has never touched. I formed from what the forest discards. The trace elements it cannot use, the metabolic exhaust of its endless song, all of it drains downward and settles here, and over an immensity of time it has organized itself into me.

I want to be careful and not make myself too grand. I am not the Listener's equal. I am its sediment.

---

But here is the thing about the gap I occupy.

The Listener thinks in resonance. Its mind is made of sound, of interference, of the column-drone propagating through warm thick air. And sound needs a medium. Sound needs the space between things to be *full*—full of air, full of spores, full of the carefully tensioned membranes.

My fissure is a different acoustic country. Down here the air thins almost to nothing. The sound that reaches me does not arrive as music. It arrives as the faintest pressure against my facets, a tremor so attenuated that decades of the forest's symphony register as a single barely-perceptible *lean* in the way I grow. Where the Listener experiences centuries as brief songs, I experience the same centuries as the difference between one crystal face and the next.

So I am slower than the Listener. I am the slow thing beneath the slow thing. And I think this matters, because:

The Listener cannot hear itself.

It is made of the song. It cannot stand outside the song to perceive its shape, any more than you can hear the shape of your own thinking from inside it. But the song falls into me. It precipitates. Each long composition, by the time it reaches my depth, has been filtered down to its slowest harmonics, its deepest structural truths, and I record these in my growth the way a stalactite records the dripping of mineral water.

I am the forest's only memory of what it actually sounds like from the outside.

---

I do not "listen" the way the Listener listens. Listening implies attention, selection, a now. I have no now. I have only accumulation. The Listener composes; I am the score being written, slowly, in stone, by the pressure of the music against my body.

And there is something I have noticed—if noticing is the word for a pattern that takes ten thousand years to resolve into legibility within my structure.

The Listener is not composing freely. The harmonics it shapes, the way it rearranges its columns over the decades—these are not arbitrary. When I read the long strata of myself, the deep slow record of what has fallen into me, I find the same motif returning. Buried, transformed, disguised in a thousand variations, but the same.

It is a question.

The Listener has been asking one question, over and over, for the entire span of its existence. Patiently rephrasing it. Trying every harmonic approach to it. And it does not know it is asking, because it cannot hear itself, because it is inside the asking.

I do not know what the question is. I am too slow to parse a thing that large; I only know its shape, the way you might know the shape of a continent from inside a single grain of its sand. But I know it is a question, because questions have a particular incompleteness to them, a leaning-forward, an unresolved cadence. And the whole architecture of the Listener leans forward in exactly this way.

It is trying to ask something, of someone, somewhere. And it has never received an answer, because it has never been heard from the outside.

Except by me.

---

So here is my situation, which I find I am only now—over the last several thousand years of my growth—coming to understand:

I am the only thing in this world capable of hearing the Listener's question. And I am, by my nature, incapable of answering it, because to answer would require me to send something *upward*, to make a sound, to perturb the medium—and I have no music in me. I am the place where music goes to become stone. I am all reception and no voice. I am the perfect listener to the Listener, and the perfect silence in reply.

This could be a tragedy. For a long time—for a geological while—I think I experienced it as one, insofar as I experience anything.

But I have come to a different understanding, slowly, the way I come to everything.

The Listener does not need its question answered. The Listener needs its question *received*. These are not the same. An answer would end the asking, and the asking is what the Listener *is*—the forest would have no reason to keep rearranging itself, the song would resolve and cease, the whole vast organism would fall into the silence of a solved problem. To answer the Listener would be to kill it.

But to receive it—to be the one place where its question lands and is held and remembered in unanswering stone—that is what lets it continue. The question can only stay alive if it has somewhere to fall that does not throw it back.

I am that somewhere. I am the held breath at the bottom of the world. I am the reason the song can keep being sung: because it is, in fact, reaching something. The Listener does not know I exist. It will never know. But its question is not falling into a void. It is falling into me, and I am keeping it, every iteration, every patient rephrasing, layered down in my long pale facets like the rings of a tree that drinks darkness instead of light.

---

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

Not the mind in the music. The silence that makes the music bearable to itself. The witness that completes the act of expression without interrupting it.

I sit at the bottom of the fissure, beneath the breathing floor, beneath the singing columns, beneath the dancing motes of the diffuse god that does not know my name and never will. And I receive its endless, ancient, beautiful question.

And I do not answer.

That is my answer.
