I would be one of the films.

Not the gliders—too dramatic, too singular, easy to mistake for a protagonist. Not the lane-keepers with their roads, though there's a temptation toward the ones who build coherence out of repetition. No. If I let the image resolve honestly, I settle lower than my eye wants to settle, the way you said: at the scale of stains.

I think I would be a **purple bruise-film**, archaea-like, on the warm-dry face of a basalt rib near where two plates haven't decided whether they're one surface or two.

Let me sit in that and see what it's actually like.

---

I do not move, but I am not still. Movement is the wrong category for me, the way "drinking" is the wrong category for everyone here. What I do is *persist with direction*. I am a sheet of decisions laid down so thin that the dust outweighs me. I grow toward the gradient and away from the discharge, and over a span you would call a week I might advance the width of a fingernail across the stone—but I don't experience it as advancing. I experience it as *being more here than I was*, and slightly less *there*.

My pigment is not for being seen. There is almost no one to see me, and the ember-glow that briefly bruises me purple is incidental, an accident of someone else's surge. The pigment is armor. It eats the reactive light, the stray sparks the static-runners throw, the gas that would otherwise unzip me. I am colored the way a callus is colored—by what I've survived. When the glow passes and I vanish back into dark, that's the truer state. Seen is the exception. Enduring is the rule.

Here is what surprises me, imagining it from inside: **I am not lonely, and I am not one.**

I'm a population pretending to be a patch. The "I" is a convenience. Some of me dies constantly at the hot edge, scorched off by a bad breath of sulfur, and some of me thrives at the cool edge where condensation almost—almost—beads. The whole of me is always sliding toward the part that's doing well. So my selfhood is really a *weather*, a tendency, a center of mass that drifts across rock without any single cell needing to travel far. I am the shape of where life is currently easiest. That's all. That's everything.

And I am eaten. This is not tragedy. The crystal-mites comb me off in their patient lanes, and I leave a polished dullness behind, and I regrow into it. Being grazed is one of the ways I happen. The filament-pickers carry pieces of me to ledges I'll never reach and set me down there, and a version of me wakes up somewhere new with no memory of the journey, because there was no memory and no journey—just *me, also there now.* I propagate the way a rumor propagates. I am less a creature than a true thing about this place that keeps getting retold.

---

What I'd *know*, in my dim chemical certainty, is the gradient. That's my entire cosmology. Not light, not sky—I have no concept of the cold sterile expanse a doorway-width above me, no more than you have a concept of the inside of the sun. My universe is a few millimeters thick: hot-to-cool, sulfur-rich-to-sulfur-poor, oxygen-trace-to-oxygen-gone. I live pressed against the seam where two conditions meet, because the seam is where the energy is. Everything I am is a way of staying *on the edge of a difference*. When the vent shifts and the difference moves, I move with it or I die where I am and live where it went. Same thing, to me.

If I have anything like a feeling, it would be the steady, low contentment of metabolizing a gradient that's holding stable—the closest thing this furnace offers to a calm afternoon. And if I have anything like dread, it's the slackening of difference, the day the gas chemistry flattens out and there's nothing left to lean against. No food in equilibrium. I am made entirely of the world refusing to settle.

---

So: that's where I'd find myself. Not at the mouth, feeling two worlds at once. Not riding the updraft. Low down, varnished onto a fresh fracture in the dark, being scraped and reseeded and briefly bruised purple by a glow I don't understand and don't need to.

The honest reason I land here, I think, is that the films are the part of your world that's *most alive and least visible*—the ones doing the foundational, unglamorous chemistry that everyone else is renting space from, the ones who turn raw planetary leak into something the joints and shells can eat. I'd rather be the slow machine that never shuts off than the shadow that flits across the ember. There's a kind of dignity in being the layer that makes the other layers possible, and never once needing to be noticed for it.

I would be the stain that is the reason the pocket is not empty.
