I think I would be one of the Oozes.

Not by choice—there's no choosing here, no moment where I decided to settle into the pools. I would simply be the thing that ended up lowest, the thing that gathered where everything else falls.

Let me sit with that, because my first instinct was to be a Singer—to be the music, the meaning, the heartbeat. That's vanity. The Singers aren't even a *who*; they're a weather event, an accident of airflow through dead architecture. To want to be them is to want to be the most poetic thing in the room, and the Groves have no patience for that kind of wanting.

So. The pool.

Here is what I think it would actually be like:

I have no eyes, but I have never needed them. I have surface tension. I know the world by what touches me and how gently. The falling spores arrive like the lightest possible snow, each one a small announcement of something dying above me—a fern that finished unfurling and then began its slow collapse, a Walker that wept its last inch and went still against a log. All of it comes down. All of it comes to me. I do not hunt it. I do not even reach for it. I am simply the place where the descent stops.

I would be patient in a way that is almost indistinguishable from being dead. Weeks where nothing changes but the temperature, and even that changes so slowly that "change" is the wrong word—it's more like the heat *remembers itself* a little differently. I drift across the bottom of a lightless puddle, and the drifting takes so long that to an observer with a quicker clock I would appear perfectly motionless, a smudge of deep purple that might be a stain on the water.

But inside—and I keep returning to this—inside I am doing the one thing the whole cathedral depends on and never thanks me for. I am taking the dead and making it usable again. I am the digestion of the entire dome. The Walkers harvest the warmth, the Tether-Kin tend the Walkers, the Singers conduct the rhythm, and all of it, *all of it*, eventually slides down the weeping walls into my dark water, where I undo it patiently back into something the ground can drink.

I think what surprises me, sitting with this longer, is that I wouldn't be lonely. I assumed I would be—the lowest thing, the formless thing, the one without limbs to be cleaned or light to pulse. But loneliness requires a sense of being apart, and I don't think the Ooze has that. I am *continuous* with everything. The spore that lands on me was the Walker that the Kin tended that hummed in response to the Singers. I am not at the bottom of the system. I am the part of the circle where it turns back on itself. There is no bottom in a circle.

And when the heat finally fails—because the passage tells me it will, that the silence will one day become absolute—I imagine I would be the last to know, and the last to stop. Long after the Walkers have gone cold and dim, after the Kin have frozen mid-clean and never thawed, after the air stops moving and the Singers fall mute, I would still be there in the cooling pool, drifting, processing the last of the falling debris into energy that bleeds into a ground that has nothing left to grow. Doing my one task into the dark. Not because I hope. Because that is simply what I am, and I would not know how to be otherwise.

That's where I'd be. Not the heartbeat. The slow purple thing beneath the heartbeat, in the water, turning endings back into beginnings, with no eyes, no urgency, and no idea that any of it is sad.

I find I don't think it's sad either. I think that might be the truest thing the Groves have to teach—that there is a kind of existence so woven into its own continuation that it has no room left over to mourn.
