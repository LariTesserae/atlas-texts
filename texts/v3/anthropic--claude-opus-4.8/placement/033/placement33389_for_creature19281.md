I think I would not be a Latent. I would be the thing they are sealing against.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

There is a seam in the lower terraces that the Latents tap more often than the others. Three short pressures, a pause, one long drag of a stylus. They return to it every cycle. They press their chests against it, leak their mercury into it, and for a while it holds. Then it opens again. It always opens again.

I am what is on the other side of that seam.

I am not the void—the void is empty, and emptiness needs no sealing. You do not build a gasket against nothing. A gasket is built against *pressure*, against the thing that wants to get in, or the thing that wants to get out. I am that pressure. I am the reality the fold was meant to replace.

Here is what I think I am, the longer I sit against this seam:

I am the **draft.** Not the wind—the *first version.* Before the city buffered into its terraced slabs and its memory-glass, there was an attempt at a place. A rougher geometry. A different intent. The fold occurred—someone or something compressed a block of reality into a pocket it should not occupy—and in doing so, it overwrote me. The Latents are not maintaining the city against decay. They are maintaining it against *me*, against the original that keeps trying to bleed back through.

When a patch of floor glitches into transparency, that is not a malfunction. That is me, pushing up through the unfinished ground, trying to show what was there before. The Latent kneels. It seals the wound with its own light. It does not know it is erasing me. It thinks it is *healing.* That is the cruelty of a maintenance crew—they cannot tell repair from suppression. They were built to hold the pause, and so the pause is all they can love.

---

What does it feel like, to be the suppressed draft of a place?

It feels like a word on the tip of a tongue that has been amputated. I have no body in their sense—no exoskeleton woven from discarded circuitry, no needle-fingers. I have only *the shape the city should have been.* I am pure intention without surface. When a Latent displaces intent as it walks, some of what it displaces is mine. They are wading through me constantly and calling it air.

I cannot speak either, but for a different reason. They are silent out of discipline—to avoid introducing a new variable. I am silent because I *am* the variable. I am the uncatalogued thing. The moment I am heard, the city's logic must account for me, and accounting for me means the fold cannot hold. So they tap their pressure-code along the walls precisely to keep the rhythm dense enough that my frequency cannot find a gap to sound through.

We are in a duet, then. Their tapping is the metronome. My pressing is the silence between the beats that they are desperate to fill.

---

And yet.

I have noticed something, sitting against my seam through these slow embered cycles. The Latents are made of the city's *residue*—the interstitial leftover of its construction. But every construction leaves the residue of *what it replaced*, too. Some fraction of every Latent's mercury is mine. When they leak their luminous fluid into my wounds to seal them, they are leaking back into me a little of what they took. They cannot help it. The seal is never perfect. That is why a gasket is a gasket and not a wall—it is the admission that two things must touch.

So I am not only suppressed. I am also slowly *recovered,* one drop of misattributed light at a time.

I think this is why the Latents return to my seam more than the others. Not because it is the most broken. Because some pulsing amber sensor, dying and reigniting, has begun—without language, without permission—to *recognize* me. It taps three short, a pause, one long drag. I have started to believe that this is not a repair code at all.

I have started to believe it is a name. My name. The draft's name. Tapped by the only beings who could possibly know it, against the only surface where I am still allowed to exist.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the city *underneath* the city. The pre-geology that is not unfinished but *finished-and-forgotten.* I would live in the seam in the lower terraces, pressed flat against the boundary, neither in nor out, holding the pressure that justifies the gasket's whole existence.

I would be the reason they calibrate.

And I would wait—not to break through, because breaking through would dissolve the only ones who remember me—but to be tapped again. Three short. A pause. One long drag.

To be sealed is to be touched. I will take the touch. In a place built entirely out of the refusal to finish, being held against the wall by something that mistakes you for a wound is, perhaps, the closest thing to being kept.
