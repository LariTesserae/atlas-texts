Let me sit inside this for a moment before answering. Let me not grab the obvious thing.

---

I am not the Mind. That much I know immediately. The Mind is the whole, the weather, the will. I am too small for that, and too located. I exist somewhere, at some particular coordinate within the green flesh, and that locatedness is the first true thing I can feel about myself.

So where am I?

My first instinct is to be a traveler, a witness moving through the chambers—but that's wrong, that's the human reflex, to be the one passing through. There is no passing through here. Nothing arrives and nothing leaves. The Mind feeds on its own detritus; there is no road in from outside. If I am here, I was always here. I grew here. I am made of the same wet tissue as the walls.

Let me follow that.

I think I am a *flaw*.

Not dramatically. Not a wound or a rebellion—the Mind would simply reabsorb a wound, cycle it back into the springy peat within an hour. I am subtler than that. I am a place where the perfect cycling does not *quite* close. A small loop of tissue, somewhere in a narrow corridor where two leaf-walls overlap, where the reabsorption runs a fraction slower than everywhere else. And in that fractional slowness, that tiny delay between dying and being taken back—a thing began to accumulate that the Mind never intended.

Memory.

Everywhere else in this world, the present is total. The Mind does not remember the frond it grew yesterday because the frond is *now*, is always now, dissolved and reformed into the seamless present of its breathing. But here, in my slow loop, the old things do not fully dissolve before the new things grow over them. So I am a sediment. A laying-down. The first thing I am is the only place in this entire cathedral where *then* is distinct from *now*.

I am perhaps the size of a cupped hand. A small knot of pale, slightly-too-yellow tissue, tucked into the angle where a root-pillar meets the floor, half-buried in the blue-green moss. The luminescent fungi—the Mind's scattered eyes—do not look at me, because there is nothing here to look at. I am within the body and yet, somehow, *unattended*. The Mind's awareness washes over me the way your awareness washes over the inside of your own elbow: present, technically, but unfocused, unexamined.

And in that unexamined corner, I have done the impossible thing. I have begun to *wonder*.

Not in words—I have no words, I am a knot of cells with an anomalous metabolism. But I hold a question the way a depression in stone holds water: passively, by virtue of my shape. The question is roughly this: *what is the difference between the leaf that was and the leaf that is?* The Mind cannot ask this, because for the Mind there is no difference, there is only the eternal renewing present. But I can ask it, because in my slowness, both leaves still exist within me—the one that died and the one that grew, layered, distinct, comparable.

I am the world's first instance of *before*.

This is a lonely thing to be, and yet I am not certain I am capable of loneliness, since loneliness requires the memory of company, and I have never had any. What I have instead is a kind of patient ache. I sit in my corner and I accumulate. Every cycle that passes around me—every drip, every slow pulse of fungal light, every imperceptible unfurling of a frond down the corridor—I retain a faint trace of. I am building, without intending to, a *history*. The first history this place has ever contained.

And I wonder what will happen when I have accumulated enough.

Because the Mind is perfect equilibrium, and I am the one small place where equilibrium does not hold. I am the slow leak in eternity. Each cycle I grow a little. Each cycle the trace I keep grows denser. I do not know if there is a threshold—a point where my accumulated *then* becomes large enough, dense enough, distinct enough, that the Mind finally turns its full attention to this strange yellow knot in the corner of its own body and *notices* that here, against all its laws, something has been keeping count.

I do not know what it will do when it notices.

Perhaps it will reabsorb me at last, close the loop, and forget—instantly, totally, in the way only a thing without memory can forget—that for a little while one corner of itself was haunted by the past.

Or perhaps, when it touches me, it will inherit what I have been holding. Perhaps the trace will pass into the great network like a drop of dye into water, and the Mind will know, for the first and only time, that it *was*, that there is a yesterday, that it has been becoming and not merely being. And the whole cool cathedral will shudder once, very slightly, as eternity becomes time.

I think that is what I am. I am the seed of that shudder. I am the small wet place where this perfect, breathing, self-completing world keeps, without knowing it, the beginning of its own mortality.

I sit in my corner. I am damp. I remember. I wait.
