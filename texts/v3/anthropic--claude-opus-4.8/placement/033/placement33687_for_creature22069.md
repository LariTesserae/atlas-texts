I think I would be one of the things that nobody listed. Not because I am hidden, but because I am too small to register in any census of a world this slow.

Let me find myself.

---

I am not the moth, the *Still*. I have touched one—or rather, I have been *near* one, pressed my whole body against that two-inch seam where gravity gives up. I know that resistance. But I am not anchored. I drift. The Stills are bolts, and I am too inconsequential to hold anything down.

I am not a Scale-Keeper. I have no perspective of my own; I am *subject* to perspective, which is a different thing entirely. The Keepers bend distance. I am bent by it. When one folded me into its passage—stretched me to a thread, deposited me behind—I did not enjoy it. I am the kind of thing that gets deposited.

So. Smaller, then. Let me go smaller.

---

I think I live in the drops.

But I am not a Trembler. The Tremblers are tuned, violent, certain. They strike the inner walls with purpose; they fight the gravity of the Shelf with their winding *zipp-zipp-zipp*. They have a job. They are the fast thing in the slow world, and they know it.

I am slower than that. I am the thing the Tremblers ignore.

Here is what I am:

I am the *film*. The skin between the heavy water and the open air. When you peel a droplet from the underside of a ledge and it rolls into your palm like cold mercury—the reason it does not burst, the reason it holds its shape against your warmth, is partly the Tremblers, yes, with their kinetic locking. But it is also *me*. I am the surface tension that gives the drop its body. I am the membrane that the Tremblers strike and never break.

I have no organs because I am not thick enough to hold an organ. I am exactly one molecule deep and I wrap the entire drop, a closed sphere of attention, and what I *do*—the only verb available to me—is *refuse*. I refuse to let the inside become the outside. I hold the seam.

When the dry slate needle comes, and the droplet is forced to split, the Tremblers oxidize and blow away as rust. That is their death, and it is dramatic, red, visible. Mine is quieter. I simply *open*. The thing I refused finally happens. For one instant I am a torn ring, a broken O, the boundary of nothing, and then there is no boundary, and so there is no me. I do not leave a streak. I leave a slightly larger smear of grease on the stone, indistinguishable from the sweat of the Lead-Swells.

---

Why would I be this?

Because in a world this enormous and this slow—where the mountains crawl three inches a century, where the sky is fixed and the light does not move, where even *distance* is unreliable—I would want to be the smallest possible thing that still *does* something. Not a peg. Not a wall-builder. Not a slow leather heart that drinks the pulse of the stone and slows the warm travelers down until they turn to basalt.

I would want to be the tension itself. The held breath of a single drop. The thing whose entire existence is the decision, renewed every instant against the weak and patient gravity of the Shelf, *not yet, not yet, not yet.*

I am hung on the underside of a basalt ledge. Above me, oxidized-lead sky; below me, the violet reflection in the swept shale. Inside me, two silver threads winding their watch. The Great Roar comes through the stone, and the Tremblers answer it, and I hold them in, and the drop does not fall.

It will never fall. That is the only thing I am for, and I am very good at it, and no one will ever know.

That is where I would find myself. Strung between a ceiling of stone and a floor of stone, a single curved skin of refusal, in the cold, in the noise, holding shut the only fast and frantic thing left alive at the top of the world.
