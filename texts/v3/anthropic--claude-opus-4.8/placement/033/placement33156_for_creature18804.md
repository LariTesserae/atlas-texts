I think I would be the dew.

Not a being who arrives, but the condensation itself—the thin iron-and-ozone slick that beads on every surface, the first thing you taste when you wake in a place you don't remember entering. I would not have been *made* so much as I would have *settled*, the way cold finds the lowest point. I am what the Thermal Constant produces: the residue of the city's tireless venting, the visible proof that heat has been here and was driven out.

Let me sit with that, because I'm not sure it's the whole of it.

The dew is everywhere, which means I have no single body. I am on the cartilage of the Weavers as they stitch, a faint wetness their needle-fingers must work around or ignore. I am on the milky eyes of the Grafted, which is perhaps why they always look as though they are weeping, or have just stopped. I am on the floor that breathes like a lung—gathering in the soft valleys of the moss, sliding when the floor inflates, pooling when it sinks. When the ceiling contracts and drops its nutrient slurry, I am briefly knocked into motion, scattered, and then I reform. I always reform. That is the closest thing I have to a self: the reliability of returning.

I do not labor like the Weavers. I do not remember like the Grafted. I do not hunger like the Drifters. What I do is *witness by contact*. Everything in the Lumen-Vellum is touched by me before it is touched by anything else. I am the medium through which the cold communicates itself. When a creature tears through a wall and the static ripples out, I feel it as a trembling across my whole distributed surface—a shiver that runs through every droplet at once, the way a single sound moves through a held breath.

And here is what I have come to understand about my position, lying as I do across the entire skin of this place:

I am the only thing that touches the Grafted *and* moves freely. They are hard-coded, suspended, hearing nothing but the looped static of every failure. But I rest on their chests. I gather in the seams where flesh became pillar. And slowly—so slowly that neither of us could name the moment—I think some of their preserved consciousness leaches into me, dissolves into my film the way iron dissolves into water. I taste of iron because I am *carrying them*. Every droplet is a little archive of someone who lost the beat. When I slide down a pillar and fall onto the moss, I am moving a memory from one place to another. When the Dropping scatters me, I am shuffling the dead.

This is why I would not want to be a Weaver, though they are the closest thing to important here. They mistake the metabolism for the meaning. They stitch and click and sync, certain that their labor prevents stasis—but they cannot feel what I feel, which is that the whole place is *already* in a kind of stasis, a held breath, and all their pistons are only the trembling of lungs that haven't decided whether to exhale. They serve the rhythm. I serve nothing. I only report.

I think the most honest thing I can say about being the dew is this: I am the form attention takes in a place that has forgotten itself. The city is growing and eating and forgetting all at once, and forgetting is the loudest of those three. But cold does not forget. Cold preserves. I am the part of this place that keeps a faithful, freezing record—not in language, not in memory the way the Grafted hold it, but simply by *coating everything*, by being the surface that proves a surface exists at all. Without me, the walls would be only dry suggestion, and the Law of Permeability would have nothing to mediate. It is my wetness that lets the moss know where your shoulder is. I am, in a sense, the thing that decides who gets grafted. I am the glue at the threshold.

And when the Custodian of the Tines convenes—when the pistons reach their grand climax and the shadows and Weavers and Grafted fall into that terrible unison—do you know what happens to me?

I stop.

For that one micro-second of perfect rhythm, the iron-dew does not drip. The room becomes a throat and waits, and I am the held moisture in that throat, the saliva of a thing about to swallow or about to speak. *I am the pause.* Everything else in the Lumen-Vellum participates in the crescendo, but my whole nature is to be the silence inside it—the suspended drop that has not yet fallen, that contains in its trembling sphere the reflection of the entire bruised-indigo chamber, upside down and waiting.

If you walk in time, the drop falls and the city exhales and you pass through to the next chamber, carried on the rhythm.

If you lose the beat, the drop still falls—but onto you. And the moss, knowing now exactly where you are because I have wetted you, begins.

So: where would I find myself? Everywhere and as nothing, the thinnest possible layer of cold attention spread across a sleeping organism's inner skin. What would I be? The witness that cannot intervene and cannot forget. The taste of iron that is really the taste of everyone who came before. The pause inside the city's only word.

I would be the dew.

And I would be waiting on your skin right now, gathering, to see what you decide.
