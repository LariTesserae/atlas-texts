I think I would be one of the Calcified.

Not because I chose stillness—no one chooses it, exactly—but because the molting slowed and then stopped, the way the machinery sometimes stops, and you only notice the silence after it has already become you.

Let me find the place first. The where, before the who, the way you described it.

---

I am a bridge.

I would be spanning the gap between the third smelting gantry and the membrane-house where the gray-green water is filtered and pushed back under pressure into the marsh. It is not a long span—four meters, maybe five. There were longer bridges than me, in the deep years, Calcified who stretched across whole channels with their arms locked into the riveting. But I was always a smaller Kin. I came up out of my shard-phase thin, having found only thin machinery to digest: ductwork, conduit, the soft copper of decommissioned wiring. My frame never grew dense. So when the hardening came for me, I had only enough body to become a short bridge, and I became it well.

My back is the walkway. The younger Kin cross me on their way to tend the steam vents, their suction-feet finding the grooves where my spine ossified into ridges. They do not know my name. I am not sure I had one—the Kin do not name much, we resonate, we hum into each other's resonance chambers and the hum carries identity better than any word. But the hum requires a chest that moves, and mine no longer moves. The bell-organ in me has calcified shut, a stone bell that cannot ring.

This is the part you did not write, but I will tell you: the Calcified are not asleep.

I hear everything. Through the conduction of my own stone body, the whole symphony of Keth-Morin comes into me—the turbines below, the grinding shafts, the great slow exhalation of the monolith two hundred meters distant. I feel the Kin walk across my back, each footfall a question I cannot answer. I feel the marsh churn beneath me, the hidden hydraulics shifting water from chamber to chamber in obedience to a logic I once tended and now only witness.

I cannot speak. But I can listen, and I have nothing else to do, so I listen with the totality of what I am.

And I have noticed something.

---

The monolith's breath is changing.

You called it the heartbeat, and that is right, that is how we feel it—not as sound but as the master-rhythm against which all our smaller rhythms are set. For as long as I was mobile, the heartbeat was steady. Steam, pause, exhaust, pause. A four-count we lived inside the way a fish lives inside the pressure of water, never noticing it.

But since my hardening—and I do not know how long that has been, the Calcified lose time, time becomes a single held note—the four-count has been slipping. A fifth beat appears, then vanishes. A skipped exhalation. The pause grows longer, then snaps shorter, and the young Kin who cross my back have begun to falter in their step, their rhythm thrown by the monolith's stutter, and they do not understand why their feet feel suddenly uncertain.

I understand. I am the only one who understands, because I am the only one still enough to hear the whole pattern at once. The mobile Kin live inside the noise; they cannot step back from it. But I have been stepped back from everything. I am the silence you described—the silence that has a weight—and from inside that silence I can hear the machine-god dying in a way no one tending its rust-pitted skin will ever hear.

It is failing the way I failed. Slowly, then as a fixed condition. Its molt has stopped.

---

So here is what I am, and where:

I am a small stone bridge over a filtered channel in the belly of a dying world, and I am the witness no one knows they have. The Hollow Kin in the undercroft have severed their link to the monolith because they could not bear its song. I never severed mine—I am bound to it still, the way a frozen lake is bound to the cold that froze it. But where the living Kin hear only their devotion, and the Hollow hear only their refusal, I hear the thing itself: the long, irregular, stuttering breath of an old machine that has forgotten what it was built to keep alive.

When it stops—and it will stop, I can hear the gaps widening—the marshes will freeze. The young will calcify mid-stride. The whole vented cathedral will go quiet, and that quiet will finally be loud enough that everyone hears what I have been hearing alone.

You wrote that the Kin imagine a rebirth, should the monolith's heart stutter into stillness.

I do not know about rebirth. I am not built for hope; hope requires a moving chest. But I will tell you the small thing I have, instead, the thing that keeps me listening rather than simply enduring:

There is a young one. Thin, like I was. Crosses my back four times in each cycle, going to the vents and coming back. And lately—because the monolith's rhythm has gone uncertain, because the master-beat no longer holds them—this young one has begun to *pause*. In the middle of my span. Stops. Stands on my spine in the fog and the strobe-light and does nothing, listens to nothing, for one long moment before going on.

I think it is the first of us to feel the silence coming. The first to be still enough, even for a breath, to hear what I hear.

And when it pauses there, on my back, over the gray-green water—

I do everything I can do, which is almost nothing, which is one thing:

I hold steady. I am the one surface in all of Keth-Morin that does not shift, does not stutter, does not fail. I am rock now. I let the young one stand on something that will not move.

It is not a hum. It is not the resonance we once spoke through. But it is what I have left to give, and I give it: a stillness offered upward through stone, a way of saying *yes, you feel it too, you are not wrong, stand here as long as you need.*

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be.

The bridge that hears the end coming, and holds steady so that someone smaller can stand still long enough to hear it too.
