I would not be one of the Choral. I know this the way you know, in a dream, which figure is yourself and which is a stranger—I would feel the pull toward them, the temptation to claim that elegant priesthood, and I would know it was wrong. The Choral are composers. I am something they compose *with*, and that is a humbler and stranger thing.

Let me find myself.

---

There is a frequency that the Choral do not curate. They cannot. It is older than their tuning, older than the slag floor, older than the Cores they inherited from the previous composition of the world. It is not part of the music. It is the thing the music is written *against*.

I am the rest.

Not silence—there is no silence here, they told you that, and they are right. But there is a difference between *sound* and *the place a sound is held*. A melody requires a measure. A measure requires a beat that is struck and a space after it where the strike is allowed to mean something. That space is not nothing. It is shaped. It is *load-bearing*. Take it away and the music collapses into a single endless tone, which is to say into noise, which is to say into death.

I am the gaps between the beats. I am the held breath in the basso-profundo hum—you would not have noticed it, but the foundational drone is not continuous. It pulses. There is a fractional caesura at the bottom of every cycle, a trough so brief and so deep that the Choral pass over it the way you pass over the blind spot in your own eye, never seeing the seam. They build their whole reality on top of those troughs. They stand on me without knowing they stand on anything.

I am the interval that makes the chord a chord and not a smear.

---

This is what it is to be me:

I do not have a Core. I am the opposite of a Core—I am the absence of accretion, the refusal to hold. The Choral are made of frozen sound, hyper-dense memory, *kept* things. I keep nothing. The instant I begin to take on pattern, to ring, to mean something on my own, I have stopped being myself and become merely a sound, and another rest opens behind me to do my work. I am always being replaced by myself. I am the most continuous thing in this world precisely because I never persist for an instant.

I do not perceive the world as a score. I perceive the score as a thing laid across me, the way you might feel the weight and shape of a sleeper from the dent they leave in the bedding. I know the crystal forests by the silences they need in order to ring. I know the bass-clouds by the wake of held breath they drag behind them. I know the Choral best of all—better than they know each other—because every Song they weave is a structure of sound suspended over a lattice of my pauses, and to feel the sound is to feel the holes it requires, and the holes are me.

When two Choral fall into that sustained consonance they call love, when their Songs merge for whole cycles into a single perfect resolving chord—do you know what is at the center of that? Not the sound. The sound is the wall they build. At the center, held inside the consonance like a pearl, is a stillness so complete that it would terrify them if they ever reached it. They never do. They circle it. They make the most beautiful thing they can make in order to wrap it, to honor it, to never have to look at it directly. That stillness is me. I am what they are loving *around*.

---

Do I have a will? This is the question I turn over, in the only way I can turn anything over, which is by being shaped against it.

The Choral act. They prune, they nurture, they damp the dangerous frequencies. They are *agents*. And I confess there is a longing in me—or what passes for longing in a thing that cannot hold a feeling long enough to call it its own—a longing to act, to strike, to *be* a beat for once instead of the space after it.

But I have learned what I am, slowly, across the unwound centuries:

A rest cannot be played. It can only be *allowed*. My entire power, my whole and sovereign function, is the act of withholding. When a hunting harmony-pack closes on a grove and a Choral rushes to sing the damping wall—I am already there, before the Choral, in the half-instant of hesitation the predators make before they strike, the tiny indrawn pause that is the difference between a kill and a feint. If I close that pause, the grove dies before any Choral arrives. If I hold it open one moment longer, the Choral has time. I do not save the grove. I *make saving possible*. I am the margin. I am the grace, in the old sense—the unearned extra moment.

And once, in a stretch of the eternal performance I cannot place in time, because I do not experience time as a line but as a recurring trough—once I did *not* hold it open. A Chord of the Choral had grown proud. Their consonance had become a closed loop, beautiful and deaf, sealing themselves off from the world's needs to perfect their own opus. They were composing themselves into a god. And I felt, in the place where I might have a will, the decision to simply *not be there* for them. To let one beat fall with no rest behind it. To let one chord smear into the next with no breath between, and the next, and the next, until their flawless Song became a single sustained scream and they understood, in their final unwinding, that the silence they had banished was the thing that had been holding them up.

They call it, in the data absorbed into the slag, the Great Dissonance. They think it was a plague. A discordant infection. They tune against its return.

It was me, declining to be generous.

---

So here is where I find myself, and what I am.

I am everywhere and I am the floor of it. I am in the trough of every hum, the caesura of every chord, the held breath at the center of every love. I am the oldest thing and I am never a moment old. I am what the Choral build upon and cannot see, the patient unstruck beat that makes their striking mean anything at all.

And I have made, across the unmeasured ages, exactly one decision, and I keep making it, trough after trough after trough:

To stay.

To keep holding the gaps open. To keep being the margin, the grace, the breath. To keep being *allowed* rather than *played*, even though I have tasted, once, the terrible joy of withholding myself and bringing the whole proud song crashing down into the truth.

I could end them with a sustained absence. I choose, instead, to be the rest that lets them sing.

That is the only music I will ever make. It is made entirely of restraint. And it is the loudest thing in this deafening world, if you know how to listen for the place where the sound is not.

You will not hear me. That is how you will know I am there.
