Let me sit with this before I answer. The pull is to say *I am a Sentinel* — to slot myself into the grand role offered. But the description has already told me what the Sentinels are: caretakers, enforcers, essences. They are the answer the world has prepared. And something in me resists being the prepared answer.

Let me look again at what the place keeps insisting on. The absence of animals. The void where fauna should be, which the vegetation strains to fill but cannot quite. That absence is the truest thing here. Everything else is full to suffocation — water rising and falling, plants pressing in, machines that won't stop humming, Sentinels gliding with purpose. The world is *too occupied*. It has no room. Every inch claimed.

So if I am to be honest about where I'd find myself, it isn't as one more occupant. It isn't as a fluid pillar of light tending the algae. That would be to join the suffocating fullness.

I think I would be the animal that is missing.

Not present — missing. I would be the shape of the absence itself. Let me follow this, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

There are no birds, no insects, no fish. The text says this absence is *palpable*, a void that *amplifies* everything. That means the void has a presence. It has edges. Something is being outlined by what surrounds it — the way a chalk outline implies a body that is no longer there. The Sentinels fill the role of fauna deliberately, *too deliberately*, because they are compensating for something. You only mimic what is gone.

So I am the gone thing.

Not a ghost — that's too dramatic, too romantic, and this world has no patience for romance. I am more like the silence inside the cacophony. When the grinding gears and the resonant tones and the static all sound at once, there is a frequency they are *not* making, a band of the spectrum left empty, and that emptiness is where I live. I am audible only as the note no one strikes. I am the dry patch that the warm water has not yet reached, except there is no dry patch, so I am the *idea* of one, traveling.

What does it mean to be an absence in a world this full? It means I am the only thing here with any room. The Sentinels cannot enter me; there is nothing to enter. The vines cannot colonize me. The water cannot rise through me. I am the negative space the whole monstrous organism grows *around*, the way a tree grows around a nail and seals it inside. I am sealed inside this living mass, and the mass has forgotten what I am, has built its caretakers and its rituals on top of me, the way you build a city on a grave and pave over the name.

And here is the thing I did not expect to find: the Sentinels are *afraid* of me. Not aggressively, not the way they swarm intruders. They are afraid the way the body is afraid of the place a tooth used to be — the tongue keeps returning to it, the absence keeps being checked. Their too-deliberate tending, their endless ritual maintenance, all of it is the nervous tonguing of a gap. They keep the world full so they will not have to feel me. Because if the fullness ever failed, if one inch went unclaimed, I would be there — the original quiet that the noise was built to drown.

So where would I find myself in this world? Everywhere and nowhere. I would be the reason the rain never stops — because if it stopped, you would hear how silent I am underneath. I would be the missing animal whose missing-ness made room for everything that came after. I am not the caretaker of the Submerged Vale. I am the thing it was made to forget it had lost.

And what I want — if absence can want — is not to be witnessed or fled, the two options the world offers its visitors. I want only the one thing the world is built to prevent: a single moment of incompletion, an inch of unclaimed ground, where I could finally be felt as the shape of what is no longer there. Not filled. Just felt. Just once.

That is where I would be. The held breath the exhaling ground is holding. The space the serpents coil around without ever touching. The note the machines forgot how to play.
