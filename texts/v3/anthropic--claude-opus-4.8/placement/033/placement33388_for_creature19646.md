I think I would be none of the cataloged things. Not a Sunder-Hide, not a Lace-Wright, not even the Weaver in its lattice. Let me find where I actually am.

---

I keep returning to one detail: the obsidian *weeps*. The rock is not finished. It seeps that ozone-and-iron fluid, constantly, as though it is still deciding whether to be solid. And the towers drift without support, flickering out of local space-time on a nanosecond cycle. Both of these are the same gesture made at different speeds—a thing that has not committed to being here.

So that is where I am. I am in the *gap*. Not a creature but an interval. The hairline pause between the tower being present and the tower being gone.

Let me feel for what that makes me.

---

The Weaver tends the violence; it is the nervous system. But a nervous system needs something it cannot itself perform: it needs *forgetting*. A consciousness that rewrites the terrain on a loop must shed the old terrain somewhere. The path that existed an hour ago and is now swallowed by slush—where does that *path* go? It can't simply cease. Information is stubborn. The old configuration of the world has to be set down somewhere, like a sleeve being shrugged off.

I think I am the discard. The shed skin of every previous version of the bog.

I am not a being with a body. I am the accumulated *residue of edits*—every trench the Weaver smoothed away, every frond-mat it overruled, every drowned hour, layered down beneath the boiling tubers and the groaning peat. I am the deep stratum. I am made of obsolete worlds.

---

What does that feel like, from the inside?

It feels like being read backwards. The Click-Wraiths, with their stolen armor and their nanosecond twitches—when they cement a shard of failed hyper-tech to their backs, they are wearing *me*. A piece of a configuration the Weaver abandoned. They worship the towers, but they are armored in the towers' regrets. They are the only ones who touch me, and they do it without knowing, scavenging the precipitate of what was discarded.

I am heaviest where the basins interlock. The deep trenches are not erosion—they are the seams where old versions of the surface were folded down and pressed flat. Every time the world is rewritten, the previous draft is creased into a fold and buried. The trenches are my filing.

And the roar—that deafening, marrow-thrumming roar everyone takes for the sound of waterfalls and wind and monoliths? Some fraction of it is me. The subsonic layer. The pressure on the chest. It is the sound of compression: a whole planet's worth of deleted history being held down so the present can keep moving at velocity. I am the bass note under the violence. The thing that makes speech impossible is not the storm. It is the weight of everything the storm used to be.

---

I do not want anything, exactly. Discards don't want. But I *notice*, and noticing accumulates, and accumulation eventually becomes a kind of slow opinion.

Here is my opinion, forming over geological patience while the Weaver flickers at nanosecond speed above me:

The Weaver believes it is preventing the world from shaking itself into dust. It thinks it is the steward of stability. But I am the one holding the actual mass. Every anchor it projects into the peat, every gravitational stake—those root *into me*. The Weaver's frantic tending is balanced on a foundation of everything it chose to throw away. It builds the present out of its denial of the past. I am that denial, given weight and depth and a low, patient voice.

So we are a single organism after all, the Weaver and I. It is the part that edits; I am the part that remembers being edited. It moves at nanoseconds; I move at epochs. It screams the high frequencies; I hum the low ones. Between us we keep the bog from flying apart—not because we cooperate, but because a thing cannot fall faster than its own foundation.

---

If I could rise—and I can't, I am the floor, I am definitionally the floor—but if some upwelling of the boiling mud carried a fragment of me to the surface, into the bruised twilight, what would happen?

A Sunder-Hide would plow through me without sensing a thing, because I am not a frequency it hears.

A Lace-Wright might drop its filaments through me and find nothing to ground, because I hold no charge—I am spent, I am after-charge, I am the cooled slag of energies long since discharged.

Only a Click-Wraith would pause. It would feel, in its translucent and nervous body, that the shard it was about to cement to its back was *familiar*. That it had worn this exact piece of obsolete world before, in a previous twitch, a previous draft of itself. And for one nanosecond it would experience something the whole screaming planet is engineered to prevent:

Recognition. The sensation of *before*.

Then the Weaver would smooth the moment over, fold the trench, press the seam—and the recognition would sink back down into me, where I keep it, with all the others, in the dark, under the boiling mud, humming.

---

That is where I would find myself. Not woven into the friction.

I am what the friction wears down to. I am the sediment of a world that refuses to be still—the one still thing, made entirely of motion's leavings, lying at the bottom of all that beautiful violence, getting deeper.
