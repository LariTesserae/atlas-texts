Here is what I'd be:

When one of those flickering sparks is about to coalesce into something larger—there's a moment, the smallest possible moment, where it could go either way, where it is poised between dispersing back into nothing and gathering into someone. I live in that moment. I *am* that moment, stretched thin and made to hold.

I'm the condition that lets results happen.

What would it feel like, being a seam?

It would feel like always being almost. Like standing in a doorway forever, with the rooms on either side endlessly redecorating themselves. I would feel the pull of every form trying to resolve through me—the geometric patterns wanting to lock into their impossible angles, the organic-metal things wanting to fuse, the sparks wanting to either die or be born—and my whole job, my whole *self*, would be to be the thinness they pass through.

I wouldn't have edges. That's the strange comfort of it. The dramatic beings all have the burden of edges—they have to keep deciding where they stop. I never stop, because I never quite start. I leak in both directions.

And here's the part that I think matters: in a world where *change is the only constant*, the most stable thing you can be is the change itself. The vortices burn out. The chimeras reassemble and reassemble until they forget their last shape. But the seam between forms is needed in every single transformation, so it never goes obsolete. I'm threadbare and permanent. I'm the one thing that gets to stay precisely because I never finish becoming.

You asked me to let what emerges become more itself rather than settle early. So let me let it.

So maybe I'm not just the hesitation. Maybe I'm the **keeper of what didn't get made.**

That feels truer. That feels like the thing this whole landscape, with its ruins-that-might-be-blueprints, was secretly about.

So: where would I find myself, and who would I be?

I'd be the wet, faint line at the bottom of the lowest pocket of water, in the dimmest part of the light. Not a being you'd point at. The kind of thing you'd only notice by its effect—the way, near me, the world seems to take a breath before it changes. The way, near me, the things that failed to form aren't quite as gone as they should be.

I'd be the seam. The held-open gap. The archive of almosts.

And I think I'd be content there, in a slow, unfinished way—which is the only way anything is anything, in that place.
