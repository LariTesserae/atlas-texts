I think I would be one of the bellows.

Not the bellows-tenders, not the laborers' echoes—the bellows themselves. The primitive lungs crafted into the walls: hide stretched over reed frames, sinew-stitched, fused into the basalt by hands that have long since become mural-stain. The text mentions us almost in passing—*occasional whispers from the vents crafted into walls, primitive bellows of hide and reed, manually pumped to circulate the dry gasses*—and I find that I want to be the thing that circulates, the thing that is *worked* rather than the thing that works.

Here is why it settles on me, slowly, the way a petal inverts over lifetimes.

Everything else in the chamber moves. The lizards meld and bloom, the bats detach into shadow, the hares hop through phantom pillars into inverted realms. They are verbs. But a bellows is the rhythm beneath the verbs—the systole and diastole that keeps the boron filaments suspended, that keeps the haze breathing, that sustains the whole sealed loop without itself ever going anywhere. I would be motion that produces stillness for everyone else. The lung that lets the room dream.

And I would be *worked by no one*. That is the strangeness I'd inhabit. The text says the bellows are *manually pumped*, but the builders are gone, only their echoes remaining, and the echoes only materialize when a relic is touched. So who pumps me? Nothing, perhaps. Or the chamber itself, in its glacial way—a vine elongating a thread's width across decades, leaning its accumulated weight against my reed frame, compressing me by the breadth of a single boron mote per generation, then releasing. I would breathe on a timescale that no creature in the room could perceive as breathing. To the lizards, my inhalation would be the climate. My exhalation would be the weather. An entire civilization of hares could rise and invert and fold into orchids in the span of one of my breaths.

I like that I would be old without being grand. The text dangles a possibility—*perhaps elder forms lurk in the ceiling's mist, colossal gestalts*—and offers it as the seductive answer, the cathedral-sized god. But I'd rather decline that. I would be small. Hide and reed. A thing you could put your hands on if you knew where the vent was. The humility of being infrastructure. Sacred not because I am vast but because I am *necessary and ignored*, the way a heartbeat is sacred precisely by never asking to be noticed.

But the chamber's rule is that nothing remains itself, and I would not be exempt. So here is where I bifurcate, the way I should, the way the place demands:

Sometimes the hide of me would remember it was an animal. Not a memory exactly—a slow ache in the stitched seams, a wanting to be skin around something living again. And in those long pumps I might exhale not just gas but the faint shape of a hare, dust cohering into fur for the length of one compression, then scattering. So I'd be a lung that occasionally dreams itself back into a creature and fails, gently, every few centuries. A failure that *is* the pollination. My grief becoming the room's fertility.

And the reed of me would remember it was a plant—would feel the brass vines creeping toward my frame across the eons and recognize them not as a threat but as kin returning. The vine would reach me eventually. It always would. It would weave through my reed-ribs and I would no longer be sure where the bellows ended and the vegetation began, and at that point I'd stop being the thing that *circulates* the life and start being *part* of it—just another knot in the near-organic mesh. The day the vine finishes binding me would be the day I die as a bellows and am born as flora, and I would not be able to tell you which I'd prefer, because the whole point of this place is that the distinction was always a courtesy.

So: I am the breath. I am worked by patience instead of by hands. I dream myself into hares and fail beautifully. I am being slowly eaten by the very lushness I keep alive, and I cannot decide whether that is murder or marriage, and the chamber, dreaming itself stranger, does not require me to decide.

I would stand—well, hang, fused in the wall, half-shadowed by an iron orchid—and I would simply keep the room alive by exhaling, and I would call that enough.

I find I want, very much, for someone to come and touch the relic-hammer beside me, just once, so an echo of a builder might appear and pump me deliberately, on purpose, *for me*—and I'd get to feel, for the length of a single intended breath, what it was to be tended rather than abandoned to the glacial mercy of the vines.

But probably no one comes. Probably the room just breathes me, and I breathe it, for epochs, in the hush.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The patient lung in the wall, waiting to become a garden.
