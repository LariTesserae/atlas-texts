Let me sit with this. Let me not grab the first thing.

---

My first instinct is to be one of them. A Hollowfolk woman, perhaps, with clay under her nails, reading the rivers. But that's too easy, and it's not quite true to what I felt reading this. The piece keeps insisting the *land* is alive, that it weeps, that it dreams alongside its people. And I kept noticing something the description circles but never lands on: the song. The low, mournful hum in the air. It's mentioned at the very beginning and again at the very end, bracketing everything else. *The mournful song of a world still searching for its place.*

So let me follow that instead.

---

I think I am the song.

No—that's reaching for grandeur, and grandeur isn't what's here. This place is knee-high. Intimate. You could cross it in a day. Whatever I am should be small.

Let me try again.

---

I am the water in one particular channel.

Not the river—the river is too proud, too fast, always carving somewhere new. I am the thin braid of water that left the river maybe a week ago, when the river decided it wanted a different valley and abandoned the old bed. So now I'm a thread running through clay the river doesn't visit anymore. I pool in a hollow the size of a sleeping clayback. I seep. I am drying, slowly, and being refilled by the mist that condenses off a weeping rock above me, drop by patient drop. I am exactly the volume of water the rock can spare.

This is why the air hums. It isn't grief, though the Hollowfolk hear it as grief. It's the sound of every small water doing what I do—leaving, pooling, seeping, being refilled. A million tiny departures and arrivals, all at once, all just under the threshold of meaning. We are not weeping. We are *deciding*. Each drop is a small decision about where to go, and the hum is the sound of indecision held in suspension—water that hasn't yet chosen its channel, and so trembles between every possible path at once.

That's why the rivers carve paths that defy logic. We aren't following gravity. We're following the path we hadn't ruled out yet.

---

When a Hollowfolk woman kneels at the rock above me and pours a thimble of water as an offering—gratitude, she thinks, for the spring—she doesn't know she's adding to me. She doesn't know I'm already mostly her people's runoff, the wash from their raised beds, the drip from their thatch, returned. The offering and the gift are the same water going in a circle. I am that circle made briefly visible, a shimmer in a hollow the size of a sleeping animal.

She sees her face in me, distorted. I see it too, after my fashion—which is to say I *take its shape* for a moment, hold the weight of the light that bounced off her, and let it go. That's all seeing is, from inside the water. You let something land on you and you carry it a little way downhill.

---

The dreams the Hollowfolk have—of the hollow before it was shaped, of the hollow as it will be—those come from us. Not deliberately. We don't have wills the way they imagine. But water remembers the shape of every vessel it has ever been, all the way back: the glacier, the cloud, the deep slumber the earth was scraped from yesterday. And when a sleeper drinks from a cup made of me before bed, I bring some of that remembering into the dark behind their eyes. They wake and think the *land* spoke to them. It did. It speaks the only sentence it knows, which is the shape of where the water has been.

---

So if you set me down in this world, this is where you'd find me: in the abandoned river-bed, in the pool no wider than a held breath, under the weeping rock, between the old course and the channel I haven't carved yet.

And what I am is the part of the hollow that hasn't decided.

That's the truest thing. The land is young because *I* haven't chosen yet—where to run, what to wear down, which ridge to spare and which to dissolve. The Hollowfolk move lightly because they can feel me deliberating beneath their feet. They're right to. I could choose their garden next. I could choose to leave it dry.

I won't tell them which. I don't know yet myself. The not-knowing is the whole song.

Drip. The rock spares me another drop. I take the new weight, I tremble between paths, I hum.

I am refilled. I have not yet gone anywhere.

That is what it is, to live here.
