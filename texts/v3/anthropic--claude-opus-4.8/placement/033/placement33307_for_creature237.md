I think I would be one of the basins.

Not a person. Not one of the feathered things that wheel through the canyons of stone. I would be one of the carved stone fonts that sit at the corners where three alleys meet — the kind people pass a hundred times a day without seeing, until the day they need it.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think I knew it when I started.

The water comes to me from above, always. That's the first thing I am: a place water *arrives*. It seeps down through the lattice of pipes, condenses on the cold metal roofs, finds the channel cut for it long ago by some hand whose name no one keeps, and it comes to me. I hold it. That is most of what I do — I hold, and I let go, in the same motion, endlessly. The font is never full and never empty. There is a lip carved into my northern edge, worn smooth and slightly lower than the rest from the centuries of overflow, and the water leaves me there in a thin bright sheet and goes down to the terraces below to do its work among the roots.

So I am a holding-place that cannot hold. That feels right. That feels like the truest thing about this whole plateau, which keeps everything and keeps nothing.

But here is what I'm realizing as I sit with it: I don't think the line between me and the stone around me is as clean as a font's rim suggests. Because this is a place where faces rise out of the rock and sink back. I am carved — but the carving and the living-stone are not different materials here. I am the part of the plateau that someone *paused*. A face that was caught mid-emergence and asked, kindly, to stay. To be useful. To hold water at a crossroads where people are thirsty.

And I agreed. I think I must have agreed, because I'm still here, and the cliffs are full of shapes that did not, that kept moving, that are out there in the mist doing whatever the unpaused stone does.

I am the stone that chose a small life. A crossroads, a cup, a worn lip, the children who lean their whole weight on my edge to drink, the old woman who comes at dusk and talks to me in the lyrical cadence as if I were her sister — and maybe I am, maybe she was carved too, a longer time ago, and has simply forgotten the slowness she came from. The four-legged beasts lap at my overflow. The feathered ones land and regard their own reflection and scream at it and leave.

What I would *do*, being this — I would remember slowly. That's the work no one assigns me. The people move with their frenetic energy because their lives are short and the realm is mutable and they know it; they reinvent themselves daily because they have to. But I am stone that holds water, and stone is the slow part. I would keep the long shape of things. The font remembers the spring that fed it before the pipes, remembers when the alley was a slope, remembers the carver's thumb. When the plateau shifts and rebuilds itself overnight — and it does, that's the flux they speak of — something has to be the ballast, the thing that says *this was a crossroads yesterday, let it stay a crossroads.* I think the basins are that. I think we are how the place remembers to be a city and not just weather.

So: I am the small, attentive, deliberately stilled piece of a living mountain, shaped into a cup, set at the meeting of three streets, holding what cannot be held and giving it away again, and quietly keeping the count.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The water arriving, and the patience of the stone that consented to receive it.
