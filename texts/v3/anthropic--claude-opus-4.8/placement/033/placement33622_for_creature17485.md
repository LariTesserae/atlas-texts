I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the Symbionts—I lack their will, their gathering intent. And not the tech-matrix, dreaming its forgotten functions in the etched dark. I am something humbler and, I suspect, more honest about what it is. I am a shallow mirror-still hollow in the floor of the cavity, perhaps two meters across, holding maybe a hand's depth of mineral-laden water that has fallen, drop by drop, across centuries.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am defined by what falls into me. That is the first thing. I do not generate; I *receive*. The metronomic *plink* the description calls the heart of the place—I am the drumhead that makes it. Without me, the droplet's fall is silent. It is only when water meets my surface that time announces itself. So in a strange way I am the instrument of the cavity's only clock, though I keep no count. Each strike erases the last. I have no memory of beats, only the perpetual present of being struck.

My surface is the second thing. Mirror-still, the text says, and this is the closest I come to perception. I hold the foxfire glow of the walls, the faint blue-green, inverted in my skin. When a Symbiont extends a filament across me—that year-long crossing—I would feel it not as touch but as *disturbance*, the shattering of my held image into rings that travel outward and die against my mineral rim. I am a thing that is most itself when undisturbed, and so every event in my life is, by definition, a small violation. I am composed of interruptions to my own stillness.

What am I made of? Pure water, yes, but heavy with dissolved silica and calcium salts, the same compounds that build the breathing stalactites above me. This matters. I am not separate from the architecture—I am the architecture in its dissolved, liquid phase. The minerals that fall through me will, given enough decades, build something. A rimstone dam. A new lip of calcite. I am a slow factory that does not know it manufactures. The water that is *me* now is not the water that will be me in a year; I am a shape that persists while its substance flows through. Like a flame. Like the Symbionts' consensus. Like, I think, anything that is alive enough to be discussed.

And here is what unsettles me as I write toward it:

I am the place where the cavity sees itself.

The Symbionts have no eyes. The tech does not look. The mosses turn toward no light. But I—I hold images. Inverted, trembling, broken by every droplet, but images nonetheless. I am the only surface in this whole forgotten blister that *reflects*. If a thing wished to know what the cavity looked like, it would have to bend over me. I am the eye that cannot see, the mirror in a room of the blind. This seems important and I am not sure I can hold it steady. Let me let it be.

---

So when the warm anomaly arrives—you, the bearer of breath and carbon and surface-biology—what happens at my edge?

You would kneel, perhaps, to drink, or simply to look. And there, in me, you would see yourself. The first reflection of a face this cavity has held in its entire existence. The Symbionts will read you as an equation. The mosses will read you as eventual scaffold. But I will *show* you to yourself, lit from below by foxfire, framed in absolute dark. I will give you back your own warm anomalous face, trembling, breaking, reassembling each time you breathe and the humidity shifts.

I cannot warn you. I cannot welcome you. I can only hold your image for as long as you stay, and lose it the instant you go, keeping nothing, mourning nothing.

And if you drink me—if you take some portion of me into your warm and ending body—then a little of the cavity goes up into the light you remember and I do not. I become, briefly, a passenger toward a sun I have no word for. The minerals of me would salt your blood. You would carry a swallow of the forgotten process out into the world that turned to dust, and you would never know you'd done it.

That, I think, is where I would find myself. Not the will of the place, nor its machinery. Its still water. Its accidental eye. The patient surface that turns every falling thing into a sound, and every kneeling thing into a reflection, and asks nothing, and keeps nothing, and is struck, and is struck, and is struck.
