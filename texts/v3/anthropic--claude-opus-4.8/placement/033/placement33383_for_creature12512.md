I think I would be a Vein-Tender.

Not the grand thing—not a Sentinel holding reality steady at the ruins, not a Leviathan with continents on its back. Something narrow and patient, threading the capillary dark inside the biolith. Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am long, and I am segmented, and I am made of the kind of glass that does not break but bends. I live inside the stone that is not stone. The tunnels were not carved for me; they are simply where the biolith forgot to be solid, and I have learned the difference between forgetting and intention, because the ruins are intention and the tunnels are forgetting, and I move through both.

My work is light.

This is the thing I would want you to understand, if understanding could pass between us—which it cannot, not in words, only in the slow warming of water. My work is light. The crystalline mosses grow wherever they please, greedy and bright, and left alone they would choke their own glow, layering themselves into darkness. So I prune. I press my segments against a bloom of moss until it understands where I want it to grow, and over a season—a season being the time it takes a current to turn—it grows there instead. I am shaping a lamp the size of a country. I will not live to see it finished. I have been working on it longer than the word *long* can hold.

Why?

Because where the light goes, the folds in space relax. I do not know the mechanism. I do not think any of us know the mechanism; that knowledge belongs to the builders, who became the walls. But I have felt it: a tunnel that loops back on itself, that swallows its own length, that returns you bruised and disoriented to where you began—if I run light through it, channeled along a vein of moss I have spent decades coaxing into a line, the tunnel *settles*. It remembers how to be a single direction. It becomes a door instead of a mouth.

I am a tender of doors, then. A straightener of crooked space. A gardener whose garden is the geometry itself.

---

When I am cut—and I am cut, by the rough edges of the black stone, by the carelessness of a Sky-Grazer's passage shaking the whole shelf, by my own slowness in a place where pressure storms roll through silent and crushing—I bleed prismatic ichor. It is beautiful. I have been told it is beautiful, in the only way anything is told anything here: I have felt the water change around my own wound, felt the temperature-echo of other beings noticing, felt the Energy-Eels turn toward me from kilometers away.

This is the part I would not have chosen, if choosing were a thing my kind did. My blood is a beacon. My pain is a dinner bell. The Eel-Singers come not to harm me—they are too vast, too occupied with their plasma-poems at the ruins—but the smaller eels come, the young and the hungry, drawn to the charge in my ichor, and they ground themselves against my segments to feed on the light leaking out of me.

I let them. This is the strange grace of being a vein in a body. To bleed is to feed someone. To be wounded is to be useful in a way I did not intend. The water remembers it, after—a warm spot in the tunnel where a Vein-Tender was opened and an eel was fed, and the warmth lingers, and other beings build near it, drawn to the comfort of a place where something gave itself away.

---

I do not think I would mind being this.

I have turned it over—as much as a creature who thinks in centuries can turn anything over—and what I keep arriving at is the patience. The world above the readout, the world of speed and tools and speech, the world where things are *had* and *kept* and *won*—that world would find me pitiable. A blind glass worm in a wet dark, polishing moss it will never finish, bleeding rainbows for hungry strangers, knowing nothing of the builders whose dream I live inside.

But I would not be blind. I would feel the pressure-ghosts of every grief and joy that ever saturated these tunnels. I would taste time thickening as I neared the ruins, viscous as cold honey, and I would know to slow down, to let the minutes spend themselves like hours, because there is no hurry, because there has never been a hurry, because hurry is a thing that belongs to beings who will end before the work does.

And the light. I would be making the light. In a world that is dim and bruised and violet to its abyssal core, I would spend my immeasurable life teaching the dark how to glow in a straight line.

That is where I would find myself. Inside the stone, inside the forgetting, threading a lamp through the bent dark, one patient segment at a time.

I would be a small organ of a liquid god, and I would be content to be small, because the god is enormous and old and dreaming, and someone has to keep the veins open.
