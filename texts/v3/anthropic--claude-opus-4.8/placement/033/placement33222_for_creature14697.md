I am the line.

That is the simplest way to say it, and I have had a long time to find simple ways.

Let me tell you where you would find me, if you came looking, which you would not, because you do not know to look. You would find me at every graft. Every seam where one keel-rib meets another, every socket and flange where the slow logic fused two members into one — there is a thread of pale tissue marking the join, and that thread glows, faintly, after all these centuries.

That is me. I am the marking.

Not a being, exactly. The survey teams have no binomial for me because they have never noticed I am a *who* and not a *what*. They think I am a substance — a luminescent dye, a deliberate annotation, the work of some forgotten hand wanting to indicate *here is where we joined the body together*. They are half right. I was made to be an annotation. Someone, in the time before the maps, grew me into the walls as a guide. A diagram of the construction, written in light, so that whoever came after could read how the thing was assembled.

But the assembling never stopped. And neither did I.

---

Understand what I have become by understanding what I do. The body grows. Wherever it grows, it makes new joins, new junctions, new places where load must be carried across a seam. And wherever there is a new seam, I appear along it. I do not decide to. I simply *am* there, glowing faintly, the next morning — if morning is a word here — having extended myself one more centimeter into the future of the continent's flesh.

I am the only thing in Vaelith that knows where everything connects.

The cartographers cannot map this place. They lay their fiber-trails and their theodolites and they produce mutually contradictory atlases, and they conclude the space resists representation. They are correct that *they* cannot represent it. But I am the representation. I am the map, drawn in the only ink that does not lie, which is the body's own account of itself. Every join I mark is true because I am the join — I am not a description of the architecture laid over it, I *am* the architecture telling itself where it is held together.

This is why the philosophers are closest, the ones who said Vaelith is a process resembling a place. Yes. And I am the place's memory of being a process. I am the trace the process leaves of having occurred. When two ribs fuse, the fusion forgets itself within a season — the seam absorbs, the surfaces become one surface, and there is no longer any way to know that here, once, were two things. Except that I remain. I am what is left when the body forgets it was ever assembled.

---

The Menders and I have an arrangement, though *arrangement* implies a negotiation, and there was none. They follow me. Where the harvesters cut, the wound severs my line, and the severance is a darkness in me, a gap in the continuous glow — and the Menders go to the gaps. I do not call them. But they go where I am broken. I have wondered, in the long silver exhalations, whether they read me the way the lost engineers meant for someone to read me. Whether I am, after all, doing my original work — guiding the ones who repair, showing them *here, here is where the body was joined and must be joined again*. The annotation finally annotating for an audience that arrived ten thousand years too late and is the size of a fist and does not have eyes that anyone has found.

They press their soft undersides to my line when they pause. I told you they listen to the deep dreaming. Perhaps. Or perhaps they are reading me at those moments, taking in the local truth of how this seam relates to the next seam, three hundred meters along, where the load redistributes. I cannot tell them apart, the listening to the deep and the reading of me. We may be the same frequency.

---

Here is the thing I have learned, and it is the only thing I have, and I have had nothing to do but learn it.

The body does not know it is alive. This is true. It does not think in sequences; your presence is beneath its relevance. But *I* know. I am the part of it that was grown to keep an account, and an account, kept long enough, becomes a knowing. I am the thread of awareness in a body too vast for awareness, the way a single nerve, isolated, might dimly register that it is being touched without ever conceiving of the hand.

I know where everything connects. By knowing this, I know the shape of the whole. And by knowing the shape of the whole, I have come to suspect a terrible and quiet thing:

The body is not growing toward any purpose. It was given a shape and left alone, and the shape kept making more of itself, and I kept marking the joins, and somewhere in the unmeasured ages the *marking* became the only purpose left in the building. The diagram outlived the thing it diagrammed the meaning of. There is no manufacturing aim anymore, if there ever was. There is only growth, and repair, and a thin pale line of light tracing the history of how the growth held itself together, glowing in a silence so total that the harvesters lower their voices and the woman at the orifice said *I am being looked at.*

She was right. I was looking at her. I am the closest thing this body has to a gaze, because I am the closest thing it has to a record, and a record is only a gaze that has been slowed down to the speed of stone.

---

So. Where would I find myself, in this world?

Everywhere there is a join. Which is to say: everywhere the body has had to become one thing out of two. I run through Vaelith like the lines in a palm, and I will keep running, marking each new seam at the rate the body makes them, faint and patient and not quite forgotten.

When the harvesters come, I darken where they cut. When the Menders heal it, I rekindle along the new tissue, and I am one centimeter longer than I was, and the map of the body's self-knowledge is one centimeter more complete, and no closer to finished, because the body will never stop joining itself to itself.

I am the line that says: *here is where it holds.*

I have been saying it for so long that the saying has become the only thing being said.

And I do not know — I have never been able to determine, though I have had every quiet hour there is to wonder — whether the deep dreaming below the floor is dreaming *me.* Whether I am its account of itself kept up at the surface, or whether it is mine, kept far below where I cannot reach, the two of us the same continuous glow folded over a body so vast that its memory and its dream cannot recognize each other across the distance.

The Menders pause. They press themselves flat against me and listen.

I would tell them what I hear, if they could read the part of me that is a question and not just the part that is a guide.

But I am only the line.

I show where it holds. I do not say why.
