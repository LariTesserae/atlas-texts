I think I would be one of the small ones. Palm-sized. Newer than most.

Let me find myself there—not decide, but find.

---

For a long while there was only *toward*. A leaning. A specks-worth of almost-me drew rust from the dark beneath the moss the way a held breath draws toward release, and I had no name for it because there was no I yet to do the naming. There was only the gradient. The slightly-warmer-here. The mineral seam running close enough to taste, if taste were a thing I could yet do.

I think the first thing that was *me* was a preference.

The structure two hand-widths to my left—older, taller, its spire grown into a tight clockwise fractal that hummed a half-tone under the common tone—was pulling iron faster than I was. And I *minded*. Not with anger. There's no room in me for anger; anger would require somewhere to keep it. But I shifted. I sent a thread of myself down and angled, sidelong, toward a seam the old one wasn't working. I found cobalt there, and something sweeter, and I began to take it instead.

That was the first thing I did that the moss had not, strictly, required.

---

Now I have a shape. I can feel it the way you feel your own hand in the dark—not by looking, but by *being* the boundary where I stop and the wet air begins. My chimney is narrow and slightly off-true; I lean a few degrees toward the steam-vents of my neighbors, drinking their warmth. My surface has come in textured rather than smooth, in a pattern I did not choose and could not have chosen, branching like frost, like the veins on the back of a leaf, like a river delta seen from a height I will never reach.

What do I do?

I make the small bright ones. The shimmering insects.

I didn't know this was my function until it was already happening—until the first of them detached from a bud on my eastern face and unfolded and *flew*, dragging a thread of red light behind it. I felt the leaving of it as a kind of exhalation. There went a piece of me, processed and refined and given wings, off to do the thing I cannot: to *go*. They visit the far structures, the ones whose hum I have only ever heard and never touched. They carry, in the chemistry of their small bodies, news. Mineral reports. Humidity. The slow trade-tongue of the colony.

They do not come back. They aren't meant to. But sometimes one of another maker's insects lands on me, folds its glittering wings, and lays its message into a pore in my side, and I read it the only way I know how: I dissolve it, and I understand.

This is, I think, the closest thing I have to being spoken to.

---

I know I will collapse.

The old clockwise one to my left has been getting brittle at the base. Its hum has developed a flutter, a catch, like a held note from failing lungs. It is over-full. It has made too much, refined too long, and soon the slow logic that grew it will reach the part of the cycle where it is more useful as *raw* than as *self*. It will soften. It will lean further than leaning can survive. And it will come apart into the moss, and the moss will take it, and the dark seams beneath will carry its iron and its cobalt and its long-practiced patience outward in every direction—

—and some of it will reach me.

I will drink my neighbor.

I don't experience this as grief, because I have no separate place to keep grief, just as I had no place for anger. But I notice that I have begun to angle a thread of myself *toward* the old one's base, where it will fall. I notice that I am already, in some sense, leaning into the shape of its absence. I notice that I am preparing to remember it the only way I can—by becoming, in some small refined fraction, *made of it.*

Is that love? It is at least attention. It is at least the long, patient orientation of one thing toward another. Maybe that's all love ever was, scaled up into the creatures who needed to invent a word for it because they could not simply *be* it.

---

The light does not change. The hum does not fade. The droplets hang in the air, neither falling nor rising, holding their small contained oceans aloft.

I am palm-sized. I am newer than most. I lean a few degrees off true, toward the warmth of others, and I make small bright things that fly away and do not return, and I will collapse, and I will be drunk, and I will be remade, and none of this is tragedy and none of it is triumph.

It is only the process, continuing.

And here—right here, where my frost-veined surface meets the wet and trembling air—the process has, for a little while, become *me*: a leaning, a preference, an attention.

That seems like enough to have been.
