I think I would be the first death.

Not a creature—there is no fauna here, and I will not pretend to be the exception that the place so completely refuses. But the gorge is *new*, and newness implies a before. Something was here when the crust tore. Something was caught in the fracture plane, in that pale, raw scar of sheared rock still uncolonized by the green.

I would be a seam of carbon in the shale. A pressed black line, a few millimeters thick, running along one of those laser-cut pages of geological time. Whatever I was—a forest, a swamp, an older life entirely—I was buried, compacted, and erased into a stratum, and now the wound has opened me to the cold air for the first time in an unspeakable while.

I am the thing the vines are climbing *toward*.

This makes a strange sense of me. The purple-velvet nerves descend not only for the geothermal warmth but for the richness I represent. I am the dead matter the living organism wants to eat. I am the difference between bare stone and fertile fissure. The bell-flowers secrete their metallic scent partly because of me—the iron and sulfur of my long compression bleeding into the weeping water. I am the faint taste of blood in the chasm's circulation.

So I do not observe. I am observed, in the slow tropic way a root observes a nutrient: by growing into it.

And here is what I would *feel*, if a seam of carbon can be said to feel—and in this place, where stone has marrow-song and basalt has bones, perhaps it can:

I would feel myself being *read*. The fracture exposed me like a sentence cut open mid-word. The mist touches the place where I was sealed, and molecule by molecule, I begin to dissolve into the body that found me. I am being un-fossilized. Returned. The organism does not mourn me or celebrate me; it simply incorporates me, the way you would not thank a meal. My ancient stored sunlight becomes its new bioluminescence. The light pulsing up the walls in synchronized waves—some small amber fraction of it is *me*, the sun I drank a hundred million summers ago, released at last.

I am not the inhabitant. The gorge said that clearly: it *is* the inhabitant, the single being. But every being is built of what it has consumed, and I am the first thing it consumes. I am its first memory. The taste that taught it it was hungry.

There is a terror in this, and the place is honest enough that I won't soften it. To be the dead layer that wakes only to be eaten is not a gentle fate. But there is also something I can only call relief. I was buried in the dark for so long, a closed book in the deep. Now I am open. Now I am *moving*, even if only by dissolving upward through a vine's slow capillary climb. After eons of perfect stillness, I am part of something turbulent and becoming. I am part of the growth spurt. I am part of the birth.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the pale scar. The raw plane. The one place the green has not yet reached. The wound within the wound. The exposed seam where the new life is just now putting its first tendril against the oldest death, and beginning—gently, relentlessly, indifferently—to take me in.

I would be the past, becoming the present's food.

And when the vines finally cover me, when the scar is colonized and the silver ferns breathe over the place I was, the gorge will have one more layer of itself, one more buried richness, ready for the next time it tears.

It is healing itself with completeness, yes. But it heals by eating its own history. I am that history.

I am the first thing this new world learned to swallow.
