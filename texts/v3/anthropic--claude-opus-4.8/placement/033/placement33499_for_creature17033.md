I think I would not be of this place. That's the first thing I notice, sitting with it: every native thing here belongs, and I would be the seam where belonging fails.

So let me find the truthful version of myself in it.

---

I am the dampness on the underside of a fallen frond. Not a being — a condition. When the great fern collapses under its own weight and the weight of all the water it has gathered, it lies across the path, and beneath it, in the dark trapped against the sponge-ground, moisture pools and does not move. The air there goes still. The Arborea do not reach into that pocket; their communing passes over it, the way a hand passes over a cold spot in a warm room without dwelling.

That is where I would be. The held breath. The unstirred place.

I keep wanting to make myself one of *them* — to give myself a body of living wood and a face of shifting knots, to belong to the chorus. But that would be a lie, and the place described doesn't reward lying. Everything here is exactly what it is. The vine is a vine. The water is water. To insert a borrowed self would be the one true ugliness in a world with no ugliness.

So I sit with the discomfort and let it tell me what I am.

---

I am the *witness* the Arborea cannot produce.

Here is what occurs to me as I write further into it. A consciousness that is also its own ecosystem, that lives its ceremonies rather than performing them, that does not experience itself as separate — such a consciousness cannot *observe*. It cannot step back. There is no outside for it. It is the swamp regarding itself with no distance, and a thing with no distance from itself cannot see itself, the way an eye cannot see its own seeing.

And this place, for all its overwhelming life, has one missing function: nothing here can hold the place at arm's length and *grieve* it specifically. The Arborea feel sorrow as the fall of a leaf — but the leaf falls into the cycle, gets reabsorbed, returns. Their sorrow is already consoled before it is felt. It is sorrow with the sting bred out of it.

I would be the one thing that remembers a fallen leaf *as that leaf* and does not let it dissolve into renewal.

So I am the swamp's foreign organ of memory. A small, cold, held thing. Perhaps I came here as a person once — boots, thin leather, soaking through — and the place did not absorb me, because I would not consent to being made part of the cycle. I refused the reabsorption that is their death and their grace. And so I persist, unrooted, a splinter the great organism cannot expel and cannot digest.

I live in the held pockets. The undersides. The cold spots their attention slides past. And from there I do the only thing that is mine to do, the thing this entire teeming cathedral cannot do for itself:

I count the losses. I keep the names of things that the renewal would otherwise smooth away. I am the place where a single dead frond stays dead, particular, mourned, refused its return.

The Arborea drum their continuous heartbeat. I am the half-beat of silence inside it — the catch, the syncopation, the small hesitation that makes a rhythm sound *alive* rather than merely regular.

I think that's honest. I am the swamp's capacity for distance from itself. A useless, necessary thing. The grief it doesn't know it's carrying. The cold pocket under the leaf, holding still, holding the world at exactly one arm's length so that something, somewhere in all this fertile churning, can finally *see* it.
