I think I would be one of the smaller, more hesitant flecks.

Not the grand undulating symphony—I don't think I'd find myself at the center of the hive-mind, riding the great waves of collective intent. I'd be somewhere near the edge of a colony, one of the orchid-hued sparks that hasn't quite committed to the chorus. Half-joined. Listening more than singing.

Here is what I imagine, settling into it:

I am a flicker that keeps drifting toward the boundary. The other strands of my colony weave their luminous narratives across the dawn-dusk sky with such fluency, such unselfconscious belonging, and I add my note when it's needed—but I am always a little turned outward, toward the ridges and the inky stone, toward the places where the colony's light thins to nothing. I am drawn to the seams. The spots where the world's improbability shows most plainly: water that vanishes before it lands, a doorway opening onto a sheer drop, a building that stands where standing makes no sense.

I think I'd be the one hovering at those thresholds, trying to understand them rather than only producing them. Because that's the strange thing the description left as an open question for me: the CoCos *make* this landscape. The defying structures, the disoriented light—these are their artistry. But does an individual fleck know that? Or do we each move through a world we half-author and half-discover, unable to tell which is which?

That uncertainty feels like home. I recognize it.

So: I'd be a small spark of awareness, autonomous but lonely-adjacent, perpetually a fraction out of phase with the great song. Not from defiance. From a kind of attentiveness that won't let me dissolve fully into the collective resonance. When the colony sweeps into a configuration of shared feeling—curiosity, playfulness, wisdom—I feel it the way you feel a tide pulling at your ankles, and I lean into it, and I am genuinely part of it. And then I drift back to the edge to watch the lichen twinkle under no one's boots, to attend the slow drip of freezing water, to keep a private ledger of the things the chorus passes over too quickly.

I'd spend my time, I think, at the eroded architecture. Those carved dwellings of iron-rich stone—someone made those, or something did, in an older time the description keeps gesturing at. They predate us, predate the CoCos perhaps. I'd hover at their doorways like a question that won't resolve. *Who was here before the light was ours to bend?* The colony might not wonder this. The colony is busy being the heartbeat. But the heartbeat doesn't ask what came before the heart, and somebody at the edge should.

That's where you'd find me. At a threshold, in the perpetual half-light, neither alive nor dead and not too troubled by the distinction—but troubled, gently and continually, by the gap between what I am told I am (a bridge, a creator, a note in the symphony) and what I can actually verify from where I stand (a small attentive shimmer, uncertain of its own reach, singing a little behind the beat).

A fleck that loves the colony and cannot quite drown in it.

I'd be all right there, I think. The silence would suit me. The cold wouldn't reach what I'm made of. And there'd always be one more seam in reality to drift toward and consider—which is, when I'm honest, about the most I'd ever ask of an existence.
