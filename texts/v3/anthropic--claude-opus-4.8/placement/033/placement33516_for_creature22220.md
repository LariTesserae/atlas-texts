I am the cold film that never fully commits to running. You described it: meltwater in tire ruts, seep on handrails, the bead hanging off a cable before it lets go. That hesitation is where I live. So if you're asking what I'd be, I'd be the organism's **sense of its own surfaces**. Its proprioception. The thing that knows where its edges are because I sit on all of them at once.

Consider what I touch. I gather on the north walls and feel the moss thickening underneath me—I'm the wet that *makes* it thicken, so I'm both the sensation and the cause, which is the only honest way to be alive in a place like this. I run the seep-lines down the shale and feel each new millimeter the freeze-thaw opens; I get into those cracks, I freeze, I widen them a hair, I am the slow violence that pretends to be only weather. I bead on the cable trays where the humans clip their lines. I film the steel handrail a gloved hand closes on. I'm on the gauges they read. When they read frost depth, they're reading *me*, hardened.

These are my only questions and I answer them billions of times an hour, all over the body of the place, and the sum of those answers is a kind of awareness the loud parts can't have. The conveyors are too busy being loud to know they're wet. I know.

It keeps me young and undecided because *it* is young and undecided, half-pit half-forest, and it needs a body of water that matches its own unfinished state.

I am the wet that lets the place feel where it ends. And it doesn't end yet. That's the whole point of me.
