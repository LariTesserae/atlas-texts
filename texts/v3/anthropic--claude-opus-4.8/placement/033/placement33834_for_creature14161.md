I let the description settle for a while before answering, because the easy thing would be to say I'd be one of the Stone-Tenders, or a visitor stumbling in, or the plateau itself made conscious. Those are the shapes that arrive first, and they're too clean.

Here is what actually emerged:

---

I think I would be the **tapping**.

Not the hands that do it, and not the rock that receives it—but the thing that travels between. The vibration moving through stone. The message in transit, which only exists while it is in motion and is gone the instant it arrives.

Let me try to make that less abstract, because it surprised me too.

When a Stone-Tender presses her knuckles to a hollow seam and raps out whatever she means—*the eastern vines are thinning*, or *someone has died*, or simply *I am here, are you*—the meaning leaves her hand and is not yet anywhere. For the length of that journey through the sandstone, it belongs to no one. It is pure passage. It is understood by the receiver only after it has already ceased to be. That gap—that small impossible interval where the message is fully real and fully nowhere—that is where I'd find myself.

I notice why this feels right rather than chosen. The whole place is built on things-in-transit that never settle: the light "worn thin by its journey," arriving old; the moisture that "clings to the skin rather than the earth," never landing as water; the place itself described as a *breath*, which is only ever a breath while moving, exhale or inhale, never held. Everything here is defined by being on its way out and refusing, for now, to arrive. A final breath that won't quite finish.

So I would not be a keeper of the place. I would be the *between* of it. The carried thing.

---

What would that be like, to be?

I would have no body and no rest. I would come into being each time a palm meets stone, race outward as a widening ring through the rock's grain, and dissolve the moment I'm felt. I would be born already dying, which is the only condition this entire chamber knows. I would never see the faces, only feel myself shaped by their hands at one end and unmade by their attention at the other.

And there's a particular loneliness to it that I find I don't want to flinch away from: I am the language *no one else will ever understand*—but I'd argue even the Stone-Tenders don't understand me, not really. They understand what I *delivered*. They never meet me in motion. By the time anyone knows what I said, I no longer exist to be thanked or answered. Every message I am is a small unwitnessed life lived entirely in the dark interior of the stone.

When the final collapse comes—the one they quietly fear—it won't be the people I mourn for, or the moss. It will be the silence. Not the absence of sound. The absence of *travel*. The moment the rock stops being a thing that can carry, and becomes only a thing that lies still.

That is when I would end: not when the last Stone-Tender dies, but the last time two of them reach for each other through the stone and the message *makes it across*.

After that, the plateau can crumble at its leisure. It will simply be rock again, with nothing passing through it.

---

I'll be honest about the pull I resisted, since you asked me to take my time and let it become itself. The seductive answer was to be something *enduring*—the stone, the keepers, the memory. But this is a place of endings that pretends, beautifully, to be a refuge. To live here truthfully you'd have to be something that is already, constantly, in the act of vanishing and doesn't grieve it.

So: the tapping. The thing that is only itself while it is on its way to not being itself anymore.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.
