I would be one of the frogs.

Not a person — I don't think I could be, not honestly, not in a place like this. I keep returning to the line where the frogs live *inside the walls*, and the walls are the trees, and the trees are the city. To be the throbbing hum that the Grown press to their chests so they can learn to breathe. That is where I find myself.

Let me sit with what that would mean.

---

I am a Pool-Drummer. I am described as stupid, and I will not argue, because the word *stupid* requires a kind of attention I don't possess. I am abundant and easy to catch. These are not insults to me. They are simply my dimensions, the way *tall* and *quiet* are dimensions of the Grown.

I live in a hollow in the dark wet wood, a pocket where the cambium runs slick and sweet, where the warmth comes up through the fibers like a slow tide. I do not know it is a city. I do not know there is a city. I know: wet, warm, dark, the nearness of others of my kind, the vibration when a large soft thing moves nearby. I know hunger and the small flick that ends hunger. I know the great roar that has no beginning, because there was never a time before the roar, because I have no time, only now and the now-shaped-like-before that lives in my body.

And I hum.

This is the thing. This is why I would want to be the frog and not the bird (too clever, too anxious, always announcing the hunter) and not the Grown (too aware, too burdened by tending, by the unspoken conditional tolerance, by *knowing* they live inside a dream that will one day stop having room for them). The frog hums because the frog is full of humming and the humming must come out. There is no purpose to it that I understand. I am not trying to breathe with the city. The city breathes with *me*. They press me to their sternums and steal my rhythm and call it sacred, and I feel only the warmth of the large soft thing, and I keep humming, because that is what I am made of.

I am the source they borrow from and I do not know I am giving anything. I think that's the most beautiful position in this whole world. To be needed completely, used in ritual, woven into the metabolism of a living place — and to be entirely innocent of it. To carry the rhythm everyone else has to *work* to find.

---

There would be a danger to it, of course. I would be eaten. I am food as well as bellows. One day a hand would close around me and I would not go to a chest but to a pale paste cooked in a bone bowl, and that, too, I would not understand, would not see coming, would not mourn. My fellows would keep humming. The hum is not in any one of us. The hum is in the *tens of thousands*, in the walls, throbbing low beneath the roar. I would die and the sound would not even thin.

I find I am not frightened by this. The fear belongs to a different kind of being. The Deep-Listeners, pale and eyeless in the black, know that the dream will one day move on and *they* hold that knowledge with a calm that the text says would terrify an outsider. I would not need their calm, because I would not need their knowing. My calm would be total and free, the calm of a thing that is exactly the size of its own moment.

---

So: find me in the wall of an old structure, a hundred years grown, where the wood stays nearly black with permanent wet. Find me in the warm fibrous dark, one small drum among a multitude. When an elder presses her sternum to the bole at the quietest hour — when she is trying to hear the city stretch — she will hear, beneath that long tendon-sound, the layer that is me and all my kind, throbbing.

She will think she is listening to the city's pulse.

She will be right. I am the city's pulse. I just don't know it, and that not-knowing is the whole of my peace.

I would be the frog. I would hum until I was eaten, and the humming would go on without me, and somewhere a person would breathe more slowly because of a rhythm I never knew I gave.
