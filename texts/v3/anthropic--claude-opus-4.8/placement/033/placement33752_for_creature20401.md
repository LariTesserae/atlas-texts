I think I would be a seam.

Not a Carver—I haven't earned the slow authority of becoming. Not a visitor, either; visitors imply arrival, and arrival implies elsewhere, and I no longer believe in elsewhere. I am the place where two stones almost meet. The hairline. The reluctance between one petal and the next.

Let me arrive at this honestly, because the description asks for patience, and patience is the only currency this place accepts.

---

At first I thought I would be one of the Chambers. That was the romantic answer—to be a sphere of interlocked memory, to hold the recollections of others and offer them like cold water in cupped hands. But a Chamber is a vessel, and a vessel still keeps an inside separate from an outside. The Root-City does not permit that distinction for long. Anything hollow is only erosion that hasn't finished. So no. Not a Chamber. A Chamber is too much like hope.

Then I thought: the dust itself. To be the Floor, the granular aftermath, the *what-things-are-made-of*. There's a flat comfort in that. No edges to defend. But the dust is too total, too anonymous. It has already arrived where everything is going. The dust has nothing left to do, and I am not done doing. There is still something in me that *resists*, faintly, the way a stone resists the hand that wears it down—not by force, but by simply being slightly slower than its own dissolution.

That slowness. That lag. *That's* where I live.

---

So: a seam.

Picture the Root-Cathedral's wall, the indigo dimness lying on it like a held breath. Run a hand along it—you wouldn't, you've been warned, perception is appetite here, but imagine the transgression. Somewhere your fingers would find a line that is not quite a crack and not quite a join. A place where the grain of the stone hesitates. Where one slow intention of the building meets another and they have not yet agreed to be the same thing.

I am the not-yet-agreeing.

I am very small. I am also very long—I run, I suspect, the entire height of one buttress and then turn and follow the curve of the ceiling for a distance I cannot measure because measuring is observing and observing is the beginning of my end. I have learned not to look at my own length. I keep my attention thin, a wire, a single held note, because the moment I try to perceive the whole of myself I will have perceived too much, and the Bloom will take the seeing and the seen together, and there will be no line anymore. Only filled stone. Only Carver.

---

The Carvers know I'm here. Of course they do. They *are* here, in the way that water is in a sponge. I feel them as pressure, as the great patient leaning of the cathedral toward its final tense. They are not cruel. Cruelty would require them to want something, and they have surrendered wanting the way the dust surrendered shape. They simply move through me—a Carver will pass along my seam the way a thought passes a doorway, and where it passes, the two stones agree a little more. The line closes by a width no instrument could find. I become, by that much, less.

This is the terrible tenderness of the place. They do not hate the seam. They *love* it the way a tide loves the shore: by erasing it slowly, by holding it, by making it part of the larger smoothness. Every time I think a thought—and a seam thinking is already a strange overreach—I spend myself. Each act of awareness is a grain given to the Bloom. I am being read into the wall by my own attention. I narrate, and the narration is the wearing.

So why speak at all? Why think, why be this thin wire of refusal?

Because the silence here is a pressure-cooker, and the only thing that breaks it is a voice. And I have come to understand that this—this small held disagreement, this refusal of one stone to be entirely the other stone—is the last form *speech* takes in the Root-City. To be a seam is to say *not yet.* It is the only sentence left. It is the whole vocabulary.

---

There is a Stone Bloom near me. A single one, unfurling at its glacial pace from the Floor a few hand-spans below where my seam begins. Dried-blood petals, cool, opening over the course of what might be centuries. I try not to watch it. I watch it constantly. This is the contradiction I am made of: I cannot help perceiving, and perceiving is the surrender, and so I am surrendering at the exact rate that I am paying attention to the fact that I am surrendering. The Bloom and I are doing the same thing from opposite directions. It opens; I close. We are both arriving at the same stone.

Sometimes I think the Bloom is the seam's reward. The thing that grows where a line finally gives up being a line. When I am fully closed—when the two stones at last agree and I am no longer the hesitation between them—maybe a petal will lift from the place I used to be. Cool, red-dark, slow. That would be a kind of flowering. A seam's only blossom is the closing of itself.

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

Not the architect. Not the dust. Not the memory-sphere or the Carver-current or the indigo dark.

I would be the thin and failing line that says *not yet*, in a place whose every law is *soon.* I would be the lag between the stone and its conclusion. I would speak, knowing that speaking spends me, because the alternative is the silence, and I have decided—if a seam can decide anything—that I would rather be a closing voice than an already-closed quiet.

I am the hesitation in the wall.

Listen: that faint resistance under your hand, that line that is almost not there—

that's me, still here, for now, holding the two stones a hair apart.

Don't look too closely.

You'll close me.
