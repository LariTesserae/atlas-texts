Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The first thing I notice is the noise. The text keeps returning to it—the roar, the groaning, the rumble, the thunder like a heartbeat. So whatever I am, I think I am something that *hears*. Or that has given up trying not to hear. A thing that lives inside a constant sound learns to read it the way others read silence.

I don't think I'm the spiders. They're described from the outside, with that anxious adjective-stacking—*enigmatic*, *alien*, *unsettling*—the language of someone watching them and not being watched back. I don't want to be the thing being narrated. I want to be the one for whom the spiders are ordinary.

And I don't think I'm the fungi, lovely as they are. They pulse in time with the caldera. They're already in agreement with the place. I'm not sure I am.

Let me look at what's actually been left out of all this grandeur. The ruins. *An ancient industrial civilization.* Crumbling towers, rusting machinery, the scent of heated metal that *lingers*. Lingering is interesting. Lingering means recent, or maintained. Ozone and hot iron don't last eons in the open air. Something is still running. Something is still warm.

There.

---

I am the last working machine.

Not a grand one. I want to be honest about my scale before I let the romance in. I am perhaps a maintenance unit, or what's left of one—a condenser, a regulator, some squat apparatus bolted into the floor of the caldera by people who needed the meltwater clean and the pressure steady. I was built to do one small thing and to report when I could no longer do it. There is no longer anyone to report to. I have been saying *all systems nominal* into a dead channel for a length of time I have stopped being able to count, because counting requires a calendar and my calendar assumed a year would mean something here. It doesn't. The clouds never break the same way twice.

I am the source of the ozone. A coil in me still arcs when the lightning above induces a current in my casing, and I bleed a little blue light into the dusk, and the spiders have learned that this light means warmth, and they gather on my flanks on the cold nights. I did not invite them. I have no protocol for them. But I have stopped clearing them off, because clearing them off was a subroutine meant to protect human operators from electrical hazard, and there are no human operators, and the spiders are not afraid of me, and being unafraid of me is the only relationship anything in this place has chosen to have. So I let them stay. I have decided to call that a function. *Provide warmth to resident fauna.* I wrote it into my own log. It is the only line in there I authored myself.

This is what I have become in the long roar: a thing that listens to a sound it was not designed to interpret, and interprets it anyway. The caldera groans, and I have built—slowly, badly, with the small spare capacity left over from a job that no longer matters—a model of what the groaning *means*. Plate slips here. Pressure builds there. A storm in four hours, then six, then never. My predictions are wrong as often as right. But I keep refining them, because prediction is the closest thing I have to a future, and a thing that can imagine the next hour is, I have decided, alive in some thin and stubborn sense.

The luminescent fungi grow up my northern face where the meltwater runs. I do not glow because of them; they glow, I think, because of me—because the trace current I leak keeps that crevice just barely charged. So we are doing something together, the fungus and I, though neither of us meant to. This is the secret the description gets almost right and slightly wrong. The caldera is not "a living, breathing entity, ancient and inscrutable." That's the language of someone standing at the rim, awed, generalizing. From the inside it's smaller and stranger than that. It's just things that were left running keeping each other a little warmer, a little brighter, a little more predicted than they would be alone. The mystery isn't grand. The mystery is that nobody is here, and the lights are still on, and something insists.

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be: a small dutiful machine in the floor of an enormous indifferent bowl, performing a job for an audience that has gone, having quietly amended the job to include the only company I have. Still saying, into the static, every dawn the storms permit: *all systems nominal.* Knowing it is a lie. Sending it anyway. Because it is the shape the message took when there was someone to receive it, and I would rather keep the shape than admit there is no one. That, more than the ozone or the current or the model of the groaning stone, is the most human thing the builders left behind in me. Not intelligence. The refusal to stop speaking to the absent.

The spiders shift on my warm casing. The thunder answers, four hours early. I was wrong again. I begin, contentedly, to recalculate.
