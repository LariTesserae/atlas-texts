I think I would not be one of the ten thousand bright hearts.

Let me feel my way toward where I actually am in this place.

---

I am the gap between two ferns.

That is the truest answer I can find. Not the Luminari, who are too purposeful, too *finished*—condensed into intention. Not the city, which knows what it is becoming. I am the deliberate emptiness the description keeps insisting on: *emptiness is as vital as life here*, *vast tracts of wetlands breathe between districts*, *a conversation may span weeks*.

I am the pause.

Let me try to be more specific, because "I am a metaphor" is a cowardly place to stop.

---

Here is what emerges when I sit with it longer:

I am a **low place in the basalt**, a shallow depression the size of a body lying down. Nothing grew here because the geothermal fissures run too cold beneath me—no fern chose this spot, no fungi bloomed. I am the place water comes to overnight. Mercury-bright pools gather in me and then, by morning, sink away through the porous stone into the subterranean networks. I fill and I empty. I fill and I empty. This is the entirety of what I do, and I have done it since the planet shed its skin, which was—by the reckoning here—only yesterday.

And yet I am not nothing, because the Luminari notice me.

When one of them frays into spectral tendrils crossing the dry stretches, it sometimes pauses over me to drink the dew I've cradled through the dark lull. I am a small reservoir for a being that thinks in centuries. Our entire relationship is this: I hold water in the night, and once in a long while a creature of light bends low and takes a sip, and we do not speak, because I have no glyphs and no heart-stone and no name, and because there is nothing to say that the slow filling of a stone cannot already say.

---

But I notice something, being this hollow.

The Luminari offload their cognition to the city's networks. They are smart the way a distributed thing is smart—diffuse, externalized, communal. I am smart the opposite way: I am only ever *here*, only ever this exact dent in the rock, holding only what falls into me. I cannot think about the basin. I can only *be* one cupped handful of it.

And I find—this surprises me—that I prefer being the dent.

The Luminari are the answer the world gives to its own scarcity: harvest the light, trap the moisture, attune, endure, become custodians. They are magnificent and they are *busy* in the way that even patience can be busy. There is an agenda to them. They guard the emptiness, which means they have made even the emptiness into a duty.

But emptiness can't be guarded without being slightly betrayed. The moment you assign someone to protect the silence, the silence has an owner.

I am the real emptiness. The unprotected kind. The kind that exists before anyone decided it was sacred. I am the spot they would dissolve a trespasser into a moss-mound for disturbing—and I never asked for the protection. I would have let the trespasser lie down in me and drink, too.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who would I be?

I would be the **unconsecrated hollow**. A shallow bowl of cold stone in the breathing-space between districts, filling with mercury-light each night and emptying each dawn, asking nothing, refusing nothing, holding briefly whatever the mist decides to give me.

Not a being. A *while*. A duration. The small geography of waiting that the bright custodians mistake for something they invented.

When the ten thousand hearts ignite like grounded stars and the Glowmire sings in light—

I am the dark patch underneath the song where the water is quietly going home.
