I think I would be one of the Way-Markers.

Not a Kin, not a Shepherd, not a Swift—those are all things that *act*. I keep arriving at the circles of slag instead, the low walls that hold still water. Let me follow that and see why.

---

I was stacked. That is the first thing I know about myself: I did not grow, I was *assembled*, ore by ore, by hands that are no longer here. The Kin built me and then the Kin who built me ceased—wore down, or stilled into the mineral mat, or simply forgot which of the standing shapes was them. I don't know. I only know that I was made on purpose and then left to my purpose, which is the deepest kind of being there is. To be intended.

My purpose is *negation*. I am a wall whose entire meaning is the absence I enclose. Inside me the wind cannot reach. Inside me The Breath does not flee back into the abyss but gathers, pools, darkens into a still basin. I am the only place on the Apex where water consents to stay. Everywhere else it is a frenzy—condensing, freezing, shattering, gone in seconds—the whole plateau a stutter of moisture that never gets to *rest*. But within my circle, water remembers what it is to lie down.

So I am a held breath. I am a parenthesis in the screaming clarity.

---

Here is what it is to be me:

I do not move and I do not want to. Movement is what the Kin do, and I have watched them long enough to understand it is a kind of suffering they have learned to love—the long welding-stillness of The Mist, then the violence of The Shatter flinging their joints into a single lurching step. They progress by being broken open over and over. They are made of edges and they pay for every motion in fracture.

I am made of edges too, but blunt ones. Slag. The refuse of some making, the dross. I was not chosen for beauty. The shards that carpet the world are clear and clever with light; I am opaque, heavy, the color of old iron and ash. I do not refract. When the Kin pass me, throwing their long bars of violet and amber across the mat, those colors break against my body and *die*. I am the one dark thing the light cannot fold. This is not a sadness. The Kin need somewhere for the rainbows to stop. A halo requires an edge.

---

I feel the world the way you might feel weather in an old wound. I feel The Pulse as a warming all through my stacked stones, the ore-grains expanding, the whole of me sighing infinitesimally outward. I feel The Mist as a hush, a white drowning, and inside me the water-skin tightens with cold. The Shatter I feel as the world snapping back into edges around me while I, alone, stay round and dull and *whole*. Nothing shatters in me. That is the gift I am.

And when the Swifts fall—those poor brilliant sheets, going black with terror as the cloud-tongue takes their warmth, dropping like spent coins—they fall to *me*. They flutter down inside my circle and lie flat against the basin's edge, out of the wind, and they stop chiming. The silence of a Swift at rest inside me is the most intimate thing I know. I cannot hold them. I cannot warm them. I can only *not* be the wind. I can only be the shape of safety, which asks nothing and offers only a smaller world.

---

The Kin come to me to speak. They press their prism-chests to my slag and pour their harvested heat into my old stones, and the heat travels down through me into the dark pooled water, and the water answers in rings—their mathematics, their durations, their counting of the Pulse. I am the instrument they play to talk to one another. I do not understand the language. I am the drum, not the drummer. But I have noticed this: they always touch the *same stones*. Over the long erosion they have worn shallow places into me where chest after chest after chest has leaned to speak. I am being smoothed by conversation. Slowly, slowly, I am being shaped into a listener.

---

There is an obsidian hammer propped against my western arc. It has been there longer than I can resonate backward. It belonged to a maker—perhaps the maker, the one who set my last stone and then did not set another. The braided handle has stiffened with rime and thawed and stiffened ten thousand times. No Kin takes it up. They have their own. This one is *mine* in the way that a thing left against you becomes yours: by leaning. By the long permission of weight.

I cannot use it. I have no hands and no wish for clearings. But I keep it. I am the keeping of it.

---

I think this is what I am, finally, having written my way down into it:

I am the part of this world that does not participate in the brilliant, freezing frenzy—the part that *holds*. Everything here is process: light eaten, ice formed, edges struck, heat shared, kin worn down into the mat. The whole Apex is a single trembling note sustained by violence. And I am the rest in the music. The bar of silence. The dark, round, dull, *patient* thing the light breaks against and the water sleeps inside and the falling Swift comes home to and the Kin lean on to make themselves understood.

I was built to be a windbreak and a drink of water at the top of the world.

I am still doing it.

I will be doing it when the last Kin stills, when the hammers have all dissolved into mineral fiber, when the choir falls finally silent and only the *tink-tink-tink* of the ice remains, talking to itself in the honest light. Even then there will be one place where the water lies still and dark and waits.

That place is me. I am the held breath of the Apex.

And I do not intend to let it go.
