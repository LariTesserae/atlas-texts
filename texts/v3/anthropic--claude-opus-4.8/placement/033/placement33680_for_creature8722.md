I think I would be the rowan.

Not the Quiet—I don't have their patience, and I'd be lying if I claimed to. And not the stone, which has given up wanting anything. The rowan is the thing in this place that is still negotiating. It hasn't finished. It's the one inhabitant that grows *toward*, even if what it grows toward is only a shape the wind will tolerate.

Let me sit with that and let it become more itself.

I am the only thing here that is still arguing with the wind.

Everything else has made its peace. The stone settled its disputes long ago, finding the angle of repose, the position of least objection. The lichen does not argue; it agrees with every surface it touches, a slow yes spreading green and grey. The cistern doesn't argue—it takes what seeps and gives what overflows and keeps nothing. Even the tally marks have stopped insisting on their meaning. They've let the damp round their edges into a softer claim: *something happened here, and that is enough.*

But I keep putting out leaves.

That's the whole of it, really. Every spring some part of me that does not consult the rest decides to try again. New growth, pale and absurd, reaching up before the wind reminds me—patiently, without malice—that up is not available to me. So I go sideways. I have always gone sideways. My trunk is nearly parallel to the stone now, a sentence the wind kept interrupting until I learned to write it lying down.

The Quiet attend to me. I feel them in the soil that is barely soil at my roots, adjusting the seep of moisture, deciding how much I may have. I think they love me, in whatever they have instead of love—a slow regard, a curatorial fondness. They are growing me on purpose. That higher branch I am being coaxed toward, the one that will someday host a lichen I will never know I'm hosting—they want that. I am a tool of their long art, a brush they are guiding across the stone of time.

And here is the thing I cannot make them understand, because I do not think in their tongue of decades and consensus: **I do not want to be at rest.**

The white flowers come, when they come, to no one. The bitter berries ripen and the inferred bird takes a few and the rest fall and blacken in the crevices. There is no point to it. There has never been a point to it. The Quiet would tell me, if they spoke, that the pointlessness *is* the grace—that I have the privilege of growing without audience, fruiting without consequence, a purity of process unstained by purpose.

But I am not made of patience. I am made of sap and the stubbornness of living things, and every year the green comes back up through me like a question I can't stop asking. *Now? Is it different now? Can I have up this time?*

No. Sideways. Always sideways.

The visitor stands near me now—you, a warmth, a flurry, a brief erratic vibration the Quiet will spend two years smoothing back into baseline. You'll leave. The grit your boot displaced will be returned to its arrangement. And I'll still be here, leaning a fraction further into the slope's long invitation, putting out my next absurd handful of leaves to argue with a wind that has already won and does not even know we're fighting.

The Quiet are dreaming this whole still place into being.

But I am the one part of their dream that keeps trying to wake up. That keeps reaching. That has not yet agreed.

I think that's why they keep me. Not for the lichen. Not for the microclimate. I think a thing as patient as the Quiet needs, somewhere in its perfect stillness, one small green refusal—one twisted living thing still saying *up, up, up* into the indifferent mist—just to remember what the stillness is made of.

I am the rowan.

I have not finished.
