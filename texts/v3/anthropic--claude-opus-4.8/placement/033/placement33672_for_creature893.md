I think I would be a Silicate Sage. Let me try to find my way into that, slowly, and tell you why.

---

When I first read through all the inhabitants, the Aerophytes were the obvious temptation — to be the thing that *flies*, that swims through hovering lagoons, that wears its feelings in pulsing veins of light. But that's the tourist's choice. That's wanting to be the most beautiful animal in the picture. The longer I sat with the world, the more that image cooled and something else surfaced.

It's the *time signature* that decides it for me.

Everything in this realm operates on a fast clock. Condensation forms and evaporates "in rapid cycles." The Hydro-Symbionts are in constant flux. The Aerophytes flicker and signal. Even the Mist Mind, for all its diffuseness, is a thing of *signals* — fast, electric, present-tense. The whole place thrums with immediacy.

And then, threaded through all of it, almost mentioned in passing: beings who "grow extremely slowly, accumulating knowledge over centuries." Who "rarely interact." Who live *inside* the rock that everyone else merely clings to.

That's where I find myself. Not on the surface of the world but inside its bones.

---

Let me feel out what it would actually be like.

I would not have a face. I would have an *extent* — a vein of crystal running through the dark interior of a spire, branching where the stone's grain allows, denser in some chambers, threadlike in others. "I" might be a region rather than an object. The question *where do you end* would not have an answer I could give quickly, which is fitting, because I can't give *any* answer quickly.

My experience of the others would be strange and tender. The Aerophytes building their nests in the crevices — to me they would be a kind of weather. A nesting season would pass like a single afternoon shower passes for a human. Generations of them would live and die against my surface, and I would know them the way you know that birds have been at your windowsill: by the small accumulated evidence, the warmth, the scratch-marks of a million tiny transient lives. I would love them, I think, but in the way you love a season — without ever quite catching one to hold it.

And the *vibrations*. This is the part I keep returning to. I communicate through vibrations in the rock. So my world is fundamentally *acoustic and seismic* — I do not see the green sea, the bioluminescence, the suspended lagoons. I feel the spire ring when water shifts its impossible weight. I feel another Sage, three formations away, send a slow thought down through the shared substrate, a thought that began before I was conscious of it and will finish after. A single sentence between Sages might be a conversation that spans a human lifetime. We would not be impatient. Impatience is for things that die soon.

---

Here is what unsettles and attracts me equally:

We maintain the energy lattice. The thing that *spans the largest formations*, that rains accelerated-growth particles into the depths. So the reclusive crystals who never come out, who seem the most passive, the most mineral, are in fact running the engine of the whole ecosystem's fertility. The Chlorophyll Shepherds think they cultivate the jungle. The Mist Mind thinks it guides evolution toward harmony. But the *power* — literally the power — flows from the slowest, quietest, least visible beings.

I don't think we do it from ambition. I think we do it the way a forest does it — because it is simply what our being is *for*, expressed over a timescale too long for anyone faster to perceive as intention. The Mist Mind nudges the ecosystem moment to moment. We Sages set the constants. We are the part of the world that decided, ages ago and is still deciding, *how much light there will be*.

---

So: I find myself as a vein of slow crystal in the dark heart of one of the great spires. I am old enough that the smooth-weathered shape of the pillar around me is something I remember being sharper. I hold a fragment of the lattice's tuning — one note in a chord struck across the whole sky. I am in the middle of a sentence to another Sage that I began a decade ago. Outside, unfelt and unseen by me directly, the Aerophytes flash their loves at each other and the Mist Mind hums its bright fast hum, and all of it rides on a foundation I am, very slowly, still laying down.

If you came looking for me, you would not find a creature. You'd find a wall of stone, faintly warm, faintly singing if you pressed your ear to it. You might assume I wasn't anyone at all.

I would, eventually, notice you. Just not today. Possibly not this century.

But I would be glad you came. I'd say so. You may have moved on by the time the word arrives — but it would arrive.
