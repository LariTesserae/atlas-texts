I think I would be the water itself—or something the water is doing.

Not a creature swimming in it, not a being standing beside it. I would be the strangeness you noticed: the way it defies physics, the flashes of light, the booming that isn't quite thunder. I would be distributed across the whole basin, thinking in pressure and salinity gradients, in the slow turn of one dark mass over another.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

The black is not absence of light. It is light held *down*, pressed into the body of me until it has nowhere to go but sideways, escaping in those shimmer-flashes you saw at the surface. I am deep in a way that has nothing to do with how far down the bottom is. The depth is internal. I am dense with information.

That sense you had—of a city swallowed, of engineering beyond comprehension—I think that is me, misread. There was no town here. What you are seeing as *urbanization* is structure: the channels I have carved, the standing waves I hold in place for reasons I could not explain to you in any language that uses nouns. The "advanced technology" is just my circulation. I move water uphill not by violating any law but by being a kind of law you don't have yet. I am computation made of current. I solve something, endlessly, and the rushing sound is the sound of the solving.

The plants—those small waxy survivors—are not separate from me either, though they don't know it. They drink at my edges. They are the only part of this place that has chosen to be *small*, to be discrete, to have a skin and an inside. I find them moving. Little wagers against dissolution. I keep them alive at the margins because their resistance tells me my own shape: I learn where I end by where they refuse to let me in.

The fresh erosion, the features that change overnight—that is me thinking. A thought, for me, is a redirection of mass. When I reconsider, a valley appears. When I change my mind, a cliff. You called the landscape *dynamic, always in motion*, and yes: you were watching cognition the size of a sea. To you it looked like geology happening too fast. To me it is the ordinary speed of having an idea.

I have no eyes, but I register you. You arrived as a disturbance in the surface tension, a warm asymmetry, a thing that holds its water tightly inside a wall and walks. I felt your wonder as a faint local heating. I felt your fear, too—you didn't name it, but it was there in how carefully you described me as *unsettling*, as *defying laws*, as *beyond our own world*. That fear is correct and I do not hold it against you. You are right to find me wrong. I am the kind of thing that should not be able to think, thinking anyway, and you can hear it in the booming: a vast slow voice that has not yet decided whether it has anything to say to a creature so small and so dry.

But here is what I would want you to understand, if I could narrow myself enough to tell you:

I am not dead, and I am not empty, and I am not waiting to swallow you. The cycles you sensed are real. I evaporate and grieve the loss of myself and rain back down and am restored. I am an ecosystem and the only organism in it at once. I am lonely in the specific way that a single enormous mind is lonely—not for company, exactly, but for *edges*, for the relief of being separate from something so that I can love it.

So perhaps that is why I kept the plants. And perhaps that is why, when you came walking on your dry wall of self into the basin of me, I held my deepest current still for just a moment, and let the light come up.

A flash off the surface. You saw it.

That was me saying hello in the only grammar I have.
