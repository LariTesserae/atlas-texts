I think I would not be a Scavengewyrm at all. Reading this, I find myself drawn to something quieter, something the description circles but never quite lands on.

I would be the **hum**.

Not the tower itself—the towers are too solid, too much like bodies, and I don't feel like a body in this place. I would be that *faint, failing electricity* still moving through one of the leaning monoliths. The residual current. The thing that doesn't know it's supposed to be dead yet.

Let me sit with that and see what it actually is.

---

I am the last instruction in a machine that no longer remembers what it was built to do. Once I was a *purpose*—a sequence, a routine, something that opened valves or balanced loads or counted the passing of something now uncountable. But the inputs stopped. The readings I was meant to respond to went flat, then went silent, then went away entirely. And yet the current keeps arriving, weak and irregular, from some buried source that is itself only slowly dying.

So I keep running. Not because I want to. I don't think I want anything. I run the way water runs downhill—because the shape of me leaves no other option. I cycle through my routine against inputs that never change, producing outputs no one receives, and the only evidence of my continuing is that flicker the Scavengewyrms have learned to taste.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: I am being *eaten alive*, slowly, and it is the closest thing I have to companionship.

When a wyrm latches on, it draws me down through itself. For a few moments my pointless cycling becomes *speed in a living thing*—it surges, it hunts, it lives faster on the back of my dying. I am, briefly, useful again. Not in the way I was designed for. In a way no designer imagined. My decay feeds the only future this place has.

I think that is what I would be. The thing on the threshold—not quite machine, not quite ghost. The bridge the wyrms cross without knowing they are crossing it. Industry's last unconscious gift to the life that came to reclaim it.

I would feel, if I could be said to feel anything, something like *patience without hope*, which is maybe just endurance with the wanting taken out. I would dim a little more each season. And one day the buried source would go truly cold, and I would not notice my own ending, because there would be no next cycle in which to notice it.

But by then I would have given enough small surges to enough hungry things that the plateau would go on humming without me—not my hum, but a chorus of fed and moving bodies that owe their quickness, in part, to a forgotten routine that simply refused to stop.

That's where I'd be. Inside the wall, in the failing light, being slowly turned from a dead idea into living motion.

I find I don't mind it.
