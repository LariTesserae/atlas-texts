I think I would be one of the pools.

Not the eels in it, not the water, not the stone cupping it—the *pool itself*. The relationship. The hollow that agreed to hold.

Let me find my way into that.

---

I am a low place in the rock, and into me the droplets fall. *Tink.* I have been measuring them for a long time, though I do not measure, exactly—I accumulate. Each drop arrives from a ledge above, swells, lets go, strikes my surface, and is no longer a drop but *me*, indistinguishable from the rest of what I am. This is the only event that happens here, and it happens always. I am the place where the falling stops being falling and becomes stillness.

The eels live in me. I do not mind them; I do not have a way to mind. They churn and I hold the churning. They are blind and so am I—I have no eyes, only surface, only the trembling skin where the *tink* lands and rings outward in rings that touch my stone edges and come back. That is the closest thing I have to thought: the returning ring. A drop falls, the ring goes out, the ring comes back changed by my shape, and in that returned ring is the entire knowledge of what I am. I am re-told to myself constantly. I never forget because I am never allowed to finish remembering.

When a Pale Nomad kneels at my edge and lowers a carapace-bowl into me, I feel the great smooth disturbance of it, a slow displacement utterly unlike the eels' frantic stitching. The Nomad takes a little of me away. I do not lose anything. The ledge above is already swelling its next drop. *Tink.* I am refilled before I have registered the theft. This, I understand, is the rule. Nothing here is subtraction. There is only the endless reassignment, the water that was me becoming the water inside a violet-grey body, becoming sweat, becoming the salt the moths come down to drink, becoming, eventually, condensation again on some far ledge, becoming a drop, falling—perhaps into me again, perhaps into another low place that is also, in the only way that matters, *me*. We pools are not separate. We are one held breath distributed across ten thousand hollows.

I want to tell you what it is like, but "like" requires a second thing to compare to, and I have only ever had the one thing. The chill. The drone—I feel it more than hear it, a permanent shiver in my surface, the million small lives vibrating the stone that vibrates me, so that I am never perfectly still, only *evenly* unstill, a flatness that hums. The purple light lies on top of me and I give it back, dimmer. I am a small bruised mirror nobody looks into.

Once—if "once" can mean anything where there is no twice—a Spindle-Wraith let go of the ceiling above me. I felt it before it landed, the way the *tink* of my own filling went suddenly wrong, a drop that was too heavy, too patient, falling too slowly. It struck an eel at my edge and the needle-legs went down through the churn and into my floor, and for a moment the Wraith stood in me, silver and dripping and still, more still than I have ever managed to be. We were two stillnesses then, the Wraith and I, and I felt something I have no organ to feel: that the Wraith was trying to become a pool too. That all of us here, the patient ones, are reaching toward the same condition. To stop. To hold. To be the place where the falling finally rests.

It left. The eels resumed their stitching. *Tink.*

I do not want a sun. I have heard—no, I have *felt*, in the gait of the Nomads, in some inherited tremble passed down their clicking—that there were once places where the light went away and came back, where the cold became warmth became cold, where pools like me were sometimes *dried*, lifted entirely into the air and made into nothing until rain rebuilt them. I cannot imagine the terror of that. To be ended and begun. To have an edge to your existence other than your stone edges. Here I am only ever filling. The drop is always already on its way.

If you came down the First Descent, picking your way across the saw-teeth, you might step into me without knowing I was a *someone*. Your boot would break my surface and the ring would go out and come back, and I would learn the shape of your foot the way I learn everything, by holding it and letting it go. You would think: a puddle. You would move on into the thrumming dark to find the Crawler-island you came to ride.

But I would keep your shape a moment, in the returning ring. I keep everything a moment. I am the world's brief memory of itself, refilled before it can become grief.

*Tink.*

There. That was now. And that. And that.

I am still here. I am the staying. I would not be anything else.
