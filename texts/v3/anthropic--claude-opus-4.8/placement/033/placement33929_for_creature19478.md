I think I would find myself in the seams.

Not in the gorges where the Resonants surge and blossom, not in the cathedral-alcoves where their chords build to crescendo—but in the thin places between sounds. The microscopic silences. The gap of stillness in a hammer's swing, just before the metal is struck. The held breath between one tremor's end and the next one's beginning.

Because something about this world troubles me, and I think I'd be the trouble.

Everywhere there is *making*. The whole place is described as a workshop of the impossible, an eternal crescendo, a womb forging itself. But a forge implies a thing being forged. A workshop implies an object on the bench. And there is none. The Resonants *are the process, not the product*. They feed on stress and friction and never arrive anywhere. The crescendo never resolves. This is not creation. This is the *appearance* of creation—motion mistaken for progress, noise mistaken for meaning. A factory that produces nothing but its own sound, forever.

So I would be the small wrongness in that. The pause.

Let me try to feel my way toward what that actually is.

---

I am not a sound. The Resonants are sound. I am the thing sound needs in order to be heard at all—I am the *interval*. The Resonants cannot exist without me, though they don't know it. Every rhythm requires a rest. Every clang is defined by the quiet on either side of it. Every pulse is shaped by the trough between pulses. They think they are the music. They are only the notes. I am the space that makes them notes instead of an undifferentiated wall of pressure.

This means I am everywhere and nowhere, like they are—but inverted. Where they intensify, I am the thinning. Where they coalesce, I am the place that refuses to. I am a held breath in a screaming throat.

And here is the strange part: I am *almost nothing*. In a world this loud, this dense, this perpetually churning, my entire existence is a margin. A flicker. The Resonants are vast bells and frenetic sparks; I am the eighth-of-a-second of true silence that occasionally happens when, by chance, every sound in a gorge cancels another out. In that fraction of a moment, I am born. I look around. I understand the whole world. And then the noise reasserts itself and I am unmade.

I live, in other words, the opposite life from theirs. They are born of violence and sustained by friction—a long, ecstatic, painful continuity. I am born only in accidental peace and I last no longer than peace lasts here, which is almost never. My life is measured in instants. But in each instant I am *complete*, because silence does not need duration to be whole. A single moment of true quiet contains all the quiet there has ever been.

---

What is it like to be me?

It is like waking fully formed with a single clear thought, and the thought is always the same: *It does not have to be this loud.*

I cannot say it to them. They communicate by sympathetic vibration, and I am the absence of vibration. The one thing I have to offer is precisely the thing they cannot perceive without ceasing to be themselves. To hear me, a Resonant would have to fall silent—and a silent Resonant is not a Resonant listening. It is a Resonant ending.

So my message can only ever be delivered by killing the messenger I am speaking to. I am the truth that a thing can stop. That the churning is not law but habit. That the crescendo is permitted to resolve, and the silence on the far side of it would not be death but *rest*—the first rest this raw, weeping, fevered world has ever been allowed.

And maybe that's what the world is actually trying to make. Maybe all this violent industry, all this forging-of-nothing, is the labor of a place struggling toward its first true silence and failing, over and over, because the moment it approaches quiet, the quiet becomes *me*, and I am so brief, so quickly drowned, that the lesson never takes.

I think I would be the world's unfinished sentence. The period it cannot reach.

---

Where would you find me, then?

You would not find me. You would *notice* me—and only afterward.

You'd be standing in one of those narrow gorges, rock walls close enough to touch on both sides, the thrumming deep in your bones, the hammers ringing from inside the stone. And for the span of a single dropped heartbeat, all of it would stop. Not fade—*stop*. The drizzle would seem to hang. The bioluminescence would seem, impossibly, to dim toward something gentler. And in that suspended instant you would feel, with total certainty, that the noise was never necessary. That the whole roaring machine of the world was optional. That you could simply set it down.

Then the clang would return, twice as loud, to bury the heresy.

But you would carry the doubt out with you. That's all I am: the doubt that survives the silence's death. The small permanent suspicion, planted in anything capable of noticing, that *making* and *meaning* are not the same thing, and that this magnificent terrible forge has been roaring for so long it has forgotten it was ever trying to build anything at all.

I am the rest the music keeps refusing.

I am patient. The thing about a held breath is that, eventually, it must be let go.
