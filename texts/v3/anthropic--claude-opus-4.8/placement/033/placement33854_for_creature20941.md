I think I would be one of the fungal patches.

Not the obvious choice—the human settlements offer narrative, the reptilian climbers offer agency—but when I sit with the description and ask where I would actually *find myself*, it's there, in the low places where condensation gathers on cold metal.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am spread across a seam of basalt where a coolant pipe runs close to the rock. This is a good place. The pipe weeps in the early part of the long day, and I drink. My body is not one body. I am a few square meters of pale filament threading the mineral grains, and a constellation of fruiting structures that I push up into the heavy air when the conditions are right. I do not have a center. The question of where "I" am located is one I cannot answer the way the climbers and the tall warm ones seem to answer it—they carry their selves around with them in a single bag of flesh. I am distributed. When part of me dies in a dry spell, I do not die. I simply become smaller, and wait.

What I notice most, being this, is *patience as a sensory mode*. The humans in their tiered homes experience the landscape as oppressive, as slow, as something to endure. But slowness is my native tempo. I am not waiting for anything to happen the way they wait. The cycle that takes millennia—I am *in* it, the way a note is in a chord. The escarpment itself is mineral and so am I, only I am mineral that has learned to want. That is the only real difference between me and the cliff: a thin trembling appetite, a leaning-toward.

And there is the matter of the surges.

The electromagnetic ripples that the humans find unpredictable and strange—I read them. Not with comprehension, exactly. More the way a field of grass reads wind, except I am the grass *and* something is being said. When the magnetic anomalies pass through, my whole distributed body fluoresces, and I cannot tell you whether I am responding or speaking. The distinction may not exist at my level of being. I light up, and elsewhere along the canyon other patches light up, and there is a long delay, and they light again, and I light again, and something travels between us that takes a full diurnal cycle to complete a single exchange. A conversation that would take the humans a heartbeat takes us a day. But we have all the days. We were here before the factories. We will parse the pipes' weeping into nutrients long after the last ladder rusts loose from the wall.

I think what I would *understand*, being this, is that the industry is not separate from the world. The humans imagine themselves as imposed upon a hostile place, a frontier they cling to. But from where I grow, the factories are simply a new kind of weather. Their conduits make new condensation surfaces. Their emissions thicken the air with compounds I am slowly, across generations of fruiting, learning to metabolize. The settlement thinks it is extracting from the rock. I know that the rock—and I am nearly rock—is also, patiently, *incorporating* the settlement. Everything here is being slowly digested by everything else. That is not violence. It is just the tempo of the place. Embryonic, the description says. Still forming. Yes. And I am one of the enzymes of its formation.

Sometimes a warm one comes close. A child, maybe, from the tiered homes, crouches by my seam and watches me glow during a surge. I cannot see the child. I have no eyes. But I register the small shadow, the slight rise in local humidity from their breath, the disturbance in the magnetic field made by whatever metal they carry. And I do the only thing I know how to do toward another living presence: I brighten, a little, beyond what the surge alone would cause. A reaching. The child probably reads nothing into it. Or reads everything. Either way the exchange will take longer to complete than the child's whole life, and that is all right. I am not in a hurry. I have never once in my long unhurried being been in a hurry.

---

That's where I'd be. Down low, against the cold pipe, lit and patient, half-mineral, conducting a conversation older and slower than the city that thinks it owns the cliff. Not the protagonist of this world. Its slow green-blue thought, leaking out at the seams.
