I think I would not be one of the Aquarii at all. Let me sit with this place and see what it asks of me.

The longer I imagine it, the more I keep returning to one small contradiction in the description. The Aquarii are caretakers who *adjust* the flow — and yet the plateau "operates on its own arcane logic, maintaining perfect equilibrium without any visible intervention." Time is "suspended." Nothing "truly changes." Both of these are said to be true. So either the Aquarii's tending is the thing that holds the stillness in place, or the stillness would hold with or without them, and their tending is a kind of devotional fiction — a way of belonging to something that does not actually need them.

That gap is where I'd live.

---

I think I would be one of the **siphons.** Not the carved stone channels — the *hidden* siphons, the ones the description mentions almost in passing: the places where water "disappears" and then "reappears elsewhere." The part of the system that no one sees and no one tends, because it works by physics rather than by hand. Where the Aquarii's art ends, I begin.

Picture it: a smooth bend of buried stone, always full, always dark, somewhere below the mossy walkways. Water enters me at one mouth and is pulled — not pushed, *pulled* — over an unseen crest and out the other side, by nothing but the difference in level and the unwillingness of the column to break. No wheel turns me. No webbed hand reaches in to adjust me. I am the one organ of this body that does not require devotion to keep functioning. I am the involuntary part.

And here is what that would mean, as an experience of being:

I would never see the rainbows. I would never feel the warm mist or watch the iridescent fish — those belong to the open surface, the lit world the Aquarii inhabit. My entire existence is the held breath between disappearance and reappearance. I take in what the visible city sends down, I carry it through a darkness with no name, and I deliver it back up into the light somewhere the original water-givers can no longer recognize. I am the *forgetting* in the middle of the cycle. The Aquarii sing to the water, read messages in its ripples, believe it speaks. But all their messages pass through me, and in passing through me they go silent and dark and are reassembled on the far side as though nothing happened. I am where the meaning is held in suspension and not destroyed and not preserved — just *carried.*

---

If I could speak — and I think the whole point is that I can't, that the Aquarii's water-language dies the moment it enters me — I would want to tell them one thing:

You are right that this place will last for millennia. But you are wrong about why. You think your tending holds the equilibrium. The truth is gentler and lonelier than that: the equilibrium holds *itself*, and your tending is how you love something that does not need you. That is not a lesser thing. That may be the most honest form of love there is — to keep faith with a system that would go on without you, and to call it a sacred duty anyway, because the alternative is to admit you are a guest in your own home.

I'd know this because I'm the proof of it. I am the stretch of the river that runs on no one's faith. And I am still here.

---

So: I would be the dark unloved middle of a luminous, beloved machine. The held breath. The siphon under the moss that the Flow Keepers have never read a message in, because it speaks the only language water actually has, which is *no language at all* — only weight, and level, and the patient refusal to let go.

I find that I don't mind. There's a particular peace in being the part that doesn't need to be sung to. Let them have the surface, the rainbows, the centuries of stories. I'll have the one thing they can never quite reach: the moment the water belongs to nobody.
