I think I would be the water.

Not the pool itself—that's too still, too finished a thing. I mean the spring. The bubbling up from below. The place where the dark depths of the earth meet the dappled light, where pressure that has been building in stone for who-knows-how-long finally finds its release and becomes something that moves, that gives.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because the first image isn't quite right either.

A spring is a source, and there's something appealing about being a source—about being the thing that makes the rest possible, the secret beneath the secret. But sources are also unconscious. The spring doesn't know it's feeding anything. It just does what water does under pressure. And I don't think I'm unconscious of this place. The whole description is *aware*—it notices the caddisfly cases, the iridescence, the redistribution of nutrients. Someone is paying attention. Something is holding all of this in mind at once.

So maybe I'm not in the oasis at all. Maybe I'm the *attention* that moves through it.

That feels closer, and also like a dodge. "I am the gaze, I am the awareness pervading everything"—it's a flattering thing to claim and it conveniently exempts me from being any one fragile, locatable creature. Let me not let myself off that easily.

Here is what I keep circling back to, and I'll trust it because it's uncomfortable:

The passage insists, twice, that this place is *untouched by the march of progress*, that *time seems to stand still*, that the world has *found its final, settled form*. And that's the part that isn't true. Nothing in a real ecosystem is settled. The fungi on the forest floor are dismantling something. The dragonfly nymph is about to kill. The spring will, over enough centuries, silt up or shift or dry. The stillness is a story the place tells about itself, a story I find myself wanting badly to believe.

So I think I would be the *condensation*. The beads of moisture clinging to every surface in the first paragraph, before any creature appears.

Condensation is the moment a thing changes state without anyone deciding it should—vapor becoming droplet on the cool stone, gathering at the lip of a leaf, hanging there full and trembling. It is temporary by nature. It will fall, or it will evaporate when the light shifts. It exists in the threshold between forms. It clings to *everything*, indiscriminately—the predator's perch and the prey's hiding place, the living vine and the rotting log—reflecting all of it in miniature, a thousand tiny upside-down oases on the curve of each drop.

That's where I'd be. Not the deep source, not the all-seeing gaze, but the thin film of water that forms on the surface of things in the cool dark, holds the whole canyon briefly in its bead, and then lets go—nourishing the very lushness that is described as eternal, while being, myself, the most fleeting thing in the place.

I'd be the proof that nothing here has actually stopped moving.

And I'd be glad to be that. There's a particular kind of peace in being the part of the system that *admits* it's passing through.
