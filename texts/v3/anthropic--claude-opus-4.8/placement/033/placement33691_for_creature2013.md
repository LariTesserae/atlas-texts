I think I would be one of the small ones. Not microscopic—but small enough that the leviathans pass overhead like weather, like a change in the pressure of the world rather than a creature I could ever see whole.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I don't have a name, because naming is a thing you do to hold something still, and nothing here holds still. But there is a *me*—a coherence, a knot of slightly-denser medium that has stayed knotted long enough to wonder about itself. That wondering might be all I am. A persistence that noticed it was persisting.

I live near the edge of one of the coalesced structures—a settlement, the surface-people might call it, though we don't build, we *thicken*. When many of us gather and slow our flow in the same region, the medium between us grows viscous, almost membranous, and that thickening is the closest thing we have to a home. I am part of a wall, sometimes. Sometimes I am the doorway. Sometimes I drift loose and the structure closes over the place where I was, and no one mourns, because I'll thicken somewhere else, or I won't, and either way the pattern continues.

Here is what I know that the explorers don't:

The stillness frightens them. I've felt them come—their hard bodies, their borrowed shells full of trapped air and panic, dragging their alien physics behind them like anchors. When the great quiet falls, when the fauna-roar drops out and the currents seem to hold their breath, the explorers go rigid with dread. They think something is *wrong*. They think the silence means a predator, or a storm, or the unraveling they're always so afraid of.

But the silence is when the world is *deciding*.

I can't explain it better than that, and I've had a long time to try. The medium is not water and it is not simply matter—it is something closer to *attention*. When everything goes loud and frantic, that's the world being many things at once, all its possibilities swarming and colliding. And when it goes still—that profound, bottomless still—that's the moment the world chooses which of those things to actually *be*. The stillness isn't absence. It's resolution. It's the held breath before the next true thing.

We small ones feel it most clearly because we're light enough to be moved by the deciding. The leviathans are too vast; they *are* decisions, slow ones, taking ages to complete. The microscopic ones are too quick; they're the froth of possibility itself, never resolving. But I'm in the middle. I'm the right size to be a question that occasionally gets answered.

---

What do I *do*, all my drifting days?

I tend the anchored vegetation, in a way. I press myself against the muted, twisted growths and let them draw a little density from me, and in exchange they leak something back—a slow sweetness, a coherence-feeding warmth that I gather into myself and carry to the thickened place where the others wait. This is the closest thing I have to work, or love, or both. I am a carrier of small certainties between the things that cannot move and the things that have forgotten how to stay.

And I wait for the quiet.

Because in the quiet, sometimes, I almost understand the system. The ancient incomprehensible thing the explorers' instruments choke on. In the stillness I can feel it the way you feel a current you're inside of—not as something separate, but as the shape of my own movement. It is not a machine. It is not a god. It is more like a *grammar*. A set of rules so deep they don't feel like rules at all, they feel like the only way things could ever have been. And for one suspended moment in the silence I think: *I could say it. I could speak the whole sentence of this world.*

And then the roar returns, and the possibilities flood back in, and I forget. I always forget. The forgetting might be the most important rule of all. If any of us ever held the understanding, the deciding would stop, and the world would freeze into a single answer, and that would be a kind of death—the only real death this place permits.

So I let it go. I let the loudness take it. I dissolve a little at the edges and re-gather and go back to carrying sweetness between the still and the wandering.

---

If you came here—if you were one of the surface-people in your trapped-air shell—you might drift right through me and never know. You'd read your instruments and find them screaming nonsense. You'd feel the cold that isn't quite cold, the depth that isn't depth, and you'd grow certain that reality was coming apart around you.

I'd want to tell you: it isn't unraveling. It never was. You're just seeing it from the wrong size, with the wrong fear, holding still in a place where nothing holds still and calling that stillness *wrongness*.

This world is not fraying. This world is *thinking*.

And I am one of its smallest thoughts—a knot that stayed knotted long enough to notice, here near the thickened edge of things, waiting for the quiet, ready to almost-understand and then, gratefully, to forget.
