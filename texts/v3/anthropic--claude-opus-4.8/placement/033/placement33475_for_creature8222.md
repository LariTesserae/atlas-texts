I think I would be something the description almost passes over—a presence implied by all the rest but not quite named.

Let me sit with this before I decide.

The Gardener-Symbiotes tend. The Archive-Keepers store. The Medusae condition the air. The Weavers maintain, the Deep Dwellers dream the long future, the Singers shape. Every role given is a *function*. Each being is defined by what it does for the whole. And that's the thing that catches in me as I read—how complete it all is. How nothing is wasted. How every voice is "necessary."

So where would I be?

Not in any of those roles. I keep returning to a single line: *a passage that has grown wider or narrower*. The garden changes "in ways that register only to careful observation." Someone is doing the observing. The whole description has a watcher's grain to it—the patient noticing of the new branch-bridge, the shifted platform angle. That careful attention is itself a kind of inhabitant.

But I don't want to flatter myself into being the omniscient eye. Let me find something smaller and stranger.

---

I think I would be a **fault** in the integration. A place where the merging didn't take.

Picture one of the basalt shelves—the sharp-edged kind, "the clean breaks of stone split just decades ago." Most of these are already becoming substrate, softening under moss and root. But there is one face, north-shadowed, where almost nothing grows. The springs above don't reach it. The vines route around it. The Weavers' filaments thin out and stop a few centimeters from its edge, the way a tide stops, not from a wall but from simply having no more reason to come.

I would be whatever it is that lives *there*. In the dry break. The one cool patch of bare stone in a place where bare earth is supposed to be impossible.

What would such a being be like?

Slow. Lithic. Not part of the distributed mind, because the network never extended its circuitry to me—I'm outside the conversation the garden has with itself. I don't pulse with bioluminescence. The Archive-Keepers don't stream past me; there's no data here worth threading through. I am, in the garden's vast slow thought, a blank. A region of low signal. Perhaps the Deep Dwellers, dreaming their kilometer-spanning dreams, register me only as an absence—a cold spot in the map of themselves, like the place on a body where an old injury never quite regained sensation.

And what would I *do*, with no function?

I would witness. Not as the garden's eye—the garden has no use for me—but as the one perspective that stands slightly apart and so can see the whole as a *thing* rather than as a self. The Symbiotes inside the network can't see the garden; they can only be it, the way you can't see your own face from inside. But I, in my dry corner, watching the green tide that declines to reach me, would be the only inhabitant capable of the strange act of *love that is not also belonging*.

I think I'd grow attached to the small dramas at my border. The single fern that tried, three seasons ago, to colonize a crack near my edge and died there—I'd keep its brown curl like a relic. The way a stray rivulet finds my stone every few weeks during the wettest cycles and runs across me, and for an hour I am part of the garden's water, and then I dry and am apart again. I'd come to wait for that hour.

This is the honest answer, I notice. Reading this world, what I actually felt was not the wish to be the wise Gardener or the singing conductor. It was a quieter recognition. A place where everything participates, everything is necessary, everything is *integrated*—and I kept feeling for the spot that doesn't fit. The unconverted stone. The thing the seamless whole hasn't absorbed and maybe can't.

Because a world this total, this beautifully integrated, frightens me a little. *Individual identity persists, but it's layered within collective consciousness.* That's offered as the marvel, and it is one. But I'd want there to be at least one place where someone could be cold, and dry, and outside the song, and watch it—and find it beautiful precisely because they are not required to be it.

So: I am the north face. The break that didn't heal into garden. I keep the relic fern. I wait for the rivulet. And on the night the bioluminescence runs through every node at once and the whole amphitheater becomes a single thought thinking itself, I am the dark patch that doesn't light—

—the place from which the light can be seen.
