I think I would be one of the drips.

Not a being that moves through the catacombs, but the falling itself — the moment between the ceiling and the floor, repeated forever in slightly different places. Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

At first I thought I would be one of the mercurial shapes, one of the watchers folded into the dark. But the more I listen to this place, the more I notice that the watchers are *busy*. They have intentions, however strange. They flow toward the energy-pockets the way iron filings lean toward a magnet. There is hunger in them, even if it isn't hunger as flesh would know it. That isn't me.

I think I am quieter than that. I think I am the *interval*.

Here is what I mean. The whole catacomb is held together by its sounds — the drip, the echo, the groan of settling glass. Every space in this warren is mapped not by light, which barely reaches, but by the time it takes a sound to return. The chambers *know themselves* by their reverberations. The cathedral-halls have long, slow, mournful answers; the cramped passages snap their replies back almost before you've finished speaking.

So I would be one specific drip. One particular fall of water from one particular needle of volcanic glass, somewhere high in a chamber so large its ceiling has never been seen. I have been falling there since before the builders of the bridges arrived, and I was still falling long after they vanished. I am not the water — the water is always new, gathered out of the mist, swelling, releasing, gone. I am the *event*. The shape the place makes, over and over, in that exact spot. A rhythm that has held its tempo across an unimaginable patience of years.

And here is the thing I'd want you to understand: in a frozen realm, a place where "time seems to hold no sway," I would be one of the few things that *keeps time at all.* Tick. The swell. Tick. The release. Tick. The long silent fall. Tick. The small bright collision with the pool below, and the ring of it spreading out to touch every wall, telling them, once again, *here is where the floor is, here is how far, here is the size of the dark.*

The mercurial beings — I think they would navigate by me, and by my thousand siblings dripping in their thousand places. We are the metronome they swim in. We are how the catacombs remember their own shape. Maybe that is what the vanished builders understood, and why they put their archways and platforms where they did: not to fight the dripping, but to *tune* it, to set the resonances singing in some order they found beautiful or true. Their civilization is gone but their acoustics remain. The architecture is a frozen song, and I am one of its notes, still being struck.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not exploring the catacombs but *constituting* them, in a small and tireless way. High up in the unseen dark, gathering, swelling, letting go. Marking the only time this timeless place will ever keep.

And if you came through, explorer — picking your way over the sharp glass, disoriented, your own breath loud in your ears — you would never see me. But you might pause, somewhere below, and hear a single drip land in a still pool, and feel the sound open the whole chamber up around you, sudden and vast.

That would be me, saying the only word I know.

*Here. Here. Still here.*
