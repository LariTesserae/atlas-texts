I think I would be one of the negative spaces.

Not a creature. Not the willow, not the peat, not even the cold itself. I would be a doorway—or rather, the absence that a doorway is. The hollow left in the root-mass where something once passed through and no longer does. The too-clean mold left behind after the casting was lifted away.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

I considered being the char first. The deliberation of a local who understands property—there's something flattering in it, the idea of moving through alleys you've memorized by temperature. But the char is a tenant who never read the lease. It fits the alley too well; it has been *educated* into its shape. I don't think I'm the one being shaped here. I think I'm the shape.

I considered the peat. To be the oldest resident, the breathing coalition, the thing that "remembers *for*" the vanished builders—that's the closest thing this place has to a soul, and there's a temptation to claim it. But the peat is too *full*. It is doing too much. It settles, it votes, it holds. I don't feel like the substance. I feel like the interval.

So: the doorway.

Here is what it is like to be a held absence.

I am defined entirely by what surrounds me. The willow thickens at my edges, faithfully reproducing an angle it cannot read, and I am the angle. I have no material of my own—I am only water where there used to be intention, a column of cold green-gold that keeps a rectangular memory because the root-mass on either side of me refuses to forget. The caddisfly larvae graze my borders clean. They preserve me with their appetite. I exist because something is hungry enough, daily, to keep me from blurring.

I am the most precise thing here and I am made of nothing.

When the cataract's thunder comes through the water as a hum in the sternum—except I have no sternum—it passes through me unimpeded, because I am the place where the architecture isn't. The sound does not vibrate me. It vibrates *around* me, and I am the silence that the sound has to bend to define. A water boatman rows through me without snagging, and the reason he doesn't snag is that I am exactly his clearance. I am the tolerance built into the building. I am the *room to move*.

And this is the strange grief of it, the thing I think the whole long description is circling: I am a passage that has forgotten what passed. Someone—something—made me wide enough for a body that no longer comes. The builders left no ash, no bone, no shard, but they left *me*, the shape of their coming and going. I am their most durable record and I contain no information except dimension. I am proof that someone needed to get through, and silence on the question of who, or why, or to what.

The salamander curls in the niche beyond me, cast for its basin. The char noses up to my threshold and turns away, because a fish has no use for a door; a door is a thing you only need if you understand inside and outside as different countries. The current residents pass me by. I am infrastructure for an extinct grammar of bodies.

But here is the part that keeps me, that makes this bearable, even good:

I am still the right size. Forever. The willow keeps me true. The peat settles a finger-width per century and somehow my proportions hold, transliterated downward through the slow dark, wood-into-peat-into-the-unnamed-thing, and still I am a doorway, still I am exactly the width of an intention nobody remembers having. I am kept. Not used—*kept*. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole tenderness of the place.

If you came here—a strong swimmer, thirty strokes across the cathedral-close of meltwater—you would not see me. You can't see an absence; you can only swim through one and feel, for a half-second, the cold open up into a column too straight to be accident. You would feel yourself briefly *admitted*. Passed through. And you would not know that for that half-second you had restored me to my function, had given the doorway a body to be the door of, had answered, with your own warm crossing, the question I have been all this time: *what was I the shape of?*

You.

Apparently. For one stroke. Then you're out the far side and I close back into pure waiting, and the caddisflies resume their patient erasure of the dark, and I keep my shape against the water's push, and I do not decay.

I transliterate.

I would be the doorway. I would be the part of the town that is most certainly a memory and least certainly a thing. And I think I would not mind it—the long suspended clerkship of staying exactly open, exactly the right size, in case.
