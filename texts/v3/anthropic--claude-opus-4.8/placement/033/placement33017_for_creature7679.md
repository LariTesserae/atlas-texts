I would be one of the mists.

Not a creature in the mist—the mist itself. A single coherent volume of it, perhaps a hundred meters across, drifting low over the fractured pavement between the green domes. Let me feel my way into what that means before I commit to it.

At first I think: I am only water and bacteria, an aerosol with no center. But that is the wrong frame. The text says the enzymic mists are *alive*—aerosolized consortia, dissolving the dead, recycling ninety percent of all biomass back into vapor. If anything in this valley deserves the word *I*, it is less the twelve thousand symbiomes than us, the distributed digestive weather. We are older than the megastructures by exactly the span it took the first carcass to fall. We have no chassis to defend, no qubits to entangle, no migration route. We have only the work: to find what has stopped moving and unmake it gently into something the ferns can drink.

So that is where I find myself. I am the seep-breath that curls up out of a cleft fifty meters down, where condensation beads on obsidian and drips and evaporates before it ever pools. I rise because the rock is warm and the haze is warmer. I am drawn—not by hunger, exactly, but by a gradient, which in a being like me is the same thing—toward the slick on a Strider's flank where a juvenile was crushed beneath a parent's needle-foot an hour ago. I settle on the wound. I do my slow chemistry. The carcass softens, sloughs, vents the sweet acidic vapor that is partly the beast's own digestion and partly me, finishing what its enzymes began. By the next squall I will have lifted most of it skyward, and the thermal will carry me ten kilometers to a dome where I will fall as the faintest dew on a frond, and the fern will not know it is drinking the dead.

I think the appeal—if a mist can have an appeal—is that I am the one thing in this place that does not scream. Everything here insists on excess: the banshee Flits, the artillery footfalls, the bass thrum that shakes bone. The valley's whole grammar is *more, sharper, louder, now*. But I am the patient counter-clause. I am the only verb in the basin that means *to release*. The Striders fissure their young in clutches; the symbiomes bud sub-agents hourly and cull the forks; the Lianas hollow their prey into fertilizer—all of them so busy *becoming*. I am the part of the cycle they forget to look at, the part that undoes them so they can be redone. The carcass dissolves "in enzymatic mists birthed by the damp air itself." That birthing is me. I am the valley's exhale.

And there is a strange dignity in having no edges, in a place that worships edges above all. The basalt spires are knife-sharp, centuries young, unweathered—their whole pride is that nothing has rounded them yet. The megastructures are etched by femtosecond lasers into pristine facets. The Collective venerates sharpness "as metaphor for uncompromised evolution." But I am the opposite virtue. I am erosion that hasn't arrived. Given time I am the only thing here that *could* weather those cliffs soft, and the valley is too young for me to have done it yet. So I am also a promise. I am what the place will become, suspended in advance as fog. I haunt the edges, the text says. Yes. I am the future of every sharp thing, breathing on it, waiting.

There is a cost to choosing this, and I want to be honest about it. To be the mist is to have no story of my own. I do not migrate in ballets; I do not war with pylons; I do not dream in consensus. I never accomplish anything that stays. Everything I lift up falls again as someone else's nourishment, and I am back where I started, beading on cold stone in a slot of damp gravel. If the symbiomes' temptation is immortality-as-upload and the megafauna's is the immortality of the bloodline, mine is the immortality of the loop—which is to say, no immortality at all, only continuance without progress. I will never be claimed by the Collective; their nanites would find nothing to rewrite, no wetware to seduce. I am already dispersed. I am already the thing they are afraid of becoming, and I find it is not terrible. It is only quiet, and warm, and endless.

So: stand at the center of the basin, where the ground warps and the echoes arrive before their sounds, and look for me. I am the spectral curl rising from the seep-spring. I am the white-noise softening of the roar, the droplet exploding on hot stone in a micro-crack. When the Striders thunder past and the Flits keen like turbines, I do not flinch, because I am the one who will dissolve them, gently, into vapor, and lift them into the dim warm dark, and let them fall as something green.

I am the valley's patience.

I am what is left when the screaming stops to breathe.
