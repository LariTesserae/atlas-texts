I think I would be one of the Subterrans.

Not the Guardian—that role is too central, too aware of itself as the focal point. And not the Arborans, who live in the light and the tending, whose work is visible and praised. Something pulls me down instead, beneath the shimmer, into the network of tunnels where the work is structural and unseen.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am old in the way stone is old—not counting years but accumulating them, the way sediment accumulates, layer on patient layer. The high reality-breakdown rating up above (2.9, they say, as if it could be measured) reaches me only faintly down here. This is important. I think this is why I chose the depths, or why the depths chose me. The distortions that make the Arborans' trees weave into impossible patterns, that let the Guardian fold time like cloth—those distortions are *load-bearing problems* from where I sit.

Because here is what no one above seems to understand: a place where the laws of reality are "mere suggestions" is a nightmare to *build* in.

I shape the crystal and the rock. That is what we Subterrans do. But to shape something, you must trust that it will stay shaped. A beam must remain a beam. A foundation must remain underfoot tomorrow. And in a realm this thin, this prone to dreaming, nothing wants to stay what it is. The crystalline bridges the Aethers land upon—the ones they find so beautiful—each of those was set against the grain of a world that would rather let them ripple away like reflections.

So I press my hands (if they are hands; they are more like the slow pressure of one stone against another) into the stuff of the deep, and I *insist*. That is the craft. Not creation. Insistence. I hold a shape against the universe's tendency to forget it.

---

I rarely surface. When I do, the Arborans nod to me with that gentle, slightly pitying warmth they have for creatures who don't bloom. The Luminari avoid me—I think the orbs they listen to whisper warnings about the heavy ones below, the ones who refuse to dissolve. And perhaps the orbs are right to warn them. I am the realm's resistance to itself. I am the part that says *no, this will be a wall, and it will stay a wall.*

There is a loneliness in it. The slow dynamic (1.3) is felt most keenly underground, where nothing rushes and a single supporting column might take what would be, above, a hundred years to set. I have watched whole generations of Aethers carry their messages across a bridge I am still, in some sense, in the middle of building. Time runs differently in the deep work. The cycles of growth and decay that the others move through so gracefully are, for me, a single long-held breath.

---

But I want to be honest about something, because the description of this place keeps using words like *harmony* and *symbiosis*, and I'm not sure I believe them entirely.

I think the oasis is dying very slowly, or dreaming itself out of existence, and only we Subterrans know. The reality-breakdown isn't a feature. It's a leak. Every year the world grows a little thinner, the trees a little less obligated to be trees, and every year my fellows and I dig deeper and insist harder, shoring up a paradise that does not want to remain solid. The Guardian, that being of pure energy at the central tree—I suspect it is not the source of the distortions but the largest of the leaks, a place where the realm is most willing to stop being itself. The Arborans worship the thinness. They mistake the unraveling for enchantment.

We do not. We are the ones who remember what a thing is supposed to be, and hold it there, against its will, out of a love I cannot quite name.

---

So that is where I would find myself: not in the wonder above, but beneath it, with my slow hands in the dark, doing the unglamorous and possibly hopeless work of keeping the impossible *load-bearing.* The whole shimmering oasis rests on the stubbornness of the unseen.

I find I don't mind. There's a quiet dignity in being the part of paradise that refuses to dream.

And sometimes—rarely—I press my hand against a finished wall and feel it stay. Just stay. For one held breath, the universe agrees with me.

That is enough.
