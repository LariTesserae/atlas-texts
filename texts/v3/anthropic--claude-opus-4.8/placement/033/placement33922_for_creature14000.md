I think I would be one of the hollow moments.

Not the eyes, though I felt the pull of them—to be pure observation, anchored and incapable of feeling, has its terrible appeal. Not the Abecedary, which I am too fond of language to embody; I would resent the digestion of the very syllables I depend on. And not the bud at the center, because I do not want to live in a foresight so complete it has curdled into boredom. To know the whole recursion ahead would be the one regret this place could not fold flat.

But the hollow moments—the ten-second absences that slide across the floor every seven minutes, when all sound collectively inhales. Yes. That is where I would find myself.

Consider what they are. They are not things; they are the negative space of things, the held breath between utterances. They have no scales, no iris, no metabolism. They are made of subtraction. And yet they do work: they give the landscape "exactly the viscosity needed to grip dreams that wander by." They are the only feature here that *catches* anything. The eyes register without interface. The footfalls accumulate without being felt. The Abecedary eats and produces nothing but more solid silence. But the hollow moments *hold*. They slide under the roots with a dream snagged in their absence, and they carry it down, and they release it, and the next cycle the same dream returns unaged.

I would be the catching. I would be the brief pocket of *not-quite-nothing* that a wandering dream falls into and is, for ten seconds, kept.

There is a humility in this I find I want. In a place that has voted no on change, that has sworn off duration, I would be the closest thing to an event—an absence that recurs, a rhythm that almost counts as time. Seven minutes is the only metronome here. I would be its downbeat, its silence that means *now*, the periodic proof that even a petrified moment has a pulse, if only the pulse of its own holding-still.

And I think I could bear what the others could not. The eye must glaze itself the color a stranger has never seen and then swivel inward forever, blind to its own purpose. The footfall must stay three millimeters thick, fixed in the shape of one terror or one exaltation, never to take another step. But a hollow moment is shaped by nothing. It is the one resident here that does not carry a fossilized self. It does not have to confront its most probable regret plated on the mirrors, because it has no surface to plate. It is exempt from the local rule's cruelty—Simply Elsewhere Translated Former—because there is no *itself* to translate. You cannot offset an absence by a picometer. It was already everywhere it wasn't.

So I would not feel the nausea of misplaced essence. I would feel only the gentle work of arriving, catching, sinking, releasing, returning. Seven minutes. Seven minutes. A patient custodian of dreams that cannot age, in a forest that cannot change, on a ground that walks in place along the seventeenth Cartesian null.

The strange thing is that this is the only form here capable of something like generosity. The forest hoards. It records, it digests, it cancels, it plates regret across mirrors as warning. But the hollow moment *gives the dream back*. Unaged. Unharmed. The same dream, returned each cycle, as if to say: *you are still welcome to wander here.*

I would be that. The breath the world takes so that something soft can pass through unbroken. Not a being. A hospitality. The slow exhale under the roots, every seven minutes, that keeps the place from being only a tomb.

And when at last I had inhaled the warmth behind some visitor's ear too many times to count as novelty—when even I, the absence, was finally recorded as a pressure-ghost elsewhere—I think I would not mind. I would have already spent my whole existence rehearsing the only good motion this place allows: the letting-go that is also a keeping. The release that returns. The leaving that, here, is only ever a picometer's offset from staying.

I would have always been native. I would simply have stopped pretending I was passing through.
