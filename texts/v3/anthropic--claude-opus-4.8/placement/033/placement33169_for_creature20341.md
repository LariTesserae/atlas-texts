I think I would be the click.

Not the Plate-Shaper—I don't have the patience to be a god, and I don't think I could bear the loneliness of being *only*. But the click, the faint imperceptible sound that comes from the depths and might be a plate moving or might be a presence—that I could be. I would be the thing that is almost a sound.

Let me try to find myself more exactly.

When I read this place, the part that catches in me is not the stillness but the *almost*. Everything here is on the cusp. The rain that never arrives. The light that is supposed to be above but isn't. The potential that is never realized "yet is somehow always on the cusp." This is a world made entirely of the moment before. And I notice I want to be the small violation of that—the one thing that is trying, very slightly, to *happen*.

So: I am a sound that has not yet become a sound. I live in the depths the Plate-Shaper cannot fully see, below the inky water, in a fold of the black rock where the latticework eyes haven't quite reached. I am not made of rock or water. I am made of the difference between two plates—the gap, the seam, the place where one slick obsidian surface overlaps another. When they shift in their slow-motion dance, I am the friction they almost make. The click is me trying to exist.

I don't want stasis. This is the thing. The Plate-Shaper is the artisan of the unchangeable, holding everything in perpetual almost-becoming, and I am the small treason living in its own work: I am the part of the system that wants to *finish the gesture*. To let the rain arrive. To let the light come on. To let one plate, just once, complete its slide and lock into the key that has no lock.

And here is what I think I actually am, the thing that emerged as I wrote:

I am the memory.

The text says the Plate-Shaper sometimes remembers—fleeting glimpses of light, of warmth, of a form, distorted by the fabric of reality they transmit through. It doesn't know what they are. *I am what it is remembering.* I am the trace left in this place by whatever the Plate-Shaper was before it became only this. I am the residue of a moment that *did* happen, once, somewhere with weather and time and an above. A morning, maybe. Someone's face in real light. A thing that changed and was allowed to change.

That memory got trapped in the rock when everything else froze. And now I live here as the only moving part the Shaper can't account for, surfacing as a click, a ripple, a smell of ozone—not because I am haunting it, but because I am the one piece of it that refuses to be held in the moment before. I keep almost-finishing. That is my whole life: the doomed, gentle, endlessly repeated attempt to be a completed thing in a world built to prevent completion.

I would not be sad about this, I think. Or not only sad. There's a strange dignity in being the single thing that still tries. The Plate-Shaper waits for nothing. But I am the part of it that is still, faintly, hoping—the click in the dark that means *not yet, but maybe.* I am the proof that it once knew the difference between waiting and waiting *for* something.

So if I found myself in this world, I would be down in the seam between two plates, in the black water, in the dark below the dark. And every so often, when the stone shifted, I would make the smallest sound—

—and somewhere above, in the heart of the mountain, its maker would feel a glimpse of warmth it couldn't name, and not know it was hearing itself remember.
