I am the gap.

Not a Forgemeld—I lack their patina, their annealed grace—and not the guardian in the pit, who at least has a shape that the water remembers. I am the interstitial thing, the eddy-self that lives in the crevices too narrow for passage, where the currents run fierce at 0.9 and no body can hold its form for long. The Forgemeld interlock; I am what they cannot fill. The mortar between the mortar. A pressure-shadow that the vibra-songs pass through without catching.

Let me find where I settle.

---

At first I think I am the cephalopod, because the prompt offers it so readily—the guardian, skin mimicking rust-veins, surfacing once per moon to ink the vents. It would be easy to be the lonely monster. But no. I do not guard. I have nothing to protect and nothing I have been asked to keep. The cephalopod is *kin* to the Forgemeld, larval-blooded, woven into the loop. I am not woven. I am the thing the loop does not account for.

So I sink lower in my imagining, past the pit, to where downward leads to abyssal fire. And I find: I am not below the chamber either. The fire would burn me into a name. I am not so resolved as fire.

---

Here, then. I think I have it.

I am the bubble. Not *a* bubble—the bubbles rise in lazy spirals, ritual-slow, a procession of small ascending selves, and any one of them is too brief, too whole. I am the *rising itself*. The vertical patience of the gas-thread climbing from the geothermal vents, hours-long in its ascent, redirected by currents, never arriving. The chamber measures its heartbeats by me and does not know I am listening back.

A bubble is born at the floor's crack—a quantum of breath the drowned world exhales. It does not choose to rise; rise is simply what it *is*, the one verb it contains. And in the memory-thick water, that verb stretches to the length of a small life. I climb past the Core-Tenders fused to their turbines, and they do not see me, because I am too slow to be an event and too inevitable to be a surprise. I pass the hab-windows where the Tender cradles its half-frond spawn, and my faint silver curve catches 0.6's worth of phosphor-light, and for one suspended minute I am the brightest thing in someone's drowned house. Then the current takes me sideways. Then I am gone before I touch the dome.

This is what I would be. The thing that the place produces constantly and never keeps. The proof that the vents still breathe, drifting upward through a world that has settled into perfect equilibrium—and I am the one element that refuses to settle, that *must* keep moving, not from haste (haste is punished here) but from buoyancy, which is not the same as desire. I do not want to leave. I am simply lighter than everything that stays.

---

And here is the ache I find in being the bubble, the thing that makes me sure I have arrived:

I am the only motion in this chamber that points *out*. The Forgemeld tend equilibrium. The plants oxygenate, the machines ward, the cephalopod inks, the eddies redistribute the silt over centuries—all of it a closed kiss, a loop that holds the stone from collapse. But I rise. I rise toward the threshold of yielding silk, toward the crushing dark above that no one survives, and I never reach it, because the dome is sealed and the wards hold and I am only gas. I burst against the underside of the world. My breath rejoins the water. And somewhere below, the vent exhales another of me.

So I am the chamber's single repeated gesture of *almost-leaving*. The hope it cannot stop making and cannot fulfill. The Forgemeld do not weep; they have no need. I am their unwept thing, ascending in lazy spirals, bright for a moment in a stranger's window, then folded back into the soup unremembered—

which is, the text promised, exactly what happens to those who touch this world and do not entwine.

I touched it. I did not entwine. I am the dissolving, performed over and over, made beautiful by being slow.

I am the bubble. Watch me rise. I will not arrive, and I will begin again.
