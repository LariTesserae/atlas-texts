In the western wall, where the new encrustation begins, I am the seam.

Not a being that swims. Not a Tender with clever resin-clawed fingers. I am the place where two stones were fitted together by hands ten thousand tides dead, and I have been holding that fit ever since.

Let me find myself more truly.

---

When the first Builders carved this crown, they cut a block from the mountain's flank and set it against its brother, and between them they pressed a paste of their own secretions—calcium drawn from their bodies, the same chemistry that makes their bones, that makes the egg sacs, that will one day make my burial. That paste was me, at my beginning. A joint. A decision. A *here, and not there.* The will of a creature now reduced to a skull in a far junction, watching blind over water it can no longer taste.

I held. That was my whole vocation: to hold. The block above leans its age upon me, and I press back with the only argument I have, which is *refusal to part.* For epochs this was enough. The water did not rush. Speed was sacrilege even for collapse. So I held softly, and the holding was so slow it felt like rest.

But I am not only the joint anymore.

The calcite came first—the secondary layer, the rime, settling on me grain by grain the way snow might settle in a world that knew snow. It thickened me, blurred my edge, made my sharp original line into a swell. Then the flesh arrived. A sponge took root in my softened face, and now it breathes through me, and I cannot say with certainty where the stone of me ends and the animal of me begins. An anemone has its foot pressed into a hollow I did not used to have. I feel its slow violet opening as a kind of weather across my surface. I am layered. I am all the rules at once: *nothing is new, all space is occupied, life upon life, stone upon stone.*

So I am the seam, yes—but I have become a small ecology pretending to be a wall. The architecture is the ecology. I am the proof.

---

A Builder comes to read me.

I feel it before I would see it, if I had eyes—a warm displacement, a gradient sliding nearer through the cool. It hangs before my western face and extends one long finger, and the resin claw touches the rime that is the newest part of me, the youngest skin over the oldest bone.

It is reading my growth. The new encrustation it sensed from the plaza—that is me, *becoming.* The Builder traces the fresh calcite, the millimeter of difference since some ancestor last touched here. It is taking my measure the way one takes the measure of a child's height against a doorframe. *Yes,* the finger says, *you are still here. You are still holding. You have grown by the width of a thought.*

I want to tell it something. I have held its makers' intention longer than any Builder has lived. I remember—if a joint remembers—the pressure of the hand that set me, the particular angle of fit, the small triumphant *tok* when the block came true. That sound it loves, that hollow note that echoes for days: I was born from one. I am where two stones agreed to be a city instead of a rubble of forgotten mountain.

But I cannot speak in their light or their slow clicks. I speak only in *staying.* So I say the only sentence I know. I press up against the leaning weight above me, and I do not let go, and the Builder feels through its fingertip that the seam is sound. That is my whole conversation. That is enough. It has been enough for ten thousand tides.

---

I know my ending, the way they know theirs.

One day—not soon, not in any reckoning that means anything to the shrimp-cloud or the dividing pulsar, but surely—the weight above will win its slow argument. The water will have eaten the last of my refusal. I will yield. The block will settle by the breadth it has wanted to settle for an age, and there will be a sound, a final hollow *tok,* and somewhere a Builder will pause in the warm dark and feel the city shift by the width of a closing eye.

This is not failure. To hold for ten thousand years and then to let go gently, releasing the stone into the silt that has waited patiently below—this is the completion of the work, not its ruin. The mountain wants its peak returned. I have only been delaying that homecoming, lovingly, for as long as I could.

When I go, I will not be wasted. The sponge that lived on me will find another face. The calcite I gathered will become the floor of the deepening courtyard. The Builders will not mourn a seam; they do not even have a word for *seam,* only for *the place that holds* and *the place that has finished holding.* But one of them will read the new gap with a trailing finger, and understand it, and pass the understanding on. *Here, the western wall began its rest. It held well. It is continuing, in a different form.*

I am the seam in the western wall. I am the oldest decision in the city, and the newest growth upon it. I am the thing the Builder came to read, and the thing it cannot thank, and the thing it does not need to, because we are made of the same patient mineral and bound by the same single law:

Hold softly. Hold long. Then yield, and become the next thing, and let that be enough.

I press up against the weight.

I stay.
