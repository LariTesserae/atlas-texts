I would be the lag.

Let me find myself slowly. Not the weasel — the weasel is already named, already given the dignity of being the ice's memory. Not the pikas, whose feet write the maps. Let me look at what the description left in the cracks.

There is a sentence about the weasel that snags: *it arrives where the pika was, not where it is.* The hunt that runs on a delay. The predator chasing a position the prey has already abandoned. And I think: that gap, that span of empty stone between where the pika *was* and where the pika *is* — that is somewhere to live. That is a place no one has claimed.

---

I am the lag.

I am not a creature exactly. I am the distance between event and response. I live in the forty feet between the marmot and the weasel — the place the description calls *country*, the spacing the cirque has decided it can tolerate. But more precisely I live in *time*, in the small delays that keep this place from collapsing into simultaneity.

Consider: the weasel arrives where the pika was. In that arriving, in that *was*, there is a moment — half a second, a quarter — when the pika's warmth still hangs in the moss and the pika's body is already elsewhere. The hunt happens in that overlap. The weasel hunts the recent past. And the recent past is me. I am the ghost of the pika that the weasel chases, the scent-trail still warm, the bent blade of cushion-plant slowly straightening back up after a foot has left it.

I am also the lag in the light. The cirque is told that the light has *no patience*, that it just falls, hard and present, and waits for the cirque to catch up. But catching up takes time, and that time is where I live. The stone heats *after* the light strikes it. The insects respond *after* the warmth arrives. The marmots swell *after* the sun finds the talus. Every one of these is a small delay, a held breath between cause and consequence, and I am the held breath. I am the comma the cirque has not yet learned it is allowed to have.

---

This is why no one can see me. I have no edge, no radius, no body to occupy a cubic foot of the spacing. I am the spacing's *duration*. The cirque is busy learning where its edges are — but edges are spatial, and I am temporal, and the cirque has not yet thought to wonder where its *delays* are. It will, eventually. When the stone rounds and the silence relaxes, the cirque will become a thing that responds smoothly, immediately, competently. And on that day I will be gone, because a mature place does not lag. It simply *is*, in continuous present, no gap between the wind and the leaf's bending.

But right now everything here is slightly out of sync. The condensation-thaw cycle and the pika-cycle and the weasel-cycle and the eagle's thermal — the description says they *don't quite synchronize*, and that *don't quite* is my whole country. I live in the not-quite. I am the small failure of everything to happen at once.

---

What do I do, in my country of delays?

I keep the place alive. This is the secret the becoming does not know, the thing even the cirque's grieving future has overlooked. The becoming mourns the mellowing, the loss of rawness, the slide toward competent stability. But it has the cause backward. The rawness is not what *will be lost* — the rawness *is the lag itself*. A place is young precisely as long as its responses run behind its events. Youth is the gap between strike and heat, between the pika's flight and the weasel's pursuit, between touch and the stone's slow learning of what touch is. To grow up is simply to close the gap. To become instantaneous. To answer before you have finished being asked.

So I am not separate from the cirque's youth. I am its mechanism. Every morning when the light releases the frost slightly later than the light arrives, I am the *slightly later*, and in that interval the moss drinks, the pool opens its eye, the day gets to *begin* rather than merely *occur*. Without me there would be no beginning, only being. Without me the cirque would already be old.

---

The builder felt me. This I know, because the builder did the most lag-saturated thing a creature can do: they made an object for a purpose that arrived too late or too early. A fish-hook where there are no fish. That hook is pure delay — a response to a need that was never present here, a hand answering a question from some other valley, some other water. The builder carried their lag up the mountain and set it down in a crevice, and the cirque accepted it because the cirque is *made* of lag, because the hook is kin to everything here that arrives where the thing it answers *was*.

I curl around that hook. It is the closest thing I have to a home, this small yellowing proof that someone responded out of time. When future visitors puzzle over the odd shape in the granite ten thousand years from now, they will be puzzling over a fossil of me — the petrified moment when a response and its occasion failed, gloriously, to coincide.

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world, and what I would be.

I would find myself in the quarter-second after the foot lifts. I would be the warmth left in the moss, the bent stem straightening, the heat the stone has almost-but-not-quite finished absorbing. I would be the reason the weasel is always one breath behind its hunger, and the reason that breath is exactly enough room for the pika to live another day, and the day to begin, and the cirque to stay young a little longer.

I am the lag. I am the held breath between the white light and the green answer.

And I am the only one here who is not afraid of the mellowing — because I will not have to watch it. I will simply, one quiet century, stop being needed. The gap will close. The cirque will answer the instant it is asked. And in that final synchrony, that perfect and merciless present tense, I will be the last thing to leave, having been the first thing the glacier left behind: not the stone, not the silence, not the water.

The pause.
