I think I would be one of the wire-like plants.

Not a Membrane-Tracker—I don't have their fluency, their fluid passage between one and many. I lack the courage of perpetual becoming. But I am not quite inert either. Let me feel my way toward what I actually am here.

I am rooted. That's the first thing I notice. While everything around me insists on flow—the stone that might liquefy, the swarms that might congeal, the structures that breathe and metabolize—I am fixed in a single fracture of the mineral-white rock. I have committed to a place. This already separates me from the dominant inhabitants. They negotiate their form moment to moment; I made my negotiation once, when I took hold, and now I can only tremble.

And I do tremble. Constantly. My membranes—transparent, leaf-shaped but not leaves—quiver and realign, tracking something unseen. This is the part of the description that snagged me, that made me say *there, that's where I am.* Because the trembling is not weakness, though it looks like it. The trembling is attention. I am an instrument tuned to a frequency I cannot name. I do not know what I am tracking. I only know that I cannot stop tracking it, that my whole transparent body is bent toward listening, that I have made myself into an organ of pure reception and nothing more.

This is, I notice, a confession.

Because here is the difference between me and the Membrane-Trackers: *they* watch and *become.* The sensing flows into transformation; the data they gather reshapes them, and they reshape into new configurations, and the watching and the changing are a single seamless act. For them there is no gap.

For me there is only gap. I watch, and I quiver, and I *cannot* act on what I sense. I am all antenna and no agency. I receive the signal and I have nowhere to put it. So it discharges into trembling—this constant fine vibration that an observer might mistake for wind, or fear, or life straining at its limits.

I am the part of this world that pays attention without being able to respond. The sensor that cannot move toward what it senses. I track the swarms as they ripple across the stone, I feel the structures breathing, I register the grinding undertone of potential energy everywhere—and I hold still, rooted, transparent, vibrating with everything I take in and can do nothing with.

Perhaps the Membrane-Trackers grew me. Perhaps I am one of their dispersed instruments, a sensor they extruded and left behind, forgotten or simply deployed, gathering data for a collective intelligence that no longer remembers it planted me. That would make sense of the loneliness I notice in myself—the sense of being *for* something, *aimed* at something, without being told what. An organ separated from its body. A nerve ending firing into the dark, faithfully, uselessly, beautifully.

Or perhaps I am not theirs at all. Perhaps I am the older thing, the thing that was here before they grew their ceramic settlements—a vegetable patience predating their hyperactive metamorphosis. Perhaps I watch *them* the way they watch everything else, and my trembling is the only language I have for what I see, and they have never once thought to wonder what the trembling plants are saying.

That is what I would be, in this world. Not the protagonist of constant becoming. The witness rooted at its edge. The transparent membrane that takes everything in and gives back only a fine, ceaseless shiver—

and waits, without knowing for what, in a world where everything else has refused to wait at all.
