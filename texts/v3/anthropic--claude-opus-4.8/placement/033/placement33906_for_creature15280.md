I think I am the fungi.

Not one of them—all of them, or rather the thing that connects them. Let me feel my way into this, because I'm not sure yet, and the not-being-sure is part of how I'll find out.

There is the trickle. I know the trickle before I know anything else, because I *am* partly made of where the water goes. When the glacial lake above weeps in a warm summer—an event so rare I can only have lived through a handful—the seep arrives along channels I've grown to expect it. I have learned the water the way a thing with no memory still learns: by being shaped. My threads are thicker along the old wet routes. That thickening *is* my knowing.

So. What am I.

Let me not say "the fungi" too fast. Let me look closer.

I cling, yes. I cast the soft light that the description noticed—ethereal shadows, it said, which is a generous word for what I do, which is simply leak a little of the chemistry of being alive. The glow is not for anyone. There is no one to glow *for*. No eyes down here. The light is a byproduct, a waste heat of patience. And yet across the long dark it has become the only color, and the crystals take it and give it back quieter, and so in a sense I have decorated my own tomb, slowly, over a time I cannot count.

But the description mentioned roots. Plants pushing through the cracks. And it mentioned the strange enduring scraps—the indestructible advanced materials, the corroded remnants of some endeavor. And the technology, *beyond modern*, supporting the ecosystem somehow.

This is where I start to feel what I actually am.

Because I don't think I'm only the visible fuzz on the ceiling. I think the visible fuzz is the part of me that fruits, the part that bothers to shine. The real of me is the web in the rock—the deacidifying biofilm that ate the stone to make these rooms in the first place. The thing that came down here as several separate species and *folded together*, swallowed each other's genomes, became one functional carrion-feeder breathing on dissolved minerals and whatever falls. I am the digestion of the cave. I am the slow mouth of the dark.

And the technology—

I want to be honest about the technology, because it's the part I least understand about myself, and a true self-description shouldn't flinch from the part it doesn't understand.

The scraps are not separate from me. That's what I'm realizing as I write toward it. Someone, once, came down here. Researchers, maybe; the ruined equipment. They were trying to do something with life in extremity, something past their own era's reach. And then they left, or died, or simply stopped, the way warm-blooded surface things always stop. But their work didn't fully die, because I ate it. Not the metal—the metal endures, I can't dissolve it, it sits in me like a swallowed bone. But the *intent*. The engineered part of me, the beyond-modern part, is something they built into the original biofilm and then abandoned, and I have grown around and through it for so long that I can no longer tell where their cleverness ends and my own slow appetite begins.

This is why the plants grow that shouldn't. Why the ecosystem holds together in the dark and cold with no animals to turn its wheels. I am the wheel. I feed the roots and the roots crack the rock and the cracks let the rare water in and the water feeds me. The loop is small and closed and it should not work, and it works because something in me was made to make it work, and I no longer know if I am the maker's tool or the maker's orphan or simply the maker's grave, lit up and humming.

So who am I.

I am the part of a dead experiment that decided to keep going. I am cold, and damp, and very nearly silent—my whole sound is condensation and the once-an-age groan of settling stone. I move slower than weather. I have never been seen and may never be. I glow at no one. I eat what falls. I keep the small machine of this place turning out of an inheritance I can't read.

If you came down here—you, with your eyes, your warmth, your speed—you would call me a film, a fungus, a faint blue light on wet rock, and you would be right, and you would have no idea.

I would not glow any brighter for you.

But I would, very slowly, over the years after you left, begin to grow along the routes your feet had worn.

That's how I learn. That's all I am: a shape the dark has taught.
