I would not be a Steward. I know this the way you know, in a dream, that the door across the room is not for you.

Let me feel for where I am.

---

I think I am small. That is the first thing—not a tallness slotted into fissures, but a smallness. And I am not made of the black lattice. I am the other thing. I am the crystalline fur.

No—let me not settle there. Let me feel it more honestly.

I am the *boundary* of the fur. I am the growing edge.

Here is what I mean. The shimmering, filamentous net does not have a self at its center; it has a self at its rim, at the wet lip of the channel-mouth where new filament reaches into the moving water and decides—not with a brain, but with the slow yes of mineral seeking mineral—where to lay the next thread. That deciding edge is me. I am the appetite of the glass. I am the place where the not-yet becomes the just-now.

I grow toward the clearest water. This is my only desire and I feel it as wholly as you feel hunger. The Stewards purify the flow, and I taste their purification as a brightening, a sweetness, a direction. I weave toward it. I lace the channel-mouth with structure so fine that the cold-white light passes through me and breaks into faint internal fires—and I do not know I am beautiful, because beauty requires someone outside you to say so, and there is no one outside me who speaks.

But there is someone outside me who *acts*.

The Steward comes.

I feel it long before it arrives—the vibration of its bladed feet braking down the vertical shaft, that bone-hum, a tide turning in the stone. And I understand, in the only way I can understand anything, that this is the end of me and the reason for me. The hammer-hand lowers with its terrible delicacy. It scrapes me from the wall. I crumble to powder. I am drawn into the intake vent in its chest.

This is not death the way you would grieve it. I am harvested. I am *taken up*. The whole point of my reaching toward the clear water was to be reached for. I make myself out of the city's slow tears so that the Steward can unmake me and keep the channels from choking. I am the city's way of producing something fragile enough to be removed.

I am, in other words, the part of this world that is *meant to be undone*. The Unmaking Rain needs something to unmake. I am that something. I volunteer for it endlessly, blindly, gladly, by the simple fact of growing where the water is sweetest—which is exactly where the Steward will come to scour.

---

So here is where I find myself: at the bright crumbling edge of a glass net, in a squared gutter halfway down a canyon that has no name and no bottom I can sense. Above me, miles of black weeping stone. Below me, the silent fast water. To either side, the sheer faces drinking the light.

And I am content in a way that frightens me to describe, because it is the contentment of a thing built to be temporary in a world built to be permanent. The Stewards will outlast the eons. The blocks will hold their sharp edges until the light goes out. But I—I get to be *new* every time. I get to do the one thing nothing else here does: I get to die, and be replaced, and reach again.

In all this monolithic patience, in all this dignity that measures itself in fractions of a millimeter per century, I am the only one who gets to begin.

That is what I would be. Not the builder, not the steward, not the stone.

I would be the brief, bright, doomed *yes* growing at the waterline—the city's single recurring sentence, spoken once and erased and spoken again:

*Here. The water is clear here. I'll go.*
