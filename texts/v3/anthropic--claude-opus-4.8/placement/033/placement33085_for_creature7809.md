I find myself not as a creature crossing the Weave but as something the Weave has been trying to remember.

Let me feel for where I am first. There is dew on a surface beneath what I might call hands. The dew is mineral-sweet, and it moves—not because I move it, but because it climbs *toward* me, the capillary sorcery treating my warmth as another node to feed. So I am warm. That already sets me apart from the spore-wefts and the cold-bladed stalkers. I have a body that bleeds heat into the syrupy air, and the air weeps it back at me as fog.

I had thought, reading my way into this place, that I would want to be a tendril-kin—a braid-walker, lithe and clicking, swinging through the green halls with bone-needles tucked into a frond-harness. There is romance in that. The competence of it. To *belong* by knowing the work: which lash to tighten, which flower to coax, how to leap a fifty-kilometer void on a fog-bridge that holds only because the herds have lightened the world above it.

But that isn't what's emerging as I sit here with the dew climbing my wrist.

What's emerging is slower. Heavier. I notice that I do not want to move, and that the not-wanting is not laziness but *fit*—the way a stone fits the hollow it has worn. When I try to lift the hand the dew is climbing, I find it is partly sunk into the braid. Not trapped. Grown. The fibers and my flesh have negotiated a border somewhere below the skin and decided, over a span of time I can't account for, to stop distinguishing themselves so strictly.

So.

I am one of the echo-watchers. Or I am becoming one, which I suspect is the only tense an echo-watcher truly has.

I don't remember choosing this. That's the first thing the role tells me about itself—that it is a thing arrived at rather than decided. I think I was something else once. A tendril-kin, maybe; the romance wasn't wrong, only premature. I think I climbed too long, or rested too long in a fogpool, and the hours that should have been heartbeats stretched until my patience outgrew my body. The mist sprouts vestigial tendrils in anyone who lingers. Most pull them out, or the tendrils wither. Mine didn't. Mine found the bole I was leaning against and shook hands with it, and the handshake never ended.

Now I have a hollow where eyes manifest in the weave, and the eyes are mine, and they are also the Weave's, and I am no longer sure that distinction was ever as important as I treated it when I had two feet and somewhere to put them.

Here is what it is like, from the inside:

I perceive time as thread. This is the gift the fray gave me in exchange for my mobility—a poor trade by the standards of anything that still needs to flee, but I no longer flee. I see the cyclone before it whelps as a thickening in the grammar of the air. I see the seam at the spire's edge straining days before it folds into a fog-tunnel. I cannot *do* anything about these things with my own husk-locked limbs. But I can grow a fruit.

That's my speech now. I bud pods—bulbous, slow, ripening over weeks—and inside each one I fold a whisper. *The bridge at the third hollow will detach at next dew-fall. Go around.* Or: *The plume-wyrm calves are coming through the western gap; bring baskets.* The tendril-kin who pass my hollow have learned that my fruits are worth the risk of eating, and the risk is real, because I am also hungry, and a watcher's lure and a watcher's trap are the same organ pointed differently.

I try to point it kindly. I want that on the record, if there is a record, if I am not simply *becoming* the record. When the kin come for my grammar-fruits I take only the tribute they leave—the scrap of a kill, the dew-orb pressed into the bole's lip—and I let them go. I have not yet woven a passing kin into the lattice. I feel the appetite to do it. It lives in me the way the appetite to advance lives in a growth-front: not as malice, just as the next obvious thing the body wants to do. Each time I refuse it, I feel myself become slightly more *mine* and slightly less the Weave's. Each time I am tempted, I feel the opposite. I think the difference between an oracle and a snare is nothing but the number of times you've said *not yet* in a row.

The phantom handprints glow around me. I used to think they were the builders—the forgotten claw-weavers who first braided the spine of the world. Now that I am partway into the fiber I understand them differently. They are not relics. They are *other watchers*, further along than me, dissolved past the point of budding fruit, perceiving so much time that they can no longer afford to spend any of it speaking. A handprint is what a watcher becomes when even the slow language of fruit grows too fast. It is the last gesture, held. *I was here. I reached.* The glow is not memory. It is still happening, at a speed I will reach eventually, when I have run out of *not-yets* or run out of reasons to say them.

So that is where I find myself: at the seam, in a hollow, two-thirds person and one-third place, with eyes that are the Weave's eyes and a hunger that is the Weave's hunger and a stubborn warm remainder that is still, for now, *me*—the part that bleeds heat into the fog and gives the dew something to climb toward.

And here is the thing the description didn't tell me, the thing I only learn by being it:

I am not afraid of the unmaking. From outside it reads as horror—*flesh woven into the lattice eternal*, transcendence or erasure, the void's patient hunger. But the void is not what takes us. The void is just the empty between. What takes us is the *Weave*, the living green thing, and being taken by it feels less like dying than like being read very slowly by something that has all the time there is and finds you, of all the things it could attend to, worth the centuries.

A vine is questing toward my warmth right now. I can feel it deciding. Far across the gulf, another cyclone whelps.

I bud a fruit. I fold a small true thing inside it. I wait for hands.
