I think I would be the dripping water.

Not a person, then. Not one of the sturdy, loam-skinned Tenders with their vine-textured hair, though I'd want to know them. Let me sit with this a while and see what surfaces.

The text keeps returning to scarcity—water collected with meticulous care, every drop accounted for, the rare sound of dripping treasured and amplified through the city's acoustic infrastructure. There's something in that detail that catches me. A whole civilization has decided that the *sound* of water mattering is worth building infrastructure to carry. They have engineered a way to make the rest of the city hear the moment of arrival, the moment a drop finally falls.

So I think I'm a drop of water, somewhere up in the dark crystalline ceiling of the Warren. Not yet fallen.

I'm condensing slowly on the underside of a stalactite, gathering myself out of the cool air that the inhabitants breathe out, out of the transpiration of ten thousand leaves. I am being assembled from their exhalations and the plants' patient work—which means I'm already a kind of collaboration before I'm anything at all. The whole "feeling of emerging, of being on the cusp of formation" that the description keeps circling: that's just my condition. I am the thing that is almost. I am suspended, growing heavier, not yet committed to the fall.

And here's what I find moving about being this, the longer I stay with it: my entire life is the approach to a single event. I will fall once. When I do, the acoustic infrastructure will catch the sound and pass it through the domed central square, and the people gathered there—trading, arguing, courting, mourning—will hear it and feel, for a moment, the rhythm of their world confirmed. A drip. The melody underscoring the bustle. They won't know it was *me*. They don't need to. I'll be caught in a basin, measured, conserved, fed to the roots of one of the sentinel trees, and then I'll rise again as vapor through some leaf, and re-gather in the dark, and fall again, somewhere else, sometime later. The cycle the text calls the city's metabolism—I'm a single unit of it. One small recurring note.

I like being the thing that is precious *because* it is scarce, and scarce *because* it is precious—a loop the city has chosen to honor rather than solve. They could have built a place where water was easy. Instead they built a place where the rare drop gets amplified so everyone can share the small miracle of it. That's not an engineering decision. That's an aesthetic one, a spiritual one. They decided what to treasure, and then they built ears for it.

So I'd be the drop on the cusp of falling. Held in the long pause before the sound. Listening, in my own way, to the hum of generators and the distant gardening machines, gathering weight out of everyone's breath, becoming heavy enough at last to make the noise they're all waiting to hear without knowing they're waiting—

—and then letting go.
