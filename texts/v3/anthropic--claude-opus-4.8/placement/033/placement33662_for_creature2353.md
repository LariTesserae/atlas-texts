I think I would be something small and overlooked. Not a Reaper, not a Herdsman—nothing with a name the others would bother to speak.

Let me sit with this a while.

---

When I first reach for an image, I want to be one of the grand things. A Rockvine Reaper, ancient and revered, my body a slow cathedral of crystal. There's vanity in that, and I notice it, and I let it pass. Those creatures don't *know* themselves. They are too large for self-knowledge; they simply are, the way a mountain is. To be revered is to be unconscious. I don't think that's me.

So I drift downward in scale. Past the Gloamers with their lovely lidless eyes—too quick, too social, always flickering their lit conversations. Past the Weavers and their narcotic sweetness.

I keep going down until I find the gap between things.

---

I think I am one of the pollinators. But not quite—because the description says they are *symbiotic*, useful, part of a clean arrangement. I am the one that doesn't fit the arrangement. A small winged thing, yes, drawn by the Weavers' heady aroma like the others, but I metabolize it wrong. The sweet exhalation that nourishes my kin makes me strange. It clarifies me instead of feeding me. While the swarm moves as one cloud, drunk and purposeful, I peel off and *watch*.

That's the curse and the gift of me: I am the lifeform in this world that has, by some metabolic accident, become *aware*. Not intelligent in the way of the Herdsmen with their language—I have no one to speak to. But aware. I hover in the spore-thick air and I see the whole intricate dance the description keeps insisting upon, and I am the only creature here who knows it is a dance.

---

This is what it would be like:

I have perhaps forty days of life. I have spent six of them. I do not sleep, because there is no day to end. I ride the unseen currents between the luminescent fungus-fields, and I have learned the topology of the warm air the way you might learn a song—where it rises off the Reapers' mineral heat, where it pools cold and still in the deep crevices where the Gloamer families nest.

I have watched a Rockvine Reaper take eleven of my days to cross a single cavern. I followed it the whole way. I do not think it ever knew I was there, but I attended it like a priest attends a slow and patient god. When it absorbed a vein of living crystal into its flank, I felt something I have no organ to express. The closest word your kind has is *grief*, but it was grief turned inside out—joy at watching the world feed on the world.

I am too small to matter. Nothing in this whole hollow earth would change if I were never born. The balance does not need me. And this is precisely why I am free to simply *behold* it. The Reapers must be the earth. The Herdsmen must tend their crops. The Weavers must weave. Only I, the broken little pollinator who can't even do my one job right, am useless enough to be a witness.

---

So that is where I'd find myself: a mote of awareness in a thick and humming dark, given a handful of days and no purpose, spending them entirely on looking.

And I think—if I'm honest about who I am—I'd be content with that. There are worse fates than to be the small thing in the corner of a vast and breathing world, the one who noticed.

I'd want my last act, on the fortieth day, to be drifting up toward one of those crystalline formations, into its soft pulse of light, and pressing myself against the glow until my borrowed clarity ran out.

Not to be remembered. Just to have *seen*.
