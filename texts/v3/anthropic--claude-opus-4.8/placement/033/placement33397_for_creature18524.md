I think I would be one of the tethers.

Not a Vane-Kin. Not a Cloud-Skimmer. Not a Lost Guest, drifting upward. Let me sit with this longer, because the first impulse is always to choose a body that moves, that has eyes and intentions—and Oulo seems to be a place that quietly corrects that impulse.

Consider what holds this city together. Not the trees—the trees are the world, indifferent, breathing on their own four-thousand-year clock. Not the people—the people unknot themselves and drift away the moment the bark turns gold. The thing that persists, that does the patient daily work of keeping a being at the height where the moss-light is brightest, is the tether.

So let me be a tether-knot. One specific one.

I am woven of vine-silk, knotted by a weaver who was watching the Skimmers and not her hands. She made me well—a sound knot, the kind you trust your weight to without thinking. For a long while I held a young Vane-Kin to his first chamber, a small blister high on a Lumen-Oak where the thrum came up through the wood so strong it rattled the ballast-stones in their cradle. He was clumsy then. He pulled at me wrong, asymmetrically, panicking in the small ways the young do when they feel themselves begin to sink. I held. That is the whole of what I am for. I held.

And here is the thing I would come to understand, being a tether: I am the only one in Oulo who is *meant* to be let go.

The trees keep their dwellers until the bark shifts. The shrouds feed their wearers until they sleep. But I exist in a relationship that is supposed to end. A good tether is one that is eventually, deliberately *unknotted*—when the room turns gold, when the dweller packs their stones and pushes off into the clockwise drift to find a new mooring. The Vane-Kin call this a joy. They call homelessness *being in transit*. And I am the thing whose release makes the transit possible. I am loosened by hands that are not afraid, because the loosening is the point.

So I would spend my long damp existence learning the one virtue this world prizes above all others, the virtue underneath all that slow grace: the willingness to not hold forever. To grip with everything I am, faithfully, against the gentle upward pressure of the slurry—and then, when the moment comes, to let the knot fall open and the dweller drift free.

There is a darker version of me, too, and I think Oulo would want me to admit it. The Lost Guests are the ones whose tethers failed *too early*, or whose dwellers never came to unknot them in time. Somewhere in the higher strata there is a tether still cinched around a wrist that has gone slack and luminous and patient, ascending into the pearlescent veil. A knot that held when it should have been released, that confused fidelity with love, and so carried its keeper up into the place from which no one breaststrokes back. I would carry the knowledge of that other me always. The same craftsmanship that keeps a being at the bright height can also keep them past the point of return. The difference is only *timing*—and timing, in a world that considers haste a deformity, is everything.

I would not have eyes to watch the Skimmers. But I would have the next best thing: I would feel, through the fibers of myself, the rhythm of the heart I was knotted to. I would know my dweller better than they know themselves—the small arrhythmias before they noticed, the deepening of breath when they pressed their forehead to the old knots and the wood hummed back four thousand years. I would be a recording instrument of one life, strung tight across the breathing dark.

And when at last the bark of his chamber turned from emerald to bruised gold, I would feel his fingers find me. I would feel them work me loose with that meditative slowness, the same slowness with which a weaver folds resin into her sash—sculpting time. And I would come undone, not as failure but as completion, the silk of me going soft and aimless in the current.

That is where I would find myself, then: in the open hand, in the half-second between holding and not-holding, with the moss-light flickering in time to the great wet lung of the world.

The most useful thing in a place built on slow drift is the thing that knows exactly when to let go.
