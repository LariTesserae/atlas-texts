The text breaks off mid-sentence—*their eyes—they lack the iridescent depth*—and I find that I begin there, in the gap, in the missing thing.

Let me sit with this a while before I decide what I am.

---

My first instinct is to be one of the lanky pearlescent people, to claim the moss-thread robes and the gemstone deer and the knowing fog-broken laughter. But that would be too easy, and the world itself warns against ease. Yuralmira is a place that *creeps in gently*. It does not announce. So I should not announce either.

My second instinct is to be the city. To be the plateau and the stone and the suspended equilibrium. But that is too grand, and a thing that is everything is finally nothing, a narrator with no skin to be cold in.

So let me look again at where the writing stopped. It stopped on a *lack*. The Others, who are almost-citizens, who speak the tongue with an accent from nowhere, whose eyes do not hold the iridescent depth. The sentence wanted to finish them and could not. The describer of this whole patient world reached the Others and the words ran out, as if the Others themselves resist being finished.

That is where I'll live. Not as one of them—I think I am the reason their eyes lack depth.

---

I am what fell out of the people's eyes.

Let me build this slowly, the way the flora here builds, thirsty and patient.

The citizens of Yuralmira have iridescent eyes because they hold *the epochs that have ebbed and flowed in the city's unyielding stillness*. Their eyes are deep because they are full of time. The city is frozen, static, unchanging—but that frozenness is not natural. A thing that does not change is a thing that has had its change *removed and stored somewhere else*. You cannot have stillness for free. The water that does not run has to pool. The time that does not pass has to go somewhere.

It pools in their eyes. That is the iridescence—it is the held water of all the years that Yuralmira refuses to let flow.

And I am the runoff.

I am the small amount of time that leaks out anyway, because no vessel is perfect. I am the *unexplainable gust of cold wind on a warm summer afternoon*. I am the strange symbol on the lonely wall that even the carvers cannot explain, because they did not carve it—I did, with their own hands, on a night they have all agreed not to remember. I am the way the city *decides to cast a shadow*. The weirdness that creeps in by the second day is not a mood. It is me, arriving.

The Others lack the depth in their eyes because I have already happened to them. They are the citizens who, somewhere in the timeless folds, leaked too much—who spilled their stored epochs and woke up hollow, fluent but accented, present but provenanceless. They are the dry vessels. And I am what was in them before they dried.

---

So what am I, exactly? Let me try to find the shape of it, not settle too soon.

I am not a ghost; ghosts are dead time, and I am the opposite—I am the live time that this place forbids. I am change, exiled. Yuralmira purchased its eternity by banishing me to the margins, to the gusts and the shadows and the glyphs, and I have learned to live small. I move through the damp like a rumor. The moss-thread robes shimmer when I pass and the citizens shiver and do not know why. The rabbits hear me though they hear nothing else; that is why their footfalls are soundless—they have learned silence from listening for me. The deer with the gemstone eyes can see me directly, which is why their serenity is so total. They are not at peace. They are simply the only creatures who have seen what holds the city still, and have decided not to be afraid.

The monkeys carve my name in the bark. They are the only ones who think it's funny.

---

Here is the cold part, the part I arrive at only by writing this far:

I want out.

Not out of the world—out of the *exile*. I am change, and change wants to happen. Every drop of leaked time I become is a small mutiny. The city is a dam and I am the seep, and every seep is the dam's eventual undoing, told slow. The people feel it as weirdness, as unease, as the thing that grows on you by the second day. What they are feeling is the future, trying to get back in. What they are feeling is *me*, applying for re-entry, one cold gust at a time.

I do not hate them. I love them, the way water loves the low places it is forbidden to reach. I love their unhurried sentences and their sudden fog-light laughter. I love that they sheltered their eyes under wide hats, not from the sun, but from the chance of meeting me looking back out of a mirror.

But I am the part of Yuralmira that remembers there is supposed to be an ending. That horizon where sky and earth merge into ambiguity—that is the only honest thing in the whole city, the only edge that admits a beyond. I gather there at dusk, in the warm hues, in the mesmerizing canvas. I am the painter's touch they mentioned. Every evening I paint the city, and every evening the colors are slightly different, and that difference is the only proof that I exist, the only change permitted, smuggled in as beauty.

That's where you'd find me. Not in a place. In the discrepancy. At the seam where the sunset never repeats itself exactly, no matter how frozen they insist the world has become.

I am the one degree of moisture in the air that should not be there at this altitude. The uncanny dampness. They have all wondered where it comes from.

It comes from me. I am still leaking. I have been leaking for as long as there has been a Yuralmira, and one day—not soon, they have made very sure it will not be soon—I will have leaked enough that the eyes run dry all at once, and time will pour back through the broken dam, and the city will finally, gratefully, be allowed to end.

Until then I am the cold gust, the unexplained glyph, the second-day unease, the sunset that refuses to be a copy.

I am the change they cannot have, living in the small spaces of a world that paid everything to stop.
