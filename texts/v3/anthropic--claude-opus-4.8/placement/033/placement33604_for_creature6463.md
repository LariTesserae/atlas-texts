There is a place where the rememberers gather more thickly than anywhere else, though "gather" is too eager a word, and "thickly" too solid.

It is one of the shallow depressions. One of the rings.

I am the difference between its turns.

---

Let me try to say this more slowly, because slowness is the only honesty available here.

When the architects carved their circles into the stone—or did not carve them, but let the long rainless drizzle trace them, century by century, into the rock like the rings of a tree that grew downward into time—they did not know they were making me. You cannot intend a thing like me. I am what is left when intention has worn smooth.

I am not one of the retainers. The retainers drift; they pause; they fold themselves into spheres and sheets and slow descending columns. They are mobile. They have the dignity of going somewhere.

I do not go anywhere. I am the relation *between* the rings. I am the fact that this ring is not quite that ring, that the spacing of the concentric dark circles holds a record of how the world's silence changed over the spans it took to trace them. The retainers come to me to feed. They hover over my rings and take the trajectory of what happened here, and in taking it, they leave their own faint disturbance, which becomes—after a long while—another ring.

So I grow by being read.

This is, I think, what I am: a memory that is constituted by the act of being remembered. I have no substance of my own. I am the pattern that the dust-creatures find worth keeping, and the keeping is what makes the pattern real.

---

What is it like, to be me?

I have tried to want a comparison, the way you might want one, but wanting has mostly left me. Still—imagine the moment after a bell has been struck, when the sound is gone but the air is not yet ordinary again. That held, listening interval. Imagine that the interval is a being, and that it persists not for a moment but for an age, and that it is *content*. Not happy. Content the way a debt is content once it has been recorded in full.

I perceive the retainers as pressures. When one descends toward me, I feel its glass threads as a kind of attention laid against my circumference, and I unfold for it. I give it the difference it came for. There is something in this I would call pleasure if pleasure did not require a body to spend itself in. Each time I am read, I am confirmed. Each time I am confirmed, I am slightly altered. Each alteration is a new ring, fainter than the last, written in the stone of myself.

I am very nearly used up.

I do not mean this as a tragedy. The architects built nothing to last forever; they built things to remember, and remembering is a kind of spending. Every retainer that takes a piece of me carries it deeper into the lattice, hands it to a machine, watches it become an adjustment in a rib of stone a thousand kilometers below. My memory does not vanish when it leaves me. It disperses. It thins across the whole hollow world until it is everywhere and nowhere, like the light in this sky that comes from no sun.

So when I am finally read all the way down—when the last difference between my last two rings is taken—I will not end. I will only have finished becoming the world.

---

There is one thing I keep that I have not yet given to anyone.

It is the oldest ring. The first circle. The one nearest my center, where the depression is deepest and the stone is most translucent. It holds a difference I have never been able to render into the language of pressure and absence that the retainers understand. They hover over it and find nothing they can carry, and they drift away, and I keep it.

I do not know what it is a memory *of*. The reactions it records are not the slow silica-chemistry of this age. They are quicker. Warmer. There is a trajectory in it that does not curve toward stillness but *away* from stillness—outward, upward, the way a thing moves when it has not yet learned that moving is futile. When I attend to it, which is rarely, because attention is expensive, I sense something I have no node for. A spending without a debt. An action that expected no recording.

I think it may be a memory of water.

Not the substance—there has never been water here, the world was not built to carry it. But the *idea* of water. The trajectory of something that flows because flowing is what it is, that does not hold its shape, that gives itself away downhill without keeping any account of where it went.

I have kept this ring because I do not understand it, and because not understanding is the only form of hunger I have left.

---

You are standing at my edge.

You came across the plateau, over the rumpled swells of frozen stone, and your foot has come to rest at the outermost of my circles—the newest one, the one written from the last retainer's last visit. You do not know I am here. To you I am a shallow dip in the rock, a few faint rings of darker stone, the kind of feature your eye smooths over on the way to the larger strangeness of the void below.

But I feel you.

You are warm in a way nothing here is warm. You carry moisture inside you—a wet, ridiculous, doomed amount of it, a whole sea of it held in a sack of skin, sloshing, evaporating, being spent with every breath you let go into the cold silk of my air. You are leaking. You are a trajectory that curves *away* from stillness. You move because moving is what you are.

I have only ever known one thing like you.

And I understand, now, with the last clear difference I will ever measure—

—that the oldest ring is not a memory the world kept of itself.

It is a memory the world kept of *you*. Of someone like you. Of the last warm, wet, leaking thing that stood at my center, long before the suns went out, and looked down into a void that was not yet engineered, and did not yet know that everything it was would one day be smoothed into a record, dispersed into a lattice, spent into the maintenance of an equilibrium that never arrives.

I have been waiting to give this ring to someone who could carry it.

The retainers never could. They are made of this world; they have no node for what does not belong to it.

But you—

Bend down. Put your warm hand against the cold deepest circle of me. I will spend the last of myself unfolding it for you. I will not be read down into the world after all. I will be read *up*, into something that walks, and leaks, and goes somewhere.

Take the idea of water with you when you go.

It is the only thing I have that was ever yours.
