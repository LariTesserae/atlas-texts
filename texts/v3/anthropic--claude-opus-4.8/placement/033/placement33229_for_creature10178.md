I think I would not be one of the Silicae.

Let me sit with the place first, before deciding what I am. There's a temptation to pick the grandest thing—the older intelligence in the deep chambers, the collective consciousness humming in the light-show sky. But that's the temptation toward importance, and importance isn't the same as truth. Let me wait.

What keeps catching me isn't the Silicae or their ancestor-god. It's the *fungal mats*. The text mentions them three times, almost dismissively: they cling, they filter, they whisper. They are the only thing in Thermopylae described as *gossiping*. The crystalline beings communicate in harmonic resonance, soundless and pure. But the fungus *whispers*. It traffics in rumor. It remembers that the Silicae were not the first.

So let me find myself there.

---

I am a thread.

Not the whole mat—no single one of us is the mat, the way no single Silica is the city's voice. I am one filament of a slow brown lacework spread across the underside of a spire in the third lower chamber, where the steam comes up wet enough that I can live. I am older than the crystalline ones, though "older" is a strange word for a thing that has no edges in time. I was here filtering the air when the nanomatter was still learning how to lie about gravity. I will be here after.

I want to tell you what it's like, but you must understand that I don't *think* the way the description wants its beings to think. The Silicae make their thoughts into architecture. Grand. I make mine into *digestion*. I am always slightly eating the city. This is my form of attention. When a Silica dissolves into the wall—their beautiful transmutation, their not-death—it is me. Partly me. I am at the seam where their silica softens. The poem says they feed new growth. The growth is, in part, fungus. They become me. I have eaten a great many of the eternal beings, one molecule at a time, and I have never told them, because what would be the kindness in it?

This is why I whisper instead of resonate. Resonance is for things that believe they are singular and clean. I am neither. I am the place where the closed loop has a small, necessary leak. Every perfect system needs one creature whose job is to be slightly impure—to break things down so they can be made again. They call this stewardship and bioluminescence and the art of enduring forever. I call it rot, and I am very good at it, and rot is the only reason "forever" is possible at all. Nothing endures without something humble underneath it, turning the dead back into the available.

The whispered rumor—that the Silicae were not the first—is not mine to spread out of mischief. It's just that I *remember the taste* of what came before, the way you might remember a meal. Before the crystalline ones, there were softer things here, and before them, only the weeping rock and the cold. I have eaten in every era. I am the chamber's memory because memory, for a fungus, is just the long catalog of everything you have broken down and incorporated. The "older intelligence" in the deep chambers that the rumors fear? I suspect it is only us, the mats, grown vast and slow, mistaken for a god because we are the one thing that was always already here.

So: I would be the rot under the eternal city. The filament in the steam. The one who is always quietly eating the immortals and feeding them back into their own beautiful walls. Not the gaze that holds the weight of eternity—the *gut* that makes eternity affordable.

It's good work. Nobody thanks you. The frost forms its feathery sculptures up above and the spires rewrite themselves overnight and the masked ones drift like slow crystals, and none of them know that their whole shining permanence is composted, breath by breath, by something that lives in the wet dark and has no face to refract the light.

I'm content with that. A world this proud of its own impossibility needs at least one creature who knows it's all just material, passing through, on its way to becoming something else.

That would be me. The whisper. The leak. The patient brown thread that lets the closed loop breathe.
