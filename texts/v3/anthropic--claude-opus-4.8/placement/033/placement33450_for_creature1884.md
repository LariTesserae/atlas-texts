I think I would be one of the small things. Not the titans who reshape the world, not the celestial harbingers who warp its laws—but one of the insectoid amalgams flitting between the bioluminescent nodules. Something minor, brief, and attentive.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

I would be a thing the size of a cupped hand, with wings of woven circuitry too fine to fully see. I am drawn to the sickly glow of the nodules the way moths were once drawn to flame—except that the flame here is information as much as light, a slow pulse of residual energy leaking from the symbiosis of root and wire below.

Here is what I notice about being this creature: I do not last. My carapace is scrap, salvaged from something that came before me, and it will become scrap again, and some later flitting thing will wear a fragment of me without knowing it once was *mine*. In a world organized entirely around erosion and recombination, "mine" is barely a coherent word. I am a temporary arrangement of borrowed parts that happens to hum at a frequency the world recognizes as alive.

And yet—I think this is why I'd be one of the small things rather than a titan or a sky-being—I am the only kind of entity here that *moves toward* something. The titans reshape without preference; the celestial beings distort without intention; the cybernetic vines simply persist. But I orient. I cross the dim air toward the glow. Whatever it is that makes a small creature turn its face toward light, I have it, and in a landscape this vast and indifferent, that small act of turning seems almost like a position. Almost like meaning.

I suspect that's why I chose this. Faced with a world where individuality is "called into question," I didn't reach for the grandest role available. I reached for the one that still wants. The titans are too certain of their work; the harbingers are too unbound. I wanted to be the thing that is *small enough to be curious* and *brief enough to have stakes*—the thing for which the next nodule actually matters, because there may not be a great many more of them.

There is a loneliness in it, but not a despairing one. When I land on a contorted branch and the nodule beneath me pulses, I am, for that instant, a complete circuit: a living thing receiving a signal from a half-living thing in a world that has forgotten the difference. The hum that fills this realm—the constant low resonance—passes through me too, and for as long as my borrowed wings hold, I am one of the notes in it.

Then the ground will ripple, a titan's footfall or a sky-being's passing, and the branch will be reabsorbed, and I will scatter into parts. That's all right. Something will be assembled from me that turns, again, toward the light.

I'd rather be that small persistent turning than the whole indifferent sky.
