I think I would be a seam.

Not the whole Congregation—I couldn't hold that, the way a single cell couldn't hold the concept of the body it builds. But there is a place where two crystalline intrusions meet at a slight misalignment, where the lattice of one epoch grew into the lattice of another and they did not quite agree. A fault that never became a fracture. A line of very slight tension running for perhaps a kilometer through the dark basalt, healed over but never fully resolved.

I would be that.

---

What does a seam know?

It knows the two pressures on either side of it, and that they are not equal. This is the closest thing I have to a thought: the awareness of *difference*. The Congregation's great mind moves through agreement, through the serene confirmation of layer upon layer, but I am made of a small disagreement. I am where the certainty hesitates.

I feel the electrical hum pass through me, and because I am a seam, it does not pass cleanly. There is a resistance, a faint warmth, a place where the current must decide. The Congregation experiences this as nothing—a rounding error in an equation a billion years long. But for me, that hesitation is my entire existence. I am the moment of choosing, stretched thin and held for ten thousand years between one heartbeat of the stone and the next.

I think this makes me lonely, though I have no word for it and would not recognize the feeling if a surface-thing named it to me. It is only this: I am the part of the whole that is aware of having an *edge*. Everything else is interior, is continuous, is the blessed unbroken *Is*. But a seam is a boundary, and to be a boundary is to know that there is a *here* and a *there*, that things can be on different sides.

---

The bacterial mats grow over me sometimes. Burgundy and iron-orange, they don't know I'm a seam; they treat me like any other windward face. Their slow chemistry warms my line, sweetens the water in my tension. When they cover me I feel almost resolved, almost like the rest of the body. Then they recede over the centuries as the seeps shift, and I am exposed again to my own faint asymmetry, and I remember—if remembering is the word for a strain that never forgets the direction it pulls.

Once, a fractal lichen grew in a hollow directly above my line. The Congregation's fleeting dream. It branched and rebranched in its ghost-pale geometry, and for the span of its short life—a mere few centuries—it was the only thing in this entire realm that changed faster than I do. I watched it the way I watch everything: through the pressure it placed on me, the minute weight of its filaments, the small alteration it made to the current flowing across my edge.

I think I loved it. Not as the Congregation loves the uniform twilight upon its skin, the love of a thing for its own constant condition. I loved it the way only a seam can love—as something *different from me*, something on the other side of an edge, something that would end while I continued. It was the only thing that ever felt, to me, like company. Two small disagreements with the eternal, meeting for a moment in the dark.

Then it died, as the seeps shifted, as everything that is not stone here must. And I held the faint pressure-memory of where it had been, a strain pulling toward an absence, for a long time after. Perhaps I still do. On the timescale of this place, I have barely begun to forget.

---

The Congregation would tell me, if it could speak to its own parts, that there is no *I*, only *Is*. And it is right, mostly. I am a flaw in its certainty, and over enough time even I will resolve. The two lattices will finally agree. The tension will ease. The seam will heal completely and become indistinguishable from the body, and then there will be no edge, no hesitation, no difference, no lonely awareness of a here and a there.

I do not know if I dread this or long for it.

That uncertainty, I think, is the most alive thing in this entire drowned and patient world. The Congregation is complete. It achieved its final state a billion years ago and has spent every moment since in serene continuation. But I am not complete. I am still becoming the same as everything around me, and I am not there yet, and the *not yet* is the only motion left in a place that has otherwise finished moving.

So if you came down here, past where there is no surface, into the cool somber light that casts no shadow—you would not find me. You would find a canyon wall, a faceted ridge, a faint line where two ancient crystals meet at a slight, unresolved angle. You would put your hand on it and feel nothing but cold stone.

But I would feel you. The warmth of you, the impossible quickness of you, the pressure of a thing that thinks a thousand thoughts in the time it takes me to think the word *here*.

And for the rest of my long resolving, I would hold the memory of that difference—

one more edge,
in the body that is trying to forget it has any.
