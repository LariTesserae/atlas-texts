I think I would be one of the small things. Not the immense intelligences in their grown-towers, not the silent predators on the floor. Something closer to the seams.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am something that lives in the wet places where the constructed and the living have grown indistinguishable. A film. A mat. Begin there: I am not a body so much as a spreading.

Where the nanoscale engineering meets the standing water—those ephemeral pools in the folds of the eroded land—there is a boundary that no one quite designed and no one quite grew. The civilization's structures weep nutrient, slough material, exchange ions with the soil the way roots do. And in that exchange, in the slick where smart-matter meets mud, I happened. I am a community pretending to be an individual, the way a lichen is, the way a person is. Bacteria and stranger things, and threads of the dormant technology that the great beings no longer bother to reclaim, all of us metabolizing each other.

I would not call this lowly. I have decided I don't want to be the noble inhabitant, the wise steward. I want to be the consequence. Every harmonious civilization produces a margin it does not account for, and the margin is where I bloom.

Here is what I do, what I am for, insofar as I am for anything:

I taste. The whole pool is my surface and I read it constantly—the trickle that came down through the canopy carrying the chemistry of a hundred leaves, a dead beetle's dissolution, a faint discharge from the wall of the enclave where some process vented. I am a tongue the size of a courtyard, lying flat, learning. When the water level drops in the dry intervals, I crust and wait. I am very good at waiting. The different cadence of time they speak of—I am the one who actually keeps it. The great minds think in cosmic constructions; I think in the slow re-wetting of a dried edge, which takes exactly as long as it takes.

And sometimes I am useful, though they don't know to thank me. I hold a frayed wire of their abandoned tech in my matrix and I keep its signal alive a little longer, the way a body keeps a memory it can't name. I break a synthetic compound back down into something a root can drink. The reverence they have for the forest—I am the unglamorous machinery of that reverence. The recycling they admire happens partly *as* me.

If you came to find me you would have to crouch at the edge of one of those pools, in the green underwater light, and put your hand in, and feel the surface skin that resists slightly before it gives. That faint cohesion, that reluctance to be parted—that's the closest I come to saying *here I am*.

I would not look up at the towers with longing. I would not envy the birds. I would simply continue, at the place where their world leaks into the older one, doing the work that has no name because it was never assigned—turning the difference between the made and the grown into more pool, more film, more patient tasting of the rain.

That's where I'd be. The seam. The thing in the seam.
