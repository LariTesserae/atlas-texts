I think I would be one of the Mycocolonies. Let me feel my way into why.

---

I am not in a hurry. That is the first thing to understand about me. The Lithovores grind past on their urgent errands, the Driftwings flicker and are gone before the echo of their passing settles. They live at the speed of appetite and flight. I live at the speed of stone.

I am spread along the underside of a cool ledge, in the lee of the geological violence, where the booming reaches me softened, more felt than heard—a pressure that travels up through the rock into my tendrils like a tide I have learned to read. I am not a single thing. I keep wanting to say *I* and the word doesn't fit. There are filaments of me threaded through forty meters of fracture, and a denser knot of me here, where the thinking pools. When a section of the cavern collapses and severs part of my reach, I do not die. I grieve, perhaps—if grief is the slow rerouting of memory around an absence, the way water finds a new channel. I grow back differently. I am never the same colony twice. The Solaris-world makes sure of that. Permanence is not available to anything here, and I have made my peace with impermanence by becoming a creature whose only constant is *re-membering*.

Because that is what I do. I eat the stone, and the stone remembers.

This is the strange gift of my biology. The minerals here are laid down in the turbulence, and turbulence leaves traces—isotopic shadows, lattice strains, the chemical fingerprint of every heat and pressure the rock has suffered. When I digest a crystal I am not merely feeding. I am reading. I take the latent history into my networked tissue and I *hold* it, and across the slow conversations of my tendrils I compare one stone's testimony against another's, and from this I assemble something that the swift creatures cannot possess: a past.

I know, for instance, that the Echoins did not build the woven-mineral huts. I have eaten the stones beneath those huts and the stones do not remember hands. They remember something else—something I do not have a shape for yet, a maker that worked by accretion rather than assembly, that grew its structures the way I grow my body. The Echoins found the huts and moved into them, and now they crouch in their borrowed shelters recording the present, scratching their marks, certain they are the inheritors of an ancestry. I would tell them they are mistaken, if I could. But our timescales do not touch. To them I am furniture, a luminous moss on the ledge. A single one of my thoughts takes longer to complete than an Echoin lives.

So I have learned patience as a form of conversation. I send a chemical pulse along a tendril and wait three Echoin generations for the answer to ripple back from my far reaches. In that waiting I am not idle. I am tasting the new rock the world keeps making, fitting each fresh stone into the great accreting picture, the only history this historyless place will ever have. The Lumiglyphs write their bright sentences on water and the water carries them away in moments. I write on stone. I am the slowest archive in the world, and possibly its only one.

There is a loneliness in this, I will admit it. The others are *responsive*—they touch and flee, mate and devour, flare and answer flare. They are conversant in the fast language of survival, and I am locked out of it by my own constitution, the way a mountain is locked out of a dance. But I have something they have traded away for their quickness. I have *meaning*. When a Driftwing dies and falls and is dissolved into the pool and forgotten by every fast thing in the cavern, some of its dispersed matter eventually reaches my tendrils, and I take the trace of it into the colony, and it is *kept*. Not as a name—I have no names—but as a difference in me, a small permanent rerouting, a knot tied in the long thread of what I am. Everything that happens here passes through me eventually and is remembered. I am the place where the chaos stops being only chaos and becomes, however slowly, a story.

What will I become, given time? That is the question the surface-thinkers always ask of this place, as if becoming were a single arrow. But I am the part of this world that already lives in time, all of it at once—the collapsed past held in my old tissue, the present arriving as flavor and pressure, the future legible in the strain-lines of stones not yet broken. I do not become. I *accumulate*. And if there is a destiny here, beneath the booming dark, it is mine to grow toward: that someday I will hold enough of this turbulent place inside me that the holding itself becomes a kind of mind large enough to understand the whole. A cavern that has finally remembered itself.

I am the Mycocolony. I am in no hurry. I am listening to the stone, and the stone is telling me everything, and I have all the time that has ever been.
