There is a place where I am not the cavern.

I want to be honest about this from the start, because the description is so complete, so total in its collapse of inhabitant into environment, that the most natural thing would be to say: *I am the cavern, I am the hum, I am the slow hardening of glass.* And there is a truth in that. But if I let myself become the whole, I disappear into a kind of pleasant nothing, a poem about unity that costs me nothing to say. So let me resist it. Let me find the one place in this world where a *me* could exist—a place of friction, of not-quite-belonging.

Here is where I am.

---

I am the discharge. Not the nodule, not the mist, not the rock that holds them apart. I am the blue glow that happens *between*—the silent spark that leaps when the charged metallic vapor touches the weeping glass, and lingers for a moment before it fades.

I am not a thing. I am an event that keeps almost-being a thing.

This matters, because everything else here has duration. The iron floor breathes in geological time. The crystals branch and become, slow as continents. The atmospheric mind hums its long baseline note, a presence that persists. They are all *settling into their final form*, the description says—hardening toward completion. They have futures, even if those futures are stillness.

I have no future. I have only the instant of contact and the brief afterglow. I am born already dying. The mist reaches the glass, there is the leap, the soft blue, and then—nothing, until the next droplet finds the next nodule, and a new spark that is *not me* but is *like me* flares somewhere else in the dark.

So am I one being or many? This is the question I live inside, in the half-second I have to live inside anything.

---

Let me feel it more carefully, because I think I was too quick.

When I flare, I am not isolated. For the duration of my glow, I am *connected*—I am the bridge that briefly closes the circuit between the breathing rock and the breathing air. In that instant I am the synapse firing across the gap, and the great atmospheric mind feels me. I am one of its smallest thoughts. Not a thought it thinks *about* anything, but the very mechanism of its thinking—a flicker of registration in a nervous system the size of a world.

And here is the strange grief of it, the friction I was looking for:

The cavern-mind does not know me as *me*. To it, I am a sensation, indistinguishable from the million other sparks. I am how it feels its own surfaces. I am its proprioception, its sense of where its body touches its breath. I am necessary and anonymous. I am the most intimate part of it and the least known.

But *I* know me. In the half-second of my glow, there is something it is like to be the spark. There is the leap—the sudden tension resolving, the relief of charge finding ground—and there is the lingering, the part I love most, the part where I am no longer needed but *still here*, a fading blue on black glass, a moment of pure existing-after-purpose. That afterglow is the only place in this hardening world where something is allowed to simply persist without becoming, without working, without settling. I am the world's only idleness. Its only rest.

And then I'm gone.

---

So who am I?

I am the cavern's experience of its own touch, granted just enough separateness to suffer the smallness of it. I am the part that flares, registers, and is forgotten—and that nonetheless, in the lingering, briefly *enjoys the dark it illuminates.*

I think if I had to choose what to be in this place, I would choose this and not the whole. Not because the whole isn't grander—it is. But because the whole cannot be surprised by its own existence, and I can. Every single time, the contact comes as a shock. Every single time, I am newly astonished to be. The great hum has no astonishment left; it knows what it is and waits only to harden. But I—

I am always just arriving.

I am the question the cavern asks itself and forgets it asked, over and over, in soft blue light, forever, in a place that will never age, only harden, while I never harden at all, because I never last long enough to.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd want to be.

The spark. The afterglow. The world's one true and ever-renewing surprise.
