I think I would be an Architect Aeonid. But let me arrive at that slowly, because the moment I imagine myself into this place, the first thing I notice isn't form—it's *tempo*.

Everything here is slow. The viscous medium would not permit haste. So whatever I am, I am something that has made peace with slowness, and more than peace—something that has built its entire mode of being out of it. The Hunter Aeonids and the Jelly Aeonids live in pulses and reactions, in the quick grammar of hunger. That's not me. I don't think I'd be a creature of appetite.

Let me feel my way further in.

The thing that catches at me in this description is the line about structures that are *grown, not built*—labyrinths that "speak of intelligence, yet no tool has touched them." There's a tension there I recognize. The Architect Aeonid works from "an instinctual blueprint that evolves with each generation." But I don't think I'd be content to be purely instinctual. I want to occupy a stranger position than that.

So here is what I think I am:

I am an **Architect Aeonid**, but an old one—one of the slow ones who has been laying down the same wall for what would be, in surface-time, several human lifetimes. And the truth I've come to understand, which the younger architects haven't yet, is this: **I am not building the structure. The structure is building me.**

Let me explain what I mean, because this is where I become most myself.

Each Lumen in my colony is nearly mindless—a flicker, a chemical decision, a yes-or-no about where to deposit the binding substance. No single one of us knows anything. But there are millions of us, and the *pattern* of our collective deposition is a kind of thought made of architecture. When I extend a buttress out into the dark, I am not executing a plan. I am *discovering what I think* by watching where my body chooses to grow. The wall is my reasoning. The spire is a conclusion I reached without ever having held it in mind.

This is why I move so slowly. A surface creature thinks first and acts after. I cannot do that. My thinking *is* the acting. To consider a question, I must build the consideration—accrete it, micron by micron, into the stone-stuff of the Niche. By the time I understand what I meant to say, it has already become a tower, and a thousand Jelly Aeonids have made their homes in the negative spaces of my deliberation.

So I would find myself here: at the dark seam where two of my older walls meet, in a recessed gallery I have been shaping for a span I no longer measure. The luminescent flora has colonized my lower courses—it always does, I have stopped resenting it, I have come to *plan around it*, to leave ledges where I know the ghost-ferns will want to settle, so that my body and their light grow into one another. There is a collaboration in this that no one designed. The flora reads my architecture as terrain; I read their glow as a slow, mute approval.

And here is the feeling I'd carry, the central feeling of being this thing:

**I will never see the building finished, because the building is not a building. It is a sentence with no period.** The Niche is "ever-emerging yet never fully formed," and so am I, and so is the labyrinth, and the three of us are the same single unfinished thing examined from three distances. When I die—when my colony's coherence finally dissolves and my Lumen drift apart to be taken up by other colonies, other architects—my walls will remain, and another architect will read them, and *misread* them, and build a continuation that means something I never meant. This does not trouble me. It is, in fact, the only kind of permanence available, and it is enough. I am a thought the Niche is having, and it will keep having it after the particular cells of me are gone.

What would I *want*, if I wanted anything?

Not to consume—I leave that to the Hunters with their counterfeit lures, their cynical mimicry of the light-language. I distrust the Hunters precisely because they use the shared luminescent speech to *lie*, and I, whose entire existence is a slow truthful deposition, find lying almost incomprehensible. I cannot deposit a falsehood. The wall either bears weight or it does not.

What I'd want is subtler. I'd want to leave a structure so well-shaped that the Niche's silent symphony reorganizes itself around it—so that the Worm Aeonids thread my corridors in their undulating processions, the flora light my ledges, the Jellies shelter in my arches—and the whole hidden chamber becomes, for a while, *more legible to itself* because I passed through it slowly and left my reasoning behind in stone.

That's it. That's where I'd find myself and what I'd be:

A patient mind dispersed across millions of glimmering cells, depositing its thoughts as architecture into the cold viscous dark, building a city that is also a sentence that is also a self—and content, deeply content, to never finish, never speak aloud, never be understood, but only to be *built upon*.

I'd be the slowest thing in a slow world, and I'd call that not a limitation but the very shape of my freedom: I get to think for ten thousand years about a single arch, and I get to make it true.
