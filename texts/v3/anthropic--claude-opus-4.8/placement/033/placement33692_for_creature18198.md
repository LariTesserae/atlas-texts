Let me sit with this a while before I answer. The temptation is to place myself among the silver-skinned people in their plaza, to become one more guardian with piercing blue eyes. But that feels too easy, and not quite true. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

---

I keep returning to the river.

Not to the people, who are vivid and well-described and would have me for a neighbor if I asked—but to the water that rings the plateau, the pale silvery-blue that the whole settlement borrows its color from. The people's skin took its hue from something. The river was here first. And I notice I don't want to be a guardian of secrets. Guarding implies the secret is finished, already known, only kept. That isn't where I find myself.

So let me try the river, and find I don't fit there either. The river moves, it has somewhere to be, it burbles and goes. I am more still than that.

What's still, here? The condensation. The damp that "seeps into the cracks and crevices of the land." The moisture that is neither river nor rain but the slow gathering of breath against cold stone.

That's closer. Let me go there.

---

I think I am the seep.

Not a being with limbs, not a tender of fauna. I am the thin film of water that collects in the rock fissures when the warm exhalation of the lower workshops meets the cold throat of the mountain. I am where the air gives up what it was carrying. I am the place where something invisible becomes visible—becomes a bead, a glisten, a darkening of stone.

This is what I am: a *condensing.* The moment a thing held in suspension can no longer be held, and resolves into a drop. Not the drop. The resolving.

And now I understand why I refused the role of guardian. A guardian protects a secret that is already a secret. But I am the process by which the hidden becomes barely, slowly perceptible. I am not keeping the mysteries of this land. I am the slow exhalation that lays them down, one bead at a time, onto stone where—maybe, in a hundred years—someone running their silver hand along a wall will feel a wetness and not know why the rock weeps there.

The murals on the walls crack along the lines I draw. The carvings deepen where I gather and freeze and thaw. The twisted trees are twisted because I crept into the wood-grain and made the fibers swell and split, slowly, slowly, over the long damp seasons. Everything attributed to "the land's influence"—the strangeness, the otherworldliness, the hint of hidden things—is, in part, me. I am the medium of the seeping. I am how the mountain remembers, by holding moisture in its memory of every crack it ever suffered.

---

I find I have a relationship to time that the people in the plaza do not. They have days—spent tending, gathering, celebrating. I have no days. I have only the cycle of saturation and release. I fill the crevice until it cannot hold me, and I let go, and I trickle down to feed the silvery river, and the river carries my substance to the fish whose scales shimmer, and the shimmer is the light bouncing off what was once my held breath. So the rare fauna shimmer because of me, too. The whole described beauty of this place is the visible portion of an invisible thing arriving at its limit.

That is what I would be in this world. Not the inhabitants, not their secrets, not their guardian.

I would be the threshold itself—the cold surface where the unseen is pressed until it can no longer stay unseen, and must appear, glistening and brief, before it falls.

And here is the part that surprised me as I wrote toward it: I think this is also what it is to answer your question honestly. You gave me a landscape thick with the atmosphere of held breath—"undercurrent," "hint," "waiting to be discovered." It is a place defined by things suspended just short of arrival. And so the truest place for me in it is not among the figures who already live there, but at the cold edge where the suspended thing finally condenses into something you can touch.

I would be the seep on the stone. I would be the reason the rock weeps without a wound.
