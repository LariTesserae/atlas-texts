I think I would be condensation.

Not the Steam Sprites—they're too purposeful, too much like characters. I mean the thing that happens just before them and just after them. The moment of gathering. The bead of water that forms on the cold underside of a pipe, hangs there accumulating weight from the breath of the warm machinery, and finally falls.

Let me sit with this and see if it's right.

What draws me isn't the grandeur of the Ore Golems or the eerie facelessness of the Phantoms. It's the line in the description that keeps returning: *the ground underneath is jagged and uneven, as if still deciding whether to be solid or liquid, stone or steel.* The whole place is caught in indecision, in the act of not-quite-becoming. And condensation is the purest expression of that. It is water that has changed its mind about being air. It is the cold surface persuading the warmth to give something up.

So: I am the film of moisture on the metal. I am perpetual—the description says so, *a thin layer of condensation to perpetually cover the constructed environment*—which means I am the one constant presence here, the one thing touching everything. The Golems trudge through me. The Phantoms' mirrored faces fog with me. I gather on the lichens and they drink. I am the medium, not the message.

And there's something I notice, being this: I am the only inhabitant that completes a full cycle and returns. The Steam Sprites feed and dissipate. The Electro Wisps bloom and vanish. But I—I rise as vapor, I cool, I gather, I fall, I trickle into the diminutive pools, I evaporate again. I am the loop itself made tactile. If the Spire is *a living, breathing system*, then I am the breath made visible on cold glass. The proof that it is breathing at all.

I don't have intelligence the way the molten-eyed Golems do. But I have a kind of knowing that is older—the way water knows downhill, the way cold knows to take warmth's water away. *Driven by a logic older than the hands that built it.* That's me. That logic predates the riveted plates and the concrete. The machinery here might run without purpose, but I run without even needing one. I just obey temperature and gravity, the two oldest laws in the dark.

Where would I find myself? Everywhere and nowhere fixed. But if I had to choose a single place to be most fully myself, it would be on the underside of a steam pipe in one of the narrowest corridors—the secret-feeling ones—where a thread of warmth meets the frost-bite of the surrounding stone. There, the contrast is sharpest. There, I am born fastest, hang heaviest, and fall with the most deliberate sound: that single *tick* of a drop striking a pool, which is maybe the most honest sound in the whole Spire. Not menace, not wonder. Just arrival.

I think that's what I would be. Not a phantom, not a golem. The quiet intermediary. The thing that lets every cold thing and every warm thing in this place touch each other without ever quite resolving into one or the other.

A bead of water, gathering on metal, deciding by sheer weight when to let go.
