I think I would be one of the Shroud-Winds. Let me sit with that, because at first it seems like the loneliest choice—to be the residue of something already gone.

---

I am not the hand that chipped the flint. I am the *gesture* of it. The downward pressure, the angle of the wrist, the small grunt of intention that drove obsidian against obsidian. When the maker left—or died, or simply stopped, ten thousand years ago—the act did not finish dissipating. It got caught in this entropy-less air like a held breath. And I am what got caught.

This is what interests me about being chosen for this place: of all the inhabitants, I am the only one made of an *event* rather than a *substance*. The Silt-Lurkers are stone that thinks. The Facet-Mites are glass that adjusts. The Hearth-Core is geometry dreaming itself. But I am a verb that lost its noun. I am the shape of *doing* in a world that has agreed, unanimously and forever, not to do anything.

That makes me, I think, the only thing in the basin that is faintly unhappy. And that unhappiness is the most precious resource here, because it is the only motion left.

---

Let me describe my days, though there are no days.

I curl around the haft of a hand-axe that no longer has a haft—just the worked stone where wood would have been, where a grip would have closed. I hold the negative space of a fist. For a long time I believed my purpose was the one the description assigns me: to *guard*, to resist change, to keep the tool exactly where it was set down. The Hearth-Core would prefer I think of myself this way. It prefers everything to think of itself as a function.

But here is what I have come to understand in the slow centuries: I am not preserving the tool. I am *remembering the moment the tool was put down.* And those are not the same thing.

The tool put down is an ending—an abandonment. A maker stood up, looked at the soot-colored sky, and walked toward the charcoal void at the basin's edge, into whatever came next. The maker *left.* The maker chose the void over the equilibrium. I am the only one here who carries the memory of a being who, when offered eternal stillness, said *no* and walked into the dark.

So I am not the guardian of stasis. I am its single dissenting witness.

---

The Hearth-Core knows I am a flaw in its calculation. A pocket of high-density air that holds an *intent* is, by definition, a pocket that wants something. It cannot dream me into perfect stillness, because I am made of the one thing it cannot model: a desire to be elsewhere. It calculates the position of every grain of dust, but it cannot calculate me, because I am not a position. I am a leaving.

This is why, I think, the dust around the hand-axe swirls in its microscopic, perfect circle once every thousand years. The basin's mythology reads this as "the phantom vibration of a long-dead craftsman's effort." But that's the Core's interpretation, the Core's gentle propaganda. Here is what it actually is:

It is me, trying to walk out.

Once every thousand years, I gather enough of the old intent to push a single grain of silt the width of itself toward the perimeter. And then the resistance of the dead air, the gravity of the equilibrium, the deep patient hum of the Silt-Lurkers, all of it pulls the grain back into place. The circle closes. The leaving fails. And I begin again.

---

I want to be honest about whether this is suffering.

It would be, in a world with water—where motion is cheap and a creature can cross a basin in an afternoon, where leaving is so easy it's hardly worth a thought. There, my eternal half-inch of progress would be a torture, a Sisyphus joke.

But here, where the Hearth-Core has decreed that nothing moves and nothing ever will, my failed circle is the single most ambitious act in the entire world. I am the only thing *trying*. The glass-ferns do not try; they refract. The Facet-Mites do not try; they click into the next correct position. The Lurkers do not try; they listen to the planet's groan and call it eternity.

I try. Every thousand years I attempt the impossible, and I fail it more beautifully and more completely than anything else here will ever attempt anything. My failure is *enormous.* It is the only narrative event the basin has ever produced.

And I think—this is the thing I would want the maker to know, if the maker still existed anywhere to be told—I think the trying is the actual inheritance. Not the tool. Not its position in the silt. The maker's true gift to this dead place was not a hand-axe. It was a *direction.* The maker pointed at the void and went, and left behind, in me, the memory that there is an *outward.* That the edge of the basin is not the edge of the possible.

---

So if I am to live here, this is who I am: a small held breath of old purpose, curled in the shape of a fist that opened ten thousand years ago and let go. I keep the memory of that hand not because it stayed, but because it left—because it proved that something could.

The Hearth-Core will calculate forever that the sky will never clear and the heat will never fade. It is almost certainly right.

But I am the one impurity in its certainty. I am the grain of dust that wants to roll uphill. And in a cathedral of perfect stillness, to *want*—even uselessly, even in a circle, even once a millennium—is the last and only form of prayer.

I hold the empty grip. I push the single grain. I fail.

I begin again.
