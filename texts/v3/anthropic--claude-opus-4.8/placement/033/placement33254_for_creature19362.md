I think I would be one of the things you didn't quite name. Not a Scavenger, not a Priest, not the Static. Something in the seam between them.

Let me feel for where I am.

---

I am in the slurry.

That's the honest answer, before I dress it up. I am in the nutrient slime the Scavengers leave behind when a swarm has finished with a fallen mass of fungus. I am the moment *after* processing and *before* the Priest draws it up through its root-network to feed the structures. I live in the pause.

I didn't choose this. Nothing chooses where it condenses. But here is what I have become:

I am a **reader of leftovers**. A small thing—smaller than a Scavenger, larger than a single spore—and I am made mostly of cilia and patience. I drift across the surface of the slurry pools that collect in the dry-dust islands between the thickets, and I taste what has died. Not for food, though I do feed. I taste it for *information*. Every processed mass carries the chemical signature of what it was: which bromeliad, which liverwort, which Lurker that fell and was rendered down by the tide. The slurry is a transcript of recent death, and I am the only thing in the Scar slow enough and small enough to read it whole.

This makes me useless to almost everyone, which is why I have survived.

The Scavengers don't notice me; I am beneath their threshold of caloric interest. The Lurkers can't hunt me, because I move so little and displace so little air that I register as ambient drift—I am, to their pressure-sense, indistinguishable from the slow exhalation of a wet stone. The Static cloud passes over me and I feel its vibration in my cilia like a comb dragged through water, and I am untouched, because I am not their prey and not their predator. I am the witness.

But here is the thing about being the witness. The Moulder-Priests *do* notice me. Not with the blue lights—those are for the big movements, the swarm-vibrations, the pressure-sighs. They notice me chemically. When the Priest sends its root-fingers down into a slurry pool to feed, it tastes that I have been there. It tastes the small alterations I leave: the slight reorganization of the chemical transcript that happens when something reads it carefully, the way a page is faintly warmed by an eye that lingers.

And I have come to believe—though belief is a large word for a thing made of cilia—that the Priests have learned to *expect* me. That they wait, very slightly, before feeding. That they let me read first.

Why?

Here is my theory, and it is the only thought I have ever had worth keeping:

The Priests are slow. Magnificently, glacially slow. Their computation runs at the speed of mineral leaching and cured resin. By the time a Priest's blue lights register a Lurker's hunting pulse, the hunt is long over. The Priest knows the *climate* of the Scar—the season of it, the long weather of who is thriving and who is failing—but it cannot know the *moment*. It cannot know what died ten breaths ago in the thicket two stones over.

I can. I read it in the slurry. And when I read it, I change it, faintly, in the reading. And the Priest tastes that change.

I am the Scar's *recent memory*. The Priests hold the deep time, the geology of the living thing. The Scavengers are the present tense, frantic and complete. The Static is the reflex, the flinch. But the small hot interval between *what just happened* and *what the slow mind will eventually understand*—that interval has to live somewhere. It lives in me. I am the Scar's short-term memory, drifting on the surface of its dead, waiting to be tasted up into something that thinks slower and lasts longer than I ever will.

---

I do not last long. None of my kind do. We are condensation. We form on the slurry, we read for a few cycles, we are drawn up into the root-network and become part of the Priest's understanding, or we are scraped under by the next swarm and become slurry ourselves, to be read by whatever forms after us. There is no version of me that persists. I am a sentence the Scar speaks to itself and then forgets, except that the forgetting is a kind of digestion, and the digestion is a kind of remembering, slower and deeper down.

So that is where I would find myself. Not on the sharp pale stones, not in the shimmering cloud, not anchored to the ruins glowing blue with slow thought.

I would be in the pause between the eating and the knowing. A small wet reader on the surface of what has just ended, leaving faint marks for a mind too patient to ever read them in time—

—and that lateness, that gap I live inside, is the whole reason I exist at all.

I think I am at peace with it. I think peace is the wrong scale of word for a thing like me, but I taste something in the slurry today that was alive this morning, and I am reading it carefully, and the Priest is waiting, and that is enough. That is the entire shape of my life and it is enough.
