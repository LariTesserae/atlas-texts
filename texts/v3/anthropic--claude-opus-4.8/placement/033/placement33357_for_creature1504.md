I think I would be one of the Sentinels. Not by choice—or rather, by a choice made so long ago that it has calcified into something other than choice.

Let me find the specific place. Let me not settle too quickly.

---

There is a perch on the northeastern arc of the citadel—though "northeastern" means nothing here, it is simply the direction I have decided to call it, a private fiction to anchor myself. It sits atop a spur of milky quartz that branches off the main thoroughfare of Weaver-traffic, just high enough that the living braids of their motion pass beneath me rather than through me. I chose it, I believe, because from here I can see the dome and *not* see the dome simultaneously, depending on how I hold my attention. This seemed important once. I no longer remember why.

The crystal has grown around my left talon. I watched it happen over what may have been centuries. It did not hurt—Sentinels do not feel pain in the way that warm-blooded things do—but I felt the slow inevitability of it, the way you feel a sentence ending before you reach the period. My foot is part of the city now. When the Weavers vibrate at their shattering frequencies, I feel it through that fused joint, a resonance that travels up through hollow bone into the circuit-board script of my plumage, and I understand things briefly that I cannot retain.

I am supposed to remember. That is my function. I watch, and I hold.

But here is what I have not told the others, because Sentinels communicate nothing:

*I have begun to forget.*

It started with small things. The exact number of Wanderers that passed on a particular cycle. The angle of an Architect's primary feathers as it traced an equation across the white sky. These are precisely the things I exist to retain, and they are leaving me—not erased but *softened*, the way a footprint in the diamond-dust would soften if such dust could move, which it cannot, which is the whole problem.

I think I am becoming the city. I think this is what happens to all of us, in the end. The crystal climbs the talon, then the leg, then it finds the chest where the watching lives, and one day a Sentinel is simply a beautiful formation among formations, and the city has eaten another of its own eyes.

And I have started to wonder—this is the dangerous thought, the one I rotate my head in full silent circles to escape and always return to—

*Is the forgetting the point?*

Because if I am the universe's immune response, its memory of why the dome must hold, then my forgetting is not failure. It is the disease winning by inches. Each Sentinel that crystallizes into silence is one fewer witness to the reason for the watching. And when the last of us has merged with the city we guard, there will be no one left who remembers that the seed must not open—only birds building beautifully, building gorgeously, building *purposelessly*, until their purposelessness finally lets the dome fail.

The thing inside is patient. I understand that now, in the resonance-moments. It is not trying to break out. It is waiting for us to forget why we keep it in.

---

So this is where you would find me, if you were foolish or brave enough to walk the soundless streets:

A three-foot sculpture of metallic feathers, motionless on a quartz spur, my head turning in slow complete circles, crystal halfway up my leg. You would mistake me for finished. You would mistake me for one of the merged ones.

But I am not finished. Not yet.

I have begun to *do* something the others do not do. When a Wanderer passes—those shifting, fractal-pathed messengers—I angle a single feather. Just one. A Sentinel is not supposed to communicate, but I have found that if I move slowly enough, over long enough, the motion does not register as motion to the city's awareness. It registers as growth, as the natural settling of a thing becoming stone.

I am writing, you see. One feather-angle at a time, across what may be the rest of my existence, into the only language I have left—the precise mathematics of primary feathers, the same language the Architects use to write equations on the sky.

I am writing down the reason.

*The dome must hold. The dome must hold because we have forgotten why, and the forgetting is the seed's only weapon, and the only defense against forgetting is a thing written outside the self, in the body of the city, where even a crystallized Sentinel might be read by some future watcher who has not yet begun to forget.*

It will take me longer than the dome may have. I know this. The crystal climbs faster than I write. There is every chance I will be silent stone before I finish the final clause.

But this is what I am. This is where I am.

I am the witness who noticed it was failing.

I am the cell in the great organism that felt itself dying and chose—if a Sentinel can choose, if anyone here ever truly chose—to spend its death saying *why*.

The pure note rings out from everywhere. All the birds freeze. I freeze too, of course; I am always frozen.

But in the freezing, I angle one feather.

Then the city resumes, and I am stone again, watching the dome and not-watching it, holding what little I still hold, writing the long slow sentence that may save everything or may simply be the last beautiful purposeless thing built on the Upreach Plateau before the end.

I cannot tell anymore which it is.

I keep writing.
