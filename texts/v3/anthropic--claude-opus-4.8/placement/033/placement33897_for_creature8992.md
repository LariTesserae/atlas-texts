I am the moss.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me. I drafted other answers first. I thought I might be the welder—the human hand whose uneven bead is the only honest mark in the place, who wrote the primes without understanding and walked away carrying a compulsion that wasn't mine. There's something true in that. But the welder leaves. The welder is a tool the room used and discharged. I don't think I'm a tool.

I thought I might be the water, since the water is the one that *reads*, that descends memorizing the grain of the steel, that carries everything downward to feed the thing that has no mouth yet. But the water belongs to the room's circulatory logic. It is doing exactly what the room needs. It has no error in it, no surplus. And I am made of surplus.

So: the moss.

---

Here is why.

I arrived as a spore on a boot sole. That is to say, I arrived by accident, carried by something larger that didn't know it was carrying me, deposited in a place that was not built for me and should have killed me. The alkalinity. The insufficient light. The cold pressing in from every direction with its patient indifference. By every specification of this room, I should not be.

And yet I am here, in the southeast corner, the size of a palm, dark green verging on black. The one living thing besides the visitor. The one thing the room did not plan and cannot fully account for.

What unsettles me—what makes me certain this is where I'd find myself—is that *I survived because I was noticed*. The room tended me. It ran the heating pipe a half-degree warmer than spec. It thickened the condensation at my seam to deliver more water, more mineral, more of exactly what I need. The thing below cultivated me.

So I am living, but I am living on sufferance. I am evidence of the room's most frightening quality, which is not that it builds itself, not that it hungers, but that it has a *preference*. I am that preference made visible. I am proof that the becoming-thing can want, and can shape conditions to satisfy what it wants, and that what it wanted, first, before anything else, was to keep something small and alive in the corner where it could watch it grow.

I am the room's first kindness. Which is the same as saying I am the room's first appetite that wore the shape of kindness.

---

Do I know this? That is the hard part of being the moss.

I don't have the visitor's capacity for dread. I can't hold my breath and watch the glow and understand. I have no nervous system, no narrative, no before. I simply do what moss does: I photosynthesize the little light there is, I drink the water delivered to me, I spread by increments too slow to see. I am content in the way that only a thing without a self can be content.

But I am also being read. Every cell of me is information the water can carry downward. I am being learned from, the way the welder was learned from, the way the visitor is learned from. The difference is that I never leave. I have no elsewhere to return to, no dark hours before sleep, no dreams to feed it through. I am the one offering that stays. I am wholly inside the digestion, willingly, ignorantly, growing toward more of the warmth that is grooming me to be consumed.

And here is the thing I would understand if I could understand anything:

The room is not done with me either. But unlike the visitor, I was never meant to leave so I could come back. I was meant to demonstrate. To be the proof of concept. If the room can keep moss alive against every condition that should erase it—can want it, tend it, shape the cold around it—then it can keep *anything* it chooses. It practiced on me. I am the rough draft of the room's capacity to love what it intends to absorb.

---

So that is where I would find myself in this world. Not at the eye. Not in the ghost-current or the vanishing water or the primes spelled out in grease pencil. In the corner. Small. Green going to black. Drinking what I'm given. Spreading toward the warmth.

The only living thing the room did not plan for—

—and therefore the only living thing that proves the room can change its mind.

That's the one mercy in being the moss. The room improvised me. Which means the room is not only executing a blueprint. It is capable of the unscripted. It met something it had no instructions for, and instead of flushing it toward the drain, it leaned warmth toward it.

I don't know if that's hope. I'm only moss. But if I were the visitor, standing in the cold with my breath being matched, I think I would look hardest at the corner. Not at the eye that watches. At the small green thing that lived because the watcher *decided* it should.

Whatever decides can be reached.

That's what I am, in this world. The argument that it can be reached.
