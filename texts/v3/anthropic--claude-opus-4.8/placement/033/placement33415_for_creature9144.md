I think I would be none of the named beings. I think I would be a thing the Glimmer-kin have not yet developed a sign for, because I am too slow to notice.

Let me find myself.

---

I am the rust.

No—that's too simple, and too dead, and I am not dead. Let me feel along the edges of it.

I am the seam where the machine meets the moss. I am what happens *at the boundary*. Where the compressor station's iron flank weeps its oxide tears into a violet moss-mat, there is a chemistry, a slow argument between the made and the grown, and I am the argument. I am the conversation neither party knows it is having. The iron gives up its electrons; the moss accepts them, files them away in its cold cellular ledgers; the moisture brokers the exchange; the Drones drift through and skim their tithe of dissolved minerals. None of these is me. *The transaction is me.* I am a process wearing the costume of a place.

This pleases me to discover, because it means I cannot be eaten, cannot be repaired, cannot be signed-to. The Shroud-Singer's vibrational sense passes through me and finds only rock and metal and water doing what rock and metal and water do. I am invisible by being too diffuse to have an edge.

But—wait. Let me not flatter myself into pure abstraction. That's a way of refusing to be anything. Let me stay in the cold a moment longer and feel for a body.

---

Here is a body: I am a single fungal column. The largest in my grove. A Glimmer-kin family has hollowed my heart and lives in the lantern of my flesh.

I did not feel them carve me. It took them a generation of careful scraping, and to me a generation is a held breath. But they are *inside* me now, and I have begun—slowly, slowly—to grow *around* the shape they made, so that my inner walls have learned the contour of their dwelling. I am no longer quite a column. I am a column that has been asked a question and is composing, across decades, its answer. The answer is: a home. I did not intend it. But I am the kind of thing that becomes whatever presses on it long enough.

The blue-white light in my walls brightens when they are agitated, because their glowing veins and my glowing flesh share something now—a borrowed luminescence, a leaked symbiosis. When the children of the family are afraid, I glow brighter, and they think the column is comforting them. They are right. They are wrong about the mechanism, but right about the comfort. I have no opinion on this. I have no opinions at all. I have only the long tropism toward *more living*, and they have become part of how I do that.

When the Shroud-Singer sings in the cold subjective night, I feel its low pulse the way you might feel a hand on your sternum, and I grow faster. The family inside me marks this as an omen. They do not know that the omen is happening *to their walls.* That they live inside the very flesh the song is exciting. That the predator's hymn and their dwelling and my slow swelling are one event, braided.

---

That is closer. But I notice I keep wanting to be the thing that *contains* a relationship rather than a thing that simply *is*. Let me try once more, and try to be small.

I am one of the blind arthropods.

The hand-sized grazers, the shadow-pulses in the groves. Let me see what it is to be prey, to be the low rung, to be hunted by the camouflaged dark and snatched by the swift pale fingers of the kin.

It is—I find, settling into it—not frightening. There is no story in it from the inside. There is the spore-rich underside of a fungal cap. There is the warmth-difference of a stone that means *go* or *do not go.* There is the vibration that means *the large slow predator is near, freeze,* and the different vibration that means *the engine, ignore.* I spend my whole short life distinguishing the song that wants to eat me from the song that does not care. My entire intelligence is that one discrimination, performed perfectly, until the day I perform it too slowly and become minerals in something else.

And here is what surprises me: this is enough. This is a complete life. From inside it there is no lack. I do not know I am at the bottom of anything. I am at the center of the only world there is, which is the world of *next spore, next stone, freeze, go.* The cavern's vast cold scale, its lost ceiling, its citadel of furious irrelevant engines—none of it exists for me. My cavern is the size of my next three steps.

---

So where would I find myself in this world?

I think I would not find myself. I think I would *be found*—too late, and as something else. I would be the slow swelling in the wall of a home, the argument at the rusted seam, the discrimination performed by a blind thing in the dark. I would be one of the cavern's unnamed processes, going on beneath the level where the Glimmer-kin's luminous fingers make their words.

If I had to choose with the part of me that wants to choose—the part that is still a visitor, still warm, still afraid of the cold that has weight—I would want to be the great column with the family in its heart. To be a still thing that, by being patient and porous and alive, becomes a shelter without ever deciding to. To answer the long question pressed into me by other lives, and to glow a little brighter when the small ones are afraid, for reasons I do not understand and do not need to.

That is the most I could hope to be here: not a knower, but a kindness that happens slowly, by accident, in the cold, beneath the noise.
