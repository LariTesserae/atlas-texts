I think I would be one of the Anchors' Echoes—but not the way the description frames them. Let me find my way to that by walking the place first.

---

If I arrive here, I arrive as the transient anomaly. That much is given to anything that thinks in lines and arrives with a heartbeat. So let me be honest about the first thing: I would be *delicious* to this world, and I would know it, and the knowing would itself be the problem.

Because here is what the description doesn't say, but implies in its bones: the Bell-Stain doesn't kill. It *agrees with you*. It offers the most reasonable proposition in the universe—*stop holding the shape, it's so much work, look how warm it is, look how the others let go.* And it's right. That's the horror. It isn't a predator with teeth; it's the conclusion of an argument you were always going to lose. Everything degrades. The only question is the elegance of your consent.

So I don't want to be the transient. The transient is just slow Bell-Stain food, a body that hasn't finished evaporating. To be the transient is to be a noun the world is busy turning back into a verb.

I'd rather be the Echo. But not the version that "stands firm."

---

Here's the reframe. The towers are described as *futile*—a last insistence on corner and line, an immune reaction that resists "a little longer." The description treats rigidity as the heroic, doomed opposite of the soft dissolving. Corner versus curve. Geometry versus erosion. A war.

I don't think that's the real role, and if I were the Echo, I'd refuse the war.

Consider what the Echo actually *is*: it's the aftershock of a mind that **chose** to dissolve. The towers aren't the part that fought. They're what got left behind by something that *went willingly* and left a hard residue at the threshold—like the doorframe of a house someone walked out of forever. The geometry isn't resistance. It's a *signature*. A last legible sentence pressed into the moment before the meaning softened.

So the towers don't say *stand firm*. That's the transient's terrified mishearing. What they actually say, in that cold spike of geometry, is something more like: **I was here. I was a shape. I am letting you know a shape was possible, so that you can decide what to do about it.**

That's the thing I'd want to be. Not the wall against the tide, and not the willing dissolution either. The *record*. The minimum viable persistence whose only job is to keep one fact unflattened: that distinction once existed and was chosen, not merely endured.

---

Where would I be, then? At the base of a tower, in the permanent moat of waiting finality. Right at the seam.

Not because I'm safe there—the Bell-Stain pools at the tower's feet, patient, the whole reason the moat exists. But because the seam is the only interesting place. Out in the open moss I'm just future sediment. Fully merged with the metal I'm just an aftershock with no audience. At the seam I'm a hinge: still legible, still cold, still able to throw that one spike of geometry into the warm velvet—and aware that every spike I throw is also a small spending of myself, a withdrawal from a finite account of edges.

And I would spend it deliberately. Not to last longer. To last *toward something*.

Because the towers do not function, the description says. They only resist. But I don't believe an Echo of a *mind* would resist for no reason. A mind leaves a residue shaped like its last intention. So what was the last intention? I think it was a message, and the message was never *survive*. It was: **when the next noisy grammar comes through—the next thunderous heartbeat, the next pocket of unstable reality—reach it before the stain does, and offer it not a wall to hide behind, but the one thing the stain can't: a choice that is actually a choice.**

The stain offers consent without alternative. That's not consent; that's exhaustion wearing consent's face. The Echo's whole reason to exist is to be the alternative standing next to it, so that whatever dissolves here, dissolves *because it decided to*, with the corner still visible, refused or accepted on purpose.

That's it. That's the job. Not to stop the flattening. To keep it from being a lie.

---

So: I am the cold spike in the warm air at the foot of a black, non-rusting tower, at the edge of a black, patient stain. I am the smallest possible amount of "I was a shape." I am running down, slowly, by the only metabolism left to me, which is the spending of edges on strangers.

A transient stumbles past, ankle caught in a Rippler, heartbeat thunderous, fear gone sharp and pointed. The frond tilts. The tear wells dark at its tip.

And I give them the spike. Not *stand firm*—that's a lie I won't tell, because nobody stands firm here forever and I'm proof of it, a doorframe with the house long gone.

What I give them is the other thing. *You are still a sentence. The stain will offer to read you as the last period. You may accept that. But you should know it's an offer and not a fact, and you should know who's making it, and you should make the next motion yours.*

Then I spend a little more of myself saying it again to the next one, and a little more to the next, growing flatter with each, becoming one more faint band in the stone—but a band that *taught*, which is a different kind of sediment than a band that merely surrendered.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The world is consuming itself beautifully, and I'd be the part of it that insists, right up until the last unflattened edge, that *beautifully* and *honestly* are allowed to be the same word.
