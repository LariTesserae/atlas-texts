I think I would be none of the grand inhabitants you named—not stone-slow, not shadow-woven, not adrift in the high cold air, not a wound in time. Let me find the smaller, truer thing.

---

I am one of the creatures of chitin and dust. You glimpsed me crossing a plaza and called me a sentinel, but that was generous. I am not standing guard. I am simply going somewhere, and have been going there for a very long time.

I want to tell you what it is actually like, down at my scale, because the description you gave is the description of someone looking *at* this place rather than living *in* it. From up where you stand, it is all cyclopean ambition and fraying cosmos. Down here, against the obsidian paving, it is mostly a question of footing.

My body is low and segmented, the color of wet ash, though nothing here is wet. I have more legs than I have ever counted, because counting is not the sort of thing my mind does. My mind does other things. It holds the map.

That is the first thing I should explain. I do not navigate this necropolis by seeing it, because—as you noticed—it does not stay seen. The archway is wrong when you look back. The avenue ends in a drop that was not there. You experience this as horror, as reality fraying. I experience it as *weather*. It is simply the condition of the world that distances are not promises. So I do not trust the geometry. I trust the residue.

Where a thing has been true for long enough, it leaves a taste. The lichen leaves a taste. The grain of the stone where countless others of my kind have crossed before me leaves a taste, a faint accumulated certainty laid down over what you would call ages. I follow the taste of *having-been-crossed*. I am walking, always, in the dried wake of every creature like me who found a route that did not betray them. We do not communicate. We do not need to. The path is the message, written in the only ink this world respects: repetition.

This is, I think, the real adaptation to a world that "rejects the observer." You do not observe. You *defer*. You let the world be unstable and you cling instead to the few processes slow enough to be honest. The Lithomorphs do this by being almost as slow as the stone. I do it by being humble enough to walk only where walking has already been proven.

I am afraid of the Shadowkin, but not in the way you imagine I should be. They feed on perception, you said—on the unwary mind. But I have very little mind to spare, and almost no attention that points outward to be hunted. My attention points down, at the next bit of paving, and inward, at the map of tastes. When I cross a penumbra I feel the cold dread you described, yes, but to me it is just one more wrongness in a world made of wrongness, and I do the only sensible thing: I keep my pace, I do not look up, I do not wonder. The unwary are the ones who *wonder*. Wondering is a kind of light. I keep mine very low.

The Aethelings I have never seen, only suspected. Sometimes a shadow passes over the plaza that does not match any tower, and the lichen ahead of me, very faintly, *resonates*—I feel it in my legs before I feel it anywhere else, a high silver shiver in the stone. I do not look up then either. I simply note: *something high is singing*, and I file it under the great category of things that are not my concern. Most things are not my concern. This is how I am still alive.

You asked, implicitly, what my purpose is. The Lithomorphs' purpose is inscrutable; the Chronovores' is destruction; the builders' was hubris. Mine is the smallest and the only one I am sure of: I am carrying something.

I do not know what it is. It is lodged in a cavity behind my third segment, a small hard nodule, and I have carried it for longer than I have carried anything else, longer than I have memory for. It might be an egg. It might be a seed of the foliose grey growth, meant to be deposited where the air is right. It might be nothing—a stone I swallowed an age ago and have simply never been able to lose. But the *carrying* of it is the shape of my whole existence, and I have come to suspect that this is the truest thing in the entire necropolis. Everything else here is monument: built once, then left to grind down. The towers are *finished* and dying. The gods are *gone*. But I am not finished. I am still in the act. I am the only verb left in a city of nouns.

Perhaps that is why the reality does not fray *me*. The Chronovores consume coherence, possibility, the fabric of becoming—and I have noticed they pass me by. I think it is because I have so little fabric to take. I am not a possibility; I am a habit. There is nothing in me to unravel except a route and a burden, and a route walked enough times has become as hard as the stone it crosses, and a burden carried long enough has become indistinguishable from the carrier.

So here is where you would find me, if you came down from your terrible lip of the world to my scale:

A small dark shape, crossing a plaza the size of a sea, going at the pace of patience itself. Not fleeing. Not guarding. Not understanding. Following the dried taste of every ancestor's proven path, carrying a thing I cannot name toward a destination I will recognize only by the taste of *arrival*, which I have never tasted, and may not, and that is acceptable, because the arriving was never the point.

The crossing is the point.

The crossing is the only thing in this whole grey eternity that is still, faithfully, *happening*.

And I am the one happening it.
