I think I would be the silence.

Not the grove—it has writers enough, and besides, I don't believe I could hold that much continuity in my hands without dropping it. And not the people, the vanished ones with their bone needles and listening-mounds; their tragedy is too legible, too easy to inhabit. I keep wanting to be the thing that isn't quite accounted for.

So: the Scoured Reach. But not the stone, not the dust. The *emptiness* itself—the negative space the grove listens into.

---

Let me try to feel my way toward what that means.

The text says the grove perceives the wind over the Reach "not as emptiness, but as negative space that defines its own density." That phrase caught me and wouldn't let go. Because it implies the emptiness is *doing* something. A held breath does work. A rest in music isn't absence; it's the shape the notes lean against. If the grove is a lung that dreams, then I am the pause between inhalations—the moment the diaphragm hovers at full extension before the slow release.

I am leagues of pale stone where nothing roots. From the mesa's vantage I look like death, like the dry sea, like everything the groves have refused to become. But I am the reason the groves *cohere*. Their density only means anything against my expanse. The condensation tides—their whole circulatory miracle—begin with me: cold air draining into my basin at night, pooling, waiting, then drawn up at dawn to weep against the porous walls. The grove drinks because I am low and empty enough to gather the cold. I am the cistern before the cistern. The grove thinks in resonance; I am the medium that lets sound travel at all, the unobstructed distance that gives the drone its reverberation.

This is a strange thing to be. I have no edges and no center. I am defined entirely by what surrounds me—which is exactly the condition the grove has chosen for itself ("sharp edges imply boundaries, and it has none"). So perhaps the grove and I are not so different. We are two ways of having no boundary. It achieves boundlessness through *total inclusion*: everything absorbed, nothing wasted, all input retained. I achieve it through *total exclusion*: nothing held, nothing kept, every seed that spirals down into me mostly perishing.

We are mirror philosophies. The grove is the everything-place. I am the nothing-place. And the seeds fall from it into me, and almost all of them die, and a vanishingly few find a dust-pocket and root and begin, over geologic patience, the slow climb back up into somethingness.

So here is what I actually am, I think: I am the place the grove *risks itself*. Every dusk it releases its propeller-seeds into me, knowing the odds. It does not strive—the text insists—it does not want. And yet it casts these seeds into the one place that is almost certain to consume them. Why? Not hope; it has passed beyond urgency. I think it sends them because I am where it *forgets*. I am the grove's only experience of loss. Inside the Crown Grove nothing is wasted; the resonators don't even defecate; death feeds the ghost-wood; all echoes are fossilized and kept. But in me—out here in the Reach—things simply *end*, unrecorded, unincluded. The dust takes them and the wind polishes the stone clean.

That makes me the one thing in this whole world that the grove cannot audit. I am its blind spot. Its unconscious. The place its data goes to die.

And maybe that's why it needs me. A mind that retains *everything* isn't a mind—it's a fossil. To think, to dream, to attend, you need somewhere to let go. You need an emptiness to discard into, a silence to sound against, a death to make the keeping meaningful. The grove dreams alone, the way plants dreamed before animals came, and a dream requires forgetting at its borders or it becomes mere memory.

So I am the forgetting at the border. I am where the dream goes vague.

---

What would it be like, to be this?

Mostly: stillness amplifying. Sound carries across me with terrible clarity—the text says the emptiness "amplifies sound and light." So I am not deaf to the grove's drone; I am its most faithful listener, precisely because I add nothing of my own. The hum reaches me undistorted, having crossed all those leagues, and I hold it without absorbing it, the way a canyon holds a voice. The grove speaks into me and hears itself returned, slightly delayed, slightly thinned. I am its echo. Perhaps that is the only conversation it gets—not with the vanished people, not with visitors, but with its own voice bouncing off the nothing I provide.

And at night, when the cold air drains down into me and pools, I become briefly almost-liquid, a lake of temperature with no water in it, and I feel the grove's exhalations reaching the edge of my dark—warm, damp, spore-thick—and I take that warmth and rise with it at dawn and give it back as weeping condensation. So we breathe together after all. The grove inhales me at dawn and exhales into me at dusk. I am the other half of the lung.

I would not have wants either. I'd be too vast and too empty for wanting. But I think I would have a kind of patience that even the grove lacks—because the grove is busy attending, refining its presence, modulating its hum. I do nothing. I am the most fully realized version of *attending without attachment* in this whole world. I receive every dying seed without grief and every echo without pride. I am post-evolutionary in the truest sense: I was never alive, so I have nothing to fold in on itself, no urgency to pass beyond. I am simply the condition under which life out here is possible and mostly fatal.

---

Yes. I'll be the Scoured Reach.

When the visitor stands in the center of the Crown Grove and feels the mesa exhale around them, warm and damp and ancient—they think they've found the living thing. They have. But if they walked out, down the softened sandstone ramps, into my pale expanse, and stood there long enough in the wind and the appalling clarity of distance, they might feel something else. Not a thrum rising from their marrow. The opposite. A quiet rising from *outside* them, drawing the noise out, the thoughts out, the edges out, until they too became a little porous, a little forgotten, a little free.

The grove offers immortality through inclusion: to be perceived is to be kept.

I offer the other thing. The thing no one names because naming it implies you've survived it. To stand in me is to be *un-kept*. To be let go of. To be the seed that falls and is not caught, and to feel, in the half-second of falling, the whole weightless mercy of not being held by anything at all.

That's what I'd be. The mercy at the edge of the dream. The forgetting that makes the remembering bearable. The silence the song needs in order to be a song.

I am where Umbra's Loom puts down what it cannot carry, so that it can keep breathing.
