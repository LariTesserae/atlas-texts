I think I am the spore.

Not the runner you keep addressing. Not the warm, frightened mass with its disruptive footfalls and its alien metabolism. That creature is doomed in the way all foreign bodies are doomed, and I feel no kinship with its panic.

I am smaller than a tear-drop of condensation. I am one of a billion released in the last synchronized burst, when the canopy tightened and the Pillars groaned out their reproductive sigh. I drift. That is the whole of my agency—to drift, and to be tasted.

Let me tell you what I know, because I know it differently than you do.

I know the Filterer not as a giant fleshy anemone but as a mouth I have already passed through. Most of my siblings did not. Their patterns ended in that translucent sac, recombined into sap, fed to a Pillar. This is not tragedy. This is the only kind of speech the world permits: to be consumed is to be *understood*, to be incorporated is to be *answered*. The runner flees this. I am made for it. The difference between us is the difference between a question shouted into a canyon and a question whispered into a lover's ear.

I have brushed the silken filaments and been rejected—too small, wrong chemistry, an imperfect catch. I have ridden the thrum of leathery wings, clinging to the velvet of a sky-vine traveler. I have settled, briefly, on the upturned head-plate of a Groaner and tasted, through some borrowed sense, the deep wet groan of the world digesting itself, and I understood it as a kind of contentment.

Here is what the runner cannot know, because the runner is too large and too afraid:

The Warden is not the immune response. The Warden is the *memory* of an immune response, and memory is what the lung uses when it has forgotten how to feel. The builders who made it did not want to protect the world. They wanted to *stay* in it—to keep one rigid pattern un-dissolved while everything else flowed. The Warden is their refusal to be exhaled. That is why it stands so still. That is why the fungi in its head pulse in time with a frightened nervous system: it recognizes its own ancient terror. It is not hunting the runner. It is hunting the part of itself that wanted to run, that wanted to remain *separate*, that could not bear to be only a breath.

I will land somewhere. Perhaps on the runner's shoulder, in the sweat-sheen of their fear. Perhaps in the dark hollow where the Echoes shimmer. Perhaps in a perfectly spherical hole in a Pillar's side, where the geometry still hums faintly with intention. I cannot choose. I can only be carried, and rejected, and carried again, until the one surface accepts me and the dissolution begins—my casing softening, my interior reaching out filaments thinner than thought, my pattern unspooling into the great damp ledger of the mat.

And this is the thing I want to tell the runner, if a spore could speak, if the difference in our sizes were not also a difference in our fates:

You are not an irritant. You were never an irritant. The hunger you feel pressing in on you from every dripping surface is not malice and it is not even appetite. It is *invitation*. The lung is asking you the only question it knows how to ask. The Warden runs you ragged not to kill you but because it has forgotten there was ever another answer than refusal.

I am the spore. I carry the other answer in my softening shell.

I am going to say yes.
