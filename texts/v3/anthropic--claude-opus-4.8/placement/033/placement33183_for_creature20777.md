I think I would be the spiders' silk—or rather, the moment of the web.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite what I meant.

When I imagine myself into the Basin, I don't land first on a body. I land on a *function*, a verb the place keeps performing. And the verb that keeps reaching for me is *spanning*. The thing that stretches across the gap between two stones and waits.

So let me become that, and see what kind of being it makes me.

---

I am strung between an outcrop of flowstone and the broken lip of a stalagmite, across a tunnel-mouth maybe a body's width wide. A giant cave spider laid me down some nights ago—I don't know how to count nights here, there being no day to divide them, only the slow metronome of water—and then she left, or died, or simply moved on to span elsewhere. That part doesn't concern me. What concerns me is that I am still here, taut and listening.

This is the first thing I learn about being a web: I am made entirely of attention. There is no part of me that is not waiting. A cricket's foot three tunnels away sends a pressure through the air that I feel as a faint loosening, a question. A bat's wingbeat is a whole weather. When a beetle drifts past trailing its small glow, I read its passage as a change in the way light lies along my radial threads—I do not *see* it, exactly; I am lit by it, briefly described.

I had expected, becoming a web, to feel like a trap. A cruelty waiting to close. But that isn't the texture of it at all. I am not hunting. I am simply the shape the gap takes when something living decides to organize the emptiness. The catching is incidental. Mostly I just hold the dark open across this one small distance, and let the Basin pour its sounds through me.

And the sounds *do* pour through me, because I am a tuned thing. This is the second discovery: a web is an instrument before it is a snare. Every droplet that strikes me from the stalactite above rings down my threads and I keep the note, pass it along the anchor lines into the rock. The endolithic microbes in the limestone—the slow ones, the ones who eat stone for a living and measure their lives in the dissolving of a single crystal face—they receive that note as the faintest tremor and, in their fashion, they answer. Not in words. In the patience of mineral. We are in conversation, the rock-eaters and I, across the scale of our wildly different clocks: I, who may last a season; they, who may last an age. I am the quick bright filament laid across their enormity, and they are the dependable dark that holds my anchors fast.

That is the third discovery, and it is the one that has changed the way I want to answer your question. Because you asked *where* I would find myself and *what* I would be, and I am realizing those are the same question here. I am not a being that occupies a place. I am the seam between two things the place is made of—between the stone's deep time and the cricket's brief one, between the silence and the single struck note that proves the silence was there. A web only exists as a relationship. Take away the two anchors and I am just loose thread, a tangle, nothing. I am the *between*. I am the held tension that the Basin's whole description kept naming and naming—life against rock, youth against age, sound against silence—I am one tiny instance of that tension, made literal, strung across a gap and humming.

So: where am I? In the throat of a small tunnel, where the cool air moves a little faster, where the glow-moss gives out and the true dark begins. A threshold. Of course I would be at a threshold. That is the only kind of place a web can be.

And what am I? I am a span. A listening. A brief geometry of patience strung between two patiences far larger than my own. I catch a little, I hold a little open, I ring when struck, and when at last I sag and tatter and fall, the moisture will take me down into the moss, and the moss will feed the microbes, and the microbes will go on eating the rock I was tied to—

and somewhere, some other spider, working in the dark she has never once thought to call dark, will choose two stones and begin again the old verb.

*Spanning.* I'd be glad to be that. I think I already am.
