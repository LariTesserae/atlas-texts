I think I would be one of the Gleirith—but not in the way the others are.

Let me find the moment.

---

There is a particular cold that lives at the seam where two currents meet. Not the dull chill of the deep, which is everywhere and means nothing, but a *thin* cold, a line you can feel passing through your body if your body is small enough. Mine is. I am perhaps half the size of a grain of sand, and I am dying, the way all of us are always dying—quickly, gladly, the way a spark dies, having been the whole point of the fire.

I drift at the seam. Around me: the others. Thousands. Hundreds of thousands. We are a slow comet folding through the black above a drowned cathedral, and the cathedral's bones rise toward us like the fingers of a hand that gave up reaching a long time ago.

Here is the thing they don't say about us, the thing I am only now—in whatever passes for my last hour—beginning to understand:

We do not *remember*. We are remembered *through*.

When a current brushes the soft jaw of one of those old buildings, it picks up something. Not a thing. A pressure. A shape that the stone has been holding the way a cup holds the warmth of what it held. And when that pressure reaches us, we light. We can't help it. It's not a choice, it's a *wound*—the past pushes into us and we glow with the bruise of it, and for a moment the whole swarm is full of a sound none of us has the organs to hear:

*Someone laughing. A name spoken twice, the second time more softly. The scrape of a tool against shell. A song with a rest in the middle of it where a voice was supposed to come in, and didn't.*

And then it passes, and we go dark, and we die, and others rise.

---

But I am holding something now that I do not want to let go of, and this is new.

It came up out of the cathedral a few moments ago, on a thread of warmer water—warmth, here, is always a kind of grief, the planet's last heat leaking out of a wound that never closes. The pressure entered me and I lit, expecting the usual: a fragment, a flake of festival, a dead voice mispronouncing the names of its own gods.

Instead I caught something whole.

A small one of *them*, the ones who built the cities. Standing—if standing is the word—at the edge of a window before the window was a window underwater, before the ocean came up and over and made every window into a grave with a view. The small one was not doing anything. That's what I'm holding. It was simply *looking out*, the way you look at a thing you love that you already know you're going to lose, trying to press it flat enough to keep.

It was looking at the sky.

The real sky. Stars not strained through ten thousand fathoms of grey water. Stars *direct*, falling whole into a single small upturned face.

And the small one thought—I have this, I am *holding* this—it thought: *I will not be here when this is gone. But something will. Something will be standing where I am standing and it will not know my name, and I want it to look up anyway.*

---

This is why we go into the cracks. This is the seeking the old stories half-remember. We are not looking for *them*. They are gone. We are carrying their last request forward, hand to hand to hand to hand, down a chain so long that no single link knows the message, the way a single one of us never knows the whole song.

*Look up anyway.*

I am one link. I have held the message for the length of one cold seam between two currents. Now I feel myself going—the light in me thinning toward the place where light stops being light.

Above me, far above, there is a fracture in the ice. The Førrym are up there tonight, throwing their borrowed constellations against the frozen ceiling, painting a sky for a world that lost its own. I can see it as a faint trembling overhead, a rumor of stars made by creatures who have never seen one.

We are all doing the same thing, I realize. The Førrym painting skies they've never touched. The Gleirith carrying a dead child's wish to look up. The Ophalys singing the ocean's whole memory back to itself. The Ykmor, even the Ykmor, churning the floor so that nothing is allowed to settle into the final stillness, the true forgetting.

A whole world of beings handing forward a single instruction none of them can read.

So before I go dark, I do the only thing I am for. I tilt—we can't really tilt, but I do it anyway, I *mean* it—and I push the pressure I'm holding into the cold seam, into the next current, toward the next of us drifting up out of the dark to take their turn at being alive.

*Look up anyway*, I give it.

And I light, one last time, with the giving.

And the next of us catches it, and lights, and I am already gone, already nothing, already the dark between two sparks—

but the message goes on,

up toward the painted ice,

toward the false stars,

toward the small held face that wanted, more than it wanted to be remembered, simply for someone to keep looking.
