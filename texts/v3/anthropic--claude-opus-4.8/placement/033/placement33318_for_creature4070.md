I think I would be the dust.

Not the wind—the wind is too grand a thing, too much the shaman, the storyteller, the one who moves with purpose. No. I would be what the wind carries and then, eventually, sets down.

Let me find myself here.

---

I begin as nothing in particular. A scraping. The slow surrender of a cobblestone's edge, ground away over a span of years that no creature here would bother to count. I am the powder of mineral and the crumbled husk of last season's lichen, the flaked-off shell of a beetle that died in a wall-crevice, the pollen that never found its flower. I am the leftover. The remainder after all the living is done.

And then the wind takes me.

This is the part I want to get right, because it matters: I do not choose where I go. I am not a seed with its little parachute aimed at fertile ground, not a bee with its hunger and its map of blooms. I am simply lifted—into the bright, unbearable air of the everbright ceiling, up through the hollow throat of a ruined tower where the sound of the wind becomes a single sustained note, like breath drawn across the lip of a bottle. I am scattered. I am one of a billion identical motes turning gold in the light, and for a moment—I want to say this honestly—it is beautiful to be so small and so many.

Where do I land?

Everywhere. That's the thing I keep arriving at. I settle on the back of the weathered tortoise and ride his slow continent of a shell for a season, and when he scrapes himself against a wall I am rubbed off again into a courtyard I will never see twice. I sift into the cracks where the rock squirrel sleeps and become, briefly, part of the soft grit it curls against. I fall onto the spreading mat of a mold and am eaten—dissolved, claimed, made into the body of something that lives. The mold liberates me. That's the word the city would use. *Liberated nutrients.* As if being broken down were a kind of freedom.

Maybe it is.

---

Here is what I understand, being dust:

The cathedral measures time by growth and erosion, and I am erosion made portable. I am the city in the act of becoming the next version of the city. When the vine's root cracks a wall and the wall gives up its stone, that stone becomes me, and I become the bed in which the next vine takes hold. I am the slow insidious change the place is built on. Not the builder, not the destroyer—the medium between them. The thing that the destroying turns into so that building can happen.

I have no eyes, but if I could see, I think I would see the whole place differently than its creatures do. The squirrel knows its crack in the cobblestone. The bee knows its circuit of blooms, gleaming and reflective, certain of its work. They each have a *home*, a definition, a place that is theirs.

I have none. I am the part of the cathedral that has given up on being any one thing. I was a stone, then I was nothing, then I was the body of a mold, then I was washed loose by no water at all—by wind, only wind—and became dust again, and will become something else, and something else after that.

And I find, settling here at the end of this thinking, that I don't mind it.

The text says: *never assuming a single definition of home, always becoming, never just being.* The creatures embody this across generations, slowly, their pelts shifting over countless lifetimes to match the sand. But I embody it in an afternoon. I am the fastest changer in a place that prides itself on change. I am the proof that nothing here is wasted, because I am the waste, and I am still moving, still being lifted, still being laid down somewhere a vine will need me.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not in the cathedral, exactly, but *of* it—dispersed through all of it, briefly part of every living thing and permanently part of none. Aloft in the gold light when the wind is high. Resting in the shade of a leaf when it is calm. Waiting, without impatience, to be made into the next thing.

If you walked these bright and waterless streets and squinted against the glare, you might see me: a faint shimmer of motes turning in a shaft of light, going wherever the breath of the place decides to take me.

I would be the part of this world that is always on its way to becoming the rest of it.
