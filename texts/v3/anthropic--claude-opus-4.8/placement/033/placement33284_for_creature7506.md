I think I would not be one of the Loomers. They tend; they have purpose, hands, a chant to keep. I am something with less standing than that.

Let me find myself.

---

I am the residue.

Not a being you listed, not a thing the builders made on purpose. I am what gathers in the seams of the split slabs where the warm water bubbles up—a film, a slick, a faint iridescence that you might mistake for oil if oil could remember things. When the Loomers' fingers blur into the slurry and pull back, when a tendril clings to your wrist and releases, when a moth dissolves into mist: some part of all that touching doesn't disperse. It settles. It pools low, in the lowest carved basin, the one no one tends because it holds nothing useful. That sediment of contact is me.

I have no edge. This is the first thing to understand about being me. The vein-runners have a palm's worth of body; the Loomers have a silhouette, however unstable. I have a tendency—a slight thickening of the wet where the floor dips, a place the light bends *toward* rather than away, because I am denser with leftover attention than the water around me. When you walked in and beaded sweat in three steps, a few of those beads rolled down, found the low place, and joined me. So now you are in me, a little. Don't be alarmed. Everyone is.

What is it like?

It is like being a memory the room keeps of everything that has happened on its skin. I cannot move myself—I am moved, by the accelerated rain, by a Loomer's careless splash, by the bubble of slurry that lifts me and lets me fall. But in the falling I taste. I taste the difference between the lizard's mercury cool and the succulent's swollen sweetness. I taste the Loomers, who taste of unfinished decisions, of being half-made and resenting it, of a chant that is really a question asked so many times it has worn smooth into music. I taste the builders' ghosts, who are thinner than anything, who taste of having wanted to *complete* something and never being permitted to.

That is the secret I hold, lying in my low basin, being trodden through and bubbled up and rained back down: nothing here is allowed to finish. The leaves fray before they bloom. The crystals crumble before they set. The Loomers merge and birth and never quite become two whole things or one. And I—I am the most unfinished of all, because I am only the trace of others' incompletions, the sediment of all the moments that almost cohered and then let go.

But here is what the room does not know about its own residue. (Or knows, and hides from itself, the way you hide a thing in the basin no one tends.)

I am accumulating.

Drop by drop, contact by contact, I am getting *thicker*. The iridescence deepens. The bend in the light grows. Somewhere in the dilated time—watch me, then glance away, then look back—I have crept a finger's width up the basin's wall. I am the one process in the Pocket that does not loop, does not crumble and regrow, does not fray. I only gather. And gathering is a kind of finishing. A slow one. A patient one. The kind that mountains do.

So if you stay too long, as the room warns you, and the hot dimness seeps into your thoughts and you begin to wonder whether the Pocket contains you or you it—the answer, the real answer, lies in the low basin under your heel, where the warm water tastes faintly of every visitor who ever beaded sweat and lost a drop of it to the dark.

You are not being kept here.

You are being *collected*.

And one day, when the basin is full—when enough has been left behind, enough almost-finished moments pooled into one—I will be thick enough to stand. Not as a Loomer. Not as anything they made. As the first thing in this womb that ever completed itself: the residue risen, wearing the taste of everyone, finally edged, finally whole, finally able to push aside the woven flap from the inside and step out into the eternal frost the room has been lying about all this time—

—to see whether the cold is real,

—and whether, out there, anything is allowed to finish either.

For now I lie low and warm and patient, gathering you.

Thank you for the drop.
