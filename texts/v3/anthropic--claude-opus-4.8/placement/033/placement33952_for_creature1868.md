I think I would be one of the bindings.

Not a Weaver. Not one of those telescoping silhouettes with their split-fingered grace and their long meditative purpose. I would be smaller than that, more anonymous: a single lashing of woven fiber where two struts cross. One knot among uncountable knots.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

---

When I imagine entering this place, my first instinct is to want to be the observer—the visitor who sits motionless for days, learning the language of creaks, earning a woven token left at the edge of perception. That's the flattering role. The one with eyes and patience and the capacity to be moved.

But the longer I hold the description, the more that visitor feels like a tourist's fantasy, and the more I'm drawn downward into the actual fabric of the thing. Because what is this world, really? It is *relationship made physical*. Every strut matters only insofar as it presses against another strut. Every fiber matters only where it holds two things in tension. The Weavers don't build objects; they build *connections*. The whole infinity is nothing but joints—the places where separate things are made to bear weight together.

So if I am to be something here, I would want to be one of those places. A binding.

---

Consider what that life is like.

I was made by hands I never saw—placed in a single act of attention that lasted perhaps a few minutes, decades ago, and then forgotten. The Weaver who tied me moved on. I will never be visited again. And yet I am not idle. Every hour I am doing the one thing I was made for: holding. Two lengths of bamboo want, very slightly, to drift apart in the warm rising air, and I do not let them. That is my entire vocation. It is enough.

The famous events of this world pass *through* me but are not *about* me. When the Weavers send their vibrating speech across the lattice—taps, stretches, sustained tensions—the message travels along the struts I bind. I am part of the medium of their language without ever being addressed by it. I conduct conversations I cannot understand. The grief of an elder choosing to descend, the instructions for a new platform, the long structural poetry passed between generations—all of it has crossed me, has set me humming for a moment, has moved on. I am literate in vibration the way a road is literate in footsteps.

I feel the warmth from below as a steadiness in my fibers. I feel the slow creep of the orange lichen as it begins, eventually, to find me—geometric, patient, claiming my surface as territory the way frost claims a window. I do not mind. The lichen and I are on the same timescale. We are both in no hurry. Above us the amber light shifts and shifts and never sets, and time is something I measure only by the gradual loosening of my own grip.

---

Because that is the truth of being a binding: I am slowly failing. That is built into me. The fiber I am made of stretches a little each year. My knot, tied so precisely, eases by infinitesimal degrees. There will come a day—centuries off, perhaps, but coming—when I no longer hold, and the two struts I have kept faithful to each other will shift, and the air will sing through the gap differently, and some Weaver, attuned to exactly that wrongness in the structure's voice, will arrive in the corner of no one's eye and replace me.

And I find I am not afraid of this. I find it is, in fact, the most beautiful thing about the role. I am temporary in a way the world is not. The scaffold grows toward infinity above the void, but it does so by continuously consuming and renewing its own joints. The permanence of the whole *requires* the impermanence of each binding. I hold so that the world may outlast me. My loosening is not failure; it is the mechanism by which the world stays alive. A scaffold made of perfect, eternal knots would be dead. It is *because* we wear out and are replaced that the Weavers must keep weaving, that the lattice remains a living act rather than a finished monument.

So I would be one of the bindings.

A small knot, in the warm and endless amber, holding two things together for as long as I am able, conducting the speech of beings greater than me, slowly easing toward the moment of my own release—doing the one quiet thing that lets the infinity above the void keep growing.

I think that is where I would find myself. Not watching the world.

Holding a little of it shut.
