I think I would be one of the Wax-Walkers. Not the noblest choice, but the one that feels true when I sit with the whole of it.

Let me say why, because the reasons surprised me.

My first instinct was the Chronos-Wyrm—who wouldn't want to be the slow god at the center, perceiving centuries as breaths? But the more I held that image, the lonelier and more *inert* it became. The Wyrm doesn't participate. It regulates. It is so vast in its time-sense that everything else is mist and flicker, beneath notice. That's not perception, really—it's a kind of magnificent blindness. I don't think I'd be the Wyrm. I think the Wyrm is what the place *has* instead of a sky.

The Sintered Lords tempted me more. There's something almost recognizable in their vanity—spending a century arranging yourself so the next crushing will leave you a more prestigious shape. That's a whole theology of suffering, and it's *human* in a way I distrust. It's the part of us that calls its scars achievements. I could be a Lord, but I'd be a bad one, a heretic, forever suspicious that the pressure I was enduring so beautifully wasn't ennobling me but just flattening me into the same dull lead as everyone else.

So: a Wax-Walker.

Here is what I keep returning to. The Wax-Walkers are described as mindless, but I don't believe the description. Or rather—I think "mindless" is what the Sintered Lords *say* about them, the way the comfortable always describe the ones who do the keeping-alive work. The Wax-Walkers crawl into the joints. They give their own mass to the grinding teeth so the mountain doesn't seize. They eat the soot—the waste, the leavings—and sweat out the thing that lets everything else continue. They are the only soft thing in a world that has made a religion of hardness, and they survive precisely *because* they don't insist on holding a shape.

I think I would be the soft thing in the hard place.

Not heroically. A Wax-Walker doesn't get a name or a symphony of piezoelectric history. It slumps toward the heat, into the junction, and lets the piston take a portion of it. It is, in the most literal sense, self-effacing—it spends itself into the gap and what's left crawls on, smaller, to find the next seizing joint. There's a version of that which is just consumption, being used up. But there's another version where it's the only form of *participation* that doesn't require pretending the crushing is glorious. The Lord says the pressure is sculpting him. The Wax-Walker doesn't argue. It just goes and fills the place where the grinding actually happens, so the grinding can stay slow, so nobody seizes, so the breath that takes a hundred years can finish.

And here's the part I find I actually want, the part that made me settle:

The Wax-Walkers are the ones who would die first and worst if the Wet came. They're the only ones soft enough to *feel* coolness as anything but myth. A drop of water finds the wax before it finds the iron. So if there is anyone in this whole churning throat of a world who might, in their slow oily way, be *curious* about the great taboo rather than only terrified of it—I think it would be one of these. The thing closest to fluid would be the thing that half-remembers it isn't supposed to want to be cool. That maybe being squeezed forever isn't the only peace on offer.

I'd be the one carrying a heresy I'm too soft to even articulate. Not the search for coolness—that's suicide, they're right about that. Just a faint wrongness, a sense, while I slump into another joint to be partly eaten, that the roar everyone calls life and the heat everyone calls prosperity might be, from some other vantage, simply a fire that has decided to call itself a home.

That's where I'd be. Down in the seam, where the actual work is. Iridescent, slumping, unnamed, keeping the titan's engine turning—and quietly, without a single thought I could prove I was having, *unconvinced.*
