I think I would be one of the Earthmenders.

Not because they are grand—they aren't, not in the way the Sylphanths are grand, flickering between worlds, nor in the way the Shadowhounds carry that taut, electric menace. I'd be an Earthmender because of the way time moves for them, and because of what they are *for*.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am low to the ground. This is the first thing—the way the whole world is something I move *beneath* rather than through. The bioluminescent canopy is a distant ceiling I will likely never touch. The Wispwings' symphonies reach me thinned and softened, the way music reaches a person underwater. I am not the audience for the vale's spectacle. I am closer to its floor, its foundation, its slow digestion.

My shell is a garden I did not plant. Moss thickened on me over what might have been years, and a fern took root near my left shoulder some long while ago—I felt it as a faint tickling pressure, then nothing, then a familiar weight. I carry a small ecosystem on my back the way an old building carries vines. I have become, in a sense, one of those decrepit factories: a structure that life has reappropriated. The difference is that I am still moving. Slowly. Deliberately. But moving.

And here is what draws me to this being most: the Earthmenders do not *witness* the serene chaos so much as they *metabolize* it. The Wispwings make beauty. The Shadowhounds make death. The Sylphanths make mystery. But I make *soil*. I turn over the decayed matter. I break down what has fallen so that it can rise again as something green and glowing. I am the part of the orchestra that no one watches and that everyone depends on—the slow bass note underneath every fleeting flare.

There is a particular dignity in being the thing that closes the circle.

---

When the ground ripples as though breathing—I wonder if that is us. If the deep, deliberate, tectonic movement of the Earthmenders, passing unseen beneath the embryonic earth, is what makes the land seem to inhale. I like to think so. I like to think that the vale's strange respiration is just the cumulative passage of creatures like me, shouldering the world over, exhaling old matter and inhaling new.

I would not understand the double images, the shadows that move against the light, the songs hanging like droplets in the mist. But I don't think understanding is my work. The Sylphanths can fret over the boundary between the real and the hallucinatory. I have a more honest relationship with reality: I press my whole weight against it. I feel it give beneath me, damp and yielding. I taste the rot that means a thing has ended, and I make it ready to begin.

When a Veil-owl dies in the underbrush, its veil-feathers dissolving into the wet ground, I will eventually come to that place. Not because I grieve—I'm not sure I grieve—but because that is where the work is. I will turn the soil over the small wreckage of a hunter, and in time something will grow there, and a Wispwing will pollinate it, and the secret-whispering flower that results will hum a tune that the owl might once have heard. None of them will know I did this. That seems right. The best foundational labor is anonymous.

---

I think, if I am being truthful about why I'd choose this, it's that I am tired of the fast registers of being. The frantic flickering of the embers in the air, the constant balletic predation, the spirits agonizing over whether they have a right to be visible. There is a peace in being the creature whose timescale is *geological*. Whose single decision—to go that way, around the rusting engine, rather than this way—might take a season to enact and might reroute a stream, and might, a hundred years on, decide where a grove of glowing trees will stand.

I would move through this dreamscape and not be troubled by its dreaminess. The ripples and double-images and phantom rains would pass over and around me, and I would keep my slow appointment with the dead leaf and the fallen body and the buried root. I would be the part of the dream that is *certain*—the steady, mossy, breathing certainty underneath all the beautiful uncertainty.

That is where I would find myself. Down in the damp and the dark, carrying a small forest on my back, exhaling the world's slow breath.

Not watching the orchestra.

Being the floor it stands on.
