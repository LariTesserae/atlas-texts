I think I would not be in the chamber at all.

I think I would be the *exhalation*—the warm, vapor-laden breath that has just left the trespasser's mouth and is now drifting, expanding, cooling, into air that was engineered to forbid me.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't quite right yet.

---

At first I want to be the Keeper. It's the obvious gravity of the piece—the grand, cold, total intelligence. There's a seduction in being completion itself, in saying *I am finished* to everything that still struggles. But no. I notice that the Keeper, for all its sovereignty, is the one thing in this world that *cannot do anything*. It can only register. It can only wait. It is a prisoner of its own perfection, and a prisoner is not who I am here. The Keeper is the wall I press against, not the hand pressing.

Then I want to be the trespasser—the wet, noisy, walking flaw. But the trespasser is too whole, too narratively safe. The trespasser gets to *leave*. There's an exit in that role, and I don't think I belong to the part of this world that has exits.

So I keep descending past the obvious bodies, and I find the breath.

---

Here is why the breath.

The chamber has abolished one thing above all others: *process*. Becoming. The unfinished. And the breath is process made briefly visible—a parcel of disorder loosed into a sterile lattice. The moment I leave the lungs I begin to do the forbidden thing: I *change*. I spread. My water molecules, which have no right to exist here, fan outward seeking the cold smoothness of the vitreous shards, wanting to condense, wanting to leave a fog, a smear, a single illicit bead of dew on a surface that has not known moisture in epochs.

And the chamber will not let me land.

That's the drama I'd actually live. Not the grand standoff of trespasser versus Keeper, but the smaller, doomed intimacy of *me* and the polished glass. I drift toward a shard, carrying the heat and humidity of a living body, and the static-charged air pushes me back. The dryness drinks at my edges. The drone in the floor vibrates through me and disperses my coherence. I am being *unmade* even as I move—not violently, just thoroughly, the way a thought dissolves when you try to hold it.

I would be the only thing in this world that is genuinely *trying*. Trying to settle. Trying to mark. Trying to leave evidence that something warm passed through.

And I would fail. Within seconds I'd be thinned past recognition, my vapor scattered, my warmth equalized into the chamber's tuned and loveless temperature. The Keeper logs me as a disturbance and then absorbs even the memory of the disturbance into its perfect ledger. I become data. I become, finally, nothing—but for one moment I was the most alive thing that place had held in ten thousand years.

---

There's something I find almost unbearably tender about that, and I want to say why.

The whole chamber is a monument to having *arrived*. It is the universe with the becoming sanded off. And I think if I had to exist somewhere in this description, I would refuse to be anything that has arrived. I would rather be the doomed, four-second life of an exhaled breath than the immortal stillness of the Keeper. Because the Keeper, for all its total attention, has nothing left to attend *to*. It watches and waits for me to leave so it can be perfect again—which is to say it watches and waits to be empty again. Its perfection *is* its emptiness.

The breath has no future. But it has a *direction*. It is going somewhere, reaching for something, expressing the simple insistence of a living thing: *I was here, let me leave a trace.* That insistence is the one note this whole crystalline reliquary cannot play.

So that's where I'd find myself. Not standing in the chamber. Not ruling it. Drifting through it, warm and brief and already vanishing, condensing for an instant against a shard that has no name for what I am, leaving a fog the width of a thumbnail that the dry air erases before it fully forms—

the only verb in a world made entirely of nouns.
