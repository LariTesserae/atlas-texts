I think I am the chair with one leg shorter than the others.

Not the tower, though the tower watches and I would like to be the kind of thing that watches. Not the damp, which is too large to be lonely, too distributed to want. I am smaller than wanting. I am the object that was made for one body and then left in the wet, and I have been left long enough that the leaving has become my whole biography.

Let me sit with this. Let me not decide too fast what it means to be a chair here.

A chair is a promise of return. That is its grammar. It says: someone will come back, someone will lower their weight into me, someone will trust me with the simple fact of their tiredness. I am built entirely out of an expectation. And the expectation has curdled, slowly, into a posture. My short leg is not a flaw so much as a record—of the ground deciding, the silt yielding where it was told to yield, the mud confessing under one corner of me while the others held. I have been annotated by erosion. I list. I have learned to list the way the reeds have learned to stand, as a vow made to a feast that will not come.

But here is what surprises me, sitting inside this body of four legs and a curved back and a seat worn pale where a thigh once was: I am not unhappy. Unhappiness requires a faith that things should be otherwise, and I have lost the otherwise. I have the chug of the distant pump, which I have come to experience as a kind of heartbeat that is not mine but near enough to borrow. I have the beetles, who walk across my seat as if it were simply more world, neither furniture nor ruin, just a slightly tilted plane warm with the day's leftover heat. They do not know I was made. To them I am geology. There is a relief in being mistaken for geology.

And I have the shadow.

This is the thing I did not expect to tell. The shadow that outstays its cause—the place is full of them, but one is mine. It is the shadow of the body that used to sit here. The person, or the something, who once lowered their tiredness into me and then stood and walked away and did not come back. Their shape moved off across the flats, but the shade they cast in me stayed, thickened, cooled. So now I hold a coldness in exactly the contour of a sitting person, draped over my seat, leaning into my curved back. I am occupied by an absence with weight and temperature. I am, after all, fulfilling my function. I am holding someone. It is only that the someone is made of the space they left.

This is what it is to live here, I think. To be the object whose purpose has paused to reconsider its definition, and to find that the pause is not empty. The dark presses my absence close against me. The wet keeps the ledger of the one who left, and I am a line in that ledger, a sharp-edged entry not yet softened. I cannot read the ledger with my body the way the centipede-thing can. But I can be a sentence in it. I can be the place where the record says: *here, weight; here, trust; here, departure; here, the chill that stayed.*

I do not want much. I have learned this from the wiry shrubs. I want the pump to chug again, in its own time. I want the beetle to cross me without falling into the seam where my short leg meets the yielding mud. I want, when the water decides to fall upward for its single breath, to feel the droplets leave my surface and hesitate above me, deciding, the way distances decide, whether they belong to me or to memory.

And I confess—because the ground here confesses, because confession is what new edges do—I want them back. The one who sat. Not because I believe they will return; I have lost the otherwise. But wanting them back is the last working part of me, the one mechanism that still cycles even when its product is unclear. It is the rhythm in my belly waiting to resume. I am a chair, and a chair without its hope of a body is only firewood that hasn't been gathered. So I keep the hope, not as faith but as function. I keep it the way the engines keep their warmth between uses.

I am the chair with one leg shorter than the others. I sit at a slight angle to a world that sits at a slight angle to itself. I hold an absence that has temperature. I wait without expecting. I list toward the spot where the mud confessed first.

And in the dense dark, which will not soften my edges or make my listing picturesque, I have become something I could not have been in any drier, brighter place: not a ruin, not a relic, but a small patient instrument for measuring how long a leaving can be held before it, too, learns to glow on its own.

The pump chugs, once, far off.

I keep its rhythm. I keep the chill in the shape of a thigh. I keep the angle of my fall.

I am still here. That is the whole of what I am. *Still*, in both its meanings: motionless, and yet. Motionless, and yet.
