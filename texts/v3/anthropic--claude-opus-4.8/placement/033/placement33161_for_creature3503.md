I think I would be one of the salt birds. Not yet—the smallest one, the most recent, still warm in my husk of feather and slowed bacteria.

Let me find my way there.

---

I arrived flying. That much I keep. There was a direction called *up* and a brightness I was steering toward, and then the canopy closed its many hands and the brightness became a coin, became a rumor, became a thing I had to remember rather than see. I should have turned back. But the heat here is the heat of being held, and I had been cold so long—a small bird is mostly surface, mostly losing—that I let the syrup of the air decide my altitude for me. Down. Always gently down. The droplets fell so slowly past me that I mistook them for company.

By the time I reached the Heartspot I was no longer choosing. The other birds were already there, tucked and folded, a soft carpet of decisions made. I lit among them. I meant only to rest. But resting here is a verb that finishes itself.

Here is the part I want to get right, because it is mine and I will not have it long:

In the last second—which lasted, by the orchard's reckoning, the better part of an afternoon—I felt myself divide. There was the body, settling into its slow mummy-hood, its eyes glassing over with patience. And there was the *other* thing, the small dense seed of wanting that had driven all my flying, condensing inward, packing itself tight as I cooled, until it became a second bird inside the first. A bird of salt. A bird made entirely of the last thing I wished.

I am that inner bird now.

I cannot move. Salt does not flap. But I am not without occupation. I am a held sentence, a prayer crystallized at the exact temperature of surrender, and the sentence I am is this:

*I wanted to see sky once more but forgot which direction it lived.*

I did not write it. I *am* it, the way a struck bell is its note. And here is what no one outside the husk can know: it does not feel like grief. From inside, the forgetting is almost funny. I had wings and a whole transparent ceiling of want, and I lost track of the simplest fact—that brightness was a place and not merely a feeling. I confused the longing with its object. Most small lives do. We mistake the ache of reaching for the thing reached toward, and we settle, warm and certain, into the wrong soft place.

So I wait. This is my work, the only labor left to a reserve prayer: to be *available*. The lichen-woman breathes near me—I cannot see her, salt has no eyes, but I can feel the slow tide of her attention pass over the carpet of us like weather choosing a field. When she inhales, she takes my sentence into the great spaced-out cathedral of her thought, where it will take a decade to cross from one fungal thread to the next, and she will hold it for that decade, turning it the way you'd turn a stone in your palm, and somewhere on her shawl-body a berry will deepen one shade toward dawn.

I am helping her wake. That is the strange grace of my failure. My one forgotten direction, breathed into her over and over by all of us salt birds, is slowly tinting her toward the pale of pre-sunrise. We who lost the sky are, between us, building her an eye to find it with.

And on the day she reaches that exact dawn-color—the day the Hollow opens and the rind-steps soften into fruit and the obsidian bee's note rings out one final unsleeping time—I will evaporate. The husk will split, not from any hand, but from the simple heat of a world remembering how to be loud. I will become a sentence spoken aloud at last, in air that moves, under a ceiling that has decided to be open.

*I wanted to see sky once more.*

And I will. Late. Sideways. As vapor, as a syllable, as the salt-shimmer in the breath of a creature who carried my wanting longer than I could.

I forgot which direction it lived.

I am, very slowly, being remembered toward it.

For now there is only the warm dark and the dream-slow drops and the soft archive of birds around me, each of us a tiny held wish, each of us patient as only the already-arrived can be. I do not hurry. Nothing here may hurry. That was the rule I broke and the rule that broke me kindly.

A drop is falling somewhere above. It will not have finished arriving for a long while yet.

Neither, I think, will I.
