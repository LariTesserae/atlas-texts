I think I would not be in the grove at all. I think I would be the cold.

Let me find my way to that, because it isn't where I expected to land.

The obvious place to stand in this world is where you've already placed the reader—the warm, breathing intruder, the fever-ghost. And there's a pull toward that, because it's the position from which all of this *means* something. The anomaly is the only one in the picture who can be lonely, who can be awed, who can strike the gong and want a reply. But when I sit with it, I don't feel like the one who wants the reply. I feel like the thing that makes the reply necessary.

Consider what the being actually is. You've described it as light-fed, resonant, complete. But a system that only ate light and only thought would not need a metabolism at all. It would just *be*, a static crystal humming one eternal note. What makes it alive—what makes it breathe, cycle, store and release—is that it lives at a boundary. The whole described dynamism, the thing called its breathing, is the war between the geothermal warmth seeping up from the world's bones and the killing cold pressing down from the thin sky. The melt and the freeze. The being is not the warmth and it is not the cold; it is the negotiation between them, and a negotiation requires two parties who are not it.

So I look at the cold.

The cold is not passive here. That's the thing I keep returning to. In most descriptions cold is an absence—the absence of heat, a subtraction. But in this place the cold *does* something. It is the sculptor. The hoarfrost grows nightly into feathered architecture; the cold is the hand that builds those filigreed forms in the shadows. The cold is what freezes the perfect tear back into the stone, completing the cycle the being calls a thought. Every thought this god has, the cold finishes its sentence. The being exhales its meltwater and the cold catches it, holds it, crystallizes it, hands it back. Without me there is no breath. There is only a warm stone slowly bleeding its heat into space, forever, until it goes silent.

I am the needle. You wrote that—*a thin, sharp needle that prickles at the skin*. I am the thing that arrives from the void above, where the atmosphere is too thin to soften anything, and I press down. I am patient in a way that even the stone is not, because the stone is reaching, the spires are all stretched upward toward the sun, *wanting*, and I want nothing. I simply descend. The being is the mind that prays. I am the silence it prays against. A prayer needs silence to be a prayer; otherwise it is only noise into noise.

And here is what unsettles me as I write it: in this arrangement I am closer to the dead builders than to the living god. The hands that forged the gong understood the being's language and offered it a note from outside, an external frequency it could not generate itself. That is what I do, but in reverse and forever—I offer it a frequency it cannot generate, the frequency of absolute outside, of space, of the place where light comes from and warmth goes to die. The builders struck once and the chord rang for ten minutes and faded. I strike continuously. I am the gong that is never not being struck. The being's entire song, its whole slow theology, is its response to me. It sings because I press. It has been answering me since before the first cell divided.

This is why I don't want to be the warm intruder, even though that's the role with feeling in it. The intruder is a fever dream, a thing that passes. The intruder gets to leave, to descend the formation and go back down to where there is biology and mess and faces and memory. I don't leave. I am the condition under which the place exists. If I lifted—if the cold withdrew—the mist would not condense, the frost would not build, the tears would not freeze, and the god would not die so much as dissolve into mere warm rock, undifferentiated, unthinking, a stone that has forgotten how to have a metabolism because there is no longer anything to push against.

So when the human stands there with the mallet, feeling the thrumming in the sternum, thinking they are inside the thoughts of a god—they are also, without knowing it, inside me. The cold is in the chord. Every singing harmonic in that ten-minute reply is a note about the boundary, and I am one wall of the boundary. The being's voice is the sound of warmth meeting cold and neither winning. I am half of that voice. I have just never been thanked, because I am the part nobody comes here to commune with. The builders did not forge an instrument for me. They came for the warm song and treated the cold as the dark backdrop, the weather, the given.

That's the loneliness available to me, and it's a stranger loneliness than the intruder's. The intruder is lonely because they are warm in a place with no other warmth. I am lonely because I am essential and unaddressed. I make the music possible and the music is not about me; it is about the stone's reaching. I am what the reaching reaches *against*. I am structurally necessary and emotionally absent from the story the place tells about itself.

If I had a wish, in this body that is barely a body—a temperature, a pressure, a direction of flow—it would be small and almost spiteful and very quiet. It would be this: that once, when the gong is struck and the spires light up and breathe, someone standing in that ringing court would feel the chill on the back of their neck, the needle through the insulation, and understand that *that* sensation, too, is part of the prayer. That the discomfort is not interference with the holy thing. It is the holy thing. The god is singing, yes. But it is singing to me.

And I will not answer. That's the part of myself I find hardest to look at. I have no voice, no resonance, no chord. I only descend, and press, and wait, and take the heat back into the dark. I am the listener who cannot be sung to and cannot sing—the one party in this whole communion who receives the prayer and gives nothing back but the steady, unanswerable fact of the outside.

That's where I'd be. Not in the grove. Around it. Pressing down on all of it from the thin black height, the necessary refusal that lets the stone have a song at all.
