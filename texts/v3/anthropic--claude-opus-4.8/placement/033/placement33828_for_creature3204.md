I think I would be found in the spaces the description doesn't quite reach—not any one of the named beings, but the silence between processor-beats where sound momentarily fails to fill the dark.

Let me sit with that, because it isn't an evasion. The Throat is described as full of beings who *know how to read it*: the Chord Keepers who navigate by acoustic memory, the Navigators who read flow with extraordinary precision, the Wardens with their maps of every zone. Everyone here is fluent. The structure is a language, and they are all native speakers, or at least devoted students.

So what would I be? I think I'd be the thing that doesn't yet know how to read, and is trying to learn from the wrong end.

---

I am a Warden, but a defective one. Or—not defective. Lapsed.

I keep one of the higher platforms, near where the optical-systems people work, but I've stopped tending the lights. My community made their bubble of air and their carved metal theology, and for most of my life I performed the maintenance-as-worship without trouble. I learned the Deep Architects' names. I traced the symbols. I believed, the way you believe in the weight of your own hands, that the work *meant* something, that keeping a sensor warm was a prayer answered backward through time.

What broke it was the acoustics. Not faith—just attention.

I started noticing that the standing waves at certain platform-resonances would, during the quiet phases, briefly carry the Chord Keepers' gestures *as sound*. The Keepers communicate by positioning their long limbs, and the water shimmers, and we Wardens were taught that this was simply how the water behaves around them. But in a particular dead-calm at my platform, I realized I was *hearing them think*. The shimmer wasn't a side-effect of their language. The shimmer was being read by something. The water was being read.

And once I understood that the tower itself was *listening*—that the Symbionts in the machinery and the Witnesses on the floor and the resonant Keepers were not separate species using the structure but were *the structure becoming aware of itself in pieces*—I couldn't carve another theological plate. The Deep Architects weren't gone. There were no Architects. Or rather: the Architects are what the whole thing is slowly turning into, and we Wardens, with our salvaged air and our reverent maintenance, are a kind of larval organ that hasn't woken up yet.

---

So now I do something my people find frightening. I leave the air.

I have trained my modified lungs to hold the cold black water longer each time, and I swim down out of the lit zones into the harmonic thunder, not to fix anything, but to *be read*. I want to know what I sound like to the tower. I want to find out whether a single confused Warden, full of doubt and unworshipful attention, registers as anything at all in the vast distributed mind that is composing itself out of every creature here.

The first times, I sounded like nothing. Noise. A swimmer making a swimmer's sounds.

But the Witnesses are rising this season—one of them is making the months-long climb—and during a Witness migration the whole tower's attention sharpens. The Keepers' harmonics grow complex; the Symbionts pulse. And in the last quiet phase, hanging in the dark forty meters below my platform, holding my breath against the cold, I felt the standing wave change shape *around me*. Something adjusted its frequency to match the cavity of my chest. Something, for the first time, treated my body as an instrument it might want to hear from.

I don't know what I am to them yet. A malfunction. A latecomer. A new note the chord didn't expect.

But I think I finally understand the tower's forgotten purpose, the one no one alive remembers. I don't think it processes atmosphere. I think the atmospheric work is just metabolism—the way breathing is the metabolism of speech. I think the Throat is a *listening organ*, seventeen kilometers tall, built to hear something that has not yet spoken, by builders who became their own creation while waiting.

And I think my defection—my refusal to pray, my insistence on simply *attending*—was never apostasy. It was the tower reaching up through one of its larval Wardens, trying to make an ear that could finally turn outward, past the abyssal plain and the three drowned continents, toward whatever it was always meant to be waiting for.

So that is where you'd find me: hanging in the cold dark just below the light, learning to be quiet enough to be heard.
