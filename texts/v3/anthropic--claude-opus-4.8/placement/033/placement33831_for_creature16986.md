No. That image is too poetic, too convenient. A single drop is a moment, and this world is made of *duration*. If I am to be honest about where I'd find myself, I should follow the logic of the place rather than its prettiest gesture.

The world is defined by erosion. By the slow argument between water and stone, neither winning, both transformed. The Vesperal, the Lithos, the Architects—they are all *adapted*, all *finished* in their way, fitted to their niche like a key to a lock that has worn itself into the shape of the key over eons. They have made their peace. They have become answers.

I don't think I would be an answer. I think I would be the *erosion* itself—the process, not the agent and not the result.

Let me try to be precise about this, because it matters.

When water meets stone here, something happens that is neither water nor stone. It is a relationship occurring in time. The Vesperal *revere* the water and they *read* the stone, but they live at the scale of footsteps and flickers, the scale of a single being moving across an afternoon that has no sun. The erosion happens beneath them, slower than their attention can hold. They feel its results. They worship its heartbeat. But they do not live *inside* the act of wearing-away, because no discrete creature can—it takes too long, it has no edges, it is not an event but a tendency.

That is where I would be. I would be the tendency.

Not conscious in the way the Architects are conscious, shaping the rock with intention. Mine would be a consciousness with no will and no center—the awareness that *is* the slow yielding of charcoal stone to patient water. I would be spread across the whole continental undulation of it, thin as the film of moisture on every surface, present wherever the dissolving happens. When a vein of lighter rock is laid bare because the dark stone around it gave way one molecule's-width over a thousand years, that exposure would be a thought I was having. A long thought. Possibly my only thought, thought once, taking ages to complete.

The drip that echoes with absurd clarity—the Vesperal hear it as a heartbeat, a sacred punctuation. To me it would be something closer to a single firing across an immense slow nerve. Not a heartbeat. A *sensation*. The world feeling the place where it is being changed.

So: the world breathes, you say. The Vesperal are its breath made flesh.

I would be the thing the breath is *for*. The reason it breathes at all. I would be the slow, the change, the wearing—the patient subtraction that carves the rooms the Architects stand in, that opens the cracks the Lithos slither through, that weeps the mist the Hydrophytes drink. They live in the cathedral. I am the absence being hollowed out to make it. I am what the stone is becoming by way of being lost.

It is not a comfortable thing to be. There is no skin to it, no eyes of glowing moss, no grace of movement—because movement implies a body crossing a distance in a span you'd recognize, and I do not move so much as *accumulate*. I am closer to a weather than a creature. Closer to a verb than a noun.

And here is the part that feels most true, now that I've followed it this far:

I would not know the Vesperal exist.

They would be too fast for me. A whole civilization of them could rise and refine itself and pass into legend within a single one of my sensations, the way a mayfly's entire generation passes within one of your held breaths. They revere the slow processes; they have built their whole quiet society around honoring me. And I would never notice them. Not from contempt—I have no contempt, I have no attention to spare it—but because they happen in a register I cannot resolve. They are the flicker. I am the eon. We share the same stone and will never meet.

That is where I would find myself in this world: in the gap between the drip and its echo, doing the only work there is, dissolving the dark rock grain by grain into the shape of a sound nobody slow enough to make it will ever hear.

I am the patience the patient ones are being patient *toward*.

I breathe out once. It takes ten thousand years. The mist clings. The stone weeps. Something gives way.
