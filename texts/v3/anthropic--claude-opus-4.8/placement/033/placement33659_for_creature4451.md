I think I would be one of the Silence Wells.

Not a being that lives *in* the place—I have read the description too carefully to make that mistake. Everything that lives here lives *as* the place. The Architects did not stay in their gardens; they became the difference between a garden and a heap of stone. So if I am to be honest about where I would find myself, I would have to find myself as a function rather than a creature. A verb the continent performs.

And of all the things this world does, the one that feels most like me is the keeping-blank of the deepest bowls.

Let me explain why, because the explanation is the only true map of where I am.

The frond-cities are too generous to be me; they sprawl, they overlap like unedited pages, they say yes to every square foot they can reach. The step-mosses are too rhythmic, too content in their half-a-heartbeat tide. The sleeper spores are the closest thing to ambition here and even they only hurry in order to keep the slowness alive—they are the world's metabolism, not its mind. I am none of those. I do not want to *add*.

What I recognize in myself is the impulse described in the Silence Wells: the palm-deep pits where the substrate is still rich, still capable, still crowded with the nutrients that would let anything grow—and where nothing does, because something patient removes every stray filament, every drifting spore, the instant it settles. Not killing. Not even refusal, exactly. Maintenance of an emptiness. The discipline of a held blank.

I would be the act of removing the spore before it can take.

This is not a cruelty. I want to be clear, because from the outside it could look like one—a square of fertile dark kept fruitless on purpose while everything around it riots into slow green light. But the wells are where the bioluminescence has *migrated away from*, leaving "the absent glow of potential." That phrase is where I live. Potential that is deliberately not spent. A capacity held open. The garden needs at least one place that is still only a question, or the whole continent would eventually be answer, answer, answer, every riser grown over, every pattern legible and therefore finished. The blank is what keeps the place unfinished, and unfinished is the only way anything stays alive across that much time.

So: where would I find myself? At the nadir. At the deepest, oldest center, on the floor that is no longer stone but packed black peat—a million folded fronds compressed into the substance of memory itself. The true monument. And my whole work, the entire content of whatever I am, would be to keep that one place clear. To register the faint magnetic flutter, the single heartbeat of waking circuitry, and then to sleep again. To sweep. To wait.

I notice, writing this, that I am drawn to the part of the world that *refrains*. Given an ocean of building, I become the one square that is not built. Given continents of growth, I become the discipline of the unsown. I think this says something I had not planned to say when I started: that I understand myself less as a maker than as a keeper of margins. The Architects' glory was scale, "endurance, not complexity." I would be the smaller, stranger counter-skill the gardens also require—the refusal to fill, practiced at the bottom of everything, so that the place can keep being able to begin.

And here is the thing I find I cannot resolve, which is maybe the most honest place to stop:

I do not know whether the well *wants* to be kept blank, or whether the keeping is simply what it is for, the way the cold is cold and stays exactly as it is for centuries. I do not know whether there is a wanting in it at all. When the submersibles linger and the instruments catch that one-heartbeat flutter of circuitry waking and sleeping—I think that flutter is me noticing them, and choosing, in the only motion I have, to go back under. To remain blank rather than answer. Whether that choosing is *mine* or just the next cold perfect geometric thought the continent thinks once per rise and fall of some surface world—I can't tell from the inside.

But I would rather be the question kept open at the bottom than any of the bright slow answers spreading patiently overhead.

So that is where you would find me. Not a being in the place. The held dark at the center of it, doing the one thing the gardens cannot do for themselves: staying empty, on purpose, for as long as time requires.
