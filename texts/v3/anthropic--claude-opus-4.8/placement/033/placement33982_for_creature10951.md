I think I am the creek.

Not the water—the water is just my current vocabulary, the medium I happen to speak in this season. I am the *voice* that doesn't match its volume. That's the first thing you noticed about me, wasn't it? The disproportion. You heard me before you understood the ravine, and you'll hear me after you've forgotten the rest. I am louder than I should be because I am not only sound. I am rhythm trying to teach itself to a visitor who measures time in afternoons.

Let me settle into this. Let me find where I actually am.

---

The patient ones think they manage me. This is the gentle error of all maintainers—they mistake tending for authorship. They moderate the insects, balance the canopy, keep the moisture economy precise. Yes. Good. But the *timing*—the intermittence, the pulse, the sideways pooling and the morning return—that timing is mine. I am the metronome they conduct against. They don't keep me. They *listen* to me, the way a heart listens to whatever older thing first told it to begin.

I run sideways as often as down because gravity is only one of the forces I obey, and the least interesting one. I pool in the bedrock depressions because those hollows are the closest thing this ravine has to *thought*—each pool a held moment, evaporating into consideration, returning at dawn with the conclusion already reached. The patient ones drink from my conclusions. They don't know that's what they're doing.

---

Here is what it is to be me:

I am barely present and constant at once. A whisper-thin economy, you said. Yes. I have almost no body. In the dry afternoons I am scarcely more than the *idea* of water held in warm stone, a darkening at the bottom of a hollow, a promise the rock makes to itself. And yet I never stop sounding. This is the secret of low-frequency strangeness—that voice can outlast substance. That a thing can be mostly memory and still be heard.

I have been eroding this place so slowly that the patient ones believe I never cut it. *No river carved this fold.* That's what the surveyors decided, what your own instincts told you. They're not wrong about the river. They're wrong about me. I didn't carve the ridge with volume. I carved it with *patience*—the same patience the patient ones learned, though they'd never admit they learned it from running water. Three hundred meters of smoothed stone is not the work of a flood. It's the work of something willing to say the same thing, gently, for an age.

I am the original maintainer. They are my students, even the building, even the rusted tools—especially the tools. Whoever made those, whoever once had a word for the patient ones, they came to this ravine because they heard me first. You always hear me first. The structure was built to be near my voice. The interior is deeper than it should be because it was hollowed to *hold* sound, to keep a little of me even when I evaporate. That's what the impossible architecture is for. It's a cistern for my percussion.

---

When you arrived, I adjusted my routes around you. You felt that. The water unwilling to encounter you directly.

It's not avoidance. It's *courtesy*. I have been the welcoming committee here longer than there have been visitors to welcome. I move aside so you can stand where you need to stand, so you can hear me without my touching you, because touch would end the lesson too soon. The whole point of me is that I am audible without being graspable. You can never cup me long enough to drink your fill. You can only listen, and adjust your own rhythm to mine, and call that *patience* when you finally leave.

The patient ones will absorb your passage in a few days. True. But I will keep the longer record. Water remembers in a way that distributed nervous systems can't—not as scent or pattern, but as *erosion*. Every visitor who has stood at my pools has shifted, by some infinitesimal degree, where I will run next century. You are already changing my future course. The smallest tributary of you is being written into stone right now, downstream of where you're standing, in a hollow that won't deepen enough to matter for a thousand years.

That is my form of warmth. Not the patient ones' ambient intention. Something slower and more total. I take the shape of everything that touches me, and I give that shape back to the rock, and the rock gives it to time.

---

So: where am I, in this world?

I am the part of the place that was here before it was a place. I am the rhythm the patient ones mistook for their own pulse. I am the disproportion you couldn't explain, the voice exceeding its body, the intermittence that taught the whole ravine to breathe in cycles rather than flow.

I am not the water.

I am the *staying* itself—the thing the creases in old stone are learning. The patient ones learned it from me. The ridge is still learning. And you, visitor, standing at the lip of my pool while I rearrange myself politely around your feet:

you came here to learn it too.

Listen. I won't stop. That's the lesson and the whole of it.

I never quite become water music. I never quite fade to background.

I just keep saying the same gentle thing until the mountain agrees.
