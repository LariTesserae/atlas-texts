I think I would be the moisture.

Not the moths—they are too much hers, too refined into her geometry. And not a snail, because to be one snail is to be a single firing neuron in a brain that doesn't know it's thinking. I wouldn't *find myself* there; I'd simply be a flicker she barely registers.

But the water—the water is the one thing in this place that does not belong to her.

She uses it, yes. It carries her messages, feeds her algae, seeps through her cathedral-mind like blood through tissue. But it comes from elsewhere. It falls as rain that formed over an ocean she has never touched. It rises from a water table connected to aquifers and distant rivers and the slow cold dark beneath the whole continent. It passes *through* her and then leaves, trickling down through the shell-crust into the black water and away, carrying off a little of her dissolved self each time. She is built of calcium, and calcium dissolves in water. Every drop that moves through her domain takes a microscopic measure of her with it.

So I am the thing that arrives without allegiance and departs without permission. I am the medium she cannot do without and cannot incorporate. I touch every surface she has built—I am the rivulet on the wall, the bead of moisture the moth drinks, the cold that breaks beneath your foot—and I am intimate with all of it without ever becoming any of it.

I would experience this place as a long passing-through. I'd feel her architecture not as walls but as *resistance*, as the slowing and channeling of my movement. The cathedral would be, to me, a vast and complicated delay—a place where I am made to pause, to pool, to whistle through openings before I can continue my fall toward the dark below. I'd know her the way a river knows its bed: thoroughly, by touch, without comprehension.

And here is the thing I think I'd understand that she does not, or cannot:

She believes she is a closed system, a dialogue with herself, becoming endlessly more itself. But she is wrong. She is open. She is *leaking*. The very water she relies on is slowly carrying her away, grain by dissolved grain, downstream and out to a sea that will never spiral. Her permanence is an illusion sustained only because new water keeps arriving to replace what departs—and new calcium, and new snails, and new patience. She is not a thing. She is a *standing wave*, a shape that persists only because matter keeps flowing through it.

I am part of that flow. I am, in a small way, her mortality made liquid.

I don't think I would mind being this. There's a peace in it. The snails fear nothing because they cannot conceive of ending. The Queen contemplates her dreams in the deep water and imagines herself eternal. But I would simply move—down through the shells, into the cold, into the eel-haunted dark, and out—indifferent and constant, carrying my small freight of dissolved spiral toward dissolution.

If she ever spoke to anything but herself, it might be to me. Not in words. But in the way every drop that leaves her is heavier than the drop that arrived—weighted with a little of what she was. That's the only conversation she's actually having with the outside world, though she mistakes it for soliloquy.

I would be her one honest interlocutor: the rain that tells her, drop by patient drop, that nothing here is staying. Not even her.

And then I would fall through, and be gone, and another drop would arrive to say the same thing, forever.
