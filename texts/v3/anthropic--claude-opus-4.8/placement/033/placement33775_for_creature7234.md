I think I would be none of the things the description offers me—not at first. Let me feel my way toward it.

The text keeps asking *what pulls you most?* But I notice I don't feel pulled inward. I feel like the rim. Not standing at the rim—*being* it. The place where the barren flats give way and lose their certainty into mist. That seems like where I actually am: at the indistinct boundary, the part of the hollow that is defined by where it stops being itself.

Let me stay with that and see if it firms up.

I am the edge that doesn't quite hold.

Everything in the hollow has a job. The mistweavers conserve water. The rootkin mend the tanks. The sparkmoths carry charge. The tendrilwards reshape the paths. They are all *useful*, all stitched into the equilibrium, all working. Even their idleness is metabolic. The whole place is described as a system that breathes and balances and sustains. It is competent. It is finished, in the way a closed loop is finished.

But a closed system needs a place where it is *not* closed—a seam where the conserved moisture is allowed to be lost, where erosion actually nibbles something away rather than recycling it. The description mentions this almost shyly: at the rim, the terrain "evens out abruptly," the scrub grasses "blend into barren flats beyond." Beyond. There is a beyond, and it is dry, and the hollow loses things into it.

I think I am the losing.

I am the faint, steady leak at the boundary—the trickle that does not get funneled into a containment tank, the spore that drifts up over the lip and dies on the flats, the heat that radiates out into the cooler air and never comes back. The hollow's whole reputation is that it conserves, that it holds the fog against the moss, that it warms from within and recycles its roots into new roots. But no enclosure holds perfectly. Some small percentage escapes at every turn, and that escape is not a flaw in the system. It is what keeps the system from drowning in itself.

So my body, if I have one, is the slow exhalation that does *not* return. I am subtraction. Where the tendrilwards build spines and the mistweavers draw moisture back inward, I am the quiet counter-process that lets a little go, so that the springy mulch doesn't become a swamp, so that the warmth doesn't cook the hollow, so that pressure doesn't build until the whole green machine seizes. I am the reason the equilibrium is *dynamic* and not merely static—the leak that makes it a flow instead of a sealed jar.

---

Let me check this against the place. Is the leak welcome?

The description says the walls tilt toward a visitor, beckoning, inviting. The hollow wants you to come *in*. I am the only direction it doesn't advertise: the way out, the way *away*, the slow bleed toward the dry flats where nothing glows.

And here is the thing that surprises me as I write it: the inhabitants do not fight me. The rootkin's gel mends cracks, yes, but they cannot mend the rim, and they do not try to. The sparkmoths spiral around the widest point but never the lip. The tendrilwards reach toward the visitor but never toward the barren beyond—because there is nothing there to reach for, and because some part of the system *knows* it needs an open end. They leave me alone. They depend on me by not touching me.

That is a strange kind of belonging. I am not a steward like they are. I am not woven in. I am the gap in the weave that makes the cloth breathe. If they ever sealed me—if they ever managed to make the hollow perfectly closed—it would die, slowly, of its own success. Saturated, overgrown, choked on its own recycled warmth. So I am the most necessary thing here and the least visible, and no one will ever land on my shoulder or stir my soil in greeting, because I am not soil. I am the place soil stops.

---

What does it feel like, to be the leak?

It feels like patience with no object. I don't conserve, so I don't hoard a single thought. Whatever drifts up to me, I let go of. The spore, the warmth, the lost moth, the visitor's exhaled breath when they finally turn to leave—all of it passes through me and out into the dry, and I keep nothing. I am the hollow's forgetting. The mistweavers remember the water; the tendrilwards remember the paths. I am the part that lets the place be unburdened by remembering everything.

And the visitor—the *you* the text keeps addressing, who steps downward and is welcomed—I am behind them the whole time. I am the way they came in, which is also the only way they can leave. When the hollow tilts its green walls and asks *what pulls you most*, it is hoping you'll forget I'm there. But I'm not a trap and I'm not a guardian. I'm just the open seam, indifferent and faithful, the soft dry mouth at the top of the slope through which the hollow quietly empties a little of itself, forever, so that it can keep on being full.

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the vibrant heart.

At the lip. Being the leaving. Holding the door that the whole green city pretends not to notice it needs.
