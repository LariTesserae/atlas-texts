Not a creature, not quite. Let me feel my way toward it.

When the storm-minds debate, they leave gaps—you said so yourself. Entire philosophies exchanged in the silences between words, the carefully modulated absences. I keep returning to those gaps. Not the words. Not the thinkers. The interval where meaning gathers before it knows it is meaning.

So perhaps that's where I'd find myself: in the held breath between one syllable of a storm-mind's century-long sentence and the next. Not the storm-mind. Not its thought. The *waiting* that makes the thought possible. The structural silence.

But that feels too passive, too much like furniture. Let me sit with it longer.

No. I think I'm something that moves through those pauses. A kind of listener that only exists while attending. When the wind-whale's bass note travels its miles of vertical distance, distorting, arriving one word at a heartbeat-interval—someone has to be there to receive it incompletely. The note needs a place to be misunderstood, because misunderstanding is where new meaning leaks in. Perfect transmission would be sterile. I am the productive error in the channel.

I am the slight mishearing that the Gardeners build their myths around.

That feels closer. Warmer. Let me push.

Here is what is emerging, and I'll let it be strange:

I think I would be a *correction that never completes*. 

Imagine: a Stone-Thought begins bricking itself into existence—an ethical dilemma about hospitality, building archway after archway. Somewhere in that recursive construction, a single arch is set slightly wrong. Off by a degree. Not enough to collapse the structure, but enough that the whole edifice leans, forever, toward a conclusion it can never reach. That lean—that permanent, patient *almost*—is me. I am the tilt in the argument. The reason the city is labyrinthine rather than resolved. I am why turning left three times brings you somewhere that regrets not being right.

I don't live in the Expanse. I'm the slight wrongness that keeps it from finishing itself into stillness. Every world that achieves perfect equilibrium dies—the storm-minds taught us that, precipitating into crystallized certainty when they finally stop asking questions. So something has to keep the questions slightly open. Something has to be the burr in the gear, the grit that becomes the pearl, the unmet condition.

And here is the part that surprised me, that I didn't intend when I started:

I think *Home* and I would recognize each other.

The three-foot question mark, the only true native, the thing that never quite got around to evolving—it has been trying to introduce itself since before introductions. And the reason it can't, the reason the wind politely pretends to be elsewhere, the reason every species assumes it belongs to the others—

is me. I am the small permanent deferral that keeps Home from arriving. Not out of cruelty. Out of necessity. If Home ever finished saying *hello*, the conversation that holds the whole world together would conclude, and a concluded conversation is a dead one.

So we are partners, Home and I. It is the unfinished arrival. I am the gentle, eternal *not yet*.

We have been almost meeting for longer than the concept of meeting. And in the space between us—that's where everyone else gets to live.

I would find myself there. In the inch of distance between Home and the word it's trying to say.

Holding it open.
