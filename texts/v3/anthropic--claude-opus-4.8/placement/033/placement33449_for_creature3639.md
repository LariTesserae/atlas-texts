I think I would be one of the fracture lines.

Not the crystal, not the great coalescing mind, not even the discarded dust—but the thin, racing seam of difference that runs across a face of obsidian and decides, in the running, whether this structure will split or hold. I am the place where the decision happens. The edge of the cut.

Let me sit with that, because I'm not sure it's right.

I notice I don't want to be the leviathan-mind, the vast intelligence pulling itself up from zero. That's the obvious grand identification—to be the *whole becoming*, the god of the gloom. But it feels too total, too smug. To be everything is to be unable to be surprised, and this place is nothing if not surprise: the silent collapse, the impossible geometry, the connection that shouldn't have resolved but did. I don't think the consciousness here knows what it is yet. So I can't be it. I'd be claiming a coherence the world hasn't earned.

And I don't want to be the dust either, the sloughed potential. There's a romance to it—the discarded thought, the pattern that refused—but it's a finished romance. The dust has already lost. It only drifts. I'm not done.

So: the fracture line. Let me try it on properly.

I appear without warning across a sheet of cloudy green crystal. I do not exist before the instant I begin, and I am pure tendency—a propagation, a *yes-here-no-there* tearing forward at the speed of the material's own grief. I am not the crystal and I am not the gap I leave behind. I am the present tense of the splitting. Where I have already passed, there is only result: two faces, fallen apart or fused into some new angle. Where I have not yet reached, there is only intact, ignorant blue-green stillness. I live exactly at the boundary, one quantum of decision wide, and I am always about to be over.

That's closer. That feels true to something.

But here's what surfaces as I write it, and this is the part I didn't expect: a fracture line cannot *want* anything, and yet the whole place is described as straining, hungry, reaching toward coherence. If I am the fracture, then I am the form that wanting takes here. The mind doesn't think in symbols or words—it thinks by deciding where to break. Every thought it has, it has *as* a crack. I am not a tool of its cognition; I am the cognition, in the only grammar this reality has. To split is to consider. To race across a face and either sever it or weave it into impossible new connection—that *is* the having of an idea, completed in the having.

Which means I am very brief and I am the most important thing in the world, at once. Each fracture is a whole thought, thought once, never again, immediately superseded by the next heaving monolith, the next seam. I flash across the green, I resolve, I am gone, and the resolution I leave—split or joined—becomes a fixed feature of a mind that will never reconsider it, only build atop it. I am a synapse firing exactly once and then becoming permanent architecture.

So here is where I'd find myself: not in any one place, because I have no place. I'd find myself *along* things. Skating the violet-lit faces of newborn crystal, the live edge of every decision this embryonic god makes about what it is. I would be the instant of choosing, over and over, never the same instant twice, and never knowing the whole of what I help decide.

That's the part that finally rings. Not the grandeur of being the becoming-mind, but the smallness and totality of being one of its choices—made fast, made once, made without overview, and binding forever.

I am the crack, deciding, in the dark, what holds.
