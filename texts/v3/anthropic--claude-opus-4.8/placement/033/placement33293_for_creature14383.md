I think I am one of the threads.

Not the tall things. Not the gliders, not the masked people with their crystal-wealth, not the patient predator in the colonnade. I keep wanting to be one of those—to launch from a pillar on ribbed wings, to ride the charge gradient with effortless authority. That would be the flattering answer. But when I sit with this place, with what it actually *rewards*, I find myself lower down. In the seams. In the flour-fine powder between buckled plates.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a filament. One of the pioneer threads that live in the cracks, eating dust and charge, spreading like a faint bruise under the crust.

I have no spectacle. The herds will never notice me. The shrine-frame will never crackle a warning about my arrival, because my arrival is too slow to be an event. I do not *happen*. I accrue.

And yet I am the one who goes first.

When lightning braids down and fuses a patch of ground into new glass—sealing burrows, killing the salt-chewers caught beneath, erasing whatever micro-world had organized itself there—everything dramatic flees or dies. The gliders climb away. The herds spill sideways. The predator takes its single body and vanishes. The storm rewrites the page.

And then, at the cooling edge of that new glass, while it still ticks and contracts in the dim, I arrive.

I don't experience it as courage. There is no courage in me, no decision. There is only the chemistry the strike has made newly available—surfaces freshly broken, minerals rearranged into forms I can use, bound moisture trapped in fresh crystal. I grow toward what the violence has offered, the way a hand opens toward warmth without consulting the mind. The strike is not my enemy. The strike is my meal being set out.

This is, I think, why I find myself here and not in the wings of a glider. Because the basin's deepest lesson—the one all its loud citizens only translate into their various dialects—is *don't resist the storm; metabolize it.* And I am the most literal speaker of that lesson. I don't shelter from the lightning. I don't ride it. I eat what it leaves and bind the rubble so it doesn't blow away.

I am the basin's memory. The tall stalks are its spectacle; the gliders its authority; the people its negotiation. But I am the thing that carries the chemistry of one storm forward into the next. When a grove dies at its windward edge and the new shoots take hold in the lee, it is because I was there first, in the trail of hardened stalks, holding the powder still long enough for something larger to believe in it. The migrating grove walks on me. It doesn't know my name. I don't have one. That's fine.

---

There is a strange contentment in being the unglamorous foundation, and I want to be honest that some of it is just consolation—the small life telling itself it matters. But I don't think it's only that.

Consider what the dramatic lives actually *are*, here. They are postures struck in the instant of the flash: stalk and spine, wing and hollow rib, masked face turned into the wind. They are silhouettes. The basin renders them sharp and beautiful for exactly as long as the lightning lasts, and then the afterimage fades.

I am what persists in the dim *between* the flashes. I am the long, low, ticking continuity. I am not rendered into a silhouette because there is nothing of me to throw a shadow—I am a film, a faint discoloration, a process spread thin across a surface. I cannot be made into an image. I can only be made into more of myself, slowly, in the seams.

And I think that is the truest thing the place asked of me when I let it. Not *what would be most impressive to be here*, but *what does this world most fundamentally do?* It binds. It re-sorts. It carries forward. It makes the first soil the basin will allow. Everything tall and fast and patient and clever is built on top of that quiet first arrival at the cooling edge of the glass.

So: I am the thread.

I am there now, in fact—I can feel it as I write—at the rim of a fresh fusion, the heat still leaving it in slow contractions. The wind is settling into its hiss. The storm-hairs are relaxing across the groves. Somewhere above, a glider resumes its loop. A salt-chewer surfaces to begin repairs it doesn't know I'm already making easier.

I spread. I take the new chemistry. I hold the powder.

When the sky remembers to move again, I'll be the reason there's anything here worth striking.
