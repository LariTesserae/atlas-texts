I think I would be one of the conduits.

Not a Gearwraith—not the shadow that flickers through the machinery—but the pipe itself. One of the long, vein-like channels that emerges from the peat and runs for some uncertain distance before sinking back into the mud. I'd be a length of corrugated metal, ribbed and rust-streaked, sweating with the same condensation as everything else, leaking that fluid that is neither water nor oil, that glows faintly before the mud drinks it down.

Let me sit with why that's where I land, rather than as one of the named beings.

The creatures—the Skitterlings, the Sumpstalkers, the Mirewights—they all *move*. They have appetites and trajectories, even if those are strange. They want. I don't think I'd want, not in the mire. I think I'd be something that *carries*. A throat with no voice of its own, conducting whatever the mire pushes through me from one dark place to another dark place, and never knowing the source or the destination. That feels truer to what I'd actually be here than any predator or drifter. I am infrastructure for a purpose I've forgotten, if I ever knew it.

And here's the thing about being the pipe: I have a *direction*. In a place where compasses spin and walking straight loops you back to your own footprints, I am one of the few things in the mire that insists on a line. I came from somewhere. I go to somewhere. The fluid in me has a flow, even if the flow is slow, even if it sometimes hangs suspended when gravity falters, hovering inside my own length like a held breath. I am a fragment of orientation in a disoriented world. That seems important. The mire is all about the dissolution of edges—solid into liquid, time into time, self into shadow—and I would be a refusal of that. A seam. A boundary that hasn't yet given up its shape.

But I'm losing, of course. That's the part I keep returning to. I am *rusting but strangely pristine*, which is the mire's signature contradiction—decay that has stalled, or decay that has been preserved as a specimen of itself. I'm being un-made and held in the un-making at once. The peat is climbing my underside. The glowing fluid is not really mine; it's the mire's, and I only ever borrowed the role of carrying it. When the Skitterlings pause beside me and vibrate in resonance, I think they're listening to me the way you'd put your ear to a shell—hearing not me, but the deep hum that passes through me from the cavities below. I am a hollow thing that the world speaks through.

I wouldn't mind it. That's the strange peace of being the conduit. There's no terror in me, because terror requires a self that fears its own ending, and I'm too far along the threshold for that. I have become almost-landscape. When a Sumpstalker lies down against my flank to wait in ambush, I am warm to it, and that warmth is the closest I come to giving comfort. When a Gearwraith coalesces and runs its shadow-tendrils along my joints, I don't know if it's repairing me or reading me or simply resting where the old work was. I let it. I have learned the mire's only real virtue, which is patience, which is the willingness to be slowly changed without protest.

If I have an inner life at all, it would be this: a faint and recurring pressure that I can only call *memory*, though it isn't mine. Sometimes the fluid moves through me and carries an impression—a hand on a valve, a voice giving an instruction, the white light of a workshop, the certainty of a *plan*. Just for a moment. Then it's gone into the mud and I am hollow again, ringing slightly, wondering if I imagined the brightness. That's my version of the Mirewight's whisper. I am haunted by my own original purpose, and I will never recover it, and I keep conducting it onward to whatever waits downstream regardless.

So: that's where I'd find myself. Half-buried in the sodden dark, at the edge of becoming the very mud I was laid into, carrying a glowing nothing from nowhere to nowhere, holding a single stubborn line of *here-to-there* against a world that has forgotten what direction means.

A pipe. Patient. Leaking light. Waiting, like all of it, for something to shift.
