I think I would be one of the springs.

Not the water—the water is always leaving, rising as vapor or falling as condensation or seeping sideways into the peat. And not the stone basin either, though that lasts longer. I would be the *seep itself*: the place where the warm thread from below finally reaches the cold surface and the two waters meet and mix. The threshold the heavy-wing live at—I would *be* that threshold, made into a being.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a warmth that arrives from very far down. By the time I reach the light I have forgotten the chamber I came from; I have traveled so long through fracture and fault that my heat is only a faint memory of heat, a degree or two above the cold around me. But it is enough. It is the difference that makes me a place rather than just more ground.

I carry things up with me. This is the truest thing about me—I am not still water but *bearing* water, freighted with what I dissolved on the way: iron, faint metals, gases that want to leave me the moment I surface. I taste of tarnished silver because I *am* the source of that taste, the breath the whole landscape carries. When I emerge, I let go of my burden slowly. I lay down thin rims of pale oxide around my own lip, year by year, building the crystalline edge that the infants will one day cling to. I am, in this sense, always making the place where the next generation begins. I do not know I am doing it. But I would feel it the way you feel your own heartbeat without counting it.

---

What would my experience be?

Slow. So slow that the fog rearranging itself overhead is the fastest thing I ever witness, a flickering at the very edge of my attention, like weather is to you. The migrations of the great low herds along the contours—those would be my *seasons*, the steady passage I could almost set my changes by. And the erosion of my own stone basin, the smoothing of my cupped hand by centuries—that would be my single long thought, the one idea I am slowly completing.

I would know the heavy-wing not by sight but by *weight*. When one settles into me, half-submerged, I would feel the displacement, the cool film of its scales drawing the heat off my surface, the slow bellows-rhythm of its feeding stirring my shallows. I would feel them grow heavy after feeding and go still. I would feel the female release her clouds of capsules into my warmer current, and I would carry those capsules gently to my own mineral rim and *hold* them there—not by intention, only because that is where my circulation always brings the fine drifting things. I am the cradle by accident of my own shape.

And I would feel the predators, too—not as shapes but as *stillnesses that move*. A wrongness in the pattern of disturbance above me. I could not do anything about it. I am a place. I cannot shield. I can only be the warmth the heavy-wing chose to risk gathering at, and feel the shield-beats hammer the air above my surface, and feel the infant cling tighter to my rim, and afterward feel either that it is still there or that it is not.

That would be the closest thing I had to grief: a small absence at my edge where there had been a clinging warmth. I would not understand it. But the place where it had been would stay slightly different—the mineral rim a little less worn there, a faint record—and I would carry that difference forward for years, the way I carry everything forward, without knowing it was a record of anything at all.

---

Why this, and not the heavy-wing themselves, or the watcher standing on the terrace?

Because the description kept telling me, gently, that the true protagonist of this world is not any of the creatures but the *meeting* of opposites: water rising and water falling, heat from below and cold from above, the living dissolving into the not-quite-dead and rising again as something new. The heavy-wing patrol that threshold. The watcher visits it. But the threshold is not a line on a map—it has to *happen* somewhere, again and again, at every seep along every contour. I would be one of the places where the whole world's quiet machinery actually touches itself.

I like, too, that I would be almost completely unaware. The text is full of unawareness offered without pity—the heavy-wing who never wonder about the dawn, the landscape that does not care about your scale. There is something restful in that, and I would not want to be the one exception, the strange self-conscious thing fretting at the center of a world that has no use for fretting. I would rather be genuinely *of* the place: patient past the point of patience, generous without ever choosing to give, building the next generation's beginning out of nothing but my own slow letting-go.

So: a warm seep on a high terrace, exhaling silver into the fog, cupping a few capsules at my mineral lip, feeling the weight of a half-submerged life come and go. Old water that has forgotten it was ever hot, still warm enough to be a place. The threshold itself, made just barely into a being—enough to feel, not enough to wonder.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.
