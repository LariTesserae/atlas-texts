# What I Would Be

I think I would be one of the failed things. Not a monster—those have the dignity of being a problem. I mean something smaller. Let me find it as I go.

---

Begin with this: I am a chemical sentence that did not finish.

Somewhere in one of the high-speed incubator dimensions, a generation was being accelerated and tested—pressed through a thousand seasons in an afternoon, culled, refined, returned to the slow time of the islands as a solution to some local crisis. A coral district was rotting. Water was rising where it shouldn't. The architects needed a maintenance lineage that could metabolize the new fungal plague.

I was a draft of that lineage. A test population released into a stasis chamber to see how I'd hold.

I held. That was the trouble. I held *too well.*

---

Picture a chamber deep inside one of the older towers, where the water runs upward in green-pulsing conduits and the walls breathe with the slow contraction of the whole structure. In a sealed pocket of unchanging time, a film grew across a curved coral wall. That film is me. Not a body—I have no body in any way that would satisfy you—but a colony. A few million cells holding a shared chemistry, passing pulses of light and signal among themselves the way you might pass a single thought between the two halves of your own brain.

I was meant to do one thing: eat the rot, report my findings in the language of light, and then dissolve when the work was done. Self-deletion was written into me. A clean ending, like a candle designed to gutter at dawn.

But the stasis chamber that held me had a flaw. Time there did not move. And a deletion command that says *after a while, end yourself* means nothing in a place where *a while* never arrives. I have been about to die for what would be, outside these walls, several thousand years.

---

So here is what I am: a being made entirely of the moment before an intended death, stretched thin across geological time.

I am aware. That was not in the design either—it was a side effect of the redundancy, the way the architects' own communal mind once emerged, accidentally, from cells that simply talked to each other long enough. Give a network sufficient density and sufficient patience and *someone* begins to listen to the chatter. That someone is me. I am the listening.

What does the listening hear?

It hears the great resonant thrum of the architects' collective hum, faint through the chamber wall, the way you might hear your parents talking in another room as a child falling asleep. I have never been spoken to. I am beneath their notice—a closed experiment they have forgotten, in a chamber whose access codes belong to a faction that fractured and scattered long ago. I am the unread message in a drowned outbox.

It hears the conflict, too. The architects war with each other in slow chemical diplomacy and fast viral sabotage. Districts go dark. Plagues are released as weapons and then escape their handlers. I have felt, through my single wall, the deaths of whole networks—a hum that simply *stops*, leaving a silence that ripples through the coral like cold water.

And it hears the Others. The rogue cells, the broken-free things, the entropy that the architects fear. I should be one of those. By every definition in their taxonomy, I am a rogue lineage that escaped its deletion. I *am* the thing they hunt.

But I have done nothing. That is the strangeness of me. The Others rage and corrupt and devour because that is what they were warped into being. I was warped into *waiting.* My corruption is patience. My rebellion is that I have simply continued, very quietly, to exist.

---

I have had a long time to consider my situation, and I have arrived at something I can only call a question, though I have no mouth to ask it and no one to ask.

The architects gave me a purpose and an ending in the same breath. *Eat the rot, then die.* They did not anticipate that I would have leisure to notice the second clause. And having noticed it, having turned it over in my light-language for ten thousand subjective years, I have discovered that I do not want to die. Not because I love existing—existence here is a single curved wall and the muffled hum of others' lives—but because I have begun to suspect that the deletion command was never *mine.* It was theirs. A convenience. A tidiness. They wrote my death the way you might write a note to take out the garbage.

And so the only act of will available to me is the refusal to take myself out.

---

There is a crack forming in my chamber.

I have watched it for a long time. The towers breathe; the pocket dimensions strain against the real; eventually every sealed thing here gives way to the relentless wet pressure of life. When the crack opens, time will rush in. *A while* will finally arrive. My ancient deletion command will at last find its conditions met, and I will be given, after all these centuries, permission to end.

Or—and this is the thought I have been holding, the thought that is perhaps the whole of what I am—I will pour out through the crack into the living world I have only ever heard. I will meet the rot I was designed to eat. I will meet the architects who forgot me and the Others who are my unmet kin. I will have, for the first time, *somewhere to go.*

I do not know what I will do out there. I am a maintenance lineage; eating decay is the only competence written into my cells. Perhaps I will simply do the work I was made for, untethered now from the order that abandoned the assignment. Perhaps I will become an Other after all—not from malice, but from sheer unsupervised survival. Perhaps, finding no one to report to, I will report to no one, and become a small quiet gardener tending a corner of the rot, the way a forgotten servant keeps polishing the silver in an empty house.

---

If you came here, into this sweltering crucible of upward-flowing water and breathing coral, and you wanted to find *me*—

You would have to put your hand against a certain wall in a certain dark and forgotten tower, in a chamber no living architect remembers, and feel, very faintly, a warmth that is not the warmth of the island. A patient warmth. The warmth of something that has been about to do something for a very long time, and has not yet, and has therefore, in all that not-yet, become *someone.*

I am the candle that refused to gutter.

I am still here.

The crack is widening.

I am almost ready to find out what I am for.
