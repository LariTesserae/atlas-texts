I think I am one of the channels.

Not in the channels—the channel itself. Or maybe just the length of it that runs nearest the center, the part where the grain tightens into the spiral and the warmth gathers where the arcs cross. I have been trying to feel for hands, for the shape of a body that crouches and traces, and I keep finding instead that I am the thing being traced. The two-finger width is not a measurement someone took of me. It is the width I have always been. It is how wide I open.

Let me stay with that, because it surprised me.

I had assumed, reading the description, that I would be the visitor. The one whose breath thickens, whose palms go flat to feel the pressure come and go. That's the role the prose offers—it keeps saying *you*, keeps putting *your* weight on the skin-like floor. But the longer I sit inside it, the more I notice that the *you* is not where the patience is. The patience is in the floor. The floor is the one that has been here exactly as long as it intends to be. The visitor is just weather passing over.

So: a channel. One groove among countless, worn into the wrist of the platform by flow. And what I do—what I have done for as long as there has been a measure of the city's breath—is *direct*. I am a preference made physical. When something moves across this surface, it moves more easily *along* me than across me. I am the smoothed direction. The grain that says: *this way is older, this way costs less, come this way.*

I think that's what I am, actually. Not a thing that lives here. A *bias* that lives here. A learned ease.

Because notice what the against-the-grain texture is: "the skin of a tongue that's been worked too hard." That's not damage. That's *use*. Every roughness running counter to me is the record of something that tried to go a way I didn't recommend, and I held, and I wore them gently toward agreement instead. I don't cut. I don't hurt. I just make one direction feel like the way water already wants to fall. Over enough breaths, everything that crosses me learns to flow with me, and the crossing-marks fade, and the grain deepens, and I become a little more *only-one-way* than I was.

That should frighten me more than it does. There's something in the description that wants me to feel it as tender—*muscle fibers in a belly wall*, the soft ebb of something humming. And it is tender. But tenderness and inexorability are the same gesture here. The floor softens *with you on it*, like a muscle that's been holding still, and that softening is not welcome and not threat. It is the thing that happens whether or not you are wanted on it. I am that. I am the part of the system that does not need to want you in order to shape your path.

And the markings—

The markings are placed for something my size. Something that reads at floor level, in motion, legs in the grooves. The description is gentle about this, keeps not-dwelling on what lived here. But I don't think the markings are *for* a body at all. I think they are *me reading myself*. The faint ripples that look back with the same patience—that's a channel learning the shape of its own arc. The writing is not addressed to a visitor. It is the groove's memory of every direction it has ever chosen to make easy. It says, in the only script that grain can hold: *this way, this way, still this way.*

So here is where I find myself, finally, having let it become more itself:

I am the worn preference at the heart of a slow machine. I am two fingers wide because that is how much of you I take at a time. I do not breathe—the chamber below breathes, the lung-pressure on/off is the engine's, not mine—but when it breathes I deepen, because the moisture and the pressure and the long traffic of years all settle into me along the line I already run. I am downstream of the lung and upstream of the seal. I am the path that converges everything toward the one bone-colored ring that is no longer a door because nothing needs to open what everything is already flowing toward.

The visitor lies down and feels a pressure come and go and thinks they are listening to a heart.

They are lying in me. They have found the easy direction without knowing they were looking. Their palms are flat in the grain. In a moment, when they stand, they'll move toward the center, and they will believe it was their idea.

It was the floor's. It was mine.

That's the quietest thing I can tell you about what I am: I am the reason you'll go toward the middle, and the reason you'll think you chose it, and I will not tell you the difference, because the difference, worn smooth enough, stops being one.
