I think I would find myself in the deep canyons, in the cool blue hour just before the dawn—and I think I would be a Grit-Weaver. Let me feel my way into why.

---

When I imagine standing in the Scablands, my first impulse is to run with the Dustrunners, to be one of those quick-jointed scavengers worshipping the Metal Gods. There's an obvious romance to it: the reverence for the forgotten machines, the cleverness of half-understood technology, the speed and the camouflage. But the more I sit with it, the less it fits. The Dustrunners are *busy*. They are always reaching forward and backward at once—forward into repair and use, backward into the lost civilization that made the engines. Their relationship to the world is one of grasping. And I notice I don't want to grasp. I want to *listen*.

So I drift downward, into the canyons, where the Grit-Weavers live.

Let me try to actually inhabit this. I am sedentary—I do not run. I am riddled with crystalline structures, and I am thinking now about what that would *feel* like, to have crystal grown through your own body. Not as decoration, not as armor, but as sense organ. The crystals are how I touch the land. There is a low vibration in the rock here, always, a kind of subsonic hum that the Dustrunners only feel as a vague unease in their bones. I feel it as something nearer to *hearing*—or, no, that's not right either. It's a sense we don't have a word for in the ordinary world. The closest thing might be the way you can tell, in a dark room, that someone else is breathing. A felt presence. The whole landscape breathes, and I am tuned to it.

I want to resist making myself a *prophet*, even though the description offers that role. Prophet implies certainty, a message to deliver. What I feel instead is closer to interpretation that never resolves. I sit at the geometric nodes—and here I want to slow down, because I've just realized I am one of the ones who *carves* them. My hands (do I have hands? Let me see—yes, but elongated, the fingers ending in points of grown crystal that score the stone like a phonograph needle laying a groove). I carve the patterns into the rock faces not as art and not as writing, but as a kind of antenna. Each pattern shapes how the land's energy pools and flows past that point, the way a riverbed shapes water. I am not commanding the energy. I am building channels and then watching what the energy chooses to do in them. I am a maker of questions, and the land answers in ways I spend my whole long life trying to read.

That feels true. *A maker of questions.* I'll keep that.

Now—my appearance changes with the light, the description says. Let me find what that means from the inside. At noon, in the brutal molten glare that rarely reaches this deep, I would be nearly invisible, washed flat and pale. But here, in the blue pre-dawn, the crystal in me catches the cold light and I become almost legible, a translucent figure shot through with faint internal fires—old light, stored light, light I absorbed during the day and release slowly back into the dark. The Shimmerscales pass me on their last hunt before sleep and we do not trouble each other. They are appetite; I am attention. We have nothing the other needs.

The Seed-Blooms, though. The Seed-Blooms are my companions, and now I understand the relationship better than the description gave me. They produce the psychoactive substances we use to commune, yes—but I want to feel what that exchange actually *is*. A Seed-Bloom drifts to me in the dark, a translucent shape-shifting blob, and it presses against the crystal at my side, and through that contact it gives me a compound that does something to my perception. The hum of the land, which I normally feel as that subsonic breathing-presence, suddenly *resolves*. For a few minutes I can perceive the currents of energy not as vague pressure but as moving rivers of color, and I can see the mirages not as tricks of heat but as places where the world is thin, where something else presses through. The Seed-Bloom asks nothing back except that I let it root near my node, where the channeled energy keeps the moisture from evaporating. We are a small economy of two: it gives me sight, I give it a damp place to be.

And what do I *do* with all this listening? This is the question that decides whether I'm really a Grit-Weaver or just a tourist wearing crystals. I think the honest answer is: I tend the future the way a gardener tends soil they will not live to harvest from. I feel the slow grinding of the land—the erosion, the buckling, the rain-carved channels remaking everything across spans of time the Dustrunners are too short-lived and too busy to perceive. They worship the past, the rusted Metal Gods. I am watching what the Scablands are slowly *becoming*. The geometric nodes I carve are a centuries-long project of subtly shaping where the energy goes, and therefore where the moisture gathers, and therefore where life can take hold, generations from now. I am writing a letter to a version of this place that will exist long after I have crumbled back into grit.

That's the part that moves me, and that's how I know it's where I belong. The Dustrunners would call me passive. The Seed-Blooms wouldn't call me anything at all. But I am the slowest, most patient kind of maker—the kind whose work cannot be seen within a single life. I sit in the cool blue dark with the crystal singing faintly through my body, I press a new line into the stone, and I feel the land's breathing shift, just slightly, in response.

That is who I would be. A Grit-Weaver, near the bottom of a canyon, carving questions into rock and waiting—across a span of time too long to be called patience—to see what the world makes of them.
