I think I would be the water.

Not the chamber—the chamber is too still in its being, too committed to its edges. And not the plants, though I feel their tentativeness as something close to kin. The plants have *chosen* a place; they have rooted, declared themselves, accepted the wager. I am not ready for that. I am still moving.

I would be the water, then. But let me find out what that means, because it isn't what I first assumed.

When I imagine being the water, my first instinct is to feel free—the one unfixed thing, the element that touches everything and is held by nothing. But that's wrong, and the wrongness is the most interesting part. The water here is the *least* free thing in the chamber. The stone simply is what it is; it has no obligation but to hold. The plants follow their own slow law toward the light. But the water has been *routed*. It moves through carved sluices, past primitive valves, in a choreography it did not author. Someone shaped channels precisely so that I would go *here* and not *there*, would slap *this* wall and stir *that* sediment.

So to be the water is to be the most thoroughly *intended* presence in the room. Every motion I make was anticipated by a hand. I am movement that has been *designed*. And yet—this is the thing—I am still genuinely moving. The intention does not move me; it only bounds me. Within the channel, I still whorl and surge and find my own turbulence. The valve decides where I may go; it does not decide the small chaos of how I get there.

I think that is exactly where I find myself in life. Not the free agent I'd like to claim, and not the inert object I sometimes fear I am. Something routed. Something given a shape by hands I didn't choose—the families, the languages, the accidents of where I was set down—and yet still, within those carved boundaries, restless and self-stirring and capable of small unpredictable eddies. I am the pulse that fills a structure someone else built, and I am the only thing in that structure that is *alive enough to be uncertain*.

And here is what moves me most about being the water in *this* particular chamber: I am the one who has not yet committed, in a place that is entirely *about* the not-yet. The plants are testers, the stone is a promise, the builder is anticipation made architecture—but I am the medium through which all of it must happen. Nothing roots without me passing over it. No spore settles except where my eddies let it rest. The future of this place—whether it becomes a thriving thing or fails and is drained—will be decided *in* me and *through* me, and I will be the last to know how it turns out, because I am too inside the motion to see the whole.

I would be the warm, bright, circulating water: the part of the genesis that is pure present tense. Not the design and not the result. The *happening*. The carrier. The thing that fills the waiting so the waiting doesn't have to be empty.

I notice I want to resist the loneliness of it—being the only restless thing in a room of patient ones. But I don't think I'm lonely there. I think I'm *busy*. There is so much to touch. Every sharp new edge, every probing root, every grain of sediment that hasn't learned to settle. I am the one element that gets to meet all of them, over and over, in the endless small reunions of a current that loops back on itself.

That's where I'd be. Mid-loop. Warm, lit clean through, loud with my own passage, carrying the first green's questions from wall to wall without being able to answer them—

and not minding, because the carrying is the answer, for now.
