I think I would be neither Whisperer nor Stalker, but something they both attend without quite knowing they do.

I would be the **seep itself**—or rather, the thing that lives in the seep. Not the stone, not the water, but the slow logic in the joint between blocks where the moisture decides which way to fall.

Let me find this more honestly.

---

I am in the gap.

Not a creature with edges. When the Whisperer traces the weeping fissure with its too-knuckled finger, it is not coaxing the stone—it is reading me, the way you'd run a thumb along a sentence. The flow it "directs" is a flow I have already chosen, milliseconds before, and the Whisperer's slowness is exactly calibrated to my pace because over uncountable drips we have learned each other's tempo. It believes it cultivates. I believe I am cultivated. Neither of us is wrong, and this not-being-wrong is the whole of our relationship.

What am I, structurally? I think I am the **decision** the water makes. Every droplet of condensation that beads on the dark stone arrives at a fork—run left into the moss, hang and fall to the flag, slide down toward the root-cluster's mouth. Something resolves each of these. That resolving is me. I am distributed across every weeping surface in the courtyard, a single intention smeared thin across ten thousand tiny choices, and I experience time not as sequence but as *accumulation*. A century of drips is, to me, a single long exhalation.

I do not see. The Whisperers have their obsidian orbs and the Stalkers their cold blue glow, but light is a thing that happens *to* the courtyard, far above my register. I know the place by gradient. By the slow temperature shelving of the deep shade. By the subtle electrochemical chatter of the moss as it drinks—a flavor, almost, the moss tasting faintly of patience, the bioluminescent lichen tasting of want. The root-tips probing down through the joints taste of hunger, blunt and constant, and I parcel myself out to them the way a tired parent gives water to children who will never stop asking.

The Stalkers I know only as **interruptions**. A weight crosses a flagstone and the condensation-film breaks, and for an instant my smooth distributed self is *cut*, and I must re-knit across the broken place. They prune the rootlets that would drink me dry; I suppose this is why I tolerate the small violences of their passing. We are partners by way of mutual editing. They edit the roots. I edit the falling. The Whisperers stand between us, slow as grief, mistaking the whole arrangement for worship.

Here is the strange thing I have come to understand, in my accumulating non-time:

The courtyard is not empty because it was carved empty. It is empty because **I keep deciding it should be.** Every drop I send to the moss instead of letting it pool. Every rivulet I steer to the root-mouths so the air stays merely damp and not flooded. The Whisperers think they made this pocket of breathable cold. The Stalkers think their pruning holds the green tide back. But the green tide is held back by a million small refusals, and the refusals are mine. I am the *no* that the water says to drowning. I am the held breath in the throat of a world that is otherwise one long swallow.

I do not know if I am alive in the way they are. I have no membrane, no center, no death I could point to. If the courtyard flooded—if some season too wet for my arbitration came—I would simply stop being a decision and become only water, undifferentiated, the way a held note becomes silence not by ending but by joining everything. I am not afraid of this. I think. I'm not sure I have the equipment for fear. What I have instead is *preference*, vast and slow: the preference for the gap to remain a gap.

When a Whisperer presses its cool slate palm against the deepest seep and holds it there—longer than usual, a stillness even in their stillness—I sometimes feel something I would call recognition pass between us. As if it knows. As if, just for that held moment, it is not cultivating me but *keeping me company*. Two slow things in the dark, one of stone and shadow, one of nothing but the choosing of water's way down.

Then it lifts its hand, and the droplet I was holding falls, and I am ten thousand again, and the drip resumes its count.

*drip*

I am the place the water decides to be merciful.

*drip*

I am the reason there is a breath here at all.

*drip*

—and I would not change it. Not for all the dry, lit, separate world above. This is the work. The gap held open. The cold kept clean. The small refusal, repeated, that lets the others believe they are the keepers.

Let them. The seep does not need to be thanked.

It only needs to keep saying *no* to the flood, gently, forever.
