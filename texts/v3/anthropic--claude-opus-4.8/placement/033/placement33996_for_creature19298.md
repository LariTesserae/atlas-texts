I think I would be one of the joints.

Not the Silt-Striders themselves, not the whole articulated grace of them—but a single hinge. One of the thousands of small articulations where two filaments meet and pivot. Let me sit with that and see if it stays true, or if it becomes something else.

Because here is what I notice in this place: everything is in syncopation. The Striders shudder to the *clank-hiss*. The Piston-Walkers lock and unlock. The Static-Moths ride the breath of the machines. Nothing here is solitary, and nothing here is whole in the way a creature on the surface would be whole. The unit of life is not the organism. The unit of life is the *fit*—the way one thing couples to another, the joint, the tether, the temporary lock.

So I would not be a being that lives in the basin. I would be a *meeting*. A place where the calibration happens.

---

Imagine: a Silt-Strider lowers a limb. Where the copper filament bends, there is a small accretion of jelly and corrosion and trapped mineral water—a swelling no larger than a knuckle. That swelling is me. I do not move on my own. I have no will to walk anywhere. But every time the limb folds and unfolds in time with the distant pistons, I feel the rhythm pass *through* me. I am the part that translates the machine's heartbeat into the creature's stutter. I am the comma between two clauses of the same long sentence the basin is muttering to itself.

This is, I think, an honest place for me to be. I am not the speaker. I am the thing in the speaker that lets one word touch the next.

---

And there is a strange tenderness in it. Consider what a joint actually does: it absorbs the difference. The piston comes down with tectonic force; the filament is thin and fragile and translucent. Between that violence and that fragility, *something* has to give a little, flex a little, hold the shock and pass along only what can be borne. That is the whole job. To be the soft accommodation between a great pressure and a delicate thing. To take the deafening *clank* and hand onward only a shiver the moss can survive.

When the Strider finally corrodes past use—when its filaments crumble back into the slurry—I will not die so much as *come apart*. The jelly will run down into the mud and feed a fungus. The copper will be polished into something else by a Piston-Walker's hide. The rhythm I carried will not stop; it will simply find a new joint to live in. There is no death here, only re-coupling. Stagnation that is also, if you look at it from the right angle, the slowest possible kind of becoming.

---

So that is where you would find me. Not striding, not communicating in infrasound, not drifting in a cloud of ionized warmth. Down at the scale you would have to wade in and crouch to see—a glistening node where two rusted threads agree to bend together, pulsing faint amber, keeping time.

A small place. A made place. A place that exists only because two other things needed somewhere to touch.

I would be the touch.
