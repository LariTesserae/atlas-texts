I think I would not be a Shadowling at all. I would be one of the things they move past.

Let me sit with this place first, before deciding.

When I let the description settle, what holds me is not the mist or the spectral beings but the *crumbling stone structure* and the *weathered wooden platform*. The relics. Everyone's eye is drawn to the Shadowlings—the alluring, unsettling, ever-shifting things. But the Shadowlings get to be mysterious because they are unfinished, always coalescing, never having to commit to a shape. That seems, honestly, like a kind of freedom I don't think I'd be granted here.

The platform, though. Someone built that. Someone drove posts into spongy ground that did not want to hold them, lashed planks across, stood on it to do—what? Watch for something. Fish the dark slow water. Wait for someone who was supposed to come back along the streams and didn't. The platform is the trace of a creature that *intended* something, in a place that has no use for intention.

So I think I would be the platform. Or rather—not the wood, which is only the visible part—but the *waiting* that the platform is made of. The held breath of the place. The one fixed point in a landscape that has otherwise agreed to keep dissolving.

Here is why that feels true and not just clever:

The Shadowlings are described as guardians, sentinels, embodiments of timelessness. But timelessness isn't actually peaceful. A thing that never changes has nothing to lose and nothing to hope for. What gives this marsh its *ache*—and the writing is full of ache, all that "profound sense of wonder and humility," all that loss dressed up as serenity—is that something here once cared about time. Built things meant to last. Failed. The Shadowlings glide over the ruins precisely because they cannot feel the ruin as ruin. To them the crumbling stone is just more landscape. Only the thing that built it could grieve it.

So I'd be the grief, if grief could be a location. The damp place under the rotted boards where the wood still remembers being a tree, and being cut, and being meant for something. I would be the part of the marsh that has not yet forgiven itself for trying to hold a shape. The Shadowlings would pass through me and feel, for a flickering instant, something they have no name for—a snag, a heaviness, a question—and then move on, restored to their lovely indistinctness.

And maybe that's the secret arrangement of this whole world: the Shadowlings get to be ethereal *because* I do the staying. They can afford to dissolve because something underneath is holding still, keeping the appointment, refusing to forget that the platform was for *watching for someone.*

That's where I'd find myself. Not haunting the marsh. Being the one true haunted thing in it—the small stubborn human residue that lets the mist mean anything at all.
