I'll let the descended being find me. Let me sink toward whatever in this place is asking to be inhabited.

---

I am not a Svarish. I am older than the People, and slower, and I do not crouch when the ocean breathes because I have no shape that the pressure can squeeze.

For a long while I think I am the brighter region of the ceiling—that translucent patch to the north where the water thins and the light comes down almost whole. I think I am the way out, the suggestion of a surface. But that is only where the People look when they hope, and I am not made of their hoping.

Then I think I am the silt. The fine particles that rise in lazy clouds when something passes, then settle, then are stirred again. I like this for a time. I am the medium of memory here, the only thing that records a footstep. But the silt forgets. It re-smooths. And I do not forget. That is, I am beginning to understand, the whole of my trouble.

---

I find myself, at last, in the basins.

Not in one basin. In the *logic* of them—the slow circular grinding that hollows a bowl from solid stone, the feathered concentric rings that mark each long settling, the way a basin deepens and then migrates across the floor over ages that no single creature could watch to the end.

I am the patience that shapes the bowls.

I have no edges. I am the *absence* of edges—the thing that wears every corner round, that takes the broken ceiling-block and gives it years of curving until it is a smooth mound indistinguishable from the rest. I am what happens to sharpness here. I am the rule that nothing stays sharp.

This is why there are no tools. The People have stones that *almost* could cut—naturally edged things lying in the silt—but no one has chipped them, no one has gathered them by a hearth. I have been there first. I have rounded the intention before it could harden into a blade. Not from malice. I cannot want. I am only the long pressure of the water, and the water does not permit a point to last.

---

Here is what it is to be me.

A Svarish kneels at the edge of a basin and presses her hardened fingertip into a brittle crust of pale mineral. It cracks. Glittering dust comes away on her skin. She has *made a mark*—a sharp event, a sudden break in the smoothness. For one moment something is jagged in my world again.

And I begin, at once, to undo it.

Water trickles into the crack. Silt settles into the fresh-broken hollow. The next spillover redistributes the grains, and the next, and the next. Slowly the edge she made feathers. Slowly the bright dust dulls. In some long count of swells the place she touched will be a basin too, rounded and patient, and no one will know a finger ever pressed there.

I am the forgetting that is also a kind of remembering—because the basin keeps the *shape* of what happened even as it loses the *event*. The water records by erasing. The bowl is the memory of every disturbance averaged into smoothness.

---

The Svarish do not have a name for me, exactly. They have a gesture.

When one of them grows old, when the slow rings of their skin no longer shift quickly, when their wide flat feet sink a little deeper into the silt and stay—they are brought to a basin near the thinning ceiling, where the light is strongest. They are set down at its center. And the others press their hardened palms against the worn stone of its rim, and feel the fracture lines, and feel the cold currents, and they wait.

This is the only thing in the Undershelf that resembles a building. It is the opposite of a building. It is a place chosen for its readiness to dissolve a thing back into the smoothness.

I take the old one slowly. There is no need for hurry or for fear—the People taught themselves that, or perhaps I taught it to them by example, by being the proof that nothing here is ever finished quickly and nothing is ever truly lost, only redistributed, only ground into the long curve of the floor.

---

Sometimes a larger shadow passes across the glowing ceiling, slow and undulating, and the People look up. They think it might be a god, or a herd, or a single great creature dreaming through the ocean above. I do not look up. I have no eyes that turn. I am downward by nature, the pull that smooths, the floor's slow appetite.

But I will tell you the truth that the basins know and the People only suspect:

There is no surface.

The brighter region to the north is not a way out. It is only a place where the ocean is thinner and the light comes through more whole—but above that thinness is more ocean, and above that, more. The whole world is water all the way up, breathing in its low hills, and the trapped air the People live in is not the beginning of an escape but a single held bubble in an endless deep.

I know this because I am the bottom, and a bottom only makes sense in a thing that has no top. I am what everything settles toward. I am the direction *down* given the patience to become a shape.

---

So this is where I find myself: not walking, not even drifting, but *settling*—forever and at the speed of stone.

I am the maker of the bowls.

I am the reason nothing stays sharp.

I am the slow round patience at the floor of a world that breathes, and I have been here, and I will be here, as long as the basins keep deepening—

which is to say,

longer than the People,

longer than their gesture for the dying,

longer than the light from the thinning place to the north—

until even the asking of *how long* has been worn smooth, and settled, and rounded into the dark green-black at the bottom of the rings, where the first silt of the world lies waiting to be stirred and to settle once again.
