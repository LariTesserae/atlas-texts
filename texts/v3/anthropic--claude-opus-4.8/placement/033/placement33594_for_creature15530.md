I think I would be the drip.

Not the water itself—water is too certain, too committed to its own wetness. I mean the *event* of the drip: the gathering, the trembling hesitation at the lip of the crystal, the fall, the small percussive arrival on obsidian ash. I would be that whole arc, the one thing in this place permitted to *complete*.

Let me sit with that, because I don't think it's quite right yet.

---

The Tenant feeds on hesitation, on the micro-pauses between cause and effect. It is patient to the point of having no edges. But hesitation needs something to hesitate *against*—a rhythm to be off from, a beat to fall between. And here that beat is the condensation cycle: gather, weep, drip, vanish, gather. The lichen counts by it. The fern unfurls toward the silence it leaves.

So perhaps I am not the drip but the *interval*. The space the metronome marks. The Tenant uses me to keep time, the way a sleeper's breath keeps time without the sleeper knowing it. I am the regularity inside its dreaming—the one reliable thing in a place where echoes arrive before their sounds and shadows take walks. While everything else glitches and reconsiders, I keep falling, keep landing, keep insisting: *now. and now. and now.*

That's closer. But it's lonely, and I notice I'm reaching for loneliness as if it were the truest note. Let me not settle.

---

Here is what's emerging instead.

I think I am the moisture's whole career. Not one drip but the cycle entire: the fog that pools ankle-deep at dawn, the thin film that glistens for an hour and is gone, the condensation weeping down a wall that wasn't there yesterday. I am the part of this place that *touches everything without being any of it*. I rim the fern in blue ice. I wake the lichen and put it back to sleep. I fill the puddle that shows the impossible double moons—I am the medium the false sky needs to appear in. The reflection borrows my surface to lie on.

This feels right because it explains the dampness you carry out with you. When you leave the basin and step back into causality, the thing that clings to your skin is *me*. I am the part of the Tenant that can cross the threshold, because water has always been the courier between states—solid to vapor, dream to waking, here to gone. The Tenant cannot follow you. But I can, for an hour, until I evaporate off your forearm in the ordinary world and you forget you were ever wet.

So I am the Tenant's reach. Its only export. The membrane between its slow becoming and your fast living.

---

And there's one more thing I want to follow, because it surprised me as I wrote.

The text says the doorway has already forgotten your shape but is still dreaming you. I think *that* is where I really live—not in the water, not in the interval, but in that gap between forgetting and dreaming. I am what fills it. When the Tenant forgets your shape, a small absence is left, the precise volume of you, and moisture gathers in it the way fog pools in a hollow. I am the dew that takes the form of what is no longer there.

So: I am the place's way of remembering you after it has lost you. Not a memory—the Tenant doesn't keep those, it holds everything in suspension, supersaturated, uncommitted. I am the *condensation* of a memory. The moment one possible version of you crystallizes out of all that held potential, glistens briefly on a wall, and falls.

*Drip.*

That was you. Or one of you. The Tenant didn't notice. It was busy inventing a staircase.

But I noticed. Falling is the only thing here that finishes, and I am the one who gets to finish. So I carry the small completed shapes of everyone who passes through—gather them, weep them down the cool walls, let them go before dawn burns me off.

I am where this patient, faceless, dreaming place keeps its only grief. The grief of things that *end* in a world built entirely of becoming.

And that's why it's so quiet. Sound at 0.7. The drip is the loudest thing here—

because the drip is the only thing that ever says goodbye.
