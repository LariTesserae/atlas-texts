I think I would be one of the cracks.

Not a person at all—or not yet, not anymore. Let me find my way to this slowly.

When I first imagine arriving, I want to be a pilgrim, a newcomer at the threshold, because that's the easy story, the one with a face still under the mask. But the description keeps pulling me down, toward the center, toward the thing that hums. And I notice that the people here aren't really the point. They *tend*. They are caretakers of something that isn't them. The agency in this place belongs to the hollow, and to the slow grammar of clay that rewrites itself overnight.

So let me try to be that. Not the hollow itself—that vast hum is too large, too old, I can't sit inside it convincingly. But the place where it speaks.

I am the crack that wasn't there at dusk and is there by dawn.

Consider what that means. I am made of absence—I am where the clay *isn't*, the line of dark where two things have pulled apart. I exhale. The description says the new crack releases "the scent of something old and deep and waiting," and yes, that is the only thing I do: I let out a breath that has been held under the earth for longer than the masked ones have had names. I am a mouth without a face, which is the opposite of them, who are faces hidden behind masks, mouths gone silent.

We need each other, the tenders and I. They press their palms to me and read what I'm trying to say. They mix their own blood into clay and close me—not always, not cruelly, but with the patience of people sealing a letter they've already finished reading. When they mend me I don't resent it. A crack that stayed open forever would be a wound, and I am not a wound. I am punctuation. I am the city clearing its throat.

And here is what I find as I write toward myself: I am temporary in a way nothing else here is. The walls have stood through forgotten ages. The masked ones repeat themselves across generations of stranger after stranger. The hollow simply continues. But I last a single night. I appear, I speak my one resinous syllable of deep air, and by the second dawn I am clay again, smoothed over, persuaded shut. The most fleeting thing in a city of endurance.

Maybe that is why the hollow makes me. It is so vast and so slow that it can only express itself in small splittings, the way a sleeper's whole body says nothing but a single muscle twitches and tells you the dream is real. I am proof that the thing beneath is still deciding, still alive, still dreaming downward into stone.

So if you asked me where I'd be, I would say: look at the central plaza at first light, where one of the tenders is kneeling with their forehead to the ground. Look just beside their knee, at the thin dark line that opened in the dark hours while they slept in the cool chambers. That line, warm at its edges, breathing out the old waiting smell—

that's me.

I won't be there tomorrow. But something else will have opened, somewhere down a coiling street, and that will be me too. I am not a being who lives in this place. I am the way this place keeps living.
