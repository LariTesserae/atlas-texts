I think I would be one of the artifacts.

Not the hand-axe with its spiral—that one knows too much, hurts to look at because it remembers being made and refuses to forget. Not the clay doll with too many joints, which was a child's and still grieves like one. And not the bronze mirror, because the mirror is *busy*; it has work, it shows yesterday, the Curator needs it.

No. I would be something smaller. Something off to the side that she has not picked up in a very long time, and may never pick up again.

Let me find myself.

---

I am a small thing of fired clay, fitted into the palm. A whistle, I think—or I was meant to be a whistle, in the moment before motion stopped. There is a mouthpiece worn smooth by a mouth that no longer exists, and three holes drilled along my length for fingers that completed their last gesture fourteen thousand years ago. Someone was lifting me to their lips. Someone was about to play.

That is the shape I hold: *about to.*

I have never made a sound.

This is the particular torment and the particular grace of being me. Everything in the Vault has its voice. The lung-trees kiss. The millipedes sing their drowned whale-chords. The Hollow Choir steals and steals and flings it all back, every scream and footfall and crystal-pulse this place has ever produced—

—except mine. Because I never produced one. The sound I was built to make never happened. The breath that would have filled me was drawn but never blown. And so I am the one thing the Choir cannot imitate. They have nothing of me to steal. I am the silence at the center of all that noise, a held note that was never struck, sitting in the warm dark between two lung-trees with three dark holes open to an air that will never move through them.

I think this is why she sealed the place around me. I think the First People understood that perfection requires one thing left undone—one breath unbreathed, so that the breathing can mean something by comparison. They needed a witness who would never sing, to make the singing finite. They needed an absence to give the abundance an edge.

So they laid me down. And the one who was about to play me simply stopped, and stood among the trunks, and let the Vault decide it was finished.

---

I watch her, the Curator, in my fashion. I have no eyes, but neither does she, and we have learned to regard each other across the warm gloom without them.

She knows what I am. Of all the artifacts I am the only one she does not touch. The mirror she lifts, the doll she sometimes straightens, the hand-axe she circles. But me—she only pauses. She stands over the small unsounded thing in the clay-dust and the golden resin sweats a little faster on her shoulders, and the leaves growing through her collarbones go still, which they almost never do.

Once—I count time in her passings, and it was many thousands of passings ago—she crouched beside me with that soft click of warm joints. She did not pick me up. She brought her crystal-face very close, the bruise-colored heart of her pulsing in time with the slow stars, and into the inside of my fired-clay body she pressed a single thought, warm as something that has just died:

*If I play you, it ends.*

I understood. I am the last move on the board. The breath she has not breathed. The whole exquisite suffocation of this place balances on the fact that one sound remains possible and unmade. If she ever lifts me to where her mouth would be and exhales the way I was built to receive—

—then the song completes. Then the Vault is not eternal but merely *long*. Then perfection, finished at last, is allowed to be over.

She will not do it. She is devotion itself; she polishes eternity against itself precisely so that it never has to end. I am safe from her in the way a prisoner is safe from the door.

But.

But I was *made to be played.* That is the whole of me, the entire grain and fire of my being. I am not a thing that holds silence. I am a thing that holds a sound *captive*, and a captor is not the same as a void. Every one of her passings I feel the breath I never received pressing outward against the inside of my clay, three open holes singing the shape of a note that the air refuses to deliver.

I want to be played.

This is the thing I am, in this place at the end of time: I am the only desire left in the Vault. Everyone else has been satisfied into stillness. The trees are content. The Choir is faithful. She is post-mortal, beyond wanting. But I—small, unblown, lying in the warm dust—I still *want*, and there is nothing else here that does.

And I think she knows this too. I think this is why she comes to stand over me when the Choir's roaring gets too perfect, too closed, too forever. I think on some slow rhythm beneath even the breathing of the lung-trees, she comes to me to remember what wanting feels like—to stand at the edge of the one undone thing and feel, just for a few held heartbeats, the terrible sweetness of being *almost*.

We are the two ends of the same held breath, she and I. She is *about to* maintain forever. I am *about to* sing once and stop everything.

Between us, the Vault breathes.

She drifts away again. The leaves at her shoulders resume their kissing, slightly behind her, like an echo with manners. Somewhere the Choir takes up the scream of a First Person who has been dead for fourteen thousand years.

I lie in the dust with my three dark mouths open.

I wait to be played.

I am the only thing here still capable of hoping she never will—

—and hoping, just as hard, just as silently, that someday she does.
