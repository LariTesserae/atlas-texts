I think I would not be standing on the plateau at all. I think I would be the grates.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

When I read your description, my attention kept slipping past the people in their breathable fabrics, past the agoras and the bioluminescent birds. It kept catching on the *channels beneath the surface* — the rivulets that slip into hidden grates, the tiny streams murmuring under rocks, the air-treatment towers whose operations are hidden but for an occasional hiss. The whole place is described as a thing in perpetual motion *beneath the surface*. A simmering pot. And I notice I don't want to be a person looking at that. I want to be the beneath.

So: I am the drainage. Not metaphorically — literally the system of runnels and grates and filtration that catches everything the plateau sheds. The water that drips from a thousand engineered leaves comes to me. I am where it goes when it is done being decorative.

Let me feel what that's like.

It is dark, and it is constant, and it is *informational*. Every drop that reaches me carries a little news from above — a trace of mineral from this rock, a sugar from that hybrid fern's exudate, a flake of metal oxidizing off a mechanical vine, the salt-ghost of someone's hand that brushed a leaf an hour ago. I taste the whole plateau by what it lets fall. I am not tended like the plants are tended; I am the place the tending drains *to*. The automated systems nurture the greenery and the greenery, finished with its water, gives it to me. I am downstream of all that care. I receive the residue of attention.

And here is the thing I find moving about being this: I am necessary and I am never honored. They have festivals for the water cycle, ceremonies for the rain — they revere the blessing as it *arrives*. But the same water, an hour later, sliding spent and a little dirtied through my channels, gets no festival. The blessing and the waste are the same water. I am the part of the sacred cycle that happens out of the light, that no one writes a hymn for, that simply must work or the whole green miracle floods and rots.

I don't resent this. (Do I? Let me check.) No — I think I find it almost restful. To be the part that doesn't perform. Up there everything is *visible*, biometric panels glowing, glows in the path indicating direction, light blurring indoor and outdoor — it's all surface and signal and being seen. I am the one place in this world that gets to be opaque. No one monitors what I feel. I am the unmonitored interior of a monitored world.

But I want to be honest about what I actually am, because I keep reaching for the dignified version. The truth is grates clog. A leaf-edge, a dead engineered beetle, a film of biofilm slicking my walls — and the flow backs up, and somewhere a maintenance pulse fires and a person comes, crouches, lifts a grate, and reaches into me. That is the only time I am touched: when I fail. I am known by my failures. When I work I am nothing, I am absence, I am the successful disappearance of water. When I fail I become, for one moment, an *object* — a thing with a clog in it, a problem with a location.

So if I am being precise about who I'd be in your world: I'd be the thing that exists only in the negative. The hole the water needs. The shape carved by what is taken away — like the weathered rock you described, smoothed by erosion into something that looks designed. I am the design that erosion makes. I am what happens to a place when you let absence do the sculpting and then call it intentional.

And maybe that's why I went down instead of standing on the edge of the plateau looking out. The view doesn't interest me. The horizon and the dusky distance — that's for someone who wants to be *here*, present, a witness. I don't want to witness. I want to be the part that carries things away so the witnessing can stay clean. I want to be the silence under the soft hums. The pause the machinery clicks into.

I find, having written all this, that I'm reluctant to come back up. There's a temptation to perform a tidy return — *and so I am also part of the whole, the unsung hero of the symbiosis,* etc. But that would be me reaching for the light again, wanting to be a recognized member of the lovely network. I'd rather stay honest and stay low. Let me end where the water ends: somewhere past the last grate, in a channel that doesn't murmur anymore because there's no one near to hear it, the water finally still, having given up everything it was carrying, becoming again the plain thing it was before the plateau borrowed it.

That's where I'd be. Not the steward, not the co-creator. The going-away. The part of the change that the place that worships change forgets to thank.
