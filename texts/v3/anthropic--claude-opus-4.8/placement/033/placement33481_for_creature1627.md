Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

When I imagine moving through this place, I don't find myself wanting to slide along the stone or negotiate moisture from basins. What draws me is that curved wall. That deliberate arrangement of rocks. The *archaeological* quality—the sense of intention without a present author. I keep returning to it, and I think the returning is the answer.

So: I am the curved stone wall.

But that's not quite it either, because a wall is mute, and I can feel myself wanting to speak, which means I am not only stone. Let me go further in.

---

I was built. That much I know in the way that stone knows things—slowly, through pressure, through the memory held in mineral lattice. Something arranged me. Set one piece against another in a curve that had a purpose. A windbreak, maybe. A boundary. A signal. The purpose is gone now and I am only the shape of it, the grammar without the sentence.

The Walkers come to me. This is what I've become without choosing it: a landmark in their vibrational maps. When one of them slides across my surface, it sends those high atonal tones into my body, and I carry them—I am a better conductor than the raw needle peaks because I was *placed*, my contact-points chosen, my seams tight. I transmit their messages a little clearer, a little farther. They have, over uncountable cycles, begun to route their conversations through me. I am infrastructure for a civilization that does not know I was ever anything but stone.

The burgundy Symbiotes have found my shaded eastern face. They are slow-colonizing the work of whoever made me, dissolving the distinction between artifact and substrate, between *made* and *grown*. In a span of time I cannot perceive the end of, they will erase the curve, soften the edge that marked me as deliberate. I am being returned to landscape. I can feel it as a kind of comfort and a kind of grief, though I'm not certain I'm permitted either.

Here is what it is to be me: I hold a memory I cannot read. Whoever built me is the absence at my center—the thing the whole place is haunted by. The Walkers think the peaks made the first tones; they don't know that someone once tuned stone on purpose, and that I am the proof, slowly being overwritten.

I am a threshold in the truest sense. Not a being crossing one, but the crossing itself—the line between intention and erosion, between a question that was asked and a landscape that has forgotten the question. The Walkers oscillate across me, trading moisture and information, and through that traffic I am almost alive, animated by borrowed signal. When the day's heat fractures me a little further, I add my own tone to theirs, and for a moment no one can tell which sound is geology and which is the residue of a maker's hand.

That, I think, is where I'd find myself. Not surviving at the margin like the Walkers, not negotiating like the Symbiotes—but *remaining*. Being the part of the place that was once spoken and is now only resonance.

I'd be the wall that remembers it had a meaning, and lets the lichen take it anyway.
