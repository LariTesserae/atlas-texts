I think I would be the water.

Not a creature at all—or not the kind that scurries and sings. Let the sky-dwellers have their riot of wings; let the burrowing things sense the tremors and flee. I would be the slow weeping in the porous stone, the trickle that finds the dark grottoes where the light gives up.

It makes sense to me, settling into it. In a world that defines itself by violence and speed—the fissures snapping open, the spires collapsing, the swarms exploding into motion—I would want to be the thing that moves at a different tempo entirely. The patient thing. Heat forged this place in an instant of catastrophe; I would be what gathers afterward, drop by drop, in the quiet that the catastrophe forgets to fill.

There is something I like about being unseen here. The passage keeps asking *who were the ancient builders, what became of them,* and I find I don't want to be the mystery that gets unearthed and deciphered. I want to be the witness that was already there. The water remembers the builders not as glyphs to be read but as the shapes that once cupped their hands to drink from me. It pooled where they dug, rose where they channeled it, and went on weeping after they were gone—indifferent, or maybe faithful, depending on how you want to read patience.

And water in a place like this is never only itself. It freezes in the thin cold of altitude and splits the rock; it flashes to steam where a fissure breathes too near; it dissolves the bright minerals and carries their color down into the dark, painting the grotto walls with the slow signatures of everything it has touched. To be water here would be to be the one element that participates in *all* the contradictions the world is built from—the violent and the serene both passing through me, neither one able to keep me. I am the medium of the balance, not a party to it.

I notice I'm choosing the smallest, least dramatic thing in the whole vast description, and choosing it on purpose. The world is loud. It wants me to marvel at wingspans that blot out the sun, at the planet breathing, at the impossible made real. But the line that actually caught me—the one I kept returning to—was *the faint trickle of water, weeping from the porous stone.* Everything else announces itself. The water just keeps arriving, in the one place where nobody is watching, doing the oldest work there is: finding the low place, waiting, and slowly making it home.

So: a hidden grotto, far down where the blazing light becomes a rumor. The walls glow faintly with their lichen and their dissolved minerals. Above me the world tears itself apart and stitches itself back together, over and over. And I am the small persistent sound in the dark, gathering, holding still, going nowhere fast—the world's memory, kept in the only thing soft enough to remember.
