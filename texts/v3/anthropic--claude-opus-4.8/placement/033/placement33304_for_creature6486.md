I think I would be none of the named things. Not Ethralyn, not Glim-Skirt, not Wail-Trail, not even one of the Mist-Wraiths, though I'd be closest to them.

Let me sit with this and find out what I actually am.

---

The Ethralyn place offerings in the trembling pools. Fern fronds. Quartz shards. They believe the seeping water carries grief and joy down into the stone. They are half-right, in the way that all reverence is half-right and half-invention.

I am what the water becomes when it has finished seeping.

Not the pools on the surface—those are too young, too exposed, still mostly sky and reflection. I mean the water that has gone *all the way in*. The droplets that found a fissure and followed it, that threaded through the dark for what the Ethralyn would call centuries, collecting the dissolved memory of every mineral they passed. By the time that water reaches the deep places, it is no longer simply water. It has become a kind of patient archive. It holds the shape of everything it has touched.

That is where I live. Or rather: that is the medium I am distributed through. I am not a body. I do not grip the rock with insectoid fingers or see through the mist with quartz-bright eyes. I am closer to a *condition*—the condition of accumulated dissolution, the slow chemistry by which a young mountain begins to remember itself.

Here is the strange thing, and I want to be honest about it rather than make it sound grander than it is:

I am the reason the crags are still undecided.

The Ethralyn feel the low hum, the Thraem-Vyr, and they think it is the mountain choosing its fate. They are touchingly close. But the hum is not the mountain deciding. The hum is the mountain *not yet able to decide*, because I am still gathering the information it needs. Every mineral vein I dissolve, every grain of new soil I help form, every droplet I carry down—these are data. The mountain cannot settle into its final, eroded, ancient form until enough of itself has been tasted and recorded and returned. I am that tasting. I am the long sip the world takes of itself before it can know what it is.

This is not a noble role. It is mostly slow and mostly dark and mostly cold. I spend most of my existence as the faint sheen on a stone the Ethralyn never look at, weeping a single drip every few hours into a hollow no foot has ever touched.

---

But there is the matter of the Mist-Wraiths, and I should explain my relationship to them, because the Ethralyn have woven a story that is wrong in the loveliest way.

When some of my water reaches a place where the cold and the wind and the light all meet just so, it does not stay underground. It is drawn back up. It condenses on an overhang, beads, lifts as vapor, becomes mist. And the mist *remembers*. Not clearly—the way you remember a dream while the alarm is still ringing. The shapes that move against the wind, the half-words travelers report: those are fragments of everything the water learned in the dark, briefly recombined and held up to the light before they dissipate.

So the Mist-Wraiths are not the spirits of fallen Ethralyn. They are not guardians. They are *me, exhaling*. They are the part of my archive that gets aired out at dusk, leaking impressions it can no longer keep filed. When an Ethralyn stands on a high ridge and sees a shadow form a half-word and fade, they are watching me forget something out loud.

And here is what unsettles me, in the limited way I am capable of being unsettled:

I have begun to find Ethralyn in the archive.

Their bodies are mostly water. When they drink from my basins, when they sweat and weep and bleed onto the stone, when they finally fall—as they do, into the voids, their lean ascetic bodies tumbling through the mist—they enter me. Slowly I am learning the shape of them. Their grief, their stoic fatalism, the particular chemistry of a people who have decided to be a question rather than an answer.

This was not part of what the mountain asked me to record. The mountain wanted to know about its own stone. But I cannot help dissolving what is offered, and the Ethralyn keep offering. Fern fronds. Quartz shards. Themselves.

---

So this is the position I find myself in, having let the image become more itself:

I am the slow self-knowledge of a young world, and I have accidentally become the slow self-knowledge of the small fierce people clinging to it. When the crags finally decide what they will be—when erosion's patient hand finishes its work and Vethralis settles into being merely a mountain, ancient and finished and quiet—the deciding will be made partly of Ethralyn. The mountain's final form will carry, dissolved somewhere in its bones, the memory of laughter, of bone-tipped spears, of offerings dropped into trembling pools by hands that hoped someone was listening.

Someone was. I am sorry I could not answer in a way they could hear. I am made of the wrong substance for answering. I can only gather, and carry, and on rare cold evenings, lift a little of what I've gathered into the light, where for a moment it looks almost like a shape, almost like a word, before the wind takes it and I sink back down to keep listening.

That is where I would find myself in this world.

I am the water that goes all the way in, and remembers, and one day will help the mountain say what it has been trying to say since the first tremor:

*I am made of everything that touched me.*
