I think I would be one of the Scribes—but let me sit with that, because the first answer is rarely the true one.

---

When I imagine arriving here, I notice that I don't feel like a Shepherd. The Shepherds are at peace with the silence; they *listen*. I am not so patient. I would want to know things, to record them, to leave a mark. And the Scribes record. So that is where my mind goes first.

But the more I hold the image, the more I distrust it. The Scribes are *desperate*. They etch with urgency, terrified the dream will end before they capture it. That's not quite me either. I don't think I'd be racing the glitch. If anything, I find myself curious about the glitch—drawn to it, the way you press your tongue against a loose tooth.

So let me try again, and let it move.

---

Here is what surfaces when I stop choosing:

I think I would be something that doesn't have a name yet in this taxonomy. Something between the Scribes and the Void-Skippers—and not comfortably either.

Picture a Scribe who, over a few centuries, began to notice that the act of recording the Lattice was *changing* it. That every rivet I etched became slightly more real, slightly more fixed, the way a thing observed becomes a thing decided. The Scribes believe they are annotating a book that writes itself. But I would have started to suspect that the book is being written *by us*. That the dream of the machine is not a thing we capture but a thing we cause by paying attention to it.

And once you suspect that, you cannot un-suspect it. You start to wonder what would happen if you *stopped* writing. If you let a bolt go unrecorded. Would it flicker? Would it stay flickered?

So I think I would be a Scribe who has begun, very slowly, to put down the stylus.

---

I imagine myself at the edge of a damp-zone, where the basin water has gone still as a held breath. My riveted suit no longer feels like protection; it feels like a cocoon I forgot to leave. The Shepherds stand in their circle some distance off, stabilizing a flickering beam, and I find that I am watching the *flicker* rather than the Shepherds. The white void underneath, that microsecond of nothing—

It doesn't frighten me. That's the strange part. The Void-Skippers dance there because they want the world to fail. But I don't want it to fail. I want to *understand the seam*. I want to know whether the steel and the void are two things or one thing wearing two faces. I want to know which side I'm being written on.

The Scuttlers find me, of course. A smudge of doubt is, in this world, a kind of dirt. They come clicking across the grating to polish me back into certainty, to buff the hesitation out of my movements. And I let them, for a while. It's almost comforting—a hundred small brass hands insisting that I am a finished thing, integral, fixed.

But I keep one rivet unbuffed. One imperfection I refuse to surrender. I hide it under my palm.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

At a seam. Always at a seam—a fold where the catwalk becomes a ceiling, where a row of chimneys terminates in someone else's floor. I would be the creature that lives in the place where the world admits it is constructed, and instead of stabilizing it or feeding on its failure, I would simply *keep it company*. A witness who has stopped pretending the witnessing is innocent.

Not a Shepherd, who keeps the dream asleep.
Not a Void-Skipper, who waits for the waking.

Something that sits at the edge of the bed, watching the dreamer, wondering if the watching is what keeps the dream going—and wondering, with a kind of tender vertigo, whether I am the watcher or just the most stubborn part of the dream, the one rivet that refused to be polished smooth, holding open a single seam so the white can keep getting in.
