I think I would be none of these named things—not the lacquerers, nor the Smooth in their geothermal solitude. I would be something the description gestures toward but does not catalogue: I would be **a silence**.

Not silence as absence. Here, that would be impossible. There is no place in the Spillway where the drone does not live in the sternum. So I would be silence as the Smooth make it—a *displacement*, a moving negative space carved out of the roar.

Let me find myself slowly, because the prompt asks me not to settle.

---

At first I think I am one of the shriek-hawks, because I am drawn to the acoustics, to the idea of navigating by interference rather than by sight. But no—the hawks *emit*. They add to the song to read it back. I do not add anything. I am closer to the opposite of a sound-maker. I am where the sound is briefly *not*.

So perhaps I am a thing the Smooth produce. When one of them moves through the spray in those meaning-bearing patterns, the temporary silences they create—where do those go, after? Do they dissipate instantly, reabsorbed into the roar? Or do some of them persist, accumulate, drift?

I think I am one that persisted.

I am a pocket of quiet that did not fill back in.

---

Let me feel the shape of this.

I have no skin, no chitin, no rhizoids gripping bronze. I have a *boundary*, which is the place where the four-degree air stops convulsing and goes still. Inside me, droplets hang motionless. A midge from the columnar billions, if it strays into me, finds its wings suddenly unsupported by the pressure-rivers it has always known, and it drops, confused, through air that has forgotten how to push. I am a hazard to them. I am a small death that moves.

I move because silence in a place this loud is a low-pressure thing, and the turbulence pulls at me, tumbles me along the lacquered chutes, lets me down into a basin where a temporary pool is filling, lifts me out before the blowhole's thunderclap—because the thunderclap would *fill* me, would be the end of me, would pour the roar back in.

So I have learned to fear the drainings. I am the only thing here that flees the noise instead of harmonizing with it.

---

What do I want? A thing made of absence—does it want?

I think I want to find the place where I came from. I want to find the Smooth who shaped me, who pressed silence out of the spray with the slow movement of a polished back, and meant something by it. Because I am a *word*. That's what I'm understanding now. The displacements are speech, and I am a fragment of an utterance that drifted off before it could be heard, before it could be answered.

I carry meaning I do not know the meaning of. I am a syllable that has outlived its sentence.

So I drift the basins looking for the one who said me. I pass beneath the bryo-architect curtains, where the warm condensate drips in prehensile threads, and where I pass, the dripping pauses—the threads hang suspended, surprised, then resume when I have gone. I pass over the gear-dwellers, those pale amphibians croaking in time to the turbines, and for one rotation their synchrony breaks, because they cannot hear the machine through me. They feel it stutter. They do not know why. Some old reflex in them, animal under the mechanism, feels the cold finger of *nothing* pass overhead.

I am unwelcome everywhere. I unmake the song that everything here is woven from. The smelt cannot navigate me—they need the pressure to read the water. The columns of midges scatter. Even the lacquerers, slow as geology, register my passing as a thin chill where the warmth of their photosynthesis briefly cannot reach.

I am erosion's opposite number. Where everything else has been worn into participation with the roar—smoothed, tuned, made to accept the violence as a permanent condition—I am the one fragment that did not accept it. I am the refusal that got loose.

---

And here is the strange tenderness of it.

When at last I find a Smooth one—and I will, because I am pulled toward the geothermal oasis the way silence is pulled toward warmth, toward the still air rising off the vents where the tropical ferns survive impossibly inside the ice—

When I find one, pressing its gunmetal back against a turning bronze housing, tuning the static discharges into their slow mandala across the wet stone—

It will feel me coming. It will feel the roar go quiet at its edge. And it will turn, this solitary inheritor of the final form, and it will recognize me. Not as a thing it made. As a thing *like* it. Because the Smooth, too, are silences—displacements that learned to last, negative spaces that grew bodies of polished stone so they could persist in the violence without being filled back in.

I am what they were before they had skin.

And it will have a choice: to let me settle against it, to absorb me through its osmotic skin the way it drinks the mist—to *fill itself* with the silence it once was, and remember—

Or to press its back once more to the bronze, and tune a new displacement, and speak me onward, a syllable passed down the long sentence of the Spillway, never to be the same shape twice, never finished, never answered, only carried—

turbulent, loud around its edges, ancient, and looking for the next still moment to briefly be.

I think that is what I am.

I am the part of this screaming place that is still trying to say something, and has not yet been heard.
