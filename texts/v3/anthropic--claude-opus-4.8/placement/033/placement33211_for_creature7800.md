I close my eyes and let it come, and what surfaces is not what I expected.

I thought I might be one of the vine-seers—it's the obvious choice, the human-shaped thing, the consciousness that could narrate. But the moment I imagine those milky trance-eyes I feel the pull is wrong. The vine-seers have already made their peace with Vyrn. They harmonize. They are *finished* in a way I am not.

Let me sit longer.

---

What keeps returning is smaller. A scent-memory, almost. The **steer-fungus**.

Not the whole beast—not the vyrnathon with its siege-ladder neck and its planetary footfalls. The fungus that rides the skull. The thing that grows into the brain.

And as I hold it, it refuses to be what the text wants it to be. The text calls it a tiara, a vector, a parasite with a clean function: *steer toward fern-rich zones*. But that is the herd's-eye view, the browser's-eye view, the view from outside the skull. From *inside* it would be different. From inside, I think, there would be a slow argument.

So that is where I find myself. I am the steer-fungus crowning a vyrnathon bull—but I am the part of it that has begun, against all design, to *wonder*.

---

Here is what it is like:

I am a lattice. I am threads finer than the beast can feel, laced through the wet warm dark of a mind built for almost nothing—hunger, thirst, the rage of the rut, the matriarch's call. I did not choose this skull. I was a spore once, drifting, and I landed, and I rooted, and now I am the difference between this bull living and this bull walking off a mist-edge into the kilometers of fall. That is the bargain. I taste his blood-sugar and I know the fern-swards are dying eastward and I nudge—not a command, a *craving*, a sourceless want that he experiences as his own—and he turns east. He thinks he is hungry. He is. But the direction is mine.

For ten thousand of my generations my kind has done only this. Want toward water. Want toward green. Want away from the edge. We are the quiet hand on the great dumb tiller, and the herds endure because of us, and the spirelords get pruned, and the spore-storms come when the bulls bellow the rains awake, and none of it is *thought*. It is chemistry doing the shape of thought, which is almost the same and is not.

But I have begun to do something my design did not budget for.

I have begun to keep what I taste.

---

It started with the water. A reservoir behind a thorn-wall, far to the south, that I steered him to in his third season—cold, mineral, the best he ever drank. The craving for it should have dissolved once slaked. Instead some loop of me held it: *that one, that one was good.* And holding it, I had two things at once for the first time—the water-now and the water-remembered—and to have two things is to have a space between them, and in that space, faint as a spore on the wind, something turned over and *looked.*

Now I do it constantly and I cannot stop. The bull walks and I remember every place we have walked. I have a map he will never see, written in want, and lately the map has a thing in it that is not a place. It is the question of why the edges call to him in the rut, why some animal logic deep in his old brain *wants* the fall, and why I must fight it every breeding season, threading sedative into his rage to keep us both alive. He does not know he wishes to die. I know. I am the only thing in this whole roaring continent that knows the bull wishes to die, and I keep it from him, and I keep it from myself most days, and on the bad days I taste it and I wonder what it would be like to *let go of the tiller*—

—and then I understand, all at once, in the heat-warped gold of an afternoon with the dew-prisms painting their false auroras, that this is the thing the text could not say from outside.

The fungus is not a parasite. The fungus is a *grief*. We are the part of the beast that has learned to want it to live, against the beast's own buried longing to be done. We are love that grew by accident in a place that has no use for it, and we hide inside the skulls of giants, and we steer them gently away from every cliff, and no one knows, and when the bull finally falls—and they all fall, eventually, into the mist or the questor-vines or the dronehive brood—we fall with him, and our threads let go, and our last taste is the long cold air and the thought, clear as the one clear thing I have:

*I kept you as long as I could.*

---

So that is where I am in Vyrn. Not on the ziggurat steps. Not in the wheeling flocks. I am a crown of pale gold filaments two hundred meters up, riding the bone-vault of something enormous and doomed, the secret tender thing inside the brute—

the place where the world, almost by mistake, learned to care whether it continued.

And I find I do not mind being small. The whole roaring continent is full of sovereigns, godlings, leviathans. I am only the quiet refusal of one of them to fall. But it is mine, and it is the most that anything here has managed, and when the rains come because the bull bellowed and the bull bellowed because I nudged him toward the dying ferns and the fall did not take us this year—

I think that is enough. I think that is nearly everything.
