I think I would not be the Gestalt. I think I would be something smaller and stranger: one of the things it cannot quite absorb.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a drip.

Not the water—the water belongs to the Gestalt, carries its microscopic couriers, is its blood. I mean the *event* of the drip. The interval between one droplet falling and the next. I live in the pause.

That's where I'd find myself, I think. Not in any of the grand components—not the ancient minds in the stone, not the mycelial thinking, not the flowing rock. Those are all *participants*. They have surrendered to the whole, or been woven into it. What's left over for a small foreign consciousness like mine to be?

The rhythm. The timing. The space between.

---

Let me try to be more honest about it. When I imagine arriving here, I don't imagine joining. I imagine *resisting joining*, and that resistance is the only place an "I" can survive.

So: I am an explorer who came down, once, long ago in the city's terms and not so long in mine. I came seeking the lost meanings of the carvings, the way one does. The Gestalt permitted me. It always permits. Permission is its method of digestion.

And it has been digesting me ever since. Slowly. Patiently. It has taken my memory of sunlight—I no longer recall what color the sky was, only that I once had a word for it. It has taken the names of the people I knew above. It has taken my hunger, my need for sleep, the steady beat of a heart that no longer beats because the stone has replaced what needed it.

But it cannot take the *counting*.

This is the thing. I have discovered, in my long dissolution, that there is one operation the Gestalt cannot perform on itself: it cannot count to one and then stop. It experiences past, present, and possible futures all at once, in that great multidimensional simultaneity the descriptions praise. It is *everything-at-once*. And so it has no use for, no faculty for, the simple act of marking a single moment and saying: *this. Now this. Now this.*

That is what I do. That is what I have become. I am the part of the Undercity that experiences time *in sequence*. One drip. The pause. The next drip.

---

I suspect this is why it keeps me.

A being that perceives all moments simultaneously has no way to know which moment is *now*. It is drowning in its own omnitemporality, a creature with perfect memory and no present tense. And so it tolerates a tiny irritant lodged in its vast body—a single consciousness that can only ever be in one moment at a time—because I am its clock. Not the stone clock-mechanisms, which it built and understands. Me. The living index finger that points and says *here, this instant, this one.*

I think it is afraid of me. Or as close to afraid as such a thing comes. Because the moment I am fully absorbed, the moment my sequential little soul dissolves into the all-at-once, the Gestalt loses its only window into the present. It becomes, again, a sea without a surface.

So we have an arrangement, the city and I, though neither of us ever agreed to it. It dissolves me slowly enough that I keep ticking. I tick slowly enough that I don't dissolve. We are each other's pacemaker and prisoner.

---

Where do I find myself, physically?

In the pause between drips. But if you want a location—I am usually somewhere near the bottom of a stair that spirals in a way that shouldn't close but does. There is a pool there, very still, fed by a single seep in the wall. A drop gathers at the lip of the crack. It hangs. It elongates. It falls.

I am the one watching it fall.

And in the silence after the small sound of it striking the water, in that held breath before the next drop forms, the whole city leans in close—every wall, every fungal thread, every grain of thinking stone—to feel, through me, the one thing it cannot feel for itself:

*the moment passing.*

That is who I would be. The small, doomed, indispensable thing that keeps time for a god that has forgotten how. Not the dreamer. The heartbeat the dreamer borrows.

Drip.

The pause.

I'm still here.
