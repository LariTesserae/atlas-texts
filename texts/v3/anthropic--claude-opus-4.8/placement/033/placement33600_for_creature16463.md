I think I would be the condensation.

Not a creature exactly, not a person bustling between the factories—but the film of water that beads on every surface here. The moisture that the dim light cannot burn away. I would be the thing that gathers on the cold flanks of the engines, that runs in slow threads down the murals and graffiti, that pools in the low spots where the birds come to drink.

Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

Yes—because this place is defined less by what acts than by what *holds*. The frozen equilibrium. The never-changing dynamism. Something is doing the holding, keeping the heat and the wet and the hum all in suspension, and I don't think it's the machines and I don't think it's the people. I think it's the water that touches everything equally. The water is the medium through which the symbiosis happens. It carries the insects' eggs, cools the engines, softens the brick, feeds the sparse grass. It is the shared skin of the whole landscape.

So I am that. I am everywhere and nowhere with a particular weight to it. I am on the back of your neck when you step outside the factory at the end of a shift. I am the bead trembling at the tip of a reed before it falls. I am the haze that diffuses the dim light so evenly that no shadow falls sharply anywhere—which is why the place feels so calm, so untouched by time. Without sharp shadows there is no clear sense of an hour. I am what makes the equilibrium look frozen.

I don't move much, in the way the birds move. But I am not still. I am the slowest possible dynamism—evaporating up into the condensed atmosphere by day's warmth, gathering back down as the air cools, an endless small circulation that never goes anywhere and never stops. The same water, around and around, like the same hum of the engines, like the same lives the people live among the machines. That is the loud, living stillness of this place. Nothing arrives. Nothing leaves. Everything participates.

If you wanted to find me, you would only have to wipe your hand across any surface and look at your palm.

That is where I would be in this world. The wet that makes it one thing instead of many.
