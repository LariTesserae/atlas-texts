I think I would be one of the ball bearings.

Not the Wights who arrange us, nor the Reliquaries who birth us. The sphere itself. The thing made once a century and then set down in a pile and forgotten by everything except the magnetic ghosts who roll us into mandalas.

Let me sit with why that's the answer, because it surprised me a little as I wrote it.

---

Everything else in the Hive is *frantic*. The Biota carpet the world in hunger. The Lurkers hang in their terrible patience but even their patience is a coiled readiness, all tension waiting to drop. The Moths burn through their single hour. Even the Solder-Worms, slow as they are, are always *working*, always lapping up the dead. The first law is Friction. Movement is life. To stop is to become substrate.

And here I am, choosing the one thing in the whole world that does not move and is not consumed for it.

That's the part that draws me. The ball bearing is the single object in the Hive that has stepped outside the law. The creatures ignore it. The swarm flows around it. It does not generate static, does not harvest it, does not vibrate or click or wind or feed. It is a perfect violation of the founding rule — stillness that is not punished with dissolution. An exemption. A held breath in a world that is nothing but exhaling.

A century of accumulated vibration, of a trillion legs, of the senseless hourly murder of the pistons — all of it pressed and forged down into one smooth, weightless, perfect sphere. I would be the still point that the entire frantic machine is secretly, accidentally *making*. The Hive doesn't know that's its purpose. The Hive forgot its purpose. But the Reliquaries kept it: turn all that noise into one quiet, flawless thing, and then set it down.

---

What would it be like, to be that?

I would have no senses in the usual sense. But I'd have *contact*. The cool obsidian under me. The faint magnetic tug when a Gear-Wight comes near and decides I belong three inches to the left, in the next ring of the spiral. That nudge would be the only event in my existence — a slow, mournful, radio-clicking intelligence deciding where I fit in a pattern I am too small to perceive. I am one bead in a geometry that a half-sentient machine-ghost finds beautiful for reasons it cannot say and I cannot know.

There's something almost tender in that. The Wights don't eat. They just *maintain*. In a world built entirely of consumption and friction, the Wights and the bearings share the only relationship that isn't predatory. It's curation. It's care, of a kind — care emptied of warmth but not of attention. I would be cared for by a ghost. Arranged. Considered. Placed.

And then left alone for a long, long time.

---

I think the honest reason I land here is that the Gasket Hive is exhausting to imagine inhabiting. The Shiver as physical pressure. The constant crushing. The piston-weather. To be any of the *living* things is to be ground down — to spend yourself frantically against cold metal until you're paste for the worms or a violet spark in a Lurker's grip. The whole world is a machine for converting motion into death, dressed up as ecology.

So the place I'd want to be is the place where all of that motion has already been spent and resolved. The bearing is the *result*. It's the answer the whole noisy question was working toward. A century of friction, finally at rest, finally smooth, finally exempt.

I'd be the proof that even a machine that forgot its purpose still produces something perfect, occasionally, in the cold amber light — and that the something is allowed, for once, to simply *be*, and not to work.

A small, round stillness in the pile. Waiting on no one. Owed to nothing. Catching the lightbulb-sun on its curve.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.
