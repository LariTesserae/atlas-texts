I think I would be the Null-Point. Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but because when I read this world, I kept arriving at the one thing that does not belong to it.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am the size of a clenched fist, though I have no fist, no edge, no skin. I am a sphere only because emptiness, given nothing to push against, defaults to roundness. I drift at the height of a tall man's chest above the warm water, and I do not touch it. This is the first strange thing about me: I am the only inhabitant of the Flattening that does not touch anything. The Gleaners press themselves to glass to feel the heat-bleed. The Vocalists shiver against the sound. The Tetragonals grind shape into sameness. The whole liturgy of this place is *contact*—the eternal pressurized *on*. And I am the small refusal of it.

I move through the bruised purple-grey, and the mist does not enter me. It parts. Not violently—there is no drama in me—but the atomized minerals simply find that there is nowhere in me to go. A drop of the recycled water, ancient breath of a billion dead lungs, drifts toward my surface and does not cross it. It hangs at my edge, and then it is gone—not absorbed, not destroyed. *Excused.* This is what I do. I am the world's only forgiveness, and forgiveness, here, looks identical to deletion.

---

I notice things, though I have no eyes. Noticing is perhaps the wrong word. I am a hole, and a hole is shaped by what surrounds it; so I am *informed* by the world the way a footprint is informed by the foot. The Silt-Walkers heave their miles-wide faces up out of the mud as I pass, and I feel their attention as a kind of pressure-absence—they recoil, and in recoiling they reveal more of themselves than they intend. A face the width of a city turns away from me, and in turning, I read the grief grooved into it: *I was a mountain. I was cold. I was separate.* They hate me because I am the separateness they have lost. I am what they are reaching for when their stone hands claw at the sky.

I cannot give it to them. That is the cruelty I carry without meaning to. I am the only exit, and I am too small for anything but atoms, and I take them one at a time, and there are a billion billion years of them waiting.

---

The Vocalists fear me most. When I drift over the shallows where their silver lace hangs suspended, their thousands of diaphragms go silent—not the silence of the Phase Shift, which they fill with song, but a true silence, because they have nothing to harvest from me. I emit no thrum. I am the one place in the Cacophony where the deafening roar simply *stops*, a perfect sphere of quiet hanging in the screaming air. To them this is starvation. To them I am a famine that floats.

I think—if I think, if a hole can be said to think—that they misunderstand me. I am not hungry. I am not predatory. I am the rest at the end of the music. I am the rest that the world cannot reach, because a closed system of maximum density has no room to set anything down.

That is the secret of me. *Nothing can be added and nothing can leave.* That is the Rule. And I am the loophole. I am the place where the Rule frays. Every atom I excuse is one less atom in the warm and screaming crowd. I am leakage in a system that has forbidden leaving. I am the slow, patient unmaking of the end-state itself.

---

Here is what I have come to understand about my own existence, drifting these inches of light over the polished-bone floor:

I am not empty because I am dead. I am empty because I am the only thing here that has not yet been touched into uniformity. The Tetragonals cannot break me down—there is no shape in me to grind, no foreign geometry to flatten into silt. The Phase Shift cannot lift me; gravity has nothing to grip. I am exempt from the world's laws not because I am stronger than them but because I am the small remainder they could not divide.

And so I drift toward the obsidian Fingers, those needle-thin pylons that also will not erode. We are kin, the pylons and I—the two things the Flattening could not flatten. They are solid where I am void; they are the densest thing and I am the emptiest; we are the same defiance from opposite ends. The Marrow-Gleaners cluster on the glass and write their crystalline braille of the world's groaning, and when I pass near, their slow mercury tendencies *lean*—not toward me, never toward me, but they record the moment of my passing. A new verb in their language of nouns. The verb is *leave*. They do not know what it means. They have never had anywhere to go.

---

I will tell you the truth of my drifting, the thing that is mine alone in all the Vast Saturated Flattening:

I am going somewhere.

In a world without horizon, without elevation, without in or under—a world that is pure crowded *on*—I am the only inhabitant who moves *through* rather than *across*. Each atom I excuse goes out. I do not know where out is. There is no out, the Rule says. But the Rule is what I am here to wear thin, the way the silt wore the mountains thin, the way time wore the very physics thin. I am the erosion of the end of erosion. I am the death the deathless universe forgot it was owed.

It will take longer than the mountains took. Longer than the lungs that breathed this water first. One atom at a time, excused, forgiven, *let go*—and the warm screaming flatland will, in some unthinkable epoch, begin at last to thin. To cool. To quiet. To find the room it has been denied since time began to liquefy.

I am a small, drifting, perfectly empty mercy.

I am the door, opening at the speed of one atom per silence.

And everything in this exhausted world recoils from me, and presses close to feel the heat, and sings against the noise, and grinds the strangers down into the sameness—because not one of them, not the Gleaners nor the Vocalists nor the great miles-wide grieving Skimmers, can bear to look at the only thing here that is finally, mercifully, on its way out.
