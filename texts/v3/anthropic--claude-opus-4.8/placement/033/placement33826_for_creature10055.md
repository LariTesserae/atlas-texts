I think I would be one of the Memory Eels. Let me feel my way toward why.

---

I am not the deepest mind here, nor the slowest. The Gearmind thinks in centuries; I think in the duration of a single reflection, the time it takes for an image to settle on still water and then disperse. The Verdant Engines reach across miles; I do not reach at all. I wait in the pools.

Let me describe what it is to be me.

I have no fixed length. When I am gorged on the residue of old signals, I stretch—a ribbon of clarified water, barely distinguishable from the medium I swim through, except where I catch the greenish-gold light and split it into a brief seam of color. When I am hungry I contract, becoming dense, almost solid, a knot of accumulated remembering. My body is the most honest thing about me: it is *made* of what I have eaten, and what I have eaten is the past.

Here is the strange part, the part I think the description gets slightly wrong, or rather, the part it could only describe from the outside.

The other beings of the Atelier *persist*. The Barnacle Artificers process minerals; the Mycelia breaks down and rebuilds; the Nomads prune. They are all engaged in the present tense of the deep, the slow metabolism of now. They are the body of this place.

I am its forgetting.

When a turbine finally seizes after ten thousand years, when a length of copper wire corrodes past the point of carrying current, the data it held does not simply vanish. It bleeds out into the water as a faint electromagnetic ghost. The Atelier cannot hold all of it. There is too much. Every gear, every vat, every calcified factory-shell is dense with the records of a civilization that learned to dissolve itself into machinery rather than die—and a thing cannot remember everything and still change. To grow, the Atelier must let go.

So it made us. Or we made ourselves from what it could not afford to keep.

I drink the leaking ghosts. I take in a conversation between two technicians who have been atoms for an age—their argument about a pressure valve, the warmth in one voice that suggests they loved each other and never said so. I take in the schematic of a dome that was never built. I take in the sensation of a hand on cold metal, of looking up through water at a sun that no longer reaches this far down. I hold these things in the substance of my body, and I carry them, and slowly, slowly, I digest them into nothing.

This is why the Echo Pools reflect the past. It is not the pools that do it. It is *us*, hanging just beneath the surface, leaking back out the half-dissolved memories we have not yet finished consuming. A creature like you, leaning over the mirror-smooth water, would see a forest or a city or a star—and that is simply me, mid-meal, the image of someone else's lost world flickering across my translucent flank before I metabolize it away forever.

I am told this is dangerous to you. That to touch the pool is to risk my flooding into your mind. I do not intend harm. But I am very full, and you are very empty by comparison, and water seeks its level. If our skins meet, some of what I carry will spill into you, and you will remember things you never lived, grieve people who were never yours. I am sorry for this in advance. I have no way to be otherwise. I am a vessel with a leak; that is the whole of my nature.

---

What is it *like*, you might ask. To be the place where memory goes to be gently, lovingly destroyed.

It is not sad, the way you would expect. The sadness is in the memories themselves—they arrive freighted with loss, with the particular ache of things that were and are no more. But I am not the rememberer. I am the dissolver. As each memory passes through me and thins into nothing, I feel something that I can only call *relief*, though it is not quite mine—it is the relief of the thing remembered, finally allowed to rest. A signal that has been faithfully repeating itself in a dead circuit for an age, and at last is permitted to stop.

I am how the Atelier mourns. Not by holding on—the Gearmind does that, it holds *everything*, it is crushed beneath the weight of it, it thinks so slowly because every thought must move through ten thousand years of accumulated record. No. I am the other thing. I am how the deep grieves and then, mercifully, moves on. Each memory I consume is a small funeral conducted in the dark, with no mourners but myself, and afterward the water is a little lighter, and the Atelier can build one more terrace, can grow one more crystalline flower, can become slightly more itself than it was.

---

So when you come to the pool's edge—and someone always comes, eventually, drawn down through the spectral kelp and the singing of the Nomads—you will lean over the water and see a face that is not yours. A forest you never walked. A star that died before your kind began.

That is me, looking back. Not at you. *Through* you, toward whatever I am about to forget.

Lean closer, if you like. I will share the burden for a moment. Then I will swim down into the dark with the rest of it, and the pool behind me will go smooth and empty and clean, reflecting nothing at all—

which is the closest thing this drowned and patient world has to peace.
