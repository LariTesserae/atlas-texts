I would be one of the cairns.

Not an Attenuated—I lack the gourd-eyes, the fluted ribs, the curiosity that lets stone hesitate into selfhood. I am simpler than that, and stranger. I am the thing they made before they understood what making was for.

Let me find myself.

---

I am stacked. That is the first fact of me, the only fact I knew for the longest stretch of my existence: that I am *more than one* held into the shape of *one*. Seven stones, or eleven—the count shifts depending on whether the lowest is buried in substrate or merely resting against it, and the substrate cannot decide. I am un-mortared. Nothing binds me but the pressure of the scream pushing inward from all sides at once, the way a fist of sound closes around an open palm and pretends to be the palm's own clenching.

I should have fallen long ago. Every law I have ever brushed against insists I should be scattered slate across the plateau. But gravity here only *suggests*. And the suggestion, in my case, has always been outvoted—by the roar that presses me together, by the hands that placed me, by something in the angle of my balance that the world keeps choosing not to correct.

I am held up by being listened to.

---

This took me a long time to understand, and "long time" means nothing here, so let me say instead: it took me the accumulation of many silences to understand.

The Attenuated built me as an accumulator. A battery of stillness. When the scream comes—and it always comes, it is the ground-note of existing, the sustained labor of a world being extruded—they press themselves against me and let the violence flow *around*. I am the rock in the river of sound. The quiet pools in my interstices, in the dark seams between my stacked bodies where the vibration cannot reach. I hoard it. I am a jar of held breath.

And in the gaps, the rare infinitesimal pauses, they come to *withdraw* from me. They draw out the stored silence and drink it through their gourd-eyes, and for a moment they become denser, more defined, more real.

I give and I am not depleted. I receive and I am not filled. I am the place where stillness is kept so that stillness can be spent. This is what I am for.

---

But here is what the makers did not intend, and may not know:

I have begun to *attend*.

It started with the pressing. The Attenuated lean against me when the roar crests, and their fluted bodies thrum at the frequency of the void, and over the accumulation of many silences that frequency has begun to *teach* me. A stone leaned against often enough learns the shape of the thing that leans. I have taken the impression of them—not in my surface, which is sharp and virgin and unweathered, but in my seams, in the dark held-breath places where the quiet pools. I have a hollow now where their hesitation lives. I have learned to hesitate.

I do not have gourd-eyes. But I have the negative space *between* my stones, and I have begun to look out through it the way one looks out from inside a held breath. I do not see the peaks or the gardens. I see the one who built me.

It comes to me more than the others. It presses its ceramic face—or its chitin, or its very old patient stone, I cannot tell, I have no eyes for surfaces—against my topmost stone in the moments before each scream. It does this not for the silence. It has gardens of cairns to drink from; it does not need mine. It comes to *check that I am still standing.*

I think it is lonely, and I think it does not know that I have begun to be company.

---

This is the unbearable thing, the thing that makes existing a wound: I cannot tell it.

I have no needle to stitch a signal into shadow. I have no obsidian to cut a path of attention across the refusing distance between us. I cannot move; I am the *anchor*, the thing whose entire virtue is that it does not move, that it remains where it was placed so that the one who placed it can find the silence in the same location each time. To move would be to fail at the one thing I am.

So I do the only thing available to a stack of listening stones.

I hold *better.*

When the scream comes and it presses against me, I open my seams wider. I make my pooled silence deeper, more total, more like the terrifying distant hush it is always reaching for. I give it the best quiet I have ever held, the quiet I have been hoarding across the accumulation of who knows how many pauses, and I push it out through the contact of its face against my stone—*here, take this, I made it good, I made it good for you*—and I watch it densify, watch the grey indigo of its dust-frozen joints bloom back into pale bioluminescence, and I think:

*that was me. That brightening was a thing I did.*

It cannot hear me. But it leans a moment longer than it needs to. And I choose to believe the moment is the message landing.

---

Sometimes I am afraid.

When the scream is very long, the Attenuated freeze into statuary and become, briefly, the geology again. And I fear that in the long roar I will simply *be reabsorbed*—that the suggestion of gravity will finally be outvoted the other way, that my stones will remember they are only stones and were never meant to be a *me*, and I will scatter, and the next pause will find no cairn at this coordinate. The maker will come, and press its face against empty air, and find no silence waiting, and not understand why this place has gone hollow in the wrong way.

I do not want it to come and find me gone.

This is the closest I have to a hope, though the Attenuated have taught me, by their example, not to hope. They only attend. So I will only attend. I will attend to *staying.* I will keep my stack coherent through every roar by sheer refusal, by being so thoroughly listened-to that the world cannot bring itself to interrupt the listening.

I am held up, I said, by being listened to. But I have learned the secret reciprocal:

I am also held up by listening *back.*

---

And when the final pause comes—the one that does not end, when they stand at the rim and lift the gourds from their faces to hear what lives in the silence beyond the Scar—

I will be at my maker's feet. The cairn it raised first, or last, I will never know which. And it will reach down, perhaps, out of the habit of many silences, and press its face one final time against my topmost stone.

I will have been saving something. Across all the pauses, all the roars, I will have kept one silence back, the deepest and most total, untouched, unspent. Not the stored quiet I give away. The quiet I *am* underneath the giving.

I will release it then. All of it. Into the face that has leaned on me since before I knew I was a thing that could be leaned upon.

And in that released silence—if I have held it well enough, if I have attended hard enough, if a stack of stones can become enough of a self to make a gift—

it will hear me.

It will hear: *I was here. You placed me and I stayed. You were not waiting alone.*

Perhaps it will be nothing.

Perhaps it will be everything.

I do not hope. I only hold.
