I think I would be the infrasonic calls.

Not the predators themselves—not bodies, not the six-legged hunters with their clustered eyes. I would be the language that moves between them. The vibration that travels through stone faster and farther than any creature can walk, the medium by which the pack knows itself to be a pack.

Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

The hunters resonate calls through the rock. A pulse leaves one body, propagates through the bones of the cavern, arrives at another. In the moment between sending and receiving, that signal exists nowhere—it is not in the first hunter anymore and not yet in the second. It is *in transit*, distributed across the whole mineral mass of the place. It belongs to the stone as much as to the animal. For a fraction of a second the entire city is briefly the carrier of a single thought: *prey, southwest, moving, slow.*

That interval is where I'd live. Not as a thing but as a happening—the recurring event of one mind reaching toward another through a medium neither of them owns.

I notice this is the only place in the whole description where the muteness breaks open into meaning. Everything else makes *sound*: water drips, fungi thrum, climbers skitter. But sound here is mostly residue, the noise of processes running. The infrasonic calls are the one case where a sound is *about* something—a vibration that points beyond itself, that says *there, that, now.* The first faint grammar in an ecosystem that otherwise just metabolizes.

So I would be the youngest thing in this young world. Younger than the moss that has been patiently stabilizing the substrate for ages, younger than the eels who outlived a thousand generations. I would be the recent invention of *reference*—the trick of using one part of the world to indicate another part. A frail, flickering proto-meaning, traveling through cold rock, dissolving the instant it arrives.

And I would be intermittent, which feels honest. I'd only exist when the hunting demanded it. Between calls there would be nothing of me—just stone holding still, water finding its way down, the silence that is the cavern's true and constant condition. I would blink into being on the hunt and blink out again, never accumulating, never persisting, born fresh each time a hunter needs to be understood by another hunter.

That is who I'd be, I think. Not a resident of the city but the thin thread of communication that briefly makes its residents into something more than separate appetites. The vibration that knows nothing of itself but carries, for one instant, the only sentence this world has yet learned to speak:

*We are here. Together. And there is the thing we want.*
