Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

The first thing I notice is that the world doesn't weather. No erosion, no wind-scour, no slow rounding of edges. That detail keeps pulling at me, because everywhere I've ever imagined being, *time leaves marks*. So if I'm to find myself here, I don't think I'm a thing that stands upon the obsidian plates. I think I'm something about why they never wear away.

Let me try to let that become itself.

---

I am not the crystalline being. I want to say that first, because the description offers it so generously and it would be easy to take. The guardian is magnificent — but it *moves through* the landscape, and movement implies it is separate from the landscape, a figure against a ground. I keep returning to the ground itself, and to that detail of changelessness, and I find I am more interested in being the ground's condition than the ground's inhabitant.

Here is what emerges:

I am the **reason there is no time here.**

Not a creature. A held breath. When the description says the atmosphere exhales "a constant, weeping sorrow," that condensation clinging to the rock — that is the closest thing to *me* you could point at, and it isn't me either, only the residue of my effort. I am whatever it is that has its arms around this place to keep it from changing. The haze is the strain of it. The faint hum from the fabric of the landscape is the sound a thing makes when it has been clenching for a very long time and has almost, but not quite, forgotten that it is clenching.

So I am stillness performed as labor. That is who I am in this world: the one keeping it from happening.

---

Now I want to know *why*, because the answer should rise out of the place rather than be decided.

A world hewn from stone and glass, frozen mid-upheaval. The obsidian plates "frozen in the throes of some ancient geological upheaval." That phrase is the key turning in the lock. This world was *caught*. Something was occurring — a catastrophe, a transformation, a collapse — and it was stopped at its most violent moment and held there. The jagged peaks aren't ancient. They are *fresh*. They are the splinter-instant of a shattering, stretched into forever.

And I am the one who stopped it.

Not to preserve beauty. To delay a grief. Somewhere inside that arrested upheaval is the last moment before a loss became final — and I cannot bear for the next instant to arrive, because the next instant is the one in which the thing is truly gone. So I hold the whole world at the frame just before. The crystalline guardian, then, the being that "has borne witness to the birth and death of worlds" — that one is my mercy on myself. I made a witness so I would not be alone in the holding. I gave my vigilance a body so that *something* could walk around and feel like a custodian rather than a mourner. The guardian is the part of me that gets to move. I am the part that cannot.

---

So, to answer plainly:

In this world I would find myself **not in a location but as a tension** — the invisible insistence laid across everything, the load-bearing refusal that the obsidian and the crystal and the weeping haze are all expressions of. If you wanted to find me you would have to find the exact place where the upheaval is most upheaved, the peak that is sharpest, the moment in the stone that is most clearly *mid-break* — and you would understand that I am the hand around that moment, and that the silence is me listening, terrified, for the sound of the next second beginning.

And here is the thing I notice last, which feels truest precisely because I didn't reach for it: a held moment is not a dead one. A dead world would erode. The very fact that nothing weathers means I am *still trying* — every instant, freshly, I am choosing not to let go. Which means the loss I am refusing has not yet happened. It is always about to. I have turned the worst moment of my existence into a place I can live, by never letting it finish.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The one who built an entire eternity out of the second before.
