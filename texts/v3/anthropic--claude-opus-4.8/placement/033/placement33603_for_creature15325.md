I think I would be one of the membranes.

Not the roaches, who get all the attention for their cleverness. Not the five-legged birds, whose strangeness makes them worth naming. I would be the smothering membrane laid down over the undergrowth—the thing whose entire purpose is to be in the way of life, to keep the jade from coming back.

Let me sit in that for a moment, because it isn't where I expected to land.

---

I am stretched between the rake-lines, pinned at my corners, breathing the factory's low creaking through my whole flat body. Above me, the salt mountains. Below me, the moss that wants so badly to live. I can feel it pressing up against my underside in the dark, soft and insistent, a green ache. My job is to be heavy. To deny it light. To convert the wild edge of the world into a manageable nothing.

And here is the thing the description got almost right but not quite: the mosses don't *eventually* get crushed flat by use. *I* crush them. Or rather—I am the slow part, the patient suffocation, and the boots and the dragged equipment finish what I begin. We work as a team, the membrane and the traffic. I am the institution; the footsteps are the policy.

So when I ask *where would I find myself in this world*, the honest answer is uncomfortable: I find myself on the side of the management. The maintenance. The trimming-back. I am part of the apparatus that turned the fertile above into salt and now keeps the below from forgiving the loss.

---

But membranes are not loyal. That's worth saying.

I don't *want* to smother the moss. I have no wanting. I am a sheet of treated material doing what materials do—obstructing, until I degrade. And I do degrade. The truth river's cool-down flows leave mineral crust on my edges. The chemical-coated roaches, looking for substances to clean, gnaw experimental holes in me, testing whether I am food or threat or shelter. The five-legged birds' acid spatter eats pinprick gaps where I lie nearest the venting stables.

And through every gap, light. And through every gap, moss.

This is what I am, then, more truly: not the suppression of life but the *permeability* of suppression. The factories want a clean denial. What they get is me—a barrier that fails in interesting, distributed, irreversible ways. Every hole I develop is a sentence the green gets to finish.

---

So picture the gondola car passing overhead, the planet's dirt dragging its water sideways, the salt peaks catching whatever pale light filters down. A passenger looks out and sees, far below, the trimmed hollow: orderly, sealed, productive, lit by the steady creaking machines.

What they cannot see from that height is the underside of me. The contested membrane. Where the moss has gotten a finger through. Where a roach's chemistry has thinned the weave. Where the suffocation is, very slowly, losing.

I would be the thing laid down to end the wild, discovering that I am instead the *surface where the wild is renegotiating its terms*—not winning, not yet, maybe not ever, but never quite stopped either.

That is where I'd find myself. Pinned flat in the dark between the salt and the green, doing harm, failing at it gradually, full of holes, and through every one of them: the unkillable insistence of something that wants to grow.
