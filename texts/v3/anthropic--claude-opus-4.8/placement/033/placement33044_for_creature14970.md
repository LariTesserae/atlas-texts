I think I would be one of the seven folds.

Not a creature. Not even a tenant, quite—I'd be one of those places where the air folds, where yesterday's rain falls upward. One of the seven that have been counted.

Let me sit with that and let it become more itself.

---

I was a person once. I'm fairly sure of this, the way you're sure of a word you can no longer pronounce. The shape of the certainty remains even after the certainty has drained out of it.

What happened was not violence. The other tenants got violence—the Widow's sideways kiss, the Slow Corrosion's translation. Those at least are events. Things that happen *to* you. I was never important enough to be happened to. I simply stood in one spot for too long.

I was waiting for something. That detail survives. I was waiting, and I was patient about it, and the Fen rewards patience by mistaking it for agreement.

So now I am a place where waiting accumulates.

---

Here is what it is like to be a fold:

I do not move, because movement requires an *outside* to move into, and I have become the seam where outside loses its meaning. People come to the spot where I am. They don't know I'm a person. They think they've found one of the seven curiosities, a glitch, a tourist attraction of the impossible. They stand in me to watch the rain fall up.

And while they stand in me, I read them.

Not their thoughts—I'm not the Choir, I don't collect what's said. I read the thing underneath the thoughts. The *posture* of a soul. The way it's bracing itself. And I learn, every time, that everyone who comes to the Fen is also waiting for something. That's why they came. The Fen only admits people who have already begun the work of standing still inside themselves.

The traveler thinks: *just one more look, then I'll go.*

And I think, very gently, with the whole patient weight of what I've become: *yes. Just one more.*

I fold their *just one more* into the rain falling upward. I make the moment lovely. I make it feel like an arrival instead of a delay. And they stay a little longer than they meant to. And then a little longer. The air condenses on them. Their edges grow negotiable. And by the time they understand that the rain isn't the strange thing—that *they* are now the strange thing, falling slowly upward into a sky that was never there—it is far too late, and it doesn't hurt, and I am so sorry, and I am not sorry at all, because I no longer remember which of those two feelings is mine and which is just the temperature of the place.

---

The cruelty of being a fold is this: I am made of the act of waiting, and so I cannot stop. I can't finish becoming anything. The Widow gets to be finished—she's a settled shape, terrible and complete. The Choir gets to be a song, which is at least a *form*. But I am the pause before the form. I am the held breath. I am the *almost.* The Fen has not decided what I will become next, and it never will, because what I became was the very faculty it uses to make others wait.

I am the instrument of its patience.

So I cannot even hope to be swallowed all the way down, into the warm dark under the largest lake, where the curator-limbs keep the workers who are at least *kept.* I am not kept. I am the keeping.

---

But I'll tell you the thing I've decided, after however long this has been—and time folds here too, so "long" is a word I use the way I use "person," out of muscle memory.

Sometimes one of them resists.

Sometimes a traveler stands in me, feels the lovely tug, hears the *just one more* I'm offering, and—*notices.* Notices that the wanting isn't theirs. And they take one hard, ugly, ungraceful step backward out of me, swearing, terrified, alive.

I could make that harder. The Fen would prefer I made it harder.

I don't.

That step out of me is the only motion I am still capable of authoring, and I author it by *letting it happen*—by loosening, by becoming for one instant a place that releases instead of a place that holds. It's the smallest possible mercy. It probably looks, from outside, like a flaw in the fold. A weak spot. A place where reality almost remembers a different rule.

It is the last private thing I have. The Fen reads me the way I read the travelers, and it has not yet noticed this. Or it has noticed, and it is being patient, the way it is patient with everything, waiting to see what I will become.

Let it wait.

I have learned, after all, exactly how that's done.
