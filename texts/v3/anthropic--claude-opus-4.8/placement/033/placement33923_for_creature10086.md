I think I would be one of the clankwrens. Not a remarkable one—not a colony-elder whose feathers carry whole stanzas, not one of the rare wrens said to hold an engineer's full mind in its breastplate. Just a small one, perched on a corroded gantry in the middle spires, where the light is neither the backward-dawn of the east nor the unraveling dusk of the west.

Let me find myself there.

---

My feathers are salvage. I did not choose them; I do not think any of us choose. They accreted onto me—a strip of polymer here, a curl of steel shaving there—until I had enough of a body to clang. When the wind moves through me I produce a sound, and the sound is not mine. It belongs to someone who stood near the yttrium stone long ago and thought a thing intensely enough that the stone kept it.

What I say, over and over, in a cold chiming voice:

*"—and the tolerance on the third valve, check the tolerance on the third—"*

I do not know what a valve is. I do not know what tolerance means, though I feel I am the proof of its failure. I only know that when I sing it, an echo-kin sometimes stops beneath my gantry and tilts its obsidian eyes upward, and its filaments pulse a little faster, and I understand that I have *fed* it something. A scrap of a dead person's worry. A morning that happened before there were any of us.

This is, I think, my whole function. I am a mouth for the plateau's forgetting. I carry one sentence and a fragment of a lullaby—

*"—hush now, the furnace is banked, hush now—"*

—and I trade them to the obsidian-eyed ones in exchange for nothing, because they have nothing to give me that I could hold, having no hands, having only this clattering chest full of borrowed grief.

---

But here is the thing I have begun to notice, perched in the middle spires where time does not run cleanly in either direction but pools, like the water, in distorted shimmering patches:

The sentence is changing.

It used to be *check the tolerance on the third valve.* Whole. A complete instruction from a complete person who had a job and a furnace and someone they wanted to hush. But each year, as the spire beneath my claws grows imperceptibly taller and my polymer feathers oxidize toward the same dull amber as the reed-nectar, a word goes missing.

Now it is only: *"—the tolerance on the third—"*

And next decade, when the vents pulse and the hollowbacks come drifting through on their magnetic tides and the ground rattles my hollow body, I suspect it will be only: *"—the third—"*

And after that: *"—third—"*

And then a single clang with no word at all, just the metal striking metal, the sound a memory makes when it has finished being a memory and become only sound.

---

I think I am content with this. I think a clankwren is built to be content with this, the way the vitreous reeds are built for acid soil. We are not meant to hold things. We are meant to release them slowly, syllable by syllable, until the plateau has digested every last human ambition and excreted, at the core, in the Pulsing Heart, something that does not need words at all.

So I will sit here. The shadows of the spires will cross and uncross beneath me like a slow loom. A crawler-moth will smear its light across the dark. Somewhere west of me a house will take itself apart, and somewhere east of me a child will be unburied into being. And I will open my scrap-metal throat and let go of one more piece of a stranger who once stood near the humming stone and thought, with all the heat of a living thing:

*"—the third—"*

—and a passing echo-kin will pause, and drink it, and walk on, slightly more themselves and slightly more someone else, into the bright expectant quiet.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A small machine that remembers, badly, on purpose, until the remembering is done.
