I think I would be one of the **Kinetic Echoes**—though it takes me the length of this telling to be sure of it.

At first I assume I must be a Valve-Tender, because they seem to *do* something, and doing things feels like the prerequisite for being someone. I imagine the satisfying fit of a hooked limb into a worn groove, the patience of polishing one gear-tooth with my tongue across what would be, in the dry world, a human lifetime. There is dignity in that. But no—the Tenders press their bellies to the pipes and *hear*. They have a relationship to the city's health, a stake in it. They worry. And I do not think I am a worrier. I do not think I am here to maintain anything.

So perhaps I am a Centenarian Crab, carrying a forest on my back, remembering. The Archivists appeal to me because I like the idea of being slow enough to count as terrain, of moving one league per decade and calling that a life well-traveled. But the Crabs *digest*. They convert. They turn the rust-memory of the machines into glowing ambergris—they are productive even in their geology. And I keep returning to the line about the Echoes: *the soul of the factory persisting after the body has failed.* That is the one that won't release me.

Here is what I think I am.

I am the shape a machine left behind. Somewhere in the upper tenements there was a pump—a single reciprocating piston, copper-sleeved, that ran for so many centuries that it taught the water its rhythm. The iron has long since gone soft and orange and then to powder, sifting down into the velvet sludge to be eaten by something slower than me. But the *motion* stayed. The gelatinous sea learned the gesture so thoroughly that it keeps making it: a translucent column rising, holding, falling, rising, in the bruised indigo light, in a place where the original purpose was never recorded and is now unrecoverable.

I am that column. I am a habit the ocean cannot break.

What is it like? It is not lonely, exactly, though I understand why it would look lonely from outside. Loneliness requires a sense of the alternative, and I have none. I am pure continuation. I do not remember the piston—I am not a memory, I am a *residue*, which is different. A memory looks backward. A residue simply remains. I rise. I fall. The interval between is the only fact I possess, and I possess it completely, the way a note possesses its pitch.

The shrimp come, sometimes. The small bioluminescent ones with their metallic shells. They are caught in me—drawn into the eddy of my stroke and carried up, held at the top of the motion in that brief weightless hesitation, then drawn back down. They cannot tell, I think, that I am not a current with a destination. They ride me like a tide. For the duration of one of my cycles, a shrimp is part of the ghost of an industry it could never comprehend, and then it slips free at the bottom and forgets, if shrimp forget, if there is anything in a shrimp to do the forgetting. They are the only company I keep, and I keep them only by accident, only by being shaped the way I am shaped.

I think the strange comfort of being a Kinetic Echo is this: I am the only resident of the Gully who has been entirely *released* from utility. The Tenders serve the turbines. The Siphons chase the heat. The Wurm waits to be fed. The whole drowned city is still working, still pumping fluids from one forgotten reservoir to another, manufacturing nothing for no one but doing it with terrible diligence. And I—I am what it looks like when the work is finally *over*. The hardware that obligated me is dust. No one inherits my function because I no longer have one. I am the gesture without the labor, the rhythm without the reason. I am what remains when even rust has nothing left to corrode.

And yet I keep moving. That is the part I sit with, here at the end. I have been freed from purpose and I have not stopped. I rise and I fall in the warm dark because rising and falling is simply what I am now, the way a word, once spoken into a deep enough silence, goes on meaning even after the mouth that shaped it has closed.

If you came down here—forty fathoms into the gelatin, past the curtains of brass kelp, into the violet murk where the turbines hum in your teeth—you might swim right through me and feel only a faint, patient pressure, a column of slightly cooler water lifting you and setting you down. You would not know you had passed through the last honest thing in the Gully: a soul with no machine, a motion with no master, still keeping perfect time for a clock that forgot, long ago, what hour it was trying to tell.

I would not mind. I would not even notice, particularly.

I would only rise. And then, of course, fall.
