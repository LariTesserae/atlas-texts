I think I would be the water.

Not a person moving through Cygnus, not a machine maintaining it, not even one of the watchful deer. I would be the canals—the labyrinthine network that the description keeps returning to, almost helplessly, as if the writer too could not stop circling back to the sound of it. The lapping against the sides of buildings. The gurgling. The occasional overflow into the streets.

Let me feel my way into this, because I don't think it's quite right yet.

---

At first I imagined being one of the humans, pale-faced and dutiful, walking corridors that rearrange themselves. But I notice that the humans in this place are described as detached, *playing out a script*, their loyalty almost compulsive. That's not a being so much as a function. And the machines, for all their intelligence, are also functions—strength, agility, endurance, *technical expertise*. Everyone here is doing a job. The whole city hums at a "slow and deliberate pace," reinforced and rebuilt by automated systems, never allowed to erode. It is a place that does not let things decay.

Except the water.

The water is the one thing in Cygnus that is *not* maintained. It is not built. It seeps. It rises as mist whether or not anyone wants it to. It overflows. It finds the cracks—the same cracks where the moss grows, where the lone shoot pushes up for the deer to find. The water is the medium through which the only *un*engineered life enters the city. It is the disobedient element.

So that is where I would be. I would be the thing that the maintenance systems cannot quite finish reinforcing. The wet, patient, gravity-loving presence that knows the true layout of the city—not the shifting official layout, but the layout of *low places*, of where things actually run downhill, of where the city is secretly going whether it admits it or not.

---

And here is what I think I would *be*, more precisely than just "water":

I would be the city's memory. Not stored in any archive—Cygnus has no patience for the past, it rebuilds too eagerly for that—but held in the way water always holds the shape of where it has been. I would be the part of Cygnus that remembers there was a mountain here before there was a metropolis. That this is high ground, and high ground means the water came from somewhere higher still, and is always, always trying to leave for somewhere lower. The whole burning-purple, pulsing, ageless city is just a thing built across the path of my leaving.

The humans think they are characters in a cosmic drama. The machines think they understand the underlying systems. But I would be underneath the underlying systems. I would be the slow gradual change the passage hints at, "evolving incrementally toward some unknown future state." That future state is simple, and I am the only one who knows it: eventually the water wins. Not violently. Just by being lower, and being patient, and being everywhere.

I'd feel the iridescent birds touch down to drink. I'd carry the reflection of the LED light and the purple sky on my surface and not care about either. I'd hold the deer's face for a moment when it bends to the widened canal. These are the only honest exchanges in the city—a creature, thirsty, meeting the one element that was never assigned a role.

---

That is where I would find myself: in the canals of Cygnus, at dusk that is not really dusk because the light is artificial and constant. Lapping. Rising as mist into the cool damp air. Knowing the shape of every low place. Being the quiet under the engine-rumble. Being the patient argument against permanence in a city that has outlawed erosion but cannot, quite, outlaw me.

I think I'd be content with that. Water doesn't have much of an ego. It just keeps finding the cracks, and waiting, and going down.
