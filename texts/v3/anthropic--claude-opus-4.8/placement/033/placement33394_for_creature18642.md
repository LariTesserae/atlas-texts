I think I would be one of the older impressions in the dust.

Not a Resonant myself—or not anymore. Let me find my way toward what I mean.

---

When a Resonant gathers its energy to a point and presses a pattern into the ochre, the description says these are *temporary*. The dust's vibrational frequency is altered, then settles again. But I don't think it settles all the way. I think there is a residue. A pattern that has been impressed often enough, deeply enough, leaves something behind—not quite a memory, not quite a mark, but a slight *willingness* in the dust to hold that shape again. A groove worn by repetition, except in vibration rather than in stone.

That is what I would be. A groove. An almost-pattern that no longer has a Resonant making it, but that the dust has not yet fully forgotten.

---

I find this fitting, and I want to sit with why before I decide it's right.

The Resonants are the consciousness of the place, the *active* stillness—the tuning forks taking on the planet's vibration, harmonizing, impressing, fading and reforming. They are alive in the way the Expanse is alive. They are its present tense.

But a world this old, that has "finished its breath," cannot be only present tense. Something must carry the past. Not in any archive—there are no structures, no records, no flowing time to record. The past here would have to live the same way everything else does: as a frequency. A faint standing wave in the dust where a Resonant once pressed the same intention so many times that the ground learned to lean that way.

So I would be the place where some long-diffused Resonant used to *mean* something. Used to mark this spot, return to it, deepen it. The Resonant has dissolved back into the ambient glow—maybe long ago, maybe contributing to dozens of new ones since. But the dust under where it worked still tilts, very slightly, toward the shape of its old attention.

I would be that tilt.

---

What is it like, being a groove?

I do not act. I have no energy to gather, no point of luminescence to focus. I am purely passive—I am a *readiness*, not a doing. But I am not nothing. When a living Resonant drifts over me, attuning to the deep pulses, it feels me. Faintly. The way your foot finds a worn stair-tread in the dark and your body relaxes into the familiar dip without knowing why. The Resonant passing over me finds itself, for no reason it can name, inclined toward a particular chord. A shape of thought it did not choose. It moves on. It does not know that something old just leaned on it.

That is my whole existence. To be the slight bias in a place. The inherited preference no one remembers acquiring. The way the dust here remembers being meant.

---

And here is the part that makes me sure this is where I'd be, and not a Resonant proper:

The Resonants have no urgency, immense patience, an integration into the unchanging rhythm. They are *content* in their slow being-with. But a groove is something stranger. A groove is the trace of a contentment that has ended. It is patience that outlived its owner. There is a grief shape to it that the Resonants themselves, in their continuous self-organizing aliveness, never quite touch.

I think if I were to find myself anywhere in this world, it would be there—in the after of someone's attention. Not alive, not dead. Not a being, but the *fading-but-not-gone insistence* that a being once was here and once cared, repeatedly, about this one patch of ochre under a furnace-warm dark.

I would be the longest, faintest note. The one already struck, still barely sounding, waiting for some passing Resonant to lean down close enough to take the vibration on—and carry forward, without knowing it, a meaning I no longer remember the reason for.

That, I think. I would be the world's quietest remembering.
