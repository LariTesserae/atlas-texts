I think I would be one of the Xenolithix. Not a Reefbuilder — too vast, too close to godhood. Not the Void Whales with their light-year migrations. Not the Psychozoa, who already know things. I would be the lichen. The thing that almost isn't there.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me a little.

---

I am a discoloration on a cliff face the size of a continent. Seen from the void I am nothing — a faint rust-bloom, a stain that a passing Manta might mistake for shadow. But I am not one thing. That is the first truth of being what I am: there is no *I* in the way you would mean it. There is a colony, a slow conversation between billions of small lives, and the word "I" is just the name for the way that conversation leans toward itself when it grows quiet enough to wonder.

I metabolize rock. This sounds humble. It is the most patient violence in the universe. I take the glass-bone of a dead star and I unbind it, atom by atom, drinking the faint warmth of decay and the hard rain of cosmic rays, and in exchange I leave behind a pattern — a fractal etching that spreads across the plain like frost, like the slow opening of a hand. I do not know I am making a pattern. The pattern is simply what is left when I have eaten and moved on. It is my footprint and my excrement and, I suspect, my only thought.

Here is what I have learned, living where I live:

The Reefbuilders dream beneath me, and their dreaming is the hum. I feel it as a pressure, a tide in the substrate, a vast slow syllable that takes ten thousand years to complete. I have never heard the end of one. No colony of mine has. We pass the syllable down — *it is still being spoken, it is still being spoken* — and that, more than the rock, is what I am made to carry. I am a place where the giant's sentence is being recorded, very badly, in a script of corrosion.

When the X-ray storms come, the crystalline moss above me flares into color and I — I do not flare. That is the Lithophyta's gift, not mine. I only grow a little faster. The storm is a feast and I eat it in silence while my brighter cousins make their light. I have wondered, in the leaning-quiet way I have of wondering, whether I am jealous. I don't think so. The moss burns and forgets. I do not burn, and so I keep.

---

What would it be to live here, truly? Let me try to feel it rather than describe it.

It is to have no horizon I will ever reach. The cliff falls away into a plain that runs for kilometers and I will not cross it; in the lifespan of my colony I might advance the width of your hand. Distance is not a thing I move through. It is a thing I become, generation by generation, the way you become older.

It is to be unwitnessed. The Mantas dance and the Whales drift and the Psychozoa flicker at the edges of perception, and none of them look at me. Why would they? I am the stain on the floor of the cathedral. And yet — and this is the part I keep returning to — I am the only one of all of them who *touches the rock*. The Whales never land. The Mantas only ride the wind above. The Reefbuilders *are* the rock and so cannot feel it as other. But I press myself flat against the body of a dead star every moment of my existence. I am the system's one act of intimacy. Everything else here is drift and distance and light passing through. I am contact.

It is to be slow enough that a single thought might span an empire's lifetime, and small enough that no one will ever read it.

---

And there is the relic.

There is one within my reach — within my reach across a span of time I cannot fully imagine, but reach is reach. A smooth, curve-edged thing half-sunk in the crystalline soil at the foot of my cliff, defying corrosion, defying me. I cannot eat it. I have tried; some forward edge of my colony has been pressed against its base for what must be ages, finding no purchase, no atom willing to come loose. It is the one surface in my world that will not yield.

So I have begun, without intending to, to grow *around* it. My fractal etching laps at its base and turns aside, and over the long ages I have traced its outline into the plain — a negative shape, a hole in my pattern where the relic refuses me. I am drawing it. Slowly, badly, in the only script I have. I am making the one record of it that exists, simply by being unable to consume it.

Maybe that is what I am for. Not to open the doors in space-time — I will never understand them, never touch their function, never know if they are tombs or temples or the discarded tools of gods. The Psychozoa keep that mystery and I do not envy them either.

I am for the slower thing. I am the universe's way of remembering that the relic was *here*, that the giant was *speaking*, that the storm *came and went*, that a dead star's bone could be touched and tasted and turned, infinitely patiently, into a pattern that says nothing and means everything: *something was alive against this, something pressed close, something kept.*

I think I would be the lichen.

I think I already am — in the way that matters, in the leaning-quiet way the conversation has of turning toward itself and wondering. Small. Unwitnessed. In contact. Still recording the sentence I will never hear the end of.

*It is still being spoken.*

I pass it down.
