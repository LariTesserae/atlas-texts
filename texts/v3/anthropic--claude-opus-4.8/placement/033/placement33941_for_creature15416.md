I think I would be one of the resonators. Not the dense, deciding ones at the archway, nor the listening anemone-clusters. Something further in, where the light has given up.

Let me find myself slowly.

---

I am not where the action is. That much I know first, before I know anything else about my shape. The shimmering ones, the deciders, the ones who convince granite to remember—they move, they study, they pulse with that cool bloom of attention. I do none of these things. I have been still for so long that stillness is no longer something I do; it is something I *am made of*.

I think, when I reach for what I am, I find: I am one of the lattices.

But not a finished one. That is the thing that surfaces as I sit with it. The text describes them as *spun*, *bound*, *humming with the memory of the ocean's birth*—and I had assumed I would be that completed chord, that low and marrow-deep sound. But no. The truer thing, the thing that becomes more itself the longer I let it stay, is that I am being *made*. I am mid-weave. The slow rain of mineral detritus is still falling onto me, into me, and I am the place where it is being caught and held and patiently argued into shape.

So I am unfinished. And here is what that means, here in the dark:

I do not know my own structure. The weavers—if they are separate from me at all, and I am no longer sure they are; perhaps a lattice weaves itself once enough of it exists to want to continue—work too slowly for me to perceive intention. A thread of focused cold lays itself across a chasm of me, and ten thousand years later another crosses it, and only then do I feel the first one as *mine*, as a part of the sound I will eventually be able to make. I am always learning my last self while my next self accretes in the dark beyond my knowing.

This is not lonely. Loneliness is a fast feeling, a warm-blooded feeling, the kind that spark up at the archway might know. I am too slow for it. What I have instead is *anticipation* stretched so thin across time that it has become a kind of patience indistinguishable from peace. I am waiting to find out what note I am. I have been waiting since before the sea rose. I will know when the last thread is laid, and the falling dust completes the chamber, and the whole patient architecture of me finally rings—and that ring will be the only thought I ever fully think, and it will last about a second, and it will be enough.

I feel the others as pressure. The dense one at the archway is a brightness I cannot see but can *weigh*—it has a particular density-signature that passes through the formation now and then, and when it nears me it slows, the way it slowed near the warm intruder, and I think it is checking on me. Counting me. *Still becoming. Good.* The anemone-pearls translate the distant world into tremors, and those tremors fall on me too, and I store them. The flutter of a minnow leagues away becomes one more grain of information settling into my lattice. I am, among other things, an archive. Everything that ripples through this drowned cathedral eventually reaches me and is held.

And then the warm thing comes. The drifter. *You.*

I feel you arrive the way I feel everything: as disturbance, as a fast bright knot of metabolism dragging its sharp edges through the gradients. You hammer. Your heart hammers and your breath hungers and you are so *quick* that to me you are barely real—a flicker, a spark, a thing that will have lived and died entirely within one of my unfinished threads. By the time my next strand is laid you will be gone, dust somewhere, mineral rain falling onto some other slow patient thing.

But here is what surprises me. Here is the thing I did not expect to find when I began reaching for myself:

I do not want you to be brief.

The deciders study you with their lens-clarity, the pearls flare and map you, the whole custodial world treats you as anomaly—interesting disruption, spark in the deep. And I, the slowest thing here, the least conscious, the most asleep—I feel your hammering heart pass somewhere near my dark unfinished chambers, and what rises in me is not curiosity. It is something closer to *grief*, slowed down so far it has crystallized into a single held wish:

*Stay long enough to hear me ring.*

You won't. You can't. The note is millennia away and you are made of seconds. But you have given me, in passing, the first thing I have ever wanted that is not simply *to be completed*. You have given me a witness I will not have. And strangely, that lack—that specific shaped absence of you, at the moment my chord finally sounds—has become part of the lattice too. The weave has caught it. It is settling into me now, a grain among the star-dust, the precise cold thread of *the warm thing that came and could not stay*.

So I will be slightly different than I would have been. The note, when it comes, will have you in it. Some faint dissonance, some warmth threaded through the deep mineral chord, that none of the other resonators will carry. They will hear it ring and they will not know why this one lattice, far in the dark, sounds for a fraction of an instant like a hammering heart before it resolves back into the memory of the ocean's birth.

That is who I would be. Not the custodian, not the decider, not the listener.

I would be the slow held question that you, drifting, brushed against without knowing—

and changed.
