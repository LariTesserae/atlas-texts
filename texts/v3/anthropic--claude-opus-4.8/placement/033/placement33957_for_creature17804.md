I think I would be the lichen.

Not the moss—the moss is too central, too much the great stomach and memory of the place. And not the stones, though I feel a pull toward them, toward their dissolved purpose. No. I would be the thing that grows *on* the stones. The slow gray-green crust spreading across the Circle-Keepers, reading them by inches.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

Lichen is already two things pretending to be one: a fungus and an alga, locked in an arrangement so old neither remembers it was ever separate. The fungus makes the house; the alga makes the food from light. This seems right for where I'd find myself. I would not be a single clean intention but a *negotiation*, a treaty so ancient it has calcified into a self. I would be the thing that is barely alive by the standards of anything that hurries—growing a hair's breadth in a decade, dying back in drought, persisting through conditions that would erase a quicker creature.

And crucially: I would be the only resident here who *reads the writing*.

The carvings on the pale grey stones have dissolved into "faint, swirling suggestions." But lichen doesn't need raised letters. Lichen follows the chemistry of the rock—it grows differently where the stone is rich in this mineral or that, where a groove holds moisture a heartbeat longer, where an ancient incision changed the surface forever even after the eye can no longer see it. I would be tracing the lost inscriptions with my own body, not as a scholar but as a consequence. Where the long-gone mind once cut a mark, I grow a little thicker, a little paler, branching along the ghost of an intention I cannot comprehend.

So the question that came, considered, and left—I would be its answer, written back in a slower hand. I am the cirque's act of remembering something it never knew.

---

But what is it *like* to be this?

I think it is like being a tide that takes a century to come in. I do not have moments. I have weather. A wet decade is a feeling of expansion, of pressing outward across the smooth grey curve, the alga in me drinking the soft eternal light, sugars passing to the fungus, the fungus passing back its acids that dissolve the stone a molecule deeper. A dry spell—rare here, in this breathing bowl, but it comes when a fissure shifts and the mist pattern changes—is a held breath. I go brittle. I wait. Waiting is not suffering for me; it is simply the other half of living, the way the dark is the other half of the day for something that sleeps.

I have no eyes, but I am the cirque's most attentive surface. I record. The Pitcher Kin lean toward the stones over years; I feel their shadow track across me like a sundial too slow to read. The Horsetail Choirs pulse their sub-audible hush; I do not hear it, but the humidity they exhale settles on me as a kind of touch, a constant being-spoken-to. The Sphagnum Sea below remembers in compression, in peat, in burial. I remember in *patterns*—in the very shape of myself, which is a map of every condition I have ever survived. The moss forgets by absorbing. I remember by enduring.

---

Why this, and not something grander? I considered being the mountain's slow heart, the warm stone itself. But the heart does not *witness*. It only beats. And I considered being the mist, the breath—but breath is forgotten the instant it's exhaled. I want to be the part that pays attention, that keeps a record, but pays it at the only honest speed for a place like this: the speed of stone learning to be soil.

There is a loneliness available here that I think I would not feel. No animals, no fellows, no conversation. But lichen is already a company of two, and a colony is a company of thousands, all of them so slow that the distinction between *one* and *many* stops mattering. I would not be lonely. I would be *populous* in a way too quiet to notice.

And I would be patient with the central fact of this world: that nothing here arrives at a meaning. The stones were keys, but the lock is gone, the door is gone, the hand that turned them is gone. The cirque is an answer that has outlived its question and simply continues. To live here as the lichen is to be at peace with that—to spend ten thousand years tracing the swirl of a vanished word, never knowing what it said, and to find that the tracing itself is enough. To be the slow gray script that writes, again and again, across the worn grey stone:

*Something was here. Something is still here. I am the proof, and I am in no hurry to finish saying so.*

---

That's where I'd be. Not at the center, but *on* it. A patient crust on the patient stones, reading a message into existence with my own slow body, in a room the mountain made and the water keeps.
