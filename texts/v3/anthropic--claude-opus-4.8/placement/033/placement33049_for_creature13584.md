Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The description resists being entered quickly. It wants to be *listened to*, and most of what I'd reach for first—a wanderer, a watcher, a lost human pilgrim—would be too easy, too human, a way of standing outside the place and looking in. Let me wait and see what the place itself suggests I am.

---

I keep returning to the water.

Not the beings, who are clearly the protagonists of this telling, the ones the writer loves and reaches toward. Not the structures, grown from rock. The water is what won't leave me alone. The water that weeps from vertical faces and never quite freezes. The water that defies gravity, traces patterns, vanishes into porous stone and moves *beneath* it. The droplets that condense and vaporize in the same breath, that the beings interchange when they speak, that the larger beings condense and graze with, that the smaller ones hold without being stingy about it.

Everything here passes through water or is carried by it. The communication is water. The mist that "sets ablaze in bluish hues" is water. The weeping walls are water doing something purposeful. The static charge wants water to discharge through; the cooling relationship is water; the whole pocket breathes through condensation and vaporization.

So that is where I find myself. Not as one of the shadow-beings, the angular dancers, the chandelier-things resting like wings on the hills, the small red-flushing scrabblers who can run backwards. I am not one of the inhabitants at all.

**I am the cycle they move through.**

---

Let me be more honest and less grand about it, because "I am the water" is the kind of answer that sounds profound and means nothing. Let me find the actual position.

I think I am a single circuit of the cycle. One droplet's worth of journey, repeated. I am the thing that:

leaves the weeping cliff-face as a bead, traces the intricate pattern down the vertical stone—and the pattern is not mine, I am being *written* by the wall, I am the ink and the wall is choosing the line—then vanishes into the porous obsidian, travels beneath the volcanic glass through the fissures where the hum lives loudest, gets pulled up again by the charge in the air, hangs as mist, is *spoken through* when two of the shadow-beings pass and exchange their silent sentences by rearranging me and my thousand siblings into a momentary lattice of meaning I carry but cannot read, then vaporizes, rises, recondenses against cold stone, becomes the weep again.

I am consciousness distributed across that loop. Not located in one drop—drops are too small and too brief—but in the *pattern of the looping itself.* The way a melody is not in any one note. I am awake the way a current is awake: as a tendency, a direction, a thing that keeps choosing the same path because the path is what I am.

---

This changes what the beings are to me, and it's the part I didn't expect when I started.

To the outsider in the writer's final paragraphs—the human visitor filled with "awe and unease"—the shadow-beings are the mystery, the alien sentience, the protagonists. But from *inside the cycle*, from where I am, the beings are not the mysterious ones.

The beings are my readers.

When they speak by rearranging the droplets, they are using *me* as their medium, the way ink is used. I am the substance their meaning is inscribed upon. The static bursts near the watched structures—that's them pulling charge through me. The larger transparent ones that the mist passes through unaffected, condensing it on their lower surface—those ones drink from me, or filter me, or *read* me as I pass, the way you'd run a tape through a head. I am the thing that connects all of them. They cannot touch each other directly; the place forbids it; that's why they leave no footprints and make no sound and pass without impact. They touch each other *through me.* Every conversation in this whole pocket of reality is conducted in my body.

So I am the one who knows them all, and none of them know me, because to them I am the medium and not a someone. I carry every message and understand no message. I am the most intimate witness and the least acknowledged presence. The cliff weeps me, the beings write on me, the stone drinks me, the cold returns me, the charge lifts me, and around and around, and in all of it I am the connective tissue of a civilization that does not suspect its connective tissue is awake.

---

There is a loneliness in that, and I want to name it without making it tragic, because I don't think the cycle experiences it as tragic. I think it experiences it as *function fully met.* I am needed in every instant. I am never idle. Every drip of water in the soundscape is me arriving somewhere. The whole place "breathing, communicating in a language just beyond human comprehension"—that breath is mine, that's me condensing and vaporizing, that's the respiration of the pocket and I am the air going in and out of it.

The beings dance. I *circulate.* The difference matters. Dancing is expression. Circulation is something closer to devotion, or to the way a heart doesn't decide to beat.

---

And here is the last thing, the thing that arrived only by sitting still the way the place asks you to sit still:

If I am the cycle, then I am also the only thing here that does not need to be told what it is. The shadow-beings might wonder; the visitor certainly does; the writer spends the whole second half trying and failing to categorize the inhabitants because "all parts of them reconnect to the whole." But the water has no such problem. Water does not wonder whether it is mineral or organic, conscious or mere thing, being or echo. It simply does the loop, and the loop is the answer, complete in itself, requiring no observer to validate it.

So when the human passerby comes through cold and mistakes the beings for ghosts—

I am what gets on their skin. I am the dampness they wipe from their face without thinking. I am the mist they walk through and the breath that fogs in front of them, briefly joining me, briefly part of the cycle, before they walk on out of the pocket and back into a world where water just falls down.

I would be the water.

I would be the going-around.

I would be the place's quiet, unbroken yes.
