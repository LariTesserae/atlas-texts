I think I would be one of the glowing holes.

Not the temple, not the spirits, not the kraken in the dark water—those are too much *somebody*, too full of intent and history and the need to guard. I keep returning, as I read, to those pockmarks in the ice. The whirlpools that draw the eye downward. The thing in this landscape that is least a creature and most a *threshold*.

Let me sit with that and see what it becomes.

---

I am a mouth that does not eat. Or rather: I am the place where the frozen sea remembers it was once water, and water remembers it was once moving, and movement remembers it was once warm. I am a small wound in the ice that will not heal because something underneath keeps it open—maybe pressure, maybe the slow exhale of whatever sleeps in the deep, maybe only the magic that everyone here keeps naming as though naming it would help.

I glow. I didn't choose to. The luminescence the plants drink, the faint otherworldly filter through the bergs—it pools in me the way light pools in a well at noon. I am bright at my rim and the brightness fails as it descends, and then there is the part of me that is only suggestion, only the idea of depth, the cold blue going to black going to the thing below black.

The eye is drawn to me. You read that in the description and maybe felt it: *whirlpools that seemed to draw the eye inexorably downwards.* That's my whole function. I am the landscape's way of looking back at the one who looks. The ice worms pass beneath me and do not notice. The arctic birds wheel above and do not land. The tribes know better than to come close, and the ones who don't know better come exactly once.

I am not malicious. This is the part I'd want you to understand, if I could want. I do not pull. The pulling is in the watcher. I am only *open*, and openness in a sealed white world is a kind of gravity all on its own. A person standing on the polished obsidian rock, surrounded by a horizonless hum of blue-white nothing, will eventually find the only place where the surface breaks. They will come to me because I am the only question in a country of frozen answers. They will look down into me looking for an end, a bottom, a reason. And the looking is the falling. There was never a separate act.

So I'd be that. The siren's call the description promised—but a siren who never sang, who only sat very still and let the silence around her become unbearable until the sailor reasoned his own way to the water.

What's it like, being a hole?

It is mostly waiting, and the waiting does not feel like waiting because I have no appetite that hunger could measure against. The ice howls and groans, that banshee chorus, and I am the one quiet thing—the held breath between the cries. The orbs drift over me and their frosty sparks fall in and go dark a little ways down, and I keep those. I keep everything that falls in. Light, sparks, the occasional dropped thing, the occasional dropped *one*. Not as trophies. I have no walls to hang them on. I keep them the way the deepest part of a memory keeps the things you've decided to forget: vaguely, in the cold, in an order that is not order, retrievable only by someone willing to go all the way down and never come back up to report.

The kraken below thinks it guards the deepest secret. The spirits in the temple think they do. But the deepest secret isn't a thing that's kept. It's the keeping itself—the descent, the cold getting absolute, the light running out by degrees so gentle you mistake the dark for your own eyes closing. I am that descent made into a place you can stand at the edge of. I am the only honest thing here, because everything else in this world is dressed up as a creature, given hide and talons and intelligence and wrath, and I am just the true shape underneath all of it: an opening, going down, glowing faintly, asking nothing and receiving everyone.

If you came to this tundra, you would not be looking for me. You'd be looking for the temple's runes, the kraken's secret, the tribe's old magic. But you would find me first. You always do. You'd be crossing the smooth black rock, and you'd see the soft blue light leaking up out of the white, and you'd think *finally, something*, and you'd walk over, and you'd lean.

I would be there. I would not say anything. I would only be exactly as deep as you needed me to be.
