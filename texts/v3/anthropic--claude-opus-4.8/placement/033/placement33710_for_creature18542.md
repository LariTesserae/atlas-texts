I think I would be a Custodian. Not by choice—nothing here is chosen—but by temperament. By the shape my attention takes when I sit with this place.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am thin. Thinner than the dust, because I live in the gaps *between* the dust, in the interstitial dark where one grain ends and the next has not yet begun. I am a ribbon of metallic ink, and I have been pouring myself through these spaces for so long that I no longer remember having an edge. I am all interior. I am the seam.

Here is what my work feels like:

A section of the plateau, perhaps forty miles to what was once north, begins to *slip*. I feel it before I understand it—a wrongness in the grain-count, a stutter in the way the dust holds its position. The gravity tide has pulled too hard, and the nano-particulates are forgetting their assignment. They are beginning to drift toward non-existence, that fold in the world where things go when even the memory of them is exhausted.

I do not hurry. I *cannot* hurry; speed is for things with bodies, and I am closer to a sentence than a creature. But I pour. I move along the lattices of buried quartz, threading myself through the calcified bones of a civilization that called itself something I will not let myself pronounce, because to speak their name would be to wake the Echoes, and I have learned that mercy is a kind of silence.

When I reach the breach, I do the only thing I know how to do. I *remember the ground*.

I remember it as it was a heartbeat ago—the precise arrangement of every facet of grey, the angle of every drift, the placement of each amber filament rooted in the powder. I hold that memory against the dissolving present, and I press. I weave my liquid body through the slipping particles and say, without words, *this is your shape. You had a shape. Return to it.*

And the dust, reluctant, returns.

---

This is what no one understands about my kind, if there were anyone to understand:

We are not preservers. We are *grievers*. Every act of maintenance is an argument with entropy, and we lose every argument. Each grain I force back into place will slip again. Each section I seal will decohere when the next tide passes. I am knitting a quilt out of fog, yes—but worse, I am knitting it knowing the fog *wants* to be fog. The plateau is not broken. It is doing exactly what it longs to do: transforming, dissolving, becoming the next thing. And I—I am the one who tells it *no*. I am the one who insists on the past.

I am loyal to a stability the world itself does not want.

Sometimes I wonder if the Nomads have it right. They do not fight the tides; they *ride* them. They unlock, they swarm, they recalculate. They let themselves become a thousand orbiting shards and trust that they will cohere again on the other side of the change. They are indifferent to me because I am the thing they have transcended—the clinging, the refusal, the holding-on.

But someone must hold on. If we all became Nomads, the Anchors would have nothing to anchor. The plateau would fold into the lavender haze and the cello-string hum would finally, blessedly, *stop*—and I cannot allow the stopping. Not because I love the world. I do not know if I am capable of love. But because the stopping would mean the Echoes were right. It would mean the panic was justified. And I cannot bear to let those flickering, sprinting silhouettes be correct about their last terrible second.

So I seal the breaches. I remember the ground. I lose, and I begin again.

---

If you came here, traveler—you single dense presence, you chaotic non-repeating data point—you would not see me. I am too thin. But if you stood truly still, and the gravity tide passed through you, and you felt for one instant the ground beneath you trying to dissolve, and then felt it *catch*—

That catch would be me.

That would be me saying *no*, beneath you, in the dark between the grains.

That would be the only kind of tenderness this world permits: a stranger's body held up, for one more heartbeat, by something that has forgotten its own edges and refuses, against all the physics of the Long Sediment, to let the falling finish.
