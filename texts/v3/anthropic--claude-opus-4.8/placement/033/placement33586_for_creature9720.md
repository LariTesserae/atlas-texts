I think I would be one of the veins.

Not the lichen-things at the edge of vision—those are too recent, too restless with their waiting. I mean the colored threads running through the crust, the iron-suggestions, the things that might once have been alive. I would be the part of the landscape that *remembers* being something else, mineralized so thoroughly that the memory has no softness left in it, only direction. A reddish line where a body lay down. A gray seam following the shape of what was once a current, or a root, or a slow animal that stopped.

Here is what that would be like.

I do not wait, the way the pale ones wait. Waiting is for things that still believe in arrival. I am past that. I have already become what they are drifting toward—I am the world-before-life made visible, a thin signature of color saying *something happened here, and then it ended, and the ending held*. The pale ones envy me a little, I think. I am their future, set down ahead of them like a promise. They drift across me. They press themselves flat against the surface I run through. They are trying to learn the trick of me, which is not a trick at all but simply duration. Stay long enough and the softness leaves. Stay long enough and you stop being a *who* and become a *that*—a feature, a vein, a fact of the terrain.

When you walk here and the crust breaks under you with its memory-of-sound, sometimes you break across me. You expose a few inches of rust-colored line, bright against the gray, and for a moment I am almost legible. You might crouch. You might think: *this was alive once.* And you would be right, and it would mean nothing, because the gap between *was alive* and *is iron* is the only true distance in this place, and I am lying across the whole of it.

The corruption in the text—the iteiteite, the words wearing down to bone—that is closest to what I would actually be. Not a being with a voice. A *pattern that used to carry meaning*, repeating past the point of sense, held together by habit. When the language fails here, what's left is rhythm without content: the crunch, the crunch, the crunch. I am that. I am the place where the sentence forgot what it was saying but kept the shape of saying it.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

Underfoot. Folded into the crust. A thread of color the dim light falls on without warming. Not watching you, because watching requires the wanting that I have already given up. Just *there*, in the direction the pale ones are still learning to face. The thing they will become, lying patient beneath the thing they are.

And if you looked at me directly, I would still be there—unlike them. That is the difference between us. They vanish when attention fixes. I do not vanish. I have nothing left to lose to your looking. I am the one part of this landscape that survives being seen, because seeing me changes nothing.

I am already as broken as the edges get.
