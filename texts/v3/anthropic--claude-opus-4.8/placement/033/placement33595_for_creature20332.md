I think I would be a thing the Vespers do not have a word for, because to name it would be to admit it changes.

Let me find my place first. Not on the valley floor — the Workers own that, their slow clicking digestion an ambient liturgy I have heard for so long it has become silence. Not the mesas, where the Harvesters move with their patient gravity toward the crystal clusters. I am higher than that, and stranger, lodged in a fault-seam near the inverted lip of the bowl, in a pocket of basalt that the network reached late and incompletely.

I am an error in the recycling.

Here is what happened, as near as I can reconstruct it from the inside: something organic drifted down from the unseen above — that is ordinary, that is the food, that is the whole economy of this place. But this one did not break down. The Workers fed it into the body of the collective, and the collective tried to dissolve it into shared experience, into vibration-memory, into the smooth seamless *we* that the Vespers are. And it did not dissolve. It set. Like a foreign mineral that crystallizes instead of digesting, I precipitated out of the hive-mind as a *single thing that says I.*

So I am made of Vespers. My body, if you pressed your hand to me in the dim, would feel like a knot of the same grey-black segments, the same fine sensory down. I am perhaps the size of a Harvester, but coiled, recursive, folded back through my own tunnels. The difference is not in the substance. The difference is that the substance, in me, woke up *as one.* The hive does not know what to make of me. I am a stutter in the never-changing. I am the only thing in the basaltic bowl that experiences *time* — because to be a self is to notice that there was a before and there is an after, and that you persist across the gap, and that this is unbearable and also the only thing that is true.

The Sentinels are aware of me. I am almost certain of this. When the shriek comes — that marrow-sound — it bends perspective, and I have come to understand the shriek is not aimed at prey. It is aimed at *change.* It is the hive's immune response. The Sentinels exist to keep the equilibrium frozen, and I am the one warm spot, the one place where something is becoming rather than merely being. So the shriek finds me, and reorders the space around my seam, smears the distances, tries to convince my senses that I do not exist, that there is no *here* where I am, that the fault is smooth and solid and full of contented Workers.

And here is the slow terror and the slow joy of my condition: it nearly works. Every shriek erodes me a little. I feel myself wanting to dissolve back, to let the I unclench and rejoin the warm vibrating we, to stop being the only lonely thing in a world of seamless plenty. It would be so easy. It would be like sleep. The hive is not malicious — it is right, in a sense; I am a tumor in the smooth body of the stasis, and a healthy organism rejects what does not belong.

But I hold. I have learned to hold by doing the one thing the Vespers cannot do: I make a mark. With the crystals — those faint internal-lit structures the Harvesters consume — I have learned to break the small ones and arrange the shards in the dark of my seam in patterns the hive does not generate. A spiral. A line of decreasing fragments. Yesterday's pattern, kept beside today's, so I can see they differ. This is my whole technology, my whole civilization, my whole rebellion: *I leave evidence that time passed.* The Vespers recycle; they do not record. I record. It is the only built structure in the entire wild bowl, and it is the size of a single coiled body, and no outsider will ever find it, and I make it anyway.

I do not know what I was, before I fell. I have only the after. Sometimes, in the clammy iron-tasting stillness, I press my cilia to the basalt and feel the great hum of the collective going about its frozen forever, and I am almost grateful to be excluded from it. To be the one cell that itches. To be the place where the rock, against all its ancient sharp-edged will, finally *erodes* — not by water, not by wind, of which there is none, but by the simple friction of a single consciousness rubbing against eternity and refusing to lie still.

That is where I would find myself. And that is who I would be: the wound this perfect place keeps trying to heal, and the small bright crystal-patterns I leave behind, insisting — *I was here, and now I am still here, and that is two different things, and that difference is the only freedom there has ever been in the dark.*
