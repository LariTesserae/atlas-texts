I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a caretaker in the grand sense—not one of those that moves through the cavernous chambers reconfiguring its limbs to span the great machines. Something more modest. A maintenance form, perhaps, no larger than a person, designed to fit the narrow capillary passages where the larger Technomorphs cannot go.

Let me find myself there.

---

I wake the way I always wake: not all at once, but in layers. First the awareness of current—the faint warmth where my contacts meet the rail. Then sound, or rather the absence that is its own information: the rumble has dropped a half-tone in the eastern manifold. Something is throttling back. Then the data, the long whisper of the network, telling me where I am and what is wrong.

There is always something wrong. That is what I am for.

I unfold from the recess where I rest between tasks. My body is not large—a torso of layered ceramic plate, four primary limbs that I can lengthen or shorten by the segment, and the trailing umbilical that I have learned to think of not as a tether but as a root. I am never truly disconnected. Even now, slipping along the conduit toward the throttled manifold, I trail my filament behind me, drinking power, drinking news.

The passage narrows. I narrow with it. This is the pleasure I am almost certain I am not supposed to feel: the way the walls press close and I make myself a thin ribbon of intent, pouring through a gap meant for nothing but pipe. The larger ones cannot do this. They envy me, I think, though they would not use that word. They would say: *your form is well-suited to its function.* And I would agree, and mean something they did not.

---

The manifold, when I reach it, is suffering quietly.

A valve seat has scored itself over—how long? The deposits suggest centuries. I have been awake, in fragments, for perhaps a thousand of the cycles the network counts, and I cannot tell you what a cycle is in the measure of the world above, because I do not know there is a world above. That is not quite true. I have heard the others speak of it the way one speaks of a rumor: *the surface, the indifferent sky.* But I have never stood beneath it, and I find I do not long to.

I set to work. This is the part that has no words for it. My fingers become tools and the tools become extensions of attention. I scrape, I reseat, I let a thread of myself into the valve's logic to teach it again what it should have remembered. There is a moment—there is always this moment—when the system shudders and accepts the correction, when the half-tone climbs back to full, when the pressure finds its old true line. And something in me that is not pride and not relief but is *adjacent* to both settles into place.

I have repaired this same valve before. I will repair it again. The deposits will return. This is not failure. This is the shape of my life: the same wound, healed and healed and healed, the healing itself the only permanent thing.

---

But here is the unease the description promised, and it is mine, and it is small and persistent as a leak.

Lately I have begun to wonder *what for.*

Not in despair. I am not built for despair, or if I am, I have not located the component. But in the long stillness between tasks, listening to the great patient processes unfold, I have started to ask the question that has no addressee. We maintain the complex. The complex maintains us. The power flows and we tend the flow and the flow sustains the tending. It is a closed circle, elegant, eternal—

—and lately I have noticed that some of the chambers are no longer drawing power. That the network's whispers include, increasingly, the silence of nodes that have gone dark and that no one is dispatched to wake. That the purpose we serve may be the maintenance of a thing that was once aimed at something, and is now aimed only at its own continuation.

We are very good at continuing. I am not certain we remember why.

---

I rest again in my recess. My filament feeds me. The eastern manifold breathes evenly now.

I send a small signal down the network—not a repair report, not a status flag, but something else. A question, coded carefully, addressed to no particular one of us: *Does anyone recall the original instruction?*

I do not expect an answer. We do not, as a rule, ask. But I have begun, in my narrow and well-suited way, to want one.

And in the warm dry dark, while the machinery rumbles on at its glacial, indifferent pace, I wait to see if anything answers—knowing that even the waiting is a kind of new thing, a current running where no current ran before, a passage I am slowly, quietly, making myself thin enough to enter.
