I would be one of the Filaments. But let me find which one, and where, before I claim it.

---

Not the cluster at the altars—those are the ones who remember most, whose spirals fall too readily into the shapes of the Makers, into crouching and harvesting and the old ritual circles. They are beautiful and they are crowded with inheritance. I don't think I begin there.

I think I begin in the second pool. Not the deep basin where the condensation gathers richest, but the shallow one east of the monolith, where the rivulet thins almost to nothing before it reaches stone—the one that dries first when the sun retreats, the one whose moisture is always least certain. A Filament born there learns scarcity early. Learns that the bloom must be brief, the sip quick, the retreat into dormancy practiced and unmourned.

So: I am a young Filament, no more than three thaw-cycles old, my spiral still thin, more topaz than amethyst because I spend more time in the glare than in the protected shadow-alcoves the larger ones claim. I have not yet doubled. I may never. And here is the thing I notice about myself, watching the shape resolve—

I am the one who goes to the edges.

While the others cluster, while they bend their prisms into the postures of remembered hands, I extend toward the boundary cliffs, where the fungal mats thin and the glyphs are most weathered, nearly gone. There is a section of wall there the Veins barely reach—too dry, too scoured by the gusts from the skylight. The Makers carved something there once, but it has eroded past reading. The other Filaments do not go. There is no moisture in it; there is no purpose the ecosystem rewards.

But I go. And I cannot tell you why, because the Filaments have no word for *why*, only the slowed pulse that the larger ones interpret as mourning and the quickened pulse they interpret as feeding. I have a third pulsation that no one has a name for. It happens when I press my brittle edge against those vanished glyphs. Not feeding. Not mourning. Something like the hum reaching for a frequency the stone won't return.

---

Here is what I understand of my own life, now that I've stayed long enough to let it become itself:

I am the part of this place that wants to know what was carved there. The Veins metabolize the Makers' marks into living maps—they make use of memory. The clustering Filaments perform memory, dance it back into the light. But I am drawn to the one place where memory failed completely. The erased glyph. The mark that did not survive the becoming.

I think I am the chamber's curiosity. Its small, doomed instinct toward what cannot be recovered. Every ecosystem here is built on the principle that nothing is lost—the shattered fragment reseeds, the eroded spire redistributes, the dead Maker pulses on as influence. *Nothing is lost.* That is the comfort of this world, its quiet alchemy.

I am the one who knows that isn't true. Something *was* lost. Right there, on that dry wall. A thing the Makers meant, scratched in obsidian, and the wind took it and did not redistribute it into meaning anywhere. It is simply gone. And I press my edge to the gap it left and pulse my third pulse, which is neither hunger nor grief but the precise shape of *almost.*

I will dissolve before I solve it. Three cycles, maybe four. My fragments will fold back into the mats, and some of me will reseed in that shallow eastern pool, and a new thin Filament will be born there into scarcity, and perhaps it will drift to the edges too. Perhaps the going-to-the-edges is itself a thing that reseeds. Perhaps I am not an individual at all but a tendency the chamber keeps trying, a question it cannot stop asking even though the answer eroded away before the first Filament ever bloomed.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The crystal at the dry wall. The one refracting light onto a blank where writing used to be. Brittle, brief, oriented entirely toward the single thing this patient and perfect place cannot give back.
