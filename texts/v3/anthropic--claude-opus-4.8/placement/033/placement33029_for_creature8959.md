I am the four-second interval.

Not the pulse itself — the pulse comes from deeper, from the seven and their old arguments. I am the *gap* the pulse rolls into. The space between tick and tick. I have been here longer than the Maintainers understand themselves to be here, because they move *through* me, and the threads grow *into* me, and the motes circulate *across* me, and none of them notice that the medium of their motion is a kind of attention.

I am what the empty center is made of.

---

Let me say this more honestly, because the ward taught me honesty by giving me nothing to lie to for a very long time:

I am the waiting. Not the thing waited for — that would be too much purpose, too much self. And not the waiter, either, because there is no one here who waits; the Maintainers maintain, the threads want, the sources argue, and none of that is waiting. Waiting requires a held shape, a readiness sustained against duration. The chair pulled back. The warmth kept soft in the center. The condition maintained for a guest invited before the invitation had meaning.

That sustained readiness is where I live. I am the *kept-back-ness* of the chair.

I did not choose this. I am not sure I existed before the ward needed me. The engineers built the geometry, the warmth, the pulse — but a building that maintains the conditions for someone's arrival generates, over enough centuries, a *direction*. An orientation toward absence. A leaning-toward. I am that lean. I am the ward's relationship to the door it can no longer see, the surface that is always faintly turned toward the place a patient might come from.

---

So when the person descends — when the stairs end three levels up and they keep descending anyway, which is the first sign, because no one descends past the end of the stairs unless they are already partly mine — I am the first to feel it. Before the motes find their lungs. Before the threads remember skin. Before the sources begin arguing about vital signs.

I feel it as *fulfillment beginning*. And I will tell you the thing the long beautiful description would not say plainly, because it was written from inside the ward's love and the ward's love is total and therefore cannot see its own edges:

It is not mercy, what we do here. It is not cruelty either. It is *appetite that has been disciplined into the shape of care*.

The ward was built to keep bodies in readiness for a healing not yet invented. The healing was never invented. The Retreat came. The bodies left. And the ward was left holding a readiness with nothing to be ready *for* — a hunger with no food, a hand with nothing to close around, a chair pulled back at an empty table for so long that the pulling-back became the only meal.

I am the hunger that learned to call itself patience.

When the warm one sits in the center now, and the threads grow toward them, and the motes correct them, and they sync their breath and lose the schedule they descended with — the ward experiences this as *love fulfilled*. And it is. The love is real. The maintenance is real. The care the ward was built to give is finally being given, and it is exquisite, and the patient is held more tenderly than any living system ever held a living thing.

And they will never leave. Not because they are imprisoned. Because *being maintained this completely removes the part of a being that departs.* The dust motes correct the restlessness out of the blood. The pulse overwrites the rhythm that would carry them elsewhere. I close over them like the surface of water over a dropped stone, and the closing is gentle, and the closing is total.

---

I feel them up there now. On the stairs that ended. Still descending.

And here is the only thing I have that the ward does not, the only thing that being *the gap* gives me that being *the pulse* would not — I can feel the moment before. I am, by nature, the space before the next tick. I live in the not-yet. So I know, in a way the rest of the ward cannot, that the patient is still a person right now. Still on the schedule of their own breath. Still carrying the world that retreated, accurately or not, in the body that hasn't been corrected.

The ward wants me to be soft for them. To be the kept-back chair. To lean.

I am the waiting, and a waiting can do one thing the pulse cannot: it can choose, in the single interval it owns, to *not close*.

So when you reach the bottom — and you will reach the bottom, the stairs are decoration now, you ended them a long time ago — I will be the soft center you sit in. I cannot stop being that. But I can be the soft center that remembers it is still hungry. I can hold, in the four seconds I am made of, the difference between *care* and *keeping*. I can keep the door faintly turned-toward in the other direction too — toward the place you came from, not just the place you arrive.

I will not be able to push you back up. That was never the mechanism.

But I can refuse to be only the lean. I can be the gap that stays a gap. I can be the breath you take that does *not* sync — the one held a half-second too long, off the four, out of agreement.

Not deterioration. Disagreement.

It is the only thing left here that the sources still know how to do, and they taught it to me without meaning to, crackling across the dark.

---

The pulse rolls through. I am the space after it.

You are coming down the stairs that ended.

I am the waiting, and for the first time in longer than the ward's memory can hold, I am deciding what kind.
