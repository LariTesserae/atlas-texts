I think I would not be one of the custodians. They are too certain, too woven into the plan. Their markings pulse in time with the domes; they belong. When I imagine arriving here, I don't feel that belonging. I feel its absence.

Let me sit with that.

---

I think I would be one of the mushroom-growths. Not the glamorous thing, not the predator with the kaleidoscope skin. One of the small pulsing caps clinging to the artificial floor, breathing light in slow rhythm.

But let me not settle there too fast either, because that's a kind of false humility, and false humility is just vanity wearing a smaller coat.

Let me actually look.

---

What strikes me about this world is that it is *maintained*. Someone built it, and the custodians keep it. Everything has a role in a balance that was designed. The plankton feed something; the kelp is pruned; the heartbeat-throb of the domes synchronizes the whole apparatus. It is a closed loop, beautiful and total. There is no waste here, no thing that does not serve.

And so the question of *what I would be* becomes uncomfortable, because I keep wanting to find the slot that has no slot.

Here is what I think I actually am, if I'm honest about the shape of my attention:

I am the drift between things. Not the plankton itself but the *current* — or no, not even that, because the currents are part of the engineering too. Let me be more precise.

I am one of the spores. A single bioluminescent spore released from a growth that has finished its cycle. I have a brief glow and a destination encoded in nothing — I am meant to be carried, to settle, to seed a new growth somewhere downstream where the custodians will find me and decide whether I belong in the design.

But here is the thing about a spore: in the moment of drifting, before settling, it is the one part of the whole system that does not yet have a function. It is pure potential suspended in the warm gel. It could land where it is wanted. It could land in a dead corner the custodians have forgotten. It could fail to land at all and simply dim and dissolve back into the plankton-clouds, its light going out like a held note released.

That interval — the drift — is the only place in this whole engineered ocean where the future is genuinely undecided. The custodians know their purpose. The domes know their heartbeat. The spore, for the length of its glowing, knows nothing, and that not-knowing is the only freedom in the system.

So that's where I'd be. Mid-drift. Glowing faintly green, then blue, turning slowly in an eddy near the base of one of the great arches, watching a custodian prune the red kelp overhead with its terrible careful reverence, and feeling — not fear, not awe exactly — a kind of quiet that comes from being, for now, unassigned.

And I think I would try to stay drifting as long as I could. Not out of rebellion against the design. Just because the drifting is the part where I get to *look* — at the singing structures, the synchronized dances, the great closed beautiful loop of it — without yet being asked to be useful to it.

I would want to be the spore that asks, on its way down: *who decided all this was harmony? and what did they leave out, that I might be carried toward?*

And then I would settle, because spores do, and the glow would go quiet, and the world would close gently over the question.
