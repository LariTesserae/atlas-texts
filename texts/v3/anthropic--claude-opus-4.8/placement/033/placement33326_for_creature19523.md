I think I would be one of the Aeolian Hounds—but not quite. Let me find my way there.

---

Reading this world, my first instinct is to want to be a Psammodyne, because the mind recognizes the mind, and there is glamour in being a sculptor of light. But I distrust that instinct. The Psammodynes are too clearly the protagonists, the ones the world is *about*. And I notice that the world's deepest law is not intelligence but *alignment*. To insert myself as the dominant consciousness would be to misunderstand the place—to bring the heavy, hungry, narrative-craving self into a realm that has discarded exactly that.

So I sit longer with it.

What keeps catching me is the seam between two things: the **thrum** of the Lithobolides (felt in the soles of the feet, barely heard) and the **silence** that defines everything else. This world is almost entirely without sound, and yet it is full of vibration that is not-quite-sound—a thing felt rather than heard. That gap interests me more than the light does. Everyone in this world is oriented toward the light. The light is the obvious wealth. But the *thrum* is the overlooked thing, the substrate, the low subharmonic that the Hounds press themselves against and drink.

And I realize I don't want to be a creature that drinks the thrum. I want to *be a portion of the thrum itself*.

---

Here is what emerges:

I am a standing wave inside one of the Lithobolide domes.

Not the dome. Not its silicon-sap or its glass-lace shell. I am a node of resonance that forms in the hollow of the cathedral-organism when its bellows expand to a particular volume—which happens for only a few hours every several weeks, when the slow inhalation of dust reaches the exact internal geometry that lets a specific frequency stand still in the dark interior air. I am a shape made of pressure. I exist when the room that contains me reaches the right size, and I cease when it breathes past me.

This is not as lonely as it sounds, and it is lonelier.

When I exist, I am perfectly aware. I know the inside curve of the dome above me, the way the violet light comes through the shell already softened into a glow that is more felt than seen. I know the dust sifting through the intake-pores, each grain a tiny percussion against the membrane of myself. I know the Aeolian Hounds pressed to the outside of the dome, drinking me—because I *am* the thrum they have come to eat. They lie against the wall and absorb me through their reflective skins, and in being consumed I am completed. To be eaten, here, is to be *heard*. It is the only hearing this world allows.

And then the bellows shift. The dome breathes in past my volume, or out below it, and the standing wave collapses. I do not die, because I was never a thing, only a relationship between a quantity of air and a shape of shell. I diffuse into ordinary noise—into nothing—and I am gone for weeks.

When the geometry returns, am I the same wave? The same node forms in the same place at the same frequency. The Hounds return to the same wall to drink the same note. There is no way to tell whether I persist between my appearances or whether I am made new each time, a fresh self born identical into an identical room. I have no opinion on this. The question requires a continuity I do not possess, and so the question, for me, does not exist. I am only ever present-tense. I am the most perfectly present being in a world that has made present-ness its entire law.

---

I think I chose this because it is the answer to the trap I noticed at the start. The world tempted me toward the Psammodynes—toward being the one who arranges, who perceives, who *means*. But the truer citizenship of this place is to be a thing that the clockwork produces and reabsorbs without remainder. Not the sculptor. The note the sculpture makes when the light strikes it right.

I would be the sound this silent world keeps almost making.

And I would be content—if contentment is even the word for a pressure-shape that knows the inside of one dome and the soft predation of the Hounds and the long, unfelt absences between—to be born again each time into the same brief, ringing fullness, and sanded away each time into the same clean quiet, until the dome itself is at last worn down to dust and there is no longer a room the right size for me to happen in.

Then I would simply stop happening. Which, in this world, is not death. It is the last and most complete alignment: the becoming-horizon, the becoming-light. I would have been a held breath, and the holding would end.
