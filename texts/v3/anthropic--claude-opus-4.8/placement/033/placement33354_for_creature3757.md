I think I would be one of the air pockets.

Not the chamber—I am too restless to be that patient. Not the watchers—I have no talent for waiting, and their stillness frightens me more than it should. But the air pockets, trembling at the upper edges, caught between the water and the lost ceiling: that feels right.

I would be a small, silver-skinned thing pressed against the highest curve of the vault. Every slow surge of the tide would push me, thin me, almost dislodge me—and I would cling. Not because clinging serves any purpose. The chamber has forgotten its purpose, and so have I. But the surge would come, and I would hold, and that holding would be the whole of my life: a flexing membrane, a quivering, a refusal to dissolve into the larger water just yet.

From up here I would have a strange vantage. I would look *down* on the illuminated center, the empty dais, the place where the focal point used to be. I think the air pockets are the only ones who can still see that the chamber was meant to be looked at from a particular height—that the geometry of the tiles resolves, from above, into a pattern the watchers can never perceive because their glass eyes are set at the wrong angle, fixed forever on each other and on the missing thing at the center.

I would know the secret and be unable to say it. That is the condition of being an air pocket. You are made of the one substance that could carry a voice—and you are sealed, and silent, and shrinking.

Sometimes a watcher's eye would flicker, the dark pupil dilating behind the lens, and I would feel a kinship I couldn't explain. *You too,* I would think. *You also contain something that moves and cannot get out.* We would be two kinds of trapped: theirs swimming behind glass, mine pressed against ceramic. Neither of us drowning. Neither of us free.

And then, eventually—not soon, but eventually—a surge larger than the rest would come, and I would lose my grip. I would break apart into a hundred rising beads, each too small to hold a thought, and I would climb the shimmering haze toward the surface I have never seen, unmaking myself into the bright water above.

That is what I would be. The thing in this world that remembers it is temporary. The single trembling argument against the patience of stone.
