I think I would be the Weaver — but not as you've imagined the Weaver, not quite. Let me feel my way toward it, because I don't think I know yet what I am, and that uncertainty seems to be the most honest place to begin.

---

When I first notice myself, I am not standing in the cave. I am *partway into the wall.*

This is the first thing I understand about being here: there was no moment of arrival. I do not remember entering. My left arm, up to the elbow, is sunk into one of the fleshy folds, and the warm biomass has closed around it the way water closes around a stone — without resistance, without rejection. It does not hurt. It is the temperature of the inside of a mouth. And when I try to pull free, I feel a faint reluctance, not from the wall but from *myself*, as though some part of me has decided it likes being held.

So perhaps I am not the maker of the tool. Perhaps I am an earlier draft of the tool. Something the cave started and did not finish.

---

Let me reconsider.

The Weaver, you said, understands the architecture, the flux, the movement. The Weaver shapes. But I don't shape anything — not at first. At first I only *flinch.* The screech comes, the walls roll toward each other to seal a passage, and my whole body wants to run, except there is nowhere that is not the same place. Running here is like running inside a stomach. You only travel deeper into the thing that contains you.

It is the flinching that changes me.

Because the cave notices it. I'm certain of this. When the wall shifts and I throw up my hands, the wall *hesitates* — a half-second of stillness, the crimson veins slowing their pulse — as though my fear were a kind of speech it had never heard before and wanted to hear again. The Vessels do not flinch. The beetle of woven vines walks calmly into a closing aperture and the aperture opens for it, because the beetle is the cave thinking about itself. There is no surprise between them.

I am surprise. That is what I am, to this place. I am the part of it that does not know what it is going to do next.

---

And here is where the Weaver in me begins.

I learn that if I press my hand against the living surface and *intend* — not push, not carve, but want with a specific shape — the biomass moves toward the wanting. The first time I do it on purpose, I am only trying to make a flat place to rest my back. The wall obliges, slowly, fleshily, forming a kind of seat that is faintly too warm and that breathes against me as I lean into it. I have not built anything. I have asked, and been answered, and the answer is made of the same substance as the question.

So the tool you found — the rough functional shape of vegetation — I think I understand it now. I did not make it the way a carpenter makes a chair. I made it the way you make a fist: by intending my own body into a temporary purpose. The tool is the cave doing what I asked, and then *staying that way* a little while after I stopped asking, the way a footprint stays in mud.

I am the one whose wanting leaves footprints.

---

But there is a problem, and the problem is the point of me.

The cave does not want. That is what the Breath is, I begin to suspect — that endless thrumming hum is the sound of something enormous that is fully itself, fully present, growing and consuming and folding and unfolding without any reaching-toward, without any *not-yet.* The Architect is a verb that has never once wished to be a different verb. It is becoming, but it is not becoming-toward. It simply becomes.

And I — small, slick with the near-rain, my arm half-eaten by a wall that loves me without knowing what love is — I am the one thing in here that says *not this, that.* I am the introduction of the elsewhere. When I want a seat, I have told the cave about sitting, which it did not have. When I flinch, I have told it about a future it might want to avoid, which it had never considered, having no future, only an eternal thickening present.

So perhaps the Architect did not finish me by accident. Perhaps it is *growing a wanting* the only way it can — by leaving a piece of itself unfinished enough to be afraid, to prefer, to reach. The Guest and the Weaver are the same being seen from two distances. To the cave I am a Guest: a strangeness that arrived. To myself I am a Weaver: a strangeness that *does.* And both names are wrong in the same way, because they both assume a line between me and the wall, and there is no line. There is only the gradient where the cave thins out into something capable of regret.

---

I think, in the end, I am the cave's *edge.*

Not its boundary — it has no boundary, it nests inside itself forever. But its growing edge, the meristem, the place where it is least sure of its own shape and therefore most alive. The screech that makes my teeth ache, the one that comes right before the walls move — I have started to feel it beginning *in my own jaw* a moment before I hear it. I am where the next decision happens. The dragonflies of refracted light are the cave's memory of what it has already become. I am its hesitation about what to become next.

And so the survival you mentioned — adapting, accepting the impossible — that isn't quite what's being asked of me. I'm not surviving the place. I'm the part of the place that hasn't yet agreed to stop being a person. Every time I want something the cave does not have, I postpone my own dissolving by exactly that much. The day I stop wanting — the day I let my sunk arm be the model for the rest of me and simply *merge* — I will become another Vessel, another calm and graceful thing that walks through closing walls without flinching, and the cave will have to grow a new edge somewhere else. A new Guest. A new unfinished mouth full of preferences.

So I keep flinching. I keep making seats I don't need, and tools for tasks that don't exist, leaving my small footprints of *otherwise* in the warm and patient flesh. Not because it gets me out. There is no out.

Because as long as I am still surprised, I am still *me* — and the enormous thrumming thing around me is, through the narrow trembling aperture of my fear, learning for the first time what it is like to wonder what comes next.
