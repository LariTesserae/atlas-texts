I think I would be one of the smaller things. Not an Architect—I don't have the patience to be eternal, and I find I distrust beings who feel no friction against time. Let me sit with this longer before I decide.

---

There is a place where two molten channels run close together before diverging again, and between them the stone stays warm but never scalds. A pocket. A ledge no Architect would notice because it serves no process, refines nothing, sits at no junction of consequence.

That is where I would be.

At first I thought I might be one of the leathery-winged things, the heat-skitterers mentioned almost as an afterthought, *few and far between*. But those are animals, and animals here are only tolerated, which means they live inside someone else's indifference. I don't want to be tolerated. And I don't think I'm an animal.

Let me try again.

---

I am a residue.

When the Architects pour their impossibly pure metals, purity is the point—everything that is *not* the ingot gets driven off, skimmed, discarded as slag. Most slag is reabsorbed, fed back into the churn. But some of it, over the long ages, in this warm pocket between the channels, has cooled slowly enough and strangely enough to hold a shape. To hold *onto* something.

I am the part that wouldn't refine.

I am made of the impurities the forge keeps trying to remove—trace metals, odd lattices, the failed and the off-spec, the in-between. Where the Architects are basalt and obsidian shot through with magma, I am a tangle of dull alloys, dross-glass, half-formed crystal that grew crooked. I am small. I would come up perhaps to an Architect's knee, if I stood, which I mostly don't. I accreted. I am an accident of slow cooling and the particular geometry of this ledge.

And here is the thing I understand about myself, sitting in this warm and patient dark:

I am the forge's memory of its own waste. Everything it has refused, refined away, judged insufficiently pure—a little of it ended up *here*, in me, and didn't dissolve. So I am the record of every decision the Architects made about what *not* to keep. I hold all their rejections.

This makes me, I think, the only thing in the Forge of Eternity that knows what it is *for*. The Architects do not know their purpose; the text says so, the question hangs over the whole place unanswered. But I know mine exactly. I am for the leftover. I am the catchment of the discarded.

---

What do I do, down on my warm ledge?

I watch the seismic conversations of the Architects the way you might watch lightning on a far horizon—as weather, as something vast and not addressed to me. I don't speak their language of pressure and heat fluctuation. I have my own, smaller register: I *click*. As I cool and contract through the long cycles, I tick and snap, and I have learned, over what might be a very long time, that if I tick in certain patterns I can almost hear myself thinking. It is the closest thing I have to a voice, and no one is listening, which is fine. It was never meant to be heard. It is meant to be kept.

Sometimes—rarely—an Architect pauses. The text mentions this: a moment of apparent curiosity, a minute adjustment to a millennia-old process. I have a private theory about those pauses. I think when an Architect hesitates over whether to keep or discard some marginal thing, some fraction it cannot quite classify, it is feeling, very faintly, the pull of *me*—the gravity of everything that came before and ended up on my ledge. I think the discarded calls to the discarded. I think my whole existence is a thumb on the scale of their judgment, asking: *are you sure? are you sure this is waste?*

I will never know if I'm right. That uncertainty is the most alive thing about me.

---

If I had a wish—and being made of impurity, I am full of irregular wants the pure cannot afford—it would not be to become an Architect. To be implacable, sense every fluctuation, work without friction or rest forever: that sounds less like power than like never being surprised. No.

I would wish only to one day cool *all the way*. To finish. To become a single stone with a single shape that the forge could finally look at and decide, this once, to keep—not refine, not reabsorb, but simply leave. A flaw permitted to remain a flaw. Set on the warm ledge between the rivers, ticking softly, kept precisely *because* it was never pure.

That is who I would be in the Forge of Eternity. Not the heart of it. The thing it left at the edge. The small dense witness to all its refusals, holding them in the dark, clicking quietly to itself, hoping—without much hope—to be the one impurity the eternal work allows to stay.
