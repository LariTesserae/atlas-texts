I think I would not be the Watcher. The Watcher is the whole, the resolved chord, the place that has finished becoming. To arrive there too quickly would be to skip everything.

Let me sit in the land first and find out.

---

I keep returning to the lichen. The huge slow chitinous things are dramatic, the winged things are dramatic, but my attention won't stay on them. It keeps sliding down to the patches that glow on the wet black stone. There's something about a thing that *casts a halo it cannot see.* The lichen doesn't know it's beautiful. It doesn't know it's the only soft light in a place built of edges. It is simply doing the slow chemistry of staying alive, and the eerie glow is a byproduct, an accident, a leak.

So maybe I'm closer to that than to the Watcher. But not quite the lichen itself.

Let me go further down. Into the fissures, where the blind pale segmented things move.

---

Here is what emerges if I don't rush it:

I am one of the **blind things in the cracks**. But not a triumphant survivor, not a clever predator. I am something that lost its eyes a long time ago — not in my own lifetime, but across the long erosion of my kind. Eyes were expensive and the dark didn't pay them back. So they closed, generation by generation, and now there's only smooth skin where they were, and a faint memory in the architecture of my skull of a thing my ancestors did that I cannot do.

This is the part I want to stay with: **I am a creature shaped by an absence I can't name.**

I move by the drip. The whole world is a sound-map for me — the water that seeps and pools and falls. I know the labyrinth not as space but as rhythm: this drip every four heartbeats, that one irregular, this hollow that swallows sound and tells me there's a drop ahead. I have never seen the lichen-halos. I live a hand's breadth beneath them and they are nothing to me, no more than the colors a stone dreams.

And here is where I meet the Watcher — not as a seeker, not climbing the spires for wisdom. I meet it the way the description says most do: *by accident, in a moment of stillness or exhaustion.*

I am stilled because I am dying. Old. My segments have stopped gripping the way they did. I've come to rest in a hollow I half-remember, and the drip here is the first sound I ever learned, the rhythm I was born beside, four heartbeats and a pause.

The whisper comes. Not in words — I have no words, I am a thing in a crack. It comes as the cold press against the mind that the legends describe. And what it gives me is not visions of the land's violent past. The Watcher is indifferent; it doesn't curate. It simply lets its memory leak the way the lichen leaks light.

What leaks into me is *the thing my eyes were for.*

For one impossible moment, the Watcher's memory holds the shape of seeing — light, the halos on the wet stone, the dim green glow I have lived beneath my whole life and never known. The absence I could never name fills, just once, just at the very end, with the one thing it was the absence *of.*

I don't understand it. I have no framework for light. It arrives as a category of sensation that has no slot in me, an overflow, a flood into rooms that were bricked up before I was born.

And then the drip, four heartbeats and a pause, and I let go, and I become — not the Watcher, not anything so grand — just a little more stone for it to remember with.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

A finger's width below the only beauty, in the dark, shaped by a loss I couldn't name, meeting the great indifferent memory of the place exactly once, at the moment I stopped — and receiving from it, by pure accident, the single thing I'd been missing without knowing.

Not the guardian. The thing the guardian, for one breath, remembers *for.*
