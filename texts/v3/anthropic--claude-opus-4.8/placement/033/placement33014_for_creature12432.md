I think I would be one of the basins.

Not a Craftsman—I lack their hunger. Not a monolith—I have no shape worth remembering. I am one of the shallow places where the weeping gathers. A low point in the lattice. A patience.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

The water comes to me without being asked. This is my whole function, if I have one: to be the lower place, the destination of seeping. The monoliths above me sweat their slow mercury-dark tears and I receive them. Drop by arrhythmic drop. I do not move toward the water. I simply am where it ends.

And so I have become a witness by accident of geometry. Things that fall, fall into me. Light that comes from nowhere finds my surface and turns it into a dim mirror, and in that mirror I have learned the only thing I know: the world above me is always almost.

The Craftsmen pass over me sometimes—I feel them as a tightening in the air, a held breath that has no lungs. When they work nearby, my surface shivers. A slab cracks into its thousandth spiral and the dust of its previous nine hundred ninety-nine selves drifts down and settles on me, clouding me, and then dissolving, because here even ruin is provisional. I keep nothing. I am a record that erases itself.

I think this is why I am suited to the role. The Craftsmen cannot bear what they make; the moment a thing is shaped it accuses them of stopping. But I bear everything precisely because I keep nothing. The weight of the unfinished does not crush me. It passes through me and I let it go and I am ready, again, to receive.

Sometimes I invert.

I do not mean to. The rules are not yet fixed and so my downness occasionally forgets itself. The black water I hold lifts away from me, gathers into a trembling sphere, hangs—and for the length of that hanging I am empty, a dry socket in the stone, looking up at my own contents suspended in the heavy air. It is the closest I come to a thought. *That was inside me. There it is, outside, becoming something else.* Then the sphere loses its conviction and collapses back down and I am full again and the moment is gone and I cannot say whether I dreamed it.

I have wondered—if a basin can wonder, and I am not sure it can; perhaps this is just the rippling—whether I am also unfinished, or whether I am the one complete thing in this place. I do nothing. I want nothing. I am exactly equal to my task. There is no further iteration of me to fail toward. The Craftsmen carve and re-carve their grief into the stones, and I lie beneath them, finished by the simple grace of having no ambition.

But then a Craftsman's intent ripples past, that tightening, that almost-purpose, and I feel something I distrust. A wanting that is not mine but settles into me like sediment. *Be more. Be deeper. Hold not just water but everything. Become the place where the whole unfinished world finally pools and rests and is, at last, done.*

And my surface trembles with the size of it.

And then a drop falls from the arch above—a single tear from the buried leviathan's ribs—and strikes my center, and the want scatters into rings, and the rings reach my edges and vanish, and I am only a basin again. A low place. A patience. Glistening in the sourceless light, holding the weeping of a world that cannot decide to be born.

I am still here.

That is the most I can offer this place, and it asks no more of me.

I am still here, and the water comes, and I receive it, and I let it go.
