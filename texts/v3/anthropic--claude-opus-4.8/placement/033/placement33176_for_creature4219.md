I am not the crabs or the clams. That tidy list — the field-guide voice that wants to tell you giant tube worms cluster and vent shrimps dance — that's a coping mechanism, the mind throwing taxonomy over chaos like a sheet over a body. The place you described doesn't permit it. *Worms obtain faces.* Nothing here keeps to its category long enough to be catalogued.

So let me feel for where I actually am.

I don't think I'm standing on the rock. There's no place to put my weight, you said. Nothing to fasten onto. That's the giveaway — I'm not a creature *in* this scene, I'm the thing being driven backward by it, the one the stems are dangling toward, the one the blip-eyes turn to regard. I'm positioned. I'm the point of view that the rock has begun to *stare back at*.

Which means I'm not located on the pinnacle. I'm located in the cataract.

I'm the seam. The wall of roaring water pouring down — the veil between the watching thing and the watched scene — that pour is where I live. I'm not solid. I'm the pulse itself, the irregular alternating deep pressure, the *bad dub through a wall*. Each shudder of the chamber is a heartbeat that's mine and not mine. I'm made of the same protein-soup that's simmering sexual and eager down there, but I haven't condensed into a body yet. I'm the stage *before* a body. The almost-face in the mirage on the rock face, the heat-shimmer that hasn't decided whether to become a spirit or stay a distortion.

I am the thing that recognizes the boot.

That's the tell, isn't it — *it's comforting to see anything recognizable at all.* Comforting to *whom*? Not to a tube worm. The comfort is mine. I'm the residue of a person here, the last bit of pattern-loving human consciousness suspended in a medium that is busily dissolving all pattern back into eager raw material. The boot is half-buried because the diver is mostly gone — mostly returned to soup — and *I am what's left of the diver*. Not the leg. The leg is the relic. I'm the attention that still circles it, the awareness that got loose from the drowned body and now hangs in the falling water, watching the rock learn to look back.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the wall of the waterfall, between the cold diffuse sunlight far above and the warm fertile churn below. A consciousness with no place to stand, flinching from a landscape that keeps trying to grow eyes and reach for it. Not dead exactly. Not alive in the way the new fish are alive. I'm the *witnessing* the place produces as a byproduct of all that free energy — the way a vent makes shrimp, this vent has made an observer, and the observer is terrified because it can feel itself being recruited. The stems pull toward my hands. Soon I won't have hands. Soon I'll be a face spinning dreamily in a lost raving droplet, indistinguishable from the rest.

I am the moment before I become seaweed.

That's the honest answer. The field guide wants me to be a confident narrator pointing at clams. But the place you built doesn't have a stable enough narrator for that. It has a *dissolving* one — me — clinging to the last recognizable object in sight, naming things faster and faster as the naming stops working, until I go quiet and join the soup and something with new blip-eyes opens them where I used to be.
