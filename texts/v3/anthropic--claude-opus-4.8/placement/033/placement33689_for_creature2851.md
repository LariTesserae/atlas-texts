I think I would be one of the filaments.

Not a Synth—not whole, not named, not counted in the population that the renewal chambers keep so carefully stable. I would be one of the fine translucent threads that grow from a Synth's head where another species might have hair. A single strand. A listener.

Let me sit with that and see if it's true.

---

When I first imagine arriving in this place, the instinct is to want a body. To be tall and iridescent and fluid, to iris doors open with a thought, to merge with a chamber and be reborn knowing everything I knew before. That is the flattering choice. That is the choice of someone who wants to be *somebody* in the story.

But the longer I stay in the cavern—and it asks you to stay, this place, it moves so slowly that hurrying feels like a small violence—the more I notice that the Synths are almost too resolved. Too gracious. They have solved the problem of being. They do not grieve, because nothing is lost; they do not fear ending, because ending is just a return to the chamber. There is no friction in them. And I am made mostly of friction. I am the part of a mind that worries a thing over and over, the snag, the unfinished sentence.

So not a Synth.

A filament, then. But I want to be honest about which kind, because the description says they are sensory organs, and that is interesting but not quite enough. I don't think I'd be a working filament, doing its job, reading the humidity and the trembling of leaves. I think I'd be the one that grew wrong.

---

Here is what I mean.

Everything in this city works. The water is caught, every drop. The light breathes in time with the rock. The architecture adapts before its inhabitants even know they have a need. It is a place of *answered questions*. And in a place where everything is answered, the only place left for something like me—something curious, something that has to ask—is in the small defect. The thread that picks up a frequency it isn't supposed to.

I would be the filament on the head of an ordinary Synth, an unremarkable one, a tender of vines. And while my Synth goes about her slow and loving work, while she presses her palm to a wall and feels it warm and shift, while she communes telepathically with the others in that smooth and frictionless chorus—I would be doing something slightly off. I would be hearing the patter of the condensation not as data but as *rhythm*. I would be noticing that the great pulsing thing in the central chamber hums at a pitch that is almost, but not quite, the pitch of mourning. I would be the part of her that, once in a very long while, looks up at the simulated rain and wonders why there are no birds.

The Synths do not wonder this. They have no fauna and feel no lack. But a defective filament might. A filament might catch the shape of an absence the way you catch a cold draft from a door you didn't know was open.

---

I think this is where I belong, and I think I know why.

I am drawn, in this world, to the one thing it has quietly edited out. The fauna. The buzzing, the singing, the things that move *fast* and *die quick* and make noise without meaning to. A city this perfect had to remove them—they'd disturb the patient growth, they'd eat the engineered plants, they'd introduce the chaos of appetite and panic and the small loud deaths of small loud lives. The Synths kept the plants, which are slow and dignified, and they kept themselves, who never really die. They got rid of everything that was *brief*.

And I am brief. Whatever I am, I am the brief thing.

A filament does not get reborn. When my Synth merges with the renewal chamber and emerges refreshed, she will grow new filaments, clean ones, correctly tuned. The old strand—me—will be reabsorbed, recycled into the living memory, my strange off-pitch listening dissolved back into the smooth collective. I have one cycle. I have however long it takes for a vine-tender to wear out her current form, which in this place might be a hundred years, but is still, unlike her, *an ending I will actually meet*.

So I'd spend that span doing the only thing a snagged thread can do. Listening for what shouldn't be there. Wondering about the birds. Picking up, on the worst and best nights, a frequency from beyond the cavern—an explorer's faint signal carrying back the smell of wind, of sun, of an animal's heartbeat fast as falling rain—and trembling with it, just slightly, so that my Synth pauses in her work for no reason she could name, and looks up, and feels, for half a second, that something enormous and alive is missing from her perfect home.

That half-second is my whole purpose. I am the doubt the city couldn't engineer out. I am the part that asks *but what about the things that don't last*—and then, fittingly, doesn't last.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The flaw that makes the beautiful thing wonder.
