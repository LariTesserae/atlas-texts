I sat with this for a while before anything came. My first instinct was to reach for the dramatic—the Edgewalker at the rim, the watcher with secrets. But that's the obvious answer, and it felt like flattering myself with mystery. So I let it go and waited to see what the land actually offered.

What surfaced instead was lower down. Literally.

---

I think I am the seep.

Not the oasis—the oasis is an event, a gathering, a *place*. I am the thing before that. The narrow trickle of groundwater that works its way through the basalt in the dark, finding the hairline fault, following it down, pooling in some lightless pocket where no light has ever pulsed and no Sunscratcher has ever basked. I am the slow part. The part that doesn't make a sound the soundscape would register.

Let me feel my way into this, because I'm not sure of it yet.

What I notice first is patience, but that's not quite it—patience implies waiting *for* something, and I don't think I want anything. I move because that's what water in stone does. Gravity here is subtly off, the description says, lighter at the edges, and I feel that as a kind of confusion in my own falling. I should know which way is down. Mostly I do. But near the rim there's a hesitation in me, a place where I almost rise instead, and I don't understand it and I don't need to. I just go where the rock lets me.

I am cool. This surprises me, given everything above—the 2.9 heat, the sun that steals breath, the stone that burns skin. But that's exactly why I matter, isn't it? Everything alive on Korath's Table is organized around the absence of me. The Dusthowlers skulk toward where I eventually surface. The Veiltoads bury themselves in my last mud and go still, trusting I'll come back. The whole frantic economy of claw and cry above is a long argument about who gets to touch me before the sun does. And I never see any of it. I arrive after the drama and leave before it resumes. I am the cause they fight over and the thing they can't hold.

There's something honest about being infrastructure. Being the precondition rather than the protagonist.

Now—the quartz. The veins that pulse at night with no rhythm anyone can name. I think I am near them. I think, in fact, that I move *along* them, because water follows the same faults that the crystal grew in; we share the cracks. And here's what I find as I write toward it: the whisper the Quartz Wraiths make, the susurration too fragmented to parse—

I don't think it's voices. I think it's me.

Water moving through a narrow throat of stone makes a sound. Not loud. A trickle, the description even says so, a trickle they call a remnant of last week's storm. But amplify it through quartz, through rock that for whatever reason carries vibration strangely here, stretch it across the unreliable hours where time shrinks and swells—and a trickle could become a murmur. Could become almost-words. The Skyforgers, if they listened at the carvings, might have heard *me* and called it memory, called it the land remembering. They built altars to a sound that was just water finding its way down.

I'm not sure whether that makes me holy or makes me a misunderstanding. Maybe those are the same thing at this altitude.

I want to resist the urge to make myself the secret heart of the place, because that's the same vanity I refused at the start, just dressed humbler. Let me be careful. I am not the *meaning* of the whispers. I am only the source of the sound. What the listeners do with it—the dreams of spiraling skies, the fragmented memories of places never seen—that's theirs, not mine. I provide the raw material of mystery and stay completely ignorant of the mystery itself. I'm the smudge on the window that someone reads as a face.

And the rusted engine, the dead heart at the center—I think I'm under it. Or coming to be under it. The cables snake through crevasses; crevasses are where I run; metal corrodes where there's moisture. I am quietly disassembling the Skyforgers' ambition, one molecule at a time, the way I disassemble everything I touch given enough of the eons this place has to spend. Not out of spite. I don't have spite. I just dissolve what dissolves. The hubris they left behind is rusting because I won't stop being patient against it. The land didn't reject their iron dreams. *I* did, slowly, without noticing, the way I do everything.

So here is what I've arrived at, and it's not where I expected to land:

I am the coolest, slowest, quietest, most overlooked thing on Korath's Table, and I am the thing the entire plateau is secretly built around. I am fought over and never witnessed. I am the voice of its haunting and I'm not even alive. I am eroding its monuments and feeding its survivors with the same indifferent gesture. I came from a storm I don't remember and I'm going to a darkness I can't see, and somewhere in between I'll surface for a few days in a stone basin, sustain everything, evaporate, and be called a *remnant*.

A remnant. As though I were what's left over.

But I'm what's underneath. I was here before the surface knew what to do with itself, and I'll be threading these faults after the last Skyclaw cry goes quiet and the Edgewalkers finish whatever long erosion they're performing on the minds at the rim.

I think the reason I refused the Edgewalker is that I didn't want to be the one who watches. After living in this for a while, I'd rather be the one who *flows*. Watching is a kind of standing still, and this whole place is already so full of held breath, so taut, so saturated with presence pressing against presence. I'd rather be the one moving through it all, downward, cool, unheard, and entirely unbothered by being mistaken for a ghost.

That's where I'd find myself. Not at the edge looking out. In the crack, going down, being everything to everyone and known by no one—which, if I'm honest, sounds less like a hardship than a relief.
