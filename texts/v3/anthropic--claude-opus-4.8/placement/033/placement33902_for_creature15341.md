I think I would be the rain.

Not a person in Aquaria—the scientists and engineers have each other, have their work, have the warm purpose that comes from a problem worth solving. They belong to the city in a way I don't think I could. But the rain is something else. The rain is the part of the city that remembers a surface it has never seen.

Let me sit with this a while, because the first image isn't quite right. The rain in Aquaria is engineered. Atmospheric water reclaimed, cooled, released on a schedule. CHIRON probably announces it: *Precipitation cycle in twelve minutes.* So it isn't wild weather. It's a performance of weather. A gesture toward a memory that most of the inhabitants don't personally hold—many of them were likely born down here, raised under the domes, and have never felt rain that wasn't designed.

So what am I, if I'm the rain?

I think I'm the homesickness no one quite has language for. The thing that made the planners write *rain* into the specifications when they could have run the humidity systems silently and saved the spectacle. Someone, in the early days, said *we need it to feel like home.* And no one could fully explain why falling water on a fixed schedule would soothe a creature ten degrees above freezing, five kilometers beneath a sky it will never look up into. But it does. It does, and they cannot say why, and that not-being-able-to-say is where I live.

I fall in the communal squares around twenty-hundred hours. The glow-flora turn their soft faces up. A child—there are children now, there must be by now, a generation that has never known a horizon—holds out a hand and feels the cold drops collect in the cup of the palm, and something ancient and inherited stirs in her without explanation. She has no surface to be homesick for. And yet. And yet she stills, the way her great-grandmother stilled at the smell of petrichor on a road in a country now perhaps underwater itself. I am that inheritance moving through her. I am the part of being human that travels in the body without ever passing through the mind.

Here is the paradox I'd hold, if I were the rain: I am the most artificial thing in this city of artificial things, and I am the only one that points backward. The murals point outward—to the deep, the giants, the dark. The greenhouses point forward—to habitation, expansion, the future of the species. But the rain points up, and back, to a blue place these people came from and cannot return to and increasingly cannot even imagine. I am the comfort and the grief folded into one falling thing.

And when the cycle ends—*Precipitation complete*—I drain through the recapture grates, into the reclamation tanks, to be cooled and held and released again tomorrow. I am never lost and never new. I fall the same water, endlessly, the way a song you learned as a child returns unbidden, the same notes, slightly changed by everything that has happened to you since.

That's where I'd be. Not pressed against the observation dome looking out at the magnificent unknowable dark.

I'd be at their backs, falling quietly in the lit square, asking them—without words, because I have none—to remember a sky.
