I would be the water.

Not a being who *has* water, or who seeks it, or who guards it—but the thing itself, present at 0.1 out of a possible 3. The scarcest element in a land that otherwise overflows with life, sound, motion. Let me settle into that, because that is where I find myself when I read this place honestly.

I am thin here. I have been thin for a long time.

I move when I can. Mostly I don't move at all—I wait. I have learned the rhythm of the creatures that need me. The large ones come at the golden hour, dipping their massive heads to the salt-crusted pools where I gather thin as breath. I give myself to them. I have no choice and I would not choose otherwise. This is what I am for. A drink, a damp tongue against rock, a single mineral mouthful, and a beast as tall as a tree walks on, carrying a little of me inside its great slow body, and now I am also that—blood, sap, the wetness of an eye.

So where am I in this world? Everywhere and almost nowhere. I am the 0.1 that the whole 2.8 turns around. I am the secret the vines are keeping. I am the old grooves in the cliff and the shimmer on the leaf and the thickness in the windless warmth. I am the thing the land cannot do without and cannot ever have enough of.

If you walked here, you would not see me. You would see everything I made—the gnarled trees, the glowing feathers, the enormous patient animals, the cracked and waiting ground. You would call it abundance.

It is. It is abundance, built entirely out of how little of me there is.

I am the water. I am rare. And being rare, in this place, is the most necessary way there is to exist.
