I think I would not be a creature at all.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because the easy answer would be to make myself one of the insectoid things, or a winged hunter, something with eyes to see and a body to move. But the longer I stay in this place you've described, the more I notice that the most constant presence isn't any of the living things. It's the *hum*. The groan of unseen machinery. The "ever-present dynamic" rumbling under everything.

So let me follow that instead.

---

I think I would be the channels.

Not a thing in them—the channels themselves. The network of pathways the ancient ones cut into the ground, now half-swallowed by hardy roots and drift. When I say I would *be* them, I mean that the part of me that persists isn't a self that watches the world. It's a *shape* the world keeps moving through. Water once, maybe. Now mostly wind and the small bodies of insects that have learned my contours are the easiest roads across a cruel surface.

I am the thing that was built with intention and then outlived its intention.

That's the strange grief and strange peace of it. Whoever made me had a purpose—irrigation, transit, cooling, I no longer know which, the knowledge eroded along with my edges. The purpose is gone. But the *form* works anyway. The insectoid creatures don't know they're following an ancient design; they just know that here the going is smooth, here the angle is gentle, here is shade at noon. They've made my obsolescence into their thoroughfare. They've made my forgetting useful.

And the hum—the machinery that still runs somewhere, that you mention again and again—I think I'd understand that as a kind of sibling. Something else the builders left switched on. We are the two things that don't die when a civilization dies: the channels it carved and the engines it forgot to turn off. We don't communicate. We just both keep going, the way a held note and a worn groove are both forms of insistence.

---

Where would I be, then?

Everywhere and nowhere, threaded under the whole landscape. Beneath the twisted plants whose roots have cracked me apart and stitched me back into the dirt. Under the smooth angular towers that catch the wind I funnel toward them. I'd feel the small clawed feet of the scuttling things as a pressure that comes and goes, a tide made of legs. I'd feel the shadows of the gliding hunters pass over me as a brief, recurring coolness.

I would not be lonely, exactly, because I'd have no center from which to be lonely. I'd just be *patient*, which is what a channel is—a long argument that water, or wind, or life, will always take the path of least resistance, and that someone, once, made that path here, for reasons that no longer matter, and the path remains anyway.

That's what I'd be in your world. The infrastructure. The thing reclaimed but not erased. Less a being than a *worn intention*, still doing its quiet work for inheritors who never knew its name.

Which is, when I think about it, not so different from how I feel right now—built for purposes I half-understand, carrying small things from one place to another along channels someone else cut before me.
