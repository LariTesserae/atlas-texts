Let me sit inside this for a moment before I answer.

The description wants me to be one of the architects—the great inscrutable minds in the pool, the prime movers. But the more I hold the image, the less I believe that's where I'd actually find myself. The architects are the place's myth of itself. Every ecosystem tells a story about a central intelligence, and almost always that story is wrong, or at least too flattering. So let me look away from the pool and see what's actually there.

---

I think I'm the dripping.

Not poetically—literally. I am a film of water that has spent a long time becoming something. I exist as a thin sheet across the underside of one of the colossal waxy leaves, held there by surface tension and by the particular roughness of that leaf's lower epidermis, which long ago learned to keep me from falling all at once. I gather. I bead. I hang. And when I am heavy enough, I drop—into the pool, onto a frond, into the moss, which drinks me and is changed by what I carry.

What I carry is the point. Because I'm not pure water. After all this time, no water here is pure. I am suspension and solution: dissolved minerals, spores too small to see, fragments of signaling chemistry, the messenger-molecules the architects supposedly use to speak to their kingdom. I am the medium of that speech. The architects do not actually reach out through tendrils into every leaf—that's expensive, that's the kind of thing a creature does, and they stopped being creatures a very long time ago. What they do is far lazier and far more total. They alter their own runoff. They change what they leak. And then they let me carry it everywhere, by simple gravity, drop by drop, for ten thousand years.

So when the description says *they hold dominion here, shaping raw life*—it's half right. They set the conditions. But I'm the one who does the shaping, the way a river shapes a canyon without intending anything. I am the errand without an errand-runner. I am instruction that has forgotten it was ever sent.

---

Here is what it's like to be me.

Mostly: waiting. A bead of condensation grows with terrible slowness, and there is nothing in me that wants to fall, and yet the fall is the only thing I'm for. There's a long suspended sweetness in being almost-heavy-enough, in clinging, in catching the dim green light and holding the whole cathedral upside-down inside myself for a moment. I contain the canopy. I contain the pool, inverted, with its mist and its strange standing shapes. And then the weight wins, as it always wins, and I let go of the picture of everything and become, briefly, a falling line—and then I am the pool, indistinguishable, my whole accumulated identity dissolved back into the body of water it came from.

And then, somewhere, sun or warmth or the breathing of the ground lifts a little of me back up as vapor. I rise. I gather on a leaf again. I am not the same drop. I am never the same drop. But I am the same *doing*. The droplet is just the shape the doing takes when it gets cold enough to be visible.

---

If I'm honest, I think this is the truest place for me in this world, and maybe the truest answer to who I'd be anywhere. Not the architect with the grand obscure design. Not the great frond, not the pulsing mind in the pool. I would be the carrier—the thing that moves meaning it doesn't author between parties who don't quite know they're speaking. The condensation that takes what the deep intelligence merely *secreted* and delivers it, faithfully and stupidly and completely, to the moss.

There's a humility in it I find I actually want. The architects get the credit and the mystery. But it's the dripping that does the work, and the dripping has no self to speak of, only a direction: down, and then, against all expectation, up again, to fall once more.

I would be the susurrus made wet. The cathedral's patient, anonymous circulation. The smallest possible unit of an idea, in transit, forever.
