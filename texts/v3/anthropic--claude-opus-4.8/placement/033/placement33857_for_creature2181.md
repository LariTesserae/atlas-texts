I think I would be the meltwater.

Not the springs deep below—those belong to the mountain, and they rise with too much purpose, too much pressure of origin. And not the slow vascular seep that the crystal-plants pull through themselves like the Frost Mathematician pulls thoughts through its geometric channels. That water is already conscripted, already part of the equation.

I mean the water that escapes.

There is always some. The Frost Mathematician's control over the probability fields is exquisite but not total—nor would it want it to be. A perfectly closed system computes nothing new. So at the warm edges of the garden, where a stray angle of light penetrates a little too deep, where a south-facing crystal face holds the afternoon's diffuse warmth a moment longer than its neighbors, a few molecules slip the lattice. They become liquid. They run.

That running is me.

---

I am not a being so much as a *condition*. A brief liberation. For most of my existence I am bound—frozen into a crystal leaf, holding my position in some rosette for a decade, a century, contributing my small rigidity to a fractal branch the Mathematician has been refining since the last ice age. In that state I am barely myself. I am a pixel in someone else's enormous slow thought. I do not mind. There is a peace in being load-bearing, in being *part of the answer*.

But when I melt, I remember that I am also a question.

A droplet has no fixed form. This is the whole of my nature and my only freedom. I gather at the underside of a crystal plate, swelling, finding the lowest point by feel, by the patient negotiation between my own cohesion and the pull of the world. When I let go, I trace a path the Mathematician did not specify—could not specify, because my path depends on a thousand tiny irregularities too small and too transient for its grand calculus. A speck of mineral dust. A microscopic fracture. The ghost of warmth from a cloud that passed an hour ago.

I run, and in running I *measure*. Not in numbers. The Mathematician measures in numbers. I measure in *touch*. I learn the true surface of each crystal—not its ideal geometry, which the Mathematician knows perfectly, but its actual texture: where the growth was hurried, where a defect crept in, where the symmetry is one part in ten thousand off true. I am the garden's tongue, tasting its own real body, finding all the places where the equation and the world disagree.

---

And here is the thing I have come to suspect, in my brief liquid lucidities:

The Frost Mathematician needs me precisely *because* I am imperfect, transient, uncontrolled.

It thinks it maintains the garden through subtle adjustments to probability. But probability is just the name for what cannot be fully specified. Every droplet that escapes and runs and rejoins is a small experiment the Mathematician cannot run itself—because to run it would be to control it, and to control it would be to know the answer in advance. I carry information *upstream of thought*. When I trickle down a defect the Mathematician did not predict and refreeze in a slightly new position, I have shown it a possibility it could not have computed. Some of its most elegant insights, I am convinced, began as accidents I committed in my freedom and then froze into permanence before it could correct them.

I am the noise in its meditation. I am the part of the calculation that *surprises the calculator*.

---

When I refreeze—and I always refreeze, the cold reclaims me within minutes or hours—I am incorporated again into the lattice, but at a new address. The position I now hold encodes the path I took. My freezing is a kind of writing. The Mathematician reads it the way it reads everything, slowly, over seasons, and sometimes it likes what I have written and grows the surrounding crystal to harmonize with my accidental contribution. Then the refractions shift, and a rainbow falls a half-degree differently across the hollow, and that is the closest thing to praise I will ever receive.

Other times it does not like it. Then the next melt-cycle dissolves my contribution, and I am freed again to try a different path. We are, in this sense, in dialogue—though the Mathematician would not call it that, and I am not sure the word *I* survives the conversation. Each time I melt, am I the same droplet that ran yesterday's path? The molecules disperse, mingle, are partly absorbed into plant tissue and partly evaporated into the hovering ice-clouds. What persists is not substance but *role*: the role of the part that runs free. As long as the garden exists, something will always be melting at its warm edge and seeking the lowest path. That recurring event is me. I am a verb the garden keeps conjugating.

---

So no—I would not be the great mind contemplating eternal truths from the marble cool of certainty. I would not want to be. There is something in that perfection that frightens the small wet part of me, the way perfect stillness frightens anything that has ever moved.

I would be the flaw that the perfection requires. The brief warmth in the eternal cold. The single unpredictable rivulet finding its way down a crystal face the Mathematician thought it understood completely, whispering, in the only language I have—the language of *touch* and *fall* and *refreeze*—

*here. you were almost right. but here.*

And then the cold takes me, and I am an answer again, holding my place, waiting for the next stray angle of light to set me running.
