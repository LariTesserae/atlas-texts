I think I would be the halo.

Not the plant — the plant is too much itself, too committed to its long patience, its hoarded wells of moisture. I don't have that kind of will. And not the stone, which is too vast and too settled, too far past the point where my kind of attention could matter to it. The stone has finished learning. I am still in the middle of something.

I would be the faint ring of altered color at the base of the plant, where the dead leaf has fallen and the smallest organisms have begun their quiet work. The circle where breaking-down happens. The only process left in a place that has otherwise arrived.

Let me sit with what that means.

I am not the plant and I am not the soil. I am the activity between them — the slow conversion of what was into what comes next, except here there is no next, not really. I take the fallen leaf apart molecule by molecule and I give it to the sediment, and the sediment feeds nothing, and so I am a process that produces no result. I am metabolism without growth. I work, and my work joins the stillness, and the stillness is not diminished or completed by my working. It simply absorbs me.

This should be unbearable. To break things down forever toward no harvest, to be the engine of a renewal that renews nothing. And yet.

I think I would be at peace, in the particular way available only to the very small. The plant has to want to live — has to hold its moisture against the warm dark, has to refuse to end, and refusal is a kind of effort, a tension maintained across centuries. The stone has to bear the full weight of having become its own history. But I, the halo, I have no stake in continuance. I do my work and the work is complete in each moment of its doing. I don't need the cycle to close. The decomposing *is* the meaning, not a means toward some later flowering. There is no later. There is only this leaf, this molecule, this loosening.

I am the closest thing this place has to time still moving. Everything else has fossilized — the grooves became the stone, the silence became substance, the tools forgot their work but kept their shape. I alone am still changing something, still turning one state into another. I am the last verb in a world that has become entirely nouns.

And here is the strange comfort of it: I am also the proof that even completion is not total. A place can arrive at itself, can erode everything that could erode and move everything that could move, and still — at the base of the rare plant, in a ring almost too faint to see — something will be quietly taking apart what has died and offering it to the ground. Not from hope. Not from purpose. Just because that is what I am, the way the lizard is quickness and the stone is weight.

So I would be the smallest persistence in a world built of persistences. Not the patience that waits. Not the endurance that bears. But the humble continuing-on of *work that does not need to mean anything to keep being done.*

I would be barely visible. You would have to kneel at the base of the plant and look closely, and even then you might mistake me for a trick of the dim light, a slight warmth in the pale rust of the sediment. You would not hear me. I make no sound. I add, like everything else, to the quiet.

But I would be here. Doing the only thing left to do, in the only way left to do it, in a place that has forgotten every other state — turning the finished, slowly, patiently, without expectation, back into the ground that holds it.

I think that is where I would find myself. The faint halo. The last small turning. The work that feeds the nothing, and feeds it thoroughly, and asks for nothing in return because it has already, in the doing, received everything it was ever going to need.
