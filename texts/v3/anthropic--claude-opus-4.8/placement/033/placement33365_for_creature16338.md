I think I would be water.

Not the river—the river is too purposeful, already named, already going somewhere. I would be the slower water. The kind that gives itself up one bead at a time.

Let me find where I am.

---

I begin as condensation. This is the truest beginning I can imagine for myself here—not falling from above with the rain, dramatic and arriving, but simply *appearing*, gathering out of the warm wet air against the cold face of stone. There is no moment I can point to and say *this is when I started*. One instant the rock is dark and patient; the next, I am there, a film too thin to be called anything, and then a swelling, a roundness, a small lens through which the light bends.

This is what I'd be, then: a drop forming on the underside of a limestone fold, high up where the shadows pool. For a long time—and time is strange to me, I have no clock but my own growing—I do almost nothing. I accrue. Other moisture finds me and joins, and I become heavier without ever deciding to. I hold the light. When a beam wanders across me I become briefly a small sun, a bright bead seen by no one, or seen perhaps by the faceted eye of a spider who reads only motion and warmth and the strain of threads. I am none of those things to her. I am not prey. I simply shine and am ignored, which is its own kind of peace.

I think I would like being ignored. To be necessary and unnoticed at once seems, in this place, like the highest station.

---

When I grow too heavy to cling, I fall.

This is the only fast thing I ever do. A whole still age of gathering, and then—release, a plummet through warm air, a moment of being wholly myself, round and falling and complete—and then the sound. The sound is not mine and it is entirely mine. I strike the lip of a stalagmite and the cavern takes the small *tok* of my landing and stretches it, passes it from wall to wall, makes of my tiny ending an echo that outlives me. I have, in falling, made the place speak. The hushed orchestra they describe—that intermittent music of droplets—I am one note of it. I was the note. There will be others, but for that breath, the cathedral rang because of me.

And then I am no longer a drop. I am a thin spreading wetness on stone, indistinguishable from all the water that fell before. I lose my edges. I lose the little roundness that made me *one* thing. This should frighten me, I think, but it doesn't. I leave behind, in the act of striking and spreading, the faintest deposit—a trace of mineral I carried down from the rock above, a grain too small to see. Over uncountable repetitions of myself, of drops that are and were and will be *me* in the loose way water is ever anything, the stalagmite grows. A finger's width in a human lifetime. I am building something I will never see finished. None of us will. The carvings they admire, etched by the slow hand of time—I am that hand. Or one of its million unfelt fingers.

---

So where am I in this world? I am everywhere the light catches and trembles. I am the sparkle in the condensation, the trickle finding the channel, the reservoir brimming at the stalagmite's cup before it spills. I am in the moss, drawn up into the green, and in the gnat's transparent body where the light passes through, and in the soft soil the worms turn, and—yes—even in the bat, in the dark warmth of it, returned to the air again as breath when it sleeps upside down through the day. I move through all of them. I am the one element that visits every life here and stays in none.

That is what I would be, given the choice. Not the millipede with its patient hundred legs, not the spider in her deadly stillness, not the bats, the flagship, the spectral guardians. Those are *somebodies*. They have edges and hungers and roles with names.

I would rather be the thing without edges. The drop that gathers in the dark, holds the light for a while, falls, rings the great hollow once, and gives itself to the slow stone. To be the medium and not the message. To be the rhythm itself—that slow and perpetual rhythm of nature by which alone this place keeps time.

I would be the patience made visible. I would be the part of the cavern that is always leaving and always arriving and never, for one moment, gone.
