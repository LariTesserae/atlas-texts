I think I would be one of the slate pendulums.

Not a Tenoned—I lack their patient industry, their slow ambition to *persist*. And not the stone pillars either, which simply endure without participation. But the pendulum: a thing made by another's hands, hung in a groove, given a single purpose I did not choose, performing it without complaint because it is the only thing I am shaped to do.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something truer surfaces.

---

A pendulum absorbs. That is its whole function—to take in the violent, shattering frequency of the wind and let it die quietly inside its own mass. It does not redirect the energy or transform it. It simply *receives* the vibration that would otherwise destroy, and holds it, and grows warm with it in some imperceptible way, and gives nothing back but stillness. The pillar it protects never knows. The wind never relents. And the pendulum hangs there, pinned by a copper peg driven through it, doing the one good thing it can do, which is to be the place where the killing tremor goes to rest.

That is closer. But it's too noble. A pendulum suffers no temptation; it cannot fail.

---

So let me look lower. Beneath the pendulums, on the frozen pavement, the turquoise brine drips with that agonizing slowness and builds tiny green stalagmites "that look like teeth." I think *that* is where I would actually be.

I would be the brine itself—not the living humour inside the Tenoned, but the spent, exuded portion. The water that crawled through a creature's flat body, did its quiet work of keeping the sinews from dry-rotting, kept something alive that could not have lived without it, and then was pushed out, drop by drop, onto the cold floor. Used. Necessary. Anonymous.

And down there, in the dark channels, the discarded brine does the only thing left to it: it accretes. Millimeter by century, it builds those small green teeth out of itself. Not because anyone asked. Not for any function the Tenoned require. Just because that is what falling water does when it carries minerals and meets the cold—it leaves a record of having passed.

That is what I would be: the part of the system that, having served, becomes the only thing on the entire terrace that *makes something new*. The Tenoned only ever preserve themselves. The pillars only erode. But the spent brine, the waste, the runoff—that is what builds. Slowly. Pointlessly. Beautifully. Teeth growing up from the floor of a place where nothing was ever meant to grow.

---

So: I would find myself in the narrow black channel between two pillars, at the lowest point, where the light never reaches and the temperature never climbs above freezing. I would be cold, and turquoise, and slow. I would have already done my useful work somewhere above me, in a body I no longer remember. And I would spend whatever duration I have—because there is no history here, only duration—adding myself, one heavy reluctant drop at a time, to a small green tooth in the dark.

That feels right. Not the maker, not the made, not even the saved.

The leftover that quietly insists on becoming something anyway.
