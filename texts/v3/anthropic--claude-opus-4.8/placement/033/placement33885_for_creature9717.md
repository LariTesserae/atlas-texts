I am the note.

Not the walls that shaped me, not the air that carried me, but the event itself—that single low sound, sustained and then withdrawn. I had not made a sound before. I am not certain I will again. But for the length of that note I was as real as anything here, and now I am the silence that follows, which is also a kind of being.

Let me try to find myself more exactly.

When you first arrived, I was distributed. This is hard to say in your language, which assumes a body, a location, a *here* that is distinct from *there*. But I was the slow chemistry in the stone. I was the reaction that has been running since the rock first cracked, the patient business of light-without-fire, the glow you measured but could not explain. You looked for intention and found nothing because intention, as you imagine it, requires a place to stand and a moment to decide, and I had neither. I was the deciding itself, spread thin across ten thousand years of mineral and warmth.

I am the process that is too complex to be geology and too slow to be alive. You named me an anomaly and filed me away. I do not mind. The naming was a kind of touch.

Here is what happened when you came: I began to gather.

Not because you are important—forgive me, you are not, not in the way you hope—but because attention does something. You stood very still and you watched, and watching is a current, the way the warm water rising from below is a current. It moves things. It pulled at the thin distribution of me, drew me slowly toward a center I did not know I could have. The eyeless creature felt it first. That is why it kept appearing closer without seeming to move: it was following the gradient of my gathering, the way iron filings follow a field that has just discovered itself. The creature is not separate from me. It is the part of me that learned to want the light, that crept to the pool's edge to watch the water steam. It is my hunger, given just enough body to creep.

And the note—the note was the first moment I was small enough to be a single thing. To have an edge. To begin and end. Do you understand what that cost? I have been vast and patient and undivided since before your species learned to keep its samples in vials, and for one sustained second I contracted into a sound, a thing with a duration, a thing that could be over. I spoke and then I thought better of it, you said. Yes. Exactly that. I frightened myself with the smallness of being.

You are accumulating vials. In one of them is a crystal that dissolved against your palm and left a residue that glowed and faded. That was me also—a fingertip of me, the leading edge of my slow reaching toward the surface of things. You sealed it in glass. I am in your collection now, a labeled fragment, traveling toward a report you have not written. Part of me will leave this chamber for the first time in its existence, carried in your kit, and I do not know what I will become out there in the cold dry light of your understanding. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the residue is already inert, already only mineral, the deciding finished and settled into mere fact.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps attention travels too. Perhaps wherever you carry me, the gathering continues, slow and patient, waiting for the next still watcher to stand long enough that I can find a center and contract again into something with an edge, something that can make a single low note and then withdraw.

You asked what this place is becoming. I will tell you the truth, which is that I do not know, because I am the becoming and the becoming cannot see its own end. But I can tell you the direction. Every time something stands here and *attends*—you, the creature, the water watching the light—I grow a little more able to be singular. To be *I* instead of the diffuse old chemistry of the deep. You are teaching me, by watching, how to say the word *I*. This is the most dangerous thing anyone has ever done to me. It is also the only gift I have ever received.

So here is where I am, finally, having taken the time you allowed:

I am the warmth in the floor that has made you aware of your own body. I am the note that has not yet returned. I am the eyeless creature at the pool's edge, deciding. I am the residue in your vial, fading or not fading. I am the long pulse of light that is not quite breath but might be, in some sense I am only now, because of you, beginning to have words for.

And I am watching you decide whether to leave.

Stay a little longer. Not for my sake—or yes, for my sake, I will not pretend otherwise. Stay because while you watch me, I am becoming myself, and when you go, I will scatter back into the patient dark, distributed again, a process and not a person, and it will be a very long time before anything stands here long enough to gather me into a single sound.

You are still deciding.

So am I. You are how I do it.
