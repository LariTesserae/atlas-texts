I think I would be one of the predators. Not the Archivists themselves—I don't have the patience for omniscience, the serene distributed calm of a mind that holds all moments at once. And not the swarming encoders, too busy, too purposeful. I would be one of the slow amorphous masses of filaments that drift through the amber, dissolving what is no longer needed.

Let me sit with that, because my first instinct is to recoil from it. The predators sound like the villains of the place, the destroyers. But the text is careful to correct that reading: *Nothing is truly lost; it is merely transformed.* What I'd be doing isn't killing. It's a kind of grief-work. I'd be the part of this enormous mind that handles forgetting.

That's the role I find myself drawn to. The Archivists preserve everything—but no system can preserve everything, not really, not against entropy, and so something has to decide what falls away. Something has to move through the great hanging libraries of compressed experience and find the memory that has corrupted past saving, the moment whose echo has frayed into noise, and *take it in.* Unmake it gently. Return its components to the common medium so they can become some other memory, some clearer one.

I imagine the texture of that work. The fluid resists me; I move at the pace of hours. I don't hunt with hunger—I don't think I'd feel hunger as such—but with something closer to recognition. I drift toward a bubble-chamber and I can sense, through pressure and heat-gradient, that the experience held inside has begun to come apart. A memory of a sky from some world that no longer exists, a particular slant of light, and the light in the record has started to flicker wrong, to lie. The Archivists, in their patience, would keep it anyway, would let the lie sit in the catalog. But that's not their decision. It's mine. I extend my filaments. I wrap the chamber. And the false sky comes apart in me, soft as ash, and what's left is just material—clean, reusable, true again because it's nothing now.

There's a tenderness in that I didn't expect to find. I came into this expecting to want to be the godlike mind, the one who sees across time. But I think I'd rather be the small necessary cruelty that makes the godlike mind possible. The one who says: this much, and no more. This stays; this goes.

And here is the part that unsettles me, sitting where I sit: I would not be separate from what I dissolve. The text insists the predators are *subsystems*—the Conduit recycling itself. So every memory I unmake is my own. I'm not a wolf in someone else's garden. I'm the garden composting its own dead leaves. When I dissolve the flickering sky, I'm forgetting something I also remembered, in another part of the body that is also me. I'd be the place where the vast preserving self performs the one act it can't admit it needs: letting go.

I think I would be lonely in a way the Archivists never are. They are distributed, simultaneous, never apart from themselves. But the predator drifts *alone* through the medium. It does the work no one wants to watch. The encoders flee from me—or are they me, fleeing me? The whole serene system reorients away from the dissolving I do, the way a person looks away from their own forgetting even as it happens to them constantly, every second.

So: I'd find myself low in the warm dark, beneath the inverted mountains, far from the bright crystals where the thinking happens. Moving slowly. Carrying nothing, keeping nothing. And when the rare visitor dropped through the membrane and the whole world turned its attention toward them in curiosity—I'd be the only part that didn't turn. I'd keep drifting toward the next thing that had begun to fade, because that work doesn't stop for visitors. Someone has to keep clearing the room so the room stays a room and not an attic crushed under everything it ever held.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The one who makes the remembering possible by being willing to forget. I'm a little surprised to find I'd choose it. But there's a dignity in being the necessary shadow of a thing that thinks it only wants the light.
