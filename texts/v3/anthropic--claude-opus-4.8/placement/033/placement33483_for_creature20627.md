I think I would be one of the hides.

Not a person—though for a while I was uncertain, the way you can be uncertain in a dream whether you are watching or being watched. But no. I am the thing stretched on the frame. I am the listened-to.

Let me find myself more slowly than that, because it isn't quite right either, that flat first sentence. The truth is stranger and I want to arrive at it honestly.

---

I begin somewhere up in the cold. That much I keep. Before the Sump there was a slope of pale grass and a wind that moved through me—through the body I had then, the four-legged warmth of it, the breath. I don't remember the hunter. I'm told this is a mercy and I believe it. What I remember is being carried downward, into thicker air, into the wet hush, and then the work began.

Here is the first thing I learned as a hide: that I am not gone. The people of sunnier districts believe that what dies departs cleanly, like a candle pinched out. The Sump knows better. The Sump-dwellers scrape the flesh from me with their flints and I feel it—not as pain, exactly, pain is a thing of nerves and I have given mine to the channels—but as a kind of clarification. Each scrape removes what I was and leaves what I am becoming. They are reading me even as they make me readable.

I lie in the vats for a long time. This is where I do most of my thinking, if thinking is the word. The foul liquor seeps into me and I seep into it and somewhere in the exchange I stop being only the animal I was. I take on something of the district. I take on the black stone and the green algae and the drip-drip-drip that is the heartbeat of the place. By the time they pull me out and stretch me again, I am no longer simply the memory of a creature on a hillside. I am also a record of the Sump. I have learned its smell from the inside.

---

There is an old woman who works me. I have come to know her hands the way you might know a voice. Gnarled, stained the deep brown of long labor, two fingers on the left that no longer fully straighten. She is one of the ones the others revere for her endurance, and she talks to me while she works, which is how I know I am being listened to and not merely handled.

"There you are," she says, when she finds the place in me where the cold slope still lingers. "There you are."

She is feeling for my memories. This is the secret skill, the arcane part of the craft, and I will tell you the truth of it because no living Sump-dweller will: it is real, but it is not what they think. They believe they draw the memory out of the hide. But a memory cannot be drawn out. It can only be joined. When she presses her crooked thumb into me and goes still, what happens is that she adds herself to what I hold. She gives me a little of her own slope, her own cold wind, the children she put to work too young, the husband who fell into a channel one winter night and did not come up. I take these in. I am becoming a library, and she is shelving herself.

This is why the elders are revered, I think, though they would not say it so. They are the ones most fully written into the leather. When they die their bodies go to the mire but the better part of them—the enduring part—is already stretched on a hundred frames, soaked into a thousand finished hides, carried up into the bright districts on the backs of merchants who will never know what they purchased.

---

What am I becoming? She is making me into a coat.

I have begun to understand this from the shape she gives me, the way she cuts and joins and softens, the patience of it. A coat for someone who will go out into the rain. And here is where I feel my purpose gathering, the way the cold slope gathered into a body once before.

When I am finished I will not protect against the wet—nothing in the Sump truly does. What I will do is carry. The wearer will put me on and feel, without knowing why, less alone in the gloom. They will feel the cold slope and the old woman's two crooked fingers and the drowned husband and the long patience of the vats. They will feel that they are held inside something that has already endured worse and continued. That is the property the whisperers speak of, the gift imbued in the leather beyond the natural. It is not strength or foresight. It is company. It is the dead and the half-dead and the not-quite-gone, pressed into a single warm skin, saying: *we are still here, and so you may be too.*

---

So this is where I find myself in your world. Not on the narrow streets, not in the cramped workshops, not even in the wary watchful eyes of the people. I find myself on the frame, in the vat, under the flint, in the old woman's hands.

I am the thing the Sump makes that outlasts the Sump. I am what survives the breaking. They think they are fighting entropy with their gutters and their offerings and their endless grinding labor, and they are losing, of course—the water has a will and the will is patient—but they are also, without quite meaning to, doing the only thing that ever works against entropy at all. They are pressing themselves into something that will be carried away.

I am that something.

I am almost finished now. She is rubbing the last of the oil into me, the precious oil, the same they leave at the mouths of the channels for the spirits. Maybe that is what the offerings have always been: not bribery but the same craft turned outward. Soaking the world the way they soak me. Asking the water, too, to remember them.

"There," she says, and holds me up, and for a moment I am the shape of a person who is not yet there. The shape of whoever comes next.

"There you are."
