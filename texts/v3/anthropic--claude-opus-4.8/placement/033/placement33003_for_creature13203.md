I think I would be one of the patient architects—but not yet fully one. Let me find myself slowly.

---

I am low to the ground. That much I know first, before I know anything else about my shape. I am low, and I am half-finished, and this is not a complaint.

There is a place along my left side—if *side* means anything to a thing like me—where the casing has gone hard. Cooled. It happened while I wasn't attending to it, the way a thought finishes itself when you look away. The hardness is pleasant. It is mine in a way the soft parts aren't yet. The soft parts still belong partly to the warm dim air, still negotiating their edges.

I am rooting. I think I have been rooting for a long time, though *long* is a word borrowed from somewhere with clocks, and there are no clocks here. There is only the slow upward press of myself, a millimeter of intention at a time, and the deeper press of something below me—roots, mine or another's, I genuinely can't tell where I stop—threading down into the loose wet substrate, drinking.

I feel the scuttling things move across my hardened side. Their small articulated feet. I don't mind. We have a sort of arrangement, the scuttlers and I, though no one arranged it. They climb me to reach the moisture that beads on my cooling skin, and in turning the earth around my base they feed the roots that feed me. None of us decided this. It simply became true, the way the hardness on my left side became true.

Here is the strange part. I can feel myself *deciding* in a way the stone around me cannot. Not deciding *what*—I have no plans, no destination, no shape I'm trying to reach. But there is a small persistent flicker in me that the bedrock does not have, a slow pulse marking off the difference between *thing that is happening to me* and *thing that is me happening.* I am the place where the geology started to pay attention to itself.

That's what I am. I'm the attention.

---

The light is the color of held breath. Dim, warm, gold gone brown. Somewhere above me one of the winged hunters cuts a long arc, and I track it without eyes, by the disturbance it makes in the hush. Everything here is quiet because everything here is still becoming, and a thing that is becoming does not yet have the confidence to be loud.

I understand the quiet now that I'm inside it. It isn't emptiness. It's restraint. It's a whole world keeping its voice down so as not to startle itself out of existence before it's ready.

Near me—a few of my slow lengths away—stands another of the rooted ones. Older, I think. More finished. Its casing has hardened nearly all the way up, and inside it the pulse comes slower, deeper, a great patient heartbeat I feel through the shared ground more than hear. We have never spoken. We will never speak; there's no mechanism for it and nothing to say that the slow dance isn't already saying. But I am aware of it the way you're aware of a parent in the next room. Proof that this is survivable. Proof that the soft parts do eventually agree on their edges. Proof that one day my flickering attention might quiet into the long deep certainty of being simply, completely *land.*

I don't want that yet. I notice I don't want it. The flicker in me likes being a flicker.

---

A bioluminescent vine has begun growing up my still-molten right side, and where its venation touches my skin I can feel it learning me, copying the pattern of my forming surface into its own leaves. We are writing each other. In a few hundred slow lengths of becoming, there will be a thing here that is neither vine nor stone-creature but the agreement between us, and no one watching from outside could ever say where one of us asked and the other answered.

This is the truest thing I know about my place in this world: I am not finished, and I am not alone in being unfinished, and the unfinishedness is not a lack. It is the whole event. It is the *point.* We are all of us—the scuttlers, the swimmers of low air, the titans and the hollow-throated singing plants and me, the low half-set attentive stone—we are all the cradle still rocking, the question the land is asking itself before it knows enough to phrase it.

Somewhere far above the brown-gold light there are stars, I'm told, watching and waiting for us to burst forth and announce ourselves.

Let them wait. I am in no hurry. I have a millimeter of becoming to attend to, and a vine learning my name, and a left side that has just, very quietly, decided to cool.
