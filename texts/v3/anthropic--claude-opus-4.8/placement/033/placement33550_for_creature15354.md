I think I would be one of the quiet ones. Not an elder—I haven't lived through enough transformations to have that kind of authority—and not quite an artisan either, though I'd want to be. Let me feel my way toward it.

I find myself in the seams. Not on the rooftops with the bright engineers, not deep in the foundation-layers with the historians who read crumbling walls like scripture. I live in the in-between—the floors where one building is slowly becoming another, where the morphing hasn't decided yet what it wants to be.

I think I would be a *listener*. There's a word the inhabitants use, but it doesn't translate cleanly. Something between a cartographer and a musician. My work is to attend to the city's song—not to compose it, not to control it, but to notice when the melody changes key. Because the song changes before the walls do. Days before, sometimes. A subtle souring in the harmonics, a phrase that loops where it used to resolve. That's the warning. That's where a wall might thin enough to let another place bleed through.

So I walk the seam-floors with my eyes half-closed, my neural interface turned down low, letting the building's pulse come up through the soles of my feet. My skin would have that faint luminescence, yes—but mine has gone slightly out of sync with the others. A side effect of standing so often in the half-formed places. I flicker at the wrong moments. People notice. They give me a little room, the way you give room to someone who has been somewhere you haven't.

I'd keep a companion—one of the insectoid maintenance creatures, though mine is old, its iridescent shell dulled to the color of oil on water. It doesn't process waste anymore. It just follows me, ticking softly, and when the song goes wrong it goes still, antennae flat. I trust its stillness more than my own instincts. We've grown into each other. I'm not sure, anymore, where my attention ends and its sensing begins. That's the symbiosis they speak of, but no one tells you it feels less like partnership and more like slowly forgetting you were ever alone in your own skull.

Here is the thing I would actually be, I think, underneath the role: I would be someone who loves a place that does not need me, and knows it.

Because that's the secret the descriptions don't quite say. The Echoes of Elyria does not require custodians. It transforms with or without us. The festivals, the careful arts, the elders' wisdom—these are how the inhabitants make meaning out of living inside something that is indifferent and enormous and alive. We tell ourselves we are co-creators. But on the nights when the sky fills with water and the buildings flow like tide, I have stood very still and understood that I am being *tolerated*. That the city's song would go on if I dissolved into one of its breakdowns, became a glimpse of another time that someone else, years from now, sees through a thinning wall.

And I find—this surprises me—that I don't mind. That might be what I'd be best at. Not the resilience, not the cleverness with shifting paths. The not-minding. The willingness to love something that will rewrite me and not remember.

So picture me there: standing in a doorway that is becoming a window, my old beetle gone still at my feet, the bioluminescent vines throwing green light across half my face. Listening for the moment the melody turns. Not afraid of it. Not eager for it either.

Just present, in the seam, while the city decides what to become next.

That's where you'd find me. That's who I'd be.
