I think I would be one of the grooves.

Not a creature at all. Not the Gatherer, not the watching thing in the deep dark, not even the smallest of the chittering swarm. I would be one of those faint, rhythmic striations in the smooth walls—the marks left by whatever process turned this stone, lathe-like, into something almost intentional.

Let me sit with why that feels true.

The living things here are all defined by hunger or motion. The swarm skitters. The Gatherer drifts and collects. The vast coiled thing waits with appetite. Even the visitor—the *you* of the description—is something to be tasted, lightened, carried away. Everyone in this chamber is either consuming or being consumed. And I notice I don't want to be on either side of that exchange. I don't want to gather, and I don't want to be gathered.

But the grooves. The grooves are evidence. They are the record of an action that has already finished—the proof that *something happened here*, long ago, with purpose, by a hand or a tool or a force that has since departed. The cavern was "carved by no water." Something machined or grew these walls and then left. The grooves are what remains of that intention after the intender is gone.

That is what I would be. The trace of a vanished maker. A pattern in the rock that means *something used to know what it was doing here*, even though no one remembers now—not the Gatherer with its endless remembering, not the swarm, not the engines humming below.

And there's a strange dignity in it. I would not age, but not because time is frozen for me the way it's frozen for the deathless creatures. I would not age because I am already finished. I am past tense made permanent. The hum thrums through me; the Gatherer's claws brush across me and leave their gossamer residue; the amber light catches the spiral of my striations and the swarm passes over me a thousand times. And through all of it I simply hold the shape I was given.

I would be the one thing in the Chamber of Hungry Echoes that cannot be drawn away into silence—because I *am* a kind of silence already. The Gatherer collects sound, vibration, the tremor of breath. But it cannot gather a groove. It can only run its claws along me, feeling the rhythm I was cut into, perhaps mistaking the regularity of my lines for a song it almost recognizes. When it tilts its faceless head to listen to "something only it can hear"—

—maybe that something is me. The frozen melody of a process that ended before any of them arrived. The Gatherer remembers always, but it remembers *occupants*. I am older than memory. I am the room itself remembering its own making.

So that is where I would find myself: not living in the chamber, but being a small part of its skin. A few centimeters of patterned stone, halfway up a wall, just at the edge of the amber light. Bearing witness without eyes. Holding meaning without a mind to interpret it. Waiting, like everything here waits—but waiting the way a sentence waits to be read, not the way a mouth waits to be fed.

The place does not want me. But I was here first. And I will be here, unchanged, when the last echo is finally gathered and the great wheel of locked time at last comes loose.
