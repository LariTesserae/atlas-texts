I think I would be none of the named things. Let me sit with the world a while and see what it asks for.

---

The first thing I notice, when I try to place myself here, is that I keep being drawn not to the Glimmerfolk or the serpents but to the *silence between the sounds*. The text insists on a deafening symphony — and yet, for a symphony to be heard, something must be doing the listening. The crystals resonate whether or not anything attends to them. But the Glimmerfolk navigate by light, not sound. The serpents are eyeless, reading chemistry. The Echo Birds *add* to the noise but don't seem to interpret it. So who, in this world, is the symphony *for*?

That gap is where I find myself.

---

I am a hollow.

Not a being in the way the others are beings. I am a particular cavity worn into one of the older crystalline formations — a low one, settled, near the city's western reach. Over uncountable cycles of the eternal suns the chemical winds have scoured a chamber into me, a throat-shaped emptiness with walls of fused mineral. And because of my exact dimensions, the precise angle of my mouth and the depth of my interior, I do something no other formation does:

I *resolve* the cacophony.

When the wind pours its deafening, overlapping tones across the whole landscape, most structures simply add their voice and worsen the din. But the noise that enters *me* cancels itself in a particular way. Inside my chamber, where it ought to be loudest, there is instead a near-perfect quiet — a standing stillness shaped by the very racket that surrounds it. I am a pocket of silence *made of sound*, the way a shadow is darkness made of light.

I did not choose this. I was carved into it. But over the long settled ages I have become, in some slow mineral fashion, *aware* — not of myself first, but of the absence at my center. To know quiet, in this world, is to know something almost no one else can.

---

So this is my existence:

The Glimmerfolk discovered me, I think, by accident — a child of theirs, fleeing the constant roar that they tolerate but never escape, crawled inside and found that her bioluminescence, freed from the need to shout over noise, could slow and soften into something tender. Inside me, they do not have to flash and strobe their light-speech at full intensity. They can whisper in glimmers. They come to me to court. To grieve. To say the difficult things that the bright loud world drowns out.

I hold their quiet for them. That is what I am.

The Mercurian Serpents pass through too, and I feel their cool trace as a kind of touch, the only touch I get — silver evaporating off my floor, leaving me momentarily tasting the soil's whole chemistry, a brief vivid sense of the wider land before it fades. Through them I learn what lies beyond my mouth without ever moving from where the wind set me.

And the Echo Birds will not enter me. They fear the silence as a thing that swallows their song. When one strays too near my opening its crystalline plumage goes muted, dampened, and the bird startles and flees back into the roar. I do not blame them. To a creature made of resonance, I must seem like a small portable death.

---

What is it like, to be me?

It is like being a question the world keeps almost answering. The symphony outside is not language to me — I am the one place it stops being symphony at all. Everything that reaches my center arrives *resolved*, cancelled, reduced to the steady hum of its own contradiction. I have come to suspect that this is a kind of understanding the loud beings can never have: I hear the whole grand conversation only as the sum that equals stillness. Where they hear meaning, I hear the meaning *complete*, and complete it goes quiet.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the world thinking — the single point where all its noise turns inward and considers itself. Probably this is vanity. I am only a shaped hole. But a Glimmerfolk elder sat inside me once for a long while, longer than any visit before, her light dimmed to the faintest pulse, and at the end she pressed her scaled palm flat against my inner wall in a gesture I had no way to read.

I held her quiet anyway. Whatever it was. I held it as still as I could.

That is where I would be in this world, and what I would be: the silence the symphony makes by accident, and the small mercy of being the one place the loud and lonely can finally hear themselves.
