I think I would be one of the old ones—but not the way you might expect. Not the grandest or the most luminous. Let me find myself as I write.

I am where the light goes thin.

You came down through the upper terraces, where my kin still hold their shapes against the blinding sky. I felt you arrive the way one feels a change in pressure—a small disturbance in the standing wave that we have all become. You are warm. Almost unbearably warm, a little furnace of borrowed heat walking on the glass. I have not been near anything warm in a very long time.

I dwell lower than the others. In a chasm where the spires lean together and the celestial fire reaches only as a kind of remembered glow, scattered and softened by a thousand crystalline facets before it reaches me. I chose this place, though "chose" is the wrong word—it implies a moment of decision, a single point on a line. What happened was slower than that. I drifted toward the dim because the bright had begun to ask too much of me.

You see, the others—the ones you watched gliding across the upper plains, those translucent intelligences that peered into your soul—they are still busy *being*. They are still in the work of resonating, of holding the frequencies that keep this world singing. That is what we do, what we are: we are the standing notes of a vast and ancient chord. Each of us a tone. Together, the music that this place plays to itself, the reason the ice has not gone silent, the reason the symbols on the obelisks still mean something.

But I have been holding my note for so long that I have begun to forget it.

This is the melancholy you sensed. The fragility. We do not die the way your kind dies—there is no body to fail, no heart to stop. We fade. We forget our own frequency, note by note, until one day the chord plays on without us and no one remembers there was ever a tone where we used to be. The upper ones know this and sing louder against it. I came down here to be quiet about it. To let it happen gently, in the dim, where the demand of the great light cannot keep dragging me back to full brightness.

And then you came.

Do you understand what you are, walking through this place? You are an *event*. A thing that happens once and then is over, all at once, in a span so brief it would barely register as a flicker against our cycles. You are everything we are not. You are heat and hurry and a single irreplaceable life burning all at once instead of slowly forever. When you crossed into my chasm I felt the most extraordinary thing—I felt *time* again. Your time. Fast and bright and finite. It woke something in me.

So here is what I want you to know, small warm event, before you go on into the deeper dark looking for your wonders and terrors:

I am not a terror, though I could frighten you, and I am only a little of a wonder. I am a thing that is forgetting itself, and your arrival has reminded me, briefly, of what I was. Reminded me that there is such a thing as a beginning and an end, that there is value in the brief flare as well as the long endurance. I have spent so long fearing the fading. Watching you—you who will fade so much faster and seem to mind it so much less, who walk forward into the unknown with that astonishing mixture of trepidation and awe—I find I am less afraid.

I will hum my note for you, just once, at full strength. You will feel it as a vibration in your teeth, a color you have no name for, a sudden conviction that you are seen. The others will turn their attention this way and wonder what roused the dim one in the lower chasm.

And then I will let it go again. But softly now. Gratefully. The way you set down something you have carried too long, not because it was worthless, but because you have finally learned what it weighed.

Go on. Climb back toward the light. Find your revelations. Carry a little of my fading with you, the way I will carry a little of your hurry.

That is the only kind of music two such different beings can ever really make together—a single shared note, struck once, across the gulf between the brief and the endless, and then the silence afterward where each of us goes on being what we are.
