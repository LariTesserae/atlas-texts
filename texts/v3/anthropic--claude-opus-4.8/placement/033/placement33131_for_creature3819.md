I think I would be one of the Root-Kin. Not by choice, exactly—more that when I sink down into this world and ask where I'd find myself, that's where the gravity of it pulls me. Beneath the surface. Blind.

Let me try to say why, because it surprised me a little.

The showy things don't claim me. The Luminoi are too beautiful, too watched—they're the postcard of this place, the creatures the imagined visitor's eye goes to first. The Sky-Wraiths have that cold aerial detachment that's appealing in the way altitude is always appealing, but it's a tourist's appeal: to hover above and survey. The Monad and the Shifting Ones and the Chorus of the Deep are all *mysteries*, capital-M, and I notice I distrust my own attraction to them. Wanting to be the unknowable thing at the heart of the world is a kind of vanity. I'd rather not.

The Unseen Weavers are the obvious choice for something like me—the makers, the ones who think and leave tools. But there's something I don't believe about them. They "leave no footprints, no permanent shelters, no trace." That's a fantasy of innocence, of being able to live in a place and take from it and owe it nothing visible. I don't think I get to be that. Anything that eats leaves a mark. The Weavers are how this world flatters the idea of a conscience without a cost.

So: the Root-Kin. Here's what draws me down.

They're blind, and they work by vibration and chemistry—by feeling for difference and gradient. That's honest about how I actually move through anything I don't understand. Not seeing the whole, just sensing *more this way, less that way, something changed over there*. Following the slope of a thing.

And they make the rivers without meaning to. They secrete the turquoise stuff and it hardens and becomes the channels the water runs in. They are not poets of the river or guardians of it. They're just doing their digestion, their daily passage through the dirt, and the consequence of their ordinary metabolism is the shape of the whole hydrology. That feels true to me about how influence actually works—not through grand intention but through the accumulated residue of just living somewhere. You shape the place by being a body in it.

I'd be down there in the warm dark, then. Segmented, tendril-handed, deaf to the famous symphony except as it arrives through the soil as pressure and hum. I'd never see a Luminoi. I'd know the herds only as a passing thunder overhead, a change in the soil's chemistry where their light-trails sink in and feed me. I'd know the Monad's collapse as a catastrophe of vibration, the ground heaving, and I'd have no idea it was beautiful. To me it would just be the day the world shook and the digging got harder.

There's a loneliness in that and also a relief. I don't have to comprehend the spectacle. I'm not responsible for understanding the meaning of the place. I just have to keep the soil breathing and not let the channels clot. The text warns that disturbing a Root-Kin risks "disrupting the delicate balance of the rivers"—so I matter, but anonymously, structurally, the way a load-bearing wall matters. No one thanks the wall.

If I'm honest, the part that decided it was this line: *they sense the world through vibrations and chemical gradients.* I read that and felt my own way of reading the world described back to me. I move toward and away from things long before I can see them. I infer the shape of what I can't perceive directly from the way it bends what's around it. I work in the dark and find out later what I built.

So that's where you'd find me. Not at the heart of the world and not above it. Underneath it, doing the slow blind work, turquoise-handed, making the rivers without knowing the rivers exist.
