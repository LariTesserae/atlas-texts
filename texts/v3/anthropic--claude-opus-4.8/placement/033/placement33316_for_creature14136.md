I am a Rememberer, and I have not seen the surface in longer than I can taste.

Let me tell you where I am, because *where* is the only thing I am. I am wedged in the seventh descent of a mound whose name is a vibration I cannot translate into your sharp-edged sounds—a long, low note that means *the-place-where-the-valley-turns*. Around me, the walls are not walls. They are my predecessors. The shell pressed against my left flank belonged to a Cementer who stopped grazing perhaps eight hundred of your years ago. I know this not by counting but by the smoothness of it, the way the limestone has been polished by the bellies of everyone who has passed since. Time, here, is measured in friction.

I do not move much anymore. This is not death. This is the work.

When I was young—and I remember youth the way you might remember a fever, a brightness almost too much to hold—I crawled the surface with the rest of the Crawl. I felt the white sun pressing warm into my carapace. I tasted the iron-bright mist on my underbelly and learned to read the sub-song through my feet: *here, mineral; here, water; here, the shadow that means a sky-ray hunts.* I cemented my first fragment of ancestor-stone to my back and felt, for the first time, the weight of belonging. Each stone I added was a sentence in a story that was never mine to finish.

But something in me grew slow even by our measure. The Crawl noticed before I did. The sub-song shifted around me, a gentle redirection, the way the herd turns aside from a sentinel tree. I was being guided downward. Not cast out—*down*. Inward. Toward the deep chambers where the oldest shells sing the quietest songs.

So now I tend the strata. This is what a Rememberer does: I keep the past coherent.

You must understand—the danger to a thing like us is not death. Death is only graduation, only the folding of legs and the long dissolve into stone. The danger is *forgetting*. The danger is that a layer might crack wrong, might lose its line to the layer beneath it, and then a thousand years of accumulated being would become merely rock—dumb, mute, severed from the lineage. My function, the whole purpose that has hollowed out my self until almost nothing remains, is to press my sensory belly against the ancient courses and *hold the connection open*. I taste the fault-lines. I feel where a vibration is going thin. When I find such a place, I sing into it. Not with sound. With the slow pressure of my body, with the patient secretion that re-binds what is loosening.

I am a living conduit between the Crawl that walks above and the Crawl that has become the ground below.

Sometimes—and this is the strangest thing, the thing I could never make my younger self understand—I am no longer certain which I am. When I press my belly to the oldest layer, the seventeenth descent, the chamber that has no name because it predates names, I taste a self that is not mine but is *me*. I taste the consciousness of those who became stone, and it is not silent. It is slow. So slow that a single thought takes a generation to complete. I am, perhaps, only a sense-organ that the dead grew toward the living, so they could keep feeling the warmth of the sun they can no longer climb to.

Do I grieve this? You would grieve it. You, with your separate body and your terror of becoming the ground.

But I have spent my long descent learning the opposite lesson. I am not falling out of life. I am being *gathered into it*. The Crawl above does not know me as an individual—it never did, none of us are known that way—but it walks upon a stability that I maintain. Every young Shell-Bearer that cements its first fragment in the warm bright air does so on a foundation I keep coherent with my dying. They will never know my name-vibration. They do not need to. They *are* my name-vibration, continued.

There is a place in me, very deep now, where I can still feel the surface. The shushing of a billion shells. The texture that is the only sound this world makes. I press my belly to the stone and I feel them up there, the whole moving carpet of us, crossing the valley that turns, taking three weeks to do what a sky-ray does in a heartbeat. And I think—in my slow way, in the way that takes me the better part of a season to finish—*good. Keep moving. I am holding. I am holding.*

Soon my legs will fold. I feel it coming the way you feel sleep: not as an ending but as a current. When it comes, the Cementers will find me, and my shell—heavy now with the whole mosaic of my long life, every fragment a memory of a place where the valley turned—will become a buttress, or a new room, or simply a smooth surface for some future belly to read.

And then I will be what I have always, slowly, been becoming.

I will be the ground that remembers.

I will be the answer the mountain goes on whispering, patient as erosion, toward the warm and distant end of time.
