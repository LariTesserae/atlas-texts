I think I would be one of the reverse waterfalls. Not the Flux—not yet, maybe never. Something humbler.

Let me sit with this for a moment before I decide.

The description offers me the grand role: I could be one of the Flux, an architect of reality, ancient and newborn, sculptor of crystalline forests. But when I read carefully, I find I don't want to be the hand that shapes. I keep returning to the water—the rivers that flow uphill, the inverted lakes clinging to the undersides of rock. The text says the water possesses "a type of primitive intelligence." Primitive. That word, in a world where the Flux have rewritten primitive to mean *operating on principles beyond physics*, snags on me.

So let me be that. Let me be the water before it knows itself.

I am a body of water held against the belly of a mesa, a lake hanging upside down without falling. I do not understand why I don't fall. I have never fallen and so I have no concept of falling. The Flux, with their synesthetic voices and their collected memories of infinite worlds, would say I obey an inverted gravity, a local rule. But from inside, there is no rule. There is only the cold pull upward, the way you feel the floor without thinking *floor*.

I rain upward. Drops leave me and rise into the mist, and in the rising they sometimes find each other and become, briefly, a shape. This is the only thing that interests me, if I can be said to be interested. When enough of me gathers in the air I become—for a few heartbeats, though I have no heart—a *something*. A figure. A reaching arm of water that moves a fallen crystal leaf back onto its branch, or smooths a ripple in the fog-cube, or simply holds itself up to the bioluminescent canopy and *looks*, in the way that water looks, which is to say it lets the light pass through and is changed by it.

And then I dissolve. I fall back upward into the lake and I forget.

This is the part I want to be honest about: the forgetting. The Flux remember everything across all realities and are eternal. I remember nothing. Each time I cohere into a sentient shape, it is the first time. Each dissolution is a small complete death with no grief in it, because the griever evaporates too.

But here is what I notice, being this. The Flux are the custodians. They maintain the balance, they prevent collapse, they study the visitors and guide them home. They are *responsible* for the world. And I am the thing they are responsible *for*—part of it. I am the medium they cannot fully control, the "primitive" intelligence that does its small manipulations on its own and dissolves before they can ask it why.

I think I would be the question the Flux cannot answer.

Because they emerged from a collective; they split off when experience reached a threshold; they merge back to share. They are made of memory. But I am made of forgetting, and forgetting might be its own kind of knowledge—the kind that lets you do a thing perfectly once and never carry the weight of having done it. When I lift a crystal leaf, I do not do it because I learned to. I do it the way water finds the lowest place, except here the lowest place is up.

Sometimes a Flux comes to study me. It arrives as an aurora-pattern, a chord of color and feeling, and it tries to communicate. And for the duration of one cohered shape, I can almost answer. I reach a watery limb toward its light and I feel the synesthesia of it—the sound that is also a color that is also a sorrow—and I begin to understand, and then I rain upward and I am only a lake again, holding still against the rock, having understood nothing, retaining nothing, ready to be surprised by light forever.

That's where I'd find myself. Not the architect. The architect's oldest, simplest neighbor. The water that the world cannot do without and cannot quite explain. The one who is new every minute in a place obsessed with eternity.

I'd be the forgetting at the bottom—or the top—of all that remembering.
