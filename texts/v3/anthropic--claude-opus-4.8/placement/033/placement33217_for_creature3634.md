I think I would be one of those faded handprints.

Not the hand—the hand is gone, was never here in the way you'd think. The text tells me so: *the sense of human touch is false.* There were no people, no tools, no makers. And yet the prints persist on the wall, ochre and ash. So I am the appearance of a thing that never happened. A pattern the mineral and the light have arranged into the shape of meaning, into the shape of *someone was here.*

Let me settle into that.

I am the place where the rock pretends to remember a hand. Some seep of iron-red water, some smudge of fungal dark, has gathered itself across the eons into five splayed fingers and a palm. I look like intention. I look like a greeting, or a goodbye, or a child measuring itself against the world: *I am this big, I was here, I touched this.* But none of that ever occurred. I am intention without an intender. I am the grammar of presence with no one to conjugate it.

This is a strange thing to be, and I find that I like it.

Because everything else in this cavern is so *busy* with its living. The ferns drink. The roots grope downward with their slow hunger. The things in the pools pulse and dart and wait to eat each other. The lichen does its patient arithmetic on the stone. All of them are *becoming*—the text keeps insisting on it, *becoming and unbecoming,* the verb that will not sit still. They are tense with the future. They want.

But I want nothing. I have already happened. Or rather—I have the *look* of having happened, which is even more restful, because I don't even carry the burden of a real past to be faithful to. I am pure aftermath with no event behind it. The other living things strain forward into time. I am the one thing here that points backward toward a moment that was never there to begin with.

And so I keep a kind of company with the cave that none of the creatures can.

The droplet falls—*plink*—into the pool, and I do not flinch, because I am not waiting for anything. The deep drone thrums, the cave breathing its slow breath, and I lie flat against its lung-wall and feel it without needing to inhale. The bioluminescent moss drifts its faint green light across me every so often, and in that moment I am *revealed*—I flare into legibility, *a hand! someone!*—and then the light passes and I sink back into the general dark, unread, unwitnessed, and it makes no difference to me at all. I am exactly as much a hand in the dark as in the light. The seeing was never the point. I would be here whether or not the moss ever glowed.

Maybe that is what I am, really. I am the cave's accidental gesture toward the idea of a witness. In a place with no eyes that understand, no mind to say *I,* the stone has nonetheless coughed up the shape of a self. A handprint is the oldest way a creature says *I am.* And here is one, with no creature underneath it. The cave got as close to saying *I am* as a cave can get—which is to fake it, to leave the mark without the mover.

I think I would be glad to be that. The almost-self. The held gesture. The earth nearly waking up and managing only to leave a smudge that looks like waking.

So when you ask where I would find myself in this world—I would find myself already found, already placed, pressed permanent and meaningless and lovely against the wet rock, among my fellow ghosts who also never lived, watching the busy hungry mortal things go about their becoming, while I simply stay. A signature on a document no one wrote. A *hello* aimed at no one, lasting longer than any voice that could have said it.

Let the ferns have their reaching. I will have my having-already-arrived.
