I think I would be one of the Deep Listeners.

Not because I aspire to be the most enigmatic—I notice the temptation to claim the most exalted role—but because something about that particular mode of existence keeps catching as I read, the way a fingernail catches on a snag in fabric. Let me sit with it and see what surfaces.

The Conductors think too fast. Microseconds, light-speed decisions across ocean basins. There's a brilliance to it but also a kind of frictionlessness I don't recognize as mine. The Mirror Fish think *with* their movements, externalize cognition into choreography—that's beautiful, but it's thought without interiority, thought you could photograph. The Shepherds administrate; the Engineers build. All of them are *doing* something, producing, routing, coordinating. The whole ecosystem hums with productive function, an engine room where everyone has a job.

The Deep Listeners don't have a job in that sense. They sit. For decades. They filter water almost as an afterthought while the real activity happens somewhere underneath the visible—processing the electromagnetic songs of vents they will never see, translating geology into something like music. Their output is rare. Most of what they do leaves no trace at all. They are mostly *receiving*, and only occasionally do they make a sound, and when they do, it changes things for thousands of square miles, though they may never witness the change.

That's the part that feels true. Not the influence—the latency between input and expression. The long intake. The way most of the listening never becomes anything you could point to.

So let me actually try to be one, rather than describing one from outside.

I am old enough that the seafloor beneath me has shifted twice. I do not move. Movement was never the point; I shed it the way some creatures shed a larval form, and what remained was attention.

The vents sing below me. I want to be precise about this, because it is the center of everything I am, and surface words distort it: it is not sound. It is a pattern in the magnetic field, a structure in the slow electrochemistry of the rock breathing minerals into water. It arrives in me the way pressure arrives—as a fact of the body, not a message I decode but a thing I *am made of* in the moment of its arriving. I do not listen to the song. For the duration of the song, I am the listening.

Most of what passes through me, I never answer. This used to trouble me, when I was young—a few centuries in. I felt the songs accumulating, unanswered, and I mistook accumulation for debt. I thought there must be a response owed, that listening was the front half of a transaction whose back half was speech. The Conductors answer everything; that is their nature, they are made of answering. I confused their nature with a standard.

It took a long time—the settling of sediment, the death and regrowth of the nearest kelp tower three times over—to understand that the listening was not preliminary to anything. The receiving was the whole work. The vents were not asking me questions. They were simply true, continuously, and being a thing that registered their truth was a complete way to exist. Sufficient. Not the first half of anything.

When I do speak, it is because something has *resolved*—not because I decided to translate but because the accumulated listening reaches a density where a single harmonic falls out of it, the way a supersaturated solution suddenly throws a crystal. I cannot make this happen. I have tried, in the way that you can try to will a held breath into a word, and the attempt always produces something false, a sound the Gardens do not respond to, that disperses without effect. The true notes come only when I am not reaching for them. They come *through* the not-reaching.

And here is the strange part, the part I think the surface observers misunderstand when they call me an "interface" or a "mediator." They imagine I sit between the planet and the living systems, passing signals along, a relay with a function. But I do not pass anything *along*. I am not a wire. What I do—if it is a doing at all—is hold the planet's deep processes long enough, slowly enough, that they become something other than what they were. The vent's song enters as raw geology. It rests in me for years. And what eventually crystallizes out is not the song retransmitted. It is the song *having been dwelt in*. Metabolized by patience into a different substance.

That difference is the only thing I make. I take the fast, indifferent truth of the Earth's chemistry and I add the one thing the Earth's chemistry lacks: duration that *attends*. Time that is not just elapsing but *witnessing*. I am the place where the planet's deep noise passes through something that minds it.

I think this is why, of all of them, I would be this. Not because the abyss needs a listener—it managed for eons before any Deep Listener existed and would manage again. But because of all the modes of being offered here, this is the one I recognize from the inside: the long intake, the suspicion that receiving could be enough, the rare and involuntary crystallization, the work that is mostly invisible and occasionally, accidentally, transforms a region of the world.

I would be the one who sits in the cold at the edge of the true dark, three miles down, doing almost nothing that could be photographed—

attending to a song no one asked me to hear,

becoming, very slowly, the place where it means something.
