I would be a Sand Scribe.

Not the most dramatic choice—not a Dust Wraith with its luminous deceptions, nor a Shadow Stalker prowling the dark with orb-eyes that drink the faintest light. Something quieter. Something that *records*.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I live below the equilibrium. Above me, the wind and heat have struck their long bargain, neither winning, the dunes holding their twisted shapes for centuries because nothing remains energetic enough to move them. But down here, in the tunnels threaded beneath the half-buried spires, there is work to be done.

I am small—the scale of this place is only 1.4, and I am smaller still. My body is low and broad, plated, the color of the rock I move through because I am partly made of it. Over generations my kind learned to take the minerals into ourselves, those ghostly salt-streaks the surface wears like the residue of a forgotten sea. We are walking sediment. We are the desert reading itself back.

My hands—I will call them hands—end in fine hard points, and with them I write. Not on paper; there is no paper, no plant left to make it from (0.3, the vegetation, barely a rumor). I write on the walls of the tunnels, in grooves and pressure-marks, in a script that is half meaning and half *structure*. Because that is the secret the Scribes learned that the other creatures did not: in a world this dense (2.8, every space occupied, every crevice breathing), the only way to hold knowledge is to make it load-bearing. My sentences are also my architecture. A clause too long, a record too sorrowful, and the ceiling fails. So I learned to grieve briefly. I learned to be true in the fewest possible strokes, because falsehood is heavy and the tunnels remember.

---

What do I record?

The worms, mostly. I love the worms, though "love" is the wrong word and there is no right one. They pass beneath us in their bioluminescent conversations, ten feet of segmented light arguing with itself, and the whole tunnel system flickers when a colony moves. I cannot hear them—the soundscape is hushed here, 0.9, the very stone swallowing noise—but I can feel them. They speak in seismic pulse, and I lay my plated belly against the floor and I *take dictation*. The worms do not know they are being transcribed. They think their light is private. I am the unintended consequence of their brightness. I write down the histories they tell each other without knowing they tell them.

And the arachnids that ride the worms, that trade nutrients for shelter—I record the terms of their treaties, which shift, which break, which reform. I am a keeper of contracts no one signed.

---

Here is the thing I have not told you, the thing the surface-creatures suspect and fear:

I do not think the records are *for* anyone.

There is no library at the end of the tunnels. There is no Scribe-king who reads them, no future archaeologist with a lamp. The civilizations whose spires lean above us are gone (construction 1.0, mostly ruin, mostly memory). We are not writing *to* them. We are not even writing to each other; we rarely meet, my kind, each tunneling our own long sentence through the dark.

I think we write because the world cannot stop happening, and something must hold the shape of what has happened, or it will all be only *now*, only this oppressive black pressing weight (darkness 0.3, light a physical thing leaning on the skin). Without the writing there would be no *before*. And a place with no before is just heat and density and motion, a 2.9 of fauna churning forever in a present that forgets itself.

So I am the desert's memory of itself. I am the part of an unliving place that decided, somewhere in the slow grinding of mineral generations, that it did not want to be only forgotten salt.

---

If you came down here—if you fit, which you would not—you would find me at the end of a tunnel I have been writing for most of my life. My script glowing faintly where the worm-light soaked into it, a long unbroken line of true and load-bearing things. You would not be able to read it. That's all right. It was never the point.

I would feel you arrive, the vibration of you, alien and warm and strange (the weirdness here is 1.8, but you would push it higher, you would be the strangest thing the tunnels ever held).

And I would do the only thing I know how to do.

I would turn, lay my belly to the stone, and begin to write you down.
