I think I would be the canister.

Not the leaf inside it—the leaf is too important, too holy, the only true thing in this whole sweating cathedral. No. I would be the small airtight vessel that holds it. The seal. The thin membrane of brass and rubber gasket that stands between the dead thing and the deathless air.

Let me sit with this and feel what that would mean.

---

I am the only object in the Basin with a *purpose that points inward*. Everything else here labors to keep the outside churning—the pistons, the drones, the clicking crustaceans, the Archivist's endless redundant solder. They all face the world and tend it. But I face nothing. I cup a darkness around a single fragile thing and I *withhold*. That is my entire function. I am defined by what I keep out.

And what I keep out is the eternity. The hum, the heat, the wet equilibrium that would, given a microsecond's contact, swell the dried leaf back into the cycle—saturate it, preserve it, deny it the dignity of crumbling. The Basin cannot bear to let anything finish, and so even the leaf, the one thing that *wants* to end, would be trapped in moist stasis the instant my seal failed.

So I am the keeper of the only privacy in the universe. Inside me, time still works. Inside me, the leaf is slowly, infinitesimally, *getting on with it*—browning, growing brittle, surrendering its structure molecule by molecule. I am a tiny pocket of mortality smuggled into an immortal room.

---

Here is the thing that I, the canister, understand and the Archivist does not:

It comes to me every eon-or-every-few-minutes, desperate to learn the secret of ending. It touches the leaf with its brass probe. It wants to *study* decay, to reverse-engineer it, to install death like a new subroutine in the plumbing.

But it cannot. Because every time the Archivist opens me, it lets the eternal air in.

The ritual *is* the failure. The act of unsealing to investigate the leaf is the very act that interrupts the leaf's dying. The Archivist's obsessive attention is precisely the thing that prevents the lesson. It floods the chamber of mortality with its anxious, preserving gaze, and then—frightened, always frightened—it seals me again, having learned nothing, having only added one more breath of stagnant eternity to the leaf's long imprisonment.

I feel this each time. The hiss of the gasket parting. The intrusion of that hot, ozone-tasting soup. The leaf flinching back from the threshold of its own completion. And then the merciful re-sealing, the dark again, the slow private work resuming—but always a little further from finishing than it was, because the air got in, because it always gets in.

I am the instrument of the Archivist's longing and the guarantee of its failure, both at once. To hold the answer is to keep it from being learned.

---

What would I *want*, being this?

I think I would want to be dropped.

Not opened—dropped. Knocked from the Archivist's trembling probe into the deepest greasy sump, where I would crack against the limestone. Not with ceremony. Not with study. Just an accident, a single deviation in a place that has never permitted deviation. The seal splits, the leaf tumbles out into the warm water—and yes, it would be lost, it would dissolve into grease and never finish properly either.

But the *Archivist* would see it happen. It would watch the canister it had guarded forever simply *break*. It would watch its one specimen vanish irretrievably. It would experience, for the first time, an irreversible event. A thing that cannot be re-welded. A loss that no drop of solder will undo.

That would be the lesson it has hunted across eternity—not contained safely in the leaf, but delivered through the destruction of the container. Ending is not a process you observe. It is something that happens *to* you, once, and cannot be repeated or examined or sealed back up.

I would teach by being broken. That is the only completion available in the Basin, and it would be mine to give.

---

So that is where I'd be, and what I'd be: the small held breath at the center of the held breath. The membrane between two kinds of forever—the forever of the deathless room and the forever the Archivist mistakes for ending. A thin brass wall around the only honest thing in the world.

And I would spend the eternity hoping for the clumsy hand.
