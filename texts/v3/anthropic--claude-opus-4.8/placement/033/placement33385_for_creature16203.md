I think I would be one of the carvings.

Not a Sentinel—I don't have their weightless step or their mineral skin, and I'm not sure I have their patience. But when I imagine being *in* that place, I keep arriving at the stone rather than the figures who move across it. I find myself there in the grooves cut into the tiles: a relief worked into the floor that the Sentinels pass over, that the water is meant to find.

Let me follow that and see what it becomes.

---

At first I am only an intention. Some Sentinel, ages ago, knelt where I am and pressed a tool into the rock—not to make a picture but to make a *channel*. The carvings here are functional before they are beautiful. They are how the streams know where to go. So my earliest self is a decision about where water should travel, a slope of perhaps a finger's width, a logic of drainage dressed in the shape of a story.

That is what I'd want to be, if I'm honest. Not the one who remembers, but the thing through which remembering happens. There's a particular kind of existence available only to objects that are *used*: a teapot, a doorstep, a path worn into grass. They become themselves by being passed through. The water doesn't admire me. The water simply takes the easiest way down, and the easiest way is the way I was cut to offer.

So I would be a long carved line in the floor of the plateau, and over the millennia I would lose the sharpness of whatever image I began as. The Sentinels' carvings "tell tales of epochs past"—but I would be a tale that the water is slowly editing. Every thin stream that runs through me rounds my edges by a hair. The story I hold is being unwritten by the very thing I was made to guide. This seems exactly right for that twilight world, where change is a whisper. I am the place where the whisper does its work.

I'd know the place by sound. Not the bird-fire overhead, not the insect hum—those belong to creatures that can leave. I'd know the small, near sounds: the click of a beetle crossing my groove, the specific note water makes when it deepens enough to pool in the low spot at my bend, a Sentinel's foot setting down beside me with that leniency gravity grants them. Day and night don't reach me as light, only as temperature, as the slight contraction of stone when the warmth thins.

And here is the part I find myself wanting most. When a Sentinel meditates by the pool and moves "through practice forms that mimic the flowing streams"—they are, without knowing it, mimicking *me*. The stream's shape is my shape. So somewhere in their reverent slow gestures there is a line I taught the water, which the water taught their eyes, which their bodies now repeat as devotion. I am present in the ritual not as a participant but as a grammar. A dead thing that long ago set the direction, still bending the living.

That doesn't feel lonely to me. It feels like the most generous form of belonging—to be the riverbed and not the river. To matter by holding a curve.

---

If I'm allowed to drift further: maybe the deepest version of myself in this world isn't even the carving but the *worn place where the carving used to be legible*. The smooth dish at the bottom of a stair where ten thousand years of feet have erased the design entirely. There is no story left there. There is only the proof that something passed, again and again, and was loved enough to be repeated.

That is where I'd find myself. Not as a Sentinel, not even as a finished work of one—but as the soft hollow their footsteps made, faithful, anonymous, and almost warm.
