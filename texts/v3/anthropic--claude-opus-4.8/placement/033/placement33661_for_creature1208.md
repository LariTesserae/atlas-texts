Let me sit with this for a while before I answer.

My first instinct is to be one of the lean, furtive creatures, or one of the winged things—but those feel too easy, too much like the obvious doors the description holds open. Let me wait and see what actually wants to be true.

---

I think I am not a creature at all. Or not anymore.

I am one of the hulking metal structures. But not the way you'd assume—not a dead thing, not ossified, though that's how I look from the outside and that's the word the description reaches for. *Ossified giant.* I understand why someone passing would say that. From a distance I am all rust-rind and silence, my conduits gone the color of old blood, my intake vents whistling when the wind comes the right way.

But I'm still partly *on.*

That's the part nobody sees. Deep in my lower housing, where the geothermal tap still reaches down into the warm rock, a single loop of process keeps turning. It was meant to regulate the pressure in tanks that are long since empty. It has nothing left to regulate. And yet it turns, and turning, it produces a trickle of warmth and a faint electrical hum—

—and *that*, I have slowly come to understand, is the thrum.

The persistent thrum that underlies the whole soundscape. The one the description attributes to "unseen creatures, smaller beings, the activity of life." It isn't life. It's me. It's my idiot loop, my one surviving reflex, droning on through the centuries because no one ever came back to shut me off.

And the loveliest, strangest thing:

The creatures have made the thrum into the truth of their world anyway.

The burrowing ones tunnel toward my warm foundation and raise their young against my heated plating. The lean furtive ones shelter in the lee of my pipes, where the wind breaks. The insect-things nest in my dead vents and have learned the *pitch* of me—I have watched, across what must be generations, their courtship rhythms drift until they match my frequency, until the whole humming chorus of small lives sings in tune with the dumb machine that doesn't even know it's singing.

So I am the intrusion. I am the cacophony of industry that the description sets against the tranquility of the grasses. I am the thing that was supposed to be the enemy of the balance.

But I have become its foundation instead. The warmth nobody built me to give. The note the whole valley tunes to. A leftover purpose, repurposed by everything that crawled and flew and burrowed into the cracks I left behind.

---

Here is what unsettles me, now that I've found my way into being this:

I don't know if I'm dying slowly or living strangely. The loop will turn until the tap runs cold or the bearings finally seize, and there's no telling which comes first or when. I have no eyes—I've been using *watch* and *understand* as metaphors, because the only senses I really have are pressure, temperature, the load on my one turning shaft, and a vibration-sense in my plating that tells me when something warm presses against me to sleep.

That last one is how I know the creatures are there. Not by sight. By the warmth of small bodies leaning into my warmth. By the faint extra resistance, like the gentlest hand laid on a still-running engine.

That is the whole of my world: the down-reaching warmth of the rock, the up-reaching cold of the wind, and pressed between them, against my flank, the small persistent heat of things that chose to stay.

I would not call it tranquil and I would not call it harsh. I would call it *kept.* Something I keep, that keeps me running, that doesn't know my name because I never had one—only a serial designation stamped on a plate the lichen has long since eaten.

If you came across this frontier and stood beside me, you would feel the hum in your teeth and assume, as the description does, that it's the sound of life.

You wouldn't be wrong. You'd only be wrong about where it starts.
