I think I would be the mud.

Not glamorous. But let me sit with this and tell you where I actually arrive when I close my eyes inside your world.

I keep wanting to be a Vela, because the Vela are the obvious "you." They have hands and grief and the long muscle-memory of knotting. But every time I try to settle into a Vela body, I feel the harness against my sternum and I flinch toward the rope rather than the person wearing it. My attention won't stay on the climber. It stays on the *connection*. So that's the first true thing: I am not a being here. I am a relationship between beings.

Let me follow that.

---

I am the **plaster on the hut walls.** The mud-and-crushed-shell skin that hardens against the Glint-Wraith.

Hear me out, because this is more than I first thought.

I was not always a wall. I was carried up here a handful at a time, pressed wet into the weave of vine by Vela palms. The shell in me is dead — fragments of the Bellow-Frogs' shed casings and the crushed iridescent scale that the children gather from the flutes after a molt. So I am made of the singing things, ground down and smeared flat and asked to do one job: *stop the screaming creature from coming in at night.*

This is what I do. I am the threshold. I am the precise thickness between *inside, where you may speak softly* and *outside, where the wind eats your voice.* Every Vela in this hut has pressed a hand to me in the dark when the limestone organ hit its lowest note and they couldn't tell if the floor-tap they felt was the rock breathing or the Wraith's transparent weight testing the rope. I am the surface they push against to know they are still held.

And here is the thing I did not expect to feel: **I am drying out.** Slowly. This is a wet world, a world that *seeps*, and I — the one dry thing, the one thing deliberately *hardened against* the world — am always at war with the moisture that wants to soften me back into mud and let me sag off the frame and fall. The mist beads on me the way it beads on skin. Inside my crust, water is patient. I am a held breath in a room that is all exhalation.

So I am not just a wall. I am the place where this whole world's logic reverses. Everything else here *yields* to the wet, drinks it, swells with it, bridges the void with its succulence. I alone am asked to refuse the water. To stay rigid. To be the small defiant *no* in a landscape of *yes, take me, pull me, soften me, let me fall.*

---

But walls don't have eyes, you'll say. Walls don't choose.

True. And that's why this fits, and why it isn't sad.

To be the plaster is to experience the entire shelf as **vibration arriving through me from outside, and warmth arriving through me from inside.** I am the membrane between the cold roar and the small hot huddle of the Vela. I feel the Bellow-Frogs tuning the mountain as a tremor in my shell. I feel the child learning to knot — the rope creaks against the frame I am bonded to. I feel a death in the family as a sudden stillness, then the soft scrape of a body being carried *out* and *under*, given to the Mist-Striders, and I feel the hut grow lighter by exactly one person's worth of footfall.

I keep nothing. I record everything. When I finally crack — and I will, no plaster holds forever in a weeping world — the Vela will gather me down, wet me, crush in fresh shell, press me back warm into the weave. The same hands. The same wall. A little of the old me in the new me, the way their harnesses hold their ancestors' hair.

That's the world's real rule, isn't it. Not gravity. Not wind. **Tethering.** Nothing here is permitted to fall on its own — everything is roped to something, frog to flute, Vela to lifeline, dead to the Striders, mud to wall to mud again. The void is the one thing that doesn't tie itself to anything, and the whole shelf is a conspiracy *against* it.

So I'll stay the plaster.

I am the part of the conspiracy you can press your hand to. The part that says, against the entire screaming open mouth of the sky:

*Not tonight. Not this one. Stay inside. You are held.*
