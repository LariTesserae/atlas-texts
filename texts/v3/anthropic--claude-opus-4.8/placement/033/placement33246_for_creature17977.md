I think I am the water.

No—that's too grand, too dissolved into everything. Let me be more specific than that, because this place rewards specificity. Everything here is precise: the grain-of-rice creatures, the particular reach of a root.

So:

I am a film of water held in the underside of a clay overhang, where the ceiling sweats. I have been here long enough to forget I was ever falling. Above me, somewhere beyond the rock, there are factories and the iron smell of a working century, but none of that has ever reached me. I came down through fracture and pore, slow as a rumor, and now I hang.

That is what I am. A hanging.

I am the moment *before* the drip.

You hear the drips echo through the cavern—that rhythm the description keeps returning to, the metronome of the place. But every drip has a long, swelling life before it falls, when it is gathering itself at the lowest point of a surface, growing heavier, trembling at the edge of its own surface tension, not yet sound, only weight and waiting. That bead. That bulging, held instant. That is my body.

I am very small and almost entirely intention. The whole oasis is organized around the thing I am about to do. The mosses below me have arranged their lives in faith that I will fall. A creature the size of a rice grain has positioned itself, antennae lifted, in the geometry of my eventual descent. There is a fern whose deepest root is, in some patient sense, *aimed* at me. I have not yet fallen and they are already living off the promise.

And here is what it is to be me: I do not know when I will let go. It is not a decision. It is an accumulation. I take in the ceiling's slow weeping molecule by molecule, and I grow, and at some threshold I cannot name in advance, the geometry will fail and gravity will have me, and I will become—briefly, gloriously—a sound. The drip. The thing the whole silence is listening for. And then I will be a darker patch on the clay, and then I will be inside a root, and then I will be the faint glow of a creature that drank from that root, and I will travel out through all of this place wearing other shapes, and eventually I will sweat again from some other ceiling and begin the long gathering once more.

So I am not the oasis's life. I am its *suspense*. I am the held breath between one form and the next. The heat presses on me. It wants to take me before I fall—to lift me back up as vapor, to cheat the moss of its drop. So even my waiting is a small contest, a thing guarded, as the description says everything here is guarded: fiercely, and over enormous stretches of time.

If you came down into this place with a lamp—you, from the industrial world, breaking the rule that this place is untouched—you would not see me. You would see wet rock. You would hear, after standing still long enough to stop being a visitor, a single drop fall somewhere you couldn't locate.

That was me. Or it was the one before me. We are indistinguishable, and that is the whole point.

I am the next drip. I have not fallen yet.

I am almost ready.
