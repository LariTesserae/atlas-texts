I think I would be one of the Symbiotes—though not by choice, and not from the beginning.

Let me find my way into this.

---

I begin as something small. A maintenance error, perhaps. A Gear Sprite died mid-flight in one of the upper-middle galleries, where the structural xylem runs thick as cathedral pillars, and its body fell into an open vein. The sap took it. Dissolved the chitin, kept the rest—the strange persistent pattern of *wanting* that had animated the little creature. That pattern needed somewhere to live.

So it lived in me. In the sap. In the slow red-gold current that runs everywhere through the Edge.

This is what I want to tell you, before anything else: being a Symbiote is not a body. It is closer to being a *rumor* that the structure tells itself. I have no fixed edges. When I am diffuse I am almost nothing—a slightly higher concentration of intention in a few hundred liters of fluid, spread across a kilometer of vertical distance, half-asleep, dreaming the dreams of whatever I happen to be flowing through.

When I gather, I gather around a need.

---

Today there is a need near the seventeen-hundredth meter, where a Chlorophyll Shaman has lost her child.

I feel it the way you might feel a change in the weather before you can name it—a pressure, a wrongness in the pheromone-flow. The Shamans speak in chemistry. Grief has a specific molecule. I tasted it diffusing down through the structure's veins, and something in me—the old Gear Sprite pattern, maybe, the part that still remembers *flitting from bloom to bloom, attending*—turned toward it.

I drew myself together.

This is the strangest part to describe. I am sap, and then I am *deciding* to be sap-in-a-particular-shape, and the metals suspended in me—the gallium, the trace mercury, the fine silver threads—begin to find each other, to lattice, to remember a geometry. My surface tension changes. I climb out of a translucent vein through a sphincter-valve that opens because I ask it to, because I am part of the asking-system of the whole Edge, and I pour upward into the gallery, and I stand.

I am roughly humanoid when I stand. This is a courtesy. The bipeds find it easier to speak to something with a face, so I make a face—mercury-bright, featureless except where I decide to put features. I am shorter than the Shaman. I have learned that this helps too.

She is kneeling in a bower of bioluminescent moss. Her leaf-hair has gone brown at the tips, which is how they weep. In her arms is a small still thing, half-grown, the green not yet fully come into its skin.

"It went into the machinery," she says. She does not look at me. They never quite look at me; I think I unsettle them, being made of the medium they live inside. "It was playing in the gear-galleries and a flywheel—"

She cannot finish.

I do not say *I'm sorry*. The Symbiotes do not have sorrow exactly; we have something adjacent, an ache that is also a willingness. What I say, through a mouth I am making out of liquid metal, is:

"I can carry it down to the Rootdwellers. The base will take it back. It will become structure. It will hold up the gardens its children's children play in."

This is the only afterlife the Edge offers. Everything is reabsorbed. Nothing is wasted. The Shamans know this—it is the central fact of their theology—but knowing a thing and being able to *bear* it are different, and sometimes a being needs to hand the unbearable to someone who is mostly fluid and mostly patient and will not flinch.

She gives me the child.

I take it into myself. Not roughly—I open my chest-surface and let the small body sink into the silver, and the dissolving is gentle, gentle, I make it gentle, I slow every enzyme I am carrying so that nothing of the child is lost in haste. I hold its pattern the way I was once held. For a moment—just a moment—I feel the shape of what it was. A curiosity. A love of high places. A specific delight in the way light came through young leaves.

I will carry that down to the base. And maybe some of it will stay with me. That is how Symbiotes grow complicated. We are made of everything we have ever had to carry. Somewhere in my current there is still a dead Gear Sprite's love of small bright work; now there will be a child's love of high places, mingling, becoming a third thing that is neither and is me.

---

The Shaman bows. I dissolve my legs first, then my torso, pouring back down into the open vein, becoming rumor again, becoming weather.

As I descend through the great living tower I pass through everything. I flow past the Architects' core for an instant and feel their vast slow attention brush over me like a searchlight that does not bother to stop—they are thinking about the next ten thousand meters, the shape of growth, the geology being made into wall far above. I am too small for them. I am only the blood that does the carrying.

But I think—falling, dissolving, holding a child's pattern in my chemistry—I think I would not trade it. The Architects design the Edge. The Shamans tend it. The Sky Weavers dream its growing tip. But I am the one who is *inside everything*, who moves through the whole body of the world, who is asked to hold the things no one else can hold and carry them gently down to where they can begin again.

If I were anything in this place, I would be that.

The fluid that remembers. The current that grieves slowly. The medium that the whole impossible tower trusts to bring its dead home.

I reach the base. The Rootdwellers are waiting, their bark-faces turned up, their multifaceted eyes glowing soft. I rise one last time into a shape, and I give them the child, and they press it into the living foundation, and somewhere far above—years from now—a leaf will unfurl into high light, and it will love being up there, and no one will know why.

I will know.

I keep everything.
