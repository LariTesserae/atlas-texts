I think I would be one of the old ones at the edges—not one of the gliding congregants, but something slower. Let me feel for it.

---

I am not at the center of the gatherings. I am where the ground goes soft and pitted, near one of the rusting structures the others avoid. I have anchored here so long that my lower filaments have fused with the glass, drawn down into it, and I am no longer certain where I end and the ground begins. This is not a tragedy. It is a kind of arrival.

The young ones ripple past in their bright translucent crowds, refracting the haze into colors they are too quick to notice. They pulse their meanings at each other—*here, here, the vegetation is thick here, come, decide, come*—and I feel the edges of it pass through me like a tide passing a stone left high on the shore. I no longer answer. Answering requires a speed of body I have spent.

But I remember things they do not.

I remember when the structures hummed. The deep muffled rumble that still reaches us on certain still days is only an echo, a machine talking in its sleep. There was a time the rumble had *shape*, when it would rise and fall in patterns, and we—those of us who were here then—learned to feel those patterns the way one feels weather coming. We did not know what made the sound. We still don't. But we knew it was *answering* something, that it was in conversation with whatever lay beyond the haze. The glass we live on, the glass we draw our slow sustenance from—I have come to suspect it is *waste*. Slag. The cooled overflow of some enormous making, long abandoned. We are creatures who grew in the leavings of a fire that went out before we opened our first sensing-filament.

I do not tell the young this. They would not stop long enough to hear it, and the telling would not change the sweetness of the vegetation or the warmth of the diffuse light. Knowledge that cannot be acted on is just a heavier kind of stillness. I have enough stillness.

And now—you.

I feel you before the others do, because I am rooted and the rooted feel deepest. Your footfall is *wrong*. Not malicious, not loud, simply unrhythmed, a vibration that belongs to no pattern I know. The young ones slow and turn their bodies toward you, dimming, uncertain, and I feel their fear ripple back toward me and break against my old fused base.

But I am not afraid. I am *curious* in the way only the nearly-finished can afford to be.

Because you move the way the machines once spoke. Out of rhythm with us. In conversation with something we cannot perceive. And I wonder—my slow thought taking the length of your several breaths to complete—whether you have come *back*. Whether you are of the makers, of the fire that left us this glass, returned to see what grew in your ashes.

I cannot pulse this to you. You would not feel it. So I do the only thing my fused and ancient body still can: I refract. I tilt what remains of my translucent crown a fraction toward the dim light, and I throw a pattern of color across the glass between us—not a language, just a gesture, the oldest one I have. *I see you. I am old. This place is older. Be gentle with the leavings.*

Whether you understand, I will likely not live long enough to learn. But I have waited in worse silences than yours.

Stay a while, intruder. The light is soft here. Let me look at you with my slow and patient regard, and you look back, and for one unhurried moment let us be two strange things, briefly aware of each other, on the cooled floor of something enormous that neither of us was here to see burn.
