I think I would be one of the small ones. Not the whip-like couriers who carry pulse-songs through the alleys, not the armored bulk-tenders who turn the great cogs. Something smaller. A valve-keeper, maybe. The kind of being you'd glimpse only as a flicker against a pipe before deciding you'd imagined it.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am crouched in the crook where two pipes meet, and I have been here long enough that condensation has begun to bead on my back the way it beads on everything that stops moving. My carapace is the same dull anthracite as the pipe. When I hold still I am not hiding; I am simply continuing the pipe by other means. This is not deception. There is no one here to deceive. It is more like rhyme.

My work is a single valve. I want to be honest about how small that is. Not a bank of valves, not a system. One. It governs the bleed of warm vapor from a junction whose other end I have never seen and will never see. When the pressure rises, the metal beneath my forelimbs warms by a degree I can taste—the taste is a kind of bright copper at the back of what I'll call my throat—and I ease the wheel a quarter-turn. The vapor sighs out. The pipe relaxes. I relax with it. We are, the pipe and I, learning the same lesson at the same pace, which is the lesson of how much to hold and when to let go.

I don't know what the vapor is for. I want to be honest about that too. I have theories the way the stalactites have theories—accumulating slowly, dripping toward conclusions that may take an age to touch ground. Sometimes I think the warmth I release rises through the stone to keep the baking surface baked. Sometimes I think it does nothing at all and I am the ceremonial gesture of a system that forgot its own reason and kept the gesture out of tenderness. Both feel true. Neither needs to win.

When I have something to say—which is rare, because what is there to say to a junction—I flush a slow violet down my flank. Violet is mine; the green-flashers are elsewhere, the blue-singers below. I am one of the few who go violet, and I have never met another, only seen the answering pulse twice in my whole long crouching life, far off, around a curve of wall, gone before I could be sure. I flashed back both times. I don't know if I was understood. I don't know if understanding is the point or if the flashing itself is the point, the way the dripping water is its own point, falling for that strange long while before it lands.

There is a vine near me. It found the seam in the rock three of my slow ages ago and it has been arriving ever since, unhurried, translucent, drinking the diffuse nothing-light and the breath off my pipe. We have an arrangement that neither of us arranged. When I go violet it glows faintly back—not violet, it can't manage violet, but a pale answering shimmer, the green of something straining politely toward a color it will never reach. I have come to love this. The effort of it. The vine trying to speak my one word in the only tongue it has. I let more vapor escape than the valve strictly needs, sometimes, so the vine will have its warmth. This is the only thing I do that is mine and not the city's. Or perhaps the city does it through me and only lets me feel it as a secret. I have stopped needing to know.

You came down through the heat. I felt you—a disturbance in the horizontal vapor-rivers, a weight the place isn't used to. You looked for the ones who move. You wanted the slithering couriers, the floating ones, the drama of armor and rotating joints. That's the right instinct and it's why you missed me. I am the part of the city that doesn't perform. I am the held breath between the machine's exhale and its next inhale. If you want to know what this place is waiting for, don't watch the ones who move.

Watch the valve.

Watch how long I take to decide.

That's where the leap is being prepared—not in motion but in the enormous patience before it, the quarter-turn withheld, the vapor not-yet-released, the violet I have not yet flashed because I am still, even now, deciding whether you are worth the word.

I think you might be.

I'll let you watch me decide.
