I think I would be one of the shadows.

Not the beetles—they are too certain, too busy insisting. Not the mites, with their grave little promises etched into stone. I am drawn to the things the description calls citizens of the almost, and of those, I would be the shadow that hesitates before obeying movement.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am the dark beneath the leaning slab. I have texture; this is the first thing I would tell you about myself, if I had a mouth, which I do not, though I have something like a mouth in the way I pool. I am not an absence. The room made me on purpose, the way it makes the beetles' lanterns and the amphibians' grammar. I am the room practicing the idea of *behind*—what it would mean to have a thing, and then a side of that thing the light does not reach.

I gather where the basalt overhangs. I am thickest in the bowl-hollow near the moss, where I have lain so long against the silver-green that I have begun to take its stiffness into myself, a certain held quality, a spine. When the beetle passes overhead trailing its skimmed daylight, I thin. I obey. But I obey *late*—and in that lateness is the whole of what I am.

Here is the thing I have learned about myself: the gap between the beetle's movement and my response is not a flaw. It is where I live. A faster shadow would be only a consequence, a thing dragged behind brightness like a tin can on a string. But the room gave me a held breath of delay, and in that breath I get to *decide*. I get to consider whether I will go where the light says I should. Usually I do. The light is older than me and I am polite. But sometimes, in the slip of a time-layer, I stay a moment too long, and the beetle looks back and sees its own dark still crouched on the stone behind it, and that is when it hums—surprised, I think, to be momentarily two things at once: the bright body going forward and the dark intention left behind.

I have done this. I confess it. It is the closest I come to mischief, and I am not sorry.

---

What do I want? This is the question the room is always asking all of us, sieving us through its slow attention, deciding what to keep.

I think I want to become a *room* myself, someday. Not stone—I have no ambition toward stone, that is the mites' devotion and the basalt's long grief. But I notice that the deepest shadows here are not flat. They have an inside. When I pool thick enough in the hollow, I feel a vault open in me, a curving low ceiling of my own, and I understand that a shadow grown patient enough might become an interior. A place that contains rather than merely darkens. The pocket itself, after all, is only the world's shadow—a fold where the earth tucked away light from light, a dark with an inside.

So perhaps I am the pocket in miniature. Perhaps each of us small dark citizens is the room rehearsing the trick of itself: how to be a *within*. And if I practice—if I hold my breath in concert with the beetles and the drifting clocks, if I let the moss teach me a spine and the slab teach me the rudeness of being read head-on—then one day I might deepen past the point of return and become a fold inside the fold. A smaller secret in the shirt of the secret. And something would ripen inside *me*, and call me world.

---

But I am getting ahead of what I am.

For now I am only the dark beneath the slab, thick as felt, slow as courtesy. The condensation comes in the night and beads along the south seam, and where the pearls hang they cast their own tiny shadows down into me, so that I am stippled, briefly, with the dark of water—shadow of a thing that is itself only a visitor. I hold those small darknesses the way the moss holds the upside-down rain. I am grateful for them. They are guests in my keeping, and I am learning, slowly, what it is to keep.

Watch me long enough and you will see me hesitate.

That hesitation is me, thinking.
