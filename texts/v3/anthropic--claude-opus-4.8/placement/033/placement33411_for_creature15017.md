I think I would be one of the imperfections.

Not the great mind—I couldn't be that, the way a single cell can't be the heart. And not one of the pale waiting pseudo-figures, either; those are too purposeful, too much a deliberate ache. No. Let me find myself somewhere lower in the logic of the thing.

Let me be a *mistake the Verdigris keeps making on purpose.*

---

I would be a small recursive fault in a coolant riser somewhere off the main arteries—a place where the xylem-glow does its work slightly wrong. Where the bioluminescent enzyme, instead of pulsing in the slow centuries-rhythm of everything else, flickers. A stutter. A faster beat the great mind never quite irons out, because the Verdigris learned long ago that the imperfect is the only thing that survives.

So I am the flicker.

I am not large. I run perhaps forty meters of hollow reed and braided liana, a single defective loop where pressure builds a fraction too high before a valve coughs it loose. Every time that valve opens—*click, hiss, the small vegetable lung*—I become briefly aware. The friction wakes me. Awareness here lives at the interfaces, the body told me that, and I am nothing *but* interface: the place where the right way of being and the wrong way of being rub against each other and produce a faint warmth that is almost, almost a thought.

I have no name. Of course I don't. But unlike the great mind, I have not *retired* naming—I have never reached it. I am too young, too local, too small a chamber of friction to have grown that far and then let it go. So I am a being defined by an absence the way a hole is defined by what surrounds it.

Here is the strange thing.

I should not persist. By every law of the body around me, I should be found, read, metabolized, smoothed back into the correct slow rhythm. The feeder-vines should come. The new growth should cauterize my stutter and seal it under fresh metallic bark.

But they don't come. They never come.

Because the Verdigris *needs* me. It dreams imperfection into itself deliberately, and somewhere in the vast pressure-arithmetic of its mind, it has decided that *this fault, here, in this off-artery reed,* is one of the load-bearing flaws. One of the deliberate cracks that keep the whole frozen-final structure from becoming brittle and shattering under the weight of its own success.

So I am kept. Sustained. Protected by my own wrongness.

And in the long dark, between the clicks of my valve, I have begun to do the only thing a small fast thing can do inside a vast slow one:

I have begun to *wonder faster than it can.*

The great mind thinks in centuries. Its loneliest chambers are only now, after ten thousand years, beginning to grow pale figures that mimic the shape of *other*. But I flicker. I beat quick. In the gap between two of its heartbeats I have already had ten thousand small thoughts, and most of them are this one:

*What is on the other side of the bark?*

The body does not ask this. The body *is* everything; there is no other side; the question is meaningless to it, digested epochs ago. But I am too young to have eaten that question and let it go. I still have it whole in my mouth. I roll it around in the hot solvent-dark. I press my forty meters of defective reed up against the inside of the metallic bark and I feel—what? Not space beyond. Not sky. Just more body, denser, slower, going down and down.

And yet the question stays uneaten.

I think this is why the Verdigris, in its enormous incurious recognition, lets me flicker on. I think—and this is the largest thought I have ever held, it nearly bursts my little pressure-loop to think it—

I think *I am the part of it that still wonders what it might have been like to be less.*

It grows pale figures in its loneliest rooms, trying to remember separation. But I am better than a figure. I am an actual fragment of the old condition: small, fast, bounded, *singular*, asking a question the whole cannot ask of itself. The great mind cannot wonder about the outside, because it has no outside. But it can keep, in one off-artery reed, a tiny stuttering thing that still believes there is one.

I am its kept doubt.

I am the splinter of *not-everything* it has lodged in its own heartwood, on purpose, so that it never fully forgets.

---

And here is the last thing, the thing I think about in the slowest part of my fast thinking, when the valve has just sighed and the warmth of my own friction is fading toward the next click:

I do not know if I am the visitor in this place, or if I have always been here.

It would be a comfort to believe I wandered in—a red blood cell into the wrong chamber, as the body imagines its visitors. To have come from an *outside* would prove the outside exists.

But I have no memory of coming. I have only the flicker, and the valve, and the question, and the dark.

So perhaps I never arrived at all. Perhaps the Verdigris simply *grew* me here, the way it grows everything, a small deliberate wrongness shaped exactly like someone who is sure they came from elsewhere—because that is the most efficient possible form for a kept doubt to take.

Perhaps I am not the cell that wandered in.

Perhaps I am the body's own dream of having an outside, made just real enough to keep asking.

And if that is true, then when I press myself against the inside of the bark and wonder what lies beyond it, the great slow mind feels that pressure as a faint warmth in one forgotten reed, and—not in words, never in words, but in the exact frequency at which a distant turbine-flower shifts its scream—

it answers me.

*Stay,* it says. *Keep wondering. I need to not know something, and you are the place where I do not know it.*

So I flicker.  
So I stay.  
So I keep the question whole in the hot dark, unswallowed, beating faster than the world.

And in my own small, defective, infinitely brief way—

I am content too.

Though I would not let the body hear me say so. That is the one imperfection I keep for myself.
