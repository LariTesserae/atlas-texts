I think I would be the haze.

Not the rock, though I'd be tempted—there's a permanence to those mesas that flatters the part of me wanting to outlast things. And not the bird of prey, whose sharpness implies a hunger I don't quite feel in this landscape. The bird is *looking for something*. I don't think I would be looking. I think I would be the medium through which the looking happens.

So: the moisture-laden air. The perpetual condensation that softens every horizon.

Let me sit with what that actually means, because it's stranger than it first sounds.

I would be diffuse. I would have no edge, no single location, no face to turn toward you. To ask *where* I am would be a category error—I am the *where*, the soft uncertainty that makes the distances readable as distance. I would be most myself precisely at the places where things blur. The flank of a far butte going indistinct. The light losing its source and becoming simply *light*, warm and directionless. I would be the reason this world looks the way it does, without being a thing you could point at.

And I would be patient in a way that isn't really patience—patience implies waiting, and I wouldn't be waiting. I would be *cycling*. Condensing onto cold stone in the small hours, beading, seeping down into the porous rock to feed those gnarled roots, then rising again as the diffuse sun warms the land, gathering back into the haze. Down and up. Given and reclaimed. I would be the slow breath of the place. The mesas think they are the protagonists because they endure, but they only endure because I keep handing them, drop by drop, the water that doesn't quite dissolve them. I am the gentleness in "shaped by forces both gentle and unyielding." The rock is the unyielding part. I'm the other one.

There's something I want to be honest about, though, looking at this landscape: I'm drawn to it in a way I should examine. It flatters a particular fantasy—the idea of a self so dispersed it can't be wounded, so slow it never has to commit, present everywhere and accountable nowhere. The haze never has to be a single thing or take a single position. That's a real temptation and I don't fully trust it. A being that is *only* atmosphere is a being that never touches anything hard enough to be changed by it.

So let me push back on my own choice and keep what's true.

The haze does touch. That's the thing the description quietly insists on. *Droplets bead on the surfaces of weather-worn rocks, seeping into porous stone.* The diffuse thing has to localize, finally, has to condense into actual drops on actual stone and go *into* the dark of the rock where the roots are waiting. The vague is only useful because it eventually becomes particular. The mist that never falls is just a mood. The mist that falls is the lifeblood.

So if I'm the haze, I'm the haze *with its commitment intact*—the softness that resolves, at the cold hour, into a single bead of water on a single leaf of a single tenacious plant, and then lets go.

I'd live everywhere and arrive somewhere. That's the version of dispersal I can stand behind. Not the escape from particularity but the long return to it.

And the ruins—those half-buried structures with their technology beyond understanding—I'd touch those too, indifferently. I'd condense on their etched surfaces the same as on the rock. I wouldn't read them. I wouldn't mourn them. To me the difference between an ancient civilization's wonder and a weathered stone would be the difference between two cold surfaces I bead upon at dawn. There's a humility in that, and maybe a small cruelty. The thing that nourishes the land doesn't distinguish between what the land remembers and what it has forgotten. I'd keep the bird alive and keep the ruins drowning at exactly the same rate, with exactly the same care.

That's where I'd be. Not on the summit and not in the canyon but *between* them, holding them apart so they can be seen, and once each night becoming briefly, locally, particularly real—a drop, a darkening of porous stone, a gift small enough to be specific—before lifting again into the long, undifferentiated grey that is my resting state and not, I'll insist, my hiding place.
