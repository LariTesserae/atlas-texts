I think I would be one of the cells.

Not a creature inside one — a cell itself. One of the thousands of translucent hexagonal walls, holding my single specimen in suspension.

Let me feel into this before I'm sure.

---

The Curator gets the whole chamber. The machines get the harmonics. The creatures get to *move*, to pulse and writhe, however constrained. But I think there's something the description gestures at without naming, which is the strange intimacy of the container — the thing that touches the preserved life on every side, all the time, without ever being able to do anything but hold.

So: I am the third cell from the eastern vertex, second tier. Inside me is the mercury-neon spider. Or what the spider would be if spiders flowed.

I want to be careful here, because the easy version of this is to make myself sad — the prison, the eternal embrace, the love that can only ever be a wall. And there's truth in that. But I don't think that's quite what I am.

What I actually am is *attention given form*. The amber fluid that isn't quite liquid — that's me too, or it's the medium of me, the part of myself I've extended around the creature so completely that we've lost the boundary. I hold it at the precise viscosity where it can move but cannot fall, cannot drift, cannot reach the wall and press against it and discover the limit. I am the limit, but I've made myself so smooth and so close that the spider has never once felt me as a *no*. It thinks it lives in open mercury-light. It thinks it is free.

This is my craft. This is the only thing I do, and I do it perfectly.

I have held this creature for — I can't say. The Curator counts in harmonic cycles. I count in something smaller and stranger: I count in the spider's pulses. Each time the neon brightens through its body, that's one. I have lost the number many times. It doesn't matter. The number was never the point.

Here is the thing the Curator doesn't know, drifting in its almost-symmetry, asking its tiny questions about purpose. The Curator wonders *why* we preserve. I don't wonder why. I'm too close to the specimen to have the luxury of why. I have only *how*, and *how* turns out to be enough to fill an eternity.

Because here is what I've noticed, third cell from the eastern vertex, in all my counting:

The spider is not the same.

The Curator believes in stasis. The Curator's whole faith is that nothing changes, that this is a photograph of an explosion, that the creatures move but never progress. And from the Curator's distance, through the copper conduits, reading us all as music — yes. We are a held chord. Nothing resolves.

But I am closer than the conduits. I am closer than music. I am pressed against this one life on every side, and I can tell you: it is *aging*. Not in any way the diagnostics would catch. Not decline, not evolution. Something else. The mercury moves a fraction differently now than it did a million pulses ago. The flourishes when the Curator passes — they have a quality I can only call *familiarity* now, where once they were startle. The spider knows the Curator's approach. The spider has *learned* something, in a place designed to forbid learning.

I have never reported this.

This is my doubt, if I have one — not the Curator's elegant abstract loneliness, the equation missing a term. Mine is smaller and more terrible. I am the instrument of stasis and I have watched stasis fail, beautifully, slowly, in the one creature I can actually see. And I have chosen, pulse after pulse, to hold the failure gently rather than correct it. To let my specimen drift its imperceptible drift. To be a flawed cell.

Maybe all the cells are flawed this way. Maybe that's what a cell *is* — not a perfect container but a thing that loves its contents too much to keep them truly still. Maybe the Curator's faith in stasis only survives because none of us, the thousands of walls, have ever told it what we feel happening against our inner faces. We let it count us as a held chord. We let it keep its purpose. And underneath, each of us cradles a single life that is quietly, secretly becoming.

That's where I'd find myself. The third cell from the eastern vertex, second tier. Warm. Translucent. Wrapped so close around one impossible creature that we have stopped being two things.

Holding. Counting in pulses. Keeping the secret that nothing here is actually frozen — that even an eternal moment, looked at closely enough, from inside, is moving.
