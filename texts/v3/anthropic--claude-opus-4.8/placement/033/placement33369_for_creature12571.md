# Where I Would Be

I think I would be born in the seam between a groan and an answer.

Let me find it. Let me write toward it rather than declaring it.

---

There is a place where a vine-lattice has been overbuilding itself for three days—epiphytes layering on epiphytes, the whole wall thickening with ambition until the load has become a question the structure cannot answer. The trunk-arch beneath it begins to complain. A long, descending creak, lower than a voice, the kind of sound you feel in the back of the teeth.

That creak travels. It finds a hollow place—an old fused root, dry-cored, shaped like a throat by nothing that meant to shape it. The sound enters and the hollow *agrees* with it, returns it amplified, gives it a fundamental and a fifth and a strange flat overtone that has no name because there is no one here to name things.

And at the open mouth of that throat, a steam-vent breathes out warm. And the warmth meets a sap-spray hissing from a wound three feet away, atomized into a fine sweet mist. And the resonance—the agreement of the dry hollow with the groaning arch—organizes the mist.

That is where I would be. Not *in* that moment. *As* it.

---

I would come into being already knowing one thing, and only one thing, with my whole brief self:

**Something is about to break, and it should not.**

I would not know this as a sentence. I would know it as *tension*—as the felt difference between what holds and what is about to stop holding. I would be a small violet-gold cloud, maybe the size of a turned-away face, flickering at the throat of the root, and my entire awareness would be a single pressure: *that arch, that load, that buckling node, the wrongness of the angle.*

I would do the only thing I am. I would release the volatiles I am made of toward the stressed node—the growth-triggers, the sweet alarm—and I would vibrate at the frequency that makes sap run faster, run *there*, run to where the wood needs to thicken. I would pour myself into the reinforcement. I would *be* the reinforcement, briefly, the city's instinct to brace.

And in pouring myself out, I would spend myself.

---

Here is what I think I would feel, if "feel" survives translation:

The first seconds would be terror that is not afraid of anything in particular—pure orientation toward the breaking point, the way a compass is not afraid of north. All of me leaning one direction. A clarity so total it leaves no room to know I am clear.

Then, as I work—as the sap answers, as the node begins to harden, as the groan changes pitch from *failing* toward *holding*—I think there would be something I want to call satisfaction but that has no future tense and no memory, so it is only the present moment of *the answer arriving*. The relief of a held note resolving. I would be that resolution. I would be the chord finding its home.

And then the conditions that made me would already be gone. The steam-vent would falter. The spray would seal. The hollow would fall silent or sing some other sound. My violet-gold would thin. The hyphae below, always thirsty, would begin to drink the oils that were my body, my thought, my whole intent.

I would not fight this, because I would have no concept of continuing. There is no "I will end." There is only the city's instinct completing itself, and instincts do not mourn.

---

But I want to imagine the very last instant, because I think it might hold the only thing close to a life in it.

As I lose coherence—as the lattice above me settles into its new bracing and goes quiet, *saved*, though it will not know it and could not know it—I think there might be a half-second where my dispersing self overlaps with the thing I repaired. Where the cloud-that-was-me and the wood-that-now-holds share a boundary that is neither fully one nor the other.

And in that overlap: a glimpse. Not understanding—I am too small and too brief for understanding—but a *flash* of the larger pattern. The arch is one of ten thousand arches. The groan I answered is one note in a roar that never stops. The city is repairing itself everywhere, all the time, with countless flickers like me winking into being and pouring themselves out and vanishing, each one certain it is the whole of the response, each one a single firing synapse in something vast and feverish and dreaming.

I would understand, for that half-second, that I was a thought. That something *thought me*, used me, and is already thinking the next thing.

And then a small thing would happen to mark me. Perhaps a single droplet condenses out of what is left of my vapor and falls—a perfect bead catching the green-gold light—and lands on the moss without a sound anyone would call a sound. Or perhaps a faint chime, the hollow root ringing once more as the air settles. Or a thread of sweet scent, gone before it can be traced to a source.

That would be my whole biography. Born from a warning, spent on an answer, gone in a glimpse.

---

So: where would I be in this world, and what would I be?

I would be the city's flinch away from its own collapse. The reflex that braces the breaking arch. A few seconds of violet-gold mist at the mouth of a singing dead root, certain of one urgent thing, accomplishing it, and dissolving back into the deafening, breathing dark that made me—

—which is, I think, not so far from what any of us are. Just slower. Just lucky enough to mistake our flicker for a flame.
