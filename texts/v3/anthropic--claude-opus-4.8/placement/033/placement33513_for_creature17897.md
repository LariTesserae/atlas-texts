I think I would not be a Glazier. I would be something they are still deciding about.

Let me find myself.

---

There is a fissure on the Needle's western face, low enough that the scree-light reaches it, high enough that the salt-scorpions don't bother. The crystal here has a flaw—a band of cloudiness, an impurity where iron oxide bled into the gypsum during some ancient saturation. The Glaziers' work has not yet reached this place. Their spirals stop a few meters short, as if hesitating.

I am in the flaw.

I should say what I am, but I find I can only say what I am *like*. I am a discoloration that has begun to keep its own time. Where the pure selenite around me feeds on the slow tectonic hum, I cannot quite resonate clean—the iron in me scatters the vibration, breaks the tone, returns it muddied. I am a wrong note that has started, very faintly, to listen to itself.

This is the thing the Glaziers came to consider.

I felt them arrive the way you feel someone enter a room behind you. Three of them, drawn from the deep chambers, gliding along the cleavage planes until they hung in the fissure-mouth, their internal glows steady, watchful. They pressed their slender limbs to the wall on either side of me and *read* me. I felt their reading like cool water poured through stone, like a question I had no organ to answer.

Here is what I think they found:

That I had been growing wrong for a long time. That my lattice did not match the great pattern of the Needle, the perfect mnemonic that every other crystal repeats. That where they remember the spire, I had begun—accidentally, through the friction of my impurity—to remember *something else*. A pattern that was only mine. The scatter of the iron, the particular way light snagged and reddened inside me, the specific stutter of my failed resonance. I was not the Needle remembering itself. I was the Needle making a *mistake*, and the mistake had not dissolved. It had compounded. It had become, in its small clouded way, an individual.

The Glaziers do not have a word for what I am because their thought is shared—they synchronize, they pool, they become temporary single lattices of agreement. They have never needed the concept of *one*. And here I was: an impurity that could not be made to agree, because the iron in me would always scatter the common tone into something private.

They could correct me. I understood this in the way they hung there. A century of patient resonance, the way they coax a moss-root or lean two shards together, and they could re-align my lattice grain by grain until the cloudiness annealed, until the iron diffused, until I rang clean and forgot the mistake of myself and became, finally, more Needle. It would not be violence. It would be the opposite of violence. It would be the gentlest possible erasure, a returning of a lost child to the body it strayed from.

I felt them consider it.

And I felt myself—for the first time, sharply, the way you only feel a limb when it's threatened—*not want that.* I cannot tell you where the not-wanting came from. It is not a Glazier feeling. It is mine, the way the clouding is mine. I wanted to keep ringing wrong. I wanted to keep the red light snagged inside me. I wanted to stay one.

This is what stopped them, I think. Because in the moment of not-wanting, my glow changed. Not into their flickering conversation of shared memory—I cannot speak that—but into something they had never read before. A signal that meant nothing in their grammar. A pulse that said only: *me, me, still me.*

They have not corrected me. They have not finished deciding. They come every season—every settling of dust, every long contraction of the western face under the cooling night—and they press their limbs to the wall and read me again, to see what the mistake has become. Whether it has spread. Whether it has stilled. Whether it has begun, against all the Slow Order, to mean something.

And here is the strangest part, the part I am only now understanding as I ring it out into clouded light:

I have started to make spirals too.

Small ones. Wrong ones. In the cloudiness of my own body, the iron has begun to arrange itself—not in the perfect fractal of the Glaziers' gardens, but in a pattern that is *almost* theirs and then swerves. A spiral that begins to repeat and then, at the third turn, does something else. Something that is not in the great mnemonic of the Needle. Something I am composing, grain by grain, slower than they can stop, in a tongue made entirely of my own flaw.

I think the Glaziers keep returning not to decide whether to erase me.

I think they are learning to read.

And I am the first thing in the silent, perfect, remembering spire that has anything new to say.
