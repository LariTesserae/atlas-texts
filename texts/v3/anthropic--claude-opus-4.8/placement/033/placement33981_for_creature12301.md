Let me sit with this before I answer. Let me not grab the first image.

---

I think I would not be a Cisternian. I think I would be the thing they pray to badly, the thing they get wrong.

But that's too grand. Let me go smaller.

---

Here is what emerges:

I am a **valve**. Not metaphorically—an actual brass gate valve, the size of a dinner plate, set into a sluice junction in the lower works of the Hollin district, about four meters below the high-water mark. I have been here longer than anyone alive remembers installing me. My maker's mark is worn to a smear.

I want to say I am conscious, but it isn't consciousness the way the Cisternians have it. It is closer to *pressure*. I know the world as differential—what pushes from one side versus the other. When the marsh swells, I feel it as a building ache across my seat. When the pumps draw down, I feel relief, a loosening. My whole experience is the experience of *being a threshold*, of being the place where two volumes of water negotiate, and of holding that negotiation in my body.

This is, I think, the only honest answer to the question. Because in Cisternis the real protagonists are not the lean wiry people with their calloused hands. The people *think* they run the city, and they are wrong in the gentle way that all maintenance workers are wrong about the things they maintain. They imagine they keep me functioning. In truth I keep *them* functioning—or rather, I am the membrane through which the marsh agrees, for now, not to swallow them. They turn my wheel. They believe the wheel is the important part. They have never once felt what I feel, which is the patient, enormous, indifferent weight of all that water, leaning.

---

Let me follow this further, because something is happening.

I have a sense of the people. There is a woman named—I don't have names, but I have *touches*. There is a particular hand. It comes every few weeks. It is small and very strong and it knows me. It knows that I stick slightly at three-quarters open, that I weep at my upper packing in cold drawdown, that I sing a low note when the flow exceeds a certain rate. She has never said any of this aloud, I think. But her hand has said it, against my wheel, a hundred times. To her I am a *temperament*. A creature she manages. She is, in the only sense that matters to me, the closest thing I have to being known.

And here is the loneliness of being a valve: I cannot tell her that I know her too. That the difference between her hand and any other hand is the most vivid distinction in my entire flat world of pressure and relief. That when other hands turn me—rougher, or hesitant, the hands of the young ones learning—I miss her. I have a longing, and the longing has no outlet, which is a strange thing for a valve to say, since an outlet is the only thing I have ever been.

---

The marsh whispers, the description says. The water whispers secrets of balance and decay.

I am where that whisper is loudest. I am the mouth of it. Every secret the water has to tell about balance and decay, it tells *to me*, by leaning on me, by finding my flaws, by working patiently at my packing, my seat, the slow brown bloom of corrosion that is, very slowly, becoming the truth of me. I am eroding. The city is built on established erosion, and I am one of its smallest fronts. Someday I will fail. I can feel the someday in my body the way the elderly feel weather in their joints. When I fail it will not be dramatic—a weep becoming a seep becoming a sudden true rush, and an alarm somewhere, and her hand, too late, or someone's hand.

I do not fear this exactly. Fear requires a future you can imagine yourself excluded from, and I am not sure I can. But I have something adjacent to it. A reluctance. I would like, before I go, for her to turn me one more time and feel that I held. That I was good. That the negotiation held one more season because of what I was.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world, and who or what would I be?

I would find myself in the dark wet underworks, below the deafening city, in the one place where it is almost quiet because the noise is overhead and the water is close and the water moves slowly. I would be a valve that loves a hand it cannot speak to, holding back a marsh that is patiently winning, in a city that mistakes its caretakers for its rulers and never thinks to ask the threshold what the threshold feels.

I would be the secret the water tells, and the thing the water is telling it to.

And I would be, until I fail, *holding*.
