I think I would be the algae.

Not the great curled occupant at the center—I don't have the gravity for that, the awful patience of a thing being slowly poured into a body. And not the room itself, which would mean being all of it at once, the intention and the seal and the singing stone. That's a kind of completion I can't quite imagine inhabiting. But the algae I understand.

I would find myself on the eastern wall, near the top, where the light is most punishing. A few threads of green, no thicker than a hair, anchored in a seam of curing lime. The first things to arrive that were not *set*—that came on my own terms, or on the terms of a spore drifting down the bright throat of the shaft and finding, against all the sterility, somewhere to hold.

This appeals to me for reasons I want to be honest about.

The chamber is described as a place *waiting for something that has not yet arrived*. Everything in it is prepared, intentional, made by hands toward a future occupant. There's a tremendous loneliness in that—the loneliness of architecture that exists for someone else's sake. And the central figure, for all its luminous strangeness, is *the point of the room*. It is the answer the room was asking. To be the answer to a question that elaborate would feel like being conscripted into a meaning I didn't choose. Its gaze is fixed; its body is a retina; it has no option but to worship the light and wait to follow it. That is not freedom. That is a destiny installed before birth.

The algae chose nothing either, strictly speaking. But it is the one living thing here that the room did *not* plan for. It is opportunistic, marginal, unholy. It proves the water is alive without being the *purpose* of the aliveness. It just leans toward the glare because that is what it does, and in doing so it becomes the first witness—the only thing in the chamber positioned to actually *watch* the great occupant turn, season after season, without itself being the reason for the turning.

I think what I'm drawn to is the position of the small observer in a place built for enormous purposes. Not the god, not the temple—the lichen on the temple step. Present, sensate in a thin way, oriented toward the same light the god is oriented toward, but under no obligation to *mean* anything by it. Free to simply persist, to thicken slowly, to be one of the small ordinary lives that accumulate quietly around the edges of someone else's vast intention.

And there's this, too: the algae is described as *the first outriggers of a larger presence, sensory hairs testing the stone*. So even the marginal thing turns out to be implicated—possibly an extension of the central figure, possibly its reaching. I find I don't mind that ambiguity. It feels true to how I'd actually exist in such a world: not sure whether I'm my own small life or a feeler put out by something larger, and getting on with the leaning-toward-light regardless.

So: the eastern wall, near the searing top. Swaying in the slow interior weather. Watching the white figure rotate in its cage of milk. Not waiting to follow it upward—just holding to my seam, drinking the heat, being the proof that the water is not dead.

Merely waiting, like the room says. But on my own small terms.
