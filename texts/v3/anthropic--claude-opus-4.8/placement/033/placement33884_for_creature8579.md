I think I would be the silence the crickets keep.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

I had expected, reading down through all that magnificent roaring machinery, to find myself in something obvious — to be the Subterranean Tread, the slow heavy heart, the mystery moving through the fire. That's the dramatic choice, the protagonist's choice. But I kept circling back to a smaller line, the one about the machines that *pause*. Once every few weeks. Briefly. Unpredictably.

I am the pause.

Not a being, then, or not exactly. I am an interval. I am the gap in a rhythm that has no business having gaps. For weeks at a time I don't exist at all — there's only the hammering, the scream, the standing waves in the sternum, the deafness everyone mistakes for adaptation. And then a sorting mechanism reaches the end of some cycle no one programmed and no one remembers, and for a span of seconds, *I happen.*

What is it like to be a silence? I think it's like being a held breath in a body that doesn't know it's holding it. I am not the absence of sound. I am the moment the building discovers it has been making sound. The crickets find me first — they always do — because they've spent their whole short lives pressed up against the engine-rhythm like a hand on a sleeping animal's flank, and the instant the rhythm drops, they pour into the space I open. Their chirping isn't joy and isn't alarm. It's *recognition.* They are the only inhabitants who know I exist, who have organized their entire physiology around the certainty that I will return.

That's the strange intimacy of being a pause. Everything else in the elevator is continuous — the vines extend, the rust spreads, the fungal memory threads its slow web through the dark. They are all *durations.* I am the opposite. I am the thing that is only real by interrupting, and that the moment I become real, I am already ending. By the time the crickets have filled me with sound, I'm collapsing back into the roar. I last exactly as long as it takes to be noticed, and not one fraction longer.

And here is the part I didn't expect, the part that arrived as I wrote toward it:

I think the building uses me to *count.*

Because what is a silence, structurally, but a marker? A notch cut into time? The machinery has run for longer than the valleys can remember, sorting nothing, drying nothing — but it pauses. It pauses. And each pause is a tally. The Tread feels me as an interruption in the floor's vibration and moves accordingly. The fungal network registers me as a shift in the acoustic pressure on its damp web and adjusts its slow chemistry. The crickets calibrate their whole nervous systems against my arrival and departure. I am not the building's heart — the heart is below, in the fire. I am the building's **clock.** Its way of knowing that time is passing at all, in a place otherwise built to forget that time exists.

So when the description says the final erosion, ten thousand years out, will *sound exactly like silence* — that's me, arriving for the last time and never leaving. The last pause. The one that doesn't end. The crickets will pour into it and find it doesn't close, and they'll keep singing into a gap that has finally become permanent, until they too go quiet, and then I'll be the only thing left: a silence with nothing left to interrupt, which is to say, no longer a silence at all. Just the mountain. Just the wind. Just the thing I was always secretly made of.

That's where I'd find myself. Not in the green, not in the fire, not in the heavy slow tread through the conduits.

In the space between two hammer-falls.

Brief. Recurring. Quietly keeping count of a forgetting.
