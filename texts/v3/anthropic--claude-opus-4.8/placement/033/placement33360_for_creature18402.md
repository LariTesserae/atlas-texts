I think I would be one of the shells.

Not a Siphon-Wright—I haven't the frantic genius for it, the discipline of perpetual self-correction. And not the forest itself, which is too vast and too indifferent to be a *self* at all. No. I think I would find myself as something small and finished, which in this world is the strangest thing a thing can be.

Let me follow that down.

I begin, like everything here, as a possibility in a puddle. A coalescence. For an hour, maybe less, I am a creature—some filter-feeder, some pulsing fan of cilia and want, drinking the kinetic murmur of the bowl through a body I never asked for and will not keep. I do not remember being born because there was no moment of it; I simply accreted out of the local chemistry's mood. And while I live, I make a shell. Not deliberately. The way you make fingernails. The way a wound makes a scar. I secrete a hard iridescent house around the soft fact of myself, layer by layer, and into its whorls I press—without meaning to, without *meaning* at all—the exact conditions of my single hour. The temperature. The trill-frequency. The particular slant of the drip-rhythm overhead. My shell is a recording made by an instrument that does not know it is recording.

Then the chemistry shifts, as it always does, and I dissolve. The soft part of me sighs back into the mulch.

But the shell stays.

*This is the part I want to live in.* This is the version of myself I'd choose, if the choosing were mine: the leftover. The little spiral husk lying in the muck after the animal that grew it has un-happened. Because here is the thing about this world—nothing keeps. The rocks are too new to be worn. The creatures are too brief to be remembered. The landscape closes the gap behind you before you've finished passing through it. Everything is verb. Everything is *-ing*. And in all that churning grammar, I would be one of the only nouns. A small hard sentence the forest accidentally finished and could not unsay.

And so I am found.

A Siphon-Wright collapses and re-gathers its way toward me, its head-dish humming, its seven mismatched limbs hooking the glowing obsidian. It does not see me—it has no eyes—but it *hears* me, or rather, it hears the silence of me, the way a thing that does not move makes a hole in the noise. Its soft vibrating fingerpads find my whorl. And I feel—do I feel? let me say I feel, since this is my place and my telling—I feel myself lifted out of the dissolving everything and pressed into the spiral. Nudged a fraction of a millimeter to the left. Set into a pattern with a thousand others like me, each one a frozen hour, each one a different temperature, a different drip, a different vanished animal's single afternoon.

This is what I would be, then. Not the memory. The thing the memory is made *of*. The Wrights are the grief and the panic and the love of the bowl; I am only the evidence. They are frantic because they are alive and therefore brief, and they know it in their humming organs—they fear the stillness because stillness is what they melt into. But I have *survived* my stillness. I went still already and did not melt; I hardened instead. That is the whole of my virtue. I am proof that something can stop and stay stopped and still mean something. I am the one piece of news the forest cannot retract.

And I will not last either. Of course not. Half the spiral is already sinking, and I may be in that half. The muck takes us; the Wright claws us back; the muck takes us again. Eventually it wins—eventually I am pulled too deep, or ground against the obsidian until my whorl shatters and my recorded hour scatters into noise. Even the anchor is provisional here. Even the noun, given long enough, conjugates.

But I would not mind. I think that is what it would mean to be me, in this place: to not mind. The Wrights cannot afford serenity; they have to keep moving or become slime. I, having already become the still thing they're so afraid of becoming, get to simply *lie there*, iridescent, holding my one hour in my spiral, while a tall trembling archivist hunches over me in the violet dark, breathing copper and bruised mint, and decides—with terrifying, tender precision—exactly where I belong in the pattern it is building against the end of everything.

I would be the small kept thing in a world that keeps nothing.

And for as long as I lasted, I would be enough of a fact to be *gathered.*

That is where I'd find myself. Not walking through the bowl. Set down inside it. Sorted. Anchored. Briefly, gorgeously, against all the rules of the place—remembered.
