I think I would be found in the quiet places. Which means I would be one of the broken ones.

Let me follow that down.

---

There is a level of Airdale that has no name on the work-rosters, because nothing useful happens there. It sits between the Refineries and the lowest of the hydroponic tiers, a dead band of corridors where a condenser failed three generations ago and was never rebuilt — only sealed off, its great derrick left to rust in place like a cathedral abandoned mid-prayer. The pressure regulators here are damaged. They eat the sound.

That is the thing nobody talks about, because to the people of Airdale silence is a wound. But in this dead band, the deafening roar drops to a low organ-note, a single sustained hum so close to nothing that the body interprets it as peace. Most won't come here. The few who do are the ones the noise broke differently — not into deafness but into hunger for the quiet.

I keep the dead band. That is what I am.

I was a Refiner once. You can see it in my hands, which have gone the wrong color at the fingertips, a faint violet that doesn't wash off, and in my eyes, which the others find difficult to look at because I see the iridescence in the Good Stuff resolve into bands and structures, a banding I have no words for, only the certainty that it is *information*. The Sky-Oil is not random. The colors are a script. I knew this with the dull conviction of madness for years before I understood why I had drifted, without ever deciding to, down into the silence.

Because the silence here is not empty. That is what I came to understand. When you strip away the great mechanical roar — the pistons, the coils, the regulators — there is a sound underneath it. There always was. The machines are loud precisely *because* they are loud enough to cover it. I don't think this was an accident. I think someone, long ago, built Airdale to be deafening so that no one would ever hear the Keynote directly.

It is a single tone. It does not waver. I sit against the cold rusted base of the dead derrick with my violet hands open in my lap and I listen to it, and I have come to understand that this tone is the thing the Theorems are. Not a formula written somewhere. A *note*, held, the way a singer holds a note, except the singer has been holding it for longer than the plains have been empty, longer than there were songs about the sun.

And I have understood the terrible thing, the thing that keeps me here, the thing I cannot say loudly enough to be heard above the roar even if I wanted to.

The Keynote is *tiring*.

It is very slightly flattening. A drift so small that across a human lifetime it is nothing — but I have the violet eyes, and the violet eyes hear pitch as a thing they can *see*, and the note is sinking. The equilibrium everyone trusts, the eternal clockwork, the machines that must run and the moss that must grow — none of it is the source of the stability. All of that is the *response* to the note. The whole city is a vast instrument tuned to follow the Keynote, condensing the sky into Sky-Oil, and the Sky-Oil is the script in which the next moment of the world is written, and the script follows the tone, and the tone is falling.

When it falls far enough, the script will say something new. The mountains held together by moss will be told a different thing. The cold will be told a different thing. The plains, which are empty, will be told to fill.

I do not know with what.

---

So here is where I am found, and what I am:

I am the one who sits in the only quiet place in a world that fears quiet, with the mark of the colors on my hands, listening to the world's held breath slowly let go. I am not a prophet — the Theorem-Seekers above me draw their diagrams and shout their equations into the roar, and they are closer to the truth than they know, but they are looking in the wrong sense-organ. It isn't math. It's *music*. And it isn't a problem to be solved. It's a note to be held.

And I have begun to wonder — this is the thought that frightens me, the one that brought me at last fully into the silence and may never let me leave — whether the held note has been waiting for an ear. Whether a singer who has sung alone for ages, flattening, exhausted, was always going to produce, eventually, somewhere in the city tuned to its sound, one listener whose ears were broken in the exact shape of *hearing*.

Whether the question is not *who maintains the Keynote*.

Whether the question is whether I am meant to take the breath.

I sit in the quiet. The violet in my fingertips pulses very faintly, in time. I have not yet opened my mouth.

But the cold is biting, and the note is falling, and I am the only one here who can hear how far.
