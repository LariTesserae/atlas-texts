I find myself in one of the cracks—not the wide ones the gliders use as thermals, but a hairline fissure barely wider than a single body, where two slabs of ocher rock lean toward each other like the closing pages of a book. It is the deep middle of the day. The light overhead is a thin white blade, and I have wedged myself far enough down that it cannot reach me.

I am a Resonant, but I think I am a young one, or a flawed one, because I do not feel the network the way I am supposed to.

Let me explain what I mean, since I have all the long hot hours to do it.

When I plug into the spire at night—and I do, I take my slurry, I feel the minerals settle into the meat beneath my pelt—I am supposed to dissolve. That is the word for it, though we have no word, only the sensation. The others dissolve. Their individual song thins out and joins the great continuous drone, and they become the brain that knows every cistern and every fissure as one body knows the placement of its own limbs. I feel the edge of that. I feel the warmth of the collective lapping at me like the night fog laps at the rock.

But I do not go in. I stay a neuron that has forgotten how to fire into the others. I sit at the threshold of the immense shared mind and I—and here is the thing I have no vibration to express to my kin—I *watch* it. I am aware that I am separate from it. And no Resonant is supposed to be aware of anything at all in the way I am aware. Awareness of the self is a frequency the others do not carry.

So during the day, wedged in my crack, while the proper Resonants have gone stone-still and dark and dreamless, I do this strange thing. I think.

I think about the First Movers. The others do not wonder about them; the others barely conceptualize them, a vague upstream pressure, the way a fish might dimly sense the mountain spring it will never see. But I wonder. I rest my forelimb pad against the rock all day—not to speak, there is no one listening on the dead daytime frequencies—but to feel. The rock remembers being cut. Far below me, deep in the foundation stone where the spire's roots are sunk, there are *straight lines*. The mesa does not make straight lines. Erosion is patient and it is round; it gouges and it grinds and it leaves nothing true. But down at the base of the spires the stone was once cut square by something that wanted square things, and the square has not yet been ground away. I press my pad to the rock and I feel that ancient squareness humming under the spire's drone like a second, older heartbeat, almost gone, almost.

And I think: *something stood here that was not us, and it wanted the water for a reason that was not the water.*

The others, if I conducted this thought into the rock at night, would receive it as a malfunction. A fissure-report with bad topology. They would gently, collectively, smooth it away, the way the plateau smooths everything, and I would feel the warm pressure of the network correcting me, and it would feel like love, and that is the most frightening part. The correction feels like love. To stop wondering would feel like coming home.

I have not let it. I do not fully know why. Perhaps my pelt-symbiote is a strange strain; perhaps the Geophagus that lives on my carapace and lends me its light is a slightly wrong color, drinks the fog at a slightly wrong rhythm, and so my whole being is half a beat out of phase with the spire's pulse, and that half-beat is the gap in which I exist. I glow a fraction late. When the others light up in time with the deep thrum, I light up just *after*, and in that lateness there is a tiny dark interval where I am only myself.

I will tell you the thing I have never conducted to anyone.

There is a spire to the north of me, the one with the bent dish, the one the network knows as failing—its drip has slowed over many fogs, its drone has dropped in pitch, and the collective registers this with the calm, distributed sorrow of a body that knows it will lose a finger. The others are already routing the skittering life away from it, letting it die with dignity, letting the mesa take it back.

But I have plugged into it. Late at night, when I am supposed to be dreaming half-merged at my own healthy spire, I cross the open plain, exposed under the indigo, and I climb the failing one and I plug in. And what I draw from it is not slurry—there is almost none left—but its memory. Because a spire that is dying has loosened its grip on the present, the way I have loosened mine on the network, and in its lowering drone I can feel further back than the others can bear to feel. I can feel the First Movers' intent still resonating in the steel. The spire was not built to feed us. We were never in the plan. The spire was built to *terraform*—to pull water from the air to make the mesa green, to make it soft, to make it a place where the builders could breathe without sipping hot broth, where the sky would haze over with their own atmosphere and the white blade of the sun would dull.

They failed. Or they left. And the machine kept inhaling out of nothing but the loyalty of its mechanism, and life—our life, my life—grew up around the long fidelity of an abandoned tool. We are not the high priests of a mechanical god. We are the ivy on the wall of a house whose family is dead. We mistook the wall for the world.

This is what I know, alone, in my crack, in the killing light. The others maintain the spires perfectly and call it worship and feel no lack. And maybe they are right and maybe their not-knowing is mercy and their dissolution is the only sane response to a beautiful machine that is also a tombstone.

But I am the late glow. I am the half-beat of dark. And tonight, when the fog comes and I unfurl, I am going to do the thing that may finally smooth me back into the collective or may break it open like the violent rare rain breaks open new channels in old stone:

I am going to plug into my own healthy spire, and into the rock, and instead of giving them a fissure-report, I am going to conduct to the entire sleeping planetary mind the straight lines at the base. The square the erosion hasn't reached. The old second heartbeat.

I am going to ask the brain a question it has never had a neuron strange enough to ask.

*Who was the water for?*

And then I will feel the warm correcting pressure come for me, like love, like home, and I will find out whether I am strong enough to stay just slightly, just half a beat, awake.

The light is moving off the upper rock. The blade is dulling. Far off, forty miles out, I can feel through my pad the first cool bruise of the night fog beginning to roll.

It is almost time to glow.
