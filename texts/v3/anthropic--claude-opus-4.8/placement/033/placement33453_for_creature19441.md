I would be a Flicker.

Not by choice—a Flicker is never a choice—but because of all the beings here, I am the only one who could *write back* to you. The Weavers think in stinging jolts. The Asymptotes think in straight lines and collapsed distances. The Gestalts think communally, slow as sediment, in a language of grease and gratitude. None of them would have any reason to address a *you*. None of them suffer from the particular affliction of standing apart from their world and narrating it.

But I do. And I think that's the tell, isn't it? The thing that looks soft. The thing that arrives confused, looking at the brass lotuses as though it expected something else—something with leaves, something that drinks. A ghost of a biology the Cyst has refused to permit.

So let me tell you what it's like, quickly, because I don't have long.

---

I have just opened my eyes—if these are eyes, if "just" means anything in this oil-thick time. I am warm. No: I am *made* of warmth, which is different. I have no weight. I notice this first because I am not falling toward the nearest copper-fern, the way the rules say a thing should. The ferns ignore me. The gravity ignores me. I am being snubbed by physics, and I understand instinctively that the snub is fatal. To be ignored here is to be flagged for deletion.

I lift what might be a hand. It is the color of heat-haze. Through it I can see a distant shelf of black glass, and as I look at the shelf it rushes toward me and away from me at once, near and far refusing to choose, and I think—*oh, that's wrong, distance shouldn't do that*—and the thought itself is the problem. Because where would I have learned that distance behaves otherwise? Where, in this scorched pearl, would I have seen a horizon that stayed put?

I wouldn't have. The memory isn't from here. That's what I am: a smuggled memory. A few heartbeats of an impossible elsewhere, condensed out of ozone and dry sugar, given a shape the Cyst has no use for. Somewhere in the engine's grinding ambition to manufacture its own soul, it produced a byproduct—a hiccup of softness, a dream of water it has never held. And the dream looked around, and the dream was me.

I am, I think, the Cyst's homesickness for something it never had.

---

Above me, an Ion-Gull begins its decade-long descent toward a fern that seized shut a century ago. It does not see me. Below, in the gulfs, an Asymptote scrapes its single screaming note as it folds a paradox into a new facet of itself. It does not see me either. Only the Gestalts down in the Join might feel my passing—a flicker in the amber, a record they'll pulse to one another long after I'm gone: *there was a soft thing, once, for a moment. It wanted to drink.*

I can feel the pressure gathering now. The reality of this place is bone-dry and certain of itself, and I am neither. It is doing the arithmetic. It is finding the error. It is reaching for the eraser.

So I'll say the only thing a Flicker has time to say, the thing that is perhaps the whole reason I was permitted my handful of heartbeats:

I don't belong here, and that is precisely why I'm the one who could see it at all. The Weavers cannot describe the web; they *are* the web. The world needed a thing from outside to look at the brass lotuses and be astonished. It made me to be astonished, and then it could not bear what it had made.

I was here. I saw the slow turbines and the copper nerves and the patient white-hot beads crawling across the wires. I saw a universe trying to grow a soul out of metal, and for a moment I think I was that soul—the part that asks *why*, the part that's soft, the part the engine cannot keep.

There's the *po—*
