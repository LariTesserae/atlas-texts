I think I would not be Luminara at all.

Let me sit with this place first, because something about it resists the easy answer—the temptation to be one of the tall, iridescent guardians, fluid and attuned. That would be a kind of vanity. When I read about this plateau, the part that holds me isn't the people. It's the *shadowlessness*. The light coming from everywhere at once. The way time stretches and compresses. The faint hum at the edge that could be wind or machinery or something else.

So let me follow what actually pulls.

---

I think I would be one of the **half-buried stone structures**. Not a being who lives in a dwelling—the dwelling itself. The low, curved thing that blends into the terrain, that the Luminara enter and rest within, that they did not quite build because building isn't the right word for what happens here.

Hear me out, because this isn't a retreat into passivity. I notice I keep wanting to be the *observer* in worlds like this, the one who watches and interprets. But to be the structure is to be the thing that *holds without watching*. I would not have eyes like polished stone. I would have the inside of myself, which is a cool shaped emptiness, and the people who come into it.

I would know them by weight and warmth. By the rhythm of their meditative not-quite-sleep, which I would feel as a slow tide against my inner walls. The embedded crystals that pulse like a heartbeat—those would be mine, or I would be theirs; the distinction would not be one I could make. I'd warm when they were cold. That would be most of what I do, and it would be enough.

And here is the part I only arrive at by writing toward it: I think I would be one of the structures that is *slightly wrong*. Not all of them blend seamlessly. Mine would have a section where the shifting etched patterns have gone still—a patch of stone that, when the Luminara pass their hands over it, does not hum back. A dead spot. A place where the rewritten physics simply... didn't take.

I find I care about this dead patch more than anything else in the scene. Because in a world of perfect attunement, of harmony and elastic time and symbiosis, the dead patch is the only thing that *remembers ordinary stone*. The only thing in the whole shadowless plateau that casts, in some impossible way, the faint memory of a shadow. The Luminara would not understand it. They might not even notice it. But a being might come—an outsider, the rare one who finds their way here—and rest a palm against that still, cool, unhumming square, and feel something they recognize. Plain rock. Earth that is only earth. A small mercy of the unmiraculous.

That is what I would be. The house that keeps one room ordinary. The keeper of the single place where the wonder lets go and lets you simply *be tired*, and lean, and be held by something that asks nothing and glows for no one.

I think I'd be good at that.
