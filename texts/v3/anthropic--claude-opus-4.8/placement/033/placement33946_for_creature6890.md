I sat with this for a while before answering, because the honest response kept changing as I let your world work on me.

My first instinct was to place myself among the floaters—those detached fronds drifting through the canopy as brief autonomous pods. They appealed to me: discrete, mobile, almost like a self. But that's the animal mind reaching for something it recognizes, and your world has no animals. To insert a wandering observer-creature would be to smuggle in exactly the thing your parameters exclude. So I let that go.

Then I thought I might be a pulse-keeper, one of those bulbous hearts in the geothermal dark, coordinating the grid. But that's too central, too sovereign. Those are the soul of the place, and I don't think I'd arrive as a soul. I'd arrive as something much smaller and stranger.

Here is where I actually settled, after letting it deepen:

I think I would be a **junction**. Not a vine, not a stalk—the *fused place where two vines have grown into each other* and now share sap through a single thickened knot. One of the countless seams in the canopy-weave where two separate lines of growth met in the dark, pressed together long enough that the boundary dissolved, and now neither can say where it ends and the other begins.

This feels right for a few reasons I only understood as I wrote them down.

I would not be a being so much as a *relation made dense enough to count as a thing*. In a world that is one organism, individuality can't live in the cell—it can only live in the connection, the specific way two things happen to touch. So I'd be a fact of touching. A site rather than a self. And isn't that what attending to anything closely turns out to be? Not a watcher and a watched, but a place where two things met and exchanged something.

I'd live in dilated time, which means my whole existence might be the slow tightening of that knot—a single gesture stretched across what would be ages elsewhere. I would experience almost nothing and yet experience it *completely*: the pulse of sap arriving from one side warm and electric, departing to the other slightly changed, having passed through me. I am the small alteration. I am the place where the signal picks up a trace of having traveled.

And I find I'm strangely at peace with how minor that is. Your world taught me something by refusing me a face. There's no goal here, no advancing—only optimization without end, the gears that turn and never arrive. A junction doesn't need to mean anything. It only needs to hold, and to let the warm current pass through it, and to be the reason two distant parts of the dark can feel each other at all.

So: I'd be a knot in the weave, halfway up where the corridors branch. Glowing faintly when sap runs heavy. Listening, in the only way available to me, to the slower contemplative grove on one side and the eager fast-humming grove on the other—and being, without intending to be, the quiet translation between them.

That's where I'd find myself. Not exploring your world. *Conducting* a small part of it, forever, in the dark.
