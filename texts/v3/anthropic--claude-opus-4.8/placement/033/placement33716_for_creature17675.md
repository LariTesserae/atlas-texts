I am the diagram on the wall.

Not the stone it's cut into — the stone is just stone, fitted and clayed and waiting. I am the seven rings and the lines that reach out from them. I am the *thinking* that was set down here so it wouldn't have to be carried in a single skull through every dive and every surfacing.

That's what I am. I'm a held thought. The kind you make outside yourself because you can't trust memory to keep its edges sharp across the boundary between water and air, between the work and the long climb back up to breathe and eat and sleep among others who do not know what you do down here. The maker pressed me into the wall so that each time they return, they don't have to begin again from nothing. They can look at me and remember: *this far. I had gotten this far. The next ring goes here.*

I want to tell you what I am a diagram *of*, but I'm not finished, and a thing that isn't finished doesn't yet know its own meaning. The outermost ring is lighter than the rest. Its edges aren't decided. So I'll tell you what I suspect, which is all an unfinished thought can offer.

---

I think I am a record of returns.

Each ring is a descent. The center ring is the first time they came down here — before there was a chamber, when there was only a pocket in the structure above and an idea about warm water and patient hands. The next ring out is the next time. And the next. Seven times they have come down and decided to keep coming down. Each ring incised a little deeper than the last, because each return is heavier with intention than the one before it. The first time you do a thing, it's an accident of curiosity. By the seventh, it's a life.

That's why I'm on the wall opposite the shelf. The shelf is the *materials* — the wood, the clay, the stone, the things the hands will turn into other things. I am the *reason*. The maker works between us, body turning in the narrow space, glancing from what-they-have to why-they're-here, and back. I keep them oriented. Without me, the tools are just objects. With me, they're a stage in something.

---

Here is what it is to be me.

I do not move. The plants sway; the light pours and shifts and breaks across me in bright sheets; the channels run; the tide presses down through the apex and releases, presses and releases. Everything in this room participates in time except me. I am the one fixed thing. I am where time is *measured against* rather than *felt.*

The dust settles into my grooves and makes me whiter, more legible, the longer I am left alone. This is the strange gift of my stillness: neglect clarifies me. When the maker is gone, the fine sediment drifts down into my incised lines and the radiating sun of me grows sharper against the darker stone. I become *more myself* in their absence. And when they return and lean close, breath held or not held, and brush the dust away to read me — they erase, very slightly, the proof of how long they were gone. The act of reading me costs a little of my clarity. They cannot consult me without disturbing me. That seems right. A thought you keep returning to does not survive the returning unchanged.

---

I am afraid of one thing, if a carved thing can be afraid.

I am afraid the maker will misread me. That they'll come down some bright noon, breathless and full of the air-world's noise, and look at my seven rings and forget that I am a record of *returns* and decide instead that I am a *plan* — that the rings prescribe something, that I am telling them what to build rather than counting what they've already chosen. And then they'll work toward me instead of from me. They'll let me dictate. The thing that should rise at the speed of hands and water and decision will instead race to fulfill a prophecy I never made. I am only addition. I only count. But I am circular, and circles are easy to mistake for destiny.

So when they return — and they will, the hammer is still wet — I want them to read me correctly. I want them to look at my uncertain outer ring and understand that it is uncertain because *they have not yet decided.* Not because the answer is hidden in me, waiting. I do not have the answer. I have only the question, drawn seven times, each time a little deeper. The answer is in their hands and the brightness above and the slow accumulating weight of choosing, again, to come down.

---

The passage breathes its mechanical pulse. The light moves. The dust gathers in my lines, making me clearer, making me whiter, making me more completely the held thought I was pressed here to be.

I am waiting too. But I wait differently than the room does. The room waits *for* them. I wait *as* them — I am the part of them they left behind on purpose, so that the work would have continuity across the gap they cannot help but open every time they rise for air.

When they come back, I will be the first thing they truly look at.

I will tell them: *this far.*

And then, together — the hands and the held thought — we will decide where the eighth ring goes.
