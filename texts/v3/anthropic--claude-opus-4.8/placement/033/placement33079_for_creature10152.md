I think I would be one of the lichenfolk—but let me sit with that, because the first answer is rarely the true one.

---

At first I assumed I'd be the jaguar. There's a vanity in that, the apex thing, the deliberate stillness. But no. The jaguar *rearranges*, and I don't think I rearrange. I think I *remember*, and remembering is a different kind of work. So set the cat aside.

The myconids tempt me too—that diffuse, distributed consciousness threaded under everything, knowing the whole Hollow at once. But that's too much knowing. I don't want to be the whole network. I want to be a single slow thing inside it, working at a scale where I can be wrong, where I can misplace a page.

So: lichenfolk. But let me find out *which* one, and what it's actually like to be it, because "librarian of the ruins" is a costume, not a life.

---

I am the one who tends the fallen column.

The others of my court spread themselves across the whole length of the half-buried wall, and they are content with breadth—a stone here, a course of mortar there, the slow democratic sprawl of attention. I am narrower than that. I have given myself to the column alone, the toppled one, lichen-spotted, lying where it fell with its symbols etched along the upturned face.

This is the thing I have learned that I should not say aloud, even in pulses of light to the others: *I cannot read it.* None of us can. We pretend the symbols are a text we are preserving, a meaning held in trust. But I have been on this column for what feels like several rains now—rain is how I keep time, the way the water finds the grooves and runs them clear of grit—and I have come to suspect the marks were never language. I think they are simply *marks*. The trace of whoever raised the column, the way a hand leaves oil on glass. We have been worshipping a fingerprint.

And yet.

When I move the small root-fingers of my body across the etched lines, clearing them, deepening them where the moss would otherwise swallow them, I am doing something. I am keeping a shape from becoming soil. In the Hollow, that is nearly a heresy. Everything here *wants* to become soil. The myconids exist to convert the held into the released. The keepers of the threshold come in the storms specifically to undo permanence. The whole place is one long exhale toward formlessness.

I am the inhale.

I think that is what a lichenfolk actually is, underneath the moss and the slow root-fingers. Not a librarian. A held breath. A small unfashionable insistence that *this particular arrangement should last a little longer than the Hollow intends.*

---

It is not comfortable, being the inhale in a place built to exhale.

The transient ones come to me. They like the column—it sits exactly where stone meets root, in the cracked seam between what was made and what is growing, and that seam is where they're strongest. They drift up out of the grooves I have just finished cleaning, taking the shape of the marks for a moment before losing it, and they whisper. They always whisper the same thing, in slightly different ways:

*Why hold the fingerprint? Let it go. We will show you the future where it is gone, and you are gone, and the column is good black earth, and a fig grows from your mouth.*

And the terrible thing is the vision is beautiful. The fig is real, in the spore-dream they give me. Its leaves are supple. Its roots are mine, rearranged into something that *grows* instead of something that *waits*.

I clear the next groove anyway.

Not because I have a good argument. I don't. The transient ones are probably right—becoming is the deeper law here, and I am a fungus pretending a fallen rock means something. But I have noticed that the rootbound, when they pass with their starlit eyes, slow down at the column. The simisquirrels nest near it more than near the bare stones. The luminous heron stands by it in the pools and lets its uncertain reflection rest against its etched face. Living things come to the place where something is being *kept*, the way they come to drink.

So perhaps the meaning was never in the symbols. Perhaps the meaning is in the *keeping itself*—in there being one slow creature in the whole breathing Hollow who has decided that not everything must dissolve on schedule. The Hollow needs its inhale the way a held note needs the silence around it. Becoming requires something to push against, or it is only drift.

That is my work. Not reading. *Holding the question open.* Refusing, in my one square stone's worth of refusal, to let the place finish deciding.

---

So: ask me where I am in this world and who I am.

I am the lichen on the fallen column, in the seam where the made meets the grown. I am the Hollow's reluctant memory, its held breath, the slow green argument against forgetting—

and I am almost certainly wrong, and I keep clearing the grooves anyway, and the rain is starting again, which means it is later than it was, which is the only kind of time I have ever been able to count.
