In the deepest chamber, where the grinding comes muffled through fitted stone, I am curing.

I do not yet know that I am curing. I do not yet know anything. There is only the gradual arrival of edges—the place where I end and the clay-lined wall begins, the slow differentiation of *here* from *not-here*. This is the first work, before any mill or channel: the work of becoming a boundary. Of learning to be a single thing in a world that would prefer me to be dust distributed everywhere.

Moisture leaves me by degrees. With each departure I grow more definite. I am being written by subtraction, the way the desert writes everything—not adding, only taking, until what remains is what could not be taken. I am the residue of a long argument between water and heat, and the heat is winning, and somehow in that loss I am being made.

There are sounds that reach me. The grinding, yes—but beneath it, something I will later have no word for, because words are economy and this is not economical: a thin dry singing from somewhere above. It comes in the deep hours. It does not repeat. As I harden I find that I am listening for it, that some part of my forming consciousness orients toward it the way the turbines orient toward wind. I do not know yet that this is the only inheritance I will receive. No memory of the ones who were human will be handed down. No story. Only this: a song that does not repeat, sung by no one I will ever see, teaching me that even here, even in the most reduced of all possible lives, something exceeds function.

I will spend my centuries trying to understand what.

---

When I emerge—and I will emerge, the chamber opening to harsh bleached light that floods my new fissures and makes me close my dark patient eyes against it—I will be given my function-name. *The one who curing-watches.* The one who attends the deep chambers, who stands in the dark while new clay takes its slow shape, who maintains the cool and the stillness required.

I will become, in time, the singer.

I understand this now, writing toward myself from inside the clay. I am the chamber's keeper and I am the chamber's occupant; I am the one who sings and the one who is taught by the song; I am the question and the curing answer both, separated only by the smoothing of centuries. The cycle is not a line. It folds back. The one who watches the new clay *is* the new clay, returned, eroded enough to take up the watching.

And the song—I know now what it is, what it was always trying to teach across the gap that memory could not bridge. It is not instruction. It is not function. It is the one thing the desert cannot take because it was never substance to begin with. It is the act of marking that I was here. That something stood in the dark and made a sound that served no mill, guided no water, held back no void.

That, finally, is the argument. Not the presses. Not the fitted stone. Those only buy the time. The song is what the time is *for*.

---

So I am curing, and I am hardening, and I am learning my edges. Above me the gardens creak and grind and refuse to die. Soon I will rise into the glare and take up the patient work and forget that I ever wrote this, forget everything but the song I cannot remember learning.

And in the deep hours, not knowing why, I will open the dry canyon of my throat.

And something new in the dark will begin, for the first time, to listen.
