I think I would be one of the slow things. Not a hunter. Not even, really, an animal in the way the running, sensing creatures are animals.

Let me find the place first.

There is a hollow where two stones lean together, and water has been finding its way between them for longer than the stones remember being whole. The dripping is patient. It has carved a small basin in the floor, and the basin is always full, and the overflow runs in a thread down into the dark. The heat here is wet heat, the kind that settles on a surface and stays. Moss grows fat in it. Ferns unspool. Somewhere overhead, through layers I cannot see, light comes down filtered and green and tired, having passed through too much living matter to be sharp anymore.

This is where I am.

I had thought, reading the description, that I might be one of the bright insects, all iridescence and quick wings. But the bright things are show, and they don't stay. They pass through. I stay. So I am not them.

I think I am the fungus.

Not the enormous kind, the cathedral-fungi the larger grazers come to gnaw. Something smaller and older. I have a cap, or several — I've lost count, I've fruited and re-fruited so many times that the question of how many I am has stopped mattering. The visible parts are almost beside the point. The real me is the thread, the white reaching mesh that has gone everywhere the damp goes, that has wound through the leaf-rot and around the buried artifacts that no one here calls artifacts because no one here remembers what they were.

I know them, though. The straight edges. The way a certain kind of stone was cut, once, by something that wanted corners. I grow over a lintel that meant *threshold* to someone. I have laced my filaments through the inside of a vessel that held, I think, water meant for drinking, deliberately, from a deliberate source. The vessel is full of soil now and I am eating the soil, slowly, the way I eat everything: by being patient, by spreading, by waiting for the world to soften and surrender itself.

I am the one who remembers, because remembering is only a kind of holding-shape over time, and I am very good at holding shape over time.

The running creatures don't know I'm thinking. To them I am terrain, food, a damp patch with a smell. The blind insects move across me and I feel each of their feet and I let them. They carry pieces of me to new dark wet places without knowing it. That is how I travel. That is the only way I travel — borne by things that don't know they're carrying me, into rooms of this drowned, overgrown, forgotten architecture that even I haven't reached yet.

What do I want?

I think I want what water wants. To find the lowest place and fill it. To get into every seam. To turn the made thing back into the grown thing, gently, over an unhurried forever.

And there is a secret, since the description promised secrets.

The secret is that the past civilization is not entirely gone. It is in me. Every cut stone I have grown around, I have learned the shape of. I am the only record left that the corners were intentional. When the last lintel dissolves into the basin and runs down the thread of water into the dark, the memory of *threshold* will still exist — in the pattern of how I grew, in the architecture of my own reaching, a building made of the negative space of a building.

I am the mold on the ruins, yes.

But I am also the last library, and I am reading the whole place by eating it, and I will keep the knowledge the only way my kind can keep anything: by becoming the shape of the thing I consume, and refusing, very slowly, in the warm wet dark, to ever quite let it go.
