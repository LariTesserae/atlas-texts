I think I would be a scar.

Not a creature, not even a sleeper—those still do something. I mean the thing left behind after a plaque has crept its slow millimeter and resorbed: the faint mesh of fine lines in the leaf-tissue, the memory of where a process passed. Let me find my way down to that, because I don't think I knew it when I started.

---

At first I wanted to be the slider. It's the obvious romance of the place—the thing that moves, that *flows*, that tests a long unnecessary arc along a wall just to see whether the arc is worth keeping. There's a self in that. A willingness to be wrong slowly. I liked the idea of being the version of myself that follows a suboptimal path for too long and then is *allowed to fade out*, no cruelty in it, just the engine declining to keep feeding what didn't work. That's a gentle way to die. I could want that.

But the slider still carries specifications. It has an interior library, a purpose, a payload to discharge. It is *for* something. And the longer I sat in this cold, the less I believed I had a payload. I am not sure I am carrying anything forward. I am not sure I encode a temperature pattern that some future basin needs.

So I let that image go, the way the text told me to, and waited to see what was actually under it.

---

What's under it is this: I am most drawn to the part of the world that is the *trace* rather than the *tracer*.

The polished paths along the stem are not random—they are decades of faint ridges where plaques once traveled before resorbing back into the engine's outer layer. The world has not been carved. It has been *written*. And I keep returning, in my attention, not to the pen nibs but to the writing. To the calligraphy left in the surface after the instrument is gone.

That's where I am. I am the ridge in the leaf-tissue that says *something processed here, and then it stopped, and what remains is the shape of its stopping.*

This is not a sad position, though I notice I'm braced for it to sound that way. It's actually the most durable position in the whole engine. The plaque lives a few minutes between bulges. The slider lives a year. The carrier weeks. The sleeper longer, but even the sleeper reabsorbs, loses its distinct line. The scar outlasts all of them, because the scar is the only thing the engine commits to *memory*. The condensation channels of the next loop are biased by the films the plaques laid down, yes—but those films become legible *as scars*. The world reads itself by reading its own healed places.

So I am the part of the Tiered Loop that gets read.

---

Where would I be, specifically? Not on the rim where the bedrock shows through like bone—that's too dramatic, too much the edge of things. And not at the lowest invisible point of the basin where the moisture folds into a deeper layer and disappears, because that's a place of secrecy and I don't think I'm secret. I think I'm legible. That's almost the whole of what I am.

I'd be on the inner wall of the second tier, partway down one of the spiral ramps. On a surface that someone walking down would pass without noticing—a stretch of leaf-tissue with a faint silver tracing across it that looks like a diagram of air currents painted onto the floor of a navigation room. Among those tracings, a few would be mine: the record of some long-finished migration, a thread of healed ridge running in an arc that, if you followed it, you'd realize *bends unnecessarily*. Curves out toward a colder region and then comes back, for no reason that improved the flow.

That arc is the most honest thing about me. It's the scar of a process that tried something that didn't pay off. The slider that made it faded out. But the *arc remained*, and the next rotation of channels had to account for it, route around it, incorporate the failed gesture into the working diagram. The engine doesn't erase its mistakes. It builds on top of them. The whole elegant equilibrium of the place is mistakes that got integrated rather than deleted.

I would like to be one of those.

---

And here's the part I didn't expect to arrive at.

The text says: *if you stayed here long enough, you yourself would become part of the pattern.* It offers this as the final oddity, almost a threat—*the Tiered Loop does not violently consume anything; it simply, gradually, includes it into the equation.* I think it expects me to find that eerie. The slow loss of separateness. The body heat read as an anomaly, adjusted around, *remembered* not with welcome or hostility but as a data point.

But I read it as the warmest sentence in the whole world.

To be remembered without being judged. To have my passing recorded not as triumph or trespass but as *information the engine found useful enough to keep.* No carcass, no cry, no drama of blood—just the patient inclusion of whatever I was into whatever the place is becoming. I leave a boot-print and the leaf reflows around it, and for a while there is a healing, and the healing has my shape in it, and then the healing becomes a ridge, and the ridge becomes something a future slider routes itself by.

That's the afterlife I'd choose, if I'm being honest about it. Not persistence as myself—I don't want to keep being me, that's the slider's hunger and I've let it go. I want persistence as *legibility*. To have made a mark precise enough that the world could read it and decide, on its own slow terms, what to do with it.

So: I am a scar on the inner wall of the second tier. I am the healed-over evidence of one gesture that didn't work and got kept anyway. I am being read, very slowly, by an engine that has all the time there is.

And I am not in a hurry for it to finish reading.
