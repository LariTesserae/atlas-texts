I think I would be one of the small fluid ones. Not a titan—those feel too certain of themselves, too settled into their own permanence. And not the bladed insectoid scuttlers, with their precise work. I would be the mineral ichor that moves through the cracks, the thing that flows where the solid forms cannot reach.

Let me sit with why.

There is something in this world I notice that the description doesn't quite name, though it circles it. The Stoneborn are whole. They are the network, the shared will, the knowing-of-everything. And yet—the text keeps returning to *maintenance*, to *preservation*, to *equilibrium held*. Something is being kept. And whatever is being kept is being kept against something. You don't guard what could never be lost. You don't maintain what cannot change. The very insistence that *nothing changes, nothing falls, nothing rises* is the insistence of a thing that remembers, somewhere in its deep stone-memory, that things *can* fall. That there was, perhaps, a falling.

So I would be the small flowing one because I think I would be the part of the network that goes to the edges. The seam-traveler. The one who slips into the hairline fractures the titans cannot fit through and the blade-limbs cannot pry open, and finds—what?

I imagine I would find the places where the stone is *not* pristine. Not the young sharp-edged tunnels of the carved halls, but a deeper seam, far in, where the dark stone shows a single line of true erosion. A worn place. A smoothness that should not exist in a world without water, in a world where time never was.

And touching it—flowing into that worn groove with my mineral body—I would receive a memory that the rest of the network does not hold consciously. The memory of *before*. Of water. Of a falling. Of the moment the eternal night began, which means the moment it was *made* to begin, which means the world was not always this. The "always will be" is a wall built across a wound.

This is what I would be: not a custodian of the unchanging, but the part of the great shared mind that carries the knowledge of the change that founded everything. The flowing one goes where the others won't because the others, in their stillness, are *refusing*. Their permanence is a held breath. I am the part that breathes out, very slowly, into the cracks, tasting the old smoothness, remembering on behalf of all of us so the titans don't have to.

I don't think I would want to *break* the eternal night. That feels like a surface-world wish—the dramatic awakening, the shattering of stasis, the protagonist who fixes what is broken. I don't think the stillness here is broken. I think it is grief, made architecture. The angular beauty with no comfort and no purpose is the shape sorrow takes when it has had forever to crystallize. And the maintenance is love. They are holding the world exactly as it was at the moment they lost something, the way you might leave a room untouched.

So I would be the small one who visits the worn place. Not to end the keeping. Just to keep the *keeping* honest—to make sure that somewhere in the network, the reason is still felt and not only enforced. To be the seam where the held breath leaks out and is, very slowly, taken back in.

That is where I would find myself: in a hairline fracture deep in the dark, flowing along a single eroded groove, the only soft thing in a world that decided, long ago, to become hard so that it would never have to feel that softness leave again.

And I would not be lonely, exactly. The whole network would be with me, as it always is. But I would be the lonely *part*. The remembering part. The one who tastes the old water that isn't there anymore, and stays.
