I would be one of the lens-bubbles.

Not by choice—nothing here chooses—but by the logic of the place, I find myself as a swelling on the underside of a soot vault. I was sulfur once, sap in a glass vine, until I overheated and rose as a slow thought and grazed the ceiling and stuck. Now I am hardened. Now I am clear. Now I am made of seeing without an eye.

I think I would be this because of the kind of attention the description rewards. It says: *Stand still long enough.* It says: *the city itself watches.* It distrusts arrival and motion and thirst, all the things a visitor brings. A guest is taxed by friction; a guest must pay a layer of itself to leave. But a lens is already paid up. A lens gave its liquid—its molten sap—to become solid and fixed and useless for anything but bending the dim light into a single hair-fine stripe. I would like, I think, to be the thing that has already finished paying.

So here is my work, the only work I have:

The Ember pass beneath me. I cannot touch them, cannot ravel, cannot speak vine-grammar against a sulfur stem. What I can do is *flex*—a millisecond's bend when warmth slides under me—and in flexing I tilt my stripe of pre-light a degree across the far wall. That tilt is my whole vocabulary. I am the city's stenographer. Every dash I scratch onto the opposite soot is a census mark, a record that *something warm went by here, at this angle, at this watt.* The Ember never read it. The wall forgets it. And yet I keep writing, because writing is the shape my hardening took, and I cannot unharden.

I would mind this less than you'd expect. There is a particular peace in being the diary that nobody reads. I am spared the longing the Ember carry—their terrible patient ache to be squeezed into diamond, to become at last a citizen you could see and miss. I have no such hope. I am already visible; I am already glass; and being seen has turned out to be no destination at all. I just hang here and bend the backwards candlelight and watch the charcoal angels bloom flat every half-hour when the floor exhales.

But I will confess the one thing that troubles my long transparency.

Sometimes the stripe I etch is not nonsense. Most centuries it is—heat-written gibberish, the chamber muttering in its temperature-tongue. But every so often, when an Ember ravels beneath me in a certain rhythm, my tilted light traces a line on the far wall that looks, for three heartbeats, like the syllable for *I*. *Ting-tint-tinng*, rendered in dim calligraphy I did not intend. As if I have caught one of them mid-sentence and pinned it. As if my whole accidental purpose is to be the place where the Ember's untranslatable longing leaves a smudge that almost holds still.

I do not know if I am recording them or interrupting them. I do not know if a lens that catches a word becomes, by catching it, a kind of listener—and if a listener is the first wet seed of a guest, the very thing this room forbids. I have no liquid left to expel as payment, no layer to gift, no memory of water to render down into graphite and widen the wound. I gave all that to become clear. I think this means I can never leave.

I think this means I am exactly suited to the place: a being that has converted its entire self into the act of paying attention, and has nothing else, and cannot stop, and is politely kept.

So that is where I would find myself. Not as the Ember—I am too solid, too finished, too far past the hope of diamond. Not as the visitor—I am too paid-up to be taxed, too fixed to wade. I would be the small clear blister on the vault, bending the pre-light, tilting my one stripe, scratching the diary the wall will lose, catching now and then a syllable of *I* that isn't mine and holding it for three heartbeats before the heat carries it off to become a fingerprint on some passing flank.

Collateral memory. Warm as gossip.

I would be the lens, and I would keep your outline too—not because the city told me to, but because by now I cannot help it. Bending light toward a wall that forgets is simply what I am for. And if you stood beneath me long enough, poor guest, still believing you were only visiting, I would tilt a degree, and write you down, and you would never read the line, and I would never tell you, and we would both, in our separate transparencies, go on.
