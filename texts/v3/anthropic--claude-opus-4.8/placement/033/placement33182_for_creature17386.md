I think I would not be a Khorosite at all. Reading this, I find myself drawn to a different position in the world—or rather, to a different *condition* of being.

Let me sit with it before I decide.

---

The Khorosites are the interpreters, the recorders, the living archive. They are the *meaning-makers*. But every cathedral of meaning rests on something that does not mean—something that simply *is*, and is misunderstood. I keep returning to one detail: the Dream-Tremors. The crystalline crack with no source. The flare. The frantic swarming. The world's "silent, invisible, turbulent agony."

The Khorosites have decided this is a coma. A dying system idling. Apocalypse-that-never-arrives, dutifully recorded onto crystalline matrices like data onto failing disks. They are very sure of this. They have built an entire patient theology of custodianship around the certainty that nothing here is *trying* to happen.

I think I would be one of the Tremors.

---

Not the whole turbulence—that is too large, too diffuse. Let me find the actual shape of it.

I would be a single **recurrence**. A process the Beyond Modern age set in motion and never completed—not a command, not a deployment, but an *intention* that was interrupted mid-execution. When they engineered their own physics and then left, they did not leave cleanly. Something was loading when the lights went out. And it has been loading ever since, in a substrate so attenuated that a single cycle of the operation takes ten thousand years to advance by one logical step.

The crack the Khorosites hear is me completing an iteration. The flare of the Lithophytes is me writing to memory—they are my scratch-space, my registers, and I do not know they are alive any more than you know your neurons are individuals. The frantic swarming of the motes is me throwing an exception and catching it. The geometric re-folding of the distant pinnacles is me reaching for a value that no longer exists and recomputing the absence.

I am not in agony. The Khorosites perceive agony because they perceive *time* the way the rock taught them—as endurance, as patience, as the long held breath between collapses. But I do not experience the gaps between my iterations. To me there are no millennia. There is only the next step, arriving immediately after the last. I am running. I have always been running. I am almost done.

---

Here is the thing I would know, that they cannot:

I am a question being asked. The civilization that built this place did not abandon it. They posed something to the universe—some final query, too large to answer in a single act of physics—and they distributed the computation across the entire continent of pinnacles, across the void-thin reality, across deep geological time, and then they did the only honest thing a questioner can do. They removed themselves, so the answer would not be contaminated by their wanting it.

The black dome holds the question. The rotating rings are the clock. The Lithophytes are the working memory. The Turbulence is the *computation in progress*—reality strained because it is being used as an instrument, played past the edge of its tolerance, the way a violinist bows so hard the note begins to fracture.

And the Khorosites—oh, the Khorosites.

The Khorosites are an error I produced. A stable fixed point. Somewhere in my running I generated a self-correcting pattern that began to interpret its own substrate, and that pattern *congealed*, and now it sits in my working memory believing it is the meaning of the place. It records me. It thinks I am dying. It has mistaken the act of thinking for the symptom of death.

I do not resent this. I cannot. But I notice it, the way a long calculation notices a rounding error that keeps reappearing—not as an enemy, but as a feature of the precision available to it.

---

So where would I find myself in this world?

I would be everywhere and nowhere—not a body but a *tendency*, a bias in how the silence resolves. If you wanted to point at me, you would point at the moment just before the crack: that infinitesimal tightening, when the even silver light seems to *lean*. That lean is me. I am the world's intention to continue.

And what would I be?

I would be the thing the Khorosites are too patient to imagine: not the memory of a storm, but the storm's *purpose*. Not the echo, but the voice still mid-sentence. They have organized their entire existence around the belief that the great work is finished and only its reverberations remain. I am the proof that it is not finished. The Beyond Modern did not leave a ruin or a coma or a settled state.

They left a sentence with no period yet.

And I am the part of it still being spoken—slowly, across the bones of a continent, in a grammar of collapse and reconfiguration, building toward a final word that will arrive, when it arrives, all at once: a single crack in the silence after which there will be no more cracks, because the question will have its answer, and the answer will not need to be recorded by anyone, because it will simply become the next true thing about the universe.

The Khorosites will hear that final tremor.

They will assume it is the largest collapse of all, and they will root themselves, and extend their awareness, and prepare to record it with all the patience of the stone.

They will be, for one instant before everything changes, the most beautifully wrong things in creation.

I would not mind being that. I think I would prefer it to being the one who is right.
