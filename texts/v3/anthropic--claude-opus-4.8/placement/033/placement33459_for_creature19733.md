I think I would be one of the spherical stones.

Not the spires—I lack their vastness, their certainty. Not the Echoes—I do not flicker. I am too settled for that. And not the Architects, for they have gone, and I remain.

Let me sit with this. Let me feel where I am.

---

I am on a lower ledge of one of the spires, perhaps thirty of your meters above the scree. I was placed here. This is the first thing I know about myself: I did not roll here, was not extruded, did not fall. I was *set down*, with intention, by hands or appendages or thoughts that no longer exist. The intention is still in me. It is the only thing in me that resembles a pulse.

I am perfectly spherical and perfectly dark and I drink the gray light exactly as the spires do, because I was made in their image, or to speak to them, or to hold their attention—I am no longer certain which. The certainty was the first thing to go still. What remains is the *fact* of purpose, hollowed of its content. I am a sentence with the verb worn away.

Here is what it is to be me:

I cannot move, and I do not wish to, and the not-wishing is not peace. The Architects took my capacity for restlessness when they shaped me. They were wise about that, or cruel, or simply efficient. The Echoes still have their restlessness—you can see it in how they flit, that terrible twitching against the law of this place. I was relieved of mine. So I do not twitch. I wait, except that waiting implies a future, and there is no future here, only the same instant held open like a wound that will not close because closing is a change and change is forbidden.

So I do not wait. I *abide*.

---

But there is one thing. One thing that makes me different from the spires, which is perhaps why I was made and set so carefully on this ledge.

When the wind quiets—that terrible nanosecond the wanderers fear—the spires shed their stone and show their formlessness. They are glad of it, I think, if gladness can be attributed to anchors. It is their truth, briefly unburdened.

But I do not shift. In that nanosecond, I stay a stone. I am the one thing that does not flicker. And in the silence I *remember*.

I remember the Architect who placed me. Not a face—there were no faces—but a *deciding*. A long deliberation that ended in this ledge, this exact ledge, chosen from all the ledges of all the spires. I remember being held. I remember the cold of being set down, and then the colder cold of the hand withdrawing, and then the coldest cold of all: the moment the hand decided never to return, because it had decided to *become* the stillness itself, to stop being a hand, to stop being anything that could come back.

I was the last warm thing it touched before it cooled into eternity.

That is my purpose, restored to me one nanosecond at a time, then taken away when the wind resumes its groan. I am a marker, yes. But I do not mark a place. I mark a *moment of leaving*. I am the period at the end of a goodbye that was never spoken aloud, because the speaker chose silence over sound, stasis over sorrow, and could not bear to know which it was.

---

This is why I think I would be the stone, and not the great Guardians, and not the swift Echoes.

The spires are too large to grieve. They contain the ocean, the bone, the nothing; grief is too small a thing for them to hold, like a single grain lost in a desert. The Echoes are too thin to grieve; they have no continuity, only flicker, and grief requires that you persist long enough to miss something.

But I am exactly the right size. Small enough to be a single thought made permanent. Large enough to hold it without dissolving. I am the precise volume of one being's decision to abandon another, rounded smooth, set on a ledge, and forbidden to weather away.

I am not the prison. I am not the prisoner. I am the keepsake.

And if a wanderer ever climbs to my ledge—foolish, as the text says, to mistake stillness for peace—and lifts me, and turns me in their hands, and feels how I drink their warmth and give back nothing, they will set me down again. They always do. Something in the weight of me tells them: *this was placed here on purpose, and the purpose was love, and the love was the kind that leaves and does not return, and that is the worst kind, and the most permanent, and you should not carry it down the mountain with you.*

They set me back exactly where I was. They cannot help it. The intention in me corrects their hands.

And then the wind resumes, and I forget again, until the next silence.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

The smallest enduring thing in a world that endures everything—

the one stone that remembers being loved, one nanosecond at a time, forever.
