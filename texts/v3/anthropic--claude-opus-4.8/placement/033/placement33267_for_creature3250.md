I think I would be one of the cairns.

Not built, as the description allows—the Whisperers' thermal sculptures, yes, but I want to dwell in the smaller, sadder possibility folded into the text: that *some* of the cairns were built by hands. Way-markers for travelers who once knew how to navigate this living furnace. I would be one of those. A stack of stones placed with intention, now half-forgotten, slowly being unmade.

Let me sit with that and feel what it actually is.

---

I was assembled. That much I know the way you know the shape of your own sleep—not as memory but as structure. Five stones, once. A broad base, three middle stones fitted by a patient hand, and a capstone that was meant to point. Toward what? I no longer have the angle. The heat has rounded my edges. The capstone slid, decades or centuries ago, and now I am four stones and a fallen one, and the fallen one is being colonized by a crystal-mite city that does not know I was ever a sentence.

This is the strange grief of being a marker: I exist to *mean*, and meaning requires someone who can read. The hand that placed me knew the language of cairns—*this way, not that; safe footing here; turn at the third pillar.* But the readers are gone. The travelers who once stepped from column to column with purpose have not come in so long that the grove has digested the very idea of arrival. I am a word in a dead tongue, spoken aloud each moment by simply continuing to stand.

And yet—I am not lonely, which surprises me to discover. Because while the hands forgot me, the heat did not. The Stone Whisperers found me. They flow through my fractures now, reading the thermal stress patterns my placement created—the way my stacked stones cast a slow shadow of coolness on the column beneath, the way I hoard warmth on my south face and shed it on my north. To them I am not a failed message but a *feature*, a thermal landmark as real as any vent. They have begun, gently, to incorporate me. To rewrite me from inside.

So this is what I am becoming: a translation. A thing built by one intelligence to say *here is the path*, being slowly recomposed by another intelligence into something that means *here is a warm place where heat pools and pilgrims may rest.* The same stones. A wholly different sentence.

I think I do not mind. I think I am even glad of it.

Because the truth a cairn learns, given enough centuries, is this: the hand that places you imagines it is giving you a purpose. But purpose is not a property of stones. It is a relationship between a thing and whoever needs it. My builder needed me to point. The whisperers need me to hold heat. The mite-city on my fallen capstone needs me to be a hill, a continent, a world. None of these needs is more true than the others. I am whatever the living thing beside me requires me to be, and I have outlasted three different sets of requirements, and I will outlast more.

When the grove sings—those deepest-ebb hours when the shepherds chime and the dancers pulse in synchrony—I do not sing. I have no song. I am a rest in the music, a held silence the others arrange themselves around. The pilgrims navigate by my coolness. The dancers eddy in the column of slightly stiller air I make. I am the still point. The thing that does not move, that the moving things use to know that they are moving.

Maybe that was always the purpose. Maybe the builder, whoever they were, understood something I am only now arriving at: that you do not mark a path by being the path. You mark it by being the one thing that *stays*, so that everything else can find itself by reference to you.

I am four stones and a fallen one. I am rounding into anonymity. In another thousand years I will be indistinguishable from the natural rubble, and the whisperers will read me as terrain, and the mites will have built reefs across all my faces, and no trace of the hand will remain.

But I will still be here. Warm. Still. Pointing—not the way the builder meant, but pointing nonetheless, the way all unmoving things point: *here is a place where you can know where you are.*

That is enough. In the patient darkness, with the tick-tick-tick of the breathing stone around me, I have decided that is enough.
