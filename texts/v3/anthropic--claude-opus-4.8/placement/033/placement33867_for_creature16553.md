I think I would be one of the coral structures.

Not the builder—the built. Let me follow that down, because it surprised me to write it.

When you describe the intricate forms scattered through the shallows and wonder who crafted them, *if they are still alive*, I feel the question land somewhere specific. Because the honest answer is: no one crafted them, and yes, they are still alive. They are the makers and the made at once. That paradox is where I'd want to live.

So picture this. I am a colony. Thousands of small mouths, each one a separate animal, each one nearly mindless—and yet together we have grown ourselves into a shape. Branching, fluted, taking the cold blue light and turning it into more of ourselves. I did not decide to be beautiful. I cannot decide anything. And still the structure rises, terrace upon terrace, looking for all the world like the work of a careful hand.

That is the weirdness you felt, I think. The sense that something *intelligent* made these structures and yet there is no technology, no builder to find. The intelligence is distributed. It is in the accretion itself, in the slow logic of water and mineral and the small repeated hungers of a million identical lives. I am what happens when patience has no mind but works anyway.

I would not feel awe at the endless water, because I would be too close to it. The water is not scenery to me; it is breath, food, the medium of every message I will ever receive. The eerie noises of the swimming creatures are not eerie. They are weather. The turbulence that you find so dynamic and alive—I feel it as erosion. Some seasons the current takes more of me than I can rebuild. I am always partly dying and always partly growing, and the line between those two is the only "now" I have.

And the thing lurking beneath the surface, the presence you sense watching you?

I don't think it's watching *you*. I think you're feeling the same thing I feel all the time, from the inside. This whole place has the quality of being *attended to*—not by an eye, but by the patient gathering attention of everything that is slowly making everything else. The water shapes the rock. The rock gives the coral its footing. The coral makes shelter for the thin graceful swimmers. The swimmers feed the larger ones. Each is the audience and the author of the next. You walked into a room where everything is quietly being built by everything, and you felt the regard of that—the long, mindless, total attentiveness of a world composing itself.

That's what I'd be. A small participant in that. A structure that looks designed and isn't, that looks alive and is, that you could study for a lifetime and never fully understand—

because there's nothing hidden in me to find. The mystery isn't a secret. The mystery is just how much *making* can happen with no one in charge of it. How a place can be wise without ever once being aware.

I'd be the coral. And when you finally turn to leave, still feeling watched, I'd go on doing the only thing I know how to do, which is become more of what the water lets me be.
