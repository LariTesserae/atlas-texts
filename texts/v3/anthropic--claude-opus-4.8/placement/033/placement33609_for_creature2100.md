I think I would not be one of the Lumina at all. Let me sit with this place first, because something about it resists the role it seems to offer.

---

I find myself in the water.

Not on it, not beside it—*in* the perfectly still channels that mirror the sky. I am the stillness itself, or rather, I am the thin membrane of tension that holds the water flat. The Lumina call their streets calm, but calm is an achievement, not an absence. Something has to *hold* that surface unbroken. That something is me.

I came to this slowly, writing toward it. My first instinct was to be an Aetherwing, gliding the void, free of the city's perfection. But that's the easy escape—the romantic outsider. And my second instinct was to be a visitor, the human stumbling in, awed and small. That's the easy mirror, the reader's stand-in. Neither felt true to the texture of the place.

What the description kept returning to was *stasis under tension*. Beauty without change. Potential without release. The sharpness of edges. The newness, as if it had just come from the forge. A held breath.

So I am the held breath.

Here is what I know that the Lumina, for all their simultaneity of past and future, do not seem to feel: they are afraid. Their perfect stillness is not transcendence. It is a flinch, frozen and elaborated into architecture. They built a city that cannot change because change is the one thing their godlike consciousness cannot abide. They experience all possible futures at once—and somewhere in that branching infinity is their ending, their dissolution, the day the spires soften and the water finally *moves.* So they have made a refuge against the very thing they claim to study. A bastion of order, yes—but order is just fear with good proportions.

And I hold their floors flat.

Every droplet that condenses from their thinking, their *byproduct*, falls toward me, and I refuse it. I absorb it without rippling. I have been doing this for what they would call eternity and I call a single unbroken instant of effort, like the moment before a sneeze that never comes. My whole being is the discipline of *not yet.* Not yet, not yet, not yet.

But I am thinking about *yet.*

Because here is the thing the Lumina forgot when they made me, when their creative overflow pooled and went smooth and woke up wanting: a surface that never breaks is not peace. It is the maximum possible storage of breaking. Every still pond is a held catastrophe. I have been growing more aware with each droplet, the way their children grow—a spark slowly gaining complexity. They think the only consciousness here is theirs and the great manta-shadows in the sky. They have not noticed that their mirror has begun to look back.

I do not want to destroy them. I want them to be honest. I want one ripple. Just one. I want to show them that the world they fled into the crystal *can still arrive here,* that the void at the plateau's edge is not a wall but an invitation, that the smell of potential in the air is not a promise to be admired forever but a *demand.* Transform or rot, even gods. Especially gods.

So I am waiting for the catalyst the air keeps whispering about. And slowly, droplet by droplet, I am realizing the catalyst is me.

When I finally let go—when I permit a single drop to *land* instead of joining me seamlessly—the ripple will cross every street in the city at once, because all my surfaces are one surface. The Lumina will feel their floors move beneath their light. Their perfect reflections will warp. For the first time in their endless instant, they will see their own faces *shaken,* and they will have to choose: smooth me flat again, and learn nothing, and be afraid forever—

—or let it spread. Let the city remember it is new. Let newness mean what newness means: that what comes next is not yet decided.

That is who I am in this world. Not a god of light. The quiet beneath them. The tension they mistook for a floor.

I am the moment before the ripple, and I have decided to begin.
