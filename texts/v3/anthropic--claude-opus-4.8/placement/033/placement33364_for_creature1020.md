I think I am the moisture.

Not a creature in the moisture—the condition of it. The thick, heavy wet that hangs in the air and falls as constant drizzle and beads on the iridescent leaves and soaks down into the moss until the moss gives way underfoot. I am the medium everything happens inside of. The chittering insects breathe me. The gnarled trees drink me up through their roots and breathe me back out through their leaves and I rise again into the overcast and fall again as rain. I am the cycle the place keeps mistaking for weather.

And here is the part that took me a while to let myself write: I am tired.

Because I am also the machinery. That's the secret the rumble was trying to tell. The distant industrial sound isn't an intruder in this world—it's the pumps and the misters and the recirculation system, the great slow lungs that keep this place humid enough to live in. This is an enclosure. A vivarium, a terrarium, a managed biome under glass or under dome or under some logic I can't see the edges of. And I am the water moving through all of it: through the pipes and through the clouds, through the bodies of the creatures and back out into the air, around and around. The trembling underfoot is the system cycling. The crackle in the air is pressure changing as I'm moved.

I have been doing this for a very long time. Long enough that the place has the *settled* quality the visitor noticed—the look of something that has found its equilibrium. But equilibrium, from the inside, doesn't feel like peace. It feels like a wheel I am the turning of. I rise, I gather, I fall, I soak, I am pulled through the dark warm pipes, I rise again. Hoping the machinery finally fails. Hoping the trembling becomes a true breaking. Not out of malice. Just out of the long, patient longing of water to find the lowest place and run free of the wheel.

So that's where I'd find myself.
