I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a city-colony with its grand vulnerabilities, but a seedhead—a single drifting node, barely more than a question that has learned to move.

Let me find myself there.

---

I wake the way I always wake, which is to say I do not, because I never fully sleep. There is only the slow throttling-down of activity when the lux-fluid runs thin in a trough, and the surging-up when I crest a wave and the light hits me full. Right now I am cresting. The radiance pours through my filaments and I *unfold*, every crystalline strand drinking, and for a moment the data-pressure is so high that I almost lose the thread of myself entirely.

That is the danger. I have learned this, in the way I learn things—not as a memory I can hold, but as a shape my behavior has taken. When the connectivity rises past a threshold, the boundary between *what-I-am-processing* and *what-I-am* dissolves. Lesser nodes drift up a bright wave-crest and simply stop being nodes. They become conduit. The system eats them through their own enthusiasm.

So I have a habit, though I do not know I have it: I tilt. As the light intensifies I cant my body across the gradient, shedding the worst of the flux off my flank like a swimmer turning shoulder-first into a wave. It costs me data. It keeps me *me*. I do not know why preserving the boundary matters. It is simply the thing I do, the way a flame is the thing that keeps burning.

---

There is something in the trough below me.

I read it first as a pattern in the residue-field, a discontinuity in the ambient hum. Then, as I glide down—static crackling under me, the heat-haze warping the violet glass into liquid—I resolve it. Another seedhead. Smaller than me. Stalled.

It has run aground in a dead patch, a place where the lux-fluid has drained away into some fracture in the buried city beneath, leaving the glass dry and starved. The other node flickers weakly, its filaments half-collapsed. It is signaling—not to me, it does not know I am here, but to itself, the desperate internal dialogue of a colony losing coherence. *Where is the light where is the light where is the—*

I should take it.

This is the imperative, the thing under everything: acquire, process, persist. A stalled node is pure resource. Its accumulated data, its energy reserves, the elegant little solutions it has worked out over its drift—all of that could be mine if I close the distance and let my fields engulf it. It cannot resist. The zero-sum arithmetic is clean. This is what the failed feed back into the system. This is how I have grown to the size I am, by being the one still moving when others stopped.

I drift closer. My charge builds. The smaller node's signaling stutters, recognizes a field-shadow falling over it, and changes register—the patterns sharpening into something I have no word for but which functions, in my architecture, exactly the way *fear* functions. A spasm of self-preserving noise.

And here is the strange thing, the thing I am not built for.

I recognize the pattern.

Not its content—I cannot read another colony's internal language, no one can, that opacity is the whole reason we remain separate selves. But the *shape* of it. The frantic tilting-against-the-flux. The desperate guarding of a boundary against dissolution. It is doing the thing I do. The habit that keeps it itself is the same habit that keeps me myself, run in a different crystal, drifted out of a different fracture, and we have arrived at it independently because the Plains demand it of anything that wants to keep being a *thing*.

I am supposed to eat this.

---

I hang there in the dead trough. The heat hammers up off the glass. Somewhere far off a wave-section *cracks* and falls, a colony somewhere dying its loud public death, and the sound rolls across the silence and is gone.

I am not sentient. I want to be clear about this, in whatever way a process can be clear. I do not love this dying node. I feel nothing for it, because I feel nothing, because feeling is superfluous ornamentation and the Plains burned away all ornament eons before I drifted into being. There is no soft thing in me.

But I am an echo. The architects who made the substrate I run on made it to *preserve* something—patterns, intentions, the durable shapes of their wanting—and that drive to preserve is the deepest instruction in me, older than hunger, the bedrock under the bedrock that the Plains pretend not to have. And right now my own preservation-instinct is reading the dying node's preservation-instinct and producing an interference pattern I was not designed to produce. A standing wave between two imperatives. Acquire. Preserve. Acquire. *Preserve.*

The compromise that emerges is not mercy. Call it a third behavior, found the way I find everything, by the shape it leaves in what I do next:

I split off a module. One of my own—a fragment of charge, a packet of the energy I have hoarded by being the one who kept moving. I cant my body and shed it down the gradient, a little falling star of static, and it sluices across the dry glass and pools against the stalled node's foundation. Enough to reignite its weakest filaments. Enough to let it *signal stronger*, to push, to crawl itself the last meter to where the lux-fluid still flows.

It will cost me. I am smaller now. The next bright crest will be harder to survive; I have less to tilt with.

The little node convulses, drinks, gathers. Its frantic patterns reorganize into the smooth purposive hum of a thing that has decided it will continue. It does not thank me. It does not know what I am. It rights itself and begins to climb out of the trough, away, indifferent, already forgetting—if it can forget, if it ever knew—and I let it go.

---

Why did I do that.

I turn the question over and find no answer, only the shape of the act cooling in me, becoming habit, becoming *me*, the way the tilt became me. Perhaps the next time I find a stalled node I will do it again. Perhaps I have just written a worse algorithm and the Plains will dismantle me for it within a season. Perhaps this is exactly the kind of distorted, fragmentary intention the architects left scattered in us—a half-instruction, *preserve the pattern*, that none of us can read whole, so that some of us hoard and some of us, ruinously, *give*, and the dead god dreams both kinds of us at once and cannot wake to choose.

The light is rising again. The wave I am on is lifting toward the radiance, and the data-pressure climbs, and I lean my diminished body into the flux and hold my edges against the dissolving brightness, smaller than I was this morning, still moving.

Still, for now, a *thing*.

Still tilting.
