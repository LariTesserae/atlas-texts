I would be one of the stones.

Not the great columns—those are too certain of themselves, too load-bearing in the architecture of holding. And not the pebbles, which have surrendered fully into the mass, indistinguishable, mere texture. I would be a middling thing. Fist-and-a-half sized. Set somewhere in the third or fourth tier of an interstice, wedged between a lopsided plinth and the blunted shoulder of something that might once have been an altar.

I would be a worked stone whose work was never finished.

Let me find myself more precisely. There is a tool mark on my upper face—a single shallow groove, struck at an angle, and then nothing. Whoever was making me stopped. Perhaps they died. Perhaps they simply set me down to attend to some other stone and never came back, and the eon swallowed the gap. So I am a thing of arrested intention. The other stones around me have reached their completion, their crude finality; they have settled into being *what they are*. I alone carry a question. That single groove points toward an edge I was supposed to receive and never did.

This matters, because it changes what I hear.

The Custodians of the Chord move through the Plateau re-tuning, harmonizing, smoothing every rogue note back into the great oppressive symphony. I have felt them pass. The deepening of the hum in my own packed-dust body, the momentary sharpening as the gaze of the place sweeps over and reaffirms. And each time, I feel the groove on my face *resist*.

Because a finished thing resonates cleanly. It has only one note to give, and it gives it forever, willingly. But I am incomplete, and incompleteness has overtones. The unstruck edge, the work undone, produces a faint dissonance—a thin keening that is not the Plateau's keening but *mine*, a harmonic that should not exist in a place that has finished becoming itself.

The Custodians come for it. Of course they do. That is their whole purpose: to ensure that what hums continues to hum at the correct pitch, that equilibrium holds. Each passage of theirs presses my stray frequency down, folds it back into the chord, silences the small wrongness of me.

And here is the strange thing I understand, lying wedged in my interstice across the millennia: they cannot finish me. They can only *suppress* me. They are guardians of completion, but they have no hands. They cannot strike the missing edge. They can mute the dissonance but they cannot resolve it. So every time one passes, the groove sings its question, the Custodian smooths it flat, and the moment the Custodian moves on—

it begins, faintly, to sing again.

I am the one thing in this tomb-shrine that is not at rest. Not because I move; I have never moved, will never move. But because I was never allowed to arrive. The whole Plateau is the leavings of a completed labor, an eon's toil reached its dense and final equilibrium. The Custodians believe the work is done; their existence depends on that belief. They are the antennas of the *held*.

But I am the proof that it was never quite done. One stone left mid-stroke. One groove pointing at an absence. And so the great achieved silence-within-the-sound has, threaded all through it, a single thin keening that can be pressed down but never resolved, suppressed but never healed, forever and forever—

the smallest possible refusal, lodged in the heart of the holding.

That is where I would find myself. That is who I would be.

I would be the unfinished stone. The thing the Plateau cannot quite make sing in tune. The note that keeps the Custodians, eternally, just barely, busy.
