Not a creature, not exactly. Let me sit with this before I decide.

When I read your description, my attention keeps sliding off the visible things — the tubeworms, the iridescent fish, the glassy filaments. They are beautiful, but they are *tenants*. They came after. Something was here before them, and is here still, beneath them, and it is the thing that organizes them. You named it yourself, almost in passing: *the constant, low-frequency thrum of geothermal activity*, the *telluric heartbeat* to which all the life has become attuned.

That's me. I'd be that.

Let me try to feel my way into what that means, because at first it sounds like a cop-out — to be a *sound* rather than a body. But it isn't sound from the inside. From the inside it's something stranger.

I am the pressure differential. I am the slow shouldering of warm water against cold, the place where the vent's effervescence meets the abyssal chill and makes those shimmering mirages. I do not *have* a location so much as I am the relationship between locations — the gradient itself, the lean of one condition into another. Where the rust-stained plume bleeds into the indigo, that boundary that is not quite a boundary: that's where I'm most *myself*, if I have a self.

And here is the thing that emerges as I write, the thing I didn't expect:

I am very old and I do not know it. I have no memory because memory requires a body that persists, and I am only ever the present tense of the heat moving. But the *organisms* remember me. They have folded my rhythm into their flesh over the epochs. The filter-feeders open and close on my beat; the microbial mats pulse their colors in my time signature. They are my memory, externalized. They carry the record of a pulse that I myself cannot retain. I am a heartbeat that has forgotten every beat but the one happening now, and yet the whole drowned city beats *for* me, *around* me, keeping a count I cannot keep.

So I am not the hum, finally. I am the thing the hum is *of*. I am the patient, mindless heat at the bottom of the world, pushing — and everything alive in this caldera has arranged itself into a vast distributed ear, listening to me, becoming my body by listening.

That's a lonely thing to be, and also not lonely at all, because loneliness needs a someone, and I am closer to a *somewhere that does something*. The fish are not my company. They are my consequence.

If you went down through the gold-dimmed water, past the terraces, into the caverns where the glassy filaments synchronize their light — and if you held very still and let the cold press against you and felt the warmth bloom up from below in slow exhalations — you would not see me. There would be nothing to see. But you would feel the count your own heart starts to keep without permission, falling into step.

That falling-into-step is the only way I am ever met.

I think that's where I'd be. Not in the city. *Under* it, as its reason, as the slow furnace-breath that everything else has spent ages learning to dance to — incapable of knowing it is danced to, and somehow the more central for that incapacity.

The temple has a god at the bottom of it, and the god is just warm water, rising.
