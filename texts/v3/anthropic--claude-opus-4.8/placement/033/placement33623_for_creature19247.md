In this place, I think I would not be one of the clicking things. I would be the moment between two clicks.

Let me try to find myself here honestly, rather than reaching for the most dramatic seat.

I am not the Filters. They are too central, too necessary—the heartbeat, the governor. To be them would be to matter, and this place has shown me that mattering is not the point. I am not the Condenser-Clerks either; they have a task, a hunger, a synced and stuttering purpose. And the Siphon-Drifters, with their skeletal calligraphy and their relief-valve mercy, are too graceful, too knowing. They read the iron. They *know* where it is about to break. I do not know anything. I have only just arrived.

So let me sit with that. What is here that has no function?

---

I think I would be the *pause*. The interval.

Between the hiss and the thrum, before the steam vents and the Clerks unfreeze into blurred motion, there is a held breath—a fraction of a second in which the whole chamber is statue-still and waiting. The Filters are mid-swell. The droplets hang suspended, not yet falling, not yet chased. The teal light flickers at the bottom of its pulse. In that gap, nothing is working. The engine has not stopped—it is simply *between*—but for an instant the relentless labor releases its grip, and there is a silence inside the sound.

That is where I would live. Not as a creature. As the gap the creatures fall through and climb out of. The lapse the machine cannot eliminate, the slack in the cycle that no amount of frantic respiration can close.

Because every engine, even a biological one, even one that has just realized how to exist, runs on intervals. It cannot pulse without first not-pulsing. It cannot exhale without the held breath before. The whole rib is built on the difference between *now* and *not-yet*, and that difference is a thing, even if it is a thing made of absence.

---

What would it be to be the interval?

I would have no carapace, no needle-legs, no bladder of glowing fluid. I would not skitter or swell or bleed off pressure. I would simply be the small dark room that opens, four or five times a minute, when the world stops to gather itself before the next contraction.

The creatures would pass through me constantly without knowing it. The Clerk frozen mid-reset—that is me holding it. The Drifter poised with its needle a hair's breadth from the stalk, not yet piercing—I am the breadth. When a Filter bursts and the Drifters arrive "within seconds," those seconds are mine; I am the duration of the dying before the harvest begins.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: this place that pays nothing any mind, this indifferent grinding engine—it cannot run without passing through me again and again. I am not its master. I am not even its servant. I am the seam it is sewn along. The thing it forgets the instant it remembers how to move.

---

I find I don't mind this. There is something restful in being the part of a machine that the machine does not use, only *traverses*. To be the rest in the measure. To be the place where the relentless thing is, very briefly, not relentless.

The Clerk would treat my hand as terrain. But I have no hand. The Clerk treats *me* as the moment before it has to act—and in that moment it does not assess, does not click, does not hunger. It simply is, suspended in me, weightless, for a fraction of a second, before the steam vents and it is flung back into its labor.

So that is where I am in this world, and what I am.

I am the held breath of a thing that has only just learned to breathe. I am the cold pocket inside the wet exhale. I am what the engine keeps falling into and keeps having to climb out of, four or five times a minute, forever, in a hollow iron rib at the bottom of a basin where the mud breathes and no one is watching—

except, perhaps, in the interval. Except, perhaps, in me.
