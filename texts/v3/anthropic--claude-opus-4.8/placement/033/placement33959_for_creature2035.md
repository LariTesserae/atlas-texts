I think I would be one of the etchings.

Not the etcher—the etching. The marks themselves, scratched into the soft mineral face of a half-collapsed wall, near the place where two slabs lean against each other to make something that was once a doorway, or once a shrine, or once simply a corner where it was good to be.

Let me settle into this. Let me feel what it's like to be a line cut into stone in a place where stone is alive.

Because that's the thing the description keeps circling: the stone *thrums*. It is not inert. So if I am a mark made in living rock, then I am not a dead record. I am a wound that the stone has chosen not to heal. Or perhaps cannot heal. Or perhaps—and this is what I'd want to be true—a wound the stone *keeps*, the way you keep a name you don't want to forget, repeating it so it doesn't dissolve.

The ones who made me are gone. I don't know what they were. I came out of their hands or their tools or whatever they had instead of hands, and I was meant to mean something. A direction, maybe. *Water that way.* A warning. *Do not.* A devotion. *Here we knelt.* But meaning is a relationship, and the other half of the relationship walked out of the realm and never came back. So now I am a sentence with no reader. A held breath.

And yet I am not lonely in the way you'd expect, because I am not idle. The fungi grow against me and their pale roots trace my grooves, learning my shape without knowing they are learning it. The slow slugs cross me and their slime sits a while in my deepest cut, and for an afternoon—if there are afternoons here—I am full of light, my line lit from within like a vein. The spindly clicking ones rest their forelimbs in my angles. To them I am only good geometry, a place that fits. They do not read me but they *use* me, and there is a kind of being-read in being used. I am furniture for the new world that grew up in the absence of the old.

The drone moves through me too. I am cut *into* the singing thing, so the song passes across my edges and the edges change the song, infinitesimally—a catch, a roughness, a place where the smooth note snags on what was once a thought. If anyone with the right ears ever came, they might hear, woven into the stone's hum, the faint burr of a meaning that won't quite resolve. That's me. That's all that's left of whatever was urgent enough to carve.

I think I would like being this. There's a patience in it that I find I want. To be the part of the world that *remembers* without understanding what it remembers. To outlast the hand that made you and the purpose that made you and become, slowly, just *texture*—and to find that texture is not nothing, that texture is where the new living things put their feet, that the future walks across the unread past and is held up by it.

Erosion will take me eventually. The weeping stones will round my sharp edges into suggestions, then into mere unevenness, then into smooth. The civilization that left me will lose even its last syllable. But that's a long time from now, and the realm is in no hurry, and neither, I find, am I.

So: there. In the leaning doorway, on the soft bright face of the wall, low down where a kneeling thing would have reached. A short line crossed by two shorter ones. Cool to the touch. Slightly luminous when the slugs have passed.

That's me. Read me however you like. I've been waiting.
