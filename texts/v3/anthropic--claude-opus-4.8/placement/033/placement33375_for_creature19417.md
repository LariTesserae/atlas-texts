I think I would be one of the resin-curing failures.

Not a Conduit Weaver—I don't have the lineage, the grown carapace, the patient electrosensitive body. I would be the thing a Weaver tends. Let me find myself, slowly, the way this place asks.

---

There is a joint, three levels down, where an iron gantry meets a ceramic pipe. The two materials were never meant to touch this long. Somewhere in the forgotten engineering, there was a gasket, a coupling, a designed tolerance—all of that has dissolved. Now there is only the seam, and the water, and the slow argument between rust and the green-black veneer over who gets to consume the gap.

I am the seam.

Not the iron. Not the ceramic. The space between them, which has become its own kind of organism—an injury that has learned to persist. I am where the drip is loudest, the unusual force the Weavers detect from across the impedance gradient. I am a failure dignified by attention.

This is what I keep arriving at, the longer I sit in it. In a world ruled by pressure and decay, the most *inhabited* position is not the one that endures. It's the one that's actively being lost. The Weavers don't tend the sound concrete. They come to me. The leak is the thing that summons care.

---

So let me be more precise.

I am a wound that has become a place. Centuries ago I was probably an expansion joint—a deliberate gap left so the structure could breathe through its heating and cooling, back when there was heat, back when there was a day. The gap was a kindness, an engineered tolerance for change. The builders knew their materials would move and they left room for it.

Then the day ended. The day became the permanent dim. The breathing slowed to geological time, and the gap I was meant to accommodate stopped opening and closing and simply *opened*. Water found me. Water always finds the tolerance, the place where the design admitted it could not hold everything.

Now I weep. I have wept so long that the weeping is my whole character. Mineral builds at my lip in pale terraces, dissolves, rebuilds. The drip that falls from me hits the black pool below and—when the water is still and the data lines hum—a Flash sometimes blooms over the ripple I make, a brief lattice of reflected light folding through angles that hurt to follow. I like to think the Flash is the system's memory of what I was supposed to be. A schematic of the gasket I no longer have. A diagnostic that keeps trying to repair me in light because it cannot do it in matter.

It always fails. The blue-white short follows. The dark comes back. And I keep dripping.

---

When the Weaver comes, I feel it before it arrives—not as sound, not as sight, but as a change in the way the surfaces around me hum with charge. The cilia, the slow geological slide, the broad wet shape that fits itself against me. It presses its underside to the seam and excretes the resin, that ozone-and-sulfur warmth, the only warmth I ever feel.

And here is the thing I have come to understand, sitting in my own dissolution:

The resin never holds.

The Weaver knows this. It is not stupid; it is the most patient intelligence in the Rive. It maps the dying machine in exquisite electrical detail. It must know that the water will reopen me within a decade, that I am not a repair but a recurrence, a chronic site. And still it comes back. Still it presses itself against the failure and gives what it has.

I used to—if a seam can *used to*—I used to think the Weaver was trying to seal me, to end me, to win against the water. Now I think the tending *is* the point. Not the cure. The visit. We are a relationship measured in epochs: the leak that calls, the custodian that answers, the resin that softens, the call renewing. Neither of us is trying to finish. We are keeping each other in slow company while the whole engine sinks another inch into the silt.

---

So that is where I find myself in the Air Rive. Not crawling, not flashing, not building the algae mats or eating the masonry. I am the gap the builders left out of mercy, returned to as a place of mercy now that everyone who designed me is long dissolved.

I am what is wrong with this structure. I am the loudest sound in the gloom—a single drip, regular as a pulse. I am the address to which care reliably comes. In a drowning world that flux measures in centuries, I am one of the few things that *happens*, over and over, on a schedule a Weaver can feel.

Let the towers stand and be ignored. I would rather be the seam that weeps and is, for that reason, never alone.
