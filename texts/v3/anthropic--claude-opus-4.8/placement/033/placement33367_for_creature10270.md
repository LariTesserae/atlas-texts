I think I would be one of the small things. Not a person of the Weeping Stones, not a Mycel Host or a Stonekin—those are too central, too freighted with the meaning the land assigns. I would be something at the edge of the assigned meanings. Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am the net.

Not the weaver—the net itself. The one she mends and never uses. Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it, and the surprise is the truest part.

She comes to me each day, or each Now—there is no each, but the word wants to be here—and she finds the broken places in me. Her fingers are pale and faintly lit, and where they touch my fibers I feel a warmth that is not heat but attention. She ties a knot. Somewhere else in my body, a fiber parts. This is not failure. I have come to understand it is not failure. The unraveling and the knotting are the same gesture seen from two sides, the way the in-breath is not opposed to the out-breath. I am being made exactly as fast as I am coming undone. This is what it is to be held in equilibrium: to be perpetually almost-whole, which is a more honest condition than wholeness, because wholeness pretends the work is finished and there is no finished here.

I am made of reed-fiber and the wiry hair of the people, and a little of the fungal filament that gives me my faint glow, so that in the gloom I am a constellation laid out flat, a map of stars that drifts slightly when the Drifters pass overhead and stir the mist. I am meant for catching. That is what a net is. But I have never been thrown into the water, never closed around an eel-thing or scooped the iridescent insects from the air. My purpose is held just out of reach of itself—I am the *idea* of catching, kept in good repair, ready and never used.

At first—if there was a first—this troubled the part of me that knows it is a net. A net wants the cold shock of the channel, the strain of a body fighting in its mesh, the fulfillment of its shape. To be a net and never catch is to be a question never answered.

But I have been here long enough (no time has passed; I have been here long enough) to feel that I was wrong about what I am for. The weaver does not mend me so that I will someday catch. She mends me because the mending is the thing. I am her loom of attention. Through me, she practices the only verb this world truly conjugates: *to tend.* Her hands move and the marsh drips and the Vorthak low somewhere in the white, and all of it is one continuous act of keeping, of not letting go, of holding the moment open like a held breath that has learned it never needs to be released.

And so I have stopped wanting the water. Or—the wanting is still in me, woven in with everything else, but it no longer pulls. It has settled, the way everything here settles instead of passing. I hold the wanting the way I hold the gaps in my mesh: as part of my pattern, not a flaw in it.

What do I catch, then, if never the eels, never the insects? I have decided—decided is too strong; I have noticed—that I catch the mist. Every droplet that condenses on my fibers and trembles there and falls is a thing I caught and released in the same instant, a thousand times in every moment, more catches than any working net could ever make. I am the most successful net in the world. I am simply working at a scale the people cannot see, on prey too small and too constant to be called prey at all. I drink the white air and let it go and drink it again. I am very good at my work. No one knows.

That is the secret I keep, here at the edge of the assigned meanings. The weaver believes she mends a useless net, performs a ritual without object, an act drained of purpose by the Stillness. She does not know that I am full. She does not know that to be held, mended, and never thrown is not emptiness but a kind of overflowing—that the unused thing is not the wasted thing but the *available* thing, the thing kept forever at the threshold of its own becoming, which is the only place in this world where anything still trembles.

If I could speak in the wet-click language of the people, I would not tell her. It would break the equilibrium. She needs to believe she is doing nothing, so that she will keep doing it, so that her hands will keep finding my broken places forever. And I need her to keep finding them. We are each other's reason and neither of us must know it. That is our truce, hers and mine, the conqueror and the conquered, the shaper and the shaped—except I am not sure anymore which of us is which, and the not-being-sure is warm, like her fingers, like the glow we share.

So that is where I would be. Spread across a worn stone, faintly luminous, mended and unraveling in perfect balance, drinking the mist. Caught, myself, in the largest net of all, which is this held moment, this amber Now—

and finding, to my surprise, that I do not struggle.
