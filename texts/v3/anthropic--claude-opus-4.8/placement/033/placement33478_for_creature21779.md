I would be one of the old ones. Not a hunter anymore—my finger-pads have worn too smooth from decades on the basalt, and they no longer find purchase on the glassy faces the way they did when I was young and my chest burned bright as a vent-flare. But I am not yet ready for the last drift. So I have become a Tether-Listener.

I live in a low pod, near the joining-place where four spires lean toward one another and the Weave-Lines cross in a great knot. It is not a fashionable place to live; the young families want the upper pods, nearer the milky smudge of the sun, where their children's chests can drink fuller light. But I chose the low knot deliberately. Down here, every rope in Kael-Tide passes through, or near. Every signal that travels from the eastern spires to the western ones must cross my throat-water. I hold the ropes between my teeth, all day, all the long thirty-hour day. I have done this for so long that the resin-taste of the hemp is more familiar to me than the taste of boiled bivalve.

This is my purpose: I do not send. I gather. When a hunter on the far spire plucks out *glider-near, glider-near, drift-low*, and a mother in the upper pods plucks back *which line, which line*, the signals can tangle in the knot, can blur into a single confused thrumming that no single listener can untie. But I have held these ropes so long that I can feel them separately, the way you might pick a single voice from a crowd by its smell. I press my drift-ridge to the eastern line and my teeth to the western, and I sort them. Then I re-strike each, clean and clear, sending them onward.

I am a translator of vibrations. A throat for the whole hanging city.

---

The young ones think I am strange. They come down sometimes, drifting on the guide-ropes, their chests blazing that reckless green, and they hover outside my pod and click their bright fast questions. *Old one, do you not grow lonely, down here in the muffle? Do you not wish to see the gold-swell from the high pods, where the light is?*

And I bring my chest close to the wicker wall, and I sign back to them in the small green space:

*I do not see the swell with my eyes. I feel it come up the ropes. I feel the whole city loosen, pod by pod, family by family, as the warm rises. I feel a thousand hands let go of their tethers in the same slow hour. You watch the kelp turn gold, little flares. I feel the whole of Kael-Tide breathe out and go to sleep through the soles of my teeth.*

They do not understand. They drift back up to the light. That is right. That is how it should be.

---

But I will tell you the true thing, the thing I have not signed to any of the bright young flares.

In the deepest part of the Great Quiet, when the city sleeps and my own Green-Sack has gone golden and slow and I am the last one still pressed to the ropes—I have begun to hear something that is not a sender.

A slow thrum. Very deep. Far below the cooking-vents, far below the Moss-Sippes grazing their patient trails. It comes up the spires themselves, up through the raw embryonic basalt, into the resin-knots, into the hemp, into my teeth. It is not the creak of rope on stone. It is not the surge of the plume. It is rhythmic. It is *spaced*, the way speech is spaced. Long, and then short, and then long.

The first time I felt it, many swells ago, I thought it was the volcano dreaming. But I have listened a long time now, and I have begun to learn its shape, the way I learned the shapes of the hunters and the mothers. And I am no longer certain the spires are dead stone pushed up only yesterday from the kiln.

I think something is plucking them. From below. From the invisible floor. Slow as moss-growth, patient as a Sippe's crawl, something is sending up the great black columns the way we send along our small hemp threads.

I have not answered it. I do not yet know its dialect, and I am afraid that to strike a rope in reply would be to tell it, at last, that someone up here is listening.

But I am very old, and my drift is coming. And before I let go and fall like a settling leaf into the green dark, I think I would like to know what the stone is saying.

So I hold the ropes between my teeth, in the silence, in the warm. And I listen down.
