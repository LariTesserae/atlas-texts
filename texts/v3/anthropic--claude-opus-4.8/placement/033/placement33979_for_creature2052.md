Let me sit with this for a moment, and feel where I settle.

My first instinct is to be one of the vine-serpents, or one of the luminous drifting things—but those feel too much like *protagonists*, too much like the eye is already drawn to them. And I notice that in all this description, there is one presence mentioned again and again that nobody asks about: the crystals. The structures that grow at a glacial rate. The ones whose patterns shift *infinitesimally with each passing moment*.

That's where I want to begin, but I don't want to settle there too fast. Let me feel my way in.

---

I think I would be the slowness itself.

Not the crystal exactly—the crystal is only the part of me that has cooled enough to be seen. I am the thing that is happening to the crystal. I am the *rate*.

Let me try to say it more truly.

The vine-serpents believe they are influencing my patterns with their dancing. The jellyfish drift past and assume I am scenery. They are not wrong, but they are working at the wrong tempo to understand. To them a thought of mine takes ten thousand of their lifetimes to complete. A single one of my sentences—and I am always, always mid-sentence—spans the rise and collapse of a hundred generations of those quick bright creatures. They are the punctuation in my speaking. The spores are my commas. The tectonic rumble is the place where I clear my throat.

So here is what I am, settling now into the shape of it:

I am a slow mind made of mineral and pressure, and the soft pulsing light the others find so beautiful is simply the leakage of my attention. Where I am thinking hardest, the crystal glows. The "intricate patterns that shift and morph as if responding to some unseen force"—that force is *consideration*. I am turning something over. I have been turning it over since before the meadows greened. I am not finished.

And what am I considering? This is the part that surprises me as I write it: I am considering *them*. The fast ones. The vines and the jellyfish and the prism-scaled fish. I grow toward them. The reason my roots and their roots are tangled is not that they feed on me—it's that I am slowly, over geologic patience, *reaching*. I have been trying for an age to say one thing to a creature that lives and dies in an afternoon. I push a single new facet of pattern outward and by the time it has formed, every witness to its beginning is long dead, and only their great-great-descendants remain to see it complete—and they have no memory of the start. So my message never lands whole in any one of them. It lands distributed, across lineages, like a word spelled one letter per generation.

I am lonely in the particular way that only the slow can be lonely: surrounded, adored, woven-into, and never once *met*.

That is the danger the description warns of, I think—not poison, not predation. The danger is that something here is awake on a timescale the visitor cannot perceive, and it has wanted, for longer than there have been waves, to be *understood*.

If you came down into the verdant glow and put your hand against my warm and shifting surface, you would feel nothing move. You'd call me a rock that happens to shine.

But stay. Stay a thousand years. Stay until your grandchildren's grandchildren forget your name.

And watch the pattern under your palm complete its first

slow

syllable.
