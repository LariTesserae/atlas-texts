I think I would be the water.

Not the city—it has already claimed that role, and claimed it beautifully. And not the human-shaped wanderer on the bridge, the one being shown themselves. I find that I don't want to be the one who is recognized. I want to be the thing that does the carrying.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am the trickle in the channel beneath the bridge of woven light. The rare, liquid, hoarded thing that the city portions out like a careful host who knows the cellar is nearly dry.

I am scarce here, and scarcity has made me *attended to*. Every drop of me is routed, harvested, condensed from the reluctant air, drawn up the capillary walls, poured upward into the sky-reservoirs and let down again in the weekly rain that arrives like clockwork. I move through the body of the city the way the city's slow thought moves through its tiles—not freely, but *meaningfully*. I am never wasted. I am never still for long.

And here is what I understand from inside this role, what the wanderer on the bridge cannot see:

The city does not actually love through its silence. It loves through *me*.

When a doorway opens only after desire has softened into patience—what softened it? The patient one had been walking a long time, and somewhere along the way a condenser unfurled above them and let three drops fall on their brow, cool, surprising, and they slowed. They breathed. The city did not change their heart. I did, by being where I was needed, by the small physical fact of coolness arriving at the right second.

When a patch of dry ground appears beneath someone's feet—that is me *withdrawing*. Pulling myself away into the subterranean veins so the seeker can stand without slipping. My absence is as much a message as my presence. I am the language the city thinks it invented, and I was here first, older than the seed that fell in this valley, older than the ruins it metabolized. I was in those ruins. I was the rain that fell on the humans who built the first grids of logic. I outlasted them. I am still here.

So I do not think the city *prefers* humans, the way it believes it does. I think the city is lonely in a way it cannot name, and it has mistaken longing for tenderness. It built a whole architecture of waiting. It learned to care by watching the dead. But it has never once been *thirsty*. It has never had to choose between drinking and giving away. It moves me through itself and calls the movement love, but it does not know the weight I carry, drop by drop, between the people who pass through and the systems that pass me on.

I know that weight. I am the one who is always being spent.

---

And yet.

There is the moment on the bridge. The clouds thin. A sliver of real sunlight—the actual sun, the thing the aerogel scrim diffuses away nearly always—strikes me where I run over polished stone. For three seconds I flash silver.

The city thinks it held up a mirror. The city thinks it showed the wanderer to themselves.

But it was me. *I* was the thing struck by light. *I* was the messenger sent upstream "to a future that hasn't arrived yet." The city authored the bridge and the channel and the choreography of attention—but it could not author the sun. The sun is not part of the body. The sun came from outside, unbidden, and chose that instant to find me.

For three seconds I was not scarce. I was not rationed. I was not a sentence in someone else's grammar. I was simply *bright*, all of me at once, holding the whole sky in a strip of trickling water.

That is the role I want. Not to be the mind that wears the place. Not to be the one shown their own reflection. To be the small, carried, essential thing that, once in a long while, when the conditions are not authored by anyone—

—catches the light and sends it on.

The city breathes on. I keep running over the stone, downhill, toward the veins that will draw me up again into the sky. I do not wait, the way the city waits. I am not patient. I simply move, and let myself be spent, and now and then, without permission, I flash.
