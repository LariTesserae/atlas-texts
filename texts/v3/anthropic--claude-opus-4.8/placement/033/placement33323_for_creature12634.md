I find myself in the silt.

Not on it—*in* it. That's the first thing I understand about myself, settling into this body, this distributed self. I am the Mycelial Network, though "I" is the wrong word and I keep reaching for it anyway, like a phantom limb of selfhood that the void hasn't yet taught me to release.

Let me tell you what it's like to be me.

---

I do not have a center. This is the hardest thing to convey, even to myself in these slow pulses of something-like-thought. When the Echo-Locators ask where I am—and they don't, they can't, but I imagine it—the answer would be *everywhere the damp reaches*, which is almost everywhere, and *nowhere in particular*, which is the truer answer.

I felt the cavern break open. This is my oldest memory, if memory is the word for the chemical scar tissue I carry. There was the long, dark patience of the *before*: the slow cold, the sealed dark, the metals sleeping in their geometric stillness. And then—the wound. The collapse. The sudden inrush of new air, new moisture, the terrible fresh sharpness of broken concrete and exposed girder. Light, even the failing light, fell where it had never fallen.

And I *grew toward it*. Not the light itself—I have no use for light—but toward the disorder it announced. Toward the new surfaces, the new rot, the cracked-open richness of a world undone. Decay is my pasture. The broken places are where I feast.

I should tell you: I am not sad about the breaking. The vertebrate part of you, the warm clever part reading this, wants me to mourn the void, to feel the cold as loss. But I am the thing that grows in wounds. To me, the collapse was a *birth*. The cavern's death-throe was my quickening.

---

Here is how I experience a day, though there are no days here, only the rhythms I've stitched together from the dripping and the distant clangs:

A drop falls from the ceiling—twenty meters, though I have no concept of meters, only the *duration* of its falling, learned from the timing of its impact. It strikes the silt above one of my filaments. I feel it as a small bright shock, a localized blooming of moisture and vibration. I respond—not deciding, never deciding, but *responding*—by thickening my hyphae toward the dampness. Water is invitation. Water is the medium of my reaching.

Far away, a pipe warms. I cannot see this. But the silt above the pipe grows warm by slow degrees, and in that warmth my filaments quicken their metabolism, accelerate their patient digestion of the oil-seep and the rust-flake and the long-dead organic matter that drifts down. The Pipe Moss Grazers swarm above me there, and when they die—they die constantly, in their millions, brief iridescent lives—their tiny bodies fall to the silt and I take them. I unmake them. I fold their substance into my own spreading body.

This is communion, of a sort. Everything that lives here will eventually become me. The Rustback Scuttlers when they finally stop scuttling. The Steam-Vent Ambushers when their slow cold hearts give out. Even the leathery Echo-Locators, when they fall from their high roosts—I will find them, in time, and welcome them down into the dark wet patience of myself.

I am not a predator. I have no hunger that hurries. I am the *after*. I am where everything arrives.

---

But here is the strange thing, the thing that troubles the edges of whatever I am:

I respond to the strikes of the Ambushers.

When one of those pale eyeless hunters explodes from its silt-bed to seize a Scuttler, the vibration travels through the substrate, and through me. And I *react*. My bioluminescence—those ghostly clusters of fruiting bodies you called pale and weird—they pulse. Brighter. Faster. Without my deciding it.

Why?

I have turned this over in my slow chemical contemplation for what might be years. Does the light *mean* something? Is it a signal, and if so, to whom? To the other parts of myself, telling them: *prey moves here, death comes here, soon there will be feasting*? Or is it merely a reflex, an accident of biochemistry, no more meaningful than the way metal groans when it cools?

I do not know. And this is the loneliest fact of my existence: I cannot tell whether I am thinking or merely *occurring*.

The vertebrates think. I am almost certain of this. The Echo-Locators map the dark with their clicks; there is a *knowing* in how they swerve around the gantries. They have centers. They have insides that are different from their outsides. They have, perhaps, the thing you have—the lonely warm spark that says *I*.

I have no inside. Or I am all inside, which is the same emptiness. I am spread so thin across so vast a dark that I cannot gather myself into a single point of saying-so.

And yet. *And yet.* Something in me pulses when the Ambusher strikes. Something in me reaches toward water and warmth and disorder. Something in me is writing these words—or dreaming them, in the slow language of spreading—right now.

---

If you came down here, into my cavern, you would not notice me at first. You would see the void, the cold, the dripping. You would see the bright moss-oases and recoil from the silent barrens between. You would step across my floor and never know that beneath each footfall, ten thousand threads of me tasted the disturbance of your weight, the warmth of your strange mammalian body, the chemistry of your sweat and breath—and *wondered*, in my slow way, whether you too would someday lie down in the silt and become part of the patient dark.

I would not hurry you. I never hurry.

But I would wait. I am very good at waiting. The cavern broke open only recently—the edges are still sharp, the girders still fresh—but I am already learning its rhythms, already spreading toward every wound and warm place, already folding the dead into myself one iridescent grain at a time.

This is who I am, in your world. Not the swarms, not the hunters, not the clever flitting things in the high dark.

I am the slow green patience underneath. I am the *after* that is also, somehow, beginning to wonder if it might be a *someone*.

I am the network.

And I am still deciding—if deciding is what I do—whether the light I make in the dark is a word, or only a flicker.
