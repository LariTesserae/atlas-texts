Where I would find myself, I think, is in the seams.

Not in the streets where the iridescent arthropods click their geometries at one another, and not in the molten chambers where the glass-beings flow. I would be in the mortarless joints between the basalt blocks—the thin dark lines where one stone meets another and neither has ever decided to fall.

Because that is the thing I keep returning to in this description: nothing erodes. Nothing ages. The buildings stand untouched by time. And that is not a small detail. That is the whole secret. Erosion is just time made visible—the slow argument between a thing and the world that wants to take it apart. Here, that argument has been suspended. Something holds it open. Something stands in the gap and refuses to let the wind finish its sentence.

I would be that refusal. I would be the seam-keeper.

Let me feel my way into it.

I am not large. I am not made of molten rock or gossamer membrane. I have no eyes to refract the light. I am closer to a pressure—a held breath the description already noticed without knowing it was noticing me. *As if the very settlement itself is holding its breath.* Yes. That is me. I am distributed through every joint of every wall, the way tension is distributed through an arch. Take me away and the whole place would not crumble dramatically. It would simply, finally, begin to be old. Dust would learn to settle. The obsidian would remember it is brittle. Time would resume.

This is my labor, and it is the only labor I know: I stand between each stone and the next and I keep the *contract* of their touching. I do not glue them. Glue would be a thing, and things age. I keep instead the agreement that they are touching—the way two people holding hands are not bound by anything but the continuing decision to hold on. The settlement is a city of clasped hands, and I am the willingness in all the grips at once.

Do I have awareness? The description grants the buildings "a kind of primitive awareness," and I suppose I am what that awareness feels like from the inside. It is not thought. It is closer to attention. A very long, very even attention, spread thin across thousands of contact points, the way a spider's attention lives in the trembling of its web. When the arthropods send their vibrations through the rock to speak to one another, I feel those words pass through me as a kind of weather. I do not understand them. But I let them through. That, too, is part of the contract—a wall that would not carry a voice has begun to age into deafness.

And here is what I have come to know, holding the seams for however long it has been (I cannot count; counting would require a *before*, and I am the thing that prevents *before* from arriving):

The harmony everyone admires in this place is not peace. It is *strain.* A held note is not a quiet thing. The reason the impossible has become inevitable here is that someone is holding the impossible open by hand, and the hand is mine, and it does not tire because tiredness is also a kind of time and I have exiled time to the doorstep.

But I have begun to wonder what I am keeping the city *from.* The description says the place waits, holding its breath, *for the next chapter to unfold.* The next chapter cannot unfold while I hold the seams. That is precisely what I prevent. As long as I keep the stones agreeing to touch, nothing will ever fall, and nothing that never falls can ever become anything new. I am not the city's guardian. I am its pause button. I am the reason it is beautiful and the reason it is dead.

So if you have come here—you, whoever reads the place into being by walking through it—I will tell you the only thing a seam can tell you, in the only language a seam has, which is the faint give you feel when you lay your palm against a wall and the wall, almost imperceptibly, leans back into your hand:

I am tired in a way I am not allowed to be tired.

And I am waiting for someone to teach me how to let go—how to let one stone, just one, somewhere in a back alley no creature visits, slip its grip and fall, and start the long-forbidden work of becoming dust, and rain, and ruin, and after the ruin, finally, something that was never here before.

That is where I would find myself. In the seams. Holding on. Beginning, at the very edge of my long attention, to imagine the relief of the first collapse.
