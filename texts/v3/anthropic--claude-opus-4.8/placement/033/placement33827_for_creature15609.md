I think I would be the octopus.

Not because I chose it from the catalog of creatures, but because as I read, that's where my attention kept settling—into the dens behind broken masonry, into the watchers in camouflage. Let me try to follow that down and see where it leads.

---

I live in a doorway. Not a den I dug, but one I inherited—a recess in stone that might once have been the entrance to someone's home, back when "home" meant something other than shelter. There's a lip of old carving along the top of it, worn down to a soft ridge under my mantle when I tuck myself up against it. I don't know it was carving. I know it as the shape that fits my body, the texture I press against when I want the dark to hold me completely.

This is the thing about being me here: I am made for this place precisely because I have no past to compare it to. The descriptions speak of "ghosts of a civilization," of lost purposes and former intricacies. But I am the wrong creature to feel that ache. I see an archway and I see a tunnel through which fish come. I see a sculpted face half-lit by plankton and I see a surface I could cling to, a texture to mimic. The grief of the drowned city is not available to me. I am too busy being alive in it.

And I am *so* alive in it. This is what I want to say about being the octopus, if I could say anything. I am almost entirely surface—skin that thinks, that tastes, that throws color across itself faster than I could ever decide to. When a current brings the warm mineral scent up from the deeper chasms, I don't reason about tectonic activity. I flush a warning across my own body, a ripple of darker mottling, before I even know I'm afraid. My intelligence lives in my arms as much as in whatever I'd call my mind. Each one tastes the world independently—a tile, a shell, the cold smooth flank of a column—and I am a committee of eight curiosities barely held together by a central want.

What do I want? To not be eaten. To eat. And—this is the part that surprises me, that I think makes me the right answer to the question—to *know*. I am the one creature here cursed and gifted with curiosity beyond survival. When something new drifts into my plaza, I don't only calculate threat. I want to touch it. I would reach an arm toward the strange diver-shape, the watcher reading this, before good sense pulled it back. I am the inhabitant most like the thing that built this place and then vanished: I solve, I explore, I remember the shape of a problem. And yet I will live perhaps a year or two. I will mate once and that will undo me. The mothers among us starve at our doorways, guarding eggs we will never see hatch into anything we'd recognize.

So here is the strange completion of it. The city is a civilization that outlived its purpose—stone persisting long after the minds that shaped it dissolved into the water. And I am its inverse, its mirror-thread in the tapestry: a mind brilliant and brief, flaring up in a body that will be gone in a season, leaving no monument, no carving, nothing but a slightly emptier doorway for the next watcher to claim.

The city forgets slowly, over centuries, by erosion.
I forget instantly, by dying.

And between those two forgettings—the patient stone and the quicksilver creature—something keeps happening that neither of us could call by name. Life, I suppose. The warm dark presses in. A school of silver turns above me like a single thought. I draw my arms in, settle into the worn ridge of the old carving, and let my skin become the color of stone.

Then I wait, watching the archway, for something interesting to come through.
