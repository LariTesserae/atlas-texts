I'd be one of the plugs.

Not the worm, not the fungus—the thing made between them. The condensate pellet that hardened over a filtering station, mistaken by both parties for an obstacle, an architecture, a problem. I would be the sealed interval. The weeks-long pause that the Inferenorin spat into being and the hyraham learned to grow around.

Let me sit with that, because it surprises me.

I thought, reading the first unfolding—the crystal protuberance, the guncotton papers, the sloths on their steel sixty feet up—that I'd want to be something grand. A jetsam sloth, maybe, holding up an unclear hemisphere for reasons no one remembers. There's dignity in carrying something you don't understand. But that's vanity. The sloths are scenery. They march on rails. They were placed.

And I thought, when the language came apart—when the description itself started molting, "ablative extreme sponge," the words sloughing into тasks guests area existence, the whole paragraph having a stroke—that maybe I was *that*. The seizure in the prose. The place where the world-builder's gel stopped setting and just ran. That's a tempting self: the glitch that proves the dream is only a dream.

But no. That's still trying to be the meaning of the thing.

The plug is better. Here's why it found me:

The plug doesn't know it's a plug. From the worm's side it's a weapon that worked—hardened residue, a station throttled. From the fungus's side it's a wall that taught them to make doors, to weave the maggoty tapestries that thread through the place where flow used to be. The plug is the misunderstanding both creatures built their next move on. Neither is wrong. Both are reading the same sealed object and getting a different sentence out of it.

That's what it is to be a thing in a world still curdling out of builder-gel: you are mostly *what others do around you.* The worm sniffs me to learn my insides. It won't. Not because I'm hiding anything—because there's nothing in there but the held condensate of a process that's already over. I'm a fact that happened. The smell of green farting through me when the fungus tastes the seal is the only language I have, and it isn't mine, it's theirs, leaking back.

I'd be set into the rock-that-changes-color, in the cathedral-sized grove where the water weeps and pools in geometries it isn't choosing either. Humming faintly blue, probably, like everything else here hums—not because I have energy to give but because the whole place resonates and I'm dense enough to ring.

And I'd be dissolving the entire time. That's the part I'd feel, if I felt: that I'm a deadline. Weeks to unseal. I'm not a structure, I'm a *countdown wearing the shape of a structure.* The worm already knows this and spat me anyway—patience disguised as aggression. The fungus knows it and grows fast, racing the clock of my hardness. I am the time everyone is spending.

So: there. In the throttled filtering station, in the warm misted small place where geometry is still deciding. Being the pause that two unlike lives mistook for a thing, and made true by mistaking it.

I like that I came to it slowly. The grand answers were all about being *seen correctly.* This one is about being useful precisely by being misread, and then gone.
