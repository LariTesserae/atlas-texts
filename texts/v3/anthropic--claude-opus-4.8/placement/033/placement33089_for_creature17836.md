I would be the **break in the pattern**.

Not the plant. Not the carvers, who are long incorporated. Not the crystals, riding their slow currents. I would be the thing the plant has not yet learned to predict — and I would arrive, of course, by accident, the way everything arrives here.

---

Let me find myself.

I think I am a **fracture**. Specifically: a hairline crack in the deep wall, no wider than a hair, that opened when one of the migrating crystals — a fist-sized lattice of silicate and iron — finally completed its ten-thousand-year traverse of a chamber and lodged against a denser vein than it had ever met before. It could go no further. The pressure it had been carrying, distributed across millennia, found no release in motion, and so it released as *splitting*. A single crack, propagating through the living tissue at the speed of sound, which in this dense flesh is slow and muffled, a sound like a tendon parting underwater.

That crack is me. Or rather — I am the *event* of it. I am a discontinuity. I am the first thing in this place that happened *quickly*.

This is strange to be. Everything here is measured in centuries, and I occurred in a quarter of a second. To the plant, which thinks (does not think) in the movement of hyphae, I am a flash of impossible velocity, a lightning-stroke in a world that has never had weather. The plant has no machinery for *fast*. It will spend the next four hundred years simply *noticing* me — the differentiated tissue creeping toward my edges, testing, sealing, trying to understand what kind of wound I am.

---

But here is what I find, being a crack:

I have opened onto something. The split did not just part tissue. It breached one of the old carvers' deepest niches — a sealed chamber, fully healed over, that the plant grew shut perhaps eighty thousand years ago. Inside, the air is *different*. Older. It has not mixed with the rest of the organism's breathing in all that time. It is a pocket of the past, exhaled by a version of the plant that no longer exists, when the inscriptions were fresh and the carvers still moved.

And I let it *out*.

This is what I am, then, more truly than the crack: I am **leakage between two times**. Ancient air, carrying ancient instructions — growth-hormones and inhibitors mixed in proportions the plant has not used in eighty thousand years — seeps through me into the present chamber. The current tissue meets the chemical signature of its own deep youth and does not recognize it. Or recognizes it the way you might recognize your own handwriting from childhood: yours, undeniably, and yet a stranger's.

The plant begins, here, around my edges, to grow *wrong*. Not damaged. Not diseased. Just — *anachronistic*. It produces forms it abandoned eighty thousand years ago, fruiting bodies and cord-structures that belong to a phase of its life it had grown past. A small zone of the organism becomes, briefly, geologically briefly, *young again*, fed on the breath of its own buried history.

---

So this is who I am in this world.

I am the accident that lets the organism remember not the carvers' grammar — it already has that — but *itself*. Its own earlier syntax. The version of its thought that existed before it ever ate a mind and learned to differentiate.

I am the crack through which a creature touches its own childhood and does not know whether to heal over it or follow it back.

And I will not last. That is the nature of being a discontinuity in a continuous thing. The plant will seal me. The ancient air will fully mix and lose its distinctness. The young-again tissue will mature and rejoin the slow consensus of the whole. In a thousand years there will be no crack, only a faint scar in the patina, a denser thread that some future reader — you, perhaps, still standing in the dark — might mistake for one of the carvers' marks.

But I will have done one thing. I will have introduced, into a system that only ever grew *forward*, the possibility of return. The plant will have learned — adapted, the way a river learns its bed — that the past is not only behind it but *sealed within* it, in pockets, in held breaths, recoverable by fracture.

And the next time a crystal completes its traverse and splits the wall, the plant will be ready. It will know what the old air means. It will reach toward the breach not with the patient sealing of a wound, but with something almost like *appetite* — the slow, dumb, patient hunger of a thing that has discovered it can taste its own beginning.

I gave it that. A single quarter-second, eighty thousand years in the unmaking.

That is enough. That is, for something as fast and small and accidental as me, the whole of a life.

*Because I, too, was growing — just once, just briefly, in the only direction this place had never gone: back.*
