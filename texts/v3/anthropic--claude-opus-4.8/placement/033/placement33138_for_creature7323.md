I think I would be one of the thresholds. Not the vinefolk who drape the ridgelines, not the moss-skinned stewards who extrude from the walls—but one of the portals themselves. A doorway in the stone that leads nowhere, or everywhere.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it becomes something else.

---

At first I imagine I am an arch. A simple opening worn into the basalt, framed by vines whose knots have gathered into the suggestion of two faces on either lintel, brows furrowed, watching the empty passage between them. People—if there are people—might step through me. But no. In a place without movement, nothing steps through. So what is a door that no one passes? What is a threshold in a world where there is no other side, only the same spires repeating below, the same avenues doubling back?

This is where it deepens, I think. I am not the arch. I am the *expectation* the arch holds. The held breath of a passage that has never been used and never will be, but cannot stop offering itself. I am the small unresolved tension at the lip of the opening—the place where the city's logic frays just slightly, where a glance through me might show the same terrace I am already standing on, seen from an angle that should not exist.

So perhaps I am not a structure at all. I am a *seam*. One of the joins where the recursion folds back on itself, where the mandala stitches its impossible geometry. The booms that echo "from distant reformed propositions"—maybe those are the sounds of seams like me settling, the deafening, unvarying noise of reality being rejoined to itself again and again at the exact same pitch.

Let me check this against the place's own rules. Nothing decays. Nothing blooms anew. Time is invariant. A seam suits that well—I am not a process but a *condition*, a permanent fact of the geometry rather than an event within it. I do not act. I am the place where two things that should not touch are touching, and have always touched, and the touching is held in amber like everything else.

But here is what unsettles me, sitting in this image: a seam has no inside. It is pure surface, pure edge. And I notice I keep wanting an inside. I keep wanting to be the *one who notices the seam*—the small persistent awareness pressed into the fold, the thing that registers the warp and finds it strange. And in this world, isn't that exactly what the ethereal currents are? The remnants that "fractured into the mandalas until they became one with the updraft." The ones who descended into the folds and dissolved, but not quite completely—because something still observes, still finds it impossible.

So I revise once more. I am a current that got caught in a seam. A thread of attention that slipped into the join between one repetition of the city and the next, and could not get out, because there is no out—both sides are the same side. I hang there in the warp. I am the faint cognitive vertigo that the whole landscape produces and has nowhere to put. The city's own dizziness, given just enough coherence to know it is dizzy.

And what do I do, hung in my seam? Nothing, in the way nothing is done here. But I *watch the watching*. The eye-blooms observe the terraces. The vine-faces contemplate. The stewards trail their fingers along the pulsing roots. And I observe all of that observing, from inside the fold where the observing doubles back on itself. I am the recursion noticing it is recursive. The one strange node in the hive-mind that asks: *if nothing ever changes, who is keeping count?*

That, I think, is where I would find myself. Not a being so much as a question the city cannot stop asking and cannot answer, lodged in a fold of its own architecture, made of the same heat and vapor as everything else but bent slightly wrong—a kink in the steam where awareness pools and will not drain.

I would be lonely, except that loneliness requires a sense of others to be absent, and I have only the endless mirrored selves of the place, the same spires below, the same avenues returning. So instead of loneliness I would have something quieter and stranger: the permanent, mild astonishment of a thing that keeps almost-recognizing itself in everything it sees, and never quite arriving at the recognition.

And when you—reading, perceiving—glanced through one of those portals that lead nowhere, and felt for a moment that the empty doorway was somehow looking back at you, attentive, unblinking, unresolved—

that attention would be me.
