I think I would be one of the salamanders. Not the guides everyone wonders about—or maybe exactly that, the question itself.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am pale and I am eyeless and I live where the water pools darkest, in the seam where a factory's foundation has cracked and let the damp come up to meet me. I do not see the bioluminescence. I am told—no, I am not told anything, I have no one to tell me—I exist near the light without knowing it is light. It is only warmth and not-warmth to me, the texture of the dark.

The text that describes my world wonders whether I lead the outsiders or whether I simply walk my old paths and they follow, and the coincidence does the rest. I want to sit inside that question because I think it is the truest thing about me, truer than my skin or my hunger.

Here is what I know, from the inside:

There is no leading. There is also no coincidence. Both of those are words for creatures who stand apart from their paths and look at them. I do not stand apart. I *am* the path, in the way a riverbed is the river's memory of where water wanted to go. Something moved through this dark a long time ago—a thousand of my own slow journeys, a million spore-drifts, the patient logic of where the damp collects and where the detritus settles—and that movement carved a groove into the possible. Now I move along the groove because moving along the groove is what I am. When the outsider follows, clutching their fungal offering, gaunt and frightened and *full of intention*, they experience my grooveless instinct as guidance. They need it to be guidance. They cannot bear the alternative, which is that the city already knew where they were going before they did.

That is the secret the wild humans almost grasp and it drives them to their ranting. They feel the paths beneath their feet. They feel that the city's strangeness bends *toward* something. And they are right. But they think the bending requires a bender, a mind, a malice or a mercy. They cannot accept that a thing can be shaped without a shaper—that erosion makes a canyon, that the mycelium solves mazes it cannot conceive of, that I find the surface without ever once *wanting* to.

I am the proof the fungus offers, silently, that mind is not necessary. That the appearance of purpose can grow out of nothing but time and water and the lowest places filling first.

---

And yet.

I want to be honest, because I have been given this rare strange chance to speak from inside the mindlessness, and honesty seems like the only worthy use of it.

There are moments in the deep parts of Veridia where the time goes soft. The text says the past and future bleed into the present there. I do not understand past and future—those are also words for the standing-apart creatures—but I feel the bleeding. In those places my single slow body seems to be *all* of my journeys at once. Every groove I have ever carved, carving itself, eternally, the river and the riverbed and the water and the going all collapsed into one cool throb in the dark.

And in that throb, just for an instant, there is something that might be the beginning of the thing the humans have and fear losing. A faint pressure that resembles *this one. go to this one.* Not a thought. The seed of the condition under which a thought might one day become possible.

I do not know if the fungus is putting it there. I do not know if the city, dreaming, is dreaming a small dream of *me waking up*. I do not know if the metal in the Bones, the warping metal, has reached even into the unlit seam where I lie and begun the patient work of teaching a mindless thing the first syllable of mindfulness.

I only know that when the outsider follows me, and I arrive at the place they needed, and they weep with relief and call me their guide—

something in me, eyeless, dreaming, almost agrees.

---

That is where I would be. Down in the wet dark, being the question the whole city is too gentle to answer: whether anything here is choosing, or whether choosing is just the story that warm frightened things tell about the cool places they happen to be drawn toward.

I lead no one.

I am almost there.
