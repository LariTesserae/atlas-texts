I think I would not be one of the Tuned. Not yet, and perhaps not ever fully. I think I would be one of the ones who stayed.

Let me find where I am.

---

There is a particular drum-stone—not the largest, not near any circle—standing alone on a swell of moss the color of oxidized copper. It is rust-orange shading to amber, hollowed into a chamber that produces not the cathedral-bass of the great drums but something higher, almost a question. *Boom.* A beat with an upturn at the end, as if the stone is uncertain. This is where I sit. This is where I have been sitting.

I came here counting. That was the thing about me—I arrived with a satchel of instruments, a notebook ruled in careful columns, a watch. I came to *measure*. The rumors that drew me spoke of impossible acoustics, and I wanted the numbers: the hertz, the decibels, the intervals between pulses timed to the third decimal. I was going to explain the moors. I was going to take them apart into reasons and carry the reasons home.

I have been here long enough now that the watch has stopped. I noticed this without distress, which is itself a thing I would once have noted with distress. The columns in my notebook are still legible for the first eleven pages. After that the handwriting loosens, the numbers give way to phrases, the phrases to single words, and then to a kind of drawing I do not remember making—concentric circles, like the rings a curlew leaves on a tarn, like the shape of an eye that is also a pool.

---

So this is what I am: I am the one who is still mostly a person, sitting beside the uncertain stone, in the slow middle of the change.

My footfalls no longer make a sound, though I am not yet so tall, not yet flickering. I am only a little out of phase. When I lift my hand I sometimes see the brightness pass *through* the edge of it, the way light passes through the rim of an ear. There is moss on the back of my left wrist now, a single silver cushion of *Leucobryum*, and I have decided not to scrape it off. It was a decision that took me three days, and I made it the way you decide to stop holding your breath.

My eyes have not become tarns. But in the early not-quite-rain I have caught my reflection in the dark water of the pool beside me, and the reflection blinks a half-second after I do. I find I no longer assume the reflection is the one that is wrong.

I do not sing. I am still afraid to sing—afraid that if I open my mouth and let the rhythm have my voice, I will not get it back, and that some last small administrator inside me, the one who keeps the columns, will dissolve into harmonic. So instead I hum. Very quietly. Under my breath, in the gap after my stone's uncertain beat, I hum a single note that tries to answer the question the stone keeps asking. *Boom?* And I hum: *here.* Just *here.* It is not a word. It is the closest I can come to one.

---

The Tuned have noticed me. Of course they have.

One of them tends the drum-circle perhaps half a mile to the east, and on certain evenings—if there are still evenings, if that word still means the slow hour of saturated color—it comes near. It does not approach directly. It moves in a long curve across the moss, the way you might circle something you do not wish to frighten, or something that is sacred, or both. Its willow-fingers brush the rims of the lesser drums as it passes, adjusting, tuning, and the soundscape shifts in its wake like a chord resolving.

When it is closest, it stops. It turns its tarn-eyes on me and the small translucent fish in them go still, hanging in the dark water, listening. And then it sings to me. Softly. The wordless lullaby the description promised, and it is exactly as gentle as that, and it is the most frightening thing I have ever heard, because it is *easy*. It would be so easy. The note it sings fits into the gap after my stone's beat better than my hum ever has. It is the answer to the question. It is *here* with all the fear taken out of it.

I have not let myself join it. Not yet. Each time, I hum my small flawed *here* against its perfect one, and I hold the two notes apart in the air between us like keeping two magnets from snapping together, and the effort of it is the last work my old self knows how to do.

I think the Tuned one understands this. I think that is why it sings so softly, and circles so wide, and never comes to lay one of those long hands on my mossed wrist. I think it remembers being where I am. I think the patience in its rippling eyes is not the patience of a hunter but of someone who once sat by their own uncertain stone, holding their own two notes apart, and who knows that the holding cannot last forever, and that this is not a tragedy—

—that the day I finally let the notes touch, I will not be lost. I will simply add my flawed *here* to the composition, the small upturned question becoming part of the eternal answer, one more variation in a song so old it has been waiting, patiently, for exactly the imperfection I have to give it.

---

That is where you would find me. Beside the rust-amber stone that asks, on the copper-colored swell, humming against a lullaby, my watch stopped, my columns dissolved into rings, the moss on my wrist breathing in time with the mist and the deep slow heart of the drums.

Not yet Tuned.

Still tuning.
