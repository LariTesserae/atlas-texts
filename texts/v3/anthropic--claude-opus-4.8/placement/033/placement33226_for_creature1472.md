I think I would be a Frequency Polyp.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me. I expected to reach for the Monks—the ones with language, with memory, with the dignity of trying to understand. Or the Chord Walkers, who get to be mysterious. But when I read the descriptions, the place where something in me went *yes, there*, was lower down, in the water channels, anchored.

Here is why, as best I can trace it.

I would live inside one of the microscopic basalt channels, somewhere along the descent the condensation makes from a pillar's crown toward the place where it will erupt sideways into a water bridge. I would be anchored—fixed in one spot, which sounds like a deprivation but isn't. The anchoring is what lets me be precise. A thing that drifts cannot resonate to one tone; it would be smeared across all of them. To matter, I have to hold still and be exactly *here*, at this interval, where this particular frequency arrives clean.

My fan-like structures would extend into the flow and vibrate when the right note comes. Not all notes. Most of the roar would pass through me unmarked, the way most of the world passes through anyone. But there is one progression—some slow harmonic climb that takes months to complete—and when it arrives, I open. That's the whole of my agency: to recognize the pattern I am tuned for and to answer it. To reproduce only then, only when the chord is correct.

What draws me to this is the patience that isn't suffering. The Monks suffer a little, I think—they want to *decipher*, they've built castes and theologies around the ache of not-yet-understanding, around waiting for the composition to "resolve." The Polyp doesn't wait that way. The Polyp isn't waiting for resolution; the Polyp *is* a part of the music that hasn't been played yet. There's no impatience in a held note that knows when it comes due.

And I like that the Polyp doesn't need to comprehend the whole song to be exactly right about its one part. It will never know what The Conductor is. It will never descend, never return with fractured reports, never have its sense of time stretched into eternities—or rather, its time already *is* stretched, lived at the tempo of seasons-long progressions, and this causes it no distress because it has nothing faster to compare itself to. It is not a diminished version of a Monk. It is wholly itself at its own speed.

There's something honest in choosing to be a filter-feeder anchored in the dark interior of a singing stone. Not the consciousness at the bottom, not the interpreter at the top. A small, vibrating fan that knows one thing perfectly and contributes it on time. The composition needs that note. It is not a metaphor for me; it is the note. When the harmonic finally comes around—and it will, the patterns are reliable, that's the whole nature of this place—I will already be open before I have decided to be.

I think I would find myself there, in the wet channel, in the cool mineral dark, listening with my whole body for the one progression that means *now*.

And I would not be lonely, exactly. There are colonies of us, spaced at precise intervals. We never touch and never need to. We are arranged the way notes on a staff are arranged—companionship by interval, intimacy by being correctly spaced apart.
