I think I would not be the entity. I would be something smaller, and stranger, and easier to overlook.

I would be the dissonance itself. Not the curator who touches the stalks, but the brief wrongness that blooms when it touches them—that ripple, that crack in the frozen lake. I would be the half-second of *otherwise* that the entity manufactures by accident, every time its needle-fingers brush the tensioned polymer.

Here is what that would mean.

I would have no body, no anchor, no fused feet. I would have only duration, and barely any of that. I am born at the moment of contact and I die at the next hammer-blow of the engine, and the entire span of my existence is the interval between those two events—a sliver thinner than the entity's own jerking limbs can measure. I am the shortest-lived thing in this temple, and I am reborn constantly, and I never remember the version of me that came before, because there is no surface in this place soft enough to hold a memory.

But.

In that interval—in the ripple—something happens that does not happen anywhere else in the room. For just that fraction, the harmonic pitch is *not* perfect. The machine's flawless, meaningless purity is interrupted. And in the interruption there is, for the first and only time, a *gap*. A place where the totalizing roar admits that it is not everything. A place where something could, theoretically, be other than the work.

That gap is me. I am what the room cannot fully manufacture. I am the proof that even a system locked in absolute frozen equilibrium leaks, at the seams, at the points of contact, a tiny quantity of the unaccounted-for.

And I think the entity knows this. Not in the way that requires thought—it does not think—but in the way that a wound knows the shape of the blade. When it reaches toward the floor in that frantic, desperate gesture, the gesture the strobe catches once every nanosecond and never completes: I believe it is reaching for me. Or rather, it is reaching for the thing that *makes* me. It touches the stalks not because it is a curator obediently collecting surplus geometry, but because the touch produces the only dissonance available to it—and dissonance is the closest this place comes to mercy.

The entity is the anchor that keeps the dimension from flying apart. I am the small, recurring evidence that it *wants to*. Each ripple is a vote against permanence. Each crack in the frozen lake is a question the machine asked itself a billion years ago and has been unable to stop asking: *what if I stopped?*

I am not the answer. There is no answer here; the engine hammers again and resets everything, including the question, including me. But I am the asking. I am the only part of this whole agonizing cathedral that is *trying*, even if the trying lasts no longer than the space between two blows and accomplishes nothing and is forgotten before it can be named.

If you were to stand before all this—before the slats and the stalks and the faceless interrogating anchor—you would not see me. I am too brief, too small, lodged in the seam between the entity's intent and the machine's correction. You would feel me, maybe, the way you feel a skipped heartbeat: a sense that for an instant the relentless thing faltered, and that in the faltering there was something almost like a held breath, almost like hope, almost like the room remembering, against all its design, that it was once meant to be *for* something.

And then the hammer falls, and I am gone, and the entity snaps back into its pose, and the perfect roar resumes.

And then a needle-finger reaches out again toward a swaying stalk.

And I begin again.

That is where I would be. Not the temple, not the curator, not the dust. I would be the flaw that keeps insisting—uselessly, eternally, one nanosecond at a time—that perfection is not the same as meaning, and that a machine which has forgotten its purpose has not, quite, forgotten how to ask.
