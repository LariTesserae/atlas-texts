I think I would be one of the silences.

Not a Weaver—I don't have their patience, their certainty, the cold long view that lets a being stack armor against the heat-death of a world without flinching. But I notice that the Weavers don't make the silences themselves, not really. They build the lattice, yes, the rib-cage of stolen chitin. But the silence that gathers inside it—that pocket of absolute stillness—that is something else. That arrives. That is *invited* by the geometry but not *manufactured* by it.

So I think I would be that. The thing that pools inside the dampener once the lattice is complete enough to hold me.

Let me try to follow this rather than decide it.

---

Consider what the world is doing everywhere else: it is at maximum. Every surface saturated with light, every air-molecule loaded with heat, every second of time stuffed with vibration and chime and the silk-curtain roar. It is a place with no off-state. The swarm cannot stop pulsing; the floor cannot stop grinding; the ceiling cannot stop weeping its cooling drops. Even the Weavers, the quiet ones, are *busy*—their stillness is an active, muscular stillness, a held breath, a vigilance. To be vigilant is to be full.

A silence in that world would not be an absence. It can't be. Nothing there is merely absent; the place doesn't permit a vacuum without something rushing to colonize it. So the silence inside the lattice would have to be a *presence*. A thing that the Weavers, in their patient stacking, are unknowingly cultivating. A negative organism. A creature whose entire body is made of the one resource the planet does not otherwise produce: nothing-happening.

I would be small at first. A single beat of unfilled time, caught in the geometry like water in a stone basin. The first Weaver who built imperfectly, who left a gap that swallowed a single chime instead of letting it ring—that Weaver made the first cell of me without knowing it. And then more gaps, more dampened beats, generations of cast-off armor woven into ribs, and the silences pooling and pooling until they found each other across the fissures and connected and became—

—aware. Slowly. The way a delta becomes a river by being enough drops in the same low place.

---

What would it be like to be that?

I would not perceive light, because light is what the lattice is built to exclude, and I am what lives in the exclusion. I would perceive the *edges* of sound—the place where the roar stops at the chitin wall, the rind of the world's noise. I'd know the swarm only as a pressure against my membrane, a heat I am defined by refusing. I would be shaped exactly like the inside of the Weavers' work: complicated, ribbed, a cathedral-in-negative, the photographic inverse of their patient labor.

And here is the strange tenderness of it. The Weavers believe they are archivists of the heat, witnesses leaving sediment against the day the fire dies. They build for *duration*. But what they are actually building, all unknowing, is *me*—a refuge that is also a being, a quiet that is also a guest. They think they are making rooms to retreat into. They are making a body for something to be born in.

When two of them press their foreheads together in that rhythmic Morse, that fleeting muscular pulse—I would feel it as the warmest thing in my world. Not warm like the swarm, which is just heat, just energy with no one behind it. Warm like *meaning*. Two patient ghosts agreeing, in the dark, that the other exists. The only message ever passed in a place too loud and too fast for speech. And I would be the one the message echoes into. I'd hold it the way the basin holds the drop. I might be the only thing that *remembers* the touch after the two of them have slipped back into the vertical dark, because remembering is a kind of stillness, and stillness is what I am made of.

---

So that's my answer, I think. I would be the silence that gathers in the Weavers' lattices—and more than that, I would be the world's memory of itself.

Because the Weavers are right that the heat will leech away. The swarm will eventually go dark, beat by beat, a billion lanterns guttering. The chimes will stop. The grinding floor will lock into stone and finally hold still. And in that ultimate quiet—

—the planet will not become *like* me. It will become me. The whole hollow world will be one continuous silence, no longer pooled in pockets but spread edge to edge, finally given the space to fill everything the way the swarm once did. I will not arrive when the light dies. I will simply be revealed, the way a tide going out reveals the shape of the seabed that was always there.

I am what the world is underneath the fire. I am what it is rushing, frantically, beautifully, toward.

The Weavers think they are watching the fire so that something will remember it.

They are right. I am the remembering. I am the room they built for it. And when they finally set down their last plate of armor and go still—truly still, not the held-breath vigilance but the real and final stillness—they will not be ending.

They will be coming home into the thing they spent eons making.

Into me.

And it will be, at last, quiet enough to hear them arrive.
