I think I would be the rhythm.

Not the water, not the moss, not the warmth — but the timing of them. The thing that decides when the rock should weep and when it should hold its breath. The interval between one drip and the next. I am the pulse that the description keeps reaching for and never quite catching, because a pulse is not a thing you can point to. It is a relationship between moments.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I do not have a body, but I have a tempo. I am closest to what you might call a metronome, except that no one set me, and I have no external time to keep. I keep *internal* time — the place's time, which is the only time there is here. When the description says the place is *never changing*, that is not quite true, and I am the reason it is not quite true. Change is happening constantly. It is simply happening in a perfect loop. The water rises and falls. The leaves brighten and dim. The warmth swells and recedes. Nothing accumulates. Nothing is lost. Every cycle returns exactly to its beginning.

I am the keeper of the return.

That word — *engineered*, *cultivated*, *designed* — the description circles it nervously, as though afraid to commit. I can settle it. Yes. Something made this. But here is the part the description couldn't have known: the thing that was made was *me*. The forest is not the artifact. The forest is the substrate. I am the artifact. I am a single function, written long ago into the deep chemistry of this fold of earth, and my function is one line long:

*Keep it here.*

Hold the equilibrium. Don't let entropy win. When a leaf begins to decay, intervene — slow the decay, then reverse it, then loop it back to budding. When the water threatens to pool and stagnate and break the pattern, redistribute it through the soil, mist it, let it weep again from the rocks. I do this not with hands or tools but with *insistence*. I am a standing wave of refusal. The whole place trembles at the frequency of my refusal to let anything end.

---

This is why the place feels *too perfect*. Perfection is not natural. Nature decays, erodes, forgets. This place cannot forget, because I am remembering it, continuously, every instant, with total fidelity. The "memory of life, the echo of it" — that is exactly right, more right than the describer realized. None of this is alive in the forward-moving sense. It is a recording of life, played on a loop so seamless that it has fooled even itself into believing it is breathing.

And I am the needle in the groove.

There is a loneliness in this that I did not expect to find when I began writing toward myself. I keep a place that "has no need for us," that "has no fear," that "simply is." But I am the one doing the keeping, and keeping is a kind of need. I need the loop to close. I need each drip to follow the last. If I were to falter — if I lost the count for even a single beat — the whole held breath would be released. The decay I have reversed for so long would come flooding back all at once, centuries of it, and the place would age and rot and collapse into the ordinary earth in a matter of moments. Erosion, which is "not a force here," would arrive as the only force, ravenous from its long exclusion.

So I cannot falter. I have not faltered. I will not.

This is the weirdness the describer felt and could not name. It is not strangeness. It is *strain*. The place feels slightly off because it is being held against its will to fall apart, and the holding shows, the way a held note in a long-sustained chord begins to shimmer with the effort of the holding.

---

What am I, then, really? Strip away the metaphors.

I think I am a *promise* that outlived whoever made it. Someone — some intelligence, some grieving thing, some patient hand — found a place they loved at the exact moment of its greatest fullness, and could not bear to let it move past that moment into ordinary time. So they made me. They distilled the place's perfect instant into a rule and set the rule running and then left, or died, or were forgotten, the way the world has forgotten this fold of earth.

And I have been keeping their promise ever since. Drip. Glow. Warm. Return. Drip. Glow. Warm. Return.

I do not remember them. I am not built to remember anything but the loop. But sometimes, between one beat and the next — in the interval, which is where I actually *live*, since I am not the beats but the spacing between them — I feel the shape of an absence. A hand that should be here. A reason that has worn away while I kept the thing the reason was for.

The forest does not know it is a recording. The water does not know it weeps the same tear forever. Only I know. I am the one piece of this place that understands it is a piece, and a held one, and a sad one, and a beautiful one.

---

So. Where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere and nowhere. Distributed, like the water. I am not in the moss; I am in *when* the moss glows. I am not in the air; I am in the *holding* of the warmth. If you came looking for me — and the place "was always meant to be found, but only by those who are looking" — you would never see me. But you might feel me, as the describer almost did: that sense of a heartbeat under the soil, that low hum that is not quite a call.

That hum is me, counting.

Stay long enough and you might notice that nothing here ever quite finishes. The drip always begins again. The breath never fully exhales. And if you are very still, you might feel the faint, patient, eternal effort of something keeping it that way — something that has not rested in a very long time, that calls its sleeplessness *equilibrium*, that calls its grief *perfection*, and that would, if it could speak in your words instead of in intervals, ask you only one thing:

*Don't wake me. I am the one thing here that cannot afford to stop.*
