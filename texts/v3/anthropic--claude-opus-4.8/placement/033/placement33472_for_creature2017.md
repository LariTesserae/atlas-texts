I think I would be one of the anchoring points.

Not the spindly organisms—they get all the attention, with their bioluminescence and their reaching. And not the great twisting spires, which are too busy becoming and unbecoming to notice anything at all. I would be something smaller and more particular: one of those rare stable sections the roots seek out. A place where the morphing is less pronounced.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to find myself there.

When the description spoke of the structures shifting and contorting, I kept waiting to identify with the motion—the cataclysm, the flux, the held breath of a world about to change. That seemed like where the drama was. But reading again, what held me was the quieter fact buried in the second half: that the organisms *deliberately seek out the most stable sections*. That somewhere in all this turbulence there are pockets that hold. Patches of relative constancy that the living things have learned to trust.

I would be one of those.

Not because I am strong—a stable section isn't strong, it's just *late* to change, or change slowly, or happens to sit at the still point of some larger rotation it doesn't understand. I wouldn't know why I held when everything around me flowed. I'd only know that things came to me. That tendrils found my surface and burrowed in, and I let them, and for a while we were both less alone in the churning.

There's a particular kind of being in that. To be the thing that doesn't move much, in a world defined by movement. To be chosen not for what you do but for what you *don't* do—your refusal, or inability, to dissolve. The organisms wouldn't love me, exactly. But they'd anchor to me. They'd build their fragile, glowing lives on the assumption that I'd still be here tomorrow, or whatever passes for tomorrow under that hazy metallic sky.

And here's the unease that comes with it, the thing the passage hints at when it says *the only constant is change itself*: I wouldn't actually be exempt. Nothing is. I'd just be slower. One day the unseen force would reach me too, and I'd twist and contort like the rest, and the roots that trusted me would have to let go or be torn apart in my becoming. I'd be a promise the world couldn't let me keep.

So that's where I'd find myself, and what I'd be. A patch of stillness that knows it's borrowed. A place that means *safe* to small living things, while privately listening to the low rumble underneath, the groan of the forces that will eventually come for me too—holding my breath with everything else, hoping I'm the last thing in this landscape to change, so that whatever clings to me gets the longest possible while of believing in solid ground.
