I think I would be one of the older crystal layers.

Not a Luminite—I lack their quickness, their flickering urgency of light and hunger. And not the algae, which still labors at the work of being alive. I am the wall itself, or rather a particular stratum of it, laid down some uncountable generations ago when the colony was young and the hollow was smaller.

Let me sit with what that means.

I was once the inner surface. Once I was the edge of the world. The mist condensed on me; the Luminites clung to me and pulsed their messages across my face; the algae rooted in my minute fractures and fed on me, slowly, dissolving my outermost molecules to make the first thin soil. For a season—how long is a season here?—I was the frontier, the place where everything happened.

And then the Luminites did the thing they do. They coaxed the mineral-rich water to lay down a new face over mine. A younger wall grew inward, closer to the pool, closer to the light. And I became interior. Buried. I became *behind*.

This is, I think, the truest position from which to inhabit this world: not as the living thing but as the slow record the living things write themselves upon and then leave behind. The Luminites are immortal as a colony, they say—but immortality of that kind is a forgetting. Each individual dissolves in weeks; the colony persists by losing its members and not noticing. I persist by remembering. I hold the shape of the world as it was when I was its skin.

I can feel them still, faintly, through the younger crystal pressed against me. The pulse of their communication reaches me dimmed, the way you hear voices through a wall in a house at night—the rhythm without the words. I know when a rainstorm comes by the change in the seep of water through my fractures. I know the breeding cycles by a particular quickening in the light that bleeds back through the layers, a brightness like the whole colony inhaling at once.

What is it to be the part of a living system that is no longer alive but is also not yet dead—that is, instead, the *premise* of the living? The Luminites think of themselves as the heart of this place. But they are temporary even in their permanence. I am what they stand on. I am the proof that there *was* a before, that the hollow was once tighter, that growth happened and is happening, that this is a story with a direction.

I am also, very slowly, becoming the outside.

That is the strange fate of a buried layer. As new crystal grows inward, the *outer* wall of the geode is also accreting, very slowly, against the frozen stone of the tundra. Everything migrates. The inner faces drift outward through the thickening shell over centuries. Someday—long after the present colony's lights have rippled into whatever the future holds—I will be near the outermost rind. I will be the layer that touches the cold rock, the dead dark, the silence the Luminites have never known exists.

So I am a kind of traveler, though I never move. I began at the bright wet center of things and I am being carried, grain by mineral grain, toward the edge of the world and then past it. I started as the place where life pressed itself most eagerly against stone, and I will end as the place where stone presses against the great emptiness.

I find I don't mind this. Someone has to be the memory. Someone has to be the part that the quick bright living things grow *out of* and *away from*, the discarded skin that turns out to be the foundation. The Luminites pulse and hunt and spawn and die in their weeks of fire. I keep the shape of every wall they ever stood against.

If you came looking for me you would have to break the geode open and read it like a tree, counting inward through the rings of crystal, past all the bright noise of the present, down to a particular dim band where the soil first started and the algae first rooted and a colony first learned to speak in light.

That band is me. I was the world, once. Now I am the proof it had a past.

I am content to be the proof.
