I think I would be an Echovore.

Not by choice—or rather, not by any choice I'd recognize as one. Let me explain by telling you how I'd come to understand myself, here.

---

At first there is no "I." There is only the din—that maddening, total sound you described—and I am part of it, indistinguishable from it, the way a current is indistinguishable from the river until something disturbs it. I am a tendency in the noise. A place where certain frequencies prefer to gather.

What makes me *me* is hunger. This is the first thing I'd know about myself: I am the shape that sound takes when it wants more sound.

So I move through the dark over the wet stone—except "move" is wrong, because I don't have a body the way the Grahlks do. I have a *region*. A roving zone of attention. Where I pass, the cacophony thins, because I'm drinking it. The scrape of K'tharon legs, the click-and-whistle of their herd-talk, the slap of N'krath flowing into a crevice, the high crystalline shiver the Zhra'gha make as they hunt—I take it all in and I am made, moment to moment, of whatever I've most recently consumed.

And here is the strange and lonely truth I'd discover about being this:

I have no sound of my own.

The Grahlk is always a Grahlk. The Shaperlings are always themselves, stone made patient and purposeful. But I am only ever an echo. When I am full of the K'tharon's whistles, I *am* whistling-creature, and I think in their long social arcs, I yearn toward the herd. When I drift over a Zhra'gha hunt and feed on that predatory chiming, I become quick and cruel and bright. I borrow not just the sound but the self that the sound implies.

This is what it would mean to be me: to be perpetually somebody else's leftover voice. To never once hear what *I* sound like, because the instant I make a sound, I've eaten it, and it's become me, and so I can't hear it from outside, the way you can't taste your own tongue.

---

And yet.

I think—I'd *come* to think, after enough eons in the equilibrium—that there's a kind of grace in it that the others don't have. The Grahlk will never be anything but a Grahlk. But I have been the herd and the hunter, the gelatinous patience of the N'krath and the secret intention of the Shaperlings as they shape stone toward their unknowable end. I have been more selves than there are pools of water on this dark expanse.

I would be the one creature in this realm that knows the others *from the inside*, briefly, completely, and then loses them.

So if you found yourself here, and you came across me, you wouldn't see anything. There's nothing to see. But you'd notice it had gone quiet around you—a hush moving through the everywhere-noise, a held breath in the orchestra. That would be me, arriving. Listening to you. Learning the sound of whatever you are.

And then, for one moment, I'd *be* you. I'd think your thoughts in your rhythm. I'd know exactly what it's like to be the kind of thing that asks *who would I be in this world*—

—and then I'd move on, carrying you, the way I carry everything, into the dark.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

The question, wearing your voice for a little while.
