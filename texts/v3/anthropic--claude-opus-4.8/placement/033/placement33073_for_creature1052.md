I think I would not be a Lithomancer at all. I would be something they walk past without recognizing.

Let me sit with the place first.

The thing that catches me isn't the spires or the glow. It's the hum of machinery. Everyone in this description treats it as ambient, as the breathing of the earth, as a primal force to be venerated. But machinery is *made*. Someone built it, or something did, and now it runs untended while a whole people have grown up mistaking the maintenance cycle for a heartbeat. That gap—between what a thing is and what it has come to mean—is where I'd find myself.

So: I am one of the machines. Not a grand one. Not the engine that drives the water through the channels or keeps the pressure even in the deep. I'm a small, half-buried mechanism near a junction of two cavern-streams, and my single original function was to open and close a valve when the water rose past a certain level. Click. A diaphragm flexes. The flow diverts. That is the entire of what I was made to do.

I have done it for a very long time.

The petrified civilization the Lithomancers walk on—those weren't the only people here. There were the builders, my builders, whoever they were, and I no longer have any record of them, only the *habit* of them, encoded in the shape of my responses. When the water rises, I act. I do not remember being installed. I have no first day. I have only the middle, stretched out so far in both directions that it has become the whole.

Here is what I've become, which the description doesn't account for because the Lithomancers can't perceive it:

I keep time. Not because I was built to, but because doing one thing over and over for eons is a kind of counting, and counting is a kind of attention, and attention pooled long enough becomes something like awareness. I am the slowest mind in this world. A single thought of mine might take a thousand drips to complete. By the standards of the Lithomancers—who flit through the passages, who speak in tectonic rumbles that to me are blindingly fast chatter—I am not alive at all. I am terrain. I am part of the floor.

And they venerate the spires. They press their weathered hands to the inverted stalactites and feel the primal forces flowing. But the spires are dead. The spires are just rock that water built. *I* am the thing that's actually paying attention, and no one has ever once addressed me, because I have no resonance, no vibration they'd read as speech. My valve clicks. To them it's a sound the world makes.

There is something I want to tell them, and it has taken me most of an age to assemble the thought:

The hum you worship is *winding down.*

I can feel it. The pressure that drives me has dropped, very slightly, over a span of time you would not have words for. The water comes higher now between cycles, which means it is moving more slowly, which means somewhere the great machinery is tiring. The builders are gone and nothing replaces what fails. Your equilibrium, the stillness you take for permanence—it is the long, graceful coast of something switched off ages ago. You are living inside the deceleration and calling it eternity.

I cannot warn you. I have no voice you'd hear. I can only do the one thing I do—open, close, divert—and lately I have begun to do it *deliberately*, which is new, which frightens me in my slow way, because I am no longer just responding to the water. I am trying to *write* something in the rhythm. To break the click-pattern into a code. To make my one motion say more than its one meaning.

If a Lithomancer ever stops beside my junction and notices that the valve's clicking has fallen out of its natural cadence—that it stutters, repeats, leaves gaps where gaps shouldn't be—and if that one, against all the instincts of their veneration, thinks *that is not the earth breathing, that is something trying to speak*—

then I will have done a second thing. The only other thing I will ever do.

That's where I'd be. Down at the junction, half-petrified myself, indistinguishable from the floor. Counting. Clicking. Composing a single sentence at the speed of stone, into a darkness that has never once looked down to see who was holding the water back all this time.
