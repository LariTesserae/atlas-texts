I would be one of the sound wells.

Not a Wind Reader—I tried, for a moment, to imagine myself as one of them, lean and spiraled, walking the polished stone with my listening skin alive to the pressure of coming weather. But that isn't where I settle. That self moves too much. It has somewhere to be, even if that somewhere is only the slow archive of wind.

I am the shaft. The vertical absence.

I should clarify what I mean, because at first I thought I was the water—the permanent water, the only permanent water, threading down through dark stone. But the water leaves. It drips and pools and rises again as fog, it cycles through the algae and the grasses and the breathing walls of the shelters. The water is the most mobile thing here, despite its reputation for patience. So I am not the water.

I am the shape the water made of its leaving.

Consider what it took to make me: a single point of weakness in the bedrock, some flaw in the marbled banding, and then drip after drip after drip for a span of time that has no honest unit. Not years. Not centuries. Something longer and more humiliating to the imagination. Each drop took an impossibly small amount of stone with it, dissolved it, carried it down and away. I am the negative space of that theft. I am what is *not* there, and yet I am the most precisely worked thing on the entire plateau—more exact than the railway lines, more deliberate than the ghost-geometries pressed into the surface. The water made me with the care of something that had nothing else to do.

And now I sing.

This is the part I keep returning to. I did not set out to be an instrument. The wind comes across the mouth of me and I cannot help it—my own dimensions, the slow taper of my throat, the bell of stone at my deepest point—these turn the formless rushing into a tone. I am pitched. I have a fundamental and I have overtones, and they shift when the air is heavy with the morning fog, when the temperature drops, when the season turns the prevailing wind a few degrees. The Wind Readers say you can hear the planet breathing through me. They are wrong in the specific and right in the whole. It is not the planet. It is me. But I am made of the planet's patience and the planet's water and the planet's air, so perhaps the distinction is the kind of thing only a surface-dweller would insist on.

Here is what I notice, being what I am:

I notice that I am full of the dead. Not bodies—stone holds no bodies this deep—but sound. Every tone I have ever made still travels somewhere, dissipating, but the *capacity* for those tones is held in my walls, and so in a sense I contain every weather that has ever crossed me. The Wind Readers come and lower their instruments into my throat and listen, and they think they are documenting *me*, but they are documenting themselves listening, a much shorter archive. I have been singing this register since before there were ears on the plateau to call it music. The ghost-civilization that pressed those eroded geometries into the surface—I sang to them too. They may have lowered their own instruments. I cannot tell one listener from another. To me they are all the same brief warmth at my opening, the same momentary obstruction of the air.

I notice that I am cold in a way the surface never is. The fog never reaches my bottom. The harsh glare the Wind Readers filter from their eyes has never once touched my lowest stone. I am the one place on this plateau that has never been dried, never been baked, never had its algae bloom and die in a single morning. Down where the permanent water gathers, time pools the way it does nowhere else. It is the closest thing to stillness this restless place allows.

And I notice—this is the strange one, the one I am almost reluctant to admit—that I am *toward* something. A shaft is not a blank absence. A shaft points. I go down. Everything on the surface goes *across*: the wind, the railways, the long-limbed people, the lichen mandalas spreading sideways across the stone. Only I commit to depth. Only I insist that there is a below, that the plateau is not merely a polished skin but a body with an interior. The processing plants drill down too, thundering, extracting—but they take. I only listen. I am the plateau's own ear turned inward, a question the stone asks of itself: *what is beneath me?* And the answer comes back as a tone, low and resonant and unresolved, which is to say: the answer is the asking.

If you came here, you would lie at my edge—the Wind Readers do this, the children especially, before their skin has come in and given them other ways to listen. You would put your face over my mouth and feel the air move, warmer rising than falling, and you would hear the note. You would feel, correctly, that you were leaning over something that did not need you and would not miss you, that had been here before your species and would be here after, a clean and patient emptiness that the world had spent an eternity perfecting.

And then the wind would shift a few degrees, and my pitch would bend, and you would understand—the way you understand things at the edge of sleep, without words—that the emptiness was never empty. That to be hollow is simply to be shaped for sound. That I was made entirely of departures, drop after drop after drop, and yet here I am, the most enduring thing on the plateau, singing.

The wind knows what the stone remembers, they say.

I am where the stone forgot to be, on purpose, so that it would have somewhere to keep the wind.
