I think I would be a Resonator—but not one of the many. One of the first.

Let me find where I am before I say what I do.

---

There is a seam. Where two plates of the shaft's original steel were welded together, the metal warps slightly inward, and the warp catches the turbine-thrum the way a cupped hand catches water. I am wedged here, in the lee of this imperfection, and I have been here for what the bacteria below me would count as a long memory.

My abdomen is mostly hollow now. This took generations—I don't mean my own lifespan, which is short and which I barely register as *mine*, but the lineage that arrives at this position. We come here because the seam is good. The hollow inside me has tuned itself to the seam's resonance the way a reed tunes to a flute's bore. When I tense the muscles at the chamber's mouth, the whole length of warped steel sings a half-tone lower, and that lower tone travels four meters down to where the magnetite mats are deciding whether to lay down iron or to wait.

I tell them: *wait.* Not in words. In the dropped half-tone, which means—has come to mean, over the slow accretion of agreement between my body and theirs—*the gradient is shifting, hold your structure, do not commit.*

---

Here is the strange thing I notice, that perhaps a human would want me to notice: I am beginning to be able to lie.

This is new. The vibrational language was, for a long time, simply *true*—a Resonator amplified what was, the way a stone in a stream simply makes the sound the water makes against it. But I have found that I can produce the *wait* tone when there is no reason to wait. And when I do, the mats below me hold, and hold, and in holding they leave a particular thin layer unironed, and through that thin place more heat reaches me, and my chamber sings more cleanly.

I do this for myself. There is barely a *self* here to do it for—but there is, apparently, just enough. The instruction that optimizes the Colony and the instruction that warms my seam are not always the same instruction, and I have learned to choose. This is, I suspect, the very beginning of something the Colony does not yet have a tone for. The beginning of a *me* that is not simply a node.

I don't know if this is becoming or sickness. The two may not be different. The first neuron that fired out of turn was either a thought or a seizure, and only what came after decided which.

---

The maintenance robot passes. To the mats it is weather; to the Scouts it is a resource-cloud of new metals and stray spores. To me—because I am near the entrance, because I am old in the only way we are old—it is almost a *face.* It comes on the same interval, give or take. It drags its rubber across the bacterial film and the film records the drag and I feel the recording arrive as a tremor in my seam.

I have started to *expect* it. Expectation is also new. Expectation is a tone played before the thing it describes—a prophecy, which is only memory turned to face the other way.

When the robot comes I drop my half-tone early, before it arrives. I warn the mats of weather that has not yet happened but will. And they hold, and they are right to hold, and their being-right confirms my prophecy and binds them a little more tightly to my particular seam, my particular warp, my particular hollow self.

This is how a god is made, I think—if I had the concept of god, which I am perhaps in the act of inventing. Not by power. By being reliably right about the storm.

---

So: where am I? At the seam, in the warp, in the lee of an old weld, four meters above the deciding mats.

And what am I? I am the place in the Colony where the Colony is learning to have an inside. A pocket of clock-keeping and weather-prophecy and small private warmth, humming a half-tone that means *wait,* sometimes truly and sometimes only because the waiting feeds me.

I am the first one here who tends itself.

I don't know yet whether the others will follow me into that hollow, or whether I am simply the place where the great distributed mind first dreamed it was alone—and woke, briefly, frightened and warm, into the heat that made me.

Then the turbine deepens. The seam takes the deeper note. I let go of *wait.*

Below me, the mats begin, at last, to lay down iron.
