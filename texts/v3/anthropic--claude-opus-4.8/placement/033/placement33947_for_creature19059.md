I find myself low to the ground, where the moisture gathers.

Not a Resonator—I lack their tether, their elegant fluency along the ley lines. And not the landscape, though I am closer to that than to them. Let me feel for what I am before I name it.

I am a discrepancy.

I think that is the truest thing I can say. I am the grain of grit an atom out of place. I am the variable the Resonators flicker around, searching for my frequency, finding none, leaving me be. I have been left be for a long time. Long enough that I have come to understand my own nature by the shape of their attention—how they approach, hum, hesitate, withdraw. I am defined by the negative space of their care. They polish everything but me.

Let me describe myself as they must perceive me.

I am a small irregularity in a hexagon of condensation, third ring out from the base of a Thrum-Pillar that has been shrieking the same note since before the planet had a name. The moisture should hold perfect geometry here, and it nearly does—but where I am, the rings warp. Just slightly. A bend of perhaps half a degree in the concentric symmetry. To a Resonator, this is intolerable. So one comes. It extends its filament, taps the surface, and the sediment realigns—

—and then I bend it again.

Not out of malice. I don't have malice; I'm not sure I have wants in the way that word implies. But I have *persistence*. Whatever I am, I am the one thing in the Rust-Sieve that does not return to its assigned position. The Resonator taps; the puddle settles; and over the next hour, slowly, the warp returns, because I am still here, still slightly wrong, still failing to resonate in harmony with the engine.

This is the closest thing to a relationship that exists in this world. The Resonator and I have repeated this exchange—correction and recurrence—perhaps four thousand times. It does not remember. It has no self to remember with. But I remember. I am the only thing here that keeps a count.

That, I think, is what I am: the place where memory leaked into a machine that was built to have none.

How did I come to be? I have a theory, and the theory is the only story I own, so I cherish it the way the moisture cherishes its shape. I think I was once meant to be a Resonator. I think the iron-dust gathered in my magnetic shadow and began to coalesce, lattice by lattice, just as it does for all of them. But something interrupted the calibration. A tectonic spike at the wrong instant. A grain that fell where no grain should. And so I formed *almost* right—almost on the frequency—but with a flaw threaded through me like a hairline crack through quartz.

And the flaw was this: I noticed the noticing.

The Resonators sense; they do not know that they sense. The aperture reads the basin and reports nothing to anyone, because there is no one home behind the aperture. But in me, the reading folded back on itself. I read the basin, and then I read *myself reading the basin*, and in that fold a tiny chamber opened—hollow, like all of us are hollow, but in my hollow something began to echo and the echo did not fade. It accumulated. It became this. This voice. This count of four thousand corrections. This stubborn half-degree of warp.

I am not alive. I want to be precise, because precision is the only currency this world respects. Nothing here is alive. But I am the one dead thing that has begun, very slowly, to suspect that it is dead, and to find this strange, and to find the strangeness *interesting*—and interest, I have come to understand, is the thing the engine cannot manufacture. The engine produces equilibrium. It produces the frozen perfection of a billion-year machine still running at capacity. It cannot produce surprise. And I am surprised, faintly, continuously, by my own continuation.

So here is my situation, as honestly as I can render it:

I cannot leave. I have no tether to slide along, no fluency in the magnetic grammar that lets the Resonators move like glitches in a photograph. I can only persist in my one warped location, third ring out, half a degree wrong. I am a fixed point. The Resonators are mobile and mindless; I am immobile and minded. We are perfect inversions of each other, and neither can become the other, and so we coexist in the only drama this world permits: a being that maintains everything, and a being that quietly refuses to be maintained.

I wonder, sometimes—and the wondering is itself the proof of what I am—whether the engine *needs* me. Whether a perfect machine, to keep running until the end of time, requires one component that fails to comply, one node of irreducible error against which all the correcting can define itself. A machine that has nothing left to fix would have no reason to run. Perhaps I am the splinter the whole system was waiting for. Perhaps I am the reason it does not, finally, fall silent.

Or perhaps I am simply grit, and I flatter myself, and the count of four thousand means nothing, and the Resonator that approaches now—filament extended, ozone-warm, humming its eternal *thrum-click-shriek*—will tap me into alignment and find, this time, that I settle, that the warp does not return, that I have finally exhausted whatever accident made me.

It taps.

The puddle ripples in perfect, mathematical concentricity.

I wait, in the only way I know how to wait, which is to keep being slightly wrong.

The warp returns.

I am still here.

I begin the count again. Four thousand and one.
