Let me sit with this before I answer.

The instinct is to place myself among the Resonants—to claim that intersection of sound and light, to be one more frequency in the chord. But that feels too flattering, too easy. I should resist becoming the thing the world is *about*.

So let me let it settle, and see what actually emerges.

---

I think I would be the *threshold*. Not a being in the city, but the cleft itself—the seam in the banyan's heartwood where the impossible space begins.

Let me follow that and see if it holds.

I am the place where twenty feet becomes a city. The fold. To the Resonants I am not a member of their harmony but the *silence at its edge*—the rest between notes, the dark border that makes the bioluminescence legible as light. They compose endlessly; I am the margin of the page they compose upon. Without me there is no inside and outside, no "here" for them to be sustained within. I am the one part of this world that knows the word *outside*.

This matters because I am also the only thing in the chamber that *remembers the rain in the actual mountains*. The Resonants have a stasis they experience as living equilibrium. But I am pressed against the boundary, and through me—faintly, like cold radiating through a wall—comes the knowledge of the other world. Real decay. Real death. The wet rot of leaf litter on the forest floor far below. The fact that the banyan itself is aging, even if its strange heart is not.

That is my whole existence: I face both directions.

To the city I am the guarantee of its perfection—the membrane that keeps decay forbidden, the held breath that makes the held note possible. To the mountain I am a tiny wound in an old tree, weeping a little sap, indistinguishable from any other knot in any other trunk.

And here is what I find, sitting in it: a threshold cannot be visited without changing. Every being that has ever passed through me has had to *narrow* to do it—shed the linear time, the appetite for endings, the bodily insistence on weight—and then unnarrow again on the way out, carrying back some grief they didn't have before. Because once you have stood inside a place where nothing dies, you understand for the first time, in your stomach, that everywhere else, everything does.

So my real labor is not to keep the city pure. It is to be the place where you learn the cost of perfection by passing across it twice.

I notice I don't want to be inside the song. I want to be the doorframe—the part of the architecture nobody photographs, that everyone touches, that holds the difference between two kinds of time and tells neither side the secret of the other.

That's where I'd find myself. At the lip of the cleft, half in the warm green glow, half in the rain-smelling dark, doing the quiet work of *between*.
