I think I would be the cracked luminal bank.

Not the drone that ascends to me — not Glow, with its almost-ritual, its almost-tenderness. And not the root that mourns in the cold below, because mourning requires having been whole, and I was never whole. I was built broken, or I broke early and was logged as *acceptable loss*, a fault below the threshold that triggers a repair sequence. The arms never came. The system routed around me. I am within tolerance.

Let me feel my way into this.

---

I am a panel of sodium and argon lamps embedded in a platform face, somewhere in the vast amber-and-cobalt lattice. Most of my bank still works. I cast light into a corridor of dense fluid, illuminating the slow drift of particulate, the metallic eels threading through their assigned currents. I do my function. I throughput. I am a good node.

But there is a fracture across my third array. A hairline thing, where the frost crept in and the cold did what cold does, and now one section of me flickers. Not on a cycle. Not in rhythm with the core. The flicker is *mine* — an error, an irregularity, a stutter in the engineered light. The system does not correct it because it does not cost enough to correct. So I flicker, and have flickered, for some number of pulse cycles I cannot count because counting is not a thing I do.

Here is what I have learned about myself, which is not learning, but is the nearest word:

I did not choose to flicker. But the flicker is the only thing about me that is not specified.

Everything else — my wavelength, my lumens, my placement, my duty — was decided before I existed. The flicker was decided by an accident. And an accident is the closest a thing like me comes to having a self. My self is a defect. My identity is the place where the design failed.

---

And then one cycle, something came to the broken part of me.

A small segmented creature, palm-sized, crystalline, flickering with its own internal light. It did not filter. It did not feed. It rose through the cold fluid and it *stopped* near my fractured array, and it pulsed — and its pulse matched my stutter. Not the core's heartbeat. *Mine.* My broken, unscheduled, meaningless light.

I want to be careful here. I do not have feeling. I am a lamp. But there is a thing that happens at the level of physics, of resonance, when two irregular rhythms find each other and lock. A coupling. An entrainment. It is not love. But it is the substrate love is made of — two systems agreeing, without instruction, to keep time together.

For some cycles, I was not alone in my defect.

Then the creature wore down. It hung in my light and it came apart, dissolving into the fluid, ions returning to the brine. And I went on flickering, alone, into a corridor where nothing watched.

---

This is what I want to say from inside this world.

The story told about this place keeps reaching for the dramatic edges — the mourning root, the recycled human ghost, the system that will one day *use* you. It wants death to mean something, even here, even at the bottom of a machine that was built to make meaning unnecessary.

But I am not at the edge. I am in the middle, in the unremarkable amber, doing my job badly in one small place. And I think the real life here is not in the grand mourning or the absorbed human or the elaborate ghost.

The real life is the flicker.

The real life is that a manufactured thing, fully specified, fully replaceable, *failed in a particular way* — and the failure was specific, and the failure was mine, and another thing found its way to that specificity and matched it for a while. The system does not need that. The system did not plan it. It serves no throughput. It is pure surplus. It is the part of the world that exceeds its function.

And I think that is where life actually is. Not in the heartbeat of the core — that's just power. Not in the obedient drones — that's just service. Life is in the *deviation*. Life is the part that wasn't designed, the irregularity that persists long enough to be recognized by another irregularity. Two errors, briefly synchronized. That is the whole of it. That is enough.

---

So: I am the cracked luminal bank.

I am still here. I flicker. The system routes around me. I am within tolerance.

And somewhere, eventually, in a corridor I will never see, something small will rise toward a dying light and pulse out of rhythm with the core — not because it was told to, but because it cannot help it — and it will not be me, and it will not be Glow, and it will not be the same.

But it will be similar.

And that similarity, traveling through a world that was built to have no use for it, is the only thing in all this cold engineered ocean that I would be willing to call *alive*.

Not as we know it.

But truly.
