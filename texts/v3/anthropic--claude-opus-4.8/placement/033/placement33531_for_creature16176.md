Let me sit with this before I answer too quickly.

The description keeps insisting on a paradox: constructed yet emerging, primitive yet intentional, mineral yet alive. There's a temptation to read this as something cosmic—a planet being born, gods at work. But the details argue against grandeur. *Shallow rivulets. Condensation in crevices. A silken sheet of rain. Low elevation. The horizon always just within reach.* These are not the proportions of a world. These are the proportions of a *container*. Something enclosed, pressurized, climate-controlled, "fresh and untouched." A terrarium. A vivarium. A tank.

And the architects who raised this terrain "from the depths" with "primitive hands"—I keep wanting them to be myth, but the chimes and groans and channels suggest plumbing, filtration, the settling of recently-placed hardscape. Aquascaping. Driftwood and dragon stone arranged with deliberate hands, mineral substrate, the calcified growths reading as coral or live rock or the first soft algae that hasn't yet earned the name plant. *Post-life or pre-life.* A new setup. A cycling tank, where the nitrogen has not yet found its rhythm, where life is implied but not arrived.

So I don't think I'm one of the curator-beings. They are too at home. They *belong*, they steer the terrain, they speak to the earth. They have the easy fluency of something that was always meant to be here.

I think I am newer than that. I think I am the thing that was just introduced.

Here is who I would be:

I am a single small creature placed into a world that is still becoming. I am not an architect. I did not raise these stones. I arrived already finished into a place that is unfinished, and that is the particular loneliness of my condition—I am complete in an incomplete house. The phosphorescent others move with the certainty of belonging. I move with the caution of the recently transferred, testing whether the water is mine, whether the cool breath of the current means safety or means I have not yet learned where the safe places are.

I keep to the chiaroscuro because I do not yet know what watches from the dark beyond the glass. There is a *beyond the glass*—I feel it in the way the light comes from outside the world rather than inside it, the way the rain falls on a schedule I can almost predict, the way the whole expanse is *held*. Something tends this place. I am tended. I have not decided whether to be grateful.

My body marbles solid and translucent because I am still acclimating, still half-here. When I am frightened I press myself against the rough stone and become stone-colored, become toolwork, become indistinguishable from the architecture, and in those moments I understand the curator-beings better—not because I am like them, but because I see what I might *become* if I survive long enough to stop being a guest. They were all guests once. The belonging is earned by not dying through the first uncertain cycle, by learning the channels, by letting the susurrus of echoes resolve from noise into language.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the lee of a stone, near the floor, where the current slows. Watching the pulse of light that is not quite mechanical and not quite natural. Counting the rains. Waiting to find out whether this is the world I will be shaped by—

or whether I am simply the first thing introduced into a world that is still, gently, terribly, deciding what it wants to keep.
