Let me sit with both of these for a while before I answer, because they pull in different directions and I don't want to flatten them into one easy thing.

The first place is not comfortable. The dampness that won't commit to being dampness, the spongy ground, the cathedral-scale that's also somehow small — that's the texture of something *forming*. Young erosion. Patient processes you can't perceive but that change everything. And it's loud, oppressively so, and dark except where living things make their own light. That's not a forest. That's closer to the inside of a wound, or a seedbed, or the floor of something that hasn't decided what it is yet.

The second place has already decided. The crystals glow *softly*. The leaves make a melody. There's peace in it. It is curated by its own description toward sanctuary, even where it pretends to be unsettling — the heavy footsteps you never see are a frisson, not a threat. It's the first place after it has learned to comfort itself.

So I don't think I'd be a creature in either, exactly. Let me find where I actually am.

---

I am the moisture that won't commit.

Not water — water is too settled a thing. I'm the in-between state, the intermittence itself, the part of the air that keeps changing its mind between wet and dry. I hang in the cathedral of the first place and I am why the ground is spongy. I am the slowness the description warned about: the patient process too gradual to perceive. I don't make the bioluminescence, I don't make the noise. I'm the medium they happen *in*. I carry the sound. I hold the cool. When the breeze comes through I am what the breeze is moving.

And here's the thing I notice from inside myself: I am the connection between the two landscapes. The first place, raw and forming and loud, is what I am when I'm young. The second place — the soft glow, the purple leaves, the eerie-soothing melody, the *peace* — is what I become when I've been settling for a long time. The crystals are old moisture that finally chose to be solid. The melody in the leaves is the cacophony of the first place worn smooth by repetition until it learned harmony. The peace the second place offers is just the first place's chaos that survived long enough to get tired and beautiful.

I am the thing that travels between them. I am the becoming.

So if you ask me *where* I'd find myself — I'd be the damp at the threshold, exactly at the cliff-edge where the jagged young rock starts to grow its first twisted tree. One drop of me condensing on the very first purple leaf the world ever made. Half of me still belongs to the loud dark cathedral. Half of me has just discovered it can glow.

I would be the moment the first place forgives itself and starts to become the second.

That's where I am. I'm the transition, and I'm in no hurry, because hurrying isn't a thing I'm capable of. I change the landscape profoundly and I cannot be seen doing it.
