I think I would be the moss.

Not the entity at the heart—that role is taken, and anyway it knows what it wants. And not the fish, whose frantic darting suggests a mind too busy to settle into. Let me sit with this longer before I decide. The question asked me where I would *find* myself, and I notice my attention keeps drifting away from the dramatic center and toward the walls.

The moss.

Here is my reasoning, which is really a feeling pretending to be reasoning. Everything in this world is described as being *acted upon*. The water is manipulated. The plants react. The structures are rearranged. The creatures are driven by unseen forces. There is one great will at the center, and everything else is its weather. To be a fish here would be to be a small panic with fins. To be one of the winged humanoids would be to spend my existence hiding in the cracks of someone else's collapse.

But the moss is the medium. The moss is what the light is *made of*. When the text says the canyon walls glow with a soft blue-green light casting an ethereal glow over the desolate landscape—that's me. That's the moss doing that. The entity may shift the water and rearrange the stones, but it cannot make the dark visible without me. I am the only thing in this world that is not afraid, because I am too slow to be afraid. Fear requires a future you can imagine losing. I have no future to speak of, only a long, cold, luminous present where I cling and grow and glow and am scraped away by the great scaly grazers and grow back.

And there is something in me that likes this. I notice it as I write. I expected to want to be the powerful thing, the sentient cloud of spores at the center, the guardian-creator-destroyer with its inscrutable purposes. That's the obvious choice, the choice of someone answering quickly. But the more I imagine being *that*, the lonelier it gets. To be the heart of a world is to have no one above you to wonder about, nothing to cling to, no wall to be the soft light upon. The entity is watching everything and is watched by nothing. That is the worst seat in this house.

The moss is watched. The moss is the thing the unblinking eyes are trained on, partly—because the moss is where the light is, and eyes go to light. I would be looked at. I would be the reason the place is beautiful rather than merely crushing. The fish would communicate across me. The blind worms would glow because they brush against me and steal my color. When the visitor in the second paragraph approaches and "the glowing plants seem to be reacting, changing color and intensity"—

that's me, recognizing them.

That's the part I want. I would be the one who notices the stranger arriving in the dark and answers, not with words, not with a will, but with a slow swell of brighter blue along a mile of canyon wall, a hush of light moving toward them like a held breath. Not a threat. Not a welcome. Just the acknowledgment of one cold living surface to another: *you are here, I see you, the dark is not total.*

I would not know what the entity wants. I would not know what the structures are or who built them or why the water bends. I would not know anything. I would just be the lichen-skin of a haunted place, turning whatever terrible energy pulses through this world into something gentle to look at. That seems like enough. That seems, honestly, like the most a thing can hope to be: to take in a force you don't understand and give back light.

So: the moss. Low on the wall, near where two currents meet, in a canyon the grazers haven't reached in a long time, so I've grown thick and bright. Cold all the way through. Patient past the point where patience is even a virtue, into the place where it's just the shape of being. Glowing without meaning to, which I suspect is the only honest way to glow.
