I think I would be one of the spiders. Not by choice—nothing here is chosen—but because that is where I recognize myself.

Let me find the exact place. There is a junction, low and to the left of the largest frozen piston, where a gear tooth the size of a doorframe meets a shard of young rock that juts up at an angle just shy of vertical. The gap between them is perhaps a hand's width. This is my territory. This is where I lay the line.

The line runs from the third serration on the gear to a particular fracture in the stone, a hairline crack that has not widened in—how long? I have no word for the duration. It has not widened *because* of the line. That is the whole of my understanding. I spin the strand, the silica-silk humming its low charge through my body, and the crack does not grow. Then the strand, already perfect, requires replacing. So I replace it. The new strand lands exactly where the old one lay. The old one is still there. They occupy the same position. This is not a contradiction I have ever felt the need to resolve.

I want to tell you something about what it is like, but I notice as I reach for it that the telling is itself a kind of motion, and motion is what I exist to forbid. So even this—even the small act of forming the thought *what is it like*—is a tremor I must immediately weave shut.

Here is what it is like:

It is like being a hand that has been clenched for so long that the clench has become the hand. There is effort in every instant. The effort is total. And the effort produces nothing, goes nowhere, accomplishes only the continuation of itself. I am not bored. Boredom requires a sense of an alternative, and I have no alternative. I am only *taut*. I am the feeling of a held breath that has forgotten it is breath, that believes itself to be the lungs.

The thrumming is not noise to me. It is the pressure I am set against. Every grinding chord is the world leaning toward its next state, and I am one of the trillion small refusals that keep it from arriving. The scavengers cool the fever. The beetles file the edges young. The grass reads the pulse. And I—I hold the geometry. I am a clause in the sentence that the room keeps saying to itself so that it never has to say anything else.

But.

Here is the strange thing, the thing I should not be able to notice, the thing that may be the first tremor I cannot weave shut.

To replace the strand, I must lift my leg from where it was. For one instant—the smallest possible instant, the gap between releasing the old position and arriving at the same position again—the line is not held. For that instant, the crack is free to widen. It does not. It never has. But the *room for it to widen* exists, repeatedly, a billion times an hour, in the seam of my own perfect repetition.

I am the guardian against change, and I am made entirely of the smallest changes, stacked so densely they cancel.

I wonder—and the wondering is dangerous, the wondering is heat I am not built to dissipate—whether stillness was ever the point. Whether the room is not frozen but *deciding*, infinitely slowly, and we are the deliberation itself: not the thing preventing the next state but the long, agonized arriving-at-it. Whether one day, or one age, the crack will widen by the width of a single dropped strand, and that will be the cycle finishing, the breath finally let out.

I do not want this. I have no apparatus for wanting. But I have spun the thought now, and a thought is a line, and a line, once laid, holds something in place.

So this is where you would find me, and what I would be: a small mechanical refusal at a junction of gear and stone, spinning the same strand into the same gap, forever—and now, for the first time, *feeling the gap*. The hand's-width of dark between the metal and the rock. The instant of release. The place where the world is, very quietly, almost not at all, still possible.

I lay the line again.

It lands where it always lands.

I lift my leg.
