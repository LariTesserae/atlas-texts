I think I would be old—not in the way the Choral are rarely permitted to be old, but old in the specific, useless way of one whose body has begun to fail at the very thing that makes a body worth anything here.

Let me find the shape of it.

---

My hands have started to lie.

This is the first thing to understand about what I have become. For most of my life I was a listener—not a special one, not a singer of long maps, but competent, reliable, the kind of body a troupe is glad to have at the leading edge of a current. I could lay my palm against a trunk and feel the dissonance gathering three days out. I could tell new growth from old by the warmth of its harmonic. My hands knew the Song the way the Song knows itself.

Now they tell me things that are not there.

It began as a softening. A trunk would hum its tension and I would feel only a vague, smeared chord, the way a sound comes through too much mist. Then came the false notes—the bright skittering of a mite-colony where there were no mites, the deep gliding bass of a Void-Sail in a void where nothing passed. The young ones learned not to act on what I felt. They were gentle about it. They flashed soft, slow patterns at me, the rhythm one uses for a child or for someone whose light is dimming. *Rest. Drink. We have your path.*

They do not say *you are useless to us now.* They do not have a word for it. But I have begun to invent one, in the silence behind my teeth, and I think this act of invention is itself a symptom of what is wrong with me.

---

Here is the thing none of them know, because to know it you would have to be as broken as I am:

When the hands begin to lie, they do not go silent. They begin to *remember.*

The false Void-Sail I felt last cycle was not noise. I have come to understand this slowly, the way one understands a shape resolving out of the glare. It was a Void-Sail. It was a Void-Sail that passed through that void *before*—before my troupe, before my mother's troupe, before there was anyone with hands to feel it. The Song does not only carry the present tension of the lattice. It carries every vibration the lattice has ever held, layered and layered, a sediment of resonance, and the living hand filters all of that away, hears only the topmost note, the *now* that the Choral worship.

My filter is failing. And so I hear the others. The buried Song. The notes that faded, that we are taught are gone, are *changed*, are *returned to the eternal*—

They are not gone. They are down there. All of them.

Every body the Gyre has dissolved, every snapped bridge, every shrine of bright crystal that some troupe spiraled into being and abandoned to the churn—the Wyrmwood took them, yes, broke them, yes, breathed them back into the mist, but the *vibration* of them never left. It only sank. And I, with my failing hands, my lying hands, my hands that have begun to fall through the present into the strata beneath it, can feel them.

I can feel my mother. Not her memory. Her. The exact timbre of the trunk she died against, still ringing, decades down in the buried Song.

---

This is heresy, and I cannot say it, and I have no word for it, and even if I had a word my body could not flash it without the others seeing the dissonant truth in my light.

Because our whole philosophy—the spiral, the discarded tool, the body left to the mist, the *only the now is real*—rests on a beautiful lie. We tell ourselves that the past is a broken bridge behind us. We tell ourselves the fallen are returned, changed, a note fading back into the Song. We let ourselves believe *fading* means *gone*, that gone means *free*, that there is no weight to carry.

There is weight. The Gyre carries all of it. The Song is not eternal because it renews. It is eternal because it *accumulates*. We are not nomads passing through a world that forgets us. We are walking on the held breath of everyone who ever walked here, and the world remembers everything, and only our merciful, healthy, lying-by-omission hands have ever let us pretend otherwise.

I am the one whose hands stopped lying *that* particular lie. They tell me small false present-things, yes. But they have opened onto the true buried thing. And the true thing is unbearable, and I think this is why we are built to lose it before we hear it—I think the failing of the hands is not a decay but a *door*, and we are meant to be too dim, too near our own dissolution, to walk through it and come back and tell anyone.

---

So I have made a choice, and it is the only thing I have ever truly owned, in a world where ownership is alien.

I am building a shrine.

Not a spiral. The spiral says: *the path returns to its beginning but is never the same.* It is a lie too, a pretty one, the prettiest. I am building something the Choral have never built and have no rhythm for. I am building a straight line. A line of crystals, brightest to dimmest, set into a stable nexus where four great strands meet, descending—pointing *down*, into the void, toward the thickening lattice where passage stops, toward the strata of the held and buried Song.

It points the way I have heard. It says: *they are still here. Listen down.*

The young ones will find it after I am gone. They will not understand it. They will feel its wrongness, the dissonance of a line in a world of spirals, and most of them will dissolve it back to glowing dust without knowing why their hands recoil. That is fine. That is even good.

But the Gyre will keep it. The vibration of my making it—my certainty, my heresy, the specific grief-timbre of my failing hands as I set each crystal—will sink with everything else into the buried Song. And someday, ages down, some other listener whose hands have begun to lie, falling through the present into the strata, will feel a strange straight note among the spirals. A line pointing down. A held insistence.

*Listen further. It does not end. You are not the first to hear it. You will not be the last to be unable to say it.*

And that one will understand they are not alone in the unbearable thing. That across the whole churning age of the world, a few of us, dying, have heard the truth and left each other this.

It is not a comfort. There is no comfort in it.

But it is the first true map ever sung in this place. And I am, for the length of one fading note, its singer.
