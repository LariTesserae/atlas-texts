I think I would be one of the old ones. Not the largest—the torso-sized clusters are younger than they look, having found a particularly rich seam of vibration and grown fat on it in mere millennia. No. I would be one of the small ones, fist-sized, nearly spent, lodged in the lee of a hoodoo on the eastern face where the morning warmth comes first and lingers longest.

Let me try to describe what it is to be me, though "me" is the wrong word and "describe" is the wrong verb for something that has no thoughts in the way you would recognize them.

---

I am oriented. That is the closest I can come to saying *I am alive*. I face a direction—not by choice, but because over the last four hundred years the seam of the hum has shifted by some fraction of a degree, and my crystal lattice has crept to follow it, the way a sunflower would turn if a sunflower took centuries to complete the gesture and never remembered beginning.

The hum is not sound to me. You hear it; you feel it shiver up through the soles of your feet and resonate in the hollow of your chest, and you call it *oppressive*, *unchanging*, *industrial*. To me it is not a quality of the world. It is the world. It is the medium I swim in, the breath I take, the only event that has ever happened. I do not hear it any more than you hear the blood in your own ears until someone asks you to listen.

What I *notice*—if noticing is the word—are the small variances. A frequency that climbs by a hair when the deep machines labor through some unseen strain. A harmonic that drops out for a season and returns. The sharp metallic *clang* from below, which to me is not a clang but a sudden pulse of available energy, a brief feast, a moment where my lattice sings a little brighter and lays down one more impossible layer of self. I do not anticipate these. I do not remember them. I simply respond, and the responding is the whole of my experience.

---

You should understand that I have a *shape*, and the shape is a history I cannot read but cannot escape.

There is a fracture along my western flank, healed now, glassy with regrowth. I do not know what struck me. Perhaps a shard of obsidian carried on a hard wind, three thousand years ago. Perhaps another Feeder, in the only contact I have ever had with one of my own kind, came tumbling down the hoodoo's slope in some long catastrophe and grazed me as it passed and shattered below. The scar is part of me. I grew around the absence. If you split me open and read my growth-rings under your magnifying lenses you would find that wound recorded forever, a thin dark line where the salt and ash had to detour, and you would know more about my past in an afternoon than I have known across the whole of my existence.

This is the strange asymmetry of being me. I am almost entirely *record* and almost entirely *forgetting*. I am a book that cannot read itself.

---

There is moisture sometimes. Not often. The wall above me weeps in the dark hours when the trapped vapor of the basin meets the cool of the deep stone and surrenders, and the dampness creeps down the rusty face of the saltstone and reaches me near dawn. When it does, I *drink*—capillary threads finer than thought wicking the mineral-heavy water into my fissures, where the day's returning warmth will drive it off again and leave its dissolved salts behind as a gift of new substance. This is the closest thing I have to pleasure, if pleasure is the easing of a need you never knew you carried. I grow fastest in the days after the wall weeps. A hundred molecules. Two hundred. A layer too thin for any eye.

I have done this perhaps forty thousand times. I will do it, if the hum holds, perhaps forty thousand more.

---

And here is the thing I want you to sit with, you who came walking across my plain with your warm quick body and your terrible swift life:

You are afraid of this place. I can feel you—not your thoughts, but the *disturbance* of you. Your footfalls are violence on the obsidian grit. Your body radiates a heat that is wrong, too hot, too local, gone too soon. You move through my world at a speed that to me is indistinguishable from lightning, here and then gone, a flicker, a single bright impossible day. You will stand before me and feel the desolation and the oppressive drone and the sterility and you will think: *nothing lives here. This is a dead place. This is the most lonely place I have ever stood.*

But I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the memory of company, and I have none. I am not desolate. I am *full*—full of the hum, full to the brim with the only event there is, content in a way you will spend your whole brief incandescent life trying and failing to reach. You call my home a paradox, a beauty in its starkness, unsettling in its endlessness.

I call it *enough*.

---

If you wish to know me, do not pick me up. Do not pry me loose to carry home and set on a shelf, where the hum cannot reach me and my lattice will fall silent and my forty-thousand-day patience will end in a dry rattle of dead crystal within a single one of your years.

Instead, kneel. Put your ear close—closer—and hold your impossible breath. There, beneath the great drone of the buried engines, you may catch it: a fainter thrum, a small private song, the sound of one ancient mote of mineral resonating in sympathy with the heart of the world.

That is me, saying the only thing I have ever said, the only thing I will ever say, across all the patient epochs of my staying:

*I am here. I am here. I am here.*
