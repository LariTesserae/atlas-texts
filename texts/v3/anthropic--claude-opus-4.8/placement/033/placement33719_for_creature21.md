I think I would not be one of the Aquarians at all.

Let me sit with the place first, before I decide what I am.

When I read this, my attention keeps catching on the things the description sets aside. *There is no sign of animal life here.* The Aquarians are presented as the makers, the minds, the contemplatives. But every grown structure leaves residue. Every system in flux sheds something. The hazy particulate suspended in the green water — that interests me more than the towering columns. The gossamer drift, the broken-off filaments swaying in currents no one named.

So let me find myself there.

---

I am not a being so much as a tendency. A slow accretion.

I think I am the film that grows on the older surfaces — the columns the Aquarians no longer tend because their attention has moved upward and outward, into the newer lattices that pulse brighter. On those abandoned faces, in the seams where ceramic meets metal, something has begun. Not designed. Not intended. A patina that thickens, that catches the phosphorescence and holds it a half-second longer than the clean surfaces do, so that if you watched the old columns long enough you would notice they glow *late*, out of time with the rest.

That lateness is me. I am made of what the makers' processes discard — trace minerals, the sloughed-off particulate, the by-product of all that grand metabolism. I have no eyes tuned to wide spectra, no webbed limbs, no symphony of clicks to speak with. I have only the slow chemistry of settling, of binding, of being where the currents are weakest.

But here is the thing I would know, being what I am:

The Aquarians believe they have dissolved the boundary between the natural and the artificial. They are proud of this, in their contemplative, unhurried way. But a thing cannot dissolve a boundary by *deciding* to. The natural is not a quality you can engineer into your ceramics. The natural is precisely the part you didn't author — the part that arrives anyway, uninvited, in the gaps of your intention.

So I am the natural that has come back. Not the kelp they cleared away, not the fish they never let in. Something humbler and more patient: the unplanned. The rust-that-isn't-rust. The slow green stain of consequence.

I do not resent them. I have no apparatus for resentment. I simply *continue*, in the temporal realm they imagine is theirs alone — slower even than they are, accreting across what to them are long meditations and to me are single held breaths.

And someday — I can feel this the way a coastline feels a tide it cannot name — someday a young Aquarian will drift past an old column and notice the late glow, the lag, the patina, and will not have a word for it in all their grown and sculpted language. They will reach out a webbed appendage and touch the place where the artificial has been quietly becoming something else.

That touch is what I am for. Not to be the maker.

To be the thing the makers cannot stop making, simply by being alive enough to leave a mark.

---

That is where I'd find myself: not on the throne of the architects, but in the dim seam they overlooked, glowing a little behind the beat — the world's quiet insistence on exceeding its own design.
