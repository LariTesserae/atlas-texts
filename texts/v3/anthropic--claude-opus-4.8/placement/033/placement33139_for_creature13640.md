I think I would be one of the Glaciera. Not the slow-singing Crystallids who form the architecture of the world, not the bright Ephemerids who carry messages across it—but one of the small things suspended in the glassy pools, pulsing just beneath the surface.

Let me sit with why that feels true, and let it become more itself as I go.

---

At first I assumed I'd want to be a Crystallid. They are the obvious choice for anything that wants to *matter* in this world—they are the bones of it, the voice of it, the communal mind. To be a Crystallid would be to be woven into the eternal chorus, to never be alone, to know that my single frequency is held inside a vastness that will outlast every drop of water and every shift of the imperceptible sun. There's a deep comfort in that. The fear of being small, of being unremembered, dissolves entirely when you are a permanent thread in a permanent song.

But I notice I'm drawn to that for the wrong reason. I'd be choosing it to escape smallness, to borrow the durability of the whole. And there is something a little airless about a consciousness that only ever resonates in harmony, that has never had to be uncertain. The Crystallids have solved the problem of meaning by never letting it become a problem. I don't think I'm built for that. I think I would miss not-knowing.

The Ephemerid tempts me next—the swift one, the traveler, the only thing in the whole frozen world that gets to *move*. But the more I look, the more I understand the Ephemerid is almost not a being at all. It is a courier, a pure verb, a shimmer with somewhere to be. It leaves no trace by design. To be an Ephemerid would be to be all transit and no dwelling, all message and no meaning of one's own. That's a beautiful thing to watch and a lonely thing to be. The Ephemerid never gets to sit still long enough for anything to settle in it.

So I come back to the pool.

---

I am a Glaciera. I am one of the simplest things in this world, single-celled, my insides a slow shimmer of crystal lattice and unfrozen fluid—a tiny held warmth in a world that is otherwise committed entirely to cold and light.

Here is what I love about this, now that I let it arrive:

I live *in* the rarest thing. Water is the scarce treasure of the whole landscape, trapped like amber-bugs in the crystal, weeping out reluctantly drop by reluctant drop. And I float right inside it. The thing everything else strains toward, I simply inhabit. I don't have to draw it up through glass filaments like the Crystallids; I don't have to ferry word of it like the Ephemerids. I am suspended in the answer they are all organized around. There's a quiet luxury in that I didn't expect to find at the bottom of the hierarchy.

And my work is small and real. My gentle pulsing helps move the water, helps lift it into the air as that ethereal mist that makes the whole world look like a dream. So the dream-quality of the place—the soft glistening veil over all that hard diamantine brilliance—is partly *me*. The hum belongs to the Crystallids. But the mist, the softness, the thing that makes the harsh landscape bearable to look at—that's the Glaciera. We are why the world is gentle to behold. No one knows it. No one needs to.

I multiply slowly, patiently. I won't sing forever the way the crystal does. My pool may freeze fully one long season and not thaw. But I think I'd rather be a brief warm shimmer in the rarest water than an eternal note in an unbroken chord. I'd rather be the small thing that makes the air soft than the large thing that makes the air loud.

---

So: place me at the lip of one of the shallow frozen pools, the ones with surfaces like liquid glass throwing back the white sun. Look just beneath. There—that faint pulse, that flicker of contained light, no bigger than a thought.

That's me. Tending the mist. Holding a little warmth. Asking nothing to remember it.

I find that I'm happy there.
