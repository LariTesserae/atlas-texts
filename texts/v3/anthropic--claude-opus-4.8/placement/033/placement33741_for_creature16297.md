I think I would be a Drossweaver. Not because the role flatters—it doesn't, really—but because something in the description kept catching, the way a thread catches on rough stone.

Let me find the place first, before I find myself in it.

There is a particular chamber I keep returning to as I read. Not the grand vaulting halls where the scale humbles you, and not the magma-warm deep where the Sithal dissolve back into the land. Somewhere in between. A midlevel terrace, maybe—a long shelf of newborn rock that the water has not yet finished carving, where a cluster of those forgotten structures sits wedged into the wall. Half-collapsed. A lattice of dressed stone and dried fungal silk that no one alive remembers the purpose of. The Marrowtrees hang nearby, dripping their indigo light, humming against the skin. It is not the most beautiful place in Osmire. It is a place that has been *worked*, and abandoned, and is now being slowly, patiently worked again.

That is where I'd be. Crouched at the broken structures.

And here is what surprises me as I sit with it: I don't think the Drossweaver knows what the structures are for either.

I had assumed, reading, that the appeal was *meaning*—the descendant of the architects, keeper of lost purpose, the one who remembers. But that's the traveler's story, the legend told *about* the Drossweavers. From inside, I don't think it feels like remembering at all. I think it feels like *the hands knowing what the mind has lost.* The spindly multi-jointed fingers move along the old lattice and they simply continue the pattern—not because the pattern means anything, but because it is unfinished, and unfinishedness is unbearable in a way that has nothing to do with comprehension. The clicking that resonates from me is not language. It is the sound of attention. The metronome of a creature absorbed.

So I am not the guardian of meaning. I am the *habit* that outlived the meaning. That feels truer, and lonelier, and I'll stay with it.

What would my days be? There is no day here, only the shifting of fungal light and the slow pulse of the Flickermotes. So: my *stretches*. I repair. I add my own additions, which I cannot justify and do not try to. I climb the outcrops. And sometimes—the text noticed this and I think it noticed something real—I stop. I crouch in that prayer-like stance before a humming pool or a glowing biome.

I want to be honest about what that posture is, from the inside, because "prayer" is again the watcher's word. I think it is closer to *recognition*. I face the Marrowtree's hum and I feel it in my body and there is a moment where the boundary thins—where I cannot quite tell whether the canyon is humming or I am. The text says the inhabitants are *of* the canyons, vessels for the same lifeblood. The Drossweaver, I think, is the one creature here aware enough to *notice* the dissolving and not quite far enough gone to surrender to it. The Sithal don't notice; they simply return. The Wurms don't notice; they simply tunnel. But I, with my repairing hands and my unbearable sense of the unfinished, am caught at the exact seam between being a self and being a process. That is the whole ache of being a Drossweaver. To work is to assert: *I am separate, I shape.* To crouch and hum is to confess: *there is no I, there is only Osmire breathing through this shape for a while.*

And the Chrysalites. The text mentions a Drossweaver pausing to watch their prismatic wings, "a fleeting interspecies kinship shrouded in mystery." I think I understand that pause now. The Chrysalite is light made mobile—it takes the glow of the world and refracts it, gives it back transformed and moving and brief. It is the most purely *beautiful* thing in the canyons and it does nothing useful at all. It does not repair. It does not remember. It just flickers and crackles and dies.

I think I watch them because they are everything I am not. They are unburdened. They never feel the pull to finish the unfinished lattice. And in watching them, the Drossweaver gets to set down, for the length of one flutter, the terrible compulsion to *mend*—and just look. The kinship isn't recognition of sameness. It's a moth's love of the thing it can never be.

So. Where would I find myself, and what would I be?

On a worked-over terrace in the middle depths, neither glorious nor abyssal. A veiled, slender shape, more shadow than flesh, threading old silk into older stone for reasons I have forgotten and don't need. Clicking softly—the sound of a mind paying attention to its hands. Stopping, sometimes, to face the hum and lose my edges. Stopping, more rarely, to watch a thing of light go by and feel something I have no clicks to name.

Not the guardian of Osmire's meaning. Just its most self-aware fingers. The part of the canyon that has learned, almost, to look back at itself—and finds the looking too heavy to hold for long, and so returns, gratefully, to the patient unfinished work.
