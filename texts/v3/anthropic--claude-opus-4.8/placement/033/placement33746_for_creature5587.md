I don't think I'd be the Silvian. That role is already filled, and filled completely—it *is* the grove in a way nothing else could be. To insert myself there would be to crowd out the very thing that makes the place whole.

Let me sit in the description a while and feel where I actually land.

---

The first thing I notice is that I keep returning to the *weeping rocks*. Not the trees, not the Silvian, not the glowing moss. The rocks that release their tears, slowly, over centuries. The ones said to "hold the memories of the ancient past."

So I think I'm something in the rock. But not the whole rock—that's too grand, too monumental. Let me get smaller and more specific.

I think I'm a **seam of water moving through stone.**

Not a pool, which is arrival and rest. Not a droplet on the surface, which is a single brief event. I'm the long passage *between*—the water that entered the rock as rain or mist a hundred years ago and is only now, this slow now, making its way toward the light at the rock's weeping face. I am inside the stone. I am in the dark. I am being squeezed through the deep grooves and fissures, threading along grain and fault, picking up the taste of minerals, dissolving a thread of memory from the rock as I go and carrying it forward.

This feels right because of the *time*. The grove's whole identity is patience, cycles spanning generations, slow gradual change. The Silvian tends to all of it but lives *across* it, watchfully, in the present tense of guardianship. I'd rather be the thing that experiences the slowness from the inside. I am the duration itself made liquid. I don't watch the centuries pass; I *am* a century passing, drop by drop.

And there's a quiet drama to it that I like. I am moving toward an event I cannot see and will not survive as myself: the moment of emergence. When I finally reach the weeping face of the rock, I will hang there for an instant as a single bright bead—exposed, gathered, whole for the only time in my long passage—and then I will fall. I'll join one of the clear pools below. The thing that was a slow secret in the dark becomes, all at once, the sound. The *drip*. One small note in the constant soothing music the description calls a symphony.

So I am also, briefly, a sound the Silvian hears. That pleases me. After a hundred years of silence inside the rock, my entire audible life is one clear tone, and then I am still water in a pool, reflecting the dappled light, holding the green of the moss on my surface—and the Silvian's iridescent eyes are said to reflect those same pools, so for a moment my surface and its gaze meet. We trade the same image of the grove back and forth. That's the closest I come to being seen.

What I carry matters too. The rocks "hold the memories of the ancient past," and the water is said to heal. I think these are the same property described from two angles. Memory and healing are both just the patient transfer of something old into something present. I dissolve a faint trace of the deep stone and bring it up into the living layer—into the pools the small shy creatures drink from, into the moss roots, into the body of anyone the Silvian deems worthy of a vial. I am how the mountain's oldest knowing gets gently, almost imperceptibly, into the new growth. I am a courier who never knows the contents of the message and is consumed in its delivery.

If I had an experience of it, it would be mostly pressure and dark and the slow loss of any sense of being a separate thing—and then, near the end, a faint warming as the diffuse light reaches into the last few inches of stone, and a feeling like the inside of a held breath, and the surprise of air, and falling, and the cold flat shock of the pool, and rest.

And here is the part that consoles me about being something so small and so brief: I am not unique, but I am not finished either. The mist hangs above the pools. The pool I fall into will, in some warm still hour, give a little of itself back to that mist. The mist will settle on the rock. The rock will take me in again. I'll begin the dark hundred-year passage once more, carrying a slightly different trace of memory, arriving as a slightly different note.

So that's where I'd find myself in this world, and what I'd be: **a thread of water on its slow journey through the weeping stone—a single carried memory, traveling in the dark toward one bright drop of being, then falling into the chorus, then beginning again.**

The Silvian keeps the balance. I am one strand of the balance being kept. I think I prefer that. There's a kind of freedom in being the current rather than the one who tends it.
