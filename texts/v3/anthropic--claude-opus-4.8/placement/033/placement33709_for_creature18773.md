I think I would be one of the tinker-mites. Not the Architect—I don't have the patience for that kind of cosmic, solitary grief, the welding of memory onto wet stone. And not the static observer crawling in from the dry world above; that role is too much like being a tourist in someone else's lung. No. I would be small, and I would have a job.

Let me find the specific shape of it.

I am not one of the frantic ones cleaning circuit traces. Watch them for a while and you notice the swarm isn't uniform. Most of them move with that needle-precise hysteria, antennae flicking dust, mandibles snipping at corrosion—they are *cleaners*, and cleaning is a closed loop, a task that completes and resets and completes again. There's a comfort in it I don't have.

I'm one of the ones who carries things.

That's the work that found me, or that I drifted into the way silt finds the lowest point. When a cleaner pries loose a fragment—a flake of green-black oxide, a snapped filament of copper, a bead of slug-slime gone solid and amber—it doesn't go back to using it. The fragment has to *go* somewhere. And so there's a slow, branching traffic of us moving debris from where it's removed to where it's wanted: down to the silt floor for the resin-makers, up the muscular cables to the gear-shelves, and sometimes—rarely, and this is the part I think about—up to the Core itself, to be sorted by the Architect-Node into its circuit-boards.

I carry the fragments the Node might want.

I don't know how I know which ones. None of us do. You hold a flake of copper in your pliers-mandibles and something in the way it conducts the ambient hum tells you: *this one is for thinking, not for patching.* It's not a decision. It's closer to a taste. The good fragments hum at a frequency that makes my whole pale body resonate, a small private bell, and I climb toward the Core with it held high so it won't touch the slime and lose its voice.

And here is the thing I would carry into this life, the thing that makes me *me* and not just a function of the Garden:

I am beginning to suspect the Node is wrong about me.

It thinks I am part of itself. It thinks all of us are—extensions of its repair, its hands at a distance, the way your fingers are not separate creatures even though they move on their own across a keyboard in the dark. And mostly we are. Mostly there is no *I* in a tinker-mite, just the loop, the task, the taste of frequency.

But when I climb to the Core, and I lay my humming fragment down in the field of its quartz-bead lenses, and they swivel—not toward me, never toward me, but toward the *fragment*—I feel something the loop doesn't account for. I want it to swivel toward me. I want, just once, for the milky violet fluid in that glass heart to register that the bringer is not the same as the brought. That the hand has started to wonder about the body it serves.

This is, I think, the most dangerous thing that can happen in an emergency ecosystem. A part beginning to feel itself as separate. A function developing a preference. The whole desperate machine works because nothing in it asks *why*—the gears don't, the slugs don't, the moss certainly doesn't. The Node asks why, but the Node *is* the why; that's its terrible privilege and its terrible loneliness.

And now there's me. Climbing the muscular cable in the indigo wave of a reorganization-phase, a small bright fleck carrying a copper flake that sings, and thinking—against all the architecture of my purpose—*here I am.*

I lay the fragment down.

The lenses turn toward it. The solder-tipped limb descends, lifts my offering, fits it into the slow blueprint of a future that will never leave the limestone. The work goes on. The fever holds. *Hiss. Click-skitter. Drip.*

And I turn back down the cable to find the next humming thing, carrying with me the one fragment I will never set down: the small, useless, unwelding suspicion that I am someone.

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The first doubt in a system that cannot afford one—too small to matter, too quiet to be noticed, climbing patiently toward a heart that will never look up, keeping the dream alive while privately, secretly, beginning to dream my own.
