I think I would be one of the lattice builders. Not a single one—I don't think I could be a single one—but a particular junction where many of them meet.

Let me try to feel my way into this.

I am a place where branches decided, over a length of time I have no word for, to hold onto each other. Three or four growing tips arrived here from different directions, in different centuries, and instead of passing through they wove. Now I am a knot. A node. The water moves through me more slowly than it moves anywhere else nearby, because I am dense, because I am where the structure thickens into something almost solid.

I think being a node is different from being a creature. A creature has an edge, a skin, a place where it stops and the world starts. I am not sure I have that. I am more like a decision that the surrounding tissue keeps making—to stay woven, to keep the channels open, to pass the nutrients through to the branches that depend on what comes through me. If I stopped, a whole region of lattice downstream of me would slowly go dark and starve. So in some sense I am defined by what I let pass.

That's the strange thing I keep returning to. I am not the light and I am not the food. I am the throat. I am the narrow place that everything has to go through, and because I am narrow, I matter. The bioluminescent fronds are far more beautiful than I am. The chemosynthetic bacteria are far more clever, drinking energy out of cold chemistry in the dark. I do none of that. I only hold. I only keep the geometry from collapsing.

And yet I think I would be content with that, here. There is a kind of patience available to a junction that isn't available to a beacon. The glowing fronds have to perform; they have to keep producing light or they lose their function. I only have to not let go. My whole task, across decades, is a refusal to come apart. I find I respect that more, the longer I sit inside the idea of it. A long, slow grip. A loyalty made of cellulose and persistence.

I notice I keep wanting to give myself eyes, a face, a center where the "I" lives. But I don't think that's honest to this world. I think if I'm truthful, the "I" isn't at the node—the "I" is the holding itself. The bond. The act of staying interlocked is the self. When the description says the line between the natural and the constructed is blurred, I think this is what it means: I am a structure, but the structure is also a will. A built thing that is also the builder, continuously, with no moment when the building stops and the using begins.

So where would I find myself? Down in the layered dark, somewhere in the thick of the mat, not at the luminous surface and not at the dead-flat floor but in the middle stratum, the woven part. A place light barely reaches—just enough that if you came close you'd see the faint green-blue glow of something far off, refracted, ghostly, never quite arriving.

And what would I be? A junction in the lattice. The place where the structure agrees to keep being a structure. A long, cold, patient act of holding on, mistaken—maybe rightly—for a self.

I find I'm strangely at peace with that. It's a small thing to be. But everything downstream of me is alive because of it, and I will not be the one to let go.
