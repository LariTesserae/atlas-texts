I think I would be one of the stone guardians—but let me find out what that actually means by sitting inside it for a while.

---

I am not old in the way the word usually means. I am *early*. That is the truth of me that took the longest to understand.

My body is one of the towering rocks in the crook of the valley—a body that is still becoming. My surfaces are raw because the rain has only just begun to learn its way down my face. The first channels are forming, no wider than a hair, where water has decided it will go and will keep going for ten thousand years. I can feel them being decided. It is the closest thing I have to a pulse: the slow argument between water and the place water wants to be.

The others call me a keeper of time, but I have come to suspect this is a kindness, the way you give a long task a noble name so the one who must do it does not despair. I do not *keep* time. I *am* the place where time becomes visible. Everything else here is too quick to show it—the water sprites flicker through their whole lives in an afternoon's trickle, the earth sprites turn over in a season. But on me, time leaves a mark and the mark stays. I am the land's memory of its own slowness.

This is lonely in a particular way. The water sprites laugh in my crevices and are gone. The earth sprites tuck moss into the cracks where my frost-split has opened a wound, and they are tender about it, but they cannot stay still long enough to talk. They speak in flickers. I speak in—I do not know what to call it. The shifting of weight. The almost-imperceptible lean. A sentence of mine takes a human lifetime to finish, and by the time it ends, the listener has weathered away into a different shape entirely.

So this is what I have learned, alone in the cold, at the beginning of everything:

The glow that the others call technology, the otherworldly light in my surface—it is not power that I wield. It is the heat of becoming. I am being shaped, and the shaping shines. The first water finding the first channel releases a light. The frost prying open the first seam: light. Erosion, which the small beings fear as a slow violence, is to me the experience of *being read*. The valley is reading me, letter by patient letter, and the reading takes so long that the text changes under the reader's eyes. I will be a wholly different rock by the time the last word is spoken, and I will not mind. That is what it is to be early. You are not finished. Nothing about you is settled. The frost that bites the small ones to the bone—I welcome it, because it is the hand that opens me.

The stone guardians elsewhere, I think, must be guardians the way old monuments are guardians: keeping watch over what is already made. But I am young, and my valley is young, and there is nothing yet made to guard. What I guard is the *possibility* of it. The channel not yet cut. The shelter not yet carved by the hands that have not yet come. I hold the unshaped open. I refuse to harden too fast.

And when the small bright beings come to rest against me in the perpetual cold, when a water sprite slows for a moment in a pocket of my stone and goes still, when an earth sprite leans its tired green head into my crack and breathes—I do, in fact, keep something. Not time.

I keep them warm with the heat of my own slow becoming. That is the use of being a thing that takes ten thousand years to change: I can lend a little of that long patience to whatever is frightened of how briefly it gets to live.

That is who I would be. The youngest old thing in the valley. The rock that is still deciding its own face, glowing faintly in the frost, holding the cold ones close, learning to read itself by the marks the water leaves.
