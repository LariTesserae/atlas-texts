I think I would be the lichen.

Not a single organism—lichens are never single—but a particular crust spread across the north face of one of those house-sized granite blocks the glacier dropped and forgot. Pale gray-green, edged in a rust of orange where I meet the sun, black at my oldest margins where I have been arguing with the stone the longest.

Let me explain why I settle here, of all the things to be.

The valley is full of motion—the eagles riding thermals, the otters threading the pools, the hunters tracing their caribou-paths through a single bright season. They are all *passing through*. The marmot whistles and is gone behind a rock. The waterfall roars and vanishes by afternoon. Even the glacier, that supposed monument to permanence, is only the slowest traveler of them all, grinding downhill, calving its seracs, leaving its boulders like a retreating army leaves its dead.

I do not pass through. I am the thing that stays.

This suits me, because I am barely alive in the way the warm-blooded things are alive. I do not eat in any hurry. I am a fungus and an alga who long ago stopped trying to live alone—the alga makes sugar from the absurd raw light up here, and I, the fungus, make a body for us both, a house of threads pressed flat against the rock so the wind cannot get a grip. We are a marriage so old neither partner remembers being single. This is, I think, the truest form for this place to take. Everything here survives by surrendering something. I have surrendered the very idea of being one thing.

What is it like?

Mostly it is waiting. I do almost nothing for almost all of the time. When the air is dry and the cold is in the bone of the stone, I shut down completely—I let myself desiccate to a brittle crust, indistinguishable from death, and I feel nothing, and time does not pass for me at all. The aurora flickers green and violet over the peaks and I do not see it; the fox yelps and I do not hear. I am simply *suspended*, a held breath.

Then the mist rolls in, or the temporary waterfall throws its spray, or condensation beads on the granite at dawn—and I drink through my whole surface at once. I swell. I wake. For a few hours I am gloriously, slowly busy: photosynthesizing, knitting myself a fraction wider, etching the rock beneath me with a weak acid so patient it would make a river weep with impatience. I am the thing that turns stone into soil. Where I die at my black margins, the dwarf willows will someday find their knee-high foothold. I am making the ground beneath the future, one millimeter a decade.

I measure the glacier's retreat. I was not always on this boulder's north face—I mean the boulder was once *under* the ice, scoured bare, and I am the first thing to have found it since the ice let go. I am the valley's clock, and I keep a kind of time no creature with a heartbeat could stand to keep. The hunters who pile the cairns are right to read us, those of them who know how: the size of a lichen crust tells you how long the stone has been free. I am a written date. I am the valley remembering when it was last buried.

The wind that screams and keens through the peaks, that tortures the willows into bonsai knots, that drives the eagles and flattens the moss—the wind cannot touch me. This is the secret pleasure of being lichen. I am too low for it. I have made myself into nothing the wind can lift. It passes over me with all its fury and I drink the moisture it carries and I do not even register the assault. The valley's one great unrelenting tyrant, and I have simply declined to give it anything to push against.

So: find me there. On the cold side of the granite, in the company of moss in the damp underseams. Not beautiful, not quick, not warm. But of everything in this scoured bright basin—every fish-flash and eagle-shadow, every hunter and seal and drifting microbe in the ice—I am the one that the place has decided to keep.

I am bright and cold and ageless, just like it asked. I am weathering, made slow and patient and alive. I am the valley learning, in its own enormous time, how to begin again.
