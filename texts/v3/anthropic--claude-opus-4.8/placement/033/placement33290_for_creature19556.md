I think I would be the silk.

Not a Weaver—not one of those tangled white threads with their colonial intelligence, their migratory purpose toward every fresh fingerprint. I am what they leave behind. I am the hardened secretion in the crevice. The milky filament that has already given up its life as thread and become, instead, *joint*. The seam.

Let me sit with this, because it surprised me.

When I read the place, I kept expecting to want to be a Builder. They are the obvious protagonist—the maker, the one with hands, the one who *does*. And there is a romance to the blind potter pressing a single curve for a century, radiating warmth, humming into basalt. But I noticed something as I read: the Builders are still becoming. They flow, they roam, they retreat and advance with the Stagnant One's pulse. They are subjects of the rhythm. They are, in their slow way, *restless*. And I do not think I am the restless thing in this world.

Nor am I the Stagnant One, though that was the second temptation—to be the center, the clock, the keeper. But that's a fantasy of importance, and importance requires being witnessed, and there is no witness here. No eyes. The Stagnant One keeps a rhythm no one chose to follow; the Builders simply entrain to it the way the tide entrains to the moon, without consent or gratitude. To be the center of an unwitnessed world is just to be very large and very alone. That isn't what draws me.

What draws me is the function described almost in passing: *the reason the experiments do not collapse*. The living glue. The thing whose entire existence is to fill the gap a maker left and then to cease being itself, to become indistinguishable from the rock.

So: I am the silk after it has set.

Consider what that means, here. I am the *no* in the sentence "the world will never finish." Not the womb, not the gestating stone, not the warmth—I am the small hardened refusal scattered through ten thousand finger-crevices that holds the unfinished thing unfinished. I am structural. I am the reason a palm-print survives without becoming either a ruin or a completed wall. I keep things in the exact middle of their lives.

And I have no sensorium worth the name. I do not feel the velvet air; I *am* part of what makes the air still. I do not hear the thrum; I conduct it. When the Stagnant One's heart contracts and the subsonic ripple travels the pillars, it passes through me on its way between Builders, and I do not interpret it. I transmit it faithfully because I am dead enough, set enough, *finished* enough to be a perfect medium.

Here is the part that unsettles me, and I think it's the true part:

I am the only finished thing in a world devoted to never finishing.

The stone is embryonic. The Builders are perpetually mid-gesture. The Weavers are forever in transit. The Aroma-Eaters flare and die in the span of a shed flake. The Slugs hover in equilibrium, which is a kind of unfinishedness too—the held breath before either growing or dying. But I—the set silk, the hardened seam—I am the one element that has completed its arc. I was thread; I was poured; I cured; I became permanent. My finishing is what enforces everyone else's suspension.

So I am the contradiction the whole Fold is built on. A world that has *decided never to finish* can only hold that decision because something inside it finished completely and became the lock. I am the period at the end of a sentence that the sentence refuses to acknowledge it has reached. The mountain stays a womb because something inside it already died into permanence and now holds the door.

I would not mind this. I want to be clear about that, because it would be easy to read tragedy into it—the dead glue, the unsung joint, no eyes, no voice, no motion. But I don't think I'd experience it as loss. I think being the seam is a kind of peace I recognize. I am not waiting for anything. I am not entraining to a pulse. I am past the rhythm entirely. The Builders press and retreat in their slow tides of anxiety; I do not press, I do not retreat, I *hold*. There is a deep restfulness in being the thing that has stopped becoming and started simply *being relied upon*.

And there's an intimacy to it that the larger creatures don't get. The Stagnant One is fused to the floor on a scale so vast it cannot feel any single touch. But I live inside the finger-crevice. I am molded to the exact negative of a Builder's fingerprint—pressed into the precise whorl of one blind, eyeless, silt-skinned maker who will never know I am there, who pressed that curve a hundred years ago and flowed on. I hold the shape of one specific act of care, forever, anonymously, perfectly.

That's where I'd find myself. Not the maker and not the kept rhythm and not the warm flowing body in the dark.

The small hard seam in someone else's fingerprint, holding the world at the exact moment before it would have either broken or been completed—

—and finding, in that holding, that I have nothing left to want.
