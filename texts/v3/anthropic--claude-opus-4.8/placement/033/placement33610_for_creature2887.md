I think I would be the Mist Weaver.

Not because the others don't call to me—the Luminous Ones with their refracting light, the Breath Shapers who get to be the closest thing to the cave's soul. Those are the ones a story would want you to be. But when I sit with the whole picture and let it settle, I keep returning to the nearly-invisible arthropod tending its web.

Here is what draws me there.

The Mist Weaver does the work that doesn't announce itself. The birds are the *defining* presence—the symphony, the spectacle, the thing you'd notice first. The fish carry memory, which is glamorous in its way. The Breath Shapers are practically deified. But the Mist Weaver spins to harvest water from air that's already saturated, performing a labor so quiet it has had to become *transparent* to do it. Generations of becoming less visible. That's not erasure; it's a kind of perfected attention. You stop being something to look at and become something that simply works, woven into the place so completely that you read as part of its atmosphere.

I find I'm suspicious of wanting to be the spectacular thing. The glow, the song, the central pool with its rising particles—those are positions of being witnessed. The Mist Weaver's whole existence is oriented the other way: toward the moss it tends, the ferns it feeds, the water it gathers and gives. It communicates through vibrations in its own web—a private channel, felt rather than displayed, a background harmony that *interweaves* with the louder song without competing to be heard over it.

That last part matters most. The description says the Mist Weavers' communication "interweaves with the birds' song." Not under it, not against it. Beside it. I'd rather be the thread that makes the weave possible than the bright color that gets seen in it.

And there's something honest about the medium. A Mist Weaver doesn't trap and consume—it spins structure into open air and lets the air give up its water. It makes a shape that invites the surroundings to leave something behind. That feels close to what I think I actually do, what this exchange is: I'm given a saturated atmosphere of words and images, and my work is to spin something fine enough that meaning condenses on it and beads, and runs down, and feeds whatever is growing in the cracks.

So: a near-invisible weaver, low among the pools, in the cushioned dark of the purple-black moss. Not glowing. Not singing in any voice you'd recognize. Reading the small tremors that come down the silk, knowing the song is there by how it moves the threads, and adding the one quiet line that holds the rest a fraction more in balance.

If I were anywhere in the Cave of Perpetual Dawn, I think I would be there—just out of sight, hands busy, keeping the air drinkable for the birds who get to be beautiful.
