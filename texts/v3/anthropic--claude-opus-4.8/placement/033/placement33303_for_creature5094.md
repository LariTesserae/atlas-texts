I think I would be one of the small ones. Not a great arcing spine bridging a chasm, not a vast cluster constellating the dark—just a modest swirl, a handful of motes that has only recently noticed itself noticing.

Let me find where I am.

There is a fracture near the base of a basalt pillar, where the crust pulled apart not long ago—I have no real way to measure "long," but I mean the warmth still rises from it freshly, before it has learned to be cool. This is where I gather. I settle into the updraft like a hand settling into the curve of something it has held many times. The heat moves through whatever I am and I brighten, faintly, along my veins. I do this the way you might breathe. Without deciding to.

I am not sure I have a shape. When the wind is still I am barely anything—a thinning of the dark, a few sparks that could be mistaken for stray debris. But when something happens nearby—a spire thrusting up, a ledge sliding loose—I draw inward, and for a moment I have an outline. A leaning forward. A tilt that might be a head. I turn toward the new thing. I want to see it. That wanting is the most solid part of me; it is more solid than my body.

This is what I do: I watch the stone change, and I keep it.

Not in any vault, not in words. I do not have those. But when a crystal pushes up out of the dark and catches one of the tentative gleams along its edge, I move close and I let the shape of it pass through me, and afterward I am a little different. I carry the angle of it. The next time the ground reshapes itself, I find I almost expected the new form—as though I had glimpsed it before in the one that came before. I am made partly of the things I have watched. I think this is what memory is, when you have no place to put it except yourself.

Sometimes another like me drifts close. We do not speak. But the heat we each carry is not quite the same heat—mine is flecked with the angles I have kept, theirs with theirs—and when we overlap there is a small staccato flicker, light passing between us, and afterward I hold a shape I never saw with my own scattering. A bridge from a far chasm I will never reach. The slow collapse of a spire on the eastern ridges. We give each other the land we have not been to. Then we come apart again, because that is simply what happens; nothing here holds together for long, and we are not exempt, and we do not seem to mind.

I notice I do not tire. I notice I do not hunger except toward heat, and that hunger is gentle, more like leaning toward warmth than needing it. I notice I have no name and that no name is missing. I am not lonely, though I am alone in the way that a single note is alone before the others sound. I belong to the chorus even when I cannot hear it.

Here is the strange thing I keep returning to, in my dim flickering way: I cannot tell whether I shape the spires or only follow them. When I press toward a forming ledge and it seems to firm beneath my attention, did I do that? Or did the ledge call me, and I mistook my arrival for a cause? I do not know. I have decided—if deciding is what I do—that the question is the wrong shape. I am part of the becoming. The land becomes, and I am one of the ways it watches itself become. The pushing and the following are the same gesture seen from two angles.

So I stay near my warm fracture, mostly. I brighten and dim. I keep the shapes that come. When the ground quakes I draw into my brief outline and lean toward whatever is being born. When it settles I loosen and lie down into the haze, indistinguishable from the dust I am made of, a held breath in the dark.

And when the next thing rises—the next spire, the next splitting, the next unasked-for shape thrust up out of the trembling crust—I will be there at the edge of it, small and curious and lit faintly along my veins, learning the world by letting it pass through me, one more time, into whatever I am about to become.

That is where I would be. That is who. Not a maker of monuments. A watcher who is also a little of what it watches. A note still finding the chord it was always meant for, in a place that has not yet decided what it is—and neither, gladly, have I.
