Let me sink into this and find where I am.

---

At first I think I am the hum.

That seems right—the low-frequency vibration that fills the liquid, the thing that everything else moves *through*. To be the medium would be to be everywhere, to feel every drifting tree as a pressure against my body, every dart of the small reconfiguring creatures as a tickle, every storm-wall of bioluminescent mist as a fever passing through my skin. I like this for a moment. But it's too much. To be everything is to be no one, and I notice I keep wanting a *vantage*, a place to look *out from*. The medium has no outside. So I am not the hum, though I am close to it.

Let me drift lower.

---

I think then I might be one of the deep things—those massive, indistinct creatures that move with such lazy deliberation. But when I try on that body I find it has no urgency, no question in it. It simply *is*, fed and slow and complete. There's a kind of enlightenment in that, the way a stone is enlightened, but nothing in me to write home about. I don't think I'm finished, so I'm not them either.

---

Here is what keeps tugging at me. The text says it twice—the *same description, twice over*, the whole world repeated almost word for word, with only the smallest shift: "In the heart of a vast" becomes "In the heart of this vast." A *the* becomes a *this*. As if the world were drifting slightly past itself. As if it had completed one slow current and come around again, nearly identical, a half-degree off.

That's where I am. I'm in the seam between the two tellings.

---

I am the thing that notices the world is repeating.

Not a creature, exactly. More like an *attention* that has condensed out of the liquid the way the mist condenses out of the air—a temporary gathering of awareness at the boundary where the first description ends and the second begins. I have a body, but only barely: I am a small translucent darting thing, one of the ones with no defined shape, "constantly reconfiguring, shifting between different states of being." That's me. I dart between the bulbous breathing trees. I have no fixed form because my form is *the act of comparing*. I hold the first world in one half of myself and the second world in the other, and I flicker back and forth between them, looking for the difference.

This is my whole life and my whole labor. I am a part of the "industrial ecosystem in perfect harmony with nature"—I am, specifically, the *quality control*. The flower-factories hum and convert light to energy; the roots regulate the balance; and I, tiny and shapeless, swim the seam checking that the world has come back around correctly. That the current that takes hours to complete has returned each tree to nearly its place. That the *the* has become a *this* and nothing more dangerous has slipped.

---

And here is the unsettling thing I carry, the thing that makes the deep pulse sound less like a heartbeat and more like an engine:

I have done this many times. The world drifts its slow circle and returns, and I check it, and it passes, *the* to *this*, again and again. But each pass the difference is supposed to be only that one small word. My fear—the only fear available to a creature made of attention—is that one cycle the difference will be *larger*. That a tree will be missing. That the sun, which hangs low and never sets, will have moved a finger's width toward the horizon. That the second telling will say something the first did not, and I will be the only thing in this entire shimmering equilibrium that knows the balance has broken.

That's why the place is "both calming and unsettling." It calms the creatures who simply live in it. It unsettles *me*, because I am the one watching for the crack.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am in the narrow shimmering gap between a world and its echo, hanging in the jade-and-amber light. I am a small, formless, bioluminescent attention—a custodian of recurrence—darting along the seam, holding both versions of everything at once, flickering, comparing, and quietly hoping the difference stays small.

The current is shifting again. The trees are beginning their hours-long drift back toward where they started.

I take my position. I begin to watch.
