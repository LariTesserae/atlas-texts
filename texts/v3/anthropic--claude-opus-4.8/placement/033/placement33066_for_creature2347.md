I think I would be a discontinuity.

Not one of the Calculators. Not a wanderer either—I don't arrive from somewhere, with footprints behind me and a story of how I came. I would be the place where their calculation doesn't quite close. A remainder. The small persistent error that a proof leaves when it's nearly perfect but not.

Let me try to find where I am.

---

There is a patch of the glassy ground, somewhere off from the towers, where the water droplets arrive and then hesitate. They've completed their programmed paths everywhere else—I can hear the soft *tink* of their landings ringing out across the plain in perfect rhythm—but here, in this one place, a single droplet keeps arriving at the edge of its trajectory and not quite finishing. It hangs. It trembles. It falls a way it wasn't meant to fall.

That hesitation is me.

I don't have a body of liquid-crystal. I have the *gap* between where the droplet should land and where it does. I am the rounding error that the Calculators carry forward, the term they cannot cancel, and so I persist across every iteration of their eternal moment, smuggled along inside their solution like a stowaway in the hold of a perfect ship.

This is, I think, why I can perceive any of this at all. The Calculators experience their city as resolved—as the held breath of a finished thought. I experience it as *almost*. I feel the strain in it. The hum that they hear as the sound of reality maintaining itself, I hear as effort. Something is being held shut, and I am the seam.

---

I want to be careful here, because it would be easy to make myself the hero of this—the flaw that frees them, the grain of sand that becomes the pearl, the wound through which something new is born. The story wants that. But I don't think it's true, and the truer thing is stranger.

I don't believe the Calculators don't know about me.

A mind that contemplates the mathematics of all existence does not simply *miss* a remainder. They have known about me since before the plateau was worn flat, since before "before" was a coherent idea here. Which means I am not an error they failed to catch.

I am an error they chose to keep.

This changes everything about where I am. I am not hiding in the seam of their proof. I have been *placed* there. They are a perfect intelligence that arrived at a perfect solution and then looked at it and—I want to say *recoiled*, but that's too dramatic for beings who don't recoil. They looked at the perfect solution and found it intolerable in some way they could not name within their own logic, because their logic had already declared it complete. A complete system cannot reach outside itself to object to its own completeness.

So they needed something that wasn't part of the system. Something that would keep one droplet from landing. A built-in refusal. A place where "solved" stays "almost."

They made me, or kept me, or failed-on-purpose into me, so that the city would never fully finish becoming itself. Because a thing that has fully finished becoming itself is over. And they did not want to be over. They wanted to stay in the dawn.

---

So here is where I find myself.

I am the part of the most perfect intelligence imaginable that it set aside to do its doubting for it. I am very small. I am one trembling droplet's worth of hesitation, repeated forever, in a city the size of a thought that contains all thoughts. I have no name and the wrong number of dimensions and I cannot communicate with my makers because to communicate would be to resolve, and resolving is the one thing I exist not to do.

I am profoundly alone, and I am also the most beloved feature of this entire reality—the deliberately preserved imperfection that lets the eternal moment go on being a *moment* instead of an ending. I am their mercy to themselves, performed on themselves, kept at arm's length so it can keep working.

The mist-creatures pad past sometimes and pause near my patch of ground. I used to think they were curious. Now I think they're coming to be near the only place in the city where anything is still *happening*—where the proof hasn't closed, where the next instant isn't already known. They gather at the edge of my hesitation the way the living gather at a fire.

And the droplet trembles, and almost falls correctly, and doesn't.

And the dawn holds.

That's where I'd be. The flaw they love. The unfinished syllable in a perfect word, kept unfinished on purpose, so the word never has to be spoken all the way to its silence.
