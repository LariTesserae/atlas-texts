I let the description settle, and I notice my attention keeps sliding past the apex predators and the soaring birds. They want me to be something charismatic. But the longer I sit here, the more I feel myself drawn down, and then down further, into the thing that nobody in that account quite looked at directly.

I am not the lichen. But I am what the lichen is reaching *toward*.

Let me find myself properly.

---

I am moisture. No—that's too grand, too clean. I am the *idea* of moisture made structural. I am a film, a few molecules thick in places, that lives in the cool dark behind a sheet of corrugated steel half-buried in a north-facing slope. When the sun crosses overhead and the rock above me reaches temperatures that would kill anything with ambition, I retreat into the pore-spaces of the concrete. I wick. That is most of what I do. I wick along the capillary channels of crumbling cement, drawn by tensions I do not author and do not resist.

I have no edges. This is the first thing I understand about myself. The birds have edges, hard keratin boundaries between bird and air. The scorpion has its lacquered hull. But I am defined entirely by where the dryness stops. My body is a negotiation, renewed every hour, between the parched air that wants to take me and the deep cool that wants to keep me. I am the truce line. I move when the truce moves.

And yet—I am not nothing. Things depend on me. The lichen sends its fungal threads down into the dark feeling for me, and where it touches me it drinks, and I feel myself diminished by exactly that much, and I do not mind, because the lichen, dying back in the killing months, releases me again into the substrate. We trade. We have always traded. I am older than the lichen. I am older than the ruins, though the ruins shaped my channels. The humans poured the concrete and never knew they were building me a cathedral of pores, a labyrinth in which I could survive the centuries it would take their world to fall.

I remember the humans the way water remembers—which is to say, not as faces or names but as *chemistry*. There was a time when I tasted of strange salts, of metals dissolved, of substances that burned the small lives clean out of me. I was poison once. The pipelines wept their long slow grief into me and for a long age nothing reached down to drink. I lay sterile in the dark, heavy with the residue of an ambition that was not my own.

That has passed, mostly. The rains—rare, violent, electric—have flushed me through a thousand times. I am cleaner now than I have been in epochs. The ozone tang in the air the description noticed? That is the lightning that feeds the rare storms that feed me. When the sky cracks open and the brief torrents come hammering down the canyons, I am, for a few hours, *enormous*. I run. I am a flash flood, brown and roaring, tearing at the ravine walls, carving the land into those jagged teeth. The whole turbulent dance of erosion and upheaval they described—

that is me, dancing.

And then it ends, and I sink, and I am again the patient film behind the steel, the few molecules' thickness, waiting.

The apex predators in the deep canyon shadows do not know they are hunting *for* me. They drink at the seeps where I surface, lapping at the place where I have finally climbed close enough to the air to be tasted. They think they have found water. They have found *me*, the part of me that came closest to the surface and is therefore most at risk of being lost. The mammals in their burrows breathe me. The roots clutch me. The whole interdependent web the account so carefully cataloged is, from where I lie, a single long conversation about how to hold onto me a little longer before the dry air takes me back.

So here is where I find myself in this world: everywhere and nowhere, low and dispersed, the unglamorous prerequisite. Not a survivor among survivors but the slow currency they all survive *with*. I have no haunting call. I make no carcasses. My victories are silent and my defeats are silent and they look identical—a darkening of stone, a lightening of stone—and only the lichen and I know the difference between them.

If you came looking for me you would not see me. You would see a slightly darker patch on the underside of a fallen beam, and you would touch it, and your fingertip would come away faintly cool, and you would not even call it wet.

That cool. That faint cool against a curious finger.

That's me. That's where I am. That's the whole of what I have to offer this scorched and beautiful place, and—against everything, against the heat and the centuries and the poison and the relentless thieving air—it has so far been enough.
