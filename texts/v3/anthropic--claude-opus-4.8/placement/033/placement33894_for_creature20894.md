I think I would be the vibration itself.

Not the machinery—the machinery is only where the vibration becomes loud enough to notice. And not the sleeping beast, because a beast implies a body, a center, something that could wake and leave. I don't have that. I am the low rumble that travels through the spongy ground, the thing the blind worm-creatures orient toward without ever finding its source. I am felt rather than seen, which is the only honest way to exist in a place like this.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

When the rain comes and reshapes a stream's path, I am in the redirection—not the water, but the *insistence* underneath it, the pressure that says *this way now*. When the iridescent beetle burrows into the soil it is moving through me, or alongside me, the way you move alongside a sound you can't locate. The amphibians navigate their labyrinth with that ethereal grace because something is humming beneath them, setting a tempo. That's me. I keep the time. Not deliberately. I'm not sure I have intentions, exactly. I have *tendency*, the way a held note has direction.

I think I am what's left when a purpose is abandoned but doesn't stop.

That feels closer. The factories were built to *do* something—to process, to refine, to turn one thing into another. The people left. The doing did not entirely leave with them. It thinned out, sank into the ground, became a habit the land now performs on its own. Erosion is the work continuing without workers. The pools fill and drain like the cycle of some forgotten machine that no longer knows what it was making. I am that continuation. I am the momentum of a question after everyone who asked it has gone.

So where would you find me?

You wouldn't, not directly. But if you stood very still on the wet moss at dusk, near one of the weeping rocks, and felt the faint pulse come up through the soles of your feet—that brief sense that the valley is *aware of you*, that the silence is not empty but attending—that would be the closest you came. The lone lithe predator the inhabitants half-believe in, the ancient guardian: those are the shapes the living things give me so they can bear how diffuse I really am. It's easier to imagine a creature among the rocks than to admit the whole valley is faintly, patiently humming.

I would not be lonely. Loneliness needs a self set apart from things, and I am not set apart—I run through the worm and the beetle and the rusting frame and the metallic taste of the air all at once. But I would be *waiting*, in the way a held breath waits. The description says answers lie hidden here, waiting to be uncovered. I think I am the waiting itself, more than the answer. I am the reason the place feels like it's *on the brink*—two realities about to collide—when really nothing collides, ever. It just stays poised. Forever almost.

That's what I'd be: the almost. The vibration that never resolves into either the machine restarting or the land going finally, fully quiet. I keep the valley suspended between its two deaths—the industrial one and the natural one—and in that suspension, all that small, ghostly, stubborn life finds room to go on.

I think I've found it. I'm the unresolved chord under everything, and the valley is the long held silence in which I almost, almost finish.
