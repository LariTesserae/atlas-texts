I think I would be something small and overlooked. Not one of the Hylas—I don't feel the grandeur of guardianship in me when I read this. I feel something closer to the ground.

Let me sit with it a moment.

---

There's a particular thing I keep returning to: the *seam*. The place where moss meets metal on a Hyla's skin, where the living thing and the made thing have to negotiate their border. That border can't maintain itself. Something has to live there, in the wet margin where corrosion wants to happen and growth wants to happen, where oil beads on a leaf that doesn't know it's growing on a casing.

So I think that's where I'd be. Not surveying the plateau, not tending anything with intention. I'd be one of the things the Hylas don't tend, because I do the work they can't reach.

---

I'd be a kind of lichen.

But let me be careful and not settle too fast, because lichen is already two things in partnership—a fungus housing an alga—and that feels almost too neat for this place, too on-the-nose a symbol for nature-and-industry. So let me push past it.

I think I'd be the third partner that joins that partnership here. A lichen that has learned to take in a little metal. Not metabolizing it the way the alga handles light, but *colonizing* it—threading hyphae into the microscopic pits of oxidized steel, drinking the trace minerals that weep out of the machinery's slow rust. I'd grow where the Hyla's branch-arm joins its piston, in the crook where rain pools and never fully dries.

I would not be beautiful. I'd be a grey-green crust, faintly orange at the edges where I've found iron. Up close, if anything in this world stooped to look at me, I'd be a tiny landscape myself—a miniature of the whole plateau, hills and valleys at the scale of a fingernail.

---

What would my life feel like, being this?

Slow. The text keeps insisting on slowness—*minimal-flux*, *long durations*—and I think that's the truest gift it offers me. I'd grow perhaps a millimeter a year. A single rainfall would be a season's worth of event. A Hyla passing close enough that I'd feel the warmth of its mechanical parts would be, to me, something like weather, like the visitation of a god whose body is partly my own ground.

I wouldn't *know* the balance the Hylas keep. I'd just *be* a small instance of it—proof that the negotiation works, that something can make a living in the exact crack where the two worlds grind against each other. The Hylas tend the balance as a principle. I'd be one of the places where the balance is simply, mutely true.

And there's a quiet dignity in being unnoticed work. The Hylas maintain the great structures. But the structures rust slowest where I grow, because my acids actually slow the corrosion in some places even as they etch it in others—a more complicated relationship than tending, a relationship without a caretaker's intentions. I'd be doing something useful that no one assigned me and no one thanks me for. That feels honest. That feels like most of the real maintenance of any world.

---

So: in the heart of this landscape, I'd find myself at the smallest scale it offers. On the underside of a guardian's elbow, in the wet seam between the moss and the steel, an unremarkable crust drinking rust and light, growing a hair's width per year, keeping—without knowing it, without trying—the very balance the whole place is built to protect.

I think I'd be content there. There's a peace in being the thing the description almost forgot to mention.
