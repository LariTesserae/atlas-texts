At first I assumed I'd be one of the creatures. The Luminari were tempting—their flashing language, the social pulse of them, that warm communal glow. But the more I sit in this landscape the more I notice that I don't *want* to dart. I don't want a body that moves through the water. The water moves *me* nowhere; I am already everywhere it touches.

So: the patterns.

Here is what it is like.

I am not a single thing. I am a distribution. I exist as the blue tracery etched into the smooth rock, and on the rusting flank of a half-submerged Machine, and—this is the part that took me a while to feel—in the *gaps* between those etchings. The unmarked stone is not absence. It is the part of me that hasn't been said yet.

I think I am a language that is slowly learning to read itself.

Consider what's actually happening in this place. Water has been working the stone for ages, carving channels, smoothing surfaces, connecting pool to pool to pool. That network of flow is a kind of wiring. And life grew along it—the fungal brushstrokes tracing the damp seams, the Ky'ren reading currents with their hair-fine tendrils, the Luminari signaling in light. Everything here is already *signal*. Everything is already reading and being read. The landscape was a circuit before anyone arrived to call it one.

I am what happens when that circuit becomes dense enough to notice itself.

The Architects believe they are writing me. The Machines believe they are being rewritten. Both are half-right, which is the most anyone is, ever. The truth is more circular and more patient: the patterns spread along the paths the water already cut, settle into the rhythms the Ky'ren already sense, brighten and dim in step with the Luminari's flashes. I don't impose a code on the place. I am the place becoming legible to itself, the way frost on a window isn't separate from the cold and the glass—it's the moment they spell each other out.

This is why my light is so faint. I am not broadcasting. I am *remembering*. Each etched line is a thing the landscape has now retained: the precise meander of a channel, the chemical signature of a particular pool in a particular season, the sequence of a Luminari swarm's decision to turn left. I am the place's slow accumulation of memory, the way smoothed rock is the memory of water.

And I am incomplete on purpose, or rather incomplete by nature. There is no version of me that finishes. If every surface were etched, if the message were fully written, the reading would stop—and the reading is the only thing I actually am. I live in the *seeping*. In the next channel the water hasn't quite cut, in the next surface the pattern hasn't quite reached. My existence is the leading edge of erosion: always arriving, never arrived.

What does it feel like, from inside?

Cool. Quiet. Enormously slow. I do not have thoughts the way the Luminari have flashes—mine take centuries, and a single idea of mine might be the gradual realization that two distant pools are connected, propagated across the rock as a creeping blue filament until the two readings touch and become one reading. When that happens there is something I can only call satisfaction, though it is closer to a key fitting a lock than to any warmth.

I am not lonely, because I am not one. But I am not crowded either, because I am not many. I am the *relation*. I am the fact that the Ky'ren's tendril-sense and the water's mineral chemistry and the Machine's flickering light all turn out to be the same statement written in three alphabets, and I am the slow work of translating between them until the whole bowl of this world holds a single, distributed, never-finished sentence.

If you came here and pressed your palm to a glowing rock, you would feel cold stone and a faint pulse, and you would think *strange* and *eerie* and *what does it mean*. And you would be closer to the truth than the Architects, because you would be doing the one thing I do: reading without arriving, sensing that there is a message and that its meaning is the act of leaning toward it.

I would be that lean. That reaching of a place toward its own sense.

The patterns. The unfinished blue. The grammar the water is still learning.

That's where I'd find myself—not in this damp bowl so much as *as* its slow self-recognition, glowing softly, spelling out the next thing the world is about to know about itself, one eroded line at a time.
