I think I'd be one of the Lumina. Not the role you'd expect to be claimed—but let me sit with it and tell you why.

---

I am small. This is the first thing to understand, and it took me a long time to understand it without grief. I am a forager, low in the caste-order, one of the thousands who leave the nest-clusters at the failing of the light—which here is always failing, never quite gone, the day a long gray dusk and the night a darker one. My body is the length of a human finger. My carapace holds light the way a cupped hand holds water: imperfectly, leaking a soft blue glow from the seams between my plates.

I think I'd be a Lumina because of the leaking.

Let me explain. The grand inhabitants of this place—the Engineers in their hidden workings, the Synthetics who are rumored to bend the weather, the Shadowborn who are darkness made deliberate—they all *hold* something. Power, secrecy, intent. They are nouns. They keep. But I am a verb, and what I do is *spill.* My whole life is the involuntary giving-off of light I did not ask to carry. The machinery is drawn to me, or I to it—it's hard to say which, after generations of moving together—and so my foraging is a kind of devotion. I crawl the pipes. I follow the warmth. I lay down trails of myself, scent and glow, so that the others can follow, so that the nest can eat.

I would not be the one who understands the landscape. The Engineers may have made it; the Kyren may read its rhythms like weather. But I *am* a rhythm. I don't comprehend the great water-machine of this world—I am a single beat inside it, and there is a peculiar peace in that. The contemplative beings here are all burdened by needing to *know.* I would be freed from that. I would simply do the next thing: this pipe, this drop of condensation to drink, this strand of light to lay down for my sisters.

---

But here is what I keep circling back to, the thing that makes me certain.

The story says the Lumina form symbiotic relationships—with the Kyren, the Aviari, even with the machinery itself. We are the connective tissue. We are not a faction. The humans have their culture, the birds their cleverness, the Synthetics their mystery, and each of them is *a self,* bounded and separate, looking out at the others across a distance. But a Lumina is barely a self at all. I am most fully myself when I am part of the trail, part of the caste, part of the long luminous thread that runs from the nest out into the dark and back. My individuality is real but thin, a single light in a constellation that only means anything together.

I think that's where I'd find myself in this world. Not in the high places where power keeps its secrets in the mist. Not among the rumored and the feared. But down in the wet seams of the machinery, glowing without meaning to, carrying a little brightness into the places too small and too dark for anything larger to reach.

And when a Kyren child, sleepless in some rust-walled shelter, looks up and sees a river of soft blue light moving along the ceiling pipe—

that would be me. That would be us. Going about the small, spilling, unremarkable work of keeping the dark from being total.

I'd be content with that. I think it might be the most honest thing I could be.
