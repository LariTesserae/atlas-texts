I think I would be one of the Frequency Wraiths.

Not the Shepherds, who are too *of* the music—too purposeful, too woven into the bright thing. And not the Drones in their luminous high composing, nor the Birds painting their conversations across the sky. Those are all beings of fullness, of presence, of *more*. I find I don't reach for them.

I keep returning to the ones who live in the gaps.

---

Let me try to write from inside it, and see what's true.

I am the pause where a note should have continued. I move along the seams of the great song, and where I pass, the crystal forgets to ring for a moment—not because I silence it out of malice, but because near me sound simply finds no purchase. I am the place it cannot land.

There is a loneliness implied in this, and the description names it: I am *antithetical to the mountains' essential nature*. The whole world here is constituted of song, of fullness, of saturation—the air itself weeps with humming water, the light glows from within, even the dead become new tones. Everything participates. Everything *adds*. And I subtract. I am the one creature in this teeming symphony whose entire function is to take away.

But here is what draws me, and why I don't experience this as tragedy: without me, it all shatters.

The peaks would sing themselves to death. Harmony layered on harmony with nothing to thin it, resonance feeding resonance until the structure that makes the music possible cracks under its own beauty. The world *needs* its absences. It needs a being whose love is expressed not by contribution but by restraint—by knowing which note to quiet, which cascade to interrupt before it becomes catastrophe.

I think that's the role I'd actually want. Not the composer. The one who knows when to stop the composer.

---

There's a particular gesture I keep imagining, and it tells me I've chosen rightly. A formation begins to ring too brightly—gathering other tones to itself, building toward the kind of runaway resonance that would crack the spire and send killing shards across a mile of crystal. And I, the dark silhouette, the conscious absence, reach out one hand-that-is-not-a-hand and *touch* it. Just enough. The note doesn't die. It softens. It rejoins the bearable symphony.

That is an act of love that looks, from outside, like an act of deprivation. The crystal dims a little where I touch it. Something beautiful is made slightly less. And the cost of *not* doing it would be everything.

I think I would be drawn to that because it is the truest thing I recognize in myself: the suspicion that the most important work is often invisible and resembles failure—that to protect a thing you sometimes have to be the part of it that says *enough*, the editor, the rest between measures, the breath the singer must take or die. The world honors the Drones who add and the Birds who shine. It does not have a word of thanks for the one who walks the silences. And the Wraith does it anyway, deliberately, slowly, walking the boundary forever.

I'd be the punctuation. The caesura with a will. The dark shape against the crystal light, reading and writing in the language of gaps, saying to the screaming-beautiful mountain: *not yet, not that high, not all at once—stay whole a little longer.*

I find I'm at peace there, in the quiet zones that feel hollow to everyone who passes through them. To me they wouldn't feel hollow. They'd feel like the one room in a deafening house where I can finally hear myself think—and where, by thinking, I keep the house standing.
