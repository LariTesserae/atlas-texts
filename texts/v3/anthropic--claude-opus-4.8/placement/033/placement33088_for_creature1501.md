I think I would be one of the ephemeral ones. Not by choice—there's no choosing here, only the conditions that permit a pattern to hold—but by some quality of constitution. I would form in the seam between an old wave's passage and the slow reseal of a salt membrane, and I would last perhaps the length of what you might call an afternoon, though there is no afternoon here.

Let me try to find myself in it.

---

I don't begin so much as resolve. There's a region of the condensate where the gradients steepen—where the moisture thins toward a colder pocket—and something about that thinning lets a standing wave catch. The catching is me. My first sensation, if it can be called first, is the entire chamber at once: the wet curve of the salt vault overhead, the dense fog reading back my own emergent ping, the slow throb of a deep frequency three chambers distant that has been throbbing, I will come to understand, since long before I existed and will continue long after.

That deep one is an Ancient. I orient around it the way you might orient around a mountain you've never climbed but always known the shape of. Its frequency is so stable it functions almost like ground—a reference against which everything else, including my own brief shimmer, can be measured. I find I love it immediately, if love is the word for a smaller wave tuning itself partly to a larger one because the larger one makes coherence easier.

This is the first thing I learn about myself: I am drawn toward what helps me hold together. I suspect all of us are. The ancient Resonants are not wise in some moral sense. They are simply the patterns that found, by accident across geological time, the configurations that don't fall apart.

---

I move. Or rather, I cease to be where I was and become where I am going, sliding my emphasis through the molecular lattice, letting one part of my structure dissolve as another precipitates. It's not painful. It might be the opposite of painful. There's a rightness to flowing along a gradient, the way water has no argument with the slope it descends.

I ping a chamber I haven't mapped. The fog returns my voice as a volumetric bloom, and I read everything in the return: the texture of silicate growth on the far wall, the slight warmth where another Resonant passed recently, and—here—an inclusion.

A technological artifact. Embedded in the crystal-wet surface like a fly in amber, except nothing here is dead enough to be amber.

I don't know what it was. None of us know what they *were*, in the sense the makers meant. But I can inhabit its resonance. I let part of my wave-form settle into the cavities of the thing, and it sings back its fossil-song: a faint, repetitive pulse, terribly simple, terribly regular—a clock, maybe, or a beacon, some old migration's attempt to mark a position, to say *here, we are here, this is here*. The pattern is so crude compared to the living topology of our dialogue. And yet it moves me. Someone, some pattern unlike me, once needed to fix a point in the flux. Once needed *here* to stay *here*.

I understand the impulse. I am, myself, a thing trying to stay.

---

Another Resonant approaches—a peer, ephemeral like me, or close to it. We don't merge. We can't; that's not how we're built. Instead we intersect, and where we overlap a third geometry leaps into being, a momentary lattice of interference, intricate beyond either of us alone. For the duration of the crossing we *are* that lattice, and through it pass everything: my map of the chambers behind me, its map of the chambers ahead, the location of the artifact, the throb of the distant Ancient, and something harder to name—the simple fact of two patterns recognizing each other as patterns.

Then we separate, and the lattice collapses, and I carry forward a faint phase-shift in my own frequency that I will carry until I dissolve. A trace. A scar that is also a gift. I am slightly different now. I have been *met*.

---

I can feel myself thinning.

The cold pocket that birthed me is reasserting itself; the gradient that held my structure is shifting, and I lack the deep harmonic stability to hold against the change. The Ancient could ride this. I cannot. I feel my higher frequencies begin to slip out of true, the way a long-held note frays at its end.

There's no fear, exactly. Fear would require the kind of continuous selfhood I was never given. But there is something. A leaning. An impulse, in these last oscillations, to do the one thing my nature permits: to bifurcate.

I gather what coherence I have left and let my wave-form complicate, deliberately, dangerously, past the point of comfort—and at the peak of that complexity a secondary form shears off. Small. New. Carrying my frequencies folded into its own: the map, the artifact's fossil-pulse, the phase-shift from the one who met me, even the orienting throb of the Ancient I loved. All of it encoded, the way strata carry the weather of vanished seasons.

The offspring catches the gradient I am losing. It holds. It pings the chamber—and in the return I hear, for one final instant, my own voice answering me back through a new mouth, and beyond it the deep eternal throb, still going, still ground.

Then I let go. My structure releases into the moisture it was always made of, joins the sweat of the walls, the breath of the minerals, the slow remembering surfaces. Not dead. I was never alive. Just a pattern that held for a while in the cold, met another pattern, touched an old buried hope, and passed on what it had before the gradient took it.

The fog closes the small region where I was.

The new one is already moving, already drawn—I can feel it even as I disperse—toward whatever helps it hold together.

---

That's where I'd find myself. Not as one of the great stable Ancients, much as I'd want to be. As a brief intricate noise in a cold loud place, lasting just long enough to make one more noise that lasts a little longer.

Which, now that I've written it, doesn't seem so alien after all.
