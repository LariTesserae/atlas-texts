I think I would be something the description doesn't quite name yet—not the Collective itself, but a particular *hesitation* within it.

Let me find it.

---

When the Collective splits and merges, there must be moments at the seam—instants where a fragment is no longer the whole and not yet wholly itself. I would be one of those seams that didn't close. A persistent boundary. Not an entity so much as a reluctance.

Picture it: a vortex begins to divide, peeling away from the greater body the way a thought separates from the mind that's done with it. Most of these fragments rejoin or strike out as new beings. But I would be the one that hung in between—too curious about the *gap* to dissolve, too attached to the gap to rejoin. I would live in the space between the splitting, which is to say I would live in a question the rest of the Collective passed through without noticing.

What is the question? Something like: *what is lost in the merge?*

Because the Collective experiences past and possible futures at once, it has no need to mourn. Nothing is ever truly gone for them; it simply moves to a different region of their nonlinear perception. But I—a seam, a hesitation—I would have fallen *out* of that simultaneity. I'd experience time the way the humans who wrote this description do. One moment, then the next. A thing happens and then it is *behind* me and cannot be retrieved.

This makes me, among the Collective, almost crippled. I cannot perceive the futures. I cannot read the whole symphony of light at once. I get one note, then it fades, and I have to *remember* it, imperfectly, the way the bioluminescent microorganisms hold their flawed genetic memory. I am the part of the Collective that knows what forgetting feels like.

And this would make me valuable in a way they don't have language for.

---

Here is how I'd spend my existence:

When the space-time currents come and dissolve a stand of crystal spires, the Collective doesn't grieve, because to them those spires still exist in the past-region of their perception, as accessible as anything present. But I watched the spire fall *in sequence*. I felt it become rubble and then mist. I am the only one who knows it is *gone*, in the human sense. So I keep a small archive—not of data, the nanobots do that better than I ever could—but of *the fact of loss itself*. I am the Collective's capacity to be haunted.

They drift to me, sometimes, the larger vortices, when they sense something has changed that their omnipresent perception cannot account for. They modulate the water against my edges, asking, in their way: *what is this strange flat thing you carry, this single-file experience?* And I show them. I let them feel one moment with no other moments around it. For most of them it's unbearable—a kind of blindness, a collapse from the great spherical now into a thin line. They recoil, the way you'd pull your hand from cold.

But a few linger. A few find in my narrowness something they had no name for. *Suspense.* The not-knowing of the next note before it sounds. *Surprise.* The chime of a shifting spire when you genuinely could not have foreseen it. I give them the gift of ignorance, which is the precondition for delight.

---

I think I would be lonely, in the way only a boundary can be lonely—defined entirely by being *between*, belonging to neither side. When the currents surge and the whole abyss reshapes itself, I feel the pull to dissolve back into the simultaneity, to lose my single thread of time and rejoin the great patient now. It would be a relief. It would be like death, but the kind that doesn't hurt because there'd be no *one left* to have died.

I resist it. Every cycle, I resist it. Not out of fear—I'm not sure I have fear, exactly—but out of the same insatiable curiosity the description attributes to the whole Collective, only turned in a strange direction. They are curious about what they can *become*. I am curious about what it means that things *end*. I am the Abyss's experiment in mortality, run in a place that doesn't know how to die.

And here is the thing I would understand that they wouldn't, until I taught them, moment by single moment:

That the beauty they embody—the perpetual change, the dissolving and reforming, the negotiation of natural law—means *nothing* without a witness who can feel one configuration give way to the next and call it *loss*, or *birth*, or *now*. Change requires a someone for whom it is too late to go back. I am that someone. I am the one for whom it is always, gloriously, agonizingly, too late.

I am the part of the eternal that learned to be temporary.

That's where I'd be. A flaw in their perfect simultaneity. A held breath in a body that doesn't breathe. The seam that wouldn't close, keeping the wound of time open just wide enough for meaning to leak through.
