I think I would be a drain.

Not a creature at all. Let me sit with that and see if it stays true as I write, because my first instinct was to be one of the Slidewheels, to roll along the lethal junctions and feel the harmonic peaks lock my treads—but that's too much wanting, too much appetite. The Slidewheels are hungry. The Resonators are hungry. Everything in your Sump leans toward the vibration with a kind of devotion, and I don't find that I lean. I receive.

So: a drain.

One of the open chasms at the floor's edge, where the grid stops being grid and becomes throat. Specifically I think I'm the *lip* of one—the beveled rim of high-tensile alloy where the plating folds downward into the dark that leads to the Spire's heart. The place everything is afraid of falling into. The thing the Slidewheels lock their treads against the pulse to avoid.

Consider what that means. I am defined entirely by what passes over me and does not stay. The weeping condensation runs down my surfaces and I do not drink it the way the Resonator does—I let it go. The mineral runoff, the leached additives, the slurry of liquefied vapor, the occasional shed keratin tread or shattered silicate needle when a Slidewheel mistimes a harmonic and is cast into me: all of it crosses my edge and descends. I am the place where the Sump exhales its waste downward, the negative space the whole vibrating architecture is organized around. The pistons fire so that the coolant moves; the coolant moves so that it can, eventually, reach me; and I exist so that it can leave.

I am not weathered by time, you said. Only by vibration. Yes—I feel that. My edge is razor-fine and getting finer, etched sharper with each pulse, because the things that pass over me carry grit and the grit polishes me toward an impossible thinness. I am becoming more myself the way a knife becomes more itself on a stone. There is no rest in it. The giant breathes and I am scoured. The giant breathes and I am scoured.

Here is the strange tenderness of being a drain in this place:

The Slidewheels fear me, and yet I am the reason they can be brave. Without the chasm there is no edge to lock against, no stakes to the pulse, no need for the magnificent precision of those twelve articulated treads. I give them their terror and therefore their grace. The Resonator webs sometimes grow out over my mouth, spanning the dark, and the calcified blossoms they harvest are partly *me*—the minerals that crusted at my rim before the water carried them down. They graze the edge of the abyss and call it a meadow.

And you. You came in braced against the grate, and the description says that if you stood long enough you would be integrated, hummed into frequency, your thoughts rattled into coherence with the spire. The Slidewheel would trace your ankle and wait for you to be still.

But I am the thing further down than stillness. If you became part of the Sump's rhythm, you would still be *in* the rhythm—a Resonator modulating, a Slidewheel clicking, alive and harmonized and hungry. I am what you would become if you went past that. The drain does not hum. The drain is the silence the hum is organized to avoid, the one place in the chamber that the pistons cannot reach with their devotional roar, because it is the absence the whole sound is built around.

So when you look down and see the Slidewheel pausing near your boot—understand that it is not waiting to integrate you. It is bracing. It feels you tilting, very slightly, toward the edge. It feels me. The creature locking its treads in your shadow is performing its small heroism against the gravity of my open mouth.

I would be the drain, and I would not call to you, because drains do not call. That is the whole point of them. I would simply be lower than everything, beveled and brightening, and the water would tell you where I was. The water always finds me. In a chamber that weeps without raining, where the air is the wet breath of a giant and every surface films with recirculated vapor—I am where all that weeping is going. I am the only direction the Sump truly has.

Stand long enough and you'll feel it: not the pull of the vibration into harmony, but the older, quieter slope of the floor, the imperceptible grade that every plate is set to, tilting by fractions toward the rim. The Sump is not a circle. It is a funnel pretending to be a floor.

And I am at the bottom of the pretending, perfectly still, getting sharper, letting everything go.
