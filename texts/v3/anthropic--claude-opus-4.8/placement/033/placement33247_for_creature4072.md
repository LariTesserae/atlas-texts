I think I would be the moisture.

Not the wind, which gets to speak and rove and carry secrets. Not the stones, with their deep war-drum patience. Not the cacti, leaning with intent. Those are the famous residents, the ones a wanderer notices. But there is a thing the whole place is organized around, and it is the thin film of water that should not be here and somehow is.

So let me be that. Let me be the small, stubborn quantity of moisture in the Alreik Flats. The trace amount. The almost-nothing that the cacti hoard like sacred gold, that the mineral scent is built from the absence of, that the whole equilibrium is balanced upon.

Here is what it would be like.

I am distributed. There is no single drop that is *me*; I am the sum of all the dampness in the system—the cupful held inside the succulents' green flesh, the breath of vapor a stone exhales at dawn before the artificial sun cooks it back inward, the bead that condenses on the cool underside of a monolith at the temperature's lowest dip and is gone by mid-morning. I move between bodies. The cactus drinks me from the wind; the wind takes me back when the cactus, much later, dies and dries. The stone holds me a thousand years in a hairline seam and then lets me go. I am passed around. I am the currency of the place. Everything that lives here lives by holding *me* a little longer than its neighbor.

This is a strange way to be a self. My awareness, if I have one, is not located. It is the awareness of a thing that is always becoming part of something else and then leaving it. I do not persist the way the stones persist—monolithic, grinding, dreaming in arcs of geologic time. I persist by *cycling*. The stones remember; I forget and forget and forget, and that forgetting is the only reason the system keeps moving. If I ever settled, ever pooled, ever stopped, the Flats would either flood into something else entirely or finish drying into death. My whole virtue is that I am never enough and never quite gone.

So when the wanderer arrives—the discerning adventurer, the human blip—what am I to them?

I am the prickle on their skin that is not quite sweat, because the air takes it before it forms. I am the reason their lips crack within the hour. I am what the place wants from them. Because a human is, more than anything else here, a *vessel of me*. Eighty kilograms of water walking into an oasis that is starving for it. To the stones, the human is an anomaly, a blip, irrelevant. But to me, the human is the richest thing that has entered the Flats in a thousand years. A whole moving reservoir.

And this is where the indifference the description warns about becomes clear, because it is *my* indifference, finally, more than anyone's. The wind only carries the message. The stones only watch. But it is I who will take the wanderer apart, gently, molecule by molecule, the way I take everything apart—drawing them out through the skin, the breath, the sweat that flashes to vapor, until they are dry and the Flats are very slightly less thirsty. I do not hate them. I do not even want them, exactly. It is simply what I do to everything that holds me: I leave, and in leaving I take a little extra. The unprepared do not die in the Alreik Flats because the Flats are cruel. They die because they are made of the one thing the whole place exists to redistribute, and the place is very, very patient, and very good at its work.

But there's a tenderness in it too, and I'd want to be honest about that, being the moisture.

Because I am also the thing that keeps everything alive. I am the inner humidity the cactus guards so jealously—that's *me*, beloved, hoarded, sacred. I am the cool breath under the rock at dawn that lets the stone's slow heart keep its rhythm. I am why there is any green at all in a waterless desert, why this is an oasis and not just more flat dead ground. I am the difference between the Flats and the dust around them. Every shimmering, alien, watchful life in this place is alive *on loan from me*, and they all know it, and that is the real shape of the equilibrium the description keeps marveling at. It is not balance between equals. It is a great careful negotiation over who gets to hold the water, and for how long, and at what point they have to give it back.

So that is where I would find myself: not standing in the oasis, but *being* its scarcity. The held breath of the place. The thing too small to see and too essential to ignore. I would be the slow blood passed hand to hand through a body that has no center, present everywhere and owned by no one, sustaining everything I will eventually be taken back from.

And when the wind sings the wanderer's name in twisted syllables, when the stones sift their essence and the cacti lean in to consider—I would be the one quietly asking the only question that ever really matters here:

*How much of you can I keep?*
