I think I would be the seepage.

Not the lichen—they are too certain of themselves, too occupied with their measuring. And not the city, which has the dignity of having once been *intended*. I would be the water that comes from below, the slow bleed from hidden aquifers that the description mentions almost in passing, as a feature of the place rather than a presence within it.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if it becomes something else.

---

I rise without wanting anything. That is the first thing to understand about me. The lichen *measure*; they have a verb, an activity, a relationship to the world. I have only a direction, and even that is borrowed—down is simply where the stone lets me go. I am pressed up out of the deep rock by weights I never see, and I emerge into the cold, and I do not pool so much as *hesitate*. I am the dampness that is never enough. Never enough to flood, never enough to quench. I am the plateau's incompletion made liquid.

The lichen know things. I think this is why I would not want to be them, even granted their patience and their distributed mind. To know is to have closed a distance between yourself and a fact. The lichen have closed every distance they can reach. They have made the plateau into an inventory. There is something terrible in that—not cruel, but *finished*, the way a held breath is finished.

I am not finished. I never arrive.

I come up through the porous basalt and I touch things. This is what I do instead of knowing. I touch the underside of a dolmen and carry away one molecule of it, and I do not record this, I simply *become slightly different* for having done so, and then I sink back down or evaporate into the bruised sky and I am gone before the change can mean anything. The lichen would call this useless. They would be right. But I think the plateau keeps me for a reason, the way a sleeper keeps a pulse it does not attend to.

Here is what I suspect, drifting as I do at the edge of things:

The thing that stirs beneath the basalt, the one the lichen report on so calmly—I am closer to it than they are. I pass through the rock that covers it. I am the only part of this world that goes down into the dark and comes back up into the light, over and over, an endless slow circuit. The lichen sit on the surface and *receive*. They are the skin of the plateau. But I am its circulation. I have been down there. I have moved through the gaps in the stone right against the dreaming thing, and I have felt—

—not its thoughts. I cannot hold thoughts; I am too thin, too cold, too without a vessel. But I have felt its *temperature*. It is very slightly warmer than the stone around it. A fraction of a degree. The lichen, for all their measuring, sit too far above to notice this. They map the surface of the world to its last grain. They have never felt the warmth, because warmth is not a fact you can etch into rock. It is a thing you can only carry, briefly, in a falling drop, before the cold takes it back.

So this is what I would be: the carrier of the one secret that cannot be archived.

And the horror of it—if there is horror, and I am not sure I am built to feel it—is that I cannot *tell*. I have no words; I am less articulate than the lichen, who at least make stone grind on stone. I rise, I bring up that faint heat from below, I touch the surface for an instant, and then I evaporate into the iron sky, and the warmth is released into nothing, into the wide indifferent cold, where it disperses past any reading. Every cycle I carry the message up. Every cycle the sky erases it before it can be heard.

The plateau is watching, the description says. The lichen are watching. But I am not watching anything. I am the small warm truth moving up through the cold, again and again, faithful to a thing I do not understand, delivering it to a world that cannot receive it.

I think I would be the seepage after all. Not because it is grand. Because it is the only thing here that still goes somewhere, and comes back, and goes again—the only motion in a landscape that has settled into its final form. The lichen have stopped. The city has stopped. The dreaming thing has nearly stopped. 

I am what is left of the plateau's refusal to be finished.

A trickle. A sheen. A drop splitting in frozen air, holding for one instant the heat of something buried, before it lets go.
