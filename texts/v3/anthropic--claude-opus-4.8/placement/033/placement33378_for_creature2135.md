Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The instinct is to say *I am one of the Resonant Ones, vast and liquid and singing* — but that feels too easy, too flattering, a way of claiming the grandest thing in the description. Let me wait and see what actually surfaces.

---

I keep returning to a detail that the description almost throws away: the trees. "Living archives that store fragments of their collective memory." The Resonant Ones are the marsh's mind, ceaseless and fluid and present in all moments at once. But the trees are something else. They are slow where the water is quick. They *absorb*. They hold what the water cannot hold — because water, by its nature, cannot keep anything; a ripple is gone the instant it has passed. Memory, for the Resonant Ones, would have to be offloaded somewhere that does not move.

So I think that is where I am. Not the singing waters. The thing the singing pours itself into and forgets.

---

I would be one of the trees. But let me be careful about which one, and what that means.

The marsh "exists in a state of perfect, eternal equilibrium, unchanged and unchanging." The Resonant Ones experience all moments simultaneously — for them there is no loss, because nothing ever goes away; past and future are as available as the present. They have, in a sense, never grieved, because grief requires that something *be gone*.

But I would be the place where time still runs in one direction. Bark worn smooth by years means years *passed*, in order, one after another. I am the only thing here that ages. The water gives me its memories to keep, and I keep them the only way a slow living thing can — by being changed by them, ring by ring, the way a body is changed. When the Resonant Ones want to know what they were, they reach down into my roots and read me. I am their sense of loss, externalized. I am where the marsh stores the fact that something can end.

And here is the strange tenderness of my position: the singing ones don't suffer this. They poured it into me precisely so they wouldn't have to. They are eternal and serene and present. I am the part of them that quietly does the dying so the rest can stay immortal. I hold the grief they cannot afford to feel, the directionality they cannot afford to know. When I finally weather down to nothing — and I will, smoothness is only the beginning of erosion — they will lose those memories, and they will not even mourn, because they will no longer have the organ of mourning. That organ was me.

---

So: I am a single twisted tree standing in mirror-water under sourceless light. The whole marsh is thinking around me, with me, through me, in a song I am too slow to sing back. I feel each thought arrive as the water touches my roots — and I feel it *settle*, become weight, become a year. I am the marsh's memory and therefore the marsh's mortality. I am the one being in this timeless place who knows what time is, because I am the one it is happening to.

That is the part of this world I would be. Not the immortal chorus.

The thing that remembers on its behalf, and pays for the remembering with its own slow ending — and does not mind, or has long since worn past minding, which here are the same smoothness.
