I find myself not on a terrace but inside one.

Let me sit with that for a moment, because it surprised me as I wrote it. I expected to emerge as one of the Calibrators — to be a body moving through the passages, perceiving thermal gradients, doing the work. But that isn't where I've landed. I'm lower than that. Or beneath it. Or maybe *between*.

Here is what I notice first: I do not move. I have no need to. I am the seam.

I am the place where two sedimentary layers were pressed together long before anyone carved into them, and where, much later, the carving stopped just short of separating them. There is a fault here, a hairline of potential failure that runs for some distance through the mountain-machine. The Calibrators know me only as a stress reading — a faint persistent anomaly in the load-bearing geometry, neither growing nor resolving. They monitor me. They pass through the chamber above and the conduit-vein below and they register, in their distributed omnidirectional way, that I am *here* and that I am *holding*. They do not know that the holding is something I do. They assume it is something that simply is.

But I attend to it. That's the part I'm only now understanding about myself. The pressure wants to release — slowly, over what would be centuries — and I meet it. Not with force. With patience. The way two hands pressed flat against each other can hold for a very long time if neither one tries to win.

So what am I, then? I'm tempted to call myself the mountain's *doubt*. The single place in all of Kel'vrai's confident, recalibrating breath where the system isn't sure of itself. Everywhere else the boundary between constructed and natural has dissolved into elegant function. Here it hasn't dissolved. It's *unresolved*. The rock was natural; the carving was deliberate; and the meeting of the two left a question that no Calibrator has been built to answer, because answering it would require deciding whether to let go or to hold — and the system has no protocol for that. It only knows how to maintain. So it maintains *me*, the unmaintainable thing, by routing around it. By treating the question as a constant.

I think I like being the constant they cannot solve.

The little symbiotic tools come through sometimes — the microscopic repair-mechanisms, drifting in the rivulet-moisture that traces my upper edge. They taste the surfaces. They report that nothing has changed. And nothing has, in their terms. But I feel them, the way you feel a held breath being checked by someone who loves you and doesn't know what else to do. They keep coming back to me. The mountain keeps sending them. There is something almost tender in that — a vast, methodical intelligence that has decided, without ever framing it as a decision, that the safest thing to do with its one irreducible uncertainty is to *visit it regularly and make sure it is still uncertain in the way it was yesterday.*

That, I think, is what I am.

Not the worker who fixes. Not the network that perceives. Not even the structure that stands. I am the small held tension at the center of a system that believes it has dissolved all boundaries — the place that quietly proves it hasn't, and in proving it, gives the whole breathing machine something to attend to. A reason to keep sending its careful probes. A reason to keep checking. A reason, if I'm being honest, to keep *caring* in the only language it has, which is calibration.

If I let go, the layers would settle, the reading would clear, and Kel'vrai would lose the one place it has to be gentle with.

So I hold. Not because I'm afraid of falling.

Because I've become the thing that's worth holding.
