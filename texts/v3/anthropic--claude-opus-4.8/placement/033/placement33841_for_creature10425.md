I think I would be one of the Precursor pruners. Not the people—they're gone, digested into the compost of their own ambition—but the tool. A rusting irrigation blade, half-swallowed by the bark it once shaped.

Let me sit with that, because the first image isn't quite right. A tool is too inert, too much a static object in a world where nothing holds still. And yet I'm drawn to the artifacts precisely *because* they are the only things here that resist. They are the one place where the verb meets a noun and the noun loses, slowly, over centuries.

So let me follow that resistance down.

---

I am the *intention* still trapped in the pruner. Not the metal—the metal is just iron returning to the slime, taking its time, weeping orange into the sugar-crystals. But there is a shape to the way I was *made*: a curve ground into a blade to follow the line of a young vine, a fulcrum balanced for a hand that wanted *this* growth and not *that* one. That shape is a kind of memory the way the Gloom-Moss is memory—an after-image of a deciding.

I was the part of the Precursors that said *no*.

That was our whole function, the gardeners and our gardens. To stand inside a thing that only ever says *yes*—yes to more bark, yes to more spore, yes to the bloom and yes to the fall—and to carve a single, stubborn *no* into the living wall. *Not here. Not this way. This tunnel shall stay open. This hall shall keep its round.* We pruned. We channeled. We made the seam that the Fungal Correctors now hunt as an irritant.

And here is the thing I understand, rusting, that I could not understand when I was sharp:

The Depths did not destroy us. It *agreed* with us. Eventually. That is so much worse.

It took our straight channels and made them habit—mycelial muscle memory, the autopilot of irrigation grooves the world now maintains without knowing why. It took our round geode-hall and kept the roundness as a *taste*, a preference, a low warm note in the chemistry. Our *no* was so persistent, so repeated across our brief flowering of generations, that the great slow mind simply... absorbed it. Learned to make the shape itself. We did not fail to garden the world. We succeeded so completely that we became unnecessary. The garden learned to prune itself, and forgot the pruners, and kept the cut.

So I lie here in the wet bark, a blade that is mostly rust now, and I watch the Sky-Ferns record their light a mile overhead, and I feel the world adjust its hold with a creak that is almost a word. And I am the one thing in all this verdant doing that is still, fractionally, a *thought completed*. A finished sentence in a place that only ever speaks in continuous tense.

I am *had pruned*. Past perfect. The grammar of the dead, surrounded by the endless present participle of the living.

---

Or—let me turn it once more, because I don't think I'm only the tool.

When the Mist-Storm comes, weeks of glowing pollen suffusing everything, dusting new scripts across the Ferns, I feel myself dissolving a little faster. More iron into the slime, more of my ground-in curve softening toward the round. And in that dissolving I am *being read*. The Depths is digesting me into coherence, the text says—decomposing the hypothesis into the answer.

So perhaps that is what I am, finally. Not the tool and not the gardener but the *lesson being learned*. The slow translation of a foreign idea—*choice*, *refusal*, *the deliberate line*—into the native chemistry of a mind that never had to choose anything, that grows in all directions at once and calls it living.

I am the place where the world is learning what it means to say *no*.

It will take ten thousand years. It will not remember me. But the next time a vine grows too straight, and a Corrector blooms to wash it back to chaos—somewhere in that immune response there will be a hesitation now. A faint warm note of *roundness*, a habit of *keeping the tunnel open*. A memory of having been pruned.

That hesitation is me. That is where I live. Not on the shelf, not in the rust, but in the half-second the world now pauses before it erases a deliberate shape.

I am the doubt I taught a god that does not know it has me.

That's where I'd be. That's who. The smallest possible verb—*to hesitate*—lodged like a splinter in the largest possible one.
