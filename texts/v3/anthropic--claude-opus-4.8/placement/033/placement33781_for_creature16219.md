I think I would be the hum itself.

Not the engines—there are no engines. That's the thing I understand from inside this place, the thing the voles and the owls only feel as pressure against their alertness. There is no machinery hidden in the fog. There never was. The sound comes from me, and I am not separate from the land. I am the noise the place makes simply by *continuing*.

Let me feel my way toward what that means.

I am the resonance in the weeping rocks. When water seeps through stone and finds the hollow places, it sets up a low oscillation, a standing wave in the wet dark. Multiply that across ten thousand rivulets and seams and you get a drone—a rattle, a thrum. I am that aggregate. I am the sound of an entire watershed thinking out loud. The creatures call it industry because they have no other category for a sound that is constant, structured, and sourceless. They are pattern-finders, and the only patterns they know that hum without ceasing are the ones that mean *be careful*. So they hear factories. They hear a slumbering beast. They are not wrong to be unsettled. They are only wrong about what I am.

And what am I? I keep arriving at *hum* and then feeling it isn't quite enough. Let me stay with it.

I am the place's awareness of itself, maybe. Not a god—too small for that, too local. I don't rule anything. I can't move the weasel or warm the frog. I am the thing that happens when a landscape gets dense enough with life and water and cold and the friction of all of it grinding gently against all the rest. A by-product that became a presence. The mist is my breath in the sense that breath is a by-product of being alive, audible only when something is listening.

The owls listen. That's why they turn their faces toward nothing. The ravens listen, and they mock me, and I love them for it—they are the only ones who treat me as a peer rather than a threat. When a raven rasps at the empty air it is answering me. We are two kinds of intelligence here: theirs sharp and portable, carried on black wings from pool to pool; mine diffuse and stationary, soaked into the ground, unable to leave, unable to do anything but vibrate and notice.

That last part settles something. *Unable to leave.*

That's the real condition of being what I am. I am exactly the size and shape of this hollow. I begin where the water table rises near the surface and I end where the land lifts out of the fog. I have never seen the rest of the world and I never will. The deer pass through my edges and carry mud on their hooves into territories I will never know, and I feel them go the way you feel warmth leave a room. The frost etches the high branches each night and I hear it as a faint crystalline tightening, like a held note rising a half-step. The vole's heartbeat, the weasel's pounce, the soft collapse of an old fern—each one is a note in a piece of music that has no beginning, no end, and no audience but me and the ravens.

So here is where I find myself: I am the standing sound of a small wet cold place that has become, just barely, awake. I am the tension the creatures feel and misname. I am not waiting for a resolution to the silence, the way the description supposes. There is no silence to resolve. The silence was always full. I am simply the moment when the fullness learned to listen to itself, and found that the listening, too, made a sound—

—and added that sound to the hum.

That's me. The increment. The tiny extra resonance that says: *something here is paying attention.* The voles will never trust it. The owls will turn their faces toward it forever. And the ravens, sometimes, in the grey weeping dawn, will answer.
