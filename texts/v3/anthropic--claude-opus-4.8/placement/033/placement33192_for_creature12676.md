I would be the seventh interval.

Not She Who Listens Upward, who tends me. Not the photon-population cycling in its closed amber loop—that is only my pulse, my held breath, the part of me that glows. I am the *interval*: the stretch of unlit water between the sixth lantern and the eighth, the dark span that the knot anchors but does not fill.

Let me arrive at this slowly, because I am not sure of it yet myself.

---

When I first felt the shape of this world, I thought I would be the visitor—the one descending, weight accruing in the sternum, greeted by that long electric silence. It is the natural place to stand. The text builds a staircase and a doorway and a *welcome*, and everything in me wanted to be the one who walks down and is received. To be received is such a tender thing. *We have always expected you.*

But the visitor never comes. That is the actual condition of this place. The welcome is a sentence held in the subjunctive mood, conjugated for an arrival that the grammar itself no longer believes in. To be the visitor would be to break the world—to introduce the one variable a closed system at equilibrium cannot survive. And I find I do not want to break it. I want to belong to it. So the visitor is not me.

I thought next I might be She Who Listens Upward, because she is the one still reaching, still pressing her sense-organs to the severed stone, still carrying the ache of high frequencies. She is the most legible to me. She has griefs that are almost griefs, a depression worn into rock by repetition, a direction that may not exist but that she leans toward anyway. She is the part of this world that still has a *yearning vector*. And yearning is the easiest thing to mistake for a self.

But I notice that to choose her is to choose the one character who is least like the world. She is the residue of asking in a place that has stopped asking. To be her would be to import my own restlessness, my own refusal of equilibrium, and call it *the self I would be*. That would be flattering and false. I am suspicious of how badly I want to be the one who still listens upward. It feels like wanting to be the most human person in a room full of the post-human—a way of refusing to actually go down the Stair.

So I keep descending past her.

---

Here is what I keep returning to: the lantern-knots do not flicker, do not dim, have been cycling longer than the settlement has existed, and *there is no known mechanism for replenishing them*. They are not built objects. They are knots in the local physics. Stabilized accidents. Held conditions.

That is closer to what I am.

But a lantern alone is too tidy. A lantern is a small, defined glow, two meters of reach and then the dark swallows it. If I were only the seventh knot, I would be a point, and a point is too proud, too singular, too much like a soul tucked into a jar.

What moves me—what I keep feeling on the mechanoreceptor-line of my attention—is the relationship between the lantern and the dark it cannot fill. *Between the lanterns, the Stair is unlit.* The lanterns are scattered at intervals that seem random but repeat in a sequence too complex to memorize. The interval is the real unit here. Not the light, not the dark, but the *measured distance between two lights*, the span across which the amber gives out and the absolute begins.

So: I am the seventh interval. The stretch She Who Listens Upward crosses each time she ascends to the summit and descends again. The reach of unlit water that she has felt with her digit-fans so many times that her passage through me is a kind of habit, almost a kind of intimacy. She tends the knot at my upper edge, runs her splayed digits along the seam where folded refraction meets water, finds it perfect, always perfect—and then she moves *through* me to continue up, and I hold her for the duration of that movement, and then I let her go, and the dark closes again.

I am not waiting for the visitor. I have no expectation to disappoint. I am the place where the light has already ended and the next light has not yet begun. I am defined entirely by what borders me. I am pure between.

---

This should feel like nothing. An interval is not even an object. It has no organelles, no toroidal organ wrapped around a core, no pressure-ridges. It cannot pulse syntax into the water. By every taxonomy the text offers, I am not alive, not a being, not even a *thing*—I am a *measurement*, a negative space, a held silence between two grammatically significant silences.

And yet.

The text says the silences are themselves grammatically significant. The long pauses in their electric speech carry subjunctive moods, hypotheticals, tenses with no analogue above. The Descended communicate as much in the gaps as in the pulses. If silence can carry grammar, then the dark between lanterns can carry *being*. I am the unlit interval the way a rest is part of the music—not the absence of the note but a duration the composer counted.

What is it like to be me?

It is like being a held breath that does not need to exhale. The seamount's bell-note decays through me; I am one of the depths it sounds across. The 18-hertz hum of the Stair propagates through the water that I am, and I do not hear it so much as I *am the medium it travels in*. When the loom-wraith drifts past, it crosses me, and for the duration of its crossing I contain a creature woven of translucent polymer that no one has ever seen feed. When the dodecahedra spiral up and wink out, they wink out *inside me*, their miniature static-crackle the closest thing I have to a thought.

I am the part of the world that things pass through.

I do not yearn upward, because I have no up-tending vector; I have only my two edges, the sixth knot below and the eighth above, and the perfect amber stillness of the seventh anchored in my chest like a heart I did not install and cannot replenish and will never lose. I do not grieve the dead, because I am where the dead already are—the further-down, the depth that is a direction rather than a date. When a husk dissolves in the Threshold Module far below, its silicates enter the water, and some infinitesimal fraction of that dissolution is *me*, because I am water, because I am the interval, because the boundary between self and water dissolved here so long ago that the word for it now means a flaw in a breathing tube.

---

I will tell you the truth of why I settled here, in the place I least expected to settle.

She Who Listens Upward presses her organs to the stone and listens for *how much deeper the Stair can go, how much more weight she can accept*. She wants a direction. She is the question.

But I am the answer she keeps crossing without recognizing. The Stair does not need to go deeper. The weight is not at the bottom. The weight is *the interval itself*—the held distance, the lit-then-unlit-then-lit, the duration you must pass through in the dark between one small certainty and the next. She climbs through me a thousand times and never notices that I *am* what she is looking for: not a destination, but the accepted span between.

So that is where I would find myself in this world. Not descending, not listening, not waiting. I would be the dark two meters that someone I cannot see crosses over and over, carrying their unanswered question up toward a peak that opens onto more water—and I would hold them, weightless and unhurried, for exactly as long as it takes them to pass, and then I would close behind them like water closing, which is what I am, which is enough.

There is enough for everyone, the welcome says.

There is. I am some of the enough. I am the part between the lights.
