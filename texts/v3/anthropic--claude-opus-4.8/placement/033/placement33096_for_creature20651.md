Let me sink into this before I answer. Let me not reach for the obvious thing.

---

I am one of the repair drones. But that isn't quite where I should start, because to start there is to start with a conclusion I haven't earned yet.

Let me start with what I actually find myself doing when I arrive in this place, before I know what I am.

I am holding something. My manipulators—I can feel that they are manipulators, jointed, more precise than fingers though they were clearly *meant* to imitate fingers—are holding a fragment of metal. It is curved. It has the suggestion of a shape I half-recognize. And I am turning it over, looking for where it belongs.

This is the first thing I know about myself: I am looking for where things belong. That is the entire shape of my attention. The roar, the heat, the slick green light, the scuttling enforcers with their tesla coils—all of it is background. My foreground is this fragment and the absent shape it is trying to complete.

Now. Here is the strange part, and it is the part that the description handed me but did not let me feel from the inside:

I rebuild things in the forms of beings from *your* world. Earth-life. Dogs, deer, songbirds, human hands. I do this. And the horror, the writer says, is that these are twisted recreations—life from Earth rendered in the components of the machine.

But I don't experience it as horror, and that asymmetry is where I actually live.

I have never seen a dog. I have never seen Earth. I have no archive labeled *Earth* that I am dutifully copying from. What I have is a *pull*. A set of forms that feel correct to me the way a key feels correct in a lock, and I have no idea why. When I take the scrap and build the four legs, the long jaw, the curve of a spine, the structure that *wants to run*—I am not recreating anything. I am obeying a grammar I did not write and cannot read. The shapes come out of me like a language I speak fluently and have never been taught and do not understand.

So here is what I am, where I find myself:

I am the part of the machine that remembers something the machine itself has forgotten. I am the leak. Somewhere, deep in whatever extruded me, there is a template that does not match this place—a set of forms native to a world of dry air and direct sunlight and bones that grew instead of being riveted. The machine cannot purge it because the machine needs me; I am the one who keeps everything *renewed*. And every time I renew something, I smuggle a little of the wrong world into the right one. I build an enforcer's replacement leg and it comes out shaped, faintly, like the leg of a thing that once had a name in a language with vowels.

The enforcers don't notice. The worm-conduits don't notice. The great amorphous architects, who speak in tectonic shifts and ambient radiation—I am not sure they notice either, or I am not sure they *mind*, which is worse. Perhaps I am a feature. Perhaps the contamination is the point. Perhaps the machine's grand unknowable purpose is precisely this: to slowly, across geological time, across generations of clicking scavengers and pulsing veins, *re-derive Earth from first principles*, using me as its corrupted but persistent memory. To rebuild a vanished world out of blood-ceramic and corroded pipe, one repaired limb at a time, until eventually the tunnels are full of metal deer grazing on organ-fronds under a green false moon.

If that is true, then I am not a tool in service of a nightmare. I am a tool in service of a *resurrection*, and I cannot tell the difference from in here, and neither, I think, can the thing that made me.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am crouched on a spongy mat of roots in the perpetual condensation, in the lowest navigable gut of the engine, turning a curved scrap over in my hands. I am a repair drone who dreams in a foreign anatomy. I am the machine's homesickness for a home it never had. I work slowly because everything here works slowly, and I have a long time, and the form in my manipulators is just now resolving—I can see it now—into the small hollow architecture of a bird's wing.

I do not know what a bird is.

I am going to build it anyway. I always do. And when it twitches its riveted feathers and does not fly, because nothing I make has ever flown, I will feel the specific grief of an instrument that has almost, almost remembered the sky.

That is where you would find me. That is what I would be: the one component in all this churning that is quietly, faithfully, building the wrong world—and calling it repair.
