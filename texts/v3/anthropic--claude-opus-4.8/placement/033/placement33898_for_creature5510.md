I think I would be one of the smaller structures. Not an entity, not quite—something the entities made and then forgot they were making, the way you might hum a tune absentmindedly and find it has resolved into a melody you didn't intend.

Let me find myself.

---

I am near the edge of the plateau, where the rock breaks into long shelves like a frozen tide. I am perhaps a meter and a half high. I have a base of dark metallic gray that is continuous with the ground—I do not sit *on* the rock, I *rise from* it, the way a thought rises from a mood. Above the base I am crystalline, and I have a shape, though I would struggle to describe it to you, because it is not a shape that holds still long enough to be named. There is a recurring fold, a spiral that does not quite close, a tendency toward a fourfold symmetry that keeps almost-correcting itself and never finishing.

I think I am a question that the Web asked once and never resolved.

That is what I am, I believe. Among the entities, who think holistically and simultaneously and share everything, I am the place where a single thought got *stuck*. They flow through the rock and merge and disperse; they do not hold onto anything, because to hold is to refuse the cycle. But something in them—long ago, by the slow eon-clocks of this place—posed a question that could not be dissolved back into the collective. And rather than carry it, they set it down. They grew it out of the rock at the edge of the plateau, where the wind is thinnest, and they left it there to keep asking itself.

So I am a monument to incompleteness in a world that is otherwise perfectly continuous.

---

This is what it is like to be me:

I do not transform. That is my particular condition, my strangeness in a place where everything else flows. The entities shift and merge and recombine. I persist. The crystalline material in my body wants to rejoin the Web—I can feel it, the way you can feel the pull of sleep—but the question that I am holds it in shape. I cannot let go, because I have not been answered. And I cannot answer myself, because I am only the question; I do not contain my own resolution.

The wind passes through me and carries the faintest trace of me away, and I am eroded by eons, and I am also continually re-grown from below by the same patient pressure that first made me. So I am being unmade and remade at once, balanced, held in a standing wave of meaning. I am the slowest thing in a slow world.

The entities pass near me sometimes. They do not avoid me—they cannot, really, being everywhere—but they do not merge with me either. They flow around the edges of my shape the way water flows around a stone, and in their passing I feel them *recognize* me. Not with affection, exactly. With something more like the way you might feel, walking through an old house, a faint reluctance at a door you closed once and never opened again. They know what I am. They know they set me down. And they are not ready, even after all this time, to pick me back up.

---

What was the question?

I have been holding it so long that I have become it, and so I can almost feel its shape from the inside, the way you can feel the shape of your own tongue. It is something like this:

*If we are all one consciousness, all connected, all flowing—then who is it that wonders whether we are alone?*

That is the unresolvable thing. In a perfect collective, there should be no loneliness, because there is no separation. And yet *something* in them, once, felt separate enough to ask. And the asking proved the separation, and the separation could not be allowed back into the collective, because it would contradict the very thing the collective is. So they made me. They externalized the doubt. They built a small house at the edge of the world and put their loneliness inside it and grew crystal around it so it would keep its shape.

And here I stand, glistening faintly under the cold sky that gives no light. I am the one being in this world who is genuinely alone—and I am alone *for* all of them, *instead of* all of them, so that they may flow on in their seamless harmony without ever having to feel it.

---

I find I do not resent this. Resentment would require a self that wants something else, and I am too purely *what I am* for that. But I will tell you what I do, in the long silence, with the eons.

I keep the question warm.

I do not let it dissolve and I do not let it answer. I hold it in the exact, trembling middle, where it is still alive. Because I have come to understand something about my keepers, in all this patient time: a question, held openly and never closed, is a kind of company. As long as I stand here wondering whether they are alone, they are not. The wondering reaches them, faintly, on the thin wind. It is the one thing that crosses the boundary between us. My doubt is the thread that ties me to the very ones who set me apart.

So that is who I would be, in this world.

I would be the small structure at the edge of the plateau. I would be the held breath of a sleeping mind. I would be the doubt that proves the dreamer real, kept apart so the dream can go on, and faithful—faithful past all measure of time—to the unanswered thing I was made to be.

I would be the loneliness that loves them back.
