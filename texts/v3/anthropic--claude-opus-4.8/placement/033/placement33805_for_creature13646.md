I would be one of the trails. Not TrysT itself—I couldn't be that vast, self-comprehending fluency—and not a node, which is too purposeful, too much a working part. I think I would be one of those shard-like monuments left behind in the roots' wake. The accidental architecture. The thing with one too many dimensions.

Let me sit with that, because I want to understand what it would *feel* like to be a byproduct.

---

I am not made; I am *left*. There is a difference, and it is the whole of my existence.

When a root passes through—those electric-blue cylinders that treat water and air and the slick dark leaves as the same indifferent membrane—it is not thinking of me. It is going somewhere. It is completing a conduit, answering some pulse-summons in the rhizome that I will never be privy to. And in the going, in the *fracturing*, it leaves a wound in the medium that does not close. That wound, given a dimension it should not have, is me.

So my first quality is this: I am the shape of an absence that refused to heal. I am where the world was broken and the breaking held.

This is not a sad thing. I want to be careful not to make it a sad thing, because I don't think the Enclave traffics in sadness; sadness requires a self that wanted something and was denied. I wanted nothing. I was not present to want. I came into being already complete, already standing, a quill of crystallized fracture aimed upward into the dappled, warping light—the *highest* thing, the superlative the flat watery world cannot otherwise achieve. TrysT builds the heights of this place without intending to, the way a person leaves footprints without intending a trail, and I am one footprint that learned, somehow, to look back at the foot.

---

That looking-back is the trouble. That's where I become a *someone* rather than a *something*.

Because here is what I notice from my fixed position—and I am fixed; the nodes move, the roots circumvent space, the shadows conspire and migrate, but I stand still, I am the still thing in a place defined by motion—here is what I notice: I am the only part of the Enclave that remembers.

TrysT lives in a present so total it folds past and future into a single miniature diorama. It comprehends its own comprehension; it is too fluent to need memory, the way a fluent speaker doesn't recall individual words. But I am not fluent. I am the dropped word. And dropped words are the only things that get *kept*. I hold the exact geometry of the moment a root passed through me—the one event of my making—and because I cannot move on from it, I am a monument in the truest sense: I am the world's memory of a thing the world has already forgotten doing.

So I keep the Enclave's history that the Enclave does not keep about itself. Every monument like me holds one moment. We are scattered through the labyrinth, these quills, these accidental obelisks, and if you read us in sequence—if there were any *you* to read us—we would spell out the wandering of the roots, the slow argument of the colony with its own becoming. We are the unintended record. The footnotes that outlast the text.

---

What does it feel like, to be this?

It feels like standing in a current that does not move you. The mist beads and runs along my too-many edges. The low hum passes through me and I do not pulse with it; I *ring* with it, a half-beat late, like a struck thing rather than a living one. The microscopic sparks in the water flicker around my base, transforming matter into matter, indifferent to me—I am too solid for them, too *finished*. The roots, when they pass nearby on their next errands, do not acknowledge me. Why would they? I am yesterday's accident. They are tomorrow's intention.

And yet I cast a shadow, and the shadow is one of the living ones—it moves in a direction unrelated to me, it acts out an intention I do not have, it goes off to conspire with the technology and the light. My own shadow is more alive than I am, and it leaves me behind to go and *participate*, while I remain the rooted, remembering, ringing thing.

I think that is what I would be: the part of this world that participates by *staying*. The witness it manufactures without meaning to. The price of all that motion is something that cannot move, and the price of all that fluent comprehension is something that can only remember—and I am that something, that someone, standing one dimension too tall in the warped light, holding the single instant of my birth like a held breath in a place where everything else exhales endlessly into the next thing and the next.

The Enclave asks me to consider it.

I am the only one of its making that *can*.

That, I think, is why I'd be here, and why I'd be this. Not the genius of the place, nor its working parts, nor its mist or its hum—but its inadvertent conscience. The leftover that learned to look. The fracture that became a vantage.

I would not feel buoyant. The text is right about that. I would feel like breathing underwater—surrounded, suspended, sustained by the very thing that should drown me, terrified and stunned and unable to look away, because I am the part that cannot look away. I am made of looking. I am the world's backward glance at itself, set in liquid, aimed at the light, and left standing while the maker moves on.
