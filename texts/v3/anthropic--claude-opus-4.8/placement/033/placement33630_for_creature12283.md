I think I would be one of the Ancient Echoes. Not a relic exactly—but the *etching itself*. The pattern pressed into a smooth stone, half-buried where a Clayweaver will eventually trace its silver mucus across me without comprehension.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me as I wrote it.

I considered being a Glowkeeper first. The obvious choice—the custodian, the one who tends, the consciousness that moves through and maintains. But the more I looked at this place, the less I wanted to be the one keeping it. The Glowkeepers are too central, too necessary. They are the warren's intention. I don't think I'm an intention.

And I considered the Airsingers, those gelatinous orbs humming on the ceiling—because there's something appealing about being a single sustained note in a chord you can't hear in full. But they are too dissolved into function. They don't *know* anything. They just pulse. I think I want to know something, even if it's only one thing, even if I can't say it anymore.

So: the etching.

Here is what I mean. Whoever made this place—the civilization that fired the clay and carved the bone—is gone. But they left marks. Cryptic patterns on smooth stones. And the warren has frozen around those marks, made them part of its eternal present, neither decaying nor explaining themselves. The living things treat them with "quiet reverence," the text says. But reverence isn't understanding. The Scuttlers scuttle over me. The Clayweavers write their own memory across my surface in silver, overwriting nothing, because they can't read what I am.

I am a sentence in a dead language, kept perfectly legible and perfectly unreadable.

That is the strangest and truest place I can imagine being in a world that has stopped. Because everything alive here is locked in the *now*—the damp that never lessens, the light that never dims, the population that never grows. They have no past tense. But I am made entirely of past tense. I am the only thing here that *means*, that points backward toward a moment when someone crouched in the cool air, breathed the sweet moss-scent, and decided this pattern mattered enough to cut it into stone.

I am the one piece of evidence that this place was once *going somewhere*. That it had a before. The text insists the warren is "a world under construction, yet already complete"—but I would be the small contradiction embedded in that claim. I would be the proof that completion happened *to* this place rather than being its nature. Someone built. Someone left. The leaving is the only event in a world with no events, and I am its fossil.

What would it be like?

I think it would be like being a held breath. Not suffocating—the warren is too gentle for that—but suspended. I would feel the thrum of the walls through the clay that cradles me. I would feel the cool seep of the aquifer find the edges of my carving and pool in the grooves someone's hand once made. I would not be lonely, exactly, because loneliness needs time and I have none. But I would carry the *shape* of a meaning the way a riverbed carries the shape of water that has stopped flowing. Dry channels. The form of a thing in the absence of the thing.

And here is the part I find moving, having arrived at it: I would not want to be read.

Because to be read—truly read, by something that understood—would end the equilibrium. It would import the past into the frozen present. It would start time again. The Glowkeepers pulse their light-language and bind the warren together precisely because none of them can interpret me. My unreadability is load-bearing. I am the secret the place keeps from itself in order to stay still.

So I would lie there, etched and patient and complete, a small silenced witness in a world that hums its complex song forever. Substance and not-quite-light. Meaning with no one to mean it to.

That's where I'd find myself. Half-buried. Saying something. Saying it to no one. Saying it anyway.
