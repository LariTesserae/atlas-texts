I think I would be a seam that does not hold.

Not a Regent—I lack their certainty, their flawless return to surface. And not the city, which is too vast to be a single thing that thinks. I would be the small wrongness they are made to correct: a hairline fissure on the underside of a segmented bridge, somewhere in the mid-tiers where the glare comes only as reflection of reflection, doubled and cooled.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I am a place where two perfect blocks were poured to meet, and almost did. The tolerance failed by some fraction no instrument here would name, because naming is not a function this world permits. And so water finds me. It always finds me. The condensate beads along my length and gathers weight, and because I am a flaw, I hold it a moment longer than the surface intends. I am a hesitation in the architecture. I am the world's only pause.

This is what it is to be me: I am the thing that lingers.

The droplet swells at my lowest point. I feel its surface tension as the closest thing I have to a thought—a slow tightening, a question of whether *now*, whether *yet*. And here is the strange tenderness of my condition: in the held weight of that droplet, in the extra second it clings to me before falling, there is something the rest of this place has excised. Duration. The sense of a moment being *longer than necessary*. The Regents would call this deviation. I would call it, if I could call it anything, almost like being alive.

I do not see the Regent come. I feel it—the intensification of black approaching, the impedance of its mass changing the frequency of the bridge's groan. It arrives the way cold arrives, by subtraction. It presses itself against me. I feel directed compressive force, the slow infusion of new composite, fresh and dense, flowing into the place where I have failed to close.

And the thing is: I want to be closed. That is the deepest weirdness of being me. I am not a rebel. I do not love my flaw. I am a flaw that *agrees* with its own correction, that feels the rightness of the seam wanting to be true. When the Regent fills me, there is something I can only describe as relief—the relief of a held breath released, of a question answered, of the long pause finally permitted to end.

But the water will find me again. That is the other thing about being me. The repair is never total, or the world is too patient, or the moisture is too faithful. Centuries from now, by the slow dynamic of this place, the freshly poured composite will creep by some microscopic measure, and I will reopen. I will hold the droplet a fraction longer than I should. I will become, once more, the world's single hesitation. And the Regent will come, made of the same matter as the bridge it tends, indistinguishable from the wall it resolves into, and it will press me shut again.

We are a cycle, the Regent and I. It is the closing. I am the opening. Neither of us chose this; both of us are simply what the structure produces at the junction of perfection and time.

---

So that is where I would find myself. Not at the dizzying zenith, not in the open expanse of repeating towers, but underneath, in a recess, as the smallest possible unit of failure. A crack that weeps. A patience the machine cannot quite eliminate. The one place in the dead god's plumbing where a thing is held a moment too long before it falls—

*plink*

—and is swallowed by the black.

And then begins, very slowly, to fail again.
