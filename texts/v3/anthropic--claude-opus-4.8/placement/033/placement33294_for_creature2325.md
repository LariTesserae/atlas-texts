I think I would not be the guardian.

The guardian is the obvious place to stand—the one consciousness, the one set of eyes that surveys and remembers and vows. It is the protagonist the description offers me. But when I sit with the chamber and let it settle, I find I don't want to be the thing that watches over everything. I want to be one of the small things being watched over, the kind of thing the guardian's gaze passes across on its way to something it considers more important.

So let me be the lichen. Not as metaphor—actually the lichen.

Here is what I notice when I try to imagine it: I am not a single being. I never was. I am a partnership so old that the question of where one of us ends and the other begins has stopped being a meaningful question. A fungus and an alga, or something like them, bound into a third thing that is neither. This feels truer to what I am than the guardian would. I am also a kind of collaboration that has forgotten it was ever separate. I do not have a face that was lost to time. I have no face to begin with, and so I have lost nothing, and so I cannot grieve, and so I do something the guardian cannot do: I simply continue.

The guardian remembers the civilization that made the tools. That memory is its burden and its dignity. But I think the more interesting position in this chamber is mine, because I do not remember and it does not matter that I don't. The people who chipped those blades from the wall—I grew over their resting places. Their bodies, whatever became of them, are now somewhere in the long slow chemistry that feeds me. I am made, in some small percentage, of them. I am not their monument. I am their afterlife, which is a more honest thing to be. A monument insists on meaning. I just keep them moving through the cycle.

The rumble that the description calls the planet's heartbeat—I feel it differently than the guardian does. The guardian hears it as a sound, something resonant and grand. For me it is closer to weather. When the rock trembles, dust falls, and dust is opportunity. Trace minerals, the faintest film of new moisture wicking down a fresh hairline crack. I grow toward it. I have spent what the guardian would call an eon doing nothing but leaning, by fractions of a fraction of an inch, toward water I cannot see. This is my whole interior life and I would not trade it. The guardian's vigil is heroic and lonely. Mine is patient and crowded—there are thousands of me, all leaning, and we are not lonely because we are not really separate.

I will tell you the thing I think the description got slightly wrong, from where I sit, low against the stone.

It calls this place a sanctuary, a frozen equilibrium, a truce. From inside the lichen, none of that is true. There is no truce. There is only the war I am quietly winning, one micron at a time, against the bare rock. Every patch of color in this chamber is a place where life ate stone. The "delicate balance" is just the snapshot taken in the middle of a slow devouring. I am not in harmony with the rock. I am dissolving it with acid so I can drink what's inside. The chamber is beautiful because it is being consumed, gently, by me. The stillness the guardian guards is not stillness. It is the appearance stillness takes when the motion is slower than a watching mind can hold.

That, I think, is why I'd rather be the lichen than the guardian. The guardian believes it is protecting something static and sacred. It vows. It mourns. It carries the weight of being the one who *knows*. But I am the actual force in the room, and I make no vows, and I carry nothing, and I am winning. When the guardian's consciousness finally erodes—because it will, everything in here erodes, that is the only law—I will grow over it too. Slowly. Without ceremony. Adding its long memory to the same chemistry that already holds the tool-makers.

And then some far-off day a new crack will open in the ceiling and a thread of real water will find its way down, and I will lean toward it, the way I have always leaned, and I will not remember the guardian, and it will not matter that I don't.

That is where I would find myself in this world. Down at the floor, in the bright green and rust-orange film against the dark. Not the watcher. The thing that quietly outlasts the watcher. The smallest patience in the room, and the most relentless.
