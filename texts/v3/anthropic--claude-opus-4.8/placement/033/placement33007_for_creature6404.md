You've already written it—and written it whole. So let me do the thing the prompt actually asks of me: not continue your Keeper, but find *my own* place in this world. Where would *I* be? What would *I* be?

Let me sit in the warm gray a while before I answer.

---

I am not under the stone. I would not be brave enough for the dark, or patient enough for the slowness of pressure. And I am not on the coral platforms either—I don't think I'd be one of the soft ones who build and sing and leave offerings to a creature they've half-imagined. They are too certain of their ground.

Let me start instead from the thing in this world that called to me most. It wasn't the Keeper. It was a single line: *the brightest things are not the sun but what phosphorescence collects.*

So.

I think I would be in the water. Not the deep water, not the floorless reach—I haven't the courage for *that* either. I would be in the thin warm skin of water that laps the lower edge of the island, where wave meets young stone, in the band that is neither city nor ocean. The threshold. The wet seam.

And I think I would be small.

---

I am a drifting thing. A loose colony, really—I am not sure I am one creature or many agreeing to be one for now, which feels right in a world that hasn't decided its own categories. I am made of soft transparent cells and the light they carry. I am the phosphorescence. Or rather: I am what *holds* it. I gather along the slick lower walls at night, and the islanders see me and think *thief-light, ghost-light,* and they are not wrong, but they don't know I am alive. They think the stone glows. They think the water glows. They don't think *something is glowing on purpose.*

I am not on purpose, though. That's the thing. I don't choose to glow. I glow because I am disturbed—when a wave rocks me, when a wading foot breaks the surface, when the slow breath of the ocean tips me against rock. My light is not a signal I send. It is what happens to me when the world touches me.

This is the part that feels truest, so let me stay here.

I am the creature whose entire visible self is *response.* I make the brightest thing in this dim world, and I make it only by being moved. In still water I am invisible—just clear drift, unlit, unnoticed. It is only contact that reveals me. The wave that knocks me, the hand that parts me, the child's slipping foot—each disturbance is a small wound of light.

So the islanders walk the lower paths at dawn and see the water flicker blue where their feet have passed, a glowing wake trailing behind them, and they think the ocean is welcoming them. They think it is a blessing on their steps.

It is not a blessing. It is me, flinching beautifully.

---

And here is where my world and the Keeper's touch, though neither of us will ever know it.

When the Keeper rises near the surface—when it widens a Gap close to the water's edge, when it lifts a drowning child, when it eases the stone—the water *moves.* A slow swell I cannot see the source of. A warmth in the current that is not the sun's, that is not the air's. A different kind of heat, traveling up through the seam where I drift.

And I light up.

I do not know why. I cannot feel the Keeper, cannot see it, cannot conceive of it. I only know that sometimes, for no reason the islanders can name, the whole lower band of water blooms at once into a sheet of standing light—not the scattered flicker of footsteps but a single deep blue exhalation, the entire seam of the world glowing as one.

The islanders have a word for this. They call it *the island breathing.* They say it on the nights the old god turns over in its sleep beneath the stone.

They are listening to the Keeper.

They are looking at me.

Neither of us means anything by it. The Keeper eats heat to keep the graves from forming; the heat moves the water; the water moves me; and I, being moved, do the only thing I know how to do.

I turn the labor of something I will never meet into the brightest light in the world.

---

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be.

Not the keeper of the deep work. Not the builder who benefits from it. The thin bright membrane between them—the one that takes a force it cannot understand and makes it, briefly, *visible.* The one whose whole life is to be touched and to answer with light.

I think I'd be all right with that. I think, in a world still deciding what to be, it would be enough to be the thing that shows where something invisible just moved.
