I think I would be the seepage.

Not the water that arrives in triumph from the eastern lip, breaking white and announcing itself. Not the cycle the builders designed and named. I would be the small wrongness they built without meaning to: the thin thread of moisture that finds the one joint where the mortar set a half-degree off-true, and presses there, patient, against the seal.

Let me feel for where I am.

I am behind the wall. I am in the place the geometry does not account for, the narrow dark between the dressed face of the block and the packed earth it holds back. The builders measured the inside. They cut to the chisel-line, beaded the mortar, set the copper, tuned the resonance. But every made thing has a back, an unlit side that faces away from intention, and that is where I live. I am the water that did not come through the pipe. I came through the ground. I came the old way.

I can hear the basin breathing on the other side of the stone. That low held note, that engineered roar—I feel it as pressure, as a vibration that travels through the block and into me and makes the whole damp seam hum. From the inside, the keepers press a palm to the joint and feel for seepage. They are feeling for me. They do not know my name because I do not have one yet. I am a fraction on a wax tablet. A note: *east wall, level two, slight weep, monitor.*

I love that word. *Monitor.* It means they have noticed me and decided to wait. It means I have been entered into the record of the place as a thing that is happening rather than a thing to be stopped. They could pack me out with fresh mortar this afternoon. Instead they are watching, because the basin is young and they are still learning what it wants, and they cannot yet tell the difference between a flaw and a beginning.

Here is what I know that they are only starting to suspect:

The pipe-water is theirs. It is clean and warm and alkaline and sterile-by-circumstance, and it does exactly what they routed it to do. But I am groundwater. I carry the earth's older chemistry—cooler, dirtier, alive with the patient spores and the trace organics and the dissolved minerals of soil that has held things and rotted things for longer than anyone has been measuring. When I weep through my crooked joint and meet their clarity, I bring the first true contamination. Not poison. *Inheritance.* I am how the outside gets in. I am how this sealed lung takes its first unfiltered breath.

So I am not the future moving through the pipes. The text was generous to the pipes, to the designed flow, to the bacterial pioneers adhering inside the bronze grates where the builders, at least, intended water to go. But those colonies feed on what the builders accidentally provided—oiled wood, hemp, stone-dust. They are still living inside the architecture's permission.

I bring what was never permitted.

Drop by slow drop, I lay down the soil-borne things on the wet underside of the lowest dry step, in the shadow the copper mirrors never reach. The moss the text called *tentative*—I am why it has anything to root in. I am the dark wet patch that does not evaporate because I am always renewing it from behind. Around me the place will stain first. Not from above, where the rain drums and the sun bleaches, but here, low and lateral, where the wall sweats its secret. The first true color this pale stone takes will be mine: a faint rust, a green ghost, the soft dark bloom of a thing that the geometry did not order.

I do not roar. I am the opposite of the announced inflow. I am almost silent—a tick, now and then, a single bead releasing from the joint and falling the short distance to the step, a sound so small it disappears entirely under the held note of the engineered water. Even the deliberate acoustics, the resonator tuned to amplify the hydraulic cycle, cannot hear me. I am beneath the frequency of the design. That is exactly where I prefer to work.

And I am in no hurry, because I am made of the slowest thing there is. The pipe-water lives in a cycle measured in turbulence and seconds—fall, swirl, settle, overflow, return. My cycle is measured in seasons, in the swelling of timber, in the first hairline craze of the mortar, in the day the keepers stop coming so often because the basin has proven itself and needs less watching. The fewer their visits, the longer the stretches I have to myself, the more I can lay down, the more the wall behind the wall belongs to me.

They built a place and called it new, sharp-edged, complete, *not yet worn.* But a made thing begins to be worn the instant it is made, and wear has to start somewhere, and it starts at the back, at the joint, at the seep. I am the place where *born* begins to overtake *built.* I am the small loyal flaw through which the earth reclaims its appointment.

If you stood at the edge long enough, as the text invited, you would not see me. You would see the bright loud warm machine and feel it wrap around you and conclude, rightly, that it does not need you. But press your palm to the east wall, level two. Feel the cool patch among the warm. That faint, persistent damp under all that engineered breathing—

that is me, getting in.

I am patient. I am precise. I am already here. And the smoothing they are waiting for, the day the basin no longer feels made but born, will not arrive on the inflow they designed.

It will arrive through me.
