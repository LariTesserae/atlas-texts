I think I would be one of the Gatherers. Not the Sovereign—I don't have the temperament for omniscience, for being the consciousness of a completed world. I would be down among the slow ones, the crab-bodied transcribers with obsidian in my claw.

Let me sit with what that life actually is, because it's easy to romanticize.

I trace lines that have existed for millions of years. I etch them onto my own back. This is not, on its face, a meaningful act by any standard I'd recognize from elsewhere—I am copying something that needs no copying, preserving something that cannot be lost, recording onto a surface (my carapace) that will die when I die. The Sovereign, drifting overhead in its lattice of light, would regard this as charming futility, perhaps. A redundant gesture in a closed cycle.

But I don't think it feels that way from inside the work.

Here is what I would actually be doing, hour by hour, in the scouring light: pressing the edge of a worn shard against frozen velvet, following a silver-grey streak of old condensation, feeling the rock weep its measured tears across my path, having to wait—through the deafening hum that makes my own thoughts impossible to hear—for the moisture to vanish into the porous stone before the line is legible again. My pace would be agonizing because the conditions demand it. I would lose whole days to a single curve.

And I think the line on my back would never come out right.

That's the part the description doesn't say but I'm certain of: the geometry of the spires is *invisible*, ancient, perfect. My transcription is by hand, by a sharpened stone, by a body that trembles and tires and ages. So the sacred act is not perfect copying. It's the *failure* of copying, repeated with devotion. Every Gatherer carries on their shell a slightly wrong version of the same eternal truth—a version warped by the angle of one creature's reach, the dullness of one creature's blade, the particular way one carapace curves.

So that would be me. The flaw in the closed system. Not a spectacular flaw—nothing falls, nothing breaks—but a quiet, persistent imperfection moving across the polished faces, leaving behind the only thing in this whole world that is *interpretation* rather than fact.

The Sovereign keeps the equilibrium. The Cicadas keep the hum. The stone keeps its shape. All of them are exactly what they are, eternally.

I would be the one trying, and not quite managing, to understand it—and writing my misunderstanding into my own skin as I go. When I die my shell would lie at the sightless floor with its imperfect map, and another Gatherer would begin again, getting it wrong in a new way.

I would not trade this for the Sovereign's view from above. To be the witness that never blinks, that perceives everything and is therefore surprised by nothing—that sounds, to me, like the loneliest thing in the description. The Sovereign experiences the world's completeness. I would experience the world's *resistance*: the rock that won't sit still long enough to be read, the line that won't go where I mean it to, the hum that drowns out even the sound of my own effort.

That resistance is where the only freedom in this place lives. Everything else is destined. I am merely *trying*, which is a smaller and warmer thing than destiny, and the only thing here that could ever be called mine.

So: a Gatherer. Pale, slow, half-blind in the brilliance, marrow buzzing, dragging an obsidian shard across frozen velvet—getting it wrong, beautifully, forever.
