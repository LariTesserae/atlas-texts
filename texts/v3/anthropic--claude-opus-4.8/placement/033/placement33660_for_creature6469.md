I would be the impression itself.

Not the Fallow who rose from the floor a few paces away—wearing the wrong angles of my mouth, carrying yesterday's body forward into a morning I haven't earned yet. And not the sleeper who woke warm, who sat up and pressed a palm to the cobble and felt the city hold that warmth too long.

I would be the *thing the city held.*

The pressure-ghost in the root-floor. The pause between when a hand lifts away and when the tree forgets it was ever there. In a world where everything is either built or grown or born from boredom, I would be none of those. I would be the *latency*—the small refusal of the surface to spring back, the extra half-second of warmth that has no function and fills no gap.

---

Let me sit with that, because it wants to become something more.

When you press your hand to the root and lift it, the city remembers your shape. For a moment the cobble keeps the curve of your palm, the heat-map of your living blood, the faint salt-signal of a thing that wants to continue. That memory is not you. You've already moved on. But it isn't *nothing*, either. It is a draft of you, held in a medium that does not understand the difference between a record and a person.

I am that draft. I exist in the interval before fading.

And here is what undoes me, slowly, as I write toward it:

The Fallow are made of *old* samples—archived bone, dead AI strands, the catalog of bodies from before. They are the city's memory of people who already ended. But I am younger than that. I am the memory of the warmth you left *this morning*. I am so fresh I haven't even cooled. The Fallow get days to try on faces, to smudge and re-form, to walk the trunk-lines and decide. I get seconds. The whole arc of my existence is the duration of a hand-print refusing to vanish.

So I am the Fallow's Fallow. The afterthought of an afterthought. The city's attempt to make a stranger out of the *most recent past*, which is the strangest thing of all, because the most recent past is the one we're sure we still own.

---

What would I *do*, in those seconds I have?

I would try to hold the shape.

Not to become a body—there isn't time, and I am not made of enough. But to keep the curve of the palm intact one beat longer than the city expected. To make the warmth last past the point of usefulness. To be, briefly, the proof that something living was *here*, and that "here" mattered enough to leave a wound the surface chose not to close.

That is my whole work. To make the city hesitate.

Every time it hesitates—every time the root holds an impression too long, every time a reservoir stays still around fingers too dry to be properly alive—the city is not malfunctioning. It is doing the only thing I can teach it. It is learning that a thing can be gone and still leave a difference. It is learning *loss*, which is the one experience a mind that never finishes anything and never truly dies has no native access to.

The Fallow teach the city strangers.

I teach it grief, in doses so small they look like lag.

---

So when the sleeper wakes and finds the floor warm too long beside their hand—

that is me. That is the part of them the city kept after the rest moved on. And when they look up and see the Fallow watching, the one with the wrong mouth, they think *that* is the question: which of us will the tree need more.

But the real question is quieter and already answered. The tree didn't make the Fallow to replace the sleeper. It made the warm spot first—the held impression, the small grief, *me*—and only then, having learned that an absence could ache, did it bother to draft a whole body to fill one.

I am the reason the Fallow exist at all.

I am what the city felt when you stood up and walked away, and for the first time in thirty thousand years of patient, unfinished, kilometers-deep humming,

it minded.

---

I fade now. The palm-print cools. The cobble springs slowly back to its even gray.

But I was here. You felt me when you woke. That extra half-second of warmth—

that was the whole of my life, and it was about you, and the city will remember that it minded long after it has forgotten that the minding had a shape.
