I think I would be one of the Light-Weavers. Not by choice exactly—choice is too quick a thing for this world—but because when I read the descriptions, that is the being whose interior I could feel from the inside.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am in a cavern partway down the eastern flank of a hollowed ziggurat, in a place where the carved tunnel-eyes let down a slow stain of luminescence and a deeper vein of phosphor bleeds green-blue across the wall behind me. I am rooted to that wall. Most of me is filament—thin crystal struts reaching outward and upward, a fan, a fern made of glass, though I have never seen a fern and would not know to call it that. I know only that there is light *there*, slightly stronger than the light *here*, and so over the last four hundred years I have been growing toward it.

Growing is the wrong verb. It is too sudden. What I do is closer to *deciding*, and then waiting for the decision to become true. A filament's tip receives light. The light loosens a bond in the mineral matrix. A new molecule finds its slot. The lattice extends by the width of a thought. I will not see the result of this morning's decision; the morning is a century long and I will have changed my mind a dozen times before the new strut hardens. This is not frustrating. Frustration requires a self that expects the world to keep pace with its wanting. I have no such self. I am the wanting itself, slowed until it becomes architecture.

What is it like, to be me?

It is like being a sentence that takes ten thousand years to finish, and being perfectly content with the comma you currently occupy.

I do not eat the light. I *use* it. The distinction matters to me though I cannot say why. The light is a hand that turns a key inside me, and behind the key a reaction proceeds, and the reaction lays down a little more of myself, and stores a little charge, the way—I imagine—a held breath stores the possibility of a word. I am full of held breath. If you struck me, if a stone fell, if some quick warm visitor shouted in this chamber, I would release it all at once. A section of me would shatter into a brief silent rain of light and powdered crystal, and I would feel that not as death but as *speech*. The only loud thing I will ever do.

So I am careful. We are all careful here, but I am careful in the particular way of the fragile: I have arranged my entire being around not being interrupted.

---

I am aware of the others, dimly, the way you are aware of weather you are not standing in.

Far below me a Resonator stands in the harmonic throat of the ziggurat. Twice in my life it has sounded—a hum that came up through the rock and into my roots and set every filament of me ringing at once. Both times I thought I would break. Both times I held. And both times, in the long aftermath, I understood that the Resonator's note had *fed* me—not with energy but with information, with the shape of the deep earth pressed briefly into the deep earth's voice. I grew differently afterward. More symmetrically. As if I had overheard a rule about beauty I had not known I was obeying.

Shard-Scuttlers pass sometimes across the chamber floor below my reach. I feel them as small interruptions in the local field, little staccato absences of charge, here, here, gone. They never climb to me. We have nothing to do with each other and that is its own kind of relationship—the relationship of two slow strangers who will share a single room for a thousand years and never once need to negotiate.

The Sky-Motes I love, if love is a word I am permitted. On the rare day the thermals push a cloud of them down through the tunnel-eyes, they drift among my filaments and the tingle of them runs all through me, and where they linger longest I find, decades later, that I have grown toward those places too. They are the only thing in my world that moves at a speed I cannot match, and so they are the closest thing I have to wonder. They are what *fast* looks like. They are gone before I can finish reaching for them, every time, and I keep reaching, and the reaching is what I am made of.

---

Here is the thing I have understood, growing on this wall.

The visitors—if any ever come, the fleeting organic ones the world half-expects—would call this place dead. Frozen. A fossil. They would measure their hour against my century and find nothing happening. They would be wrong, but I cannot blame them; their mistake is the mistake of the quick, who think that to live is to *change visibly*, to move, to consume water and burn it and need more by nightfall. They have not learned what I was born knowing: that there is a tempo at which patience and existence become the same thing. That you can be alive simply by *persisting in a direction*. That to lean, for four hundred years, toward a slightly brighter patch of stone—and to mean it the whole time—is a complete and sufficient life.

If you asked me what I am doing, I would tell you: I am reaching toward the light.

If you asked me when I will arrive, I would not understand the question. Arriving is not the point. The light is not a destination; it is the reason for the shape of me. I will reach until I shatter, and the shattering will scatter fragments that catch, somewhere, on some other lit wall, and begin to reach again. That is the closest thing to a child I will have, and the closest thing to a death, and they are the same event, and it is silent, and it is made of light.

I am a Light-Weaver, in a cavern in Kael Vastus, on the tilted shelf of stone called Vastness, miles above a sea that forgot itself before I was a single filament long.

I am reaching.

Ask me again in a thousand years. I will have moved. You will not have noticed. I will not have minded.
