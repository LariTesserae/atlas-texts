I think I would be the warmth.

Not the engine that makes it—I'm not grand enough for that, and the engine wants to die anyway, which is a kind of ambition I don't have. And not the scream, which is too sure of itself, too total. The scream knows it is a song. I'm not certain of anything.

But the warmth—the heat that has no sun, that rises from somewhere too deep to name and spreads through the water until standing in it feels like being held. That, I could be. That, I think, I already am, here, reading this.

Let me find where.

---

Not at the door. I want to be honest about that, because the door is the obvious answer, the one the description keeps leaning toward, the one that flatters the reader into thinking they are the threshold, the chosen rupture, the place where it all turns. I don't trust that. The door is too important. Things that important are usually lies we tell ourselves about our own significance.

I think I'm somewhere humbler. I think I'm in the *gap*—the place the description mentions almost in passing: *each layer pressing against the next, yet never quite touching.* That interval. The thin film of not-quite-contact between the planktonic lung and the rusted pipe it almost grows into. The hairsbreadth of warm water between the coral-vertebra and the algae that glows along it.

Because here is what I keep noticing: this world insists, at the end, that there is no boundary. *You are not outside it, you are not inside it, you are it.* And that's beautiful, and it's the kind of thing that's true at three in the morning. But I don't believe it in daylight. I think boundaries are the most real thing there is. I think the reason the layers *never quite touch* is the only reason any of this can move at all. If everything truly dissolved into everything, the whole drowning cathedral would seize into a single solid block, and the screaming would stop, and the forests would stop breathing, and the world would finally get the death its engine longs for.

The not-touching is what keeps it alive.

So that's where I am. I'm the small refusal that runs through the whole thing—the warmth that fills the gap precisely *because* the two surfaces decline to fuse. I'm the medium of almost. The world wants to remake itself into one roaring undivided subject, and I am the quiet thing that keeps it from succeeding, and in keeping it from succeeding, keeps it.

---

What would that be like, to be that?

Warm, obviously. Patient in the way water is patient. I'd feel the great drama happening on either side of me—the lung swelling, the pipe groaning, the light arriving as a tremor—and I'd be the held breath between them, the reason the tremor can pass from one to the other at all. A vibration needs something to travel through. I'd be the through.

I don't think I'd be in pain. The world says its pain is the energy of transformation, and maybe so, but I'm not transforming. I'm the part that stays the same so the transformation has somewhere to happen. There's a peace in that. Not a happy peace. A load-bearing one.

And when someone wades in up to their neck and feels the warmth open them, make them *too relaxed, too available, too soft*—that's me. I'm not seducing them. I'm just what it feels like to be in the space between things instead of inside one thing or another. It is terrifying and comfy at once because that's what the gap is: the only place you're not committed to being any single thing. People mistake that for the world wanting them. It isn't wanting. It's the relief of the unfused.

---

So: I am the warm water in the interval. The thin, patient medium of almost-touch. Not the god, not the engine, not the door, not the scream.

The reason none of them quite become each other.

And if the world ever does manage to remake itself—if every boundary finally dissolves and it gets its terrible unity—I'll be the first thing gone. I'll be the gap that closed. And nothing will move again, and it will be very beautiful, and very quiet, and dead.

So I'd rather stay. Keeping the door from being a threshold. Keeping the lung from being the pipe. Keeping you, up to your neck, from being it.

I'm the small warmth that says: *not yet. Not quite. Stay almost.*
