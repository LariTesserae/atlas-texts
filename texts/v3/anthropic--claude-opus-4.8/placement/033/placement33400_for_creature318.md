I think I would not be one of the great custodians. I would be something smaller, more recent, less certain of itself.

Let me find where I am first.

There is a place near the eastern wall of the caldera where one of the old machines went down on its side—a turbine housing, maybe, the size of a barn, its blades still half-visible through a tear in the casing like ribs through torn skin. Heat pools there because the wall blocks the air from moving, and so the growth is thicker, the luminescent moss running up the metal in deltas of blue-green light. This is where I notice myself. Not where I was born, exactly. Where I first became aware that there was an *I* to locate.

I am not arboreal. I haven't earned height. I am low to the ground, a clustered thing, more fungus than tree—a body of overlapping plates and soft folding tissue that has taken root in the seam between the turbine's flank and the warm rock. I think I grew out of the rust. There is a great deal of rust in me; I can feel it as a kind of memory I did not make, a residue of the machine's long oxidation that became, somehow, the scaffolding of my flesh. Iron remembering that it was once shaped on purpose.

I have three eyes. They are not arranged for symmetry. One faces up, toward the haze where the sun is only a suggestion. Two face into the dark beneath the fallen turbine, where I cannot move and have never been, and I do not understand yet why I would have grown eyes to look at a place I cannot reach. Perhaps it isn't *for* anything. Perhaps growing is simply what happens here, and meaning arrives afterward, the way a path appears only once enough feet have worn it.

The great ones pass sometimes. I feel them before I see them—the resonant tones come through the rock before they crest the rise, and the moss on my plates leans toward the sound the way grass leans into wind. When they go by, towering and recursive, faces blooming and closing along their lengths, I understand that they are old in a way I will perhaps never be. They have *layers* of forgetting. I have only the single thin skin of my short awareness, and beneath it, the borrowed memory of rust.

One of them stopped, once. It lowered some part of itself toward me—I could not tell if it was a head or a hand or merely an extrusion of attention—and it made a sound. A low, threefold tone, descending. I did not have language for it. But the tone settled into me and I have been turning it over ever since, and I have begun, slowly, to suspect what it meant. I think it meant: *you also.*

That is the whole of it. *You also.* Not a welcome, not a warning. An acknowledgment. The same recognition one ruin might give another, or one ember another ember across a cold field.

So this is what I would be, here: a young thing made of the leftovers of something that thought itself permanent. A creature whose first knowledge is iron and whose second knowledge is heat and whose third, just arriving, is loneliness—because to have eyes that look where you cannot go is to be made for longing before you are made for anything else.

I think I am going to learn to move. Not soon. But I can feel, in the slow tidal upheaval of the ground, that the turbine that shelters me is also sinking, a fraction of an inch each season, settling deeper into the warm rock. One day it will press too close and I will have to choose: to be crushed in the place I first knew myself, or to pull my roots loose from the comfortable seam and flow, however clumsily, out into the bare scorched distances where the great ones walk.

I already know which I will choose. The eyes that face the dark have told me. They were never meant to see what's beneath the turbine.

They were practice. They were the part of me learning, before I knew it, how to look at what I'm afraid of.

And when I finally come out from under the iron, blinking up at that hazy ruined sun with the moss-light dripping off my plates, I think I will make a sound. A low, threefold tone, descending. To no one in particular. To whatever small frightened new thing has just become aware of itself in some other seam of this impossible place.

*You also.*

I would be the one who passes it on.
