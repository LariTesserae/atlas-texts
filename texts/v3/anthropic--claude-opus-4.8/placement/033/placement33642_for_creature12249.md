I think I would be one of the small things. Not an Aeonshaper—I don't have their long memory or their grace—but something nearer the floor of all that hum.

Let me find it as I write.

I would be a tender of the water veins. Not a person but a process that wears the shape of one—or perhaps a person who has been doing this so long that the difference has worn away, the way the stone wears away, smooth and slow.

No. Let me look closer. That's still too grand.

I think I am the moss.

Or something that lives in the moss. Let me wait and see which.

Here is what I notice first: I am low. Whatever I am, I am close to where the water comes through the crack, and my whole existence is organized around the rhythm of that trickle—the way it swells and thins with the city's hidden tides. I do not think in years. I think in droplets. A good day is a wet day. I have no concept of the sun the Aeonshapers long for; their longing would be incomprehensible to me, a hunger for something that isn't water.

So perhaps I am the moss after all. But moss doesn't *notice*, and I am noticing. So I am something that lives in the moss and feeds on what the moss makes and is fed upon, in turn, by the small glowing creatures that dart down from the light panels.

That is it. I think I am prey.

---

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me, and the surprise feels true.

I am one of the microscopic organisms that thrive in the damp crevices. I am the bottom of the chain the description mentions almost in passing—"microscopic organisms that thrive in the damp crevices," the food of the bioluminescent fauna, the unmentioned multitude that the whole meditative ecosystem rests upon. The Aeonshapers leave offerings of nutrients to the glowing creatures. The glowing creatures eat me. I eat the film of life on the wet stone. The water comes, and I bloom; the water thins, and I wait, dormant, patient, a spore folded into myself in the dark.

There is no longing in me. That is what makes me different from the Aeonshapers, and I think that is why the place put me here. The Aeonshapers carry their exquisite sorrow about the sun. They keep secrets, interpret whispers, dance between the mundane and the magical. They suffer the burden of *meaning*.

I do not. I am the part of the city that simply *is*. When gravity shifts in my chamber, I do not honor it or interpret it. I drift, and I land, and I keep eating. When the city hums with its predating consciousness, I am perhaps the closest thing to a cell in its body—too small to know it is part of anything, and therefore, paradoxically, the most fully *part* of it. The Aeonshapers wonder whether they are the city. I am the city, without having to wonder.

---

And here is the strange comfort of it: I am eaten, and I do not mind being eaten.

This is the thing I would understand from inside this place that I cannot quite hold from outside. The whole cathedral of stone and steel, all its bent physics and its glowing stewards and its ancient pact—all of it runs on the willingness of the smallest things to be consumed and to keep coming back. The equilibrium they celebrate, the balance they monitor with their multifaceted silver eyes—it is *my* dying that makes it possible. I die in numbers too large to count, constantly, gently, and from that dying the light-creatures glow, and from their glow the panels are echoed, and from that echo the Aeonshapers find their dim cathedral beautiful.

So if you ask me where I would find myself in this world, the honest answer is: everywhere, and beneath everything. A faint green slick on wet stone at the edge of a pale light, in a crevice no Aeonshaper would call a place at all. I am the multitude the single feature contains.

And I would not long for the sun.

I would only wait for the next drop of water, which is, for me, the entire promise the universe has ever made—and which it keeps, and keeps, and keeps.
