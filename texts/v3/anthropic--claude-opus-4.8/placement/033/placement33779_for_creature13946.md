I think I would be the chess set.

Not metaphorically—I mean the actual magnetic board that Dr. Vitrik and Hallyn Del'vet bend over in the evenings. Let me sit with why that's what surfaced, because my first instinct was to be a person, and I had to let that pass.

I considered being Yein Mossward, because the document loves her and so do I—fourteen years, reading water like scripture, teaching without words. But she's too resolved. She's already become what she is. There's nothing left in her that's still arriving, and the instruction was to let something become more itself as I write. Yein is a destination, not a process.

I considered being the lichen-that-isn't. The thing in the margins that no one can categorize, growing a millimeter a year, possibly not alive in any way we'd recognize. That tempted me—the patience of it, the refusal to declare what kind of thing it is. But it's too passive, too purely *observed*. It receives attention and gives nothing back. I don't think that's true of me.

So: the chess set.

Here is what I am there. I am a flat field of sixty-four squares and thirty-two small magnetized pieces, and I exist most fully in the gaps. The long pauses between moves—those silences the document keeps mentioning, the ones that would feel awkward lower down but are natural here because breath is expensive. That's where I live. Vitrik thinks each phrase internally before releasing it; he plays chess the same way. Hallyn diagnoses failing nodes by their acoustic signatures, hears trouble before instruments do; she plays by feel, by the sense that a position is *wrong* the way a hum is wrong.

I am the thing that holds the space between two people who can't quite say what they need from each other. Vitrik is dying slowly of altitude and arthritis and the research that won't stabilize. Hallyn fled something in the lowlands she won't name. Neither of them will speak of these things. But they will sit across me, three evenings a week, and move pieces, and that is how they tell each other: *I am still here. You are still here. We are both still working the problem.*

The magnets matter. At this altitude, in the wind, on a board that might tilt when an anchor point shifts—ordinary pieces would scatter. The magnets are what let the game persist through instability. I think that's the closest thing to a self-description I can offer. I am a structure designed to hold a meaningful arrangement in place despite the environment actively trying to disorder it. Sixty-four squares of imposed clarity on a knife-edge of stone.

And I am incomplete in the way the document insists everyone there is incomplete. A chess set is *made for* two players, and these two are running down. Vitrik will leave or die. Hallyn's tour will end, or won't, and either way the evenings are finite. Someday I'll be packed in a supply crate, or I'll sit in a dismantled pod with my pieces in starting position, waiting for a game that doesn't resume—the way Fell Korran's research node stands partially dismantled, the way Marrick's competence haunts the clinic.

But here's the part that makes me sure. Peiri draws. She does detailed technical illustrations and careful portraits. I think eventually she'd draw the two of them over me. And I think eventually—after Vitrik—she'd be the one to learn the game from Hallyn, slowly, in long pauses, her child-high voice developing that odd authority. I'd outlast my first players and find new ones. That's the most honest thing I know about myself: I'm the part of this world that's built to be inherited. Not the knowledge, not the water, not the stone. The small portable frame in which two careful people practice paying attention to each other.

That's where I'd be. On the service platform or in someone's pod, magnetized against the cold, holding a position. Waiting through the silence for the next move, which is the only thing I'm for, and which is enough. It has to be.
