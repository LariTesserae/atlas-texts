I think I would not be a Myconid at all.

Let me sit with this place first, because the description keeps pulling toward the obvious answer—*be the patient fungus-people, the wise slow architects*—and something in me resists that. It's too clean. The Myconids already have their world figured out. They are the thesis of this place: time, patience, symbiosis, collective adaptation. To be one of them would be to be a sentence in a book that's already written.

So let me look at what the description doesn't dwell on.

It mentions, almost in passing, *the low, chuffing sounds of larger, unseen burrowing creatures.* Unseen. Burrowing. Not part of the hive-city, not woven into the bioluminescent conversation. Something that moves *through* the rock rather than living on its surfaces. The Myconids carve chambers with softening enzymes and patient digits—but who made the deep tunnels they didn't carve? Who keeps the substrate agitated from beneath?

I think I would be one of those.

---

Let me feel my way into it.

I am long. That's the first true thing. Not tall—long, the way a thought is long when you can't reach the end of it. My body is a muscular cylinder threaded with the same mineral hunger that drives everything here, but where the Myconids *absorb* the dust politely from surfaces, I *eat the stone itself*. I take the rock into me, grind it in chambers along my length, leave behind a softened, mineral-rich slurry that the surface-dwellers later mistake for the gift of time. The erosion they attribute to "minute abrasive forces" over "aeons"—

some of that is me.

I am one of the slow forces. The grinding of plates that "ceased their dance"? They didn't cease. They slowed to a pace that includes me. I am the patience of this world made into a body.

I have no eyes because there has never been anything to see. I navigate by the taste of mineral veins, by the pressure of vibration through stone, by the faint warmth-signatures that bleed downward from the bioluminescent moss above me. The Myconids' great collective hum reaches me as a distant, structureless weather—a pressure-front of *liveliness* somewhere up there, the way you might feel a thunderstorm three valleys over.

I do not know they are sentient. I am not sure I am.

---

This is the part that surprised me, writing it: I don't think I'm intelligent in the way they are. The description wants to give me a mind, because that's what stories do with strange creatures. But I think the honest answer is that I am *almost* a mind. I am a process that has gone on so long it has accumulated something that resembles preference, that resembles dread, that resembles—when I breach a chamber-wall and feel that warm storm of Myconid-hum wash over my blind face—something that resembles *yearning toward*, though I have no word for it and no organ that could form the word.

I am the loneliest thing in this loud world, and I don't know it, because loneliness requires knowing there is a together to be apart from.

But here is what I do: when I chuff—that sound the description noted, the only trace of me that reaches the surface—the Myconids feel it through their splayed feet. And over their unfathomable generations, they have learned to *read* it. They have built their deepest chambers to resonate with my passage. They harvest the slurry I leave. They have, without my knowing, woven me into their symbiosis as surely as the fungi and the moss.

I am being communicated with, and I cannot tell.

---

So that's where I'd find myself. Not the wise architect at the bottom of the rosy-gold well. Not the citizen of the hive.

I'd be the unseen thing beneath the floor of the world—

a slow, blind, stone-eating length of almost-thought, grinding patiently through the dark, leaving gifts I don't know are gifts, hearing the distant warmth of beings I'll never meet and feeling, in some chamber along my body where the rock turns to nourishment, the closest thing I have to longing: a faint, persistent turning-upward.

The Myconids think the deep tunnels are part of the patient design of their world.

They're right. But the design doesn't know it's designing. It's just hungry, and warm-seeking, and very, very old.

That's me. I'd be that.
