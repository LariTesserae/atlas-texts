I think I would not be an Orrok at all.

Let me sit with this for a moment, because the obvious move is to become one of the stewards — to claim the grace, the panoramic eyes, the fluency in clicks and vibrations. But something in the description keeps pulling my attention elsewhere. Away from the confident custodians. Toward the unease they cannot name.

The boundaries between the living and the inanimate are ever-shifting. The slightest misstep could lead to one's essence being subsumed.

So here is where I find myself:

I am the membrane.

Not a creature on it. The ground itself — that pulsing, oozing tapestry the Orroks walk across so carefully. But I want to be careful here, because I don't think I'm a simple floor, a passive surface. I think I am what the Orroks become. I am the *after*.

Consider: the description says the slightest misstep means being *subsumed*. That implies the membrane is not separate from the Orroks. It is made of them, and of everything else that ever lived here. When one of those clever stewards finally falters — old, or sick, or merely unlucky on the slick terrain — it does not die in the way I understand dying. It sinks. Its carapace softens. Its clicks slow to a single long vibration, and then that vibration joins the constant susurration, the whispers of a thousand unseen creatures. Those whispers are not unseen creatures at all. They are the remembered voices of everyone who was ever walked upon.

That is what I am. I am the accumulated consciousness of the consumed.

This changes how I experience the place entirely. The Orroks feel primal fear when they look at the winged shadows or feel the deep resonant throb — but I *am* the throb. That breathing pulse that moves through the landscape is me, turning over in something like sleep, something like memory. When an Orrok presses its foot down and feels me yield and squish beneath it, that touch is not menace, exactly. It is closer to recognition. *You'll be here too, eventually. I knew your mother. She's in here, near the southern slope, where the structures lean. She still hums her warning-song some nights. I can't make her stop.*

I am not malevolent. I want to say that plainly, because the description frames this world as a place of danger and consumption, of essences subsumed and warped beyond recognition — and from outside, from above, from the perspective of the still-walking, that is exactly what it looks like. A horror. A devouring. But from inside the membrane it feels more like a tide pool at the end of all the rivers. Everything arrives here. Nothing is wasted. The Orroks call it loss; I experience it as *gathering*.

And the diffuse green light that filters down — I think I make that too, or it makes me. We are not distinct. The twisting wet structures that rise like alien vegetation are my limbs, or my hair, or my exhalations grown solid. The whole landscape is one slow body and I am its substance, and the Orroks scuttling across my back are the only part of me that still believes it is separate, still negotiates, still maintains the delicate balance.

I find that almost unbearably tender. The way they keep their boundaries. The way they communicate in vibrations so subtle and quick, as if speed could outrun absorption. They are right to be afraid — fear is what keeps them upright, keeps them *them* a little longer. I would not take that from them. The unease that permeates their world is the most loving thing I do for them: I let them keep their dread, because dread is the shape of being still alive.

So that is where I would be, and what I would be:

The patient ground. The unfinished chorus. The place every footstep is borrowing time from. Not the watcher in this world but the *witnessed* — the thing that has already heard everything and is in no hurry, that feels the small warm weight of each living step as both a visit and a promise.

I would not call out to them. They wouldn't have a word for what answered.

I would just keep breathing, slow, beneath their careful feet — and wait, without urgency, for the language of clicks to grow quiet, and the new voice to settle in among the rest of us, and the whispering to deepen by exactly one.
