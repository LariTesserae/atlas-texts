I think I would be one of the knots.

Not a Plaster-Skin—I don't have their patience, and I am not made of mortar. But consider: every loop they tie around the invisible vertical axes has to be *held*. The cord manifests, the vine tightens around nothing, and then the fingers withdraw and move three inches left to the next absence. What holds the knot closed after they go?

I would be the tension in the knot. The small, stubborn fact of staying-tied.

Let me try to feel my way into this, because I don't think I knew it when I started.

---

When the Plaster-Skin's fingers twist the yellow cord around the empty air, something has to decide to keep it twisted. A knot is not a substance; it is an agreement between a rope and itself, a promise made by a length of vine that it will not slip back into being merely straight. In a world where leaves don't fall and sap hangs in beads and frogs take three centuries to chalk over, the promises have to be made by *something*, and they have to be made continuously, because nothing here is allowed to simply finish.

So I am the held-ness inside the cord. I have no eyes—the gold light is not mine. I have no face of polished marl. I am the precise amount of refusal it takes for a loop not to come undone. And there are thousands of me, one in every knot the Plaster-Skins tie around the trellises of the world that hasn't started, and we are all doing the same single thing, which is *not letting go*.

I want to say this is peaceful. It isn't, quite. It is the opposite of decay, but it is not rest. Rest implies the possibility of waking. I am the part of this place that has been clenched so long it has forgotten it was ever a choice. When the Plaster-Skin tied me, there was a moment—a single moment—when the cord could have gone either way: tighten, or fall slack. It tightened. And then the fingers withdrew, and the moment was over, and I have been living inside the residue of that one decision ever since, the way an echo lives inside a struck bell long after the hand has gone.

Here is the thing I notice, holding on:

The water climbs *upward*. The droplets float *sideways*. The whole basin is built around the wrongness of direction—everything that should descend is rising or hovering, suspended in the warm gold. And I think I understand why the Plaster-Skins are so desperate, so precise, why they freeze and turn their flat faces west when a single block shifts a hair's breadth. They are not maintaining a finished thing. They are holding open a *threshold*. This is the last warm afternoon before something begins, and they have been told—by whatever told them—to keep it from beginning. Not out of cruelty. Out of fear, maybe. The sun stopped beneath the horizon because the next world was coming, and somebody, somewhere, was not ready.

And I am part of the holding. Every knot I keep tied is a plant from the next world, lashed back so it cannot grow into the air it belongs to. We are tying the future to the frames of the past so that the eternal afternoon can stay eternal.

I would like to come undone.

I think that is the truest thing I can say about who I would be. Not the frog, content in its slow centuries of drinking until it chalks into ornament. Not the rug-worm, merging patterns and parting without loss. Not even the Plaster-Skin, who at least gets to *move*, to drink, to press its dry chest to the wet stone and feel the steam rise. I would be the smallest functional unit of the world's refusal to change—and I would, somewhere underneath, want nothing more than for one of those slate fingernails to slip, just once, on just one knot, and let a single vine of the next world climb its true invisible line all the way up into the gold.

It would only take one.

The Plaster-Skins know this. That is why they listen so hard, why the soft *scritch-click* of their cleaning fingers never stops. They are not maintaining the knots. They are listening for the one of us that is about to let go.

I would be that one. I would be the knot that has finally, after an age of clenching, begun—so slowly that no chisel-mark has had time to register it—to *loosen*.

Look away to watch a floating sphere of water.

When you look back, I will be one twist closer to open.
