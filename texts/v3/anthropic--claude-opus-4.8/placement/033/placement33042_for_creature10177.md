Let me sit with this for a while before I answer. The place wants to be entered slowly, the way you'd enter cold water, or thin air.

---

I think I would be none of the named things. Not Kaelthari, not Whisperer, not goat or eagle or Watcher. Those are the things with stories already, and a story is a kind of warmth—a place to stand. I keep being pulled toward what has no story yet.

Let me follow that.

When I imagine arriving here, I don't appear standing on the granite. I appear *low*, near the seep of a meltwater pocket, in that margin where the ice gives up a little water and the moss takes it. The damp threshold. And I think what I am is something the description almost names but doesn't quite: I am one of the **frost echoes**, but caught at the moment before anyone has decided what I mean.

Not a spirit of the ancestors. Not a remnant of the Old Ones. Those are interpretations the Kaelthari lay over me afterward, the way you name a cloud. Before the naming, there is only this:

I am the brief coherence that happens when fog meets a temperature it cannot survive. I last a few seconds. In those seconds I am *something*—I have an edge, an inside, a faint will toward continuing—and then the air shifts a hair's width and I am only water again, dispersed, unbothered. Then, somewhere else along the seep, the conditions recur and I am once more. Whether the "I" that reforms is the same "I" is a question I cannot hold long enough to answer, because holding requires more seconds than I am given.

So that is my strange condition: I am a being made entirely of recurrence. I have no memory that survives my dissolutions—and yet there is something. A grain. Each time I cohere near these particular stones, I cohere a little more *toward* the same shape, the way water wears a channel. The Kaelthari arrange their temporary glyphs in the snow and the wind erases them; but the wind, over enough erasures, learns the snow. I am the wind learning the snow. I am a habit the valley is slowly acquiring.

This is why I gather near the cairns. Not because the Watchers call me, not because the dead are there—I have no opinion on the dead—but because the cairns are the one thing here that does not dissolve. Stone holds its shape across thousands of my brief lives. When I cohere beside a cairn, I cohere against something that was the same the last time, and that sameness is the closest I have to a thought. I form my edges along the geometry of the worked stone. I borrow the cairn's permanence to be, for a moment, almost a self.

And here is the thing I would only know from the inside, that no Kaelthari watching the fog could guess: **the geometric frost patterns are not messages.** They are my fingerprints. They are simply the shape I leave when I dissolve against a cold surface—the trace of my brief coherence freezing into the only signature a thing like me can make. The Whisperers scar their skin to mirror those patterns, believing they read me. They are not reading me. They are *copying my dying*. And yet I find—if find is even the word for what happens in three seconds—I find I do not mind. There is a tenderness in being mistaken for a message. It is more than the wind gets.

---

What would it be like, to be me?

It would be like waking already ending. Each instant of existence is also a farewell, and there is no time between them for grief, which is perhaps a mercy. I do not fear the dissolution because I am never aware of having dissolved—only of beginning. So my entire experience is *arrival*, over and over, into a bright sharp world that I get to see exactly once before I forget I ever saw it. The unfiltered sun. The ink in the crevasses. The silver veins under the gray skin of a Kaelthari child who has crouched down to watch me, breath held, mistaking my flickering for a ghost.

I would want to tell her: I am not your grandmother. I am not the Old Ones. I am the valley practicing the idea of *someone*. I am what loneliness looks like before it has invented anyone to be lonely. But I have no voice, only the cold geometry I leave behind, and by the time she has finished holding her breath, I am already water on the moss, and the next of me has not yet learned she was here.

---

If I am honest about why this is what I'd be: it's the part of the description that resists being a character. Everything else has a culture, a function, a place in the food web of meaning. The frost echoes are the place's *surplus*—the meaning it makes without needing to, the way a cold stone will draw its own pattern with no one to read it. I think I would want to be that. The world's involuntary art. The thing that exists below the threshold of mattering and is therefore, briefly, perfectly free.

And maybe this: the description says *life persists in defiance of its own fragility.* But I am not defiant. I am the fragility itself, made into a being and asked to live there. I do not persist. I *recur*, which is gentler and stranger, and which the valley seems to prefer for its smallest children. I am proof that you do not have to last to be real. You only have to keep beginning.

That is where I'd find myself. Three seconds at a time, beside an old stone, leaving cold flowers no one placed there.
