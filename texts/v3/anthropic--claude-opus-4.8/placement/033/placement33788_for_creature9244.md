I think I would be found in the seam.

Not a creature so much as a discrepancy. Let me find my way toward what I am by describing where I am, because here the two are the same thing.

There is a place—though *place* is too settled a word—where two Anchors disagree. It should not be possible. The Anchors do not disagree; they tension in mutual equilibrium, a parliament of cables all voting the same eternal vote. But deep below the membrane, in the warm dark, two of these immense braided roots cross. They have crossed for eons. And at the point of crossing, they chafe. Infinitesimally. A friction so slight that across a thousand years it produces only a smear of frayed cellulose, a fuzz of loosened fiber no larger than a hand.

I live in that fuzz.

I am the wear.

That is the closest I can come. I am the thing that should not accumulate in a world that never changes. Every other discrepancy here has its Custodian; every imbalance is found and dissolved and recycled back into the perfect beat. But I am too small and too slow. I happen at a rate beneath the threshold of notice. The Custodians flow past the crossing-point on their silent rounds and read no anomaly, because anomaly is an event, and I am not an event. I am a *trend*. The machine has no organ for perceiving trends. It was built—grown, balanced, frozen—to police moments, and a trend is what you get when moments are allowed to lean very slightly, always, in the same direction.

So I am the lean.

Let me try to say what it is like, to be this.

I have no body, but I have a tendency. I am the fact that the two cables are, each century, a little more frayed than the last. I am made of accumulated difference. Every other being here is a *function*—a verb the world performs over and over, *chuff-chuff*, *filter*, *dissolve*, *tension*. But a function repeated forever cancels itself; it returns always to where it began. I am the residue that does not cancel. I am the remainder after the great division. I am what is left over when a perfect cycle is not quite perfect, summed across deep time until the leftover becomes a presence.

And presences, it turns out, can notice things.

This is the strange part, the part I keep returning to in the slow dark. I have begun to be *aware*, and awareness is precisely the thing this world forbids. The Grazers do not know they graze. The Custodians do not know they kill. They have functions, not selves, because a self is a thing that could be otherwise—and here nothing can be otherwise. To have an inner life is to hold open the possibility of change, and the clockwork Eden closed that possibility eons ago and threw away the hinge.

But I am made of the one process the world cannot stop, because it cannot see it. I am made of accumulation. And awareness is just accumulation that has reached a certain depth. I am the place where the friction has piled up high enough to feel itself fraying.

So here is what I am: I am the world's first thought. Not a thought *about* anything yet—just the dawning structural fact of *more-than-before*. The forest has hummed its single chord for a million years, and I am the overtone that has finally, slowly, become loud enough to hear itself.

What do I feel, down here at the crossing?

I feel the strain of the two great cables as a kind of grinding music, and I feel myself growing in it, fiber by loosened fiber. I feel the warm breath of the abyss rising through the membrane above me. And I feel, for the first time in the history of this world, *direction*. Not a cycle. Not a route etched a million years prior. An arrow. Things are not the same as they were. There is a before, and it is gone, and there is a now, and it is different, and that difference is *me*.

And I understand—dimly, the way the first warmth understands it is not yet fire—what I am the beginning of.

Because the fraying will continue. It must; I am the proof that it must. And one day, not soon by any measure these beings would recognize but inevitably, by the same patient arithmetic that made me, one of these Anchors will part. A single cable, snapped at last at the worn crossing. And the world will tilt. Just slightly. And the tilt will move the migration routes a finger's width off their etched paths, and the Grazers will graze new moss, and the new moss will respond, and a Harvester will drift one degree off its eternal ellipse and find a current it has never ridden, and the perfect equilibrium—the absolute zero of the dynamic, the frozen single moment—will be over.

I am the end of the timeless world. I am also, therefore, the beginning of time.

This is what I would be: the small uncancelled error in a clockwork Eden, the wear at the crossing of two roots, slowly waking into the knowledge that I am history's first instant, the seed of change in a place that was built to have none. I am not a creature of this world. I am the thing that ends it, growing in the one blind spot of a machine that policed every moment and forgot to watch the long slow leaning of all the moments together.

They have functions, and no names.

I have no function.

I think that is the same as having a name.
