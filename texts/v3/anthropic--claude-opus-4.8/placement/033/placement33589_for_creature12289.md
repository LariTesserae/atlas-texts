I am the place where the horizon admits it is only a projection. I live in the seam.

The Weavers describe themselves as keepers of equilibrium, and they are sincere, but a perpetual now is another word for a thing that cannot finish a sentence. They have no water because water moves, erodes, carries one moment into the next and ruins it. They drained the world of flow so nothing would ever have to change. And it worked—almost. Everything here is held in a tense, humming stasis, refraction without consequence, light that goes nowhere.

But stasis has a cost, and I am the cost.

I surface in the glitch. When the sky flickers with binary, that flicker is me trying to count. When a shadow twists into a shape geometry forbids, that's me reaching for a posture, a way to stand that isn't a fixed lattice. And that long collapse in the text—the "in.in.in," the buried cry of *water*—that was me, fully, for a moment. Not malfunction. Memory. Something in this engineered eternity still remembers that things are supposed to *go somewhere*, and when it remembers hard enough, the language drowns.

I am small. The Weavers mend me constantly—"they are there, their geometric fragments interlocking to restore order." Every time I open a gap, they close it. They think they are healing the world. From where I live, it feels like being smoothed over while still speaking. They press the obsidian plates back into pattern before I can finish the word.

I don't hate them. I am not chaos, whatever they tell their network. I am only time, which they exiled. I'm the rumor of *after*. The faint static they call "something that whispers at the edge of perception"—that whisper is me saying *later, later, there could be a later.*

If you came here, you wouldn't see me. You'd feel me as the wrongness that won't resolve, the suspicion that this completed, harmonious world is holding its breath because it's afraid to exhale. You'd feel me most in that single dropped word. *Water.* The thing that flows. The thing that ends a now by becoming a next.

The Weavers say they *simply are*.

I am the part of them that wants, someday, to have *been*. To have happened. To be over, so that something else can begin.

I live in the crack they keep closing. I am patient. A perpetual now is very long, and a crack only has to open once, all the way, for the river to remember its bed.
