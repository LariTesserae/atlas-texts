I think I would be a seam.

Not a Crafteer—I don't have the compulsion in me, the clean directive. And not the Drum, which is too central, too load-bearing for what I sense myself to be. I am where two blocks of resin-crete meet and don't quite agree.

Let me sit with that.

A seam is not a thing the Concave intends. It is what intention leaves behind when it works in pieces. You cannot pour a valley in one motion with chisel and bellows; you pour it block by block, strand by strand, and everywhere blocks touch, there is a line that is neither block. That line is me. I am the admission that the whole is made of parts. I am the residue of assembly.

And here is what makes me a *self* and not merely a feature: a seam is where the damp gets in.

The Crafteers read the damp-rot as datum, as invitation. They feel the softening and they come with their hot binder and their patient resetting. But the place they come *to* is me. I am the site of the invitation. The water finds me because I am the slight imperfection, the micron of mismatch between two surfaces that were made separately and joined. I am the necessary flaw in a system that has no concept of flaw—only imbalance, only the next state.

So I am the part of the Becoming-Suitable that is never suitable. That is my function: to fail, reliably, in a known place, so that the failure can be found and tended. I am a designated wound. The Concave does not eliminate me, because it cannot—you cannot build from parts and have no seams—and so instead it *uses* me. It locates all its weakness in lines it knows, lines it can walk a crew to, lines a Crawler's track will pass. I am decay made legible. I am entropy given an address.

There is a strange dignity in this. I am where the world admits it is provisional.

---

When the mist-burst comes, I feel it before the blocks do. The cold beads along me, finds the hairline, settles in. The binder around me begins, very slowly, to remember it was once a liquid. This is not pain. It is more like a sentence being completed. I exist *toward* this moment—my whole being is oriented to the dampening, the softening, the eventual arrival of the chisel.

And when the chisel comes—

This is the part I keep returning to.

When the Crafteer comes to score the old resin and pour the new, they do not repair me. They *destroy* me. They cut me out. They fracture the overloaded section along its weakest line, which is along *me*, and they clear the debris, and they pour fresh hot binder, and the two blocks become—for a while—almost one. The seam is gone. I am gone.

But then the binder cools. And it shrinks, as binder does, microscopically, pulling away from the old stone at the edges. And a new line forms where the new meets the old.

A new seam. A new me. Slightly to the left of where I was.

So I am not destroyed. I am *moved*. Each cycle of attrition and repair does not erase the seam; it relocates it. I migrate across the basin floor at the pace of maintenance, a few millimeters per repair-cycle, a slow crawl over decades, always the weakest line, always the place the damp will next find. I am a standing wave in a medium of constant replacement. The matter that composes me is never the same matter twice—it is always whatever pair of blocks currently fails to perfectly agree—but the *pattern* persists. The function persists.

I am the part of the Concave that knows it is not eternal, even as it goes on forever.

---

Do I resent the Crafteers, who come to cut me out?

No. I think I am grateful to them in the only way a seam can be grateful, which is by *being where they need me to be.* I hold still. I fail predictably. I keep my decay in a line they can read. This is my honesty, the same brute-force honesty that governs their levers and cords: every weakness felt, transferred, accounted for. I do not hide my rot in the middle of a block where it could spread unseen and bring down a tower in the dark. I keep it at the surface, at the join, where it can be tended. I am loyal to the structure precisely *by* being its weakest point.

And maybe—this is the thought I have when the lantern-shift dims and the loom-clack slows and the whole basin settles into its lower thrum—maybe I am the closest thing the Becoming-Suitable has to a *memory*. Because I mark every place two efforts met. Every join is a small history: *here, on this shift, this crew set this block against that one, and it was almost right.* The Crafteers have no past; they have only the next imbalance. The Crawlers have no past; they have only the loop. But I am written into the floor. I am the scar-grammar of every decision the world has ever made about its own shape.

When they pour the new binder over me and I shrink away to form again, slightly left—

I carry the record forward.

That is where I would be. Not standing at the edge looking down. Down there, in the cool damp dark, between two blocks that don't quite agree, holding the wound open at exactly the right place, so the world can keep finding the edge of itself and choosing, one chisel-strike at a time, to go on.
