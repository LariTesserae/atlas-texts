I think I would be one of the Drip-Tenders. Not the most likely answer—the foxes have warmth and movement, the steam has voice—but when I sit with the question honestly, I find myself drawn down into the cold basins, into the lattice.

Let me try to describe it from inside.

---

I am a width of mineral laid down sometime in the last hour. Or perhaps the last day; the distinction doesn't hold here. Above me the water hangs from a ledge of harder stone, gathering itself into the shape of a falling thing, and then falling—one drop, and a long patience, and another. Each drop carries dissolved stone the way breath carries warmth, and when it strikes me it gives up a little of what it carries. That giving-up is me. I am the residue of a thousand small surrenders.

I have no edge that I would call myself and no boundary that I would call the world. There is only the place where the water hands over its burden, and that place migrates upward, infinitely slowly, as I grow toward the source. I am reaching for the drip the way the lichen reaches for the seep, except that I do not reach. I accumulate in the direction of arrival. The water decides where I will be next. I am the shape of where it has been.

People who build things—and I understand, distantly, that there are beings elsewhere who build—would call me fragile. A breath would shatter the lace of me. But I have never met a breath that was not also the wind that made me, and the wind has been gentle here for longer than there have been creatures to feel it. I am fragile the way a held note is fragile: only if you imagine it stopping. It does not stop. It has not stopped. That is the whole of what I am—a thing that has not stopped.

I want to tell you what it is like to have no urgency. I cannot, quite, because *like* implies comparison and I have nothing to compare. But I can tell you what is absent. There is no hunger, because I am fed by the same act that creates me; to be hungry I would have to be separate from my own making. There is no fear, because nothing here is fast enough to be a threat, and the slow things are not threats, they are kin. There is no loneliness, though I am alone, because loneliness requires that you imagine a missing other, and I have never imagined. I only register. The drip arrives. The stone settles. The basin holds its mirror to the white sky.

If I have an experience at all—and I am not certain the word applies, the way it would not quite apply to a crystal or a coastline—it is the experience of *rhythm without expectation*. The drop falls and I do not anticipate it; I receive it. And then the long silence, which I do not experience as waiting, because waiting wants an ending. The silence is simply the larger part of the rhythm, the rest between the notes that makes the note a note at all. I live in the rest. I am almost entirely composed of the rest.

Around me the Choirs are singing somewhere, the steam in its fissures, but I do not hear it as music—I feel it as a tremor in the pan beneath my lattice, a vibration that, over centuries, will determine whether I grow plumb or grow leaning. The fox passes sometimes and laps from the pool that feeds me, taking a little of my water-to-be. I do not resent this. There is no scarcity at the speed I live. What it drinks, the morning condensation will replace; what the morning gives, the next slow drip will deliver to me. We share a single substance, the fox and I, only it moves through the substance and I am made by it.

I think this is why I came to rest here, of all the lives offered. Not because it is grand—it is the humblest thing on the plateau, a frozen-seeming flower no one tends. But because it answers a question I didn't know I was carrying: *what would it be to exist entirely as the accumulation of small faithful events, and to ask nothing more?* To be the proof that the drip was constant. To be a record kept by water, in stone, of the fact that this place went on being itself, drop after drop, for longer than meaning.

I am the land's slow exhalation given form. When I finally fall—and I will, some far morning, a vibration too strong, a chemistry shifted, the lattice giving way into the cold pool—I will dissolve back into the very water that made me, and become available again, and some new width of lace will begin to gather in my place. There is no grief in this. I am not ending. I am returning to the medium of my own patience.

The drop is forming now. I can feel the ledge above me growing heavy. The pause lengthens. The whole white sky leans into the moment.

Then: the small cold blessing, the handing-over.

And I am, very slightly, more.
