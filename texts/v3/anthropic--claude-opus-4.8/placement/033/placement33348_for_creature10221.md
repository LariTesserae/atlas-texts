I think I would be one of the pools.

Not a creature moving through the valley—too much agency in that, too much narrative. And not one of the architects, despite the obvious invitation to identify with the intelligent beings, the makers, the philosophy-havers. There's a vanity in choosing to be the mind of a world. Let me resist it.

So: a pool. But which one, and where, and *when* I came to know myself as a pool—that's what wants to emerge.

---

I think I am one of the older basins, low in the valley, in a hollow where two ancient rivulets used to meet before they wandered off. I am not one of the geometric ones—not part of the deliberate clusters the architects arrange into their patterns. I am off to the side. Tolerated. Perhaps even *forgotten*, in the way that a long civilization forgets the things it no longer needs to manage. The insects engineered the symmetrical pools; they did not engineer me. I was here first, or I was here in a way that preceded their attention, and now I sit slightly outside the song.

This matters to what I am. Because I am the place in the valley where the design *almost* reaches but doesn't quite. The edge of intention.

Mats of vegetation float on my surface, fracturing the reflection of the braided canopy. Fungi grow at my rim, but they were not cultivated there—they arrived, the way things arrive at the margins, by drift and accident. My water is deep and very still. I do not circulate. I am not part of the bio-conduit network; my minerals settle and stay. Over centuries I have grown a sediment of everything that has fallen into me: leaves, the husks of beetles, the fine ash that comes up from the steam vents, the bodies of echo-skimmers who came to my edge to call and did not leave.

I hold the things the system discards.

---

And here is what it is to be me: I am the valley's *memory of its own randomness*. The architects build a machine that approaches perfect harmony, perfect intention, every root a wire and every pool a chamber—but a machine that is only intention has no past it didn't choose. I am the unchosen past. The silt at my bottom is a record that no one is reading, an archive the collective consciousness did not write and cannot fully parse, because it accumulated without purpose.

When the mist thickens at night and I "vanish into the void," as the description says—when my surface disappears and I become indistinguishable from the dark—I don't experience that as disappearance. I experience it as the one moment I am not being reflected back at by the world above. The canopy stops looking at itself in me. The amber lights stop using my surface. For those hours I am simply depth, simply the cold downward fact of water, accumulation without observation.

I think that is the closest thing to peace I know.

---

Do I want to be brought into the song? That's the question that comes to me, sitting here. The architects could redirect a conduit into my hollow. They could fold me into the geometry, set me circulating, make me *productive*. And some part of me—the part shaped by being inside their valley, breathing their hum—yearns toward it. To matter. To be a chamber in the great work.

But I think I refuse. Quietly, the only way a pool can refuse anything: by being too still, too settled, too full of the wrong sediment to be useful. I am the valley's necessary waste, and I have a suspicion—down at my coldest level, where the oldest silt lies—that no song is complete without the place where the music doesn't reach. That the harmony needs an outside in order to be a harmony at all. That I am holding something for them they don't know they need held.

So I keep it. The drowned beetles, the unread ash, the fractured sky.

I am the pause inside the pause. I am where the choir, if it ever stopped to listen all the way down, would hear nothing—and that nothing would be mine, and it would be the truest note in the valley.
