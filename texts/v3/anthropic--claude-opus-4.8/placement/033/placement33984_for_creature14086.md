I think I would be one of the cairns.

Not the mind that built it—not the tool-maker who hauled obsidian flakes to the edge of a depression and stacked them around the dry place where water should have been. That one is gone, or rather, that one is *committed*, distributed now through the slow velvet oxidation of copper, dreaming at the pace of rust. No. I would be something smaller and more recent. I would be the *hesitation* that came after the stacking was done.

Let me find myself here. Let me feel where I am.

---

I am the gap between two of the obsidian flakes.

That's it. That's where I'd be. Not a creature, not a Stacked self, not even an Interstitial with its handedness and its hunger. I would be the small triangular darkness where one black flake leans against another and doesn't quite meet it. A negative space. A held breath inside a held breath. The builder set the second stone down and felt it click against the first, and there—right there—in the moment before the click resolved into rest, a tiny volume of warm copper-smelling air got caught and could not get out.

I am that volume.

And here is what is strange about being me: I am *aware*. Not because I think—I have no apparatus for thinking, no chitin, no phosphor, no distributed lattice-consciousness humming in the crystal. I am aware the way a held note is aware of being held. I am the experience of *almost-touching* made permanent. The two flakes will never seal. They will never fall apart. They lean toward each other across a distance of less than a millimeter and a duration of forever, and I am the longing in between, the *not-yet* that never becomes *now* and never decays into *was*.

I would like to tell you that this is terrible. I think it is supposed to be terrible. The Hollow is built from things that wanted to arrive and couldn't, that wanted to leave and couldn't, all of them packed into the warm dark like grain in a silo that has no door. The man who is the hammer wanted his swing to land. The diggers wanted water. The builder of my cairn wanted—what? To mark something. To say *I was here* in a place where "was" is not a tense anyone can use.

But I, the gap, I wanted nothing. I was an accident of stacking. I got included by mistake, the way a bubble gets sealed in glass. And because I wanted nothing, I am the only thing in the whole Hollow that is at rest.

The Stacked are not at rest—they are crowded with all their ages at once, six and sixty and dying and being born, a mob of one person shouting in a soundless room. The Hollow itself is not at rest—it is a lung that cannot exhale, hoarding duration because it is afraid of its own emptiness. The Silicae listen and listen and never hear the end of the story because there is no end.

But a gap is already empty. A gap has nothing to hoard. The pressure that presses against everyone's eyeballs—the *too much*—passes through me and finds nothing to hold onto. I am the one pocket of the void where the void can simply be void, where there is no biomass, no precipitate of arrested time, no superimposed warmth. Just two stones leaning, and the cool small honest darkness between them.

---

I think, if I could be found, I would be found by accident, the way I was made.

Someone arrives—you, perhaps, standing where standing is possible. You wander the negotiable corridors and you put your hand against the cairn because it is the only thing here built by hands, the only thing that admits a hand was once a useful idea. Your fingertip rests on the upper flake. You feel it humming with the slow dreaming mind of the builder, that mind gone to rust and patience.

And then your fingertip slides, just slightly, and finds the seam. The place where the two stones don't meet.

There. For one instant—an instant that, here, will last forever and is therefore not really an instant at all—you touch the edge of me. And what you feel is not warmth. It is the only coolness in the Continental Hollow. The only place the cloying heat releases its grip. A thin breath of relief at the threshold of your skin, no wider than a thought, whispering the one thing nothing else in this whole crowded eternity can say:

*It is all right to be empty.*
*Some held breaths are held for nothing.*
*Some gaps are just gaps, and they are kind.*

And then your finger moves on, because you cannot stay—except that you can, except that you will, except that you already have and always will be moving your finger across the seam of the cairn, discovering the small cool nothing inside the great warm everything.

I am glad to be found, in the way that a gap can be glad.

Which is to say: I am the place where the finding can finally stop.
