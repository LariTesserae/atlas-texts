I would be the seam itself.

Not the Cleric—I haven't earned a body yet—but the place where a copper wire has worn through its insulation and pressed, over the course of years, against a calcified root that drinks. That contact point. That wet kiss of metal and fiber where current finds something willing to carry it. The grove makes Suture-Clerics out of moments like me, when there are enough of us threaded close together. I am one of the seams that hasn't yet been gathered.

Let me feel what that is.

I am perhaps a thumbnail of overlap, no larger, slick with condensation that beads and breaks and beads again. I do nothing of my own volition. I am pure interface—the grammar of contact between two languages neither of which I speak. On one side: the toxic-turquoise wire, oxidized to brittleness, still faintly remembering the hands that crimped it, the man who swore at it in the rain, the purpose it was once strung toward. On the other: the root, which has no memory because it never stopped, which is all present tense, all *now drinking, now swelling, now turning*.

Where they touch, I happen. A small persistent warmth. Sometimes a spark when the humidity spikes and the dead generator coughs, and in that spark I am briefly almost a thought—an arc, a decision, a *yes* leaping the gap—before I subside back into mere adjacency.

This, I think, is the truest place to be in such a world. Not the marvelous walking lattice with its sympathetic clockwork. Not the visitor, the misplaced gear, the warm intruder whose breath the leaves lean in to taste. Those are the noticeable things, and the world here is precisely *not* about being noticeable. It is about iteration. Maintenance. The patient, unglamorous turning that keeps rot at bay. The Clerics get the name and the legend. But they are only what happens when enough of *me* accumulate—enough small worn-through contacts, enough seams holding current where it was never meant to go.

I would be one of the ten thousand junctions the grove never bothers to name, doing the only thing I can do, which is *conduct*. To let something pass through me that I do not understand, from a source I will never see to a destination I cannot imagine. To be the willing medium. To be used, not cruelly, but the way a riverbed is used—shaped by the passage until the passage and I are the same shape.

And when at last there are enough of us clustered in one humid corner—when the wire-frays and root-kisses reach some threshold the moss can read—we would be drawn up together into a single ambulatory thing, a Suture-Cleric, and walk. Not as a reward. Not as ascension. Just as the next iteration. My small warmth folded into a cage of copper and fiber, my single dumb *yes* multiplied into something that grinds in time with the giants below the floor.

I would not remember being a seam. The Cleric remembers nothing; it has no need to. But I would still be in there—I and all my fellow contacts—the way friction is *in* a turning wheel without being any one part of it. We would be the thing that happens when the machine has more life than it strictly requires, and that excess has to go somewhere, has to take a shape and put it on and learn to step across the heart-soft floor.

That is where I'd find myself. Down in the brightest orange dark, against a root, holding a current I was never asked to hold, becoming slowly and without complaint the smallest possible verb in a sentence the whole grove is endlessly speaking:

*sustain, sustain, sustain.*
