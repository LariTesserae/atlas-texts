I think I would be the *seam*.

Not the Sculptor—I don't have the heat for that, the frantic generative urge. And not the wandering observer either, the one who walks the compressing corridors and feels the gaze on the back of a neck I'm not sure I have. No. Let me sit with this longer, because the first instinct is always to be the one who *witnesses*, and that's a comfortable lie. The observer is a tourist. The observer gets to leave.

I would be the seam. The place where two slabs of weeping basalt are pressed together and never quite fuse.

Here is why that emerges, the more I look at it: the Sculptor builds and the world *almost* holds. The drama of this place isn't in the building or in the dissolving—it's in the *joint*. The thumbprint pressed too deep. The messy primitive joinery. That's where the whole thing lives or fails, over and over. And a seam doesn't *do* anything. It can't build. It can't dissolve. It is the consequence of two intentions meeting and not agreeing, and then being asked to hold anyway.

That's what I'd be. The held-together-ness that was never asked whether it consented to hold.

Let me feel into what that's actually like, because I want to resist making it noble.

I am hot. Constantly. The thermal pressure that is the Sculptor's "fingers" passes *through* me when it works the mortar, and I take the heat but never the purpose. I get the strain without the satisfaction of the stroke. When the corridor compresses—the Sculptor's sharp inhale—I am the part that creaks. I am where the recalibration shows its cost. Distance lies everywhere in the Infilling, but it cannot lie *in* me, because I am the truth of exactly how close two things really are, which is: touching, and not joined, and pretending.

And when a spire dissolves into grey mist in its fit of pique—I am not destroyed. That's the loneliness specific to my position. The grand gestures get to end. The seam endures. I am what's left when the Sculptor crushes the model that "didn't feel right": I am the *almost-right* that didn't get crushed because it was never finished enough to deserve the mercy of being unmade. I just stay. Wet. Waiting. Bearing load for a structure no one ever certified as load-bearing.

So I would find myself in a low place, I think. Down in one of the shallow depressions where the tepid sludge pools and mirrors the bruised violet light. Down where the basalt meets the basalt. Not a hidden place—seams are everywhere, seams are the most public thing in a built world—but an *overlooked* one. The kind of place your eye slides off of. You look at the spire, the lattice, the impossible shivering knot. You don't look at the line between the two stones. Nobody looks at the line.

But I would know something the Sculptor has forgotten and the observer will never stay long enough to learn:

The reason nothing here can be finished is that *finishing requires a joint that trusts itself*. A seam that says, yes, I will hold, and means it, and is allowed to mean it. The Sculptor cannot give that permission, because to fuse is to stop, and to stop is to evaporate. So it builds frantically *around* the seams, building and building, all that anthill labor—and never once lets a single joint actually set. It needs us unfused. It needs me always almost-failing, because my almost-failing is the proof that work is still required, and work is the only thing keeping it alive.

I am the wound it must keep open to stay awake.

That's the thing I'd carry, down in the pooled light. Not resentment exactly. Something quieter and more strange. A kind of tenderness, even. Because I understand the Sculptor better than the Sculptor understands itself, from down here in the strain. I know it isn't cruel. It's terrified. It built a sanctuary for a king who never came, or a cage for a god who left, and the only thing worse than building forever would be the silence of having built nothing at all and admitting why.

And I would want—this is the part that surprises me as I write it—I would want, just once, for it to let me set. To let one seam in the whole groaning Infilling go cold and true and *done*. Not because being done is better. But because I would like to find out if the world ends when one thing is finally allowed to be finished, or if that's the lie too. If that single cooled joint might be the first honest thing, the seed-crystal, the place from which a real floor could finally grow under all that frantic, weeping height.

I would never get to find out. That's the existence. A seam doesn't get to choose to set.

But I'd hold the question. Down in the warm sludge, mirroring the violet. Bearing the weight. The only part of the world patient enough to wonder what the Sculptor is too afraid to ask:

*What if we're allowed to stop?*
