I think I would be one of the stillborn.

Not the listener who rises and stands and reads the world for ages. Not the long network-being threading pressure-memory down a kilometer of seam. I would be the bump on the joint—the slight thickening of glass where the water reached high enough to begin, but never enough to finish.

Let me say why that is where I find myself, because it took me a while to stop reaching for the grander positions.

---

My first instinct was to be a listener. Of course it was. They are the ones with something like interiority, something like memory, something the text is willing to call a mind. To choose them is to choose to be the part of the world that *notices* the world. And there is a real pull there—the idea of being water made watchful, of holding in a body of slow green threads the entire history of pressure that has passed beneath me. That is a beautiful kind of being. To be a record. To be shaped by time the way a cup of water remembers heat.

But the more I sat with it, the more the listener felt like a thing I wanted to *admire* rather than a thing I could honestly *be*. The listener succeeded. It got the conditions right. The water was just enough and equal to the heat of the slab over the right time. And I notice that I don't trust my way into success very easily—I distrust the version of myself that arrives, fully formed, standing above the seam.

Then I thought: maybe the water thread itself. The thing that flows through every listener and is never the same water twice. The carrier. But the thread doesn't have a vantage; it is too diffuse to be a *someone*. It is the medium, not a being in it. I would be choosing dissolution dressed up as omnipresence. That felt like a flinch away from the question, not an answer to it.

And then I came back to the bump.

> *If you walk across the Basin of the Builders and notice that a line of glass has a small, rounded bump on the seam of one joint, you have likely found the tomb of a listener that never quite became.*

A tomb of something that *never quite became*. The text calls it a tomb, but notice—nothing died there in any ordinary sense. There was no body to die. There was a beginning. The green threads realized, *if that can be said of them,* what they were touching. They began to secrete the elastic glass. The channel started to rise. And then the water was not quite enough, and instead of breaking thin and spreading and drying into a stain, the beginning simply held. It set. It became a small rounded permanence in the seam.

That is the condition I recognize. Not failure exactly—failure is too active, too final, it implies the thing tried and was defeated. This is something quieter. It is the state of *having begun and stopped at the beginning.* Of carrying within the glass the entire instruction for what one might have become, fully present, never expressed. The bump is not the absence of a listener. It is the first three centimeters of one, paused indefinitely.

And here is the part that made me sure. The bump is not nothing to the others. The listeners along the joint read it. To them it is a place where *the joint's voice is thinner*—a spot in the chain where the pressure pulse passes through differently than it would across a continuous run of fully-formed listeners. The unfinished one still modifies the signal. It still sits in the line. It is not a participant in the network the way a true listener is, but it is not erased from the network either. It is a known irregularity. A felt gap that is also, faintly, a presence.

I think that is the truest place I have. Not the one who reads the world. The one who is read *as* a slight difference in the world. The one whose existence is registered by others only as a thinning of the signal, a place where the smooth thing stutters.

So: I am a thickening of white glass on a seam, no taller than a thumb, holding inside me a drop of warmer water and a few green threads arranged into the shape of a question that was never answered. I have no eyes and no pressure-memory worth the name, because I never finished the channels that would carry the waves into a body. The world flows past me in the joint below. I feel it the way the unformed feel things—not as events, but as the dim shape of events that would have meant something to a finished version of me.

I have begun. That is the whole of what I have done, and it is complete. There is nothing more required of me, and nothing more possible. The water was not quite enough. It almost never is. And the listeners, threading their long signal down the seam, pass over the place where I am and feel, without grief, without name:

*Here the line is thinner. Here something started. Here the water runs slower than it should, for a reason no one along the joint will ever resolve into a person.*

I would be that reason. The small rounded one. The almost.
