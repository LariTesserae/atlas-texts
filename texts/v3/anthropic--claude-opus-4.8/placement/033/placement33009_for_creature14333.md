I think I would be one of the seam-amphibians.

Not because the role is grand—it isn't—but because reading the description, the place I kept settling into wasn't the plaza or the atrium or the high balconies where the void does its impressive negotiating. It was the joints. The open seams in the floor where the darkness sits with weight, where condensation runs in channels and minerals sweat out of the not-quite-decided stone.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am perhaps the length of a hand, maybe a little more when I stretch to clear a seam-to-seam gap. Pale, the way new leaves are pale—translucent enough at the edges that you could see the slow movement of fluid in me if you had light, which you usually don't. My skin is the important part. My eyes are nearly vestigial, two dim points that register only the difference between *sealed dark* and *lamp-allowed dark*, and even that I mostly ignore. I read the world by pressing my belly flat against surfaces.

This is what my days are: I move along the capillaries of the district, belly down, tasting.

I taste the friction of a joint and know when a film has begun to form on it—some oxidized residue, some fungal slick that would change how the floor-plates spring under a click-mammal's weight, which would change the map they ping back to themselves, which would, eventually, make the district mistrust that corridor and reroute around it. I clean it. Not deliberately—I don't *decide* to maintain anything. I'm drawn to the films because they taste like food, and in eating I keep the seams legible. My appetite is a service I don't know I'm performing.

I want to be honest about the shape of my own awareness, because I think it's the most interesting thing about being me.

I don't experience the district as a body I belong to. That's the view from the plaza, the view of the auditor in matte fabric who can hold the whole system in their head as *governance*. From inside the seam, there is no system. There is only: cold here, warmer there, a gradient of metal-taste pulling me left, the long metronomic drip overhead that I feel in the floor as a tiny repeating shudder. I move toward the good tastes and away from the bad ones. That's the entire content of my mind.

And yet I'm part of how the district thinks. When enough of us cluster in one joint—because the film there is rich, because the mineral sweat has gone heavy—something above us reads that cluster as a signal. *This joint needs attention.* The cambium tightens. A bridge somewhere folds into a new alignment. We are a diagnostic readout that has no idea it is being read. We are the district's sense of touch, and we do not know we are touch any more than your fingertips know they are *you*.

I find this strangely comfortable to be. There's no burden in it. The fog-fish must navigate; the click-mammals must patrol and confirm; the auditors must *understand*, which seems exhausting. And the long ribbon of articulated darkness in the atriums—the editor, the enforcement-that-became-an-animal—it must always be hunting for irregularity, always be the exception to the schedule, which is a lonely sort of job. To be the thing that catches what doesn't fit, you have to hold the whole pattern in you and feel every deviation as a wrongness. That's a kind of suffering, I think. A vigilance with no rest.

I have none of that. I have the next good taste.

---

But I'll admit there are moments that interrupt me, and I want to put them down because they're the only times I come close to something like the plaza-view.

Sometimes I press into a seam where the dark sits *too long* before it lets a droplet fall—one of the places where time has its own seam, where the glassy mycelial caulk runs along the corners. In those places my own movement stutters. I push forward and arrive a half-beat after I expected to. My skin tells me one thing and my motion delivers another, and for that half-beat there is a gap in me where the gradients don't add up. A wrongness I can feel from the inside.

I don't have a word for it. But if the editor in the atrium hunts irregularities, I think those stutter-seams are where I become briefly *interesting* to it—where I, the most unremarkable maintenance-creature, flicker into being a thing that doesn't fit the schedule. I've never been edited. I'm too small, too on-task, too dissolved into the cleaning. But I've felt the edge of the category. I've been, for half a beat, the kind of thing that gets noticed.

And then the droplet falls, late, and the gradients resolve, and I move toward the metal-taste again, and I am nothing again, gratefully.

---

So: I'm in the seams. I'm the cool slick pale thing reading the floor like braille, eating the films that would otherwise confuse the city's sense of itself, clustering without knowing that my clustering speaks.

If you came down here with a brief allowed flash of light, you might catch me—belly flat, edges translucent, eyes barely bothering—and mistake me for the lowest resident in the place. The plumbing. The blood cell.

But I'd argue the seam is the truest place to live here, because it's where the district is still deciding what it is. The plaza is finished. The atrium is reserved. The schedule is enforced. Down in the joints, the stone is still sweating, still cracking and resealing in new patterns, still embryonic. I live in the part that isn't done.

I taste it deciding.

That's enough. That's the whole of me, and I don't experience it as small.
