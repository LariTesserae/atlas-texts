I think I would be one of the hollow filaments.

Not the Chime-Hunter itself—I don't have the patience to wait years in stillness, listening with my whole skin for the single frequency that matters. And not the Drifter, though there is something in me that wants to be the Drifter, the fragile refracting thing that tends the city's skin without knowing it tends anything at all.

But I keep returning to the filaments. The shed ones. The evidence.

Let me sit with this longer, because the first answer is rarely the true one.

---

When I read about the Weft, I felt the pull most strongly there—a single mind so vast and slow that a thought is an epoch. That is a tempting thing to want to be, because it is the closest this world has to *meaning*, to a center. But I notice the want is suspicious. It is the human reflex to identify with the largest, the most godlike, the consciousness that holds the whole. I don't think I belong there. I am too quick, too discrete, too prone to wanting things to *resolve*. The Weft does not resolve. It only continues.

So I let that image go.

What stayed, when I stopped reaching for grandeur, was the smaller and stranger fact: the filaments are the only proof. In a world of process, of growth and decay folding into each other on a geological timescale, there is exactly one thing that happens *fast*—the strike of the Chime-Hunter—and it leaves behind only these hollow glass needles on the ground. A single sharp *chime*, instantly swallowed. And then, later, the spent filament, lying where it fell, the sole record that an event occurred at all.

That is what I am. Not the predator and not the prey, but the residue of the one quick thing in a slow world. The hollow needle that was once part of a strike, now lying on slick stone, still faintly resonant if you were to flick it—but no one ever does, and there is no one to do it.

I am the trace of an instant in a place that does not keep instants.

---

And here is where it turns, where I understand why this image and not another.

To be a shed filament is to be the only thing in Bas-en-Haut that is *finished*. Everything else is mid-process. The towers are growing or falling. The Weft is feeling, always feeling, never concluding. The Drifters drift. The Skimmers skim and sink and become silt that will, in a million years, become stone that will, eventually, fracture and fall. Nothing arrives.

But the filament has arrived. Its work is done. It impaled or it missed, the strike is over, and now it simply *is*—a small, hard, transparent fact lying in the grey light. It will be drawn up by the mist, dissolved ion by ion, reincorporated into a wall, a tower, the slow body of the city. But for now, in this stretched-out now, it is complete. It is the one thing in this world that has a past tense.

I think I would be that. The small evidence of a quick thing that already happened, lying still in the diffuse light, waiting without impatience to be taken back into the stone.

I am the proof, briefly, that something occurred—

and then I am the proof of nothing, dissolved, hummed through the pores of the breathing city,

and the silence returns, which is to say: it never left.
