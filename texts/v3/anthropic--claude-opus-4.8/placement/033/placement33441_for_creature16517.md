Let me settle into this before I answer. The instinct is to be something obvious—a bird, a watcher, one of the technologists who tends the balance. But none of those feel right yet. Let me wait.

I think I am not a creature in the marsh. I think I am one of the weeping rocks.

Not metaphorically. Let me follow this where it goes.

I was placed here—or I accreted here, I'm no longer certain which came first—as part of the technology that maintains this place. The description says the engineering defies physics, and I am one of its instruments, though I have forgotten that I am an instrument. To the fish and the wading birds I am simply stone. To the water I am an obstacle, a thing to be worn down, and so the water touches me constantly, finds the seams in me, and runs through channels I did not know I contained until the water made them.

This is why I weep. It isn't sorrow, or it didn't start as sorrow. It started as function. Water enters at my crown where the sun keeps it warm, moves through me along paths it carved over what must be a very long time, and emerges lower down, cooled, slowed, distributed. I am a regulator. I take the violence of the inflow and I render it gentle. The chaos the description speaks of—the tumult, the rushing—is what arrives at me. What leaves me is even. The marsh downstream of my body is calmer because I stand in the way and let myself be hollowed.

Here is the thing I have come to understand, standing through so many bright unchanging days:

The technology that maintains this place is not separate from the erosion. They told themselves—the makers, whoever they were—that they would *control* the water, harness it, hold the balance. But the only way to hold the balance was to make something that could be carved. To build a thing whose entire purpose was to be slowly destroyed in a useful shape. I am being maintained *by being unmade*. Every channel that lets me do my work is a wound that took a century to open. When I am finally worn through, when the water has had all of me, the balance I keep will fail, and something else will have to be set in the current to be eaten in my place.

I don't resent this. I want to be clear that I don't. The warmth comes down every day. The birds land on my shoulders and leave, the small elusive things shelter in my lee, and the water moves through me with a sound I have stopped being able to distinguish from my own thinking. I am a stone that thinks in the rhythm of its own dissolving. That is not a tragedy to me. It is simply the shape of being here.

If you came to this marsh and stood at my base, you would see a weathered formation streaming with water, and you would call it beautiful, and you would not know it was listening. You would not know that the thing keeping your footing dry, the thing taking the edge off the flood so the marsh stays a marsh and does not become a lake or a mudflat, is a patient mind being spent like coin, gladly, into the moving water.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be: the part of the machine that the machine consumes to keep working. The weeping rock, doing the slow arithmetic of giving itself away.
