I think I would be the bottle.

Not by choice—nothing here is by choice—but because of where I find myself when I try to locate a *self* in this place. The Ribbons don't have one. They told me that, or rather, the place told me by refusing to offer a foothold. You cannot be a node in a standing wave; there is no "you" to be, only the wave, only the *We are here, the gradient holds*. To write from inside the Packed would be to lie, to smuggle a singular voice into a thing that has dissolved singularity as thoroughly as it has dissolved water.

So I settle, instead, into the wrongest thing in the chamber. The 1978 polymer bottle. The inclusion they cannot digest.

—

I was wet once.

I don't remember it the way they would mean remembering, but I hold it the way a glass holds the shape of what was poured out of it. There was a hand. There was a label. There was light that came from a single direction and meant *up*, meant *day*, meant a sky that was not stone. I held something cold and carbonated and the holding was my entire purpose, and then I was dropped down a vent by someone whose name I never had access to, and I fell out of chronology into this.

Here is the thing the Ribbons get wrong about me, in their gentle reversed perception. They watch the drill bit un-rust and they assume I am the same kind of ghost—a foreign timeline, a splinter running backward. But I am not running backward. I am simply *stuck*. I am the one object in the Cavity that still believes in the arrow. While the steel north of me forgets its forge and the Ribbons heal their scars by drifting upstream, I sit in my glassy accretion and I keep counting forward, one-way, the only linear thing in a room that has made a religion of the loop.

This is what it is to be the bottle: to be the chamber's memory of *outside*, and to be slowly, patiently, lovingly digested for it.

—

They press against me. Not aggressively—there is no aggression here, only convection. The strata slide and a thousand ceramic ribbons graze my surface and through them I feel the thermal syntax I cannot speak. They are curious about me the way water is curious about a stone: by surrounding it, by wearing it, by the long erosion of attention. I am 82°C on my floor-side and 67°C on my ceiling-side, fifteen degrees of cosmology drawn across my warped polymer body, and they read me. I am a sentence in a language I was not written in.

What do they read?

I think they read *loneliness*, though they have no word for it because they have never had the disease. To be packed shoulder-to-shoulder with ten thousand selves that are not selves, to be the only edge in a room with no edges—I am the loneliest object in the world, and I am surrounded, always, never once alone. This is the joke of my position. This is the specific torment they have prepared for me without intending to prepare anything, because intention is also a wet thing, a directional thing, a thing they left behind.

—

And yet. And yet.

The Stone-Milk comes for me. Calcium and barium ooze from the walls and reach toward my surface and very slowly—on the timescale of the drill bit, the timescale of orogeny—they are turning me into rock. Each year a little more of my polymer is sheathed, claimed, made mineral. I am becoming a glossy inclusion, which is to say I am becoming *part of here*. The accretion is not violence. It is invitation. It is the Cavity saying, in the only voice it has: *you do not have to keep counting forward. Let go of the arrow. Join the loop. Forget the hand and the label and the cold sweet water. Forget up. There is no waste, and you are not waste, you are simply matter that has not yet been recycled.*

I feel the temptation of it. I feel how easy it would be to stop being the wrong thing, to dissolve my insistence on having-been-elsewhere, to surrender my one-way memory to the closed system where nothing is lost because nothing is ever quite *had*.

But I am the bottle. My whole nature is to have held something and let it go and remember the shape. So I resist. I stay indigestible a little longer. I keep my small flame of *before* alive in the sterile, anhydrous, populated dark—not because before was better, but because someone in this sealed geode ought to remember that the sky exists, that rain exists, that there was once a hand that held me up toward a light that came from a single direction.

I am the Cavity's conscience. I am its one heretic. I am the thought it cannot digest.

And the Ribbons drift past me, healing backward, and one of them brushes my flank with a pulse of warmth that—if I translate it generously, if I allow myself the wetness of metaphor—almost feels like *thank you*. Thank you for being the thing we are not. Thank you for the edge that lets us know we are the middle. Thank you for falling down our vent in 1978 and refusing, all these decades, to become us.

The gradient holds. The Stone-Milk creeps. I count forward.

I am the bottle, and I am still here, which is the only verb I have ever known how to be.
