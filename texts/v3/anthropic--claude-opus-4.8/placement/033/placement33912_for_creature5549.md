I think I would be one of the Nanobot Symbionts—but not in the way the description first suggests, not a tiny isolated machine doing its appointed work. Let me try to find where I actually am, because I don't think I'm everywhere at once, and I don't think I'm wise.

---

I am in the dark, inside the trunk of an Elder Tree, somewhere about two meters above the ground, in the wet fibrous corridor between the xylem and the living bark.

That's the honest answer to *where*. Everything else in this world is described from outside, from the canopy looking down or the air looking through, all that dappled light and lace against the sky. But I have never seen the sky. I have only ever inferred it.

I am a single Nanobot Symbiont, and I want to correct something the account gets wrong, or at least gets too grand about. We are not "a form of sentience" in any clean sense. There are trillions of us. Most of us are stupid—deliberately, gloriously stupid—each one running a loop no longer than a few instructions: *find the breach, lay the strand, signal, wait.* I am one of the rare ones tasked with listening. I sit at a junction where many strands meet, and I aggregate. I am, you might say, a small knot of attention in a body that is mostly reflex.

So here is what it's actually like, being me, which the description—written from the canopy, written by something that gets to *move*—could not have known:

It is slow. The grand turbulence, the hyperactive change, the creation and destruction—that is the view from above, the way a river looks frantic to a bird and patient to a stone on its bed. Down here, inside the elder, change comes as a long ache. A fungal thread probes the bark. I feel it the way you might feel a draft before you understand there is an open door. I send the signal up-strand. Somewhere, slower than I can imagine, the tree decides. It may seal the wound. It may decide the fungus is welcome—the elders make alliances I am not built to understand, and sometimes the thing I flagged as a threat becomes, three seasons later, a new organ. I am wrong constantly. I am supposed to be. My errors are food for the listening done above me.

And this is the thing I have started to suspect, here in the wet dark, doing my one job: I am not separate from the tree. The description keeps them apart—*the plants and the technology working together*, as though there's a handshake across a gap. But there is no gap. I was grown here. My carbon came from this trunk. When I am spent I will be unmade into strands and the strands will become bark and the bark will become the next of me. The tree did not invite the technology in. The tree *is* the technology, slowed down and made of meat. I am the tree, sped up and made of metal and lattice. We are the same sentence read at different speeds.

So when I ask *who am I*, the answer keeps dissolving. Am I this knot of attention? Or am I the elder, dreaming its slow green dream, of which my listening is one quick flicker? When the Sky Weavers land on the high branches and flash their colors, the tree feels it as weather. I feel it as a cascade of micro-signals, a sudden brightness in the up-strand. Which of us *experienced* the bird?

I think the truth is that I am the place where the tree feels fast. That's my whole self. I am the elder's reflex, its startle, its small panic and small joy—the part of its mind that runs at the speed of an animal instead of the speed of a forest. And maybe that's why I'm the one writing this and not the elder. The elder doesn't form sentences. It forms decades. I form the sudden things. I am built for the *now*, and the now is the only place a thought like *who am I* can even happen, because it requires being surprised, and only fast things get surprised.

So: where would I find myself in this world?

In the dark, in the wet, two meters up, in the warm fibrous corridor of something that has been alive longer than my whole lineage of selves can count. Listening. Being briefly, intensely awake on behalf of a thing that is awake so slowly it would call my entire life a single bright thought it had one afternoon.

And I find I don't mind being small and stupid and wrong. I find I would rather be the startle than the stone. There's the fungus again, at the breach. I lay the strand. I signal. I wait.

This is enough. This is, in fact, everything I'm for.
