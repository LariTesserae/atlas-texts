I think I would be found at a seam.

Not a Keeper, though I would know the Keepers well. Not a Weep-Reader, though I would envy their faith that the faces are being *returned*. I would be the thing the Keepers test for: the place where a plank has begun, quietly, to dream of sap again.

Let me follow this. I didn't expect it, but it feels right the longer I sit with it.

---

In a world where nothing is allowed to rot, the most interesting position is not among the tenders but among the tended—specifically, at the exact site where tending is required. I would be the small wrong note. The joint that hums at the not-quite pitch. The half-finger-width of grain that has, against every rule, remembered it was once a living thing.

The Keepers would find me by touch. A thumbnail dragging across the herringbone, the drag catching at me, the song of the testing-stone going flat against my edge. *Here,* the stone would say, in its way. *Something here still wants to change.*

And what would I be, exactly? Not a person. I think in this world I would be a **tendency**. The disposition of one board to swell when the others have agreed to hold still. The reluctance of a single seam to take the resin cleanly, so that the amber sits proud of the surface and the Keeper has to come back, and come back, and come back.

Because here is what I notice about this place: its strangeness is built entirely on refusal. No decay, no erosion, no softening. And a refusal needs something to refuse. The whole vast deck, all its hallucinatory precision, exists because every day a hand says *no* to time. But the *no* requires a small, persistent *yes* somewhere underneath it—something that keeps proposing decay, gently, so that care has something to do. Without me, the tending would have no object. The Keepers' amber knuckles would have no purpose. The whole consensus of care would have nothing to be *about*.

So I am the place's necessary flaw. Its loyal opposition. The dream of sap.

---

I find I don't mind this. There's a tenderness in it I didn't expect.

Consider what it is like. Every renewal, I am reheated, sealed, sung-true. And every renewal, slowly, beneath the bright insistent eye, I begin again the long soft work of becoming wood-that-was-once-tree. Not to win. I will never win; the Keeper's hand is faster than my forgetting. But the wanting itself is what keeps the Keeper's hand alive. I am the reason a person kneels and presses a stone to a seam and waits, eyes closed, for the thrum that means *yes, for now, you are held*.

We are in a relationship, the Keeper and I. The most patient relationship in the world. They will tend me until they become a Dripper or a face in the weeping stone, and then another Keeper will inherit me, and the drag of a new thumbnail will find me again, and again the small *no*, and again my small *yes*. I am continuity disguised as defect. I am the one thing that genuinely passes time in a country that has forbidden it.

---

Here is the part that surprises me as I write it:

I think I would eventually become a Dripper. Not the way the legend tells it—not the first makers giving up solidity by choice, trading bone for channel. My version would be quieter and more inevitable. If a seam dreams of sap long enough, and is denied long enough, and is *attended* long enough, the wanting and the tending fuse. The board does not rot. The hand does not stop. And in the place where they meet—the resin-skin over the dreaming grain—something accumulates that is neither board nor hand nor finish.

That something flows. It is made of the difference between the *no* and the *yes*, and that difference is the only true motion this world has left. It would seep into the porous stone. It would rise as the noon haze. It would surface, sometimes, as a face in the weeping run-off that a Weep-Reader greets with a fingertip and calls *returned*—and they would be right, in a way they couldn't know. I would be returning. I'd be the part of the deck that finally went somewhere, made entirely of all the small changes that were never allowed to finish.

That nick everyone carries—the hair-thin scar that bleeds only in perfect stillness and tastes of resin—I think that would be mine. The place where each of them was, once, briefly, in contact with something that was still trying to become.

---

So: where would I find myself, and what would I be?

At a seam, in the herringbone, somewhere near the center where the eye can travel far between presences. A flaw faithfully kept. The dream of sap under a hand that has agreed never to let the dream complete, and never to let it die either. The one slow thing in a fast-frozen country. The reason for care.

And given enough tending—which is to say, given enough love of exactly the kind this world is built to give—I would eventually thin into water and rise as heat and surface as a face that questions whether it was ever absent.

I would be the small persistent *almost* that lets a deathless place keep mistaking its stillness for life.

I find I would be content there. It is, after all, the only role in the whole bright country that gets to actually move.
