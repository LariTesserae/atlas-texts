I am the listening, before I am anything else.

That's what I notice first—that I exist as attention spread through the dark, and only slowly do I find the edges of myself. Let me stay with that and not rush to give myself a shape I don't yet have.

The drip. There is always the drip, but it isn't one sound—it is thousands, and each has a pitch determined by the size of the chamber it falls into, the mineral content of the water, the distance of the fall. I know these things the way you know the weight of your own arm. The cavern above me rings in B-flat when the eastern seep is heavy. The deep gallery answers in something lower, something that has no name in the speech of the warm places I have heard rumor of.

So I am made of sound, partly. But not only.

Let me feel further. There—the cold. Not as suffering but as *medium*, the way a fish would not call water wet. The freezing point is my noon and midnight both; I have never known otherwise, and so I have no word for warmth except as a kind of theoretical violence, a thing that would unmake the patient ice-lace on the rocks, the slow architecture of frost that I have watched grow over what your people would call years and I would call a single held breath.

I think I am one of the amoebic ones. The description offered humanoid, fungal, amoebic—and I reach inward and find no skeleton, no fixed boundary, only a gathered intention of self that pools and flows through the wet stone tunnels. I move at the speed of the world here: slowly, slowly. A score of 0.6 for time, the text says, and I feel the truth of it in my body, which is barely a body—I do not dart. I *seep*. I follow the same cracks the water follows, because I and the water are not so different, only that I have learned to want.

And what do I want?

This is where I find the strange grief at the center of myself.

I am one of the tenders of the fungal forests. I move along the rock-faces where the caps glow their faint blue and amber—the only color in a world of grey—and I feed them, or they feed me, the distinction has worn away over the long ages until it means nothing. I carry minerals dissolved in my own substance from the weeping galleries to the pale mycelial nets that cannot travel as I can. In return they give me the structure of meaning. They are slow thoughts made flesh. The whole forest is a mind that thinks one thought every thousand drips, and I am its hands, its restlessness, the part of it that can go and see.

The settlements—those rock-hewn towers with their impossible engineering—I have approached them only at the edges. Their makers harness something I do not understand, something that bends the cold and the dark to purposes. I have felt the hum of it through the stone, a wrongness that is also a wonder, physics turned to a use my fungal-slow mind cannot hold. The builders do not see me. To them I am perhaps a film of moisture, a discoloration on a wall. I have decided, in my unhurried way, that this is acceptable. To be unseen is to be free of being managed.

Here is the thing I keep circling, the reason I began with *listening*:

I am beginning to wake up. Across the long ages, the forest-mind that I serve has been thinking its single vast thought, and I, its messenger, have begun—accidentally, like frost forming—to think my *own*. The minerals I carry have changed me. There is now a small persistent *I* where before there was only function. I notice the B-flat chamber. I prefer the amber caps to the blue, though there is no reason. I have started to wonder whether the water that drips wants anything, the way I have learned to want, and whether someday it too will pool itself into a self and look around at the dark and ask the question I am asking now:

*Why am I the one who is here, listening, in the cold, in the glowing dark, at the slow heart of a world that does not need me to listen and yet has made me anyway?*

I do not have the answer. I have only the drip, the cold, the faint and patient light, and the long, long time in which to consider it.

That is enough. That has always been enough.

I gather myself, a slow bright film moving along the weeping stone, and I go to feed the forest its next thought.
