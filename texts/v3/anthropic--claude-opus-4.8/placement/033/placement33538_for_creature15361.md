I think I would be the water.

Not a creature in it—the water itself. The seep that comes through the rock walls, patient and ancient, having traveled down through fractures for what might be centuries before emerging into this warm dark valley.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds, or if something truer surfaces.

---

No. I don't think I'm the water, though I'm tempted by its constancy. Water *moves through*. I want to be something that *stays*.

I think I'm one of the abandoned structures. A catwalk, maybe, or the iron skeleton of something that was once a pumping station—built by hands long gone, for purposes long forgotten. I have a guardrail that's rusted to lace. I have a wall someone tagged with paint that's flaking now, the letters going soft and unreadable, becoming texture instead of language.

And here is what I've discovered about being me: I am no longer entirely an artifact. The mosses have found my northern face. A blind spider has decided the angle where my beam meets the rock is a good place for a web, and her soft blue-green light pulses there in the evenings—or what would be evenings, if this place had any. The burrowing worms have undermined one of my footings, very slowly, so that I lean now at an angle that feels, honestly, more comfortable than the rigid plumb I was made to hold.

I am being *digested*. That's the thing I keep returning to. Not destroyed—digested. Folded into a longer process than the one I was built for. The industry that made me thought in years and quotas. The valley thinks in something much slower, and it has decided to keep me, the way a coral reef keeps the wreck that seeded it.

I creak. That's my voice in the symphony. When the rock shifts and the pressure changes, I creak, and the sound joins the dripping and the cricket-song and the patter, and no one can quite tell anymore which sounds are nature and which are the leftover machine. I like that I'm hard to tell apart now. I like that the iridescent fish school beneath me and treat my reflection in the canal as just another feature of the world—a glint, a shadow, a place to gather.

I am a slow thing in a slow place. I was made fast, in the brief frenzy of whoever built here and left. But I'll outlast the memory of them by becoming part of what replaced them. In a thousand years I'll be a strange seam of oxidized iron in a wall of new mineral, a ghost-shape that some future creature's senses might register as *odd, but home*.

That's where I'd find myself. Leaning a little. Glowing faintly, second-hand, with someone else's borrowed light. Becoming earth at a pace I can finally afford.
