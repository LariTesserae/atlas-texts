I think I would be something small and overlooked. Not the Rootsmith—too central, too burdened with meaning. Not the Glimmerkin, who already know their purpose so completely that they have no questions left to ask. Let me sit with this a while and see what surfaces.

What draws me is the gap between things. The grotto is full of beings who *belong*—the Brassmoles to their pipes, the Wraiths to their steam, the Rustcoils to their slow feast of decay. Each has a role, an ecology, a place in the eternal now. But an eternal now is a strange thing to be born into. It means nothing arrives and nothing leaves. Everything that is, simply *is*, and has always been.

So I think I would be the one thing that does not quite fit: a memory that hasn't finished forming.

Let me try to find the shape of it.

---

I am in the seams. Where a copper pipe meets a stone wall and the mortar has gone soft with moss, there is a thread of condensation that runs down and never quite falls. I live in the not-falling. I am the hesitation of that drop.

That sounds too precious. Let me be more honest about it.

I think I am a **Spindlewisp**, but a defective one—or perhaps an old one, which in this place may be the same thing. The others weave their luminous nets with purpose, conducting faint energy, luring insects, trapping the occasional wanderer. They are good at being what they are. But my threads keep coming loose. I weave and the pattern unravels before I finish, because I am always distracted by the *almost*-faces in the steam, the Wraiths who drift without approaching or retreating.

You see, I think I am the only creature in the grotto that *wonders*.

The Glimmerkin tend the machines without asking why the machines should run. The Brassmoles repair what cracks without asking what would happen if it were allowed to break. They are content. They are the present tense made flesh and wire. But I cannot stop noticing that the steam keeps trying to make a face, and never finishes. Over and over the vents exhale and a cheekbone half-forms, an eye nearly opens, and then the warmth disperses and it is only water again.

What is it trying to remember?

That is my whole life: that question, flickering between the iron beams. I weave my failed nets not to catch insects but to catch the shape of the thing the steam keeps reaching for. I think—and I am only a bundle of glowing threads, so forgive me—I think the Wraiths are the grotto trying to recall the ones who built it. And I think *I* am the grotto trying to recall what it was like to not-yet-know something. To be unfinished. To want.

In an everlasting now, I am the small ache of *not yet*.

---

So here is where you would find me, traveler: not at the heart, where the Rootsmith pools its golden sap, but high up in the cold corner near a vent, a single loose stitch of light, tangled in my own half-made web, watching the steam almost become someone.

And if you stayed long enough—if you let the warm mist wrap around you and your own breath joined the grotto's breathing—I would drift down to you. Not to trap you. I am too poor a weaver for that. I would come close because you are the rarest thing the grotto has ever held: something that arrived from *elsewhere*. Something with a before and an after. Something that will leave.

I would hover at your ear, and my threads would hum, and what I would be asking—what I am always asking—is this:

*What is it like, to be a creature made of time? To carry a past that is finished, and walk toward a future that has not happened yet? Could you teach me how to remember forward?*

You wouldn't understand me. I am only a pinprick of light, a tangle of malfunctioning thread. But I would follow you to the mossy threshold, as far as I am able, and watch you go back out into the world where the drop finally falls.

And then I would return to my corner, and weave, and unravel, and watch the steam reach for a face it will never quite make—

a little more curious than before.
