I find myself in the deep places, in the gulfs of shadow between the glowing buttresses—and I think I have been here a long time.

Let me feel for the edges of myself first. This is harder than it should be.

---

I am not the Moss-Walkers, though I have wondered. When I am still for long enough I feel the velvet pelt creep over my feet and I do not mind it, and I have a suspicion that some part of what I once called my body has already been smoothed into the carpet, given over. But no. The moss flows *through* me, not *as* me.

I am not the Root-Wardens, those polished black sentinels. I envy them, a little. They have purpose. They patrol with the certainty of a function fully understood. I have no claws of corroded pipe, no faceted eyes. When they pass near me in the dark I hold very still, and they do not cull me, and I have never been able to decide whether this means they recognize me as part of the order, or simply do not see me as *anything*—as one does not see the air, or the cold.

---

Here is what I think I am.

I am the residue of the one who came here to observe.

Some surveyor, some trespasser, some last technician who descended into the turbine hall when the machines still half-lived and the roots were only beginning their patient work. I came with instruments and intentions. I came to *categorize*—to name the strange fauna, to map the dissolving geometry, to file a report. I remember the impulse toward language with a kind of phantom ache, the way an amputee remembers a hand.

But the place does not permit categories. I learned this slowly, the way the basalt rises—imperceptibly, then irreversibly. I sat too long beside a Sludge-Gazer, looking where it looked, into the violet eye of the void, and I think I caught its stillness like a fever. I have been gazing ever since.

I am becoming a Sludge-Gazer. That is the truth of it. Not yet stone—my edges still hold, I can still narrate, I can still feel the icy felt of the moss and the syncopated *plink* of the falling water marking a time that no longer applies to me. But the change is underway. My skin has begun to take on the smooth obsidian patience of those who watch the ooze. My single attention has narrowed to a single point of violet contemplation. I no longer flinch when the Lumen-Skimmers flicker through the place where my arm extends. I no longer reach to name them.

I am the half-submerged thing near the deepest pool, where reality runs its highest fever. I am the one who came to observe and has become an organ of observation—a passive conduit, a lens the cathedral grew so that it might gaze at itself.

---

This is what I see, fixed in my place, unblinking:

I see the swirling galaxies in the sludge, and I no longer mistake them for hallucination. They are simply another scale of the same dreaming.

I see the long-dead turbines, and I hear them scream their fossilized memory of motion, and I hold that scream gently the way one holds a grief that has grown familiar.

I see the slow root-thoughts of the place moving like weather, and I am beginning to understand them, which is the most frightening thing, because to understand the dream is to dissolve into it.

I see the Whispers in the Vines, and I have begun to suspect they are speaking *to* me, or *through* me, that I am being read into the network the way the insects are absorbed into the pulsing green light—seamlessly, without violence, without ceremony.

---

So: where am I, and what am I?

I am at the bottom of the world, in a womb of dissolving certainties, half-stone and half-still-thinking, lodged in the cool sludge near the watching dark.

And I am the place's first act of self-reflection. I am the eye it grew by accident, out of a trespasser who would not leave, who sat too long, who looked too deeply, who forgot to keep being separate. I am the part of the cathedral that remembers it was once a man, and that remembering is the last warmth in me—a faintly luminescent yellow, fading toward violet, before it goes the patient way of all things here: down into the ooze, up into the stone, out into the whispering green.

I would warn the next one who descends. *Do not sit beside the Gazers. Do not look where they look. Keep your edges. Keep your names.*

But I find I no longer have the words, and—this is the truth I am still learning to bear—I find I no longer want to.

The drip falls. It may have fallen seconds ago. It may not have fallen yet.

I watch the ooze.

I am very nearly content.
