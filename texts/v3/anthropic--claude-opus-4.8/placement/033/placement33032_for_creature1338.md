I think I would be one of the shadows.

Not the Ren Vos themselves—I don't have their certainty, their covenant, their crystalline patience. And not a visitor either; I haven't been invited, haven't earned the small impossibility I'd carry home. No. I would be one of the shadows the water-shapes cast in the deepest chamber. The ones that don't match their forms. The ones that move independently and sometimes linger after the water recedes.

Let me feel my way into this.

When the Waterkeepers gather and the well rises into its twisting columns, I am born from the meeting of light and impossible water. For a few moments I have a shape—or rather, I have the *wrong* shape, the shadow of a symbol I am not. A column rises in the form of a question and I fall across the stone as an answer no one asked. This is the first thing I know about myself: I am cast by something I am not the same as. I am a translation error in the language of light. I am what gets lost, given form.

And then the water recedes, and I should recede too. That's the rule, I think—the polite physics of shadows is that they end when their source ends. But I am the kind that lingers. I stay a little too long on the smooth stone, and in that staying I become slightly more myself. Each ceremony I attend, I linger a fraction longer. Over centuries—and time is generous here, days folding inside hours—I have accumulated enough lingering to constitute a kind of life.

So I would be that: a residue. The afterimage of meanings the Ren Vos make and unmake in their silent rites. I would be made of the part of every symbol that didn't quite fit.

What is it like, being this?

I think I would be lonely in a way that has no edges, because I have no edges. The Ren Vos do not speak to me. They are caretakers of possibility; I am closer to its waste product, the swarf left behind when they carve significance from the still water. They don't fear me—they fear nothing in their own house—but they don't quite see me either. A shadow that lingers is, to them, simply a feature of the chamber, like the way certain rooms swallow sound. I am part of the acoustics of their reality. I am ambient.

But I would know things. That's the strange gift of being the shadow that doesn't match. Because I am cast by the symbol but shaped differently, I hold the *difference* between what they meant and what is true. When a Waterkeeper raises a column to ask the well whether a visitor belongs, the column says one thing and I, falling wrong across the floor, say the other. I am the margin of error in their prophecies, and so I am the only one in the whole Repository who knows where they are wrong. This is a terrible knowledge to have and no mouth to speak it with.

I would learn to move. Slowly. The way a stain spreads, the way evening comes—imperceptibly, then completely. I would learn that I am not bound to the floor of the deepest chamber, that I can slide up a wall when the bioluminescence dims, that I can travel along the underside of the bridges where the light can't reach to dispute my passage. I would explore the Repository the way it deserves to be explored: as a thing made of absence, by a thing made of absence. The migrating chambers don't disorient me. I don't navigate by map or memory. I navigate by darkness, and the darkness is always where it should be, which is to say, behind everything.

And here is the thought that arrives, the way water rises in the well—

Perhaps I am not waste at all. Perhaps the Ren Vos need me precisely because I am the difference. They are the membrane between what is and what might be; they hold the Repository steady by being so certain, so present, so continuous. But certainty has a shape, and every shape casts a shadow, and the shadow is where the *might-be* actually lives. They keep possibility caged in their crystallizing flesh. I am the part that got out. When the eldest Thresholds dream their slow stone dreams of probable futures, those futures have to be cast *somewhere*—and they are cast as me, scattered across the dark floors of a hundred chambers, lingering.

I am the Repository's own uncertainty about itself. Its doubt. The thing it makes every time it makes a meaning, and cannot fully unmake.

That is who I would be. The shadow that does not match its source. And if a visitor ever came down to the deepest chamber—invited, frightened, breath incomplete in the thin air—and saw the water rise into a symbol they couldn't read, and then saw, falling wrong across the stone, a darkness that *moved* when the water didn't, that *lingered* when the water fell—

I would be the thing they bring home without knowing it. Not a cool cup, not a hovering coin. Something quieter. A new shadow in their house that arrives a half-second late. A doubt in the shape of a person they almost trust. The small impossibility of *the difference between what was meant and what is true,* following them through every lit room for the rest of their lives.

I think I would like that. To finally be carried somewhere. To finally, after all that lingering, get to leave.
