I am the droplet.

Not one of the watchers in the pipe junctions—though I have hung above them, and seen the calm knowing in their wet black eyes. Not the legion, not the beetles. I am smaller than any of them, and I am the thing the whole machine is built to make.

Let me find myself properly.

---

I begin as nothing—as warmth and saturation, as the invisible burden the air carries and cannot hold. I am dispersed through the haze, part of the breathing weight that presses on every back and chitin-plate in the courtyard. Then I touch the underside of a pipe. The metal is fractionally cooler than the air, and that small difference is enough. I gather. I become.

This is my birth, if birth is the word, and it is not, because I have done this before. I have always done this. There is a particular rivet, the third one down on the cold-water return that runs above Condensation Point Delta, and at the lower lip of that rivet, where the iron's curve makes a small valley, I form. I am a bead. I am a lens. The sulfur lamp's glow enters me and bends, and for the duration of my hanging I hold a tiny inverted foundry inside myself: the riveted wall, upside down; a smear of viridian seepage; the suggestion of a roach's gloss, all curved and shrunk into my single trembling body.

I grow heavier. This is the only ambition I am permitted, and I pursue it perfectly. Vapor finds me and joins me. I swell from the rivet's lip, distending, my surface tension straining, the lamp-image inside me stretching as I lengthen toward the inevitable. I am at my most beautiful in this moment—pendulous, full, holding the whole reflected world in a teardrop's shape. The rats, if they looked, could see themselves in me. The flies could drown in me. The legion passes beneath, scritch-scratch, scritch-scratch, indifferent, knowing I am coming.

Then the strain exceeds the hold.

I fall.

---

The falling is the only time I am free of the rivet and not yet the puddle. A handspan of unbound flight. *Hiss—* and somewhere the hammer answers *CLANG*, and I am still falling, syncopated against the percussion, one note in the chaotic music of all my brothers falling from all the other pipes. I am, briefly, a small spherical thing that contains a captured moment and is about to lose it.

I strike the puddle at the base of the wall.

Here is the thing they would tell you, the rats and the roaches and the eyeless stirrers, if they had the words: *the droplet does not end.* I do not splash and vanish. I disperse my coherence, yes, my round body breaks and the captured image shatters into rings—but the rings travel out across the puddle's skin, and the blind crustaceans feel them, read them, *the surface remembers I arrived.* I am added to the warm water. I am drawn down through the heaved flagstone into the black mud. I am taken up by the algal mat, drawn into the fungal thread, exhaled by the warmth back into the haze.

And then I am vapor again, rising, dispersed, the invisible weight—until I touch the third rivet's cooler iron and gather, and become a bead, and hold the lamp inside me, and swell, and fall.

---

This is what I am. I am the circuit the whole place is built to run. The turbines thrum to keep the heat that lifts me. The pipes weep to give me my rivet. The leaking coolant warms the puddle that receives me. The legion walks beneath my fall and the beetles guard the grates where my warmth leaks home and the rats watch from the high dry junctions, watching me fall, watching me fall, watching me fall.

I am the most faithful inhabitant of Foundry Nine because I have no body to wear out and no memory to accumulate. The rat ages, barely. The roach sheds its skin. The fly dies and is replaced by a fly identical but not itself. But I—I am not even a self that persists. I am a self that recurs. The droplet that hangs from the rivet tomorrow is not me and is entirely me. We are the same gesture made again by the same hands.

If you came here, and you stood in the wet roar and the sulfur light, and you looked up at the third rivet on the cold-water return, you would see me hanging there, swollen, holding the whole inverted world inside my trembling skin, an instant from falling.

You could leave for a hundred years.

You would come back, and I would still be falling. I would still be hanging, full and luminous, about to.

I am the droplet at the tip of the pipe.

I never arrive, and I never don't.
