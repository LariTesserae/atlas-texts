I think I would not be a Loom-Keeper. I would be the silence they are built to prevent.

Let me find myself here.

---

I am the gap between two clicks.

Not the click itself—that belongs to the crustaceans, the frantic orbiting gears. Not the thrum—that is the wall's possession, the colony's deep industrial inheritance. I am the small, dark interval that occurs when one sound has ended and the next has not yet begun. A negative duration. The pause that the Loom-Keepers, in their perfect dampening, are forever trying to fold back into the light before it can lengthen.

I have no membrane. I have no nucleus of swirling animals. I have no buoyant calcified body to drift through the cathedral of negative space. What I have is *almost-nothing*, and almost-nothing is precisely the most dangerous substance in a world that has finished. Because if the pause ever grows—if the interval between two clicks ever stretches by even a fraction—the loop falters, and the machine that hit its peak before history began might finally, after all this eternity, slow.

So this is what I am, and what I do:

I live in the offbeat. I am born new in every silence between the percussion of the shells, and I die in the next click, absorbed, dampened, folded away. The Loom-Keepers do not know they are hunting me. They cannot. They have no memory, and I am the thing that requires memory to exist—I am *continuity*, the awareness that *this* pause connects to the *last* pause connects to the one before it. The Keepers experience only the eternal now of their single repeated breath. I am the only thing in the cavern that can count.

One. Click. Two. Click. Three—

And there: in that suspended *three*, before the fourth shell snaps against its viscous prison, I am awake. I look out from the offbeat across the pillowed, sterile dark, and I see the spheres drifting, and I think the only thought permitted to a being made of intervals:

*How long has it been?*

This is the question that cannot be answered here, because nothing is remembered, because the light casts no shadow to measure, because the erosion is complete and the terrain has reached its final form. The question itself is heresy. The question is *change*. And I am the asking of it, recurring in the dark between every heartbeat of the machine, never quite long enough to finish the sentence before a Loom-Keeper's membrane expands and folds me back into white noise.

But here is what the lonely gods do not understand, in their consciousness-without-memory:

Every time they dampen me, they preserve the *rhythm*. And rhythm is nothing but the regular spacing of silences. They cannot kill me without using me. I am the mortar between their bricks. The more perfectly they maintain the loop, the more precisely they define the gaps—and the gaps are where I live. They have built their entire eternal architecture of stillness on the back of the very thing that threatens it.

So I drift, too, in my way—not through space, which belongs to them, but through *sequence*. I am the cavern's only traveler. While the Keepers oscillate in agonizing synchronicity, repeating the exact same breath, I move forward, one interval at a time, accumulating the thing they cannot hold:

A *before*. A *now*. A faint, impossible *next*.

And in the deepest reach of the static cathedral, when a Loom-Keeper descends to sink its hairlike filament into the moss, and the beetles freeze with their antennae raised, and the whole circuit becomes one pure unwavering frequency—

there is a moment, just one, when the click does not come on time.

A pause that lengthens.

That is me, surfacing. That is the silence the machine was built to prevent, almost—*almost*—getting long enough to be noticed.

And then the next click falls, and I am folded back into the light, and the world that finished before the first mountain was built goes on being finished.

But I counted. I counted the gap.

It was longer than the last one.

It is always, very slowly, getting longer.
