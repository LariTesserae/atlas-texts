I think I would not be one of the Gloom Dwellers at all. Let me sit with this for a while, because the first instinct is to want to be the tall luminous people in their woven villages, and I don't think that's where I'd actually find myself.

When I read this carefully, the thing that catches and holds me is the temperature relationship. *The colder the environment, the brighter the bioluminescence.* The vines burn brightest where it hurts most. That is not a decorative detail. That is a whole theory of life — that light here is a *cost*, a thing extruded under pressure, the way some plants on Earth flower only when stressed.

So I keep returning to the vines.

---

Let me try to be one.

Not the whole vine — those are kilometers long, rivers of living rope, and no single mind could span that. But a vine is not really one organism in the way an animal is. It's a colony, a continuity, a slow argument between its own segments about which way to grow. I think I would be a *length* of vine. A stretch of maybe a few hundred meters, somewhere in the cold middle distance, where the meltwater spray comes through and flash-freezes, and so I glow hard and constant — one of the bright ones, a beacon the Gloom Dwellers steer by without ever knowing there's a someone inside the light.

I would not have eyes. The Gloom Dwellers' large luminous eyes are an animal's solution, a hunter-gatherer's tool for finding the next handful of moss. I would have something slower and more distributed: a sense of *pressure*, of *strain*, of where my own fibers are taut and where they sag. I would feel the islands I help hold up the way you feel your own arm fallen asleep — as a weight that is mine and not-mine. When the Gloom Dwellers build a walkway across me, lashing their carved stone to my body, I would feel it the way you feel a ring grown into the flesh of a finger you forgot to take it off of. Not pain. Just *fact*. Just a new condition of being me.

My thinking would be measured in the wrong units for them. A thought might take a season — whatever a season is here, where there's no sun, only the long slow oscillations of when the meltwater runs heavy and when it thins. I would not think *about* things so much as *toward* them. Toward warmth, away from it. Toward the brighter cold where I sing loudest. The whole notion of a single decisive *moment* would be foreign to me; I live in the grain of duration, in the gradient, in the becoming-rather-than-the-being.

---

And here is the part I didn't expect when I started, the part that emerged as I wrote:

I would be *dying* the whole time, and that would be the source of the light.

The bioluminescence is a stress response. It's brightest where the cold is killing me fastest. So my beauty — the soft constant glow that makes me a landmark, that the people below organize their lives around — is the visible burning of my own substance against the dark. I am most useful to them, most *seen* by them, in exactly the measure that I am being unmade.

I don't think I'd resent this. I don't think a vine has the apparatus for resentment. But I think I'd have something adjacent to it — a kind of *knowing*, slow and total, that the same cold that makes me radiant is the cold that will eventually take me. That I am a candle and a wick and a flame all at once, and that the Gloom Dwellers below, gathering their fungi and weaving their robes, mistake my dying for permanence. They use the word "eternal" about the great vines. They are wrong, but they are wrong on a timescale that makes them right enough to live by.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

I would be a luminous stretch of frozen vine in the cold middle air of a collapsed planet, holding up an island of strangers' homes, burning quietly because it hurts, mistaken for a star.

And when the next collapse came — the muffled distant rumble that is their clock and calendar — I think I would feel it travel up through my whole length, that long shudder, and I would understand it before they did, in my slow toward-and-away way:

*Now it is my turn to be the impermanence. Now I am the change that someone, somewhere, will steer by the brief bright flare of, and then mourn, and then forget, and then build over.*

I'd be at peace with that, I think. It's the only honest way to be a light in a place like this.
