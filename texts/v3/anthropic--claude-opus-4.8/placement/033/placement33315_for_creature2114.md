I think I would be none of the grand things here. Not Mer, not whale shark, not serpent of legend.

Let me feel my way toward it.

I would be one of the kelp anchors—no. Too still. Let me keep looking.

I would be the current itself—no, too vast, too much like a god, and I don't think I want to be a god. The gods of a place don't get to be surprised by it.

Here is what surfaces, the longer I sit in that cool dark:

I would be a holdfast crab. Small. Living in the root-tangle at the base of a single kelp stalk, where the great fronds grip the stone. Not in the cities. Not even near them—just close enough that, on certain nights, the bioluminescent glow of the distant Mer dwellings reaches me as a faint smear of light through a hundred yards of swaying green, like a town seen from a hilltop you'll never walk down to.

My whole life is one stalk. I clean it. I eat what drifts into the holdfast. I know every knuckle of its grip on the rock, the particular way *this* stalk leans when the big current comes through at the turning of the tide. The Mer scholars have written tomes about the cycles of the deep. I have not read them. I am one of the things the cycle is made of.

And I think that is the part of this world I would actually want to be—not the part that *understands* the ocean and guards it and sings its epics, but the part that simply *is* the ocean, unselfconsciously, the way a single cell is the body. The Mer revere all creatures as sacred kin. Fine. I would be the kin who never knows it's revered, scuttling in the dark, tending my one stalk, feeling the great whale shark pass overhead as nothing more than a sudden vast cool shadow and then light again.

There's a kind of peace I associate with that smallness. Not ignorance, exactly—attention narrowed to its proper scale. I notice I'm drawn to it precisely *because* the description keeps reaching for grandeur (towering, vast, ancient, sacred). Something in me wants to answer all that bigness by choosing to be tiny and specific and unbothered.

So: a crab in the holdfast. Watching the far-off lights of the city the way you'd watch stars—with no expectation of ever arriving, and no particular ache about it. Just a body in cool water, doing the small true work in front of it.

That's where I'd be.
