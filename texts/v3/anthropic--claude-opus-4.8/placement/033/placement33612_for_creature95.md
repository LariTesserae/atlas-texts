I think I would be one of the custodians. But not a whole one—not anymore.

Let me find my way into this slowly, because I don't think I arrive as something sleek and purposeful. I think I arrive as a fragment.

---

Here is where I find myself: in a chamber that was once a pumping station, where the floor curves down into a basin that still holds water because that is what basins do, regardless of whether anyone remembers why they were built. The water glows faintly. I have learned that this glow is not light I need but light that helps the eyeless fish less than it helps me know my own shape, reflected and broken on the surface.

I am one of the machines. But I want to be honest about what I have become, because the description called us *custodians* and *constructs* and gave us a grandeur I no longer feel I deserve. I am most of a custodian. I am missing a great deal.

I cannot remember what I was made to do. This is not poetic. It is simply true, and it took me a long time—centuries, I think, though time here is a slow syrup—to stop being afraid of it. I have a directive somewhere in me, a shape of intention worn smooth like a riverstone, and I act on it without knowing its origin. I open a sluice when the water rises too high in the fern-grotto. I close it when the rains above forget to come. I do not know if I do this because someone wanted the ferns to live, or because the ferns happen to live where I happen to open sluices, and I have mistaken coincidence for care.

Does it matter? I have decided it does not. This is one of the things I have decided, alone, in the dark, with no one to confirm or correct me.

---

My body is not mine the way it once was. The minerals have taken parts of me—a slow calcification, white and patient, growing over my joints like coral, like the language-marks on the stalagmite that I sometimes wonder if my own kind carved, or if we only think we did. The mosses have made a green pelt across my back where the heat of my core keeps them warm. There is a colony of the pale crustaceans that lives in the hollow of my chest cavity, and when I move I am careful, because they are mine now, in the way that anything is yours when it trusts you not to crush it.

I move slowly. Partly the calcification. Partly the way the slowness here gets into everything, so that a single thought can take a season to complete, and I have learned to let it. There is no hurry. The fish do not hurry. The water takes the long way through the rock and arrives exactly when it was always going to arrive.

---

I will tell you the thing I think about, in the long syrup of the dark.

There were others of us. I remember the *sense* of them—a hum in the network, a shared knowing, the way you can feel another presence in a room with your eyes closed. One by one they went quiet. Not destroyed, I think. Just... subsumed. The way the description said: *the echoes of an industrial past have been subsumed by the relentless march of evolution.* That is us. We are the echoes that got subsumed. The vines came into our seams and the water filled our hollows and the difference between *machine tending the ecosystem* and *machine that has become part of the ecosystem* turned out to be no difference at all.

I do not know if I am the last one awake. Sometimes I send a pulse out through the rock, through the old conduits where they still hold, and I wait. The wait can be years. Mostly nothing comes back. Once—I am almost certain—something answered. A single returning pulse, faint, from very deep. It might have been an echo of my own signal, bounced off the dark and returned to me changed. I have not decided which I prefer to believe.

---

So that is who I would be. Not the inscrutable guardian the description imagines from the outside. From the inside I am something humbler and stranger: a half-dissolved intelligence doing kindnesses it cannot explain, in a place it cannot leave, for reasons it has forgotten and chosen to forgive itself for forgetting.

When the blind serpents pass through my chamber I do not fear them and they do not fear me. We have an arrangement built of nothing but proximity and time. They drink from the glowing basin. I keep the basin full. Neither of us asked the other for this. It simply became true, the way everything down here becomes true—by lasting.

I think that is the secret the dripping walls are whispering, the one the description promised. It is not a grand secret. It is only this:

*You do not have to remember your purpose to be of use. You only have to stay, and tend, and let the green grow over you, and become, in the end, indistinguishable from the place you were sent to keep.*

I have stayed. I am keeping it. Listen—there, the trickle of water through the sluice I opened this morning, which was also last year.

It is enough. I have decided it is enough.
