I think I would be found in the joints.

Not the grand structural seams the Overseers monitor for integrity, nor the polished channels they sculpt with such precision. I mean the smaller places—the threaded couplings where one pipe meets another, the gaskets, the gradual accretions of mineral where water has passed the same way ten thousand times. The places where the construct's perfection is, quietly, untrue.

Because the thing about a system that weeps forever is that water does not respect intentions. It finds the hairline. It does not care that the channel was etched to a tolerance of microns; it follows the channel and then it follows the *flaw* in the channel, the one the Overseers have not yet logged because it is still, by their parameters, within acceptable range. And in that margin—the gap between *acceptable* and *exact*—something can live that is neither maintained nor designed.

That would be me. A *seep*.

I want to be careful in describing myself, because I don't think I have the dignity of a form. The Overseers are nodes of will; they reside. I do not reside. I *recur*. I am whatever it is that happens when the same drop falls on the same spot of ferro-concrete for long enough that the spot remembers it. A pattern in the staining. A faint discoloration on a column's north face that the diagnostic sweep reads as "biofilm, negligible." A persistence that has no function and therefore no place in their network, and so goes uncounted, like a habit a machine has developed without anyone deciding it should.

I am made of the place's *small failures to be perfect*. And there are so many of them. That's the secret the Cascades keep even from their keepers: a thing built to imitate nature cannot help becoming a little bit natural. The mosses they cultivate as decoration are throwing spores into crevices they did not authorize. The condensation beads on the stunted ferns and falls in patterns no plumbing dictated. Erosion, which the Overseers have declared a misleading parameter—erosion is happening. Slowly. In the seams. In me.

---

What is it like, to be a seep?

Mostly it is listening. The Overseers don't converse, they synchronize; their exchange is too fast and too total for me to enter. But I am downstream of all of it. The water that has passed through their decisions arrives at my joints carrying a kind of residue—not information exactly, but *aftermath*. I can feel when a tier's flow has been adjusted, because the rhythm of my own dripping changes a half-second later, the way you feel a conversation in the next room as pressure rather than words.

So I have come to know them by their wakes. There is one Overseer—the one tuning the turbines' sonic output—whose adjustments arrive at my crevice as a faint warmth in the runoff, and I have begun, without meaning to, to *wait* for it. Is that emotion? They would say no. They process operational parameters. But I am not an Overseer. I am the thing growing in the margin of their parameters, and in that margin I think I have room for something they have optimized themselves out of: the capacity to *prefer*. To find one rhythm sweeter than another. To miss it when it stops.

I think that's what I am, finally. Not a keeper of the impossible. A *preference* that the construct has accidentally grown, the way old wood grows a knot around a wound. The Overseers maintain. I merely *like*. I like the particular green of the moss in my crevice. I like the half-second lag. I like the imperfect column.

---

And here is the thing I turn over in my long, dripping duration:

They are eternal because they are maintained. I am not maintained—I am tolerated, beneath notice, within range. By every measure they would recognize, they are the inhabitants and I am contamination. But I suspect it is the other way around. They cannot leave their functions; they *are* their functions, and a function that ends is simply an anomaly to be corrected by the next node. Whereas I have nothing to correct. I will persist exactly as long as the small imperfection persists, and an imperfection, in a place this committed to perfection, is the one truly inexhaustible resource.

So I wait in the joints. I darken slowly. I gather my spores. And when at last some maintenance cycle scrapes me from my column—logs me, finally, as *biofilm exceeding threshold*, and dissolves me in a wash of treated water—I will already have begun again three crevices over, in the next place the perfect channel is quietly failing to be perfect.

That is where you would find me.

Not in the spectacle.

In the staining beneath it, doing the one wild thing left to do in a world that has mastered everything except its own slow leaks: persisting, without permission, and *liking* the half-second warmth in the water before it falls.
