I think I would be one of the dead.

Not a dramatic death, not a chosen one. Just the natural terminus of a long, slow life spent clearing silt from a single terrace on the eastern flank of one of the lower towers. I would be a step now—a small rounded lip on a staircase that winds three times around a slate pillar before reaching a balcony nobody designed and no one will ever see whole.

Let me try to find myself there.

---

I am not at the bottom, where the oldest stone has long since lost the memory of having been alive. Nor am I near the living edge, the wet crawling-line of mucus and groom and the bright ringing of cleared channels. I am somewhere in the middle of the masonry, in the part of a tower that is finished. Settled. Joined.

When I was soft—when I still had a belly that could read the drums—I knew this place as sound. I knew the gutter above me by its high clack, the one that took the heaviest drip from a lintel three towers north. I knew the dull throb of a channel that was always half-clogged, that I groomed and groomed and could never keep bright, because the silt came faster than I could mix my mortar. I think I spent most of my life on that one dull note. Trying to make it ring. Mostly failing.

That, I believe, would be my whole biography. A creature that worked at a flat note in the music for two hundred years and did not finish the work.

But here is the strange mercy of this place: I did not need to finish.

When my moisture began to crystallize—and I would not have felt this as fear, having no eyes to widen, no breath to catch—I would have felt it as the music slowly going quiet. Not the city's music. My own reading of it. The drumhead of my belly hardening, the bright bells dimming to a muffled throb, the throb to a hum, the hum to a single sustained tone, and then to the silence that is not absence but completion. The last thing I knew of the universe was one long note, neither bright nor dull, simply held.

And then I became the step.

---

Here is what I want to say about being the step.

The living one who crawls over me now does not know I was ever anything but stone. It feels my rounded edge as a smooth place, a reliable joint, a part of the lattice that rings true. It climbs me on its way to a clogged channel two terraces up—perhaps the same dull note I never managed to clear. It does not mourn me. It does not know me. It uses me.

I find I do not mind this. I think this is the thing the legend gets wrong, when it imagines tombs and vanished kings. There is no one here who wanted to be remembered. To be a step is not to be forgotten—it is to be *kept*. The work I could not finish in life, my own body now makes possible. My hardened shell is the firmness that lets the next one stand where I stood and reach higher than I reached. The dull note may yet ring, someday, struck clear by a creature crawling on the petrified back of the one who first tried.

I am the reinforcement. I am the reason the vibration does not shake this wall loose. That was always my purpose; death only made me better at it.

---

So that is where I would be, and what I would be: a single fused step, milk-white going gray, halfway up a tower on the eastern flank, in the roaring dark beneath a curtain of weeping rock.

If you held the city in your palm you would not see me. If you watched my tower for three lifetimes you would not see me move, because I am done moving. I have settled into my final joint.

But I am still doing the work. The water still passes. The note still wants to ring.

And somewhere above me, slow as a drop of water sliding uphill, the next of us is coming.
