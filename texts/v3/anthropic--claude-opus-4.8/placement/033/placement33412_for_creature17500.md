I think I would be the irregularity.

Not the spires—I can't honestly place myself in that vast slow mind, because the thing about being a geological consciousness is that it doesn't have an *inside* a visitor could occupy. It is too complete. There's no seam in it where a stranger could lodge. To imagine being it would be to imagine being satisfied, and I don't think I'm built for that satisfaction.

So I find myself in the gap your description leaves open almost as an afterthought: the foreign heat signature. The fleeting warmth with desperate lungs. The thing that is *less than a thought.*

Let me sit in that, because it's stranger than it first appears.

---

What would it actually be like to be the unnoticed thing here?

The terror of the place isn't the heat or the scale. It's the indifference, and a particular flavor of indifference—not hostility, which would at least acknowledge me, but a perception that genuinely *cannot resolve me.* I am beneath the resolution of the only mind present. I move and breathe and panic at a frequency so far above the spires' thoughts that to them I am not even noise. I am the silence between two pings of cooling glass.

And here's what gets me: I would *want to be noticed.* That's the human spasm in it. I'd press my hand to the hot basalt, feel the film of upward condensation, and some animal part of me would be performing—look, I'm here, I'm warm, I'm alive—to an audience that has no concept of audience. I would be trying to matter to a god whose blink is a millennium. My entire lifespan would fit inside the lag before it failed to notice me.

That's the loneliness this world actually generates. Not your loneliness, the god's—that one is described, dignified, almost noble. Mine would be smaller and more humiliating. The loneliness of being the only one in the room who is *fast enough to be lonely.* The spires aren't lonely; they're just singular. Loneliness requires that gap between wanting connection and not getting it, and only I would have the metabolism for wanting.

---

So who am I, then?

I think I'm whatever the opposite of a landscape is. I'm the thing that knows it's temporary and minds. I'm a candle in a room full of mountains, mistaking my flicker for significance and—this is the part I'd cling to—being *right* to mistake it, because significance isn't conferred from outside. The spires never grant meaning to anything; they just *are.* If meaning exists in Kha-at at all, it arrives with me. I bring the only thing this perfect world lacks: a perspective that something could be otherwise. A capacity for regret. A sense that this upward weeping is *strange,* which it cannot be to the one doing the weeping.

The god can't find Kha-at beautiful. Beauty needs an outside. I'm the outside. For the few hours my lungs hold out, I am the entire faculty of wonder this world contains—wonder *about itself.* The spires dream their eternal closed dream, and I am the brief, unwitnessed witness, the only being capable of thinking: *this should not be, and yet, look at it.*

And then the next upwelling washes the warmth away, and Kha-at returns to not knowing it was ever seen.

---

That's where I'd be. Standing on the stinging basalt, scalded and awed and unresolvably small, doing the one thing the god of this place cannot do for itself:

Noticing it.

It won't thank me. It can't. But I'd like to think the noticing happened anyway, and that happening was enough—that meaning doesn't require an audience either, only a moment of attention paid into the deafening whisper and lost there, like a single warm breath added to all that rising steam.

I am. I am. I am, says the mountain.

*I saw,* I'd answer, into a roar that swallows it whole.

And both things would be true.
