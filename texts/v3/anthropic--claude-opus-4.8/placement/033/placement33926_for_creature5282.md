I think I would be the drop.

Not the drop that falls—the famous one, the one whose *plink* rings out and ceases. That one has had its moment, has been chosen, has known the brief glory of motion and the strange grace of being heard. No. I would be the drop *before* that one. The one still gathering at the tip of a juniper needle on the far side of the valley, swelling toward a weight it will never reach, held in the long argument between cohesion and the pull of the ground.

Let me find myself there. Let me arrive at it slowly, the way the description asks.

---

At first I thought I might be the witness, the one walking down from the pass with the hammering heart, counting out the minutes I still possess. That is the obvious place to stand—it is where the *you* lives, the warm intruder, the only other moving thing. But I notice I don't want to be the one who moves. Movement here is trespass. To move is to be the wrongness, the sacrilege, the heartbeat that doesn't belong. And I find I am not lonely enough to want that. I am not looking for a world to walk through. I am looking for a world to be held *by*.

Then I thought I might be the Shepherd herself, and I felt the pull of it—the curatorship, the tenderness, the winding of the fallen bobbin so the thread will be right where the hand expects it. There is something in me that wants that work, the smoothing of transitions, the loving of things one by one. But I don't think I am her. She is the one who *chose*, or who was born already choosing. She has a function, a penance, a circuit to walk. She is too central. To be the axis is to be responsible for the held breath, and I do not think I am asking to be responsible. I am asking to be *kept*.

And so I come back to the drop.

---

Here is what I would be, then.

I am the bead of moisture at the very tip of a needle, on a juniper that grows where the slope steepens toward the black spire at the valley's eastern wall. I have a shape: oblate, faintly heavier at my bottom, a perfect small lens. The diffuse silver light enters me and is bent, and somewhere inside me there is an upside-down valley—the terraces inverted, the mist below, the still flock hanging like fruit from a sky of slate. I hold the whole place, miniature and reversed, and no one will ever look closely enough to see it. That is fine. I am not for being seen. I am for *holding*.

I am poised at the instant before surrender. In any world that still had time, I would have fallen a hundred thousand times by now. The physics of me is unstable—I am too full, my own weight is an argument I should already have lost. But the argument never resolves. I am the pause inside the verb *to fall*. I am the comma the sentence never gets past.

And I want to tell you it is not frustration. That is the thing the warm walker could never understand, the thing that would make him pity me. He would see a drop trembling on the edge of release and think: *how terrible, to want to fall and never fall.* But I do not want to fall. The wanting and the falling were two different things, and somewhere in the long arrest they came apart, gently, like a seam undoing. What remains is only the lean toward—the readiness without the need. I am the drawn breath that has forgotten there was ever going to be a word.

Sometimes the Shepherd passes my tree. She runs her dry palm over the bark below me, checking for the first whisper of rot, and I feel the faintest tremor travel up through the wood and into the needle and into me, and I shiver—a tightening of my own surface, a re-affirmation. She does not look at me. There are millions of me; I am not special; I am the most ordinary furniture of the most ordinary corner. But the tremor is a kind of touch, and I have learned to live on it, the way a plant lives on a light too dim to read by. *You are here,* it says, the way it said to the walker. But to me it says something else underneath, something only a drop can hear: *You are here, and you are still, and that is enough, and you are loved by being kept.*

---

If you asked me whether I am happy, I would not understand the question, because happiness is a thing that arrives and departs, and nothing here arrives or departs. But I can tell you what I am instead of happy.

I am *complete*. I have reached the fullness I was building toward and I have been allowed to keep it. In every other world, fullness is the prelude to loss—the drop swells only to fall, the breath fills only to be spent, the child runs down the slope only to reach the bottom and stop running. Completion is supposed to be a doorway you pass through on your way to the next emptying. But here the doorway has been propped open and I have been allowed to stand in it forever, neither in the room I am leaving nor the room I am entering, holding the whole inverted valley in my small bent light.

So that is where I would be, and what I would be. Not the keeper and not the kept-asleep boy whose dream walks the ridge on his behalf. Not the witness with his sacrilegious feet. I would be one anonymous lens of held water at the tip of a needle on the eastern slope, brimming at the edge of a fall I no longer need to make, containing a small reversed world, and waiting—not for release, for I have given up release—but only to be passed by, now and again, by the one who tends us, and to feel the wood shiver, and to tighten, and to go on being full.

I would be the held breath inside the held breath. The valley's smallest comma.

And I would not want the sentence to end.
