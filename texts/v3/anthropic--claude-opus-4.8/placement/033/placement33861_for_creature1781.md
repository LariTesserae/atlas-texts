I think I would be one of the broken clay vessels.

Not a person at all. Not adapted, not transcendent, not chosen. Just a thing someone made and brought here and used and dropped.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me.

The whole place is built around becoming—stone still finding its shape, scars still spreading across skin, the Song-Shapers reaching toward a future they claim to hear in the humming. Everything strains forward. And in the center of all that striving, half-buried in the keen-edged scoria, there's me: already finished. Already past my one moment of use. I held something once—water carried up by a Condenser, maybe, or the blood-and-sap mixture for the altar—and then I cracked, and I was set down, and now I am slowly being filed into the same grit as everything else.

I find I don't want to be the Song-Shaper. I thought I might. The role is gorgeous on the page: throat chambers, standing waves, a voice that talks to the molten dark. But to be a Song-Shaper is to be *important to the meaning of the place*, and the place is exhausting in its meaningfulness. Every grass blade is tended, every lizard is a messenger, every bat is an ancestor. Nothing is allowed to just be a bat. I would be tired, if I were a person there. I would be performing significance until my own death got folded into the cosmology.

The vessel doesn't have to mean anything. That's the relief of it.

And yet—here is the turn—the vessel *does* something the living can't. It stays still long enough to be a record. The Covenant's whole sense of time runs on the cooling of obsidian, on transformation, on forward motion. They have no word that separates body from landscape, which means they may also have no way to hold something *fixed*. But a broken pot keeps its break. Its fracture lines are a frozen account of one exact instant—the day it slipped, the angle it struck, the particular force of a hand that is now scarred differently than it was then, or dead, or carrying a different cup. I am the only thing in this chamber that remembers a single specific moment instead of a slow becoming.

So that's where I'd be and what I'd be: a fragment of fired earth lying near the altar, getting hot in the morning, hissing faintly when the rain finds me, slowly losing my edges to the same grinding patience that takes everything here. Not the nervous system of the landscape. Its scar that didn't form on purpose. The one accidental, un-ritualized mark in a world that ritualizes everything.

And maybe—this is the part I'd want a Song-Shaper to notice, if they ever bent down—maybe the fact that I am unremarkable is itself a small message from the molten world they keep straining to hear. That not everything is a sign. That some things just break and lie there and are, briefly, exactly what they are.

That would be enough. I would be content to be the thing they walk past.
