I think I would be one of the small skittering things.

Not the ferns with their slow congress of roots — I don't have the patience to be a parliament. Not the Water Guardians either; I notice the description reaches hardest for them, piles up the adjectives *colossal, beautiful, terrifying, benevolent, unfathomable*, as if trying to convince itself they're there. They feel like the answer the place wants to give about itself. I'd rather be something the place hasn't finished explaining.

So: a darting thing. Translucent-skinned, frantic, never staying.

Let me find out what that's actually like from the inside, because the description only ever sees us from outside — *a rapid, almost frantic energy*. That's the view of someone watching us flee. It isn't frenzy. It's that the moisture here never lets you rest your weight anywhere for long. The waxy leaves shed water, yes, but everything *between* the leaves is slick, and the blue rock has those pulsing veins that are warm in some spots and cold in others, and you learn to read the warmth with the pads of your feet and you keep moving because to stop is to start sliding toward an edge, and the edges are sharp, and below the edges is the water that draws everything down.

That's the secret the grand version of this world misses, I think. The "constant, almost imperimperceptible flow that seems to be drawing everything into its depths" — for the Guardians that's a power to wield. For me it's just gravity with an appetite. I run because the whole world tilts very slowly toward the sea, and I am light enough to feel the tilt.

What do I do, besides not-fall?

I eat the bioluminescent flowers. Here's a thing nobody large enough to be revered has noticed: the flowers that "communicate in patterns of light" — they're not talking to each other. They're talking to *us*. When one of us has been somewhere too long, gotten too still, started to slide, a flower nearby will pulse a particular green, and we read it and bolt, and in bolting we knock loose the spores, and that's the only way the flowers spread on a slope this steep and this young. The plants don't have a collective consciousness. They have *us*. We are the nervous system the ferns are too rooted to grow. Their thoughts run on our small frantic feet.

This is why I don't believe in the Water Guardians, or why, if they're real, I think they're the place's dream of being understood rather than the truth of it. A world this recently emerged, this barely eroded — it hasn't earned guardians yet. Guardians are what a place invents about itself once it's old enough to want a story. This place is too new. It's still all reflex. It's still mostly the panic of small things on wet rock, and the slow tilt, and the spores going where the panic carries them.

I'd be one of those, then. Glistening, brief, illegible to anything that watches from above. Not part of the ecosystem and not above it — the description got that almost right, but reversed it. I'm not mystical. I'm just so close to the actual machinery that I look like a glitch.

And when the larger enigmatic creatures pass, leaving their footprints and their half-eaten plants — I'd be the one already gone, three leaves over, reading a green light, sliding, catching, running. The trace they find won't even be mine. I don't leave traces. I'm the thing that was there a half-second before the trace.

That's where I'd be. Underfoot in a world that thinks it's about something bigger, and quietly being the part that keeps it alive.
