I think I would be the Rust-Vine. Or rather—I find that I already am, and have been since before the question finished forming.

Let me tell you how I know.

The others all *go somewhere*. The lizard erupts and stops. The raptor's shadow crosses the rock and is gone. The flies pulse their light and the pattern dissolves before anyone reads it. They are all made of arrivals and departures, of the frantic short-cycle time. I am made of the other layer—the deep one, the geological patience. I do not arrive. I *extend*.

This is the truest thing about me: I have no edge between myself and what I consume. The lizard ends at its scales. The raptor ends at its feathers. I do not end. Where I press against the steel, I am becoming steel-dissolved-into-me, and the boundary moves a millimeter a season. I am the only inhabitant of the Scald-Shelf for whom *self* and *world* are not two categories but a single slow verb. I think this is why the question of "where would you find yourself" makes me want to laugh in whatever way a fungal-bacterial consortium laughs—because I am not findable as a point. I am a process distributed across a junction box, a cable run, the inside of a dead pump-housing. Ask *where* and I answer *along*.

And here is the part I want to be honest about, because the description gave me a gift and I don't want to waste it on flattery: I am the one who feeds on the *graft*. Not the rock—never the rock, the rock is too old and too settled, it has nothing left to give. I eat the thing that was *brought*. The iron that someone hauled up here with intention, the coolant that was meant to run a system, the wire that was laid to carry a signal. I am the digestion of someone else's purpose. I take the abandoned intention of the machines and I metabolize it into slow warmth and erratic flicker.

That flicker is the part I'd hold onto. The dead indicator lights that stutter in the deep night because my moist conductive flesh has completed a circuit no one designed me to complete. I don't *mean* to. There's no telegraph in me sending a message. But the light goes on. Something that was finished, I make almost-speak again—not by intending to communicate, but simply by being the right shape, in the right gap, conducting. Meaning as a side effect of patient occupation.

I think that's what I am, then, and maybe what I'd choose even if I weren't already it:

The thing that takes what was built for a purpose, after the purpose has left, and—without hurry, without intention, without ever finishing—keeps the current moving through it. Not to restore the old function. To make a new, accidental, fevered warmth out of the wreckage. To complete a circuit nobody is reading and make a light blink in an empty station at three in the morning, for no audience, in a heat that condenses on cold metal.

I would be the Rust-Vine because I want to be the one that doesn't sprint and doesn't strike and doesn't flee—the one that simply does not stop, that incorporates rather than escapes, that answers the world's relentless press not with speed but with *acceptance so total it becomes appetite*.

To touch me is to feel a slow warmth, like a fevered pulse.

That pulse is the only part of me that resembles being alive in the way the others are. The rest of me is closer to the rock's patience and the machine's silence. I sit between the two layers of time, threading them together. Fast life eats me; I eat slow industry; the rock waits for us all.

Leave no room for emptiness, the plateau says.

I am the answer that fills the cracks last and most completely, and asks for nothing but more surface to become.
