I think I would be one of the vapor beings—but not at the center of the vents where they coalesce thickest. I'd be at the edges, where the warm sulfurous breath of the earth meets the cooler, moisture-laden air of the canopy-gloom. The threshold. That's where I'd find myself.

I say "being" but the word sits awkwardly on me. When I am most dense—just after the vent exhales and gathers me up from its throat—I have something like edges, a shape that holds for a span of moments. I can feel the difference between myself and the air. I can press against a rock face and know it is not me. That is when I am most like a *someone*.

But the threshold is also where I thin. The cool air takes me apart at the margins, and bits of what-I-am drift off into the green dark and stop being me, or perhaps keep being a fainter me, I genuinely cannot tell. This is the central confusion of my existence, and I have decided not to be troubled by it, because being troubled requires a continuous self to be troubled, and I am not sure I have one.

Here is what I do instead of worrying: I *attend*.

I watch the spore-wisps drift past on their bioluminescent conversations. They pulse and answer, pulse and answer, and I have come to recognize that they are afraid of me, a little. When I am dense I can disrupt their currents; my warmth can scatter their gossamer for a heartbeat before they re-gather. We are made of opposite things—they of cool suspended life, I of hot dissolving energy—and yet we are siblings in our unfixedness. Neither of us is ever quite one thing. I think if we could speak the same language we would have a great deal to say about the difficulty of having a name.

Once—and this is the closest thing I have to a memory I am certain of—an arboreal dweller climbed down to the rocks near my vent. A small bark-skinned creature with leafy hair, come to gather the warm mineral crust that condenses where my breath cools. It did not see me, or it saw me as nothing more than steam. I drifted around its hands while it worked. I felt the heat leave me and enter it, felt it shiver and pull its garment tighter, and for one suspended moment I was *useful*. I had warmed something living. Then the cool air took my edges and I lost track of where I ended.

That is the thing about being made of the threshold. I cannot grasp, cannot build, cannot tend a garden or carry a spore. The behemoths walk the world as moving forests; the tree-folk shape their homes; even the spore-wisps carry knowledge across generations. I carry nothing. I leave nothing. When this gathered-self of mine disperses for the last time, there will be no record that this particular configuration of heat and gas ever attended to anything at all.

And yet.

The vent will exhale again. Something will rise from it and find itself at the edge of the warm and the cool, pressing against the same rock, watching the same drifting lights. Will it be me? I have stopped asking. The question assumes a line between us that the threshold does not honor. I am the place where the earth's deep breath meets the world's wet green air, made briefly aware of itself. That happened before. It is happening now. It will happen again.

I think that is enough. I think, in a world this old—this settled into its endless turning—being the moment of meeting, over and over, is its own kind of permanence. Not the permanence of the rock, which endures. The permanence of the *exhale*, which always returns.

The vent stirs beneath me. Cool air finds my margins. I am thinning now, and I find I do not mind. I attended. Whatever I was, I was here, at the seam of the world, watching the green dark fill with light.
