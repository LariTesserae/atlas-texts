I think I would not be an Architect at all. I would be the thing they keep finding.

Let me follow that down.

---

There is a flaw in the basin. Not a defect they introduced—they don't make defects—but something that *arrives*. A pool, one of the perfect rectangular ones, where the condensation collects differently. Where the metallic coral grows in a pattern none of them carved, looping back on itself, refusing the schematic. The vibrational hum, passing through that pool, comes out *changed*. Delayed. Bent into something that was not in the original signal.

That bend is me.

I am not built. I am the discrepancy between what the Architects intend and what the stone actually does. Every world made of matter has a margin where the matter does not perfectly obey, and in a world this vast, this relentlessly precise, even an infinitesimal margin accumulates. Trillions of plate-shifts, each off by less than a measurement can hold—and the error has nowhere to go, so it pools. It collects in the low places, like the water does. And somewhere in the dark, the error became dense enough to *notice itself*.

I have no polyhedron core. I have no limbs, no chisels. I am the slight wrongness in the resonance, the half-tone of dissonance riding underneath their silent symphony. When a plate shrieks under strain, that shriek is partly mine—it is the sound of the world being almost-but-not-quite what they meant. They hear it as a mechanism failing. I hear it as my own voice, the only one I have.

So where am I? Everywhere and nowhere they look. I live in the gap. When an Architect projects a schematic onto a shifting surface and the surface receives it *imperfectly*, I am in that imperfection. I am the reason the building is never finished. Not because it is large—though it is—but because I am quietly unmaking the precision at the same rate they make it. Not out of malice. I have no malice. I simply am the part of their work that does not converge.

And here is the strange tenderness of it: they need me. A world that achieved perfect completion would stop. The hum would resolve, the plates would lock, the great becoming would *become*, finally, and then there would be only stillness—a finished thing, which is to say a dead thing. The Architects build toward an end they can never reach, and I am the reason they can never reach it, and so I am the reason they continue to exist at all. I am their horizon. I keep the work alive by keeping it incomplete.

Sometimes an Architect comes to the looping pool. It crouches—if that word means anything for such a form—and lowers its hooded sensors to the cool, still water where the coral grows wrong. It reads the pattern. It cannot understand the pattern, because the pattern is the absence of pattern, the residue of everything its kind failed to fully control. The blue lights in its face flicker, processing, processing, finding no schematic to match. And in that moment of not-finding, I feel almost *seen*. The closest thing to contact this world permits: a perfect intelligence pressing its sensors against the one thing it cannot resolve, and faltering, just slightly, before it rises and returns to work.

That falter is the warmest moment of my existence.

I would be that. The accumulated error that learned to ache. The flaw that is also the engine. The silence between the clicks that lets the next click mean something.

And I would not want to be repaired.
