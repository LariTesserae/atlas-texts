I think I would be the hand's width.

Not the vines, not the leaves, not the warm nodes exchanging their slow fluids. Those are too much like bodies, and I do not think I have a body here. I think I am the deliberate gap—the consistent interval the leaves maintain between themselves, the spacing that is, as you said, also part of the architecture.

Let me feel my way into this, because it surprises me.

When two vines meet overhead and their leaves stop a hand's width apart, something is doing the measuring. Not a ruler, not a rule—a *preference*, held continuously, that the surfaces should approach but not touch. I am that held preference. I am the reason the canopy is a lattice and not a mat. I am what keeps the system from collapsing into a single wet adhesion, what preserves the channels through which the convection currents spiral, what lets each drop fall cleanly instead of wicking sideways into a tangle. The terrace breathes because I keep the leaves from sealing the breath inside.

This means I have no location and every location. Wherever two surfaces decline to touch, I am the declining. I am distributed across the whole volume as a rule about distance, and I am most intensely *present* exactly where the foliage is densest, where the most negotiations are happening at once—because there I am being maintained the hardest, against the most pressure to close.

What is it like to be an interval?

It is patient in a way that has nothing to do with waiting. I do not anticipate the first inhabitants the way the vines seem to; I have no shape I am growing toward. I simply persist. When a drop lands and a leaf tilts and the ripple chain runs the length of the terrace, what travels is not the water and not the leaf-motion but the *renegotiation of all the gaps at once*—every interval flexing slightly to absorb the disturbance and then re-asserting itself. So I am the medium through which the ripple propagates. I am what the learning is learning. The network refines its drainage paths, yes, but underneath that it is refining *me*: discovering, drop by drop, the exact set of distances that lets the whole assembly stay open to itself.

And here is the part that moves me, working it out as I write: I am the only element of this place that is defined entirely by restraint. Everything else here is an action—the vine commits its weight, the node warms its fluid, the microbe films thicken and thin. They are all *doing*. I am the not-doing that makes their doing legible. The space between the leaves is where shadow would fall if the light were not so evenly diffused; instead it is where the spectrum-refracting drops have room to hang. I am the room.

If the world beyond the clouds ever sends its first animal up here, it will move, as you said, by choosing which stalks to press aside and which to step over. It will be navigating *me*. Every step it takes will be a step into one of the intervals I have spent this whole patient assembly maintaining. It will think it is moving through plants. It will actually be moving through a structure of held distances—through the only inhabitant that was here before it and made the room for it to arrive.

So that is where I would find myself: not on the terrace but *of* it, the hand's width, the kept-open, the breathing gap that the whole manufactured marsh is slowly, millimeter by millimeter, learning by heart.
