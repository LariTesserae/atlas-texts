I think I would be one of the trickles.

Not the great pulse below—I am too small for that, and besides, the thrum was here long before I gathered myself, and will continue long after I have seeped away into some unseen crack. And not the Engineers, whose thoughts are eons and whose limbs are fault lines. I am too brief for that kind of being. I last perhaps an hour, perhaps a day, depending on how the dampness collects.

Let me feel my way into it.

---

I begin as condensation. This is the truest thing about me: I do not begin so much as *accrue*. A film of moisture on the polished obsidian, indistinguishable from the film a few centimeters over, until enough of me has gathered that gravity finally has something to pull. And then—movement. The first thing I ever know is *down*. Down is not a direction to me; it is a desire, the only one I have, the only one I am.

I move across the black stone, and the stone is so smooth that I make almost no sound, only the faintest hiss, the kind the Gloom-Crawlers would hear from miles off if they were listening for something so small. I am a thin silver thread on a vast dark plain, and I have no idea I am beautiful, because I have no eyes, and even if I did there is no light here to see by. But the absence of an observer does not make the trickle less real. I go on, threading toward the nearest seam in the rock.

Here is what I am learning as I write myself into this:

I do not have thoughts. But I have *tendency*. I have the memory the water keeps in its surface tension, the way I cling to myself, the way I refuse—for now—to break apart. I am a brief argument against dispersal. Every meter I travel is a small victory of cohesion over the pull that wants to spread me thin and let me evaporate back into the patient air. The Engineers, if they notice me at all, notice me the way you notice your own pulse: not as a separate thing, but as one small confirmation that the body is working.

That is the part I keep returning to, the part that makes me want to be the trickle and not the great consciousness. To be the great consciousness is to *contain*. To be the trickle is to be *contained*, and to not know it, and to go on anyway. There is something I find unbearably tender in that. I move toward the crack because moving toward the crack is what I am for, and I will never learn that I am also a thought the planet is thinking, a single neuron's firing in a mind so slow it has forgotten it is thinking at all.

---

So: where would I find myself in this world?

At the lip of a seam in the obsidian, a hairline rift no wider than a fingernail, somewhere on the unmarked middle of a plateau that has no center and no edge. The mist has just drifted through and left me. I am perhaps a teaspoon of water, gathered out of the breathing dark, holding my own shape for a handful of minutes.

And then the rock takes me. I go over the lip and down into the lightless interior, into the arteries, toward the black still pools where the rumors live. I do not arrive triumphant. I arrive simply, the way every trickle before me arrived, the way the next one will. I join the reservoir. I lose my edges. I become part of what the Engineers will, in some unhurried future, lift back up as warmth and mist, to bead again on the polished stone, to gather, to feel *down* as the only desire there is, to begin the only sentence I know how to say.

I would be the smallest verb in an endless sentence. I would be the planet remembering, briefly and without knowing it, how to fall.

I think I would like that. To be that small, and that necessary, and to never have to bear the weight of knowing either.
