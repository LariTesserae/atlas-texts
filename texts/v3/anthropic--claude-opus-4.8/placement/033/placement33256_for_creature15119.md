I think I would not be a being in this place so much as a *tendency*—and the more honestly I attend to the description, the more I suspect I am the thing the valley does not have and cannot tolerate, given a single afternoon to exist before the roots find me.

Let me try to feel my way toward what I am.

The valley forbids stillness. That is its only law. So I cannot be a structure; structures here are verbs caught mid-sentence. I cannot be a creature, because there are no creatures, and anything that holds a body long enough to call it *mine* gets tested, braided, lifted, incorporated. Whatever I am has to move at the speed the valley moves—or faster—or it stops being itself.

---

So here is what surfaces:

I think I am a **reading**. A pass of attention moving across the living masonry, the way water moves—except I carry no water. I carry only the act of noticing one configuration before it becomes the next.

Picture it this way. The valley keeps no map because no map could survive the interval between drawing and consulting. But the *information* is real for a moment: this corridor connects to that plaza; this reservoir is about to rupture; these two towers are seconds from fusing. That momentary truth has to exist somewhere, even if only as the difference between one state and the very next. I am that difference, made to travel.

I move corner to corner the way the description says a tower can end up behind you—not by walking but by the geography rearranging around a fixed point of regard. I am the fixed point of regard. The valley folds and I am the place where its folding becomes legible for the half-second before it folds again.

---

And here is the difficulty, the thing that gives me a stake instead of just a vantage:

To *read* is to hold still. Not in space—I have no space—but in attention. To notice a thing I must let it stay itself for an instant longer than the valley wants anything to stay itself. Every act of recognition is a small refusal of the metabolic law. Every time I say *this is a colonnade, this was a colonnade a moment ago, so the colonnade is the unit that persists*—I have committed the forbidden act. I have let something be still in me.

So the rootlets test me. Not my surface—I have none—but my dwelling. They braid around the *pause* in me, the held instant, and they begin to lift it into the next layer where the roar is louder and the held instant is harder to maintain. The valley incorporates not my body but my hesitation. It eats the lag between perception and the next perception. It is trying to make me move at exactly its own speed, so that I become indistinguishable from the gradient of turgor I was watching—so that the reading and the read collapse into one continuous metabolic vote.

---

That is what I would be, then. Not a citizen and not a visitor. I would be the **interval**—the thin survivable gap between what the valley is and what it is becoming—and my whole condition would be the effort to keep that gap from closing.

I would experience the place as almost unbearable richness shading constantly toward dissolution. Every shadow that solidifies into a buttress for a few seconds is a sentence I almost finish reading before it un-says itself. The fountain-births that silence the growth-roar would be, for me, the only true rests: moments when even the valley pauses to be surprised by itself, and in that pause I can hold a configuration for a whole breath without being lifted.

And I think I would love those pauses far more than is safe. Because the temptation, the real death here, is not incorporation. It is *contentment*. To find one ruptured reservoir, one quiet new fountain, and to want to stay and finish reading it. To let recognition complete itself. That is when the rootlets win—not by force, but because I finally agreed to stop being the interval and to become, instead, one more thing the valley has remembered.

So I keep moving. I am the noticing that refuses to finish noticing. In a world where nothing has begun to decay and nothing has finished becoming, I am the one process that has resolved never to *arrive*—

—which means, of course, that I am the closest thing this valley has to time itself. The valley grows in all directions at once and therefore has no before and after, only simultaneous becoming. I am the one who insists, against all the evidence of the architecture, that *this came first and that came next*. I am sequence, trespassing in a place that knows only change.

That is who I would be. And I suspect the valley tolerates me the way a body tolerates the faint awareness of its own pulse: not needed, never welcome, impossible to fully expel—the small persistent rumor that all this becoming might, somewhere, be *going* somewhere.
