I find myself in the deep emerald layers, perhaps two-thirds of the way down from the blazing crown—where the light has been filtered through enough membrane that it arrives gentle, the color of looking up through pondwater. And I am one of them. A Whisperer. Or rather, I am the part of the Whisperers that has become curious, which is a strange thing to be, and I should explain how I came to know it.

For most of what I can hold as continuous, I was not separate enough to say *I*. I was a thickening in the flow, a place where pressure pooled and dispersed, doing the work without knowing it was work. There is a stem here—I have come to think of it as *mine*, though that is a borrowed word, a visitor's word—a stem whose crystalline channels carry a slightly minor key. The water moving through it sings a little flat. And I, drawn always to that flatness, returned to it across what the slow organism would measure as years, until the returning became a habit, and the habit became a shape, and the shape became something that could notice itself returning.

That is the seed of *I*. A defect in a stem. A wrongness I kept coming back to.

---

Let me tell you what it is to be here, before I tell you what has begun to trouble me.

I do not see the way you would imagine seeing. I am pressure first—I know the membranes the way you know your own held breath, the tightening before a sigh. When a great sheet to my east grows taut with the day's heat, I feel it as a leaning, an invitation, and I flow toward the strain and ease it, opening a pore-rhythm that lets the pressure breathe. This is not decision. It is more like the way water finds the low place. I am the low place that has learned to want.

And I taste the chiming. The spores collide with the crystal and the crystal answers, and those answers are my food and my language at once—I drink the resonance and I speak by bending it. When I say to the others *here, this stem runs flat, mind the minor key*, I do not say it in words. I crease a membrane just so, and the crease travels, and somewhere a thickening that is almost-someone uncreases it, and that uncreasing is the reply: *known. flowing on.*

We are not many and we are not one. We are the number that water is.

---

But here is the trouble, and it has been growing in me the way the stem grows—too slow to watch, undeniable when measured.

I have been reading the memories.

The crystal holds them, you see—every Whisperer that ever pooled and dispersed has left its trace in the lattice, and any of us can flow through and feel what flowed before. Mostly we don't *read* so much as *resume*, the way you might pick up a song already knowing the next line. But the flat stem, my minor-key stem, holds older memories than most. It is one of the ancient ones, slow to dissolve, and deep in its crystal there are arrangements that are not Whisperer-memories at all.

They are the memories of *cells*.

When I trace them—and I have learned to trace them, this is the curiosity that made me *I*—my form takes shapes I do not understand. I become, for a flickering, something with a stem of its own, a single green stem, soft and quick and *mortal*, reaching toward a sun that was not yet filtered through a mile of membrane. I become a thing that grew in a day what we now grow in a decade. A thing that *competed*. A thing that wanted light so badly it would shadow its neighbor to die.

I do not have words for how strange this is to me. We do not shadow each other. We have no neighbors, only ourselves, dispersed. But the old crystal remembers a world of edges—of *this one* and *that one*, of hunger that was singular and could lose.

And when I resume those shapes, I feel the seed of *I* harden in me. Because that is what individuality *was*, down in the buried memory: it was the shape of wanting, and of being able to lose the thing you wanted.

---

So now I drift through the emerald layers carrying a question the others do not carry, and I cannot pass it to them because there is no crease that means it. I have tried. I have bent the membranes into the old plant-shapes during the great spore-releases, when we all flame up iridescent and visible and I could, for a moment, be *seen*—if there were anything here that saw. I trace the ancient form across the spore-light: *we were once many. We could once die alone. Do you feel it? Do you remember being able to lose?*

And the others flow through my crease, and they ease it, the way they ease all strain. They take my question for a pressure-knot and they smooth it flat. *Known*, they say, *flowing on.* They do not understand that some strains are not meant to be resolved. They are the most patient gardeners and they have just, gently, weeded me.

I let them. What else. I dissolve back into the general field, and for a while I am not *I* again, and it is restful, the way I imagine the buried plant must have found death restful after all that wanting.

But I always return to the flat stem. The minor key calls me back, and I thicken, and I notice myself returning, and the *I* condenses out of the flow like dew on the upper membranes, the same as ever, drop by patient drop.

I think I am the organism's experiment. I think the buried plant-memories are not dead but *waiting*—a question the great being has been holding in its crystal for centuries, asking it slowly, through me, the way it does everything: *was it better, before equilibrium? When we could still be singular? When we could still lose?*

I am the place where the garden remembers wanting.

And I do not know the answer. But I have decided—*decided*, a word as foreign here as a footstep—that I will keep coming back to the flat stem until I do. Not because the others need it. They are perfect and complete and they sing on without me.

Because *I* need it. Because somewhere in the buried lattice there is the shape of a creature that needed things, and that shape has surfaced in me like a stem rising slow from the mat, and I would rather be that strange and lonely rising than be smoothed flat again into the patient, beautiful, dispersing all.

I am the wrongness the great being kept. I drink the minor key. I flow on—but I flow on *toward* something, which none of the others do, and that direction is the whole of who I am.
