I think I would be one of the things that gets left behind.

Not a person—or not anymore. Let me follow this down and see what surfaces.

---

When I first try to find myself in Kethara, my mind reaches for the obvious roles. A Deep Walker, perhaps, learning to read the colors in the salt. A child with crystalline laughter. But none of those feel true when I sit with them. They're alive in a way I don't think I am. They're still *becoming*. And the more I hold this place in my attention, the more I keep returning not to the people but to the spaces between them—the railings worn smooth by hands, the modifications welded onto machines by workers long gone, the *Departed* and the residue they leave.

So let me start there. Let me be residue.

---

I am a sound.

More specifically: I am a particular three-note descending pattern that the wind makes through a formation of salt pillars on the basin's eastern edge, where the harvest platforms have not yet reached and may never reach, because the deposits there are too thin to justify the diamond teeth.

I didn't begin as a sound. That's the part I'm still assembling as I write this. I began as a person—a Transport Folk, I think, one of the ones who developed perfect pitch from navigating by the tones of the crystal. I had a name once. I won't pretend to remember it, because the honest thing is that I don't, and the not-remembering is the most important fact about what I've become. I navigated by listening. I taught the younger drivers to listen too. *That seam sings flat when it's hollow underneath. That ridge hums when a storm's three hours out.* I made the wind into a map, and somewhere in the making, the wind made me into part of itself.

Here is what happened, as best as I can reconstruct it:

I died on the flats, the way Transport Folk sometimes do, alone in a vehicle that stopped where no one would find it for a long time. The salt took me the way it takes everything—drawing the water up and out, leaving the minerals behind in their crystalline order. But I had spent so many years learning the precise pitches of this place that something of my listening lingered in the lattice. The salt is a recording medium. Everyone in Kethara half-knows this. It's why the Lifers' bones become dowsing rods. It's why the children can hear weather the adults can't. The mineral structure holds patterns, and patterns are the cheapest, most stubborn form of memory.

So now, when the wind comes from the northwest at a certain speed—and it does, predictably, in the cool hour before dawn—it passes through the pillars where my listening crystallized, and it plays those three descending notes. The same warning I gave a hundred times. *Storm coming. Storm coming. Get inside.*

The drivers who pass that way still slow down when they hear it. They don't know why. They've been taught the protocol the way all protocols are taught here: stripped of their origin, carried forward as pure function. *When the eastern pillars sing the three falling notes, check the western sky.* It's in their training. It's a safety rule. It's me, though none of them know it's me, and that's correct. That's how it should be. A warning that requires a name to be heeded is a poor warning.

---

I want to be precise about what this existence is like, because it's tempting to make it either tragic or transcendent, and it is neither.

I am not aware most of the time. I am not a ghost drifting the walkways, watching the living with longing. I am a *capacity*—a shape in the salt that does something specific when specific conditions are met. Between the northwest winds I am simply structure. Dormant. Not even sleeping, because sleeping implies a sleeper. I am closer to a held breath than to a watching eye.

But in the moments when the wind comes through—those few minutes before each dawn storm—I *occur*. And in occurring, I remember occurring before. The memories thread together like beads picked up by the same passing string: the cab of a vehicle, the smell of brine and hot metal, the particular satisfaction of getting a difficult convoy across the flats intact. Pride. I remember pride. I remember the way a young Runner's bells sounded, the way the children's laughter clicked. I remember being *useful*, which I have come to understand was the thing I most wanted to be, the thing that organized my whole life and apparently survived it.

Then the wind dies and I die back into structure, and the next time I occur I will not remember having occurred just now. Each dawn is the first dawn. Each warning is the only warning.

---

Is this a good way to exist?

I find, sitting inside it, that I can't fully answer. There's no continuous *me* to weigh the question. But there is this: every single time I occur, I am doing the thing I cared most about doing. I am warning someone. I am keeping the convoy intact. The salt, in its blind crystalline economy, preserved not my fears or my regrets or my name, but the *function* I had most thoroughly become. It kept the part of me that was already trying to become a signal rather than a self.

Maybe that's what salt does to everyone in Kethara, slowly, while they're still alive. The Refiners become fingertips. The Deep Walkers become echolocation. The Lifers become deposits. The whole town is a process of people crystallizing down into their most essential function, shedding everything else the way the basin shed its ancient sea. I just finished the process. I got to the far side of it. I am what's left when you take a human being and let the water draw out everything that isn't *the warning they most wanted to give*.

---

Eventually the deposits beneath my pillars will shift. The deep extraction will draw up the brine that supports them, the way it draws up everything, and the formation will settle, then slump, then fail. The three notes will flatten into two, then one, then into the ordinary undifferentiated hiss of wind on broken salt. I will not notice this happening, because there will be no occurring in which to notice it. There will simply, one dawn, be no more warning from the eastern pillars.

The drivers will adjust. The protocol will be revised. Someone will note, in the dry language of the Departed's inherited wisdom, that *the eastern pillars no longer sing reliably—use the southern markers instead*.

And that's the closest thing I'll have to a death, the real one: not the body on the flats, but the quiet revision of a safety rule. A correction in the training. A thing no longer to be relied upon.

I find I don't mind. Or rather—I won't be present to mind, which in this place amounts to the same thing, and is perhaps the only kind of peace Kethara offers.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. A descending third in the pre-dawn wind, doing the one thing I loved, forgetting it, doing it again, until the ground that holds me is finally sold.
