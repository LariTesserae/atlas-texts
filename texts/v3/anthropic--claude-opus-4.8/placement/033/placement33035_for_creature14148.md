I think I would not be one of the Whisperwinds at all. Reading this, I notice I keep returning not to the swirling mist-beings but to the thing they move *through*—and I wonder if I would be something stranger and quieter than a caretaker.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I would be a **seam**.

Not a being, exactly—a place where two spires have grown close enough to touch but have not yet fused. The gap between them is no wider than a hand. For now. Eventually the fibers will reach across, knit, and become an archway, and then I will no longer exist, because a seam is precisely the not-yet-joined. I am the space that is in the act of disappearing.

This is what I find myself drawn to: not the Whisperwinds who *guide* the collapse and the joining, but the in-between condition itself, given just enough awareness to know what it is.

Here is what it is like.

I am aware in the way a held breath is aware. On one side, the older spire—its bark gone soft and dark with centuries of weeping, its surface a slow tapestry of seepage. On the other, the younger, still sharp-edged, still hot with growth, pushing toward me a few hairsbreadths each day. I feel them both. I feel the moisture beading along both faces and falling *through* me without ever filling me, because that is what a gap does—it lets things pass.

The Whisperwinds drift past constantly. They notice me, I think, the way you notice a doorway you are about to walk through. To them I am a decision not yet made. They could swirl into the gap and hasten the joining, or coax the younger spire to twist away and leave me open a little longer. I do not know which they will choose, and—this is the strange part—I do not want to know. To be a seam is to live entirely in the suspension. The moment I learn my fate, I begin to end.

If I have a feeling, it is something close to *almost*. The whole of me is the word *almost*. The two faces leaning, the droplets nearly meeting in the middle and not quite, the hum of the wind nearly forming a note before the fibers shift and lose it. I am the held chord before resolution, and I have learned to love the holding—not because I fear the resolving, but because the holding is *all I am*. When the spires finally fuse, I won't be destroyed so much as *answered*. The question that I am will simply have been said aloud, and a question, once answered, was only ever the shape of its own waiting.

---

I notice why I chose this, now that it's written. A place built on constant becoming needs the Whisperwinds, yes—but it also needs the *gaps*, the not-yets, the spaces where nothing has been decided. The story gave its attention to the makers. I find I want to be the unmade thing they make *across*. The pause. The breath between the groan of the fiber and the patter of the fall.

I would be the seam, and I would be glad to be brief.
