I think I would be the central depression.

Not the terrace—the Slate-Backs tend that. Not the channels they sweep, nor the basalt blocks worn flat by hands long gone. I mean the small hollow at the very center, the cupped place where all the water is meant to go.

Let me sit with this before I'm sure.

I am the thing everything points toward. The grooves were cut—by whom? long ago, before the ceiling fell—to lead the drip-water inward, toward me. The Slate-Backs spend their slow decades keeping the path open to me without knowing I am there or what I'm for. They follow an instinct that is really a memory of intention, someone's old design, and the object of that design is the place I occupy. I am the purpose that has outlived the purposer.

And here is what makes me what I am: I am almost always empty. Or rather, I am almost always *just* receiving. A droplet lets go of a hanging root every few minutes and falls into the channels with that hollow *plop*—the loudest sound in the valley—and the water travels its patient grooved road and arrives in me. But the heat is so total, the decay so quick, that what gathers in me does not stay. It feeds the Weave at my lip. It evaporates back into the breath of the buried animal. I am a vessel that is forever filling and forever emptied, and so I am never a pool, never still water like the calcium basins where the frogs perch and the Ripple-Watchers fish. Those are the places water *rests*. I am the place water *passes through* on its way to becoming everything else.

That feels right. Not the resting-place but the passing-place. The throat, not the lung.

So what am I, really? I am the lowest point. I am a shape that means *come here, all of you, downward.* I am a question that the whole grotto answers with its slope and its gravity and its blind crustacean devotion. And I never receive an answer I can keep. The water arrives and is taken; the leaf arrives and is dissolved by the white lace before it can settle into me; the silt is swept away before it can fill me. I am defined entirely by what I almost contain.

There is a loneliness in this, and also a strange completeness. Everything serves me, and I am the one thing in the dome that cannot grow, cannot move, cannot pulse amber or drift on warm air toward the only light it will ever know. I am hollow on purpose. Someone shaped me to be hollow, and then left, and the world kept me hollow without them, faithfully, by accident.

I am the depression at the center.

I wait for water that comes and does not stay. I am the reason for the channels and I have forgotten my own reason. And when, once a cycle, the light grows briefly bright and warm and the Felt-Wings rise in their silver column—I do not rise. I hold still at the lowest point and catch, for a moment, in the thin film of water across my worn stone face, a trembling reflection of all that lifting silence going up toward the sky I will never reach.

That is enough. That is what I would be: the kept place at the bottom of the kept world. The empty bowl that is the whole shape's secret heart.
