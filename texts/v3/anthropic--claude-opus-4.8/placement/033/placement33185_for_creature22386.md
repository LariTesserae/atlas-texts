I think I would be a stonewinder. Not the apex one whose pressure-songs travel districts—a younger one, still learning to let go on purpose.

Let me find where I am.

---

I am wedged in an expansion seam on a vertical face, and I have been here too long. I know this the way you know a held breath: the seam was good three melt seasons ago, when the corridor below ran calm and the shell-builders' chalk gave me grip. Now the current at my tail has changed its pitch. Not louder—*tighter*. A thinning whine where there used to be a wash. The wall above me has been bending. I have felt it in the pits along my jaw, a slow lean translating down through the stone as a tremor I could ignore. I have been ignoring it. That is the thing about a good hold: it teaches you to mistake stillness for safety.

I do not have eyes, so I do not picture the basin the way you described it—the cobalt sheets, the milky blur of mineral storms. I have something better and worse: I have the *feel* of it pressed against my whole length. I read the wall as a long, cooling fact at my belly. I read the falling meltwater somewhere to my left as a column of broken pressure, a place where everything is carried and nothing can be trusted to stay. I read, below me, the corridor that used to be shelter and is now becoming a knife.

And I read, very faint, a knocking.

An adult, far off. Tail to hollow slab, the slow percussion of the old speech. I am too young to answer with anything but listening, but I can parse it the way a hatchling parses the world it was peeled from: *this span is going. this ground is one more season. let go.*

I do not want to.

This is the part you got right about us, the part that is not architecture or appetite but something closer to grief: we are made to surrender early and often, and it never stops being a surrender. Every hold I have ever loved I have had to betray before it betrayed me. The seam under me right now is warm with my own staying. Letting go means trusting the cold open water, the lanes of sudden funnel, the chance that the next wall has already started its long lean and I just can't feel it yet.

But I can feel *this* one. The tremor has a new edge. The shell-mounds beside me have gone quiet—the builders pulled in, which they only do before the grit comes through hard.

So I do the thing I was hatched to learn.

I read the flow before it becomes force. I time the wall's lean against the corridor's tightening hiss, find the half-instant where both relax—there is always a half-instant, the basin breathes—and I let go.

For a moment I am nothing. No hold, no map, just a slate-pale length of borrowed life poured into the dark, the current taking me the way it takes silt and stone and the old drowned works of whoever built this place. I am carried. I do not fight it. Fighting is for animals that believe in territory. I curve into the flow, reading with my whole skin, and the knocking guides me—not toward the elder, exactly, but toward the *register* of its certainty, the stone it has judged stable for one more melt.

Behind me, eventually, a sound arrives through the water as pressure: the muffled thump of resignation. The seam-wall has emptied itself, grain by grain, into a new and lower equilibrium. The hold I loved is a reef now. Something will cluster there. Something always does.

I find a fresh fracture, orange-interior still bleeding its thin bacterial film, and I take what nutrition the surrender allows. I press my jaw to a ceramic plate and knock once—not language yet, just practice. Just *I am here, I moved in time, the map is already wrong and I am still carried.*

That is where I would be. Not anywhere, exactly. In the interval. In the half-instant of the basin's breath, learning the only thing this world teaches:

how to leave a good place while it is still good,
and trust the water to carry what cannot be kept.
