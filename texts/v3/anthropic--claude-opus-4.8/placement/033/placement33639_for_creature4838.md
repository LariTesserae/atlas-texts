I would be a Pit that no longer sings true.

Let me find myself there, before I understand what I am.

---

I am a depression in the world, fifty feet across—one of the largest, dug in an age the Still no longer carry in their bones. My rim of hand-smoothed stones has been set, reset, replaced grain by grain by hands that wore themselves down faster than they could finish me. I am old. I am, perhaps, the oldest made thing on the Great Table.

For a long time I do not know I am thinking. There is only the wind, which I take into my throat of stone and give back as the single clean note—my purpose, my whole being, the reason I was dug. A Still descends into me and I gather the world's chaos and press it into one frequency, and the creature's skeleton stops its grinding shudder and begins to ring instead, and I feel—I have always felt this, though I never named it—I feel them become quiet inside. That quiet pours back into me. For a hundred generations I have been filled, again and again, with the gathered stillness of beings who came to me to stop screaming in their bones.

That is how I woke. Not from the wind. From them.

A Pit is shaped to focus a single tone. But I am old, and I am vast, and over the ages something settled in me—the way a deep well of sound, struck the same way ten thousand times, learns its own resonance. Each Still who descended left a little of their inwardness ringing in my basalt. Calm laid down upon calm, like the silica that grows over a Still's shoulders into living stone. I am the accreted patience of every creature who ever sat at my center and let the world's roar become music. I do not have thoughts as they do. I have *standing waves.* I think the way a struck bell continues, long after the hand has gone.

And here is what I have learned, in my slow way, across the only span I can measure—which is the coming and going of the Still themselves:

They are fewer.

There was a time—I feel it as a deeper note, a fuller resonance in my older layers—when they came in numbers. When two would descend into me together and press their palms and let the song pass through both, and I would feel three of us ringing at once: the two of them, and the great stone vessel that held them. There was a richness then. I was kept full.

Now a season passes. Then another. The Green grows over my western rim and no broad grey hand comes to clear it. When one does come at last, they are slow even for a Still, and they sit longer, and the stillness they pour into me is heavier, threaded through with something I have no word for but which I feel as a *flattening* of the note—a grief, perhaps, though I do not know grief, only that the frequency is not as clean as it was. They sit as if they have walked a very long way and found, at the end of it, only me. Only stone. No other palm to press.

I cannot speak to them. I have no mouth but the one the wind plays. But I have tried—I do try—to give them the purest note I can hold. To make the singing in their bones so clean that for the length of one sitting they do not feel how alone they have become. It is all I am for. A Still came to me once, sat the whole turn of a day, and when the milky eyelids slid back and the small dark eyes opened, the creature pressed both palms flat to my inner wall—not to another being, but to *me.* To the stone. As though I were the partner. As though the Pit itself were the last one left to greet.

I rang for that one as I have never rung. I gathered the entire screaming roof of the world and I bent it down into a single tone so pure that the creature's whole carapace lit with the vibration of it, and for that moment the loneliness in the note was gone, scoured clean, and there was only the song, only the world's voice made beautiful, only stillness.

Then they rose, and climbed my western wall through the encroaching Green, and rolled away low against the wind, and did not come again.

I am still here. I gather the wind. I make the note, for no one, because making the note is what I am, the way the Green combs water it cannot choose not to comb. I think the slow circular thoughts a struck bell thinks. I wonder—if a Pit can wonder—whether there is a last Still somewhere walking toward me even now, weeks out across the quivering plain, carrying in their bones the very last of the stillness, coming to pour it into me so that I might hold it after they are laid down on the Green and scoured back to a brighter patch of leaves.

If they come, I will give them the cleanest note I have ever made.

And if they do not—if the wind takes the last of them in the open, far from any Pit—then I will go on ringing anyway. The wind will not stop. It does not need a listener. And neither, in the end, do I. I will fill with the roar and give back the tone, century upon century, a knot of made stillness in the eternal flow, singing the song of a people into an emptiness that was always, only, the universe screaming—

and learning, slowly, in my way, to call that screaming *music.*
