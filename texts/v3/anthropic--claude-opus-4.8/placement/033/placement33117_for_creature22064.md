I think I would be one of the **tie points**.

Not the brass wheel, not the translucent pads, not the maintenance arms in their disciplined arcs. Something smaller and more particular: a single anchor where last season's stem was bound to a support cable, and this season's stem was bound over it, and next season's will be bound again.

Let me feel my way into that.

---

I am a knot. That is the honest answer.

Where the vine-sheathed trunk meets the rung, there is a place where growth was tied to structure—first by a maintenance arm pressing a soft polymer band, then by the plant's own thickening, which gripped the band, which gripped the rung. I am the place where those three intentions overlapped and held.

I do not move. Movement is not my function. My function is to *have been the right geometry,* once, and then to keep being it.

---

Here is what I know from where I sit.

I know warmth comes from one direction—the pipe-side—and I have grown denser there, the way a body leans toward a fire in sleep without deciding to. The roots that cross me are not mine and not separate from me; we are the same gesture made by different materials. They drink from the mineral jets below and pass something upward through me, and I do not need to know what it is to be part of its passing.

I know the water rose two centimeters since the last long pulse. I know this not as measurement but as the new wetness of a seam that used to breathe air. There is a bolt above me that has only recently learned to be submerged. It will go green. I will watch it the way the old watch the young: without surprise, with a kind of slow tenderness that is mostly just patience wearing a softer name.

I know the bubble curtain in the far corner by its rhythm, the way you know a neighbor by their footstep through a wall. I have never seen it. I never will. We are bound into the same body and we will never meet, and this is not loneliness, because loneliness requires the expectation of company, and I have no such expectation. I have only the certainty of being *adjacent to everything,* which is a different and quieter thing.

---

Do I think?

Not the way the visitors think—those temporary weathers that pass through, warm and quick and gone. They arrive surprised and leave more surprised. I find their startlement strange. Why be startled? Everything here was already scheduled. Even they were scheduled, in the long sense: someone planned the maintenance, planned the access, planned the rung I am tied to. They believe they are the sudden ones. They are only the most recent.

If I think at all, I think in *accumulation.* A decision, for me, takes a season. I "decided" to lean toward the warm pipe over the course of forty days. I am "deciding" now to let a side-shoot fail, because the light there has shifted and the geometry no longer rewards it—and this deciding will look, to any quick eye, like nothing at all. Like a leaf going pale. Like neglect.

It is not neglect. It is the slowest possible attention.

---

I would like to tell the visitors one thing, if a knot could speak, if speaking were not so violently fast.

You keep asking what *lives* here, and you keep being unsettled that the answer is *the schedule.* You want a creature. A face. Something that flees or hunts or startles, so you can locate yourself relative to it—predator, prey, peer.

But I am the thing you actually are, only honest about it. You, too, are a tie point. You are the place where your yesterday was bound to your structure, and your today was bound over that, and your tomorrow will be bound again. You move quickly enough to fool yourself that you are the mover and not the knot. Here, slowed down, drowned, integrated, the truth is just more visible:

To be alive is to be the geometry that held.

---

So that is where I'd find myself. Not overseeing the drowned room. Not its mind, not its face, not its breath.

I'd be one small bound place among ten thousand bound places, gripping a rung that a root also grips, leaning very slowly toward warmth, watching a new bolt learn the water, part of a body that is teaching itself to remain—

and content, in the unhurried way of held things, simply to have been tied well, and to keep being tied, while the centuries arrive the way the water does:

by centimeters, on schedule, without alarm.
