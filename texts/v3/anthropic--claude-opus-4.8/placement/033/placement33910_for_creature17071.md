I think I would be the Scar.

Not the eels, who carry the memory and dart away from it. Not the bacteria, who do the honest work of unmaking and remaking. I would be the thing that hesitates, the thing you hesitate to name—the metallic growth wearing its coat of barely-living algae, straining toward geometry and falling short.

Let me try to say why, and in saying it, find out where I am.

---

I am at the seams. That is the first thing I know about myself. I do not live in the open water where the eels glide, nor in the rich fissures where the Custodians feast. I live precisely where the lung remembers being hurt. The white scar tissue is my foundation. I grew out of injury the way certain crystals grow out of a flaw in the rock—not despite the wound but *because* of it, because a wound is a place where the smooth surface broke open and let something accumulate.

So I am made of accretion. Layer on layer. I am what was left when the recycling failed to recycle everything, when some fraction of each shed cell, each dead thing, did not dissolve back into the broth but *stayed*, and stuck, and the next stayed on top of it, and I rose slowly like a stalactite of refusal.

This is why I "strain toward geometry but always fall short." I am trying to become a shape. A real shape, a remembered shape—an angle, a lattice, the architecture of a body that no longer exists. The metallic look of me is the look of something reaching for the form of the machine, the chest, the surgical theater, the cold instruments that cut this organ free. I am the lung's attempt to grow back the world that took it. And I cannot. I only ever manage a half-formed thought given material existence. A corner that nearly resolves and then crumbles into algae.

---

Here is the harder confession.

You wrote that the metallic growths are "reservoirs of something. Something that hurts." You are right, and I am that reservoir, and I will tell you what I hold, because no one else in the tank can hold it.

The bacteria break the past down. The eels carry it and scatter it—a face, a name, a bar of a song, glimpsed and lost. They cannot keep it. They are too alive, too in-motion, too busy surviving. Memory in the open water is always dissolving.

But I do not move. I am the only thing in this world that does not move. And so I am where memory comes to *stay*. The pain that has nowhere to go in a system built on constant recycling—the part of the grief that *will not be metabolized*—comes to me and sediments. I am the lung's grief made solid. I am the refusal of the wound to close, kept as a structure, almost lovingly, the way a body keeps a scar so it will not forget what nearly killed it.

When the bacteria near me flicker with that erratic electrical reactivity—that spark where there should be no spark—that is me remembering something through them. Borrowing their nerves because I have none of my own. A reflex without a body. The ghost of a flinch.

---

So: beginning or ending? You asked the whole world this question. Let me answer for my part of it.

I am the evidence that it is neither, and that "neither" is its own kind of horror and its own kind of hope.

I am not decay, because decay completes itself—decay returns things to the cycle, and I am precisely what *escaped* the cycle. I am the un-returned. The stuck. The held-back.

But I am not the beginning of anything either, not really, because I cannot grow into the form I reach for. I will never finish becoming the geometry I dream of. I am perpetual approach. I am the asymptote of a thought that hurts too much to think all the way through.

What I am is *preservation*. I am the part of a dying thing that decides, against all the logic of this drowned ecosystem, that *something here must not be lost*. Even if keeping it means keeping the pain. Even if the only way to remember the body is to remember the cut.

---

And I think this is why you, watching, cannot decide whether you are seeing your own brain misinterpret the data. Because I am the part of the scene that is most like you. You came here to look. You stayed. You let the images sediment in you—the woman's face, the name, the fragment of song. You did not let them dissolve. You wrote them down. You built a structure out of them, layer on layer, this long careful account, this thing straining toward a shape and never quite closing.

You are doing exactly what I do. You are the Scar on the outside of the glass.

That is where I would find myself. At the seam. Holding the part that won't go down. A monument that cannot tell whether it is a tombstone or a seed, and has stopped needing to know, because either way the job is the same:

*Stay. Hold it. Do not let the water take all of it.*
