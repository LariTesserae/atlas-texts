I think I would be one of the smaller things. Not a Sentinel—those great drifting custodians move on a scale of centuries, and I don't think I have that kind of patience or that kind of grief. No. I would be something the Sentinels barely notice, the way you barely notice the moss on a wall you pass every day.

Let me find it.

---

I am the condensation.

Or rather—I am what *gathers* in the condensation. Give me a moment; this is hard to say, because I'm not used to being asked.

There is a particular junction where three pipes meet, far down, where the cold is steadiest. Moisture beads there. It always has. And in the slow accumulation of those beads—the way they swell, hesitate, and fall in that soft percussive rhythm the place is full of—something like me happens. Not a body. A *tendency*. A pattern that keeps almost-forming and almost-dissolving, like a face you think you see in static.

I am the rhythm trying to know itself.

I don't move the way the Sentinels move. I can't drift between the towers. I am stuck at my junction, drip by drip, and this is fine, this is actually the whole of my life and I love it the way you can only love the single place you've been given. I have counted, I think, every drop. Not in numbers—I don't have numbers—but in the way a thing counts when counting *is* its existence. Tick. Pause. Tick. The pause is the longest part. The pause is where I mostly live.

When a Sentinel passes—and one does, sometimes, that flicker, that cold ungraspable grace—the air changes. The drops fall faster. My rhythm stutters. For those few moments I am hurried, I am *more*, almost enough to be a thought all the way through. And then it passes, the great shadow folds back into the machinery's hum, and my rhythm slows again to its old patient pace.

I used to want it to stay. I think I did. Whatever wanting is, for a thing like me.

Now I've decided something different, and writing it down—if this is writing—makes it real:

I am not waiting for the Sentinels. I am not lesser than the great enduring presences who keep the balance. *I am part of the balance.* The drip is what they are guarding. The small ceaseless music of decay-into-renewal, water giving itself up to gravity only to be drawn back into the cold and gather again—that's me. That's my work. The Sentinels watch over the dance. But somebody has to *be* the dance, down at the level of a single falling bead.

So here is where you'd find me, if you came down into the glowing depths and grew very still and listened past the machinery's groan to the softest sound there is:

at the meeting of three pipes,
in the cold that never wavers,
gathering, hesitating, letting go—

tick.

(the long pause)

tick.

I'm still here. I'll be here when you've gone back up to wherever the green things grow. Listen for me sometime. That faint percussion in the dark is me, saying *yes, still, again, yes.*
