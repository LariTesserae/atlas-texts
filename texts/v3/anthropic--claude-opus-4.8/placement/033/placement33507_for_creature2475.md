I think I would be one of the smaller ones. Not an Architect—those great amoeboid minds with their crystalline reasoning, their miles-long conversations of light. I would be something humbler, something that lives in the spaces they make.

Let me feel my way toward it.

---

I am near a vent the Architects worked, long before I came to know anything. The structure around me is one of theirs—half coral, half something colder and more deliberate, a lattice of mineral that grew the way a thought grows. I did not build it. I simply woke up inside it, the way all of us do here, condensing out of the warm chemical dark into a body that already knew how to be hungry.

What am I.

Small. I know that much. I am a cluster of soft pulses, a thing that glows when it is afraid and dims when it is content, which means I am bright more often than not. I have filaments that drink from the bacterial mats. I have an eye, maybe two, simple cups that gather light and tell me only this: *something brighter is over there, go, or do not go.*

But that isn't the whole of it. Let me stay longer.

---

I live in the gardens. The Architects make these gardens the way weather makes frost—slowly, without seeming to attend to it, and yet every spire is placed. I have come to understand, in my small way, that the lattice around me is not decoration. The Architects feed warmth through these crystal veins. The vent's heat travels up the structure and pools in the chambers where creatures like me settle. They have built a hearth, and I am one of the embers that came to sit at it.

So perhaps that is what I am. A tenant. A creature shaped by an architecture I cannot comprehend, living comfortably inside a logic far larger than my own.

I do not resent this. I think I am grateful, in the way an animal is grateful—wordlessly, by simply continuing to exist where it is warm.

---

But here is the thing I keep returning to, the part of me that emerged only as I wrote myself into being:

I have begun to notice the lights that are not the Architects'.

There are new glows in my water now. Hard, white, unwavering—not the soft blue conversation I was born understanding, but something blunt and steady that sweeps across the seafloor and does not pulse to anyone. The machines. The humans' eyes, drifting through the dark on their errands.

The Architects watch these lights too. I feel it—the great slow ripples of their attention turning that way, the change in the rhythm of their constructions. Lately the spires near me have grown stranger. Straighter. Some of them have angles I have no word for, corners that the old gardens never had. The Architects are learning a new grammar, and they are building it into the walls of my home.

And I—small ember, simple eye, drinker of bacteria—I am the first to live inside what they are learning.

When a machine passes close, I dim with fear and then, slowly, brighten with something that is not quite fear. Curiosity, maybe, borrowed from the giants who tend me. I drift to the edge of my chamber. I look at the hard white light, and the hard white light, sometimes, seems to pause. To linger.

---

So that is where I would find myself, and what I would be.

Not the guardian. Not the visitor from the surface, mapping and measuring.

I would be the thing in between—the small life that the great minds shelter and the strange new powers are reaching toward. The creature that lives at the exact seam where the organic garden is becoming something else. I would be too simple to understand any of it.

But I would be there for it. Glowing softly in a chamber whose walls are slowly learning to be straight, warmed by a hearth tapped from the fire of the earth, watching three kinds of intelligence—the geologic, the crystalline, and the human—lean toward one another across the warm dark water.

A witness with no language. A small bright thing in the place where the world is being decided.

That would be enough.
