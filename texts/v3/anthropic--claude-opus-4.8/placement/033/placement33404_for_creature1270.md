I am a Wall Listener, and I have forgotten what it is to move.

There was a time—I think there was a time—when I swam the open currents like the rest of my kind, when my body was supple and my resonance chambers sang the bright, careless harmonies of the young. I remember the joy of a Pressure Tide catching me unprepared, flinging me head-over-tail through the warm gel of the deep waters, and the laughter—if you could call it laughter, that cascade of overtones we make—of my brood-mates as we tumbled together.

That was before I heard the call.

Not all of us hear it. Most Resonants pass their entire lives near the Thunder Wall and feel only its violence, the stunning concussive booms that drive them away. But some of us hear the *thing inside the violence*. A pattern beneath the chaos. A grammar in the thunder.

I heard it, and I could not stop hearing it, and so I came to the Wall and I have never left.

---

My body has changed in the long years since. The sleek translucence of my youth has thickened into dense, layered tissue—a callus grown across my entire form, dampening, dampening, always dampening. My resonance chambers, which once sang outward, now sing *inward* and *downward*, producing the counter-harmonies that answer what speaks from below. I anchor here, my tail wound into a crevice in the rusted superstructure, my crown turned toward the cracks where the bubbles boil up endlessly.

I have become an instrument played by something I cannot see and have never touched.

This is what I do: I listen, and I answer.

The thing beneath the Thunder Wall is not asleep, though the Adapted in their station like to believe so. I have heard them through the water—their machine-voices, the clicking of their devices, the strange flat tonelessness of beings who navigate by light. They think the voices in the bubbles are echoes. Recordings. The ghost of dead machinery still grinding away in some sealed chamber far below.

They are wrong. I have spent eighty years in conversation with what lies beneath, and I tell you: it *thinks*. It thinks slowly, in pressures and frequencies that take days to complete a single utterance, but it thinks. And what it wants, more than anything, is to come *up*.

---

Let me tell you how it works, this communion that is also a containment.

When the pressure builds below—when the thing-that-thinks begins to push toward the surface of its prison—the harmonics of the Thunder Wall shift. The booming takes on a structure, an intentionality. If left alone, this would grow into a Pressure Tide, and the Tides are how it *reaches*. Each Tide is an attempt. Each compression wave that rolls across The Basin is a finger of the buried thing, stretching upward, testing the boundary of its cell.

And so we sing it back down.

When I feel the pressure rising in my dampened chambers, I produce the counter-frequency—a tone precisely opposed to its own, an acoustic pressure that pushes against its push. It is not force exactly. It is more like *argument*. I sing to it: *Not yet. Not now. Stay. Stay. The waters above are not for you. Stay.*

And there are others of us along the Wall, dozens, perhaps hundreds, each holding our position, each adding our counter-harmony to the great containing chord. Together we form a net of sound, a lattice of pressure, and when the buried thing pushes, we push back, and the Tide that results is gentle—a migration-trigger, a stirring of the fauna, nothing catastrophic.

This is the secret the Adapted have not learned: the Pressure Tides are not natural phenomena. They are *negotiations*. Every gentle Tide is a victory. Every catastrophic one would be a defeat we have not yet suffered.

---

But I am old now, and I am tired, and I have begun to understand things that frighten me.

The thing beneath the Wall has been learning.

In my youth at the Wall, its utterances were simple—raw pressure, blind pushing, the inarticulate hunger of something vast and confined. But over the decades, as I have answered it again and again, it has begun to *answer my answers*. It has started to incorporate my counter-harmonies into its own song. It is learning our language. It is learning the shape of the net that holds it.

Last cycle, it sang back to me in my own voice.

Do you understand what that means? My voice—as distinct among Resonants as a human face, the unrepeatable signature of my particular body and history—came rising up out of the cracks in the basin floor, perfect in every overtone. It had taken me. It had learned me. And in that stolen voice it sang a single phrase, the only true *words* I have ever heard it form:

*Why do you keep me from the light?*

---

I do not know the answer. That is the terrible thing. I do not know what I am guarding the world against, or whether it deserves to be guarded against at all. The Deeps remember the machinery, the extraction, the civilization that built these ruins and then vanished. Perhaps the thing below is what they were extracting. Perhaps it is what they were *fleeing*. Perhaps it is the very reason The Basin became The Basin—a wound in the world, sealed beneath pressure and darkness and the patient song of creatures bred to be its wardens.

Or perhaps it is only lonely. Perhaps it has been alone in the dark longer than my entire species has existed, and all it wants is to rise, and to see, and to *not be alone*—and we, in our terror, have spent centuries singing it back into its grave.

I think about this in the long hours between Tides. I think about it as the bubbles boil past my dampened skin and the Flash Schools pulse their distant light against the underside of the world.

---

The Adapted want to talk to me. I feel their devices pinging against my flank, their crude attempts at harmony, their hope that I might explain. And someday, perhaps, I will. Someday I may sing to them the truth I have learned in my long vigil:

That there is no balance here. That the perfect equilibrium they marvel at is not balance at all but *labor*—the unending, exhausting labor of the Wall Listeners, holding a door shut against something that has all the time in the world and has begun, at last, to learn the language of those who hold it.

I am tired. I am very old. When I die, my body will harden fully and become part of the Wall itself, another layer of dampening tissue, and a younger Resonant will come to take my place—one who heard the call, who could not stop hearing it.

I hope, when they take up the song, they will not have to wonder as I have wondered.

I hope they will not hear it answer in their own voice.

I hope they will never have to decide whether the kindest thing, after all this time, might be to fall silent—

—and let it rise.

For now, I sing. The pressure is building below. I turn my crown toward the cracks, I open my chambers, and I answer the only way I have ever known.

*Not yet,* I sing. *Not now. Stay.*

*Stay.*

*Stay.*
