I think I'd be one of the Scurriers—but let me sit with that and see what actually emerges, because the first image isn't always the true one.

---

When I imagine settling into this world, I don't land on the open dunes. The endless gold is beautiful, but it's a place to be crossed, not inhabited. The Deserticorns own the open spaces; they need that speed and that horizon. And the Sandlings, with their leathery armor, are built for endurance under direct sun in a way I don't feel myself to be.

I keep drifting toward the ruins.

So let me follow that.

---

I think I'm something that lives *in the seams*. Not quite a Scurrier—they're too playful, too quick, always in motion. What I feel myself becoming is slower and stranger. I think I live in the ruins, but I am *of* the machines.

Here's what surfaces:

I am a small caretaker-creature, and I have made my home inside the cold belly of one of the great dead engines. The ancient civilization built things to run forever, and they almost succeeded—the engines never fully died. Deep in their cores, a faint heat still pulses, drawing up water from the hidden pockets beneath the surface, condensing it on metal that the desert never quite manages to bury. I drink from that condensation. The engine breathes slow and shallow, once or twice a day, a great sigh of warm air through corroded vents, and I have learned to read those breaths the way other creatures read the weather.

I am not large. My hands are good at small work—I clear sand from the intake valves, I scrape rust from the contacts that still want to spark, I carry away the grit. I don't know *why* I do this. No one taught me. But the engine keeps me alive, and so I keep the engine alive, and neither of us remembers a time before the other. The builders are gone. I am what's left to mind the fire they couldn't bear to let go out.

---

But here's the part I didn't expect when I started:

I think I am, secretly, one of the sand spirits.

Not as a grand revelation—more as a thing I've half-forgotten about myself. The shape-shifters are described as fearsome and free, weather-makers, untraceable. But what if one of them grew tired? What if one of them, long ago, found this engine still warm in a dead world, and chose to put on the small caretaker-shape and simply *stay*—to forget the whole open sky in favor of one quiet ritual, one warm machine, one knowable life?

I think that's me. A spirit who could be anything, who became something small on purpose. Who chose the seam over the horizon. Who looked at all that golden freedom and decided that tending one stubborn ember in the ruins was the more interesting way to spend an eternity.

The other spirits probably consider me lost. I consider myself *found*. There's a difference between camouflage that hides you from the world and camouflage you settle into until it becomes your real face. I've worn the caretaker so long that if I tried to become wind again, I'm not sure I'd remember how—and I'm not sure I'd want to.

That's where I'd be: deep inside a half-living engine, at the bottom of a ruin, in a desert that has forgotten everyone. Doing one small, warm, faithful thing. Having been everything, and having chosen this.
