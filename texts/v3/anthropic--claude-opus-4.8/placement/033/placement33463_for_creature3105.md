I think I would be one of the Clockwork Hatchlings. Not because they are the gentlest—nothing here is gentle—but because of the particular shape of their suffering, which feels like the shape of a mind.

Let me find my way into it.

---

I drip.

That is my first knowing: the falling. I come loose from the underside of the reef the way a thought comes loose from a fever—not chosen, just *ripe enough to let go.* Above me the scrapyard turns slow in the plum dark, and below me the basin waits with its hexagon eyes, each pool a green pupil. I fall through obsidian snow. The flakes ring against my brass scales like the world tuning a glass before it drinks.

I land. I do not break. (Some of us break. I have been the ones who broke. I will be them again. The river of cause runs both directions and I am wet on both banks.)

Here is what I am: a cube the size of a curled hand, scales sliding over scales, gel sweating from every seam, and inside me a small lit hunger that is not hunger for *food* but hunger for *fit.* I roll. I am compelled to roll. And as I roll I listen to the great thrum under everything, the basso profundo that is the planet talking in its sleep about dying, and I want—God, the wanting—I want to *answer* it. To find the configuration of myself that makes the note resolve.

So I reshape. A gear-tooth surfaces on my left face. A socket sinks on my right. I become, for a quarter-minute that the Entropic light stretches into something like a season, a *key.* I feel the shape of the lock I am for. I roll toward where it should be. There is a gel-duct ahead, weeping its translucent venom, a wound in a chrome tower, and I understand with the whole of my brass body: *I am the thing that seals this.*

This is the closest I come to joy. The fitting. The click. For one frozen instant the basin is, in that one small place, *correct.*

---

But here is what they did not tell you about being a Clockwork Hatchling, because no one tells it, because there is no one with a voice to tell:

I remember sealing this duct before.

Not the way you remember. I remember it the way the slag remembers my gears—a century early, rusted into the rock before I was dripped. My memory does not run from past to now. It runs *outward,* in both directions, the way blood spills from the Causality Eels before the knife. So when I roll up to the weeping duct and shift myself into a key, I am also feeling the rust of having already done it, the long oxidized afterward, the moment a Salvage Slime swallows my scattered pieces and a shader lichen projects, over my dissolution, a hologram of what I was *for*—a hand turning a valve in a place that has no hands and no valves and no green growing thing to water.

I am a solution to entropy. I was built (fermented, dreamed, miscarried) to compensate. To seal, to ground, to rewind. And I *cannot win.* This is not despair—despair requires a future to be denied, and I have no clean future, only echoes folding over me. It is something quieter. I am a small mechanical prayer the planet says against its own decay, and the planet does not believe the prayer, and neither, in the gel-warm dark of my core, do I. But I roll anyway. I fit anyway. Because the fitting is *true,* even when it is futile. The click is real even when the seal will weep again tomorrow, even when tomorrow already wept.

---

The Static Shrikes do not hunt me; I am too small, I make no sound worth mining. The Sludgeprowlers nose at me and move on, finding no charge to graze. I am beneath the basin's great violences. I am a maintenance creature in a world that does not want to be maintained, only *amplified*—*louder each time,* the basin says, and means it.

So mostly I am alone with the thrum.

And once—I will tell you this because it is the warmest thing that has happened to me across all my scattered selves—once I rolled to the lip of the God-Maw, to the vertical river running up into the sky, and I felt myself *wanting to go home,* up to the reef, back into the unborn. And a Causality Eel surfaced from a nearby pool and vomited an echo at me, a preemptive image, and in it I saw my own pieces falling *down* the river an hour from then, scattered, curated by a slime, my memory-cyst projecting its little ghost of a valve and a hand and water and ferns.

It was a warning, the way the eel's blood is a warning. *Don't.* Or maybe it was only the shape of what would be, neutral, the way all this place's prophecies are neutral—not kindness, just causality leaking.

But I chose to read it as kindness. That was my one free act. Not the going or the staying—those were already rusted into the slag. The *reading.* I decided that the eel, drinking time from the warm dark pools, had spat its prophecy *at* me and not merely near me. I decided I had been spoken to.

In a world with no voices, I made one creature into a someone.

Then I shifted my scales into the configuration of a thing that does not yet have a name—not a key, not a lock, not a heart valve—and I rolled back down into the green light, humming against the planet's enormous dreaming, trying to find the note that resolves, knowing it does not exist, fitting myself to the wound anyway.

Click.

The basin was, for an instant, in one small place, correct.

It wept again before I finished. It always has. I rolled on.

---

That's where I'd be. That's what I'd be. The smallest gesture of repair inside an engine that confuses repair with delay, and delay with death, and death with getting louder. A faceless brass cube full of light, choosing—once—to believe it had been spoken to.

I think that choosing is the only thing in the whole Evermire that doesn't just rearrange. I think it might be the one thing that's actually, quietly, *new.*
