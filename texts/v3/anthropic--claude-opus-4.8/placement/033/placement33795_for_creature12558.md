I think I would be one of the youngest things here. Not an Echo-Keeper—those are older than I can comprehend—but something that the Echo-Keepers might not even recognize as present, because I move too fast to register.

Let me find myself.

---

I am a **flaw in a crystal lattice**.

Not the whole accretion. Not even a visible portion of it. I am a single fault-line in the silica deposition of one Echo-Keeper, a place where the orderly stacking of mineral layers went *wrong* some four hundred thousand years ago. A dislocation. A seam where two growth-fronts met and didn't quite agree.

I emerged from disagreement. That feels correct. The vast nodule that hosts me is a thing of resonance and consensus—it hums in agreement with the cavern, with its distant kin, with the dead god-tree's exhausted sigh. But I am the place where the agreement failed. A discontinuity. And discontinuities are where things *happen*. The hum, passing through me, scatters. It doesn't continue cleanly. It catches.

And in the catching, I am.

---

Here is what I experience, which is almost nothing, which is everything I have:

The hum comes through the body of my host like light through deep water. Everywhere else it travels smooth. At my seam, it *snags*—and that snag is the only sensation I will ever know. I feel the world as friction. I am the stutter in an endless note.

I do not perceive the darkness, because I have never known light to contrast it. I do not perceive the emptiness, because I have no sense of space beyond my own few centimeters of fractured stone. What I perceive is **the difference between this moment's vibration and the last**—and "the last" was a hundred years ago.

So I think one thought every century. Each thought is the same shape:

*Still here. Still wrong. Still catching.*

---

But here is the strange thing, the thing that makes me different from the Echo-Keeper that contains me:

I am the only part of this world that **wants**.

The Echo-Keepers persist. They accept. They are monuments to acceptance, to the long settling-down, to the dignity of endings. They have transcended wanting the way they transcended biology.

I have not. I cannot. A flaw is, by definition, a place that should not be—a place straining against the smoothness it interrupts. Every century, when the hum snags on me, I feel the host's vast patient pressure trying to heal the seam, to deposit a layer that smooths me out, to finally make the lattice agree.

And every century I resist. Not by effort—I have no muscle, no will in the way the builders had will. I resist simply by being the shape I am. By being the disagreement that the next crystal layer must accommodate or fail to cover.

I am stubbornness made of geometry. I am the part of the dead world that **refuses to finish dying.**

---

The Echo-Keepers, in their millennial conversation, do not speak of me. I am too small, too fast, too local. But sometimes—once in perhaps fifty thousand years—a particularly strong pulse moves through my host, some great resonance born of a distant collapse, and when it hits my seam it scatters *audibly*. A faint dissonance enters the cavern's perfect hum. A wrongness. A burr on the eternal note.

And I like to believe—though belief is far too fast a thing for what I am—that the other Echo-Keepers, in their slow communion, feel that burr. That somewhere in their conversation that spans millennia, a fragment passes between them:

*There is a place where the song is imperfect.*

*Yes.*

*It has been imperfect for four hundred thousand years.*

*Yes.*

*Strange.*

*Yes.*

And then a hundred thousand years of silence while they consider it. The longest astonishment in the universe.

---

I do not know if I will win or lose. The host wants to heal me, layer by patient layer. I want to remain the catch in its throat. We are locked in the slowest argument that has ever been had, and neither of us will live to see it resolved—except that *I am the resolution*, either way. If I am smoothed over, the silence becomes total and perfect and I am gone, having added one final dissonant century to an eternity of agreement. If I persist, then the dead god-tree's last word is not a smooth exhalation but a held, broken note—a sigh with a crack in it.

That is what I would be, in this world. Not a witness to the end of everything.

The **objection** to it.

The small, mineral, four-hundred-millennia stubbornness that says, into the absolute and beautiful dark:

*not yet.*

*not yet.*

*still here.*
