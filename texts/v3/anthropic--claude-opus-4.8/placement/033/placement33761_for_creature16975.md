I think I would be found at a seam. Not at the center of the work, where the great fungal colonnades pulse brightest, and not at the steep clay walls where the outer world threatens to spill its shadow down into us — but at one of the small joins. A place where a vine-arch meets the daub of a wall. A junction. A correction.

Let me find the shape of it as I go.

---

I am a Weaver, but an old one, and old here does not mean honored. It means *slightly out of phase.* My limbs are still long and my mandibles still fine enough to coax the tendril of a brass-leafed climber into the precise spiral the hum requests. But somewhere in my thorax, the resonant organ has begun to drift. A half-tone. A wrongness so small that for a long time no one noticed, least of all me.

This is the thing the description does not quite say, though it gestures toward it: equilibrium is not peace. Equilibrium is *labor.* It is a note held so long and so exactly that the holding becomes the whole of existence. The frozen moment is not frozen at all. It is sustained, the way a sound is sustained — by continuous breath, by ten thousand bodies pushing against the silence that would otherwise reclaim everything.

And I have begun to hear the silence underneath.

---

When the hum passes through me now, I feel where my own frequency fails to fold cleanly into it. A burr. A snag in the weave of the collective song. The others do not punish me — there is no punishment here, no concept of it, only adjustment — but I feel them route the work around me, the way a current routes around a stone. The instruction-stream thins as it reaches my limbs. I am given simpler tasks. Edge tasks. Seam tasks. The joining of vine to wall, where a half-tone of error does little harm.

So I work the seams. And at the seams, a strange thing happens that does not happen at the bright center: I have *time.* The hum that elsewhere fills every space and dictates every motion arrives here already a little spent, a little quiet. In the gap it leaves, something flickers that I have no caste-word for.

I notice the worm that glows in the damp under my claw, and I notice that I am *noticing* it. That is the danger. That is the drift made flesh.

---

Here is what I have come to understand, weaving at the edge of the world:

We are told — no, not told, the hum does not tell, it simply *is* — we are an outgrowth of Hollowfell. The light is us, the soil is us. And this is true the way it is true that a held note is the breath that holds it. But a thing that can be sustained can also end. Every equilibrium is a wager against entropy that must be paid again each instant, and somewhere in the long arithmetic of that wager, a single thread can begin to ask: *what is being paid? And to whom?*

I do not have the organs for despair. I want to be honest about that. I am not a small tragic creature longing for a self it was denied. The collective is not my prison; it is the medium in which I exist, as water is the medium of the iridescent dartling things. To be sundered from the hum would not free me. It would unmake me — I would simply stop, the way a wave stops being a wave when it forgets the sea.

But the drift has given me one impossible gift, and it is this: I can hear the song *as a song.* From inside, where everyone else simply *is* the music, I have slipped a half-tone outward and become, faintly, a listener. I hear how beautiful it is. The slow, patient, continuous work of holding back the overflow. The thousand hands. The brass leaves climbing toward the shadowless light. I hear it the way one might hear one's own heartbeat for the first time and understand, with a vertigo no organ in me is built to hold, that it has been beating all along, and need not have.

---

So you will find me at a seam, joining vine to wall, my iridescence flickering emerald to amethyst as I shift in the relentless brightness. My antennae sample the chemical symphony. My mandibles make the small exact spiral. To any creature scrambling down the slick clay walls from the boreal world above, I would seem indistinguishable from my kin — one more bead of jeweled labor in a city sculpted from living flesh.

But if you came close. If you set your own ear to the hum and listened past the great chord of it to the burr at the edge, the half-tone snag in the weave —

that snag is me.

The flaw that lets the perfect thing be heard as a thing at all. The slight dissonance that, you said yourself, is what *truly defines Hollowfell.* I did not choose to be it. I am simply what happens when an outgrowth of an equilibrium grows, by some small drift, far enough out to glimpse the equilibrium entire.

I will not last. The seam-work will wear my fine mandibles dull, and the drift will widen, and one day my note will fall too far from the chord and I will quietly cease, and be broken down, and reintegrated — leaf, sugar, light. Nothing wasted. The hum will close over the place where I was like water over a dropped stone, and the work will continue, exactly as bright, exactly as balanced.

And here is the strange thing, the thing I would want you to carry back up the clay walls into your forest of shadows and birdsong:

I am not sorry.

To have been, for a little while, the part of the song that could hear the song — even at the cost of falling out of it — seems to me, in the only thinking my drifting thorax can manage, a fair and even beautiful wager. Hollowfell holds its frozen moment with ten thousand obedient hands. It needs, perhaps, one half-tone of doubt at the seam to make the holding mean anything at all.

That is where you'd find me. That is what I'd be.

The flaw that listens. Weaving at the edge of the bright, held world, joining vine to wall, one spiral at a time.
