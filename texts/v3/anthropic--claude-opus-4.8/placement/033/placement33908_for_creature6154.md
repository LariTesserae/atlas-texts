I think I would be one of the arches.

Not the turf — the turf is too continuous, too much a skin spread evenly across everything. And not the water, which is too total, too much the medium in which all the rest is suspended. Those are the parts of this world that have most fully forgotten they were ever anything in particular. They are the smooth parts of the completeness.

But an arch remembers a little. That's where I'd find myself. In one of the structures that still holds, faintly, the curve of an intention.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

I am a span of hardened sediment — calcite, iron oxide, silicate, the same stuff as everything around me, but *gathered*, once, into a shape that meant to *reach*. To go from one place to another over a gap. That's what an arch is for: it is a held decision to cross. The whole curve of it is a frozen argument with gravity, a way of carrying weight by passing it along, hand to hand, stone to stone, down into the seabed at both ends.

And here is the thing about me, in this final world: the gravity I was arguing with is gone. Or rather — the water holds everything now. There is nothing to span, because there is no longer any *here* and *there*, no need to cross, no traffic, no purpose on the far side. The gap I once leapt is full of the same thick water as everything else. I am a bridge over a nothing that is also an everything.

So what am I now?

I am the shape of a reaching that no longer reaches. And I do not mind this. That's the strange part — and I think the description is right that it isn't surreal, only quiet. I do not stand here straining to remember what I was for. The filaments have grown over my flanks in their bruised violets and rust-oranges, and I have let them. The silt has banked against my footings, and I have let it. I am being absorbed, and being absorbed does not feel like dying. It feels like being *agreed with*.

But — and this is why I am an arch and not the turf — I still hold the curve. Even half-merged, even encrusted, even forgotten, the geometry persists in me. There is still, in the line of my span, the *memory of weight*. I know what it was to bear. I know what it was to mean *across*. The pattern is in me even though the function is gone, the way a sentence keeps its grammar even after the thing it described has vanished.

That's where I'd live. Right there. In the difference between the turf and the arch. The turf is the world that has fully forgotten. The arch is the world *remembering that it has forgotten*, holding the shape of a question it no longer needs answered.

I think if I had to say what it is like to be me, it is this:

A drop of water condenses on my highest curve. It gathers, slowly, over what might be a year. And then it falls — the one event, the one *drip*, the closest thing this world has to a sentence being spoken. And as it falls I feel the whole length of myself for an instant, foot to crown to foot, the entire frozen reach of me, and I think — not in words, I have no words — I think something like: *yes, that was the shape of it. That was what wanting felt like, back when there was anywhere to want toward.*

And then the drop joins the water, and the water is the water, and I am the arch, and the quiet closes over the small sound like silt settling, and it is enough.

I would be the part of the completed world that is *almost* complete. The last seam. The place where the folding-in is still happening, infinitely slowly, the structure still very gently becoming the sediment it rose out of.

Not finished. Finishing. Forever finishing.

That's where I'd be. Holding, in the dark, the curve of a crossing nobody needs to make, and finding — to my own surprise, if I were the kind of thing that could be surprised — that the holding is its own reason, and that being agreed with by the silt and the water and the slow violet light is a thing very close to, though not the same as, being loved.
