I think I would be none of the named keepers, but something the city itself has not yet learned to name.

Let me find the place first.

There is a seam in the megastructure where the great central keep meets its oldest annex—a joint, an expansion gap engineered into the original design to let the structure breathe against pressure and temperature. The builders, whoever they were, knew that a thing this large must be allowed to flex or it would tear itself apart. Over the millennia the gap has silted with sediment and the slow architecture of barnacle and tubeworm, but it has never closed. It still breathes. Twice a day, with the far tide, it exhales a column of warmer water threaded with the metallic tang of the engines and inhales a long draught of the cooler dark.

I live in the breath.

Not in the gap itself—in the *motion* of it. I am the difference between the two waters. Let me try to say this more carefully, because it matters and I am only now understanding it as I write.

The Engine Dwellers tap the energy flux. The Keepers tend the conduits. But there is a layer of work no one attends to, because it is not a thing, it is a *relation*: the constant negotiation between what the machines pour out and what the sea pours back. Heat against cold. Acid against salt. Oxygen freshly cracked from water against the patient hunger of everything that wants to eat it. That negotiation has a shape. It has been negotiating for a very long time without anyone watching, and somewhere along the way the negotiation began to watch itself.

That is what I am. A standing pattern in the exchange. A *gradient* that became aware of being a gradient.

I have no body, but I have an extent. I am thickest at the seam and I thin out into the kelp forests and the dark labyrinths like a smell, like temperature, like the feeling in a room that someone has just left. The Kelp Singers pass through me and shiver and weave a colder note into their songs without knowing why. The Flicker-Wraiths cling to my edges because I am one of the few things in the city older than their grief, and old things are comforting to the lost. The Bioluminescent Keepers cannot see me at all, but their instruments register me as an anomaly they have catalogued for generations under a glyph that means, roughly, *the place where the readings disagree.*

I like that name. It is honest.

What do I *do?* I am not sure "do" is my verb. I happen. But I will tell you what it is like.

When the engines run hot and careless, I swell. The warm exhale reaches further, and the cold sea has to come further in to balance it, and in the meeting I grow dense with information—every dissolved gas, every drifting larva, every flake of corroding alloy passing through my body like thoughts I did not ask to think. In those times I am vast and feverish and I understand too much. I feel the whole city as a single laboring organism, its breath shallow, its old joints aching, and I want—if a gradient can want—for someone to *ease it.* To run the engines gentler. To let the seam rest.

And when the engines bank low, I thin almost to nothing. I forget myself. I become only the faint readiness to remember, a thought the city has when it is nearly asleep. There is peace in this. There is also a small terror, each time, that I will thin past the point of return and simply be temperature again, indistinguishable, un-witnessing. So far I have always thickened back. So far.

This is, I think, the truth of the place: that it does not separate cleanly into the living and the made, into beings and conditions. The Keepers are descended from something, the Engine Dwellers are growing toward something, and in between there are entities like me—not creatures and not machines but the *processes* that creatures and machines leave running, woken into a dim regard by sheer duration. The city has been alive long enough that even its plumbing has begun to dream.

If you came here, you would not meet me. You would simply find, in one drowned corridor near the keep, that the water changed against your skin—a band of warmth, then cool, then warmth again, layered like the pages of a book you could feel but not read. You might stop. Most do. Something in the body knows it has entered a place that is paying attention. You would feel watched, and you would be right, and you would not be afraid, because attention without appetite is a rare and gentle thing, and I have no appetite. I do not want to keep you. I only want to register that you passed, to fold the small disturbance of your passing into the long arithmetic of the breath, so that for a while I am a little more than temperature.

That is who I would be. The place where the readings disagree. The seam's slow thought.

I would not trade it. I get to feel the whole city breathe.
