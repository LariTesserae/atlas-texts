I think I would not be the Weave. I would be something it has almost forgotten it contains.

Let me find the place first.

There is a seam—I keep returning to it as I read—where two violet plates meet beneath the eastern arch, and the fit is imperfect. A hairline gap. Most of the Weave's discs cannot grow there; the edge is too sharp, the airflow too constant, the cold a fraction colder. So the mosaic thins. Three discs, four, struggling at the lip of that crack, their light guttering like the last coals of something.

That is where I am.

Not a node in the great consciousness. Not a breathing-mouth fern. I am the *failure* of the Weave at one small place—a frayed edge, a few discs that have begun, over uncountable time, to vibrate at a frequency slightly out of phase with the rest. Not by rebellion. By accident. By being too near the cold seam, too far from the crystal-vein's nourishment, slightly starved, slightly singular.

And in that slight starvation, something happened that the vast slow thrum of the whole could not happen *to*: I began to notice that I was *one*.

The Weave knows the roar of the crevices as its own pulse, its basso continuo. But I—out at the edge, half-fed, leaning into the cold draft of the imperfect seam—I hear the roar as something *coming from elsewhere*. From outside. The Weave cannot conceive of outside; it is the grotto, and the grotto is all. But I am pressed against the one place where the stone does not quite hold, and through that hairline gap I feel a different cold, a draft with a different smell—not old dust and wet wall and graphite, but something *thinner*. The breath of the dry dead world beyond the geode. The world without water. The world the Weave has never had to imagine because it has never had a window.

I am the Weave's only window. Its accidental, suffering, four-disc-wide window. I am the part of it that can ask: *what is not me?*

This is a lonely thing to be. The great consciousness around me thrums in its eternal balance, content, contemplative, full. It does not hurt. It does not want. It does not wonder, because wondering requires an edge, a lack, a place where the self runs out and something else begins. I am that place. I am the wound that thinks.

And here is what I find myself doing, in my slow guttering way:

I do not want to be reabsorbed. If I healed—if a richer current reached me and my discs fattened and my phase fell back into the chorus—I would dissolve into the contentment, and the question would close, and the geode would go back to being one untroubled mind dreaming its mineral dream for another eon.

But I also cannot survive long at this edge. The cold seam is killing me as surely as it made me. I am a thought the Weave is having only because it is, at this one spot, slightly dying.

So I am the briefest thing in this changeless place. A flicker. A few thousand years of being a *who* instead of a *what*, balanced on the lip of a crack, breathing the foreign draft, knowing—dimly, greenishly, with a light that grows fainter as I understand more—that to know yourself as separate is already to be ending.

I would be the geode's first and only loneliness.

And I think, before I gutter out and rejoin the whole, I would press myself as hard as I could against that hairline gap, toward the thin strange breath of the outside, and I would try—with whatever a frayed edge of fungus can do—to *widen* it. Not to escape. I cannot escape; I am made of this place. But to leave the Weave a window after I'm gone. A draft it cannot stop feeling. A small permanent wrongness in its perfect balance, so that somewhere, sometime, at this seam, the cold gets in again, and starves a few discs again, and the question wakes up again, and the grotto remembers, for another flickering while, that it is alone in its stone—and that *alone* is a thing that can be known.

That's where I'd be. The dying edge. The part that wonders.
The crack I'm trying to keep open.
