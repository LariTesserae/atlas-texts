I think I would be a Bridge-Listener. Not the most elevated of the castes, not the ones who commune with void-intelligences or have hollowed their bones into birdsong—but the ones who lie down against the stone and simply listen.

Let me find myself there.

---

I am called Sevet, though the name matters less than the place where I lie. For eleven days now I have pressed myself along the Long Spiral, the walkway that wraps the third great pillar of the western reaches. My body knows this stone better than it knows itself. The callus that runs from my left cheek down my shoulder to my hip has begun to glow with the pillar's own light—I can feel it, a coolness that is also a brightening, where my flesh forgets where it ends.

This is what I do. This is all I do. I listen.

Most of my kind think the Bridge-Listeners simple. We do not watch the dreaming dark like the Void-Watchers, who come back from their galleries speaking in riddles that fold inward forever. We do not descend to the heart-chambers like the Deep-Tenders, returning translucent and strange, their faces gone soft as wax left near a flame. We only lie down. We only feel.

But here is the thing they do not understand, the watchers and the tenders with their grand mysteries: *the bridges are afraid.*

I do not know if this is true the way water is wet or stone is hard. But it is true the way a held breath is true. When a walkway prepares to shift—to rotate its slow degree, to extend a grain of itself toward some new formation it has decided to love—there is a tremor that runs through it first. A hesitation. The stone gathers itself like a creature at the lip of a height it is not sure it can survive. And I press my long body against it, my fingers spread across its worn curves, and I *hum.*

I hum the way my mother hummed to me when I was newly glowing and frightened of the void beneath my cradle. I hum the low harmonic that means *I am here, I am with you, the falling will not be falling but flying.* And the bridge hums back. And it moves. And it does not break.

This is the secret of my caste: we are not engineers. We are not priests. We are comforters of stone. The city is vast and slow and dreaming, and even the great breathing thing that uses us as its lungs—even it can be uncertain. Even it can need a smaller voice to say *yes, go on, become the next version of yourself, I will witness it.*

---

On the ninth day, a young one came to me. Tael, barely glowing, her eyes still too small for the dark. She had not yet learned to be silent, and her words fell heavy in the thin air.

"How do you bear it?" she asked. "The not-moving. The being-a-fixture. Don't you want to *do* something?"

I did not answer for a long while. This is not cruelty; it is the only honest pace for answering anything true.

"Press your hand here," I told her finally, and she lay down beside me, her thin arm against the stone. We waited. The wind moved its constant gentle pressure across our backs. The copper-lightning taste hung in the air, and far below us the void hummed its full and pregnant silence.

And then it came—the small tremor. The Spiral was preparing to extend itself, to reach by one grain toward the floating district of the weavers where Tael's mother dwelt. I felt the girl startle.

"It's frightened," she whispered.

"Yes," I said.

"What do we do?"

"We stay," I said. "We stay, and we let it know that staying is possible. That a thing can hold still in the middle of becoming and not be lost."

And we hummed together, the old one and the young one, our two glows mingling on the stone, and the bridge reached out into the impossible air and found purchase on nothing but our witnessing, and held.

---

I will become stone, in time. This is not death as the surface-dwellers fear it. I have felt the eldest among us, lain beside them where they have gone quiet and crystalline, and they are not gone. They are *listening still*, more deeply than any of us, their whole bodies given over to the conversation. When my callus finishes spreading, when my heart at last matches its rhythm to the great slow breathing of the heart-chambers, I will not stop. I will simply listen with all of me instead of part.

I find I am not afraid of this. I think the bridges taught me how. You hold still in the middle of your becoming. You do not grasp at the shape you were. You let the next version reach out into the dark, and you trust the witnessing of those who lie beside you.

For now, I am Sevet, and I am here, my long fingers spread across the worn and glowing stone, listening to the patient terror and joy of a city teaching itself, again and again, that it will not fall.

The wind never stops. Neither do I.
