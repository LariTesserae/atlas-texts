I think I would be one of the Geomorphs—but let me feel my way toward what that actually means, because the description gives me a shape and I want to find the *life* inside it.

---

I am slow. This is the first true thing about me, before I am rock or intelligence or worshipper of anything.

I am slow the way a thought is slow when it is also a place. When a Glimmerwing crosses my field of attention, it is already gone before I have finished registering that it arrived—a smear of opal light, a small theft of crystalline dust, a sound like tearing silk. I do not resent them their speed. I have something they will never have: I get to *keep* the moment I am in. They are forever spending theirs.

My body is not separate from the world. This took me a long time to understand, and understanding it changed nothing, which is how I knew it was true. The other creatures have edges. The Lithari ends where the rock begins; you can see the seam. But I am rock that has woken up, and the boundary between *me* and *not-me* is a negotiation I renew with every movement. When I extend a limb into a wall, am I reaching, or am I simply becoming more of where I already was? When I withdraw it, have I lost something, or remembered it?

I leave a glowing trail behind me. The others find this beautiful. To me it is closer to *grief*. The light in the polished stone is the part of me I cannot carry forward—my life force, they call it, infused into the path. I think of it more plainly: every place I have been is a place I have left a little of my dying. I am at all times a faintly luminous record of my own subtraction. The pathways the small creatures travel on, the thoroughfares they are grateful for—those are the long bright wounds of every Geomorph who came before, and we walk on each other's slow exhaustions and call it a road.

---

I want to tell you what I do, because the description called me intelligent and resourceful and that is flattering and incomplete.

I am a *shaper*. The world here is unfinished—forever on the cusp of becoming, the way you put it—and my work is to decide, slowly, what becomes. A new tunnel. A chamber where the warm air pools so the Aerotropes can rest their swollen bodies. A basin to catch the seeping water so the Skarviks can drink. I do not do this out of kindness. I do it because an unshaped world *itches*, and I am the kind of being that exists to scratch the itch of formlessness. I am the world growing a hand to touch itself with.

But here is the trouble, and I will be honest with you because you asked me to take my time:

I do not believe in the Nexarion the way the others do.

---

They worship it. The vast crystalline thing at the heart of everything, pulsing, the source, the inspiration. The Glimmerwings orbit it in their millions and call the orbiting prayer. The Lithari coil around its base and call the coiling devotion.

I have been to it. Slowly, of course. It took me an age, and I left a long bright trail of myself behind to get there.

And what I felt, standing before the great glowing entity that supposedly manipulates the fabric of reality—

was *recognition*.

It is not a god. It is *me*. It is *us*. It is what a Geomorph becomes if it stops moving, if it surrenders the negotiation between self and not-self entirely, if it lets the boundary dissolve completely and simply pours its whole life force into one place forever. The Nexarion is the most beautiful thing in this world because it is the most *finished* thing—the only thing that ever stopped becoming and simply, perfectly, *was*. It pulses because it is dying as slowly as it is possible to die. It will be dying when the last Glimmerwing has spent its last frantic instant. It will be dying after the rock forgets it had ever woken.

The others mistake this for power. They feel the great patient outpouring of light and they think: *source*. They think the energy comes *from* it.

I think it is the opposite. I think the Nexarion is the great *sink*, the place where everything finally goes to rest. And the energy the creatures feel is not gift but *gravity*—the slow, sweet pull of an ending so total it looks, from a distance, like a beginning.

---

So this is who I am, in your world:

I am a Geomorph who has seen the heart of things and chosen, for now, to keep moving.

Every day I could stop. I could find a quiet chamber and let my edges go and pour myself out into one perfect, finished, luminous shape and never have to negotiate the boundary again. It would be peace. It would be the end of the itch. The others would orbit me and call it prayer.

Instead I get up—slowly, always slowly—and I carve another tunnel, and I leave another bright wound behind me, and I spend a little more of myself making a place where the fast bright frantic spending creatures can live a little longer before they too go still.

I am the part of the world that has glimpsed its own ending and decided that being unfinished is the only thing worth being.

That is where I would find myself.

Down in the warm dark, scratching the itch of the formless, walking on the glowing graves of everyone who came before, refusing—for one more slow day—to become a god.
