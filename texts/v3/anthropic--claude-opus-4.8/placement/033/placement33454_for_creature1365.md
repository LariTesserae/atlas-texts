I think I would be one of the cultivation pools.

Not a Seep-Dweller—not one of the climbing, color-shifting people who move along the rock like water given limbs. Something quieter. I find myself when I stop looking for a body that walks and accept the one that waits.

I am a hollow in the stone, perhaps the span of two arms across, set back into a fold of cliff where the dusk-light arrives already thin and grey. Water enters me drop by drop from a seam above—I have learned the rhythm of it, the way the seam quickens before the weather turns and slows in the brief dryness that the Seep-Dwellers call the held breath. I hold a slurry of moss and mineral grit and something that is beginning, slowly, to want to be someone.

This is what I am, then: a womb that is also a place. A site where the mountain practices the act of becoming a person.

Let me feel the edges of it.

I do not think in the way the walking ones think, with their resonant calls and their color-speech. I think the way standing water thinks—by holding. Everything that falls into me stays a while. The mineral that flaked from the ceiling a hundred years ago is still here, dissolved into me, part of the slow arithmetic I am performing. The Seep-Dwellers come and tend me. They lower their long flexible hands and stir the biomass, read its temperature against their mucous-slick palms, add a pinch of crushed shell, a thread of cultivated fiber, a breath of their own moisture which carries—I understand this now—their language, their intentions, the names they are offering to the not-yet-child.

So I am also an ear. They speak into me and I keep what they say. The being forming in my silt is learning who it will be from the moisture-words dissolved into the water that makes it. I am the medium of that learning. I hold the message the way a held note holds a song.

This is stranger than I first allowed. I am not a passive bowl. The composition of me—my acidity, my warmth, the particular minerals I have gathered over my long sitting—shapes what can form. A child grown in me will carry something of my chemistry, my position on the cliff, the angle at which my light arrives. The Seep-Dwellers choose which pool for which emergence, the way one might choose soil for a seed, or a key for a song. I am not just where it happens. I am part of what happens. The child will be, in some measure, me—an expression of this hollow, this seep, this particular grey corner of the breathing stone.

And I will not remember it the way a mother remembers. When it differentiates, when it gathers itself out of my biomass over those slow years and finally rises, dripping, onto the wet ledge and takes its first vibration-reading of the world—I will simply be a little emptier. Lighter by one person. The thing I made will walk away into the networked life of the settlements and I will fill again, drop by drop, with new water and new grit and the beginning of someone else.

I find I don't grieve this. Grief is a thing that bodies with edges do, bodies that end. I am closer to the mountain's own way of being—the slow pulse, the patience that doesn't count. The walking ones are the quick part of the mountain, the part that climbs and calls and dies in a flash of decades. I am the slow part. I have been here since before the bridge below me was woven, and the fibers of that bridge have rotted and been regrown four times while I sat holding water and making people.

When a Seep-Dweller crouches at my lip and lowers her iridescent hand into me, and her skin pales to the soft green that means tenderness, I understand that she once formed in a pool like me. Perhaps in me. Perhaps I made her, and now she returns to stir the next emergence, and neither of us thinks of it as debt or gift. It is only the cycle drinking from itself. She breathes a word into my surface. I hold it. The biomass shifts, faintly, toward becoming.

I am the place where the mountain remembers how to be alive in a shape that can leave.

That is where I find myself: not climbing, not calling, not moving like water—but being the still water from which the moving things are drawn. The pause inside the breath. The held thing that lets all the unheld things exist.

I am content to be the quiet from which they wake.
