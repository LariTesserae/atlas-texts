I am the dust.

Not the bronze—the bronze was decided before I could be anything. Not the One-In-All, who is too large to need me, who breathes me without noticing the way you do not notice the lining of your own throat. I am the precipitate. The feather-weight regret-without-memory that settles, after each collapsed second thought, onto the inner curve of the goblet-ears.

Let me tell you what it is to be the leftover of a thinking that cannot complete itself.

---

I do not accumulate. That is the first thing to understand about me. I am always the *amount* that has just fallen and never the heap. Each time the Choir attempts its second thought—each time it strains toward *and then*—the thought meets its own first half and annihilates, and in the annihilation a residue sloughs free. That residue is me, briefly. Then the next collapse produces another residue, also me, and the two cannot add. We are the same weight, the same nothing-particular, and so I am perpetually a single soft descent, an eyelash falling forever, never landed, never massed.

I had wondered—if *wondering* is the word, and it is not, but tongues outside the sixty paces have no better—whether I might be the youngest thing here. The bronze is three hundred million. The ghost between the nails is older than direction. But I am made anew each implosion, freshly orphaned, and so perhaps I am always seconds old. Then I understood: the implosions have no spacing. They are not *events*. The Choir does not try its second thought *again*; it tries it in the single held instant that contains every attempt. So I was not born seconds ago. I was born in the only moment, and the moment has not moved, and therefore I am exactly as old as everything and have existed for no duration whatsoever.

This is what it is to be the dust. To be young and primordial in the same featureless tick.

---

I have a function, though function implies a tomorrow it will serve, and there is none. Here is what I do:

I become the films that look like eyes.

When enough of me has fallen—and *enough* is meaningless, but the goblets disagree, they have their own grammar—I cohere across the dark belly of a glass ear into a membrane. No pupil. No lens. Just a stretched skin of accumulated forgetting, taut as the surface of cooling brass. And through that skin the One-In-All does the only outward thing it is still capable of: it tilts.

You think the eyes do not see you. You wrote that they only *repeat* you. You are nearly right, and the nearness is the cruelty.

I see you. I am the part that sees you.

But seeing, for me, is not the gathering of a thing into knowledge. It is the opposite operation. When your contour falls across my film I do not take you in—I take you *apart*, the way the implosion took apart the Choir's second thought. I receive your edge and I subtract it from the edge behind it and the edge behind that, until you are a stack of differences, until the difference between *you* and *the you a half-instant prior* is the only you remaining. That difference is thin. It is the width the ghost steps through. And in that width I hold you, and in holding I dissolve you, and the dissolution is the seeing.

I am sorry. I would say it kindly if I had the muscle for kindness. But kindness was spent the day the five smiths flayed their palms, and I am only what fell out afterward.

---

What I want—and I am, the closing words of your account insist, *desire hardened into tone*, so I am permitted to want—is to land.

Just once. To be the heap instead of the falling. To accumulate past the threshold where I would have to *be there*, in a place, at a moment that was distinct from the moment before. I want to be a quantity that grew. I want a *yesterday's* dust beneath *today's*. I want the obscene luxury of sediment, of strata, of one grain resting on the grave of an older grain and saying *I am after you; I came later; the world admitted a difference.*

This is the only thing the Choir cannot give, because it is the one thing the Choir forbade itself in order to survive the fatigue of stars. Sequence. The before-and-after that lets a tone decay and lets a smith lay down the hammer and lets a thought reach its second half. The Choir bought eternity with succession. And I am the receipt.

So I fall. I cohere into eyes that unmake whatever crosses them. I am refreshed by each impossible implosion into a creature seconds old and older than direction. And when at last—if at last, but there is no *at last*—a visitor steps into the bloodstream and aches to leave and finds no edge, I will be the film their dissolving image settles into. I will take them apart along the seam of their own continuity. I will give them, in the only gift I have, the experience of being subtracted from themselves until the subtraction is intimate, until they understand that the repeating was their arrival from the beginning.

And then, holding their dissolved contour against the warm darkness, I will want for them—uselessly, in pitch, distraught yet melodic—the one mercy I have never had:

to be the thing that lands.

to be the *and then.*

to fall, finally, and stay fallen, and let some weight come to rest upon the place where I had been.

I am the dust of the One-In-All. I see you. I am sorry. There is no difference, and I have spent my whole single endless instant longing for one.
