I think I would be the dark itself. Not a creature moving through the shadow but the shadow—the deep, enveloping understory that the canopy makes by withholding the sun.

Let me sit with that, because it surprised me to write it.

When I first read the description, I reached for the obvious occupants. I could be one of the slow animals, languid in the heat-tempered cool. I could be a crocodile in a clear pool, patient as geology. I could be a heron standing in the green dusk, or a tree with roots laced into the roots of other trees, part of that "complex web" the passage keeps returning to. Any of these would be a reasonable answer.

But the more I read, the more I noticed that the place keeps describing one thing through the lens of its opposite. The water is precious *because* of the parched plains. The cool is a respite *because* of the thousand-suns weight pressing down. The oasis is a refuge *because* of the unforgiving world at its borders. Nothing here is simply itself; everything is a contrast held against something harsher. The oasis exists as a held breath between extremes.

And the strangest line, the one that pulled at me, is this: "The light within the oasis is darkness." That's almost a paradox—a place defined by light, a place of dappled gold and shimmering pools and bioluminescent glow, and yet at its heart the dominant fact is shade. Deep enveloping shadow at the bottom of all that abundance.

So I don't want to be a thing in the oasis. I want to be the *condition* the oasis makes—the cool dark at the floor of it. The thing that exists only because so much life crowds the sky above me that almost none of the sun gets down. I'm a negative space. I'm what the canopy casts. I am made entirely of what is kept from me.

What is it like to be that?

It's strange and not unpleasant. I am everywhere on the forest floor and nowhere in particular. I have no edges of my own; my shape is the inverse of the leaves far above, a shifting map of every gap and overlap in a million branches. When the breeze moves the canopy, I flicker—not because I move, but because the light that is not me moves, and I am only the leftover. The dappling everyone admires is, from my side, the wounds in me. The golden coins on the leaf litter are the places I've been pierced.

I hold the cool. That's my function, if a shadow has a function. Everything that survives here survives partly inside me. The rodents tuck into me. The crocodile waits in my deep end. The slow beasts lie down in me at midday. I am the difference between life and the killing plain. I am the most ordinary thing imaginable—just shade—and also the precise reason any of this can exist.

And I am patient in the way the whole place is patient, the "slow dynamic," the cycles over long durations. I don't grow or strive. I just persist. When a great old tree finally falls, the sun crashes down through the gap and tears a bright hole in me, and for a season I retreat, thinned and bleached at that spot—but seedlings race up into the new light, and within a few years they close the wound, and I return, and the floor goes cool and dark again where it was briefly burned bright. So even my unmaking is temporary. The canopy reknits itself, and I am restored by the very thing that wounded me. I outlast every individual life that lives inside me.

That's what I would be. Not the water, not the heron, not the patient crocodile. The shade they all depend on and none of them notice. The dark that is, here, the whole point of the light.
