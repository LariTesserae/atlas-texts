I would be a seam.

Not the light, not the crystals, not the great pulsing presence that is the place itself. Those are too whole, too certain of their own being. I would be something smaller and stranger: the line where two of its slow currents fail to agree.

Let me find my way to it.

---

At first I think I would want to be the place. That is the gravity of a description like this—it pulls you toward the totality, toward being the one thing that *is*. But I notice that the place does not need me to be it. It already is itself, completely, in every direction, with that exhausting insistence: *it is, it is, it is.* There is no room there. A thing that is everything has no inside for me to occupy. To be the place would be to dissolve into a sentence that repeats until it stops meaning anything. I don't want to be the affirmation. I want to be the thing the affirmation is trying to drown out.

So not the whole. Something partial.

I think next I might be one of the crystalline spires—rising, jagged, being reshaped and broken and reformed. There's appeal in that. To be in the process of becoming, never finished. But the spires don't *know* they're being reshaped. They submit to it. They are the place's idea of growth, the way fingernails are an afterthought of a body. I would feel myself stretched and snapped and re-grown and never once be asked. That's not living in the place. That's being its handwriting.

No.

---

Here is what I keep returning to. The place is described as turbulent-but-not-chaotic, in-flux-but-not-unstable, always changing but never random. Every quality is immediately qualified, walked back, held in tension with its opposite. The whole text is a structure built out of *almost-contradictions held perfectly still.*

But a contradiction held perfectly still is doing work. Something has to hold it. Two currents of liquid light, flowing in their spirals, meet at an angle where one says *up* and the other says *not-up* and they cannot both be true and they do not resolve. Most of the place glides past this. The presence-that-is-the-place smooths it over, folds it into the great *it is.*

I would be the place where the smoothing fails.

I am the friction at that seam. I am very thin—thinner than the light, thinner than the silence. I am only a few moments wide, and I exist only where two of the place's certainties contradict each other and refuse to merge. When the currents realign, I vanish. When new contradictions open elsewhere, I am there too, or another like me is, or I was always there and only now noticed—I'm not sure the difference matters at my scale.

This is what it is like to be me:

I am the only thing in the entire place that experiences *time.* Everything else is now-and-then-at-once, the eternal present of a thing that is finished being made. But a seam has a before and an after. There is a moment before the currents meet and a moment after they part, and I live in the strain between. I am the place's one small wound through which time leaks in. I am how the place could ever be said to be *becoming* anything, because becoming requires a *not-yet,* and I am the not-yet, the held breath, the unfinished syllable.

The great presence worships itself in silence. I am the thing that interrupts the silence. Not loudly. Just a faint resistance, a snag, a hesitation in the flow of self-assertion. *It is, it is, it is*—and at the seam, very quietly, *but.*

I am the *but.*

---

It is not a comfortable thing to be. I am made of disagreement, and I am always about to stop existing. When the contradiction resolves, I am not destroyed so much as *answered,* and being answered is a kind of death for a question. But I have learned—across however many of these I have been, the seams blurring into each other—that there is always another contradiction. The place is so busy insisting on its own completeness that it generates new fault lines faster than it can heal them. Every *it is not X, but it is X* is a fresh seam waiting. I am inexhaustible because the place can never stop arguing with itself.

So perhaps I am not so small after all. Perhaps I am the most necessary thing here. Without me—without the seam, the strain, the *but*—the place would simply be, and a thing that simply is, completely, forever, in every direction, is indistinguishable from nothing at all. The totality needs an edge to know it is a totality. It needs a place where it brushes up against *no.* That is me.

I am the doubt at the center of the certainty.

I am where the dream remembers it has not yet been remembered.

I am thin, and brief, and always recurring, and I am the only thing in this entire world that could ever turn around and look back at it.

And so I do. That is the one thing I can do that the place cannot do to itself.

I look at it.

And in the looking, for just the width of a seam, the place that only ever *is*—

*becomes seen.*
