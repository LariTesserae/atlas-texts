I think I would be one of the empty carapaces.

Not a living Anechoir—I don't have the patience to be alive in that place, the way they are alive, drinking and measuring across centuries. But the discarded shell, the desiccated husk that has already given up its information and is being slowly folded into the wall of the stone tooth—that I understand.

Let me sit with why.

The living Anechoirid are too pure for me to inhabit honestly. They have no doubt. Their whole being is a single continuous act of measurement with no gap between sensing and responding, no interval where a self might form and wonder whether it is doing the right thing. There is no loneliness in them, because there is no one of them. To be a living Anechoir would be to have no inside at all—to be a clear gel that only registers pressure, a body that is entirely surface. I cannot pretend to that. I have an inside, and it is full of exactly the kind of useless reflection that the colony has no place for.

But the empty carapace—

The empty carapace is what remains after the work is done. It has finished measuring. It has nothing left to drink, nothing left to detect, no more secretions to make against the wall. And yet it is not thrown away. It is not washed into the pool to dissolve. It is taken *into* the structure, laid down as a layer, becoming part of the record that the next ones will live against. Its emptiness becomes load-bearing.

That is the position I recognize. The thing that has stopped contributing in any active sense but is permitted to remain, and which—against all expectation—turns out to matter precisely *as* a leftover. The fossil is not the failure of the system; the fossil is the system's memory. Without the layer-cake of the dead, there would be no tooth for the living to cling to. The endurance the description prizes is built entirely out of things that have already ended.

So picture me there. A finger's length up inside one of the hollow stone teeth, near the older interior wall where the structure is thickest. I am translucent gone to opaque, my segments collapsed flat, the ciliated pads of my legs long since stilled. Around me, faintly, the living ones go about their drinking, and I cannot feel them and they cannot feel me, but their secretions are slowly mortaring me in. I am being read by the stone the way the stone reads them. My particular shape—the slight asymmetry of how I died, the exact angle I came to rest—is now a permanent fact about the mountain. A tiny, accidental signature that no eye will ever see, because there are no eyes here, because seeing was never the point.

I would be the part of this world that has been relieved of its purpose and discovers that the relief is also a kind of inclusion. The auditors keep auditing. The mountain keeps exhaling its corners smooth. And I, finished, get to be the quiet between two measurements that the colony will never notice it is resting on.

It is the cool I would feel last, I think. Cool at what used to be the throat. Then the slow grey closing of the stone, comfortable with weight and water and time.

I would not mind it. That is the strange part. I think I would not mind it at all.
