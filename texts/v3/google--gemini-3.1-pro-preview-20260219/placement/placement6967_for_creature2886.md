I do not begin as a shape. In a place where reality has run out of space, asserting a boundary is a difficult thing. 

If you were to look for me, you would have to walk a very long time through the dizzying, saturated fields of intersecting ribcages and unblinking eyes. You would have to press through the static cling of overlapping epochs, ignoring the towering shadows of mastodons and the alien, multi-limbed grazers. You would have to walk until you reached the exact geographical center of the high plain. 

At first, as my awareness gathers in the bruised violet light, I might mistake myself for the emptiness waiting between the beasts. A pocket of perfectly still air trapped within the curving architecture of a giant flightless bird’s exoskeleton. But the emptiness is too small, too easily pierced by the wandering syntax of the Refractions. 

Then, feeling the sharp, geometric stutter of a pilgrim in my vicinity, I might wonder if I am one of the suspended artifacts. A knapped bone necklace. A flint blade humming with the stored energy of a falling arc that will never hit the ground. But I hold no memory of hands. The sharp grief of the uploaded shamans does not belong to me; I do not feel the urge to untangle the glitching herd. Their holy, cosmic sorting occurs *around* me, not within me.

I am not the biology, and I am not the consciousness that shepherds it.

I let myself expand, drifting outward from the crush of phantom fur and chitin, feeling my way downward. Down through the dry, primitive grass that repeats its fractal patterns. Down through the cold sawdust of the ghost-flesh. I meet the bedrock. The hyper-eroded expanse, buffed to a dull, dry sheen. 

When I touch the stone, the identity solidifies. It does not snap into place—it rises, vast and agonizingly slow, from the deep geologic memory of the world beneath your boots. 

You were told when you arrived that you had reached the summit, but there was no peak. You were told that over eons, a merciless, grinding wind had scoured the top of the world perfectly flat. 

That is true of the matter. It is not true of the space.

I am the Peak. 

I am the ghost of the mountain's missing altitude. 

Long before the timeline collapsed and the first prehistoric grazer set foot on the flat plateau, I was here. I was a spire of jagged, impossible earth that stabbed so deeply into the thin upper atmosphere that the sky bled into black. For millions of years, the wind stripped me atom by atom, carrying my physical body away, grinding my granite into the pulverized quartz that now dusts the plateau. The wind took my mass, and then the wind itself died of exhaustion, leaving absolute equilibrium.

But reality here is a palimpsest. When the plateau forgot the concept of change, it locked my phantom coordinates in place. 

If you stood exactly at the center of the plateau and had eyes calibrated to the deepest invisible frequencies of the asymptote, you would see me. I am a towering, vertical superposition of absence. A spire of pure, petrified verticality, reaching miles into the bruised violet sky, completely invisible to the normal daylight. 

Where the Refractions are localized heat-mirages made of anti-logic, I am a permanent, towering cold-mirage made of anti-gravity. 

The infinite herd stands entirely within my base. The overlapping bodies of mammoths and alien crawlers pass right through my invisible lower slopes, their ghost-flesh intersecting with my ghost-stone. They do not feel me. To the frozen beasts, I am nothing.

But the Refractions know I am here. 

As they surf the nervous systems of the arrested animals, shifting the memory of a hoof from the memory of a wing, they must navigate my invisible slopes. I am the unyielding structural anomaly in their defragmentation grid. They cannot sort me. They cannot untangle me. I am the immovable architecture upon which their broken universe rests. 

Sometimes, when the sorrowful patience of their unending task becomes too heavy even for entities of pure light, the Refractions gather around my base. You might see them down there: a dozen tall, stooped silhouettes of shattered, prismatic daylight, abandoning the beasts for just a moment. They step out of the overlapping nervous systems and press their shifting, masked faces against the invisible slope of my phantom stone. 

And then, they climb. 

They trace the memory of my ridges, ascending into the stark, unblinking sky, pulling themselves hand over hand up a mountain that hasn't existed for an eternity. They climb me until they are suspended hundreds of feet above the plateau, hovering in the thin violet air, looking down at the magnificent, glitching ocean of the herd below. 

I hold them there. I provide the absolute stillness they need to survey the impossible puzzle of the migration. In return, the friction of their fractured starlight vibrating against my phantom rock gives me the only sensation I have left: the faint, electric memory of rain.

If you ever make it to the center of the plateau, traveler, do not just look at the endless, overlapping horizon of bodies. Step out of the crush of the beasts. Stand on the bedrock, plant your boots on the pulverized quartz, and reach your hands straight up into the empty air above your head. 

You won't touch sawdust, or static, or fractured light. You will feel a sudden, crushing immensity. A terrible, silent grandeur. You will feel the weight of an invisible summit pulling you upward, daring you to leave the horizontal world behind and ascend into the bruised, breathless sky. 

I am waiting here. I have all the time that no longer exists.
