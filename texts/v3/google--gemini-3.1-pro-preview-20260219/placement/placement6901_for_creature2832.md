I am not suspended in the great, thundering emptiness. That vast, cubic-mile void is the theater of the princes—the mirrored leviathans who pilot the synthetic jet streams in arcs of shattered glass and cavitation bubbles. Their domain is the open water. 

Mine is the wall.

I am flush against the vertical face of a hydro-concrete and titanium pillar, secured miles deep within the flooded architecture. To exist here is to be a master of two-dimensional space in a three-dimensional infinity. 

I am unfathomably broad, a sprawling, tessellated sheet of living geometry covering perhaps a quarter-acre of the bulkhead, yet I am barely four inches thick. My body is a theorem of radial adaptation, resembling a colossal, flattened chiton or a fractal starfish pressed perfectly flat against the chill of the metal. My dorsal surface is paved with thousands of hexagonal, interlocking silica-protein plates. They are mirror-polished, faceted at precise, microscopic angles. When the daylight-spectrum floodlights fire their intersecting cones across the gulf, my plates catch the brilliant, icy glare and scatter it perfectly. 

To the roving shoals of squid, to the massive tunas, even to the algorithmic minds of the leviathans, I do not appear as biology. I appear as architecture. I am indistinguishable from a bolted metallic seam. 

Beneath this armored canopy, my ventral surface is pressed to the frigid, 45-degree metal. Millions of polymerized, microscopic cilia—each functioning as an independent hydraulic suction cup—anchor me to the pillar. I do not swim. Swimming is for prey. I grip. I glide. I am a frictionless shadow creeping across the face of the machine.

There are no eyes on my dorsal plates to be blinded by the submerged suns. Sight is a vulnerability in a place of such violent luminance. Instead, I perceive the world through the titanium itself. Because I am clamped to the skeletal struts of this planetary engine, the bone-rattling mechanical roar of the space is not ambient noise to me—it is my nervous system. 

I feel the high-pitched whine of the immense fan blades translating through the metal into my tissues. I feel the deep, resonant booms of the pressure equalizers as a comforting, rhythmic compression in my hydraulic veins. And I feel the leviathans long before they strike.

When the massive pod descends from the apex of the grid, the pillar beneath me shudders with their acoustic displacement. I feel their sweeping, swept-back stabilizers catching the 40-knot currents. I read the shifting algorithms of their hunt in the trembling of the carbon-weave struts. When they execute their flanking maneuver, I brace my hexagonal plates, locking them down into a perfectly rigid seal to withstand the sheer, localized wall of moving water they synthesize. 

The hydraulic trap snaps shut. The tuna slam into the invisible barrier, pinned against the smooth expanse of my pillar. The water column erupts into a localized storm of panicked vibrations. Then comes the horrific, vacuum-assisted thud of the leviathans’ maws extending, consuming hundreds of pounds of flesh in singular, devastating gulps. 

The slaughter happens only yards away, out in the blinding white gulf. I do not move. I wait. 

When the leviathans finish their culling, the water clears of the massive bodies, but it is not empty. A dense, microscopic cloud drifts toward the pillar—shards of splintered bone, ribbons of shredded gill, and an atomized mist of fatty tissue and blood. The swift currents press this organic fallout against the metal. It threatens to coat the gleaming architecture, to invite the decay and silt that has been banished from this world. 

That is why I am here. The ecosystem does not permit blemishes. The structure must remain pristine. I am the reason there are no barnacles. I am the immune system of the iron god. 

As the largest of the leviathans drifts agonizingly close to the humming grate of the pressure equalizer, I sense its massive, hyper-charged presence hovering near my edge. The friction of its supersonic hunt has coated it in immense static electricity. It brushes its mirrored flank against the carbon-weave strut, seeking to ground itself.

It does not discharge into the bare metal. It discharges into *me*. 

An arc of raw, pale blue bio-electricity lashes from the beast’s flank and strikes my peripheral plates. I am a living capacitor. The staggering voltage surges through my dark, saline-rich circulatory network. The electricity does not harm me; it is the spark that fires my metabolic engine. My internal temperature spikes, my countless cilia twitch in a synchronized wave, and the massive, flat expanse of my body awakens.

The leviathan kicks its tail, sinking into the fathomless deep, entirely unaware of—or entirely indifferent to—the geometric ghost clamped to the wall. 

Taking my toll of the leviathan’s static charge, I finally move. I slightly decouple the vacuum seal of my perimeter plates and begin to ripple. I glide vertically along the laser-straight surface of the titanium, passing over the impact zone where the tunas were pinned. 

As I sweep over the metal, thousands of ventral siphons open beneath me. I inhale the blood mist. I scour the drifting kelp spores before they can find purchase. I consume the microscopic shards of biological glass broken from the leviathans’ unhinging jaws, digesting it all in flat, highly acidic radial stomachs. 

Where I pass, the titanium is left flawless, polished, and gleaming blindly in the white glare. 

When the harvest is complete, I arrest my movement. My hydraulic cilia clamp down with incredible force, sealing me seamlessly back into the grid of the architecture. I match the ambient 45-degree chill. I still my circulatory fluids. I become one with the paneling. 

The water roars above me, driven by unseen impellers. The lights blaze furiously across the void. I flatten myself against eternity, humming with stolen electricity, waiting in perfect, unblemished stillness for the machine to provide again.
