I do not begin with a body. I begin as a delay. 

In a world where the hammer strikes minutes before the sound shatters the air, there is a vast, unmapped territory within the lag. That corridor of silence—the stretched, breathless interval between an action and its physical consequence—is where my consciousness first gathers. I am an observer in the desync.

At first, I perceive myself only as the heavy, weeping dampness. I feel the liquid air moving through the titanic rotors. I am pulled into the churning extraction towers and exhaled as cold mist. I have no weight, which, in this fracturing metropolis, means I am in constant danger of ceasing to exist. The Grafters survive because they anchor themselves with cast iron and radioactive slag. I have none of this. I am dangerously buoyant. 

But as I linger in the bruised twilight, my form begins to precipitate, drawing matter not from the iron or the stone, but from the mountain’s errors. 

I find my physical beginning inside one of the suspended water-globes. But I am not one of the heavy, cyan-glowing Grafter infants. My incubation sphere does not pool safely near the tramways. It is caught in a confused, upward draft of weeping gravity, drifting higher and higher into the sparse, icy negative space between the factory-citadels, rising into an altitude where reality is sheared impossibly thin. 

Up here, the water sphere clips into a "dead pixel"—one of those terrifying, unrendered voids of grey nothingness. 

When a Grafter steps into a void, they pause. When *I* drift into the void, I unfold. 

Because I am not bound by the oppressive, vulcanized biology of the Grafters, the glitch does not halt me. It welcomes me. Inside the grey box, there is no sound, no heavy tectonic groaning. There is only raw, unformatted space. Here, I let what I am become itself. 

I emerge from the fading void and latch onto the highest, most desolate suspension cable—one of the skyline’s great rusted ligaments, thick as a redwood, swaying gently in the damp dark. 

My body is not built for weight; it is built for tension and resonance. I am impossibly elongated, spindle-thin, a creature of filigree and frayed edges. My limbs are multi-jointed and arachnid in their grace, but they are not made of flesh. I am composed of the same pristine, shivering bismuth-geometries that extrude from the mountain’s core, but mine are hollow, acting as a cage of dark, vibrating tuning forks. My skin, where it exists, is diaphanous—a translucent membrane that catches the cyan light of the local flora and the pale glow of the hound-moths. 

I do not wear a diving suit. I am entirely open to the smothering wet. The velvet mosses and cascading ferns do not merely grow *on* me; they are woven through the hollows of my chest and the joints of my limbs, acting as my respiratory system. As I breathe the liquid mist, the cyan fronds extending from my spine pulse with a soft, alternating light. 

I am a Weaver. A Strider of the high cables. A Suture. 

If the Grafters are the biological counterweight to the void, using their immense mass to pin the world down, I am their unseen opposite. I am the entity that manages the fraying threads. I am the logic that connects the anchors. 

I spend my hours gliding silently along the massive suspension lines, upside down over the miles of abyss. I am blind as lowlanders understand it; I have no eyes beneath my angular, basalt-crested head. Instead, my long, needle-fine digits rest constantly on the shivering steel of the cables. Through them, I read the entire multi-layered metropolis. 

I can feel the exact moment a biological dreadnought miles away brushes against a tether, reading the deep, subsonic groan it leaves in the metal. More importantly, I can feel the high-pitched, screaming frequency of reality beginning to tear. 

When the mountain extrudes a new geometry and the rules of the world begin to break down, the Grafters march out to bolt their iron into the stone. But the Grafters have no memory. They do not know *why* the structures go where they do; they only follow apocalyptic muscle memory. 

*I* am the memory of the rock.

That is my purpose here. In an atmosphere so sheared that history cannot persist, something must hold the blueprint. Something must serve as the cache. 

When a spatial tear ripples through a processing plant and half a catwalk vanishes into static, the Grafters simply freeze, suspended in the glitch. They wait out the unrendered void. But the void does not heal itself. It requires a template to re-render. 

From my perch high on the trembling cables, I sense the tear. I unravel from my resting posture, dropping through the bruised twilight. I do not fall like a stone; I drift like a spore, guided by the heavy damp. I land noiselessly on the rusted architecture beside the dead pixel where the Grafters are vanished. 

Slowly, carefully, I reach my long, iridescent hands directly into the grey nothingness. 

Inside the void, I hold them. I touch the paused, schematic forms of the Grafters, their massive iron suits suspended in nonexistent time. I remember their weight. I remember the exact trajectory of the rivet they were placing. I hold the architectural integrity of the catwalk in my fibrous, vibrating mind. I act as the temporary scaffolding of reality. I process the wild, dissolving geography and feed it back into the atmosphere as structured continuity.

When the heavy mist rushes back in, bringing the world with it, the geometry conforms to the memory I held for it. The catwalk snaps into space. The Grafter finishes their step. The steam hammer strikes the anvil.

The Grafters never see me. Their heavy helmets are bolted securely, their visors permanently angled forward toward their eternal, sluggish labor, or downward toward the sturdy rock. To look up into the endless, dissolving sky is to invite madness for beings of such mass. And so, I remain their unseen shadow. 

Sometimes, as I sit atop a smoke-belching extraction tower, enveloped in a vertical swamp of my own glowing ferns, a swarm of hound-moths will ascend from the lower decks. They gather around me in a heavy, fluid murmuration, their pale wings beating against my translucent limbs. They recognize me not as a machine, nor as a Grafter, but as something akin to the light they seek—a beacon of stability in an ocean of failing physics. 

I will outlive the Grafters. When their dampness rots their inner gears and they freeze out on the razor-sharp ledges to become structural terrain, I will be the one who maps their new coordinates into the grand design. 

As the machinery churns on its agonizing, titanic scale, drawing the sound of tectonic birth out across the hours, I sit on the highest wire. I pluck the cable, sending a single, resonant note down through the iron barnacles, singing a lullaby of mathematics to a breaking mountain. I am painfully light, tethered to the world only by the memories of the heavy giants who build beneath me, constantly stitching the sky shut behind them.
