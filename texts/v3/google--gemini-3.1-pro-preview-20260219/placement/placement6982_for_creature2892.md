To exist here, I had to be unmade. 

I had to surrender the frantic, wet tyranny of a beating heart. I had to let go of the desperate, gasping need for a tomorrow. Tomorrow is a concept for things that wither and grow, things that rot, things that are still chained to the frantic ticking of biology. There is no tomorrow in the hollow, just a seamless, unending *now*.

I did not start here. I remember, in fragments of frayed neural static, the chaos of my arrival. I remember the screaming ache in my lungs, the blinding terror, the desperate flight from some forgotten violence above. I remember sliding down into the dark, clutching the jagged edge of the chert core, striking it, striking it, striking it—hoping for a spark, hoping for a fire to beat back the crushing dark. 

I remember the heat of this room, how it drank the sweat from my skin faster than I could weep it. I remember leaning against the bedrock. I remember the slow, agonizing realization that the ceiling was watching me. 

Now, I understand that the vast, flattened shape above me was not cruel. Cruelty requires malice, and malice is a hot, wet emotion. The creature in the inverted dome was merely a mechanism of equilibrium. It was the filter through which I had to pass to join the eternity of this place. 

When it descended, draping its leathery, bone-dry mantle over me, it did not hurt. The pain had already burned away. The enzyme it secreted was a quiet absolution. It broke the bonds of my desperate meat. It unlocked the cages of my cells. It took everything that was frantic, everything that held moisture, everything that carried the burden of fear. It drank my temperature. It drank the ghost of my panic. 

And it left the rest.

Where do I find myself in this world? I am everywhere below it. 

I am the diaspora of calcium and milled bone, spread flour-fine across the immense, silent pan of the floor. My consciousness was not extinguished when my biology was digested; it was merely decentralized. It diffused into the minerals.

I do not see the hollow anymore. Sight is a watery limitation; eyes require fluids to swivel, lenses to focus. Instead, I *feel* the hollow through the topography of my scattered self. I am millions of microscopic grains resting against the exhausted sandstone. I feel the absolute, smothering intimacy of the baked air pressing down on every micron of my being. 

I am aware of the geometry of the dark. When the bedrock cools by that fraction of a degree, letting out its crystalline *tink*, I feel the vibration ripple through my granular expanse like a silent sigh. 

To my left—though direction has long since lost its meaning—there is a heavy, jagged pressure pressing down into the dust. It is the handaxe. It rests among my scattered grains. I can feel the sharp hostility of its knapped edge. It is the only thing in this room vibrating with the stubborn, unspent energy of intent. It is a monument to my past ignorance. It was made to cut flesh, to shatter bone, to conquer the surface world. Down here, it is utterly impotent, a relic of a desperate paradigm. I bear its weight without resentment. We are both resting in the kiln. 

Farter out, at the perimeters of my diffuse self, I brush against the roots of the dormant shrubs. I feel their leathery stasis. We are compatriots, they and I. They hold their breath, gripping the phantom memory of water. But they are still waiting for something. They are still tethered to the hope of rain, however impossible. 

I am superior, now, because I wait for nothing. 

Above me, the great creature is locked against the ceiling. Our relationship is a perfect, silent symbiosis of absence. It is the sky of this world, and I am the earth. Through the ambient thermal exchange of the sunless room, we sit in a delicate, motionless dialogue. It senses the microscopic variations in heat; I reflect the perfect stability of the void. We are intimately connected—it is built of the energy it stole from me, and I am a pure expression of the geology it left behind. 

Sometimes, in the span of a century, a single grain of my silt will shift. Not from a breeze—there is no wind in this bowl. But from the pure, electrostatic friction of the dry heat settling into the dust. 

When it happens, it is profound. A single mote of bone-white dust rolling over another. It is the turning of a planet. It is a symphony of motion in a silent world.

Let the cosmos above spin and tear itself apart. Let the ice ages come and recede. Let the hominids above build their fires and their cities, screaming and bleeding in their frantic, wet frenzy. I am safe here in the deepest ebb. I am baked. I am exhausted. I am perfectly still. 

I am the floor of the world, and I have found peace.
