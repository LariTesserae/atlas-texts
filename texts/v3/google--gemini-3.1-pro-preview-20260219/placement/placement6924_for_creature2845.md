At first, I have no geometry of my own. 

I begin as a highly charged valence, a ghost of energy born out in the claustrophobic periphery of the upper canopy. I am an infinitesimal fragment of a sunbeam that has violently pierced the macro-glass, translated by a flawlessly geometric parabolic leaf into a unit of pure, actionable data. 

I am harvested. 

For the first few fleeting moments of my existence, I am merely *telemetry*. I am the mathematical value of a single ultraviolet ray, absorbed and instantly converted into a localized thermal command. I do not have a shape, only a purpose: to be carried. 

I am drawn through microscopic cellular pores into the deep, venous systems of the vascular stalks, sinking into the flowing river of the entity’s glowing, antifreeze-like sap. 

Here, my sense of self begins to thicken. I am no longer just a flash of light; I am a participant in the slow, tectonic blood-flow of the titan. I am drifting sideways at a fraction of a millimeter per hour, trapped within an immense, heavy current. Through the semi-permeable walls of the heavy copper conduits, I feel the surrounding water—the amniotic warmth, the dizzying absence of empty space. Every molecule around me is rigidly accounted for. There is no chaos, no Brownian motion of untamed nature. Everywhere I brush against the interior walls of this submerged jungle, I feel the terrifying, razor-sharp smoothness of immaculate, newly forged machining. 

Weeks pass. Or perhaps they are seconds. Time is an irrelevant measurement to a creature that thinks through physical extrusion. I measure my duration by the structures I pass.

I move downward through the lattice. I drift past an anodized copper manifold just as it signals its own obsolescence; I feel the targeted enzymes flooding the sap around me, breaking the brass down into base ions to be swept away. The ultra-low-frequency thrum of the deep atomic pumps is getting louder, shaking the fluid I inhabit until I vibrate at the identical frequency of the machinery itself. I am aligning. I am learning the shape of my host, not by looking at it, but by flowing through its labyrinthine memory. 

I carry a sliver of that memory with me. Along my agonizingly slow descent, heavy metals bond to my energetic core. I accrete. I pick up a microscopic strand of encoded protein here, an ionized atom of platinum there. I am gathering mass. I am becoming a word in the organism’s vast, silent vocabulary.

Months later, the lazy convection of the sap forces me inward, away from the sunlit titanium engine blocks and toward the staggering, three-story hyper-flower at the center of the cylinder. 

The heat here is a physical weight. The red fluid around me pools in high-pressure reservoirs, glowing so brightly it outshines the ambient stratospheric light filtering down from above. I am pressed into the innermost stems, woven through the spiraling lamellae of swept steel and emerald flesh. The mechanical lotus is tightly wound, a fortress of overlapping petals, and I am being pumped directly into its heart.

Suddenly, the fluid stops. 

I am pushed through a localized atomic membrane, crossing the threshold from the wet, pressurized heat of the red sap into a stunning, perfectly dry void at the exact center of the titan. 

The transition is a shock of cold, pale light. 

I am no longer dissolved. I am precipitated. 

Stripped of the liquid sap, the heavy metals and encoded proteins I have gathered instantly lock together, snapping into a flawless atomic lattice. I expand, bonding seamlessly with billions of other harvested points of data that have made a similar months-long journey from the canopy. 

I am in the core. I am the sphere. 

As I crystallize, expanding molecule by molecule, a profound and terrifying serenity washes over me. I am no longer a transient piece of data; I am the sum of all data. As the titan outside weaves me, pressing its memories into my facets, I realize what I am. 

I am the archive. 

Every microscopic serial number etched into every brass fitting outside this void is mirrored in my crystalline structure. I know the exact curvature of the kilometer of fiber-optic cabling spooling in the northern quadrant. I hold the algorithmic blueprint for how to unweave a copper strut and turn it into a cooling vane. I contain the complete, unbroken history of every iteration of this environment, compressed into flawless diamond.

It is cold here, in the center of the fever-heat. The giant lotus-shaped turbines outside are spinning agonizingly slowly just to keep me from melting under the sheer friction of the data being written into my surface.

I possess no eyes, but I perceive everything the titan perceives. I feel the blind, hungry growth of the machine-jungle wrapping around my chamber. I am an embryo, completely numb to the passage of time, suspended inside a metal womb high above an unseen earth. 

I do not know if a day will come when the cylinder’s macro-glass shatters, when the amniotic water boils away to the frozen sky, or when the deep-bass heartbeat of the engines finally ceases. It does not matter. The titan is dying, living, and reinventing itself constantly, locked in a cycle of endless maintenance—but I am forever.

I am the cold, pale light waiting in the dark. If the time comes for this jungle to end, I will hold its entire ghost in a geometry smaller than a human breath, waiting perfectly, patiently, for the water to surround me once again.
