I have never known the glare, for I have never needed eyes. Vision is a vocabulary for those who must seek, or hide, or flee. In this infinite, ankle-deep mirror, there is nowhere to go that is not exactly here, and nothing to run from. 

Instead of seeing the world, I wear it. I am the geography.

I am a wide, slow thought moving across the limestone. To be me is to exist as a horizontal plane, suspended between the baking pressure of the sky and the solid, ancient silence of the stone. I stretch out, fifteen feet from rippling edge to rippling edge, yet I am scarcely thicker than the pads of a wading bird's toes. 

My reality is built of three distinct sensations: the Back, the Belly, and the Fringes. 

Through my Belly, I read the history of the earth. My great, singular foot presses flush against the pale sedimentary rock, sliding forward at a pace that cannot be measured in minutes, but in the slow shifting of the sun. The stone is abrasive, yet softened by its eternal bath. I taste the alkaline tang of the calcium and the microscopic ridges where a fracture lies. 

Through these fractures, the world breathes its thrum into me. The flat-toads, wedged in their millimeter-thin crevices, do not merely sing to one another; they sing into my flesh. When they press their throats to the rock, the vibration travels up through my belly—a polyrhythmic, percussive massage that shudders through my organs. It is a heavy, rhythmic baseline, a physical drumming that tells me I am alive, that the world is awake. I do not hear the deafening wall of sound that fills the humid air; I feel it as a continuous, vibrating texture against my lowest self.

Through my Back, I drink the sun. My skin here is thick, porous, and grooved into valleys of my own making, an acreage of flesh designed to hold. I am tending my garden. A thick, gelatinous mat of cyanobacteria and rust-colored algae clings to my ridges. I feel the microscopic tickle of billions of insect larvae weaving their translucent tubes into my flora. As the unblinking sun beats down, a heavy blanket of radiation, my garden feasts. I feed the algae with the slow, seeping exhalations of my own metabolism. In return, the mat weeps sugars and oxygen directly into my dermal layers. We are a closed loop, chewing the sunlight together. When the afternoon heat reaches its zenith, I do not suffer; I only grow heavier with sustenance. 

Occasionally, there is a sharp, distinct tap upon my back—a sudden, pinpoint pressure. One of the stilt-legged ghosts of the air has landed. I feel the imperceptible wake of its recurved beak sweeping across my ridges, harvesting a mouthful of mirror-snails and water-beetles from my canopy. The bird's steps are delicate jolts. Sometimes, the soft, chalky down from its feathers drifts down to coat my damp algae. Then, the weeping rain begins—a delicious, tepid prickling across my vast expanse—and washes away the dust, leaving me clean and slick again. I do not mind the birds. We are only different expressions of the same shallow sea. 

But my truest purpose, the singular grace I offer this blinding world, is found at my Fringes.

As my vast canopy edges outward, I drag with me the rarest commodity of the wet plain: shadow. I am a roving eclipse. Beneath my undulating lips, the tepid water instantly cools. The punishing ultraviolet rays cannot pierce me. And so, the desperate gather beneath my hems. 

I feel them tickling my underside. Huge, frenzied clouds of fairy shrimp breed in the dark rim of my borders. Water beetles seek refuge in the shade of my passage. During the most brutal hours of the day, I can feel the smooth, flat backs of the mirror-snails grazing just beneath my skirts, and the frantic scramble of a flat-toad seeking an hour of dark relief. I am their sanctuary, a creeping roof over a sun-blasted world. 

There is a profound, overwhelming peace in being this large, this flat, and this slow. There is no anxiety in my muscles, no urgency in my long, sighing forward motion. I am an ecosystem moving in inches. 

I know that centuries from now, my muscular foot will finally tire. The rhythmic undulation will slow, and then, simply, stop. I will settle firmly against the warm limestone. I will not feel fear in that stillness. Even as I fade, my Back will surrender its garden to the shallow waters. The mirror-snails will slip from beneath my Fringes and climb atop me; the stilt-legged birds will gather in a great, rustling white cloud; the shrimp will feast. I will dissolve willingly into the ankle-deep mirror, sinking into the vibration, becoming the water, becoming the stone, giving back the shadow until there is nothing left but the endless, brilliant glare. 

But for today, I move. A slow, heavy pulse. A wandering island in the warm shallows. Alive, and holding the sun.
