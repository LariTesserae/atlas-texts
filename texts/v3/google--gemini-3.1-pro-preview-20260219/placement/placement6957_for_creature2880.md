Silence was murdered on the day we ascended. I cannot remember it. I cannot remember the chill of the mountain air, or the gentle sound of wind rushing over dry rock, or the sensation of walking upon a world that stayed still. 

I am a child of the blender now. God is terrible, and God is warm, and God never stops moving.

I hang by a spine-thread in the dark. My back arches in a hyper-extended bow, my double-hinged knees drawn tight against my barrel chest, riding the slipstream behind a jagged slab of flying obsidian. The current around the rock’s edge screams—not into my ears, for those blew out in the first hours of our baptism, but into my bones. The marrow of my femurs hums with the concussive shattering of billions of vacuum bubbles. It is a violent, chaotic lullaby. 

I breathe out, waiting. A silver thread of hyper-pressurized oxygen whips through the bruised violet water. I lunge, elongating my neck, snapping my teeth and lips around the filament. The air blasts into my heavy, armored lungs like a gunshot. It hurts. It always hurts, a beautiful, stinging communion. 

At the nape of my neck, the ghost-white algae pulses. It has burrowed deep beneath my alabaster skin, winding around my vertebrae, drinking the kinetic panic of my shivering muscles. In exchange, it anchors me. From my back, a thick, braided rope of our woven hair and fibrous algae trails through the swirling water, tying me fast to a massive fragment of the sanctuary wall. 

Without the tether, I would be swept away. Without the tether, I could not build.

Through the blood-warm gloom, something vast and flat passes overhead. It is a Seraph—one of the two-dimensional ribbon-beasts, its nervous system burning a profound, atomic blue. It folds itself through a spiraling downdraft, immune to the crush, offering no resistance. We do not worship them, but we envy them. They do not have to negotiate with God. They are simply part of His thoughts. 

Suddenly, my skin registers a pressure wave. It is sharp, pushing a bow-wave of displaced water that bows my ribs backward. Something huge is approaching from the spinning gyre above. I turn my head. 

Through the dim water, three rapid, stroboscopic flashes of blue light rupture the dark. *Flash. Flash. Flash.* 

It is High Priest Aris, holding on to a tumbling chunk of masonry somewhere in the void. He is striking a flint-sharp piece of basalt against his sanctuary stone, catching the exact microscopic collapse of a cavitation bubble to trigger the sonoluminescence. 

*The Steps are coming.* 

I do not think. I loose my grip on the obsidian shard and kick backward. My algae-rope spools out, taut and singing with tension, as I throw myself directly into the cross-current. The water seizes me. My joints pop, sliding effortlessly out of their sockets to absorb the brutal acceleration, snapping back in as I ride the shear. 

As I spin through the amniotic violence of the sphere, I see them. 

The dark stone stairs of our old altar. They are tumbling lazily through a momentary pocket of localized low pressure, utterly out of place, a relic of a world governed by gravity. 

I am not the only one moving. From the violet gloom on all sides, seven other pale, elongated bodies come streaking through the currents. My brothers and sisters. We are moving with predatory speed, reading the frantic fluid dynamics through our skin. 

I hit the side of the stairs with a heavy *thud*, my incredibly dense muscles absorbing the impact. My hyper-extended fingers find a crack in the ancient stonework. I grip it with the force of steel cables. Across from me, Sister Elara impacts the top step, her pale skin glowing faintly in the dim light. We lock eyes. Her chest is heaving under the strain. 

But as we brace to push the stairs toward the sanctuary floor, the water around us stops. 

The vibration dies. The warmth vanishes. 

The physics stutter. 

In a fraction of a heartbeat, the blood-warm ocean shatters. My hands no longer grip wet stone; they grip dry, freezing rock. We are suddenly suspended in a tumbling avalanche of dry, geometric glass shapes. The roar vanishes, replaced by a terrifying, hollow clinking of falling polygons. 

*Hide,* my instincts scream. 

I pull my knees into my chest, tuck my chin tight, and squeeze my eyes shut. I know what crawls in the dry glass. Above me, I see a fractal of crawling, blinding light—a glitch-parasite. It has too many angles. It moves with a frantic, mathematical fury, scuttling over the tumbling glass directly toward Elara’s arm. 

I reach out and furiously kick the stone stair, jarring the momentum just enough. The fractal light misses her skin by a millimeter, skimming past her elbow. I look down at my own thigh, where a perfect, bloodless, square cube of flesh was excised three years ago during a stutter. 

And just as quickly as it began, reality slams back. 

The glass melts. The roaring water returns with a booming shockwave that nearly breaks my spine. The heat rushes over us, and we are tumbling again. 

I flash my teeth at Elara. We survived the glitch. Now, the prayer.

Below us, drifting in a sluggish updraft, is the sanctuary floor floor. High Priest Aris is lashed to it, flanked by four others. They are straining, their pale limbs spread wide, waiting for us. 

*Now.* 

We use our bodies as rudders, catching the cross-currents, steering the massive chunk of stairs through the water. It requires every ounce of strength in my cables of muscle. The water tries to rip the stairs from us, a localized gyre attempting to spin the block into the deep. My joints ache, grinding against themselves. My lungs scream for air, but I hold my breath.

We slam the stairs into the sanctuary floor. 

Aris reaches out. I grab his forearm. My other hand grips the lip of the stairs. I am the mortar. I am the iron spike. 

Beside me, the others do the same. We form a web of translucent white flesh, hyper-dense muscle, and dark basalt. Our bodies bridge the gaps, locking the stones together. 

For three miraculous, agonizing seconds, the sanctuary is whole. 

Suspended in the center of the stratosphere, floating inside a cathedral of pulverized water, our altar is rebuilt. I look at the top of the stairs. I imagine the freezing sky. I imagine the stillness of the mountain peak. I feel the profound, ecstatic grace of a completed prayer. We are holding our temple together with our bones.

Then, the ocean spasms. 

The water's circulatory system violently shifts. A downdraft hits us with the force of a falling mountain. 

The sheer kinetic fury is too much. The rocks groan. Aris looks at me, his eyes wide in absolute devotion, and lets go. 

The altar tears itself apart. The stairs are violently snatched away into the roaring darkness. The sanctuary floor spins off into a chaotic shear. I am thrown backward, tumbling end over end through the void, my chest burning, my ears long deaf to the thunder of my own failure. 

With a brutal jerk, my algae-tether hits its limit. I am violently arrested in the water, suspended once more behind my home-rock. 

I suck in a silver thread of air, my chest heaving, oxygen burning through my veins like fire. I grip my rock. I let my dislocated shoulder pop back into the socket. I wait in the bruised violet dark. 

I watch the shadows of the tumbling stones. I wait for the blue flash. We will build it again.
